# Keeper of Dreams: Short Fiction

**Author:** Orson Scott Card  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

Orson Scott Card has long held a revered place in the pantheon of speculative fiction, primarily celebrated for his sweeping epic novels and profound philosophical explorations. Yet, it is within the intimate, tightly wound boundaries of the short story that Card’s literary architecture often reveals its most intricate genius. Keeper of Dreams: Short Fiction serves as a monumental testament to this mastery, acting as both a companion piece to his earlier career-spanning retrospective Maps in a Mirror and a vibrant showcase of his maturation as a writer from 1990 onward. Comprising twenty-two diverse narratives, this collection is not merely an anthology; it is a curated cabinet of curiosities, offering a penetrating look into the mind of a modern master.

The prevailing thematic undercurrent binding this eclectic collection is the intricate, often fragile relationship between humanity and the unknown—whether that unknown manifests as the vast expanse of science fiction, the mystical borders of fantasy, or the quiet, devastating landscapes of mainstream contemporary life. Card’s style is immediately recognizable across these disparate genres: it is deeply empathetic, intellectually rigorous, and anchored by a profound moral curiosity. He does not write merely to entertain, though he does so effortlessly; he writes to interrogate the human condition, forcing characters and readers alike to confront ethical dilemmas that resist easy categorization.

In the collection's opening science fiction piece, "The Elephants of Poznan," Card takes a familiar speculative premise and breathes startling, bizarre life into it, demonstrating his signature ability to make the wondrous feel unsettlingly plausible. Elsewhere, stories like "Angles" playfully dismantle the mechanics of alternate realities, while the long-awaited "Geriatric Ward"—originally penned for the legendary anthology Last Dangerous Visions—delivers a biting, poignant commentary on aging and societal value. For devotees of Card’s larger universe-building, the inclusion of tales from the Alvin Maker series, such as "Grinning Man" and "The Yazoo Queen," provides a welcome return to his rich, folkloric alternate America, blending historical texture with magical realism.

What elevates Keeper of Dreams from a standard genre collection into an essential literary experience is the inclusion of Card’s personal introductions for every single story. These brief, candid commentaries transform the reading process into an intimate dialogue between author and audience. Card pulls back the curtain on his creative process, offering rare glimpses into the biographical moments, frustrations, and inspirations that birthed each tale. This meta-narrative layer adds an invaluable depth, allowing readers to witness the intersection of Card’s lived reality and his boundless imagination.

Ultimately, Keeper of Dreams: Short Fiction is a triumphant reaffirmation of why the Houston Post once dubbed Orson Scott Card "the best writer science fiction has to offer." It captures a versatile storyteller operating at the absolute height of his powers, seamlessly navigating the speculative and the mundane with equal grace and psychological acuity. Whether you are a lifelong devotee or a newcomer looking to understand the breadth of Card’s literary vision, this collection is an indispensable journey into the heart of modern speculative storytelling.
