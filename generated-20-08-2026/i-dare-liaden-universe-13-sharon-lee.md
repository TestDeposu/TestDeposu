# I Dare (Liaden Universe, #13)

**Author:** Sharon Lee  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

I Dare (Liaden Universe, #13) by Sharon Lee (with Steve Miller) delivers the long‑awaited climax of the Agent of Change sequence, a saga that has woven intricate politics, familial loyalty, and high‑stakes adventure across the glittering tapestry of the Liaden cosmos. The novel opens with the sudden return of Val Con yos’Phelium, the former Scout turned reluctant patriarch, whose demand for Balance and retribution sets a chain reaction that threatens to ignite interstellar war on the very surface of fabled Liad. Opposing him is his brother Pat Rin yos’Phelium, the charming, ever‑gambling ne’er‑do‑well who has been courted by Korval’s enemies with promises of absolute power—a Ring that would grant him dominion over the Clan’s fortunes and a world offered by a Juntavas Judge. The stage is set for a duel not just of swords and blasters, but of ideologies: the disciplined, duty‑bound ethos of the Scout versus the reckless, chance‑driven allure of the gambler.

At its core, I Dare grapples with the theme of balance—both the cosmic equilibrium that the Liaden philosophy stresses and the personal equilibrium each character must find between responsibility and desire. Lee and Miller use the brothers’ contrasting paths to explore how heritage shapes destiny while still leaving room for individual agency. Val Con’s stoic insistence on restoring order reflects the traditional Liaden belief that personal honor must serve the greater good, yet his internal struggle reveals the cost of suppressing one’s own instincts. Pat Rin, meanwhile, embodies the seductive danger of embracing chaos; his gambling metaphor extends beyond literal wagers to the risks he takes with loyalty, love, and the very fabric of Korval’s society. Their conflict forces readers to ask whether true balance can be achieved through rigid adherence to tradition or whether it sometimes requires a daring leap into the unknown.

The authors’ style remains a hallmark of the series: lush world‑building paired with tight, character‑driven prose. Lee’s narrative voice slips effortlessly between the grandiose sweep of interstellar intrigue and the intimate, almost tactile moments of sibling banter, tender affection, and sudden violence. Dialogue crackles with wit and cultural nuance, revealing the Liaden’s distinctive blend of formality and hidden emotion. Action sequences are rendered with kinetic clarity—starship dogfights, close‑quarters combat, and high‑stakes gambits unfold with a rhythm that keeps the pulse racing without sacrificing the deeper emotional undercurrents. Moreover, the novel’s pacing mirrors its titular dare: it starts with a measured, almost contemplative build‑up as the brothers regroup, then accelerates into a series of escalating confrontations that feel inevitable yet surprising, each twist grounded in the established lore while opening new avenues for future exploration.

What elevates I Dare beyond a typical space‑opera finale is its commitment to the Liaden Universe’s moral complexity. Rather than presenting a clear‑cut hero versus villain dichotomy, Lee and Miller allow shades of grey to color every decision. The agents of change—those who dare to shift the balance—are portrayed not as reckless radicals but as necessary catalysts whose motives are as varied as the stars themselves. This nuanced treatment invites readers to reflect on the cost of progress, the weight of legacy, and the personal sacrifices demanded when the fate of an entire civilization hangs in the balance.

Fans of the series will find satisfaction in the way long‑running threads—Clan Korval’s internal politics, the lingering shadows of past conflicts, the enigmatic Judge’s machinations—are pulled together with both reverence for what has come before and a boldness that points toward fresh horizons. Newcomers, though advised to start earlier in the sequence to fully appreciate the depth of references, will still be drawn in by the vivid characters, the palpable tension between duty and desire, and the sheer enjoyment of watching two brothers—each a master of his own kind of gamble—collide in a contest that could reshape their world.

In sum, I Dare is a fitting, exhilarating conclusion to the Agent of Change arc: a novel that marries Sharon Lee’s signature blend of lyrical prose, intricate world‑building, and morally resonant storytelling with the propulsive drive of a space‑faring thriller. It challenges its protagonists—and its readers—to consider what it truly means to dare, to wager, and ultimately to restore balance in a universe where every choice ripples across galaxies.
