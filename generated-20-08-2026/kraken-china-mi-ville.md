# Kraken

**Author:** China Miéville  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

When a thirty-foot specimen of *Architeuthis dux*—the giant squid—mysteriously vanishes from its formaldehyde-soaked sanctuary in London’s Natural History Museum, it does not merely constitute a bizarre theft. According to China Miéville’s wildly imaginative 2010 novel, *Kraken*, it marks the precipitating detonation of an apocalyptic war for the fate of reality itself. 

At the center of this surreal tempest stands Billy Harrow, a mild-mannered cephalopod specialist whose life is upended the moment his prized exhibit simply ceases to exist. Suddenly thrust out of the mundane world and into the secret, arcane undercurrents of the British capital, Billy finds himself hunted by an eccentric and terrifying menagerie of factions. There are the radical zealots of the Congregation of God Kraken, who worship the vanished beast as a divine embryonic god; the FSRC, a specialized police unit that combats sorcery with its own brand of bureaucratic magic; and the Londonmancers, prophets who divine the future from the urban sprawl’s concrete entrails. Lurking in the shadows are even more menacing forces, such as the Tattoo, a merciless criminal mastermind inscribed upon human flesh, and Goss and Subby, a darkly charismatic, ageless duo whose casual sadism strikes fear into the hearts of hardened wizards. 

Miéville has long been celebrated as a master of the "New Weird" movement—a genre-defying approach to speculative fiction that subverts traditional fantasy tropes by dragging them kicking and screaming into gritty, hyper-modern, and often bureaucratic landscapes. In *Kraken*, this signature style reaches a fever pitch. The author treats magic not as an ethereal, high-fantasy art of grand spells and shining staves, but as a messy, institutionalized, and thoroughly working-class phenomenon. London itself becomes a breathing, pulsing entity, teeming with a secret populace of cultists, familiars, and hoodlum wizards. Miéville’s prose is relentlessly inventive, densely layered, and crackling with a biting, chaotic energy that mirrors the manic pace of the metropolis he so vividly depicts. 

The primary theme coursing through the dark, churning waters of *Kraken* is the intersection of belief, myth, and the modern urban experience. Miéville explores how ancient faith adapts—and frequently mutates—in a world dominated by science, concrete, and late capitalism. The giant squid is not just a biological marvel or a theological vessel; it is a catalyst that exposes the fragile, constructed nature of reality. Billy Harrow serves as our bewildered proxy, a man of science forced to navigate a universe where cephalopods hold the keys to eschatology and divine creation. Through this absurd yet utterly compelling premise, Miéville questions the stories we tell ourselves to make sense of a chaotic world, and what happens when those myths literally come back to swallow us whole.

Straddling the fine line between laugh-out-loud dark comedy and genuinely unsettling cosmic horror, *Kraken* is a dizzying, triumphant showcase of an author at the absolute height of his creative powers. It is a book that demands to be read not merely for its breakneck plot, but for the sheer, jaw-dropping audacity of its world-building. For anyone willing to dive into the murky depths of Miéville’s London, the rewards are as vast and strange as the deep ocean itself.
