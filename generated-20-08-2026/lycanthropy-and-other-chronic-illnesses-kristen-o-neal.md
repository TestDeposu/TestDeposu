# Lycanthropy and Other Chronic Illnesses

**Author:** Kristen O'Neal  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

Kristen O’Neal’s debut young adult novel, Lycanthropy and Other Chronic Illnesses, takes a wonderfully bizarre and high-concept premise—what happens when your online chronically ill best friend turns out to be a literal werewolf?—and grounds it in the painfully relatable, mundane struggles of navigating youth with a broken body. Part supernatural romp, part deeply intimate exploration of disability, O’Neal’s 2021 release successfully bridges the gap between genre fiction and contemporary emotional realism, emerging as a sharp, hilarious, and profoundly comforting read.

At its core, the novel’s main theme revolves around the isolating nature of chronic illness and the radical, lifesaving power of internet-forged community. The protagonist, Priya, had her bright future at Stanford abruptly derailed by undiagnosed Lyme disease, a devastating shift that leaves her trapped in her childhood bedroom while her peers move forward. Her salvation—and her anchor to sanity—is an online Discord support group affectionately dubbed “oof ouch my bones.” Through these digital connections, O’Neal brilliantly captures the modern teenage experience of illness: the dark humor used as a defense mechanism, the shared grief over lost potential, and the profound validation of being seen by people who truly understand what it means to hurt every single day. 

When Brigid, one of Priya’s closest virtual confidantes, suddenly ghosts the chat, Priya embarks on a frantic, illegal road trip to check on her. What she finds in Brigid’s basement is not just a friend in trouble, but a literal werewolf. Yet, in O'Neal’s deft hands, lycanthropy acts as a brilliant, multifaceted metaphor. The monstrous transformation—unpredictable, painful, socially alienating, and entirely beyond control—mirrors the lived reality of severe chronic illness. Just as Brigid must learn to live with the beast inside her, both girls are forced to confront the ways society expects them to sanitize, hide, or cure their suffering to be deemed acceptable. 

O’Neal’s authorial style is defined by its biting wit, authenticity, and profound empathy. Writing from personal experience with chronic illness, she infuses the narrative with a gritty, unromanticized honesty that never bogs down the pacing. The dialogue is snappy, contemporary, and laugh-out-loud funny, anchored heavily in the internet culture and communication styles that define modern youth. Text chains, Discord banter, and pop-culture references flow naturally, creating an immersive atmosphere that makes the fantastic elements of the story feel shockingly grounded. 

Furthermore, O'Neal expertly balances the tonal tightrope between horror-comedy and heartfelt coming-of-age drama. The werewolf mystery provides a propulsive narrative drive, but the true beating heart of the book lies in the evolving friendship between Priya and Brigid. Their dynamic challenges the toxic positivity often forced upon sick teenagers, allowing them to be angry, scared, exhausted, and fiercely loyal all at once. 

Lycanthropy and Other Chronic Illnesses is a triumph of contemporary YA. By weaving supernatural folklore into the very real horrors of medical gaslighting and bodily betrayal, Kristen O’Neal has crafted a fresh, touching, and fiercely entertaining novel. It is a story that reminds us that while we cannot always wish away our monsters—internal or external—we don't have to face them alone.
