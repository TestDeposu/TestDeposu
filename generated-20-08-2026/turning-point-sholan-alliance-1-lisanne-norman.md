# Turning Point (Sholan Alliance, #1)

**Author:** Lisanne Norman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1993

In the grand tradition of speculative fiction, first-contact novels often rely on grand spectacles of starships colliding and empires crumbling. Yet, in Turning Point, the inaugural volume of Lisanne Norman’s enduring Sholan Alliance series, the vast tapestry of intergalactic conflict is woven from threads of profound personal intimacy. Published in 1993, this novel stands as a compelling testament to the power of empathy, resilience, and the difficult bridge-building required when entirely different species must face a common enemy.

Norman immediately plunges the reader into the grim reality of occupation. The human colony of Keiss is bleeding under the brutal thumb of the Valtegan invaders—a relentless warrior race dedicated to total domination. The narrative cuts deep from the opening chapters, framing the cost of war not through casualty statistics, but through the shattering bond of twin telepaths, Carrie and Elise. When Elise is captured and ultimately killed by Valtegan soldiers, Carrie experiences every ounce of her sister's agony. This horrific trauma leaves Carrie stranded in a chilling mental void, a haunting exploration of isolation that anchors the science fiction high-concept in raw, visceral emotion.

It is here that Norman’s thematic core emerges. The void in Carrie's mind is not filled by human solace, but by Kusac, a felinoid crewman from a crashed starship. Through a shared telepathic and empathic Talent, Carrie and Kusac form an immediate, unshakable connection that transcends biological boundaries. Norman uses this bond to explore a central motif: true understanding requires vulnerability. In a universe fractured by fear and xenophobia, Carrie and Kusac’s ability to touch each other's thoughts serves as a microcosm of the larger struggle. 

The author’s style is direct, character-driven, and unflinchingly earnest. Norman does not rely on overly dense world-building jargon; instead, she prioritizes emotional resonance and interpersonal dynamics. The pacing of Turning Point moves briskly, balancing the tense cat-and-mouse dynamics of hiding an injured alien from the occupying Valtegans with the broader geopolitical chess match of uniting disparate civilizations. 

While the Valtegans initially present a terrifyingly monolithic threat—an unstoppable force ready to lay waste to any civilization they cannot conquer—the true conflict of the novel lies in the prejudice and hesitation of the victims. Convincing humans and Sholans to trust one another proves far more perilous than evading Valtegan patrols. Norman paints a realistic picture of alliance-building, showing that overcoming ingrained distrust is often the hardest battle in any war.

Turning Point is more than just a nostalgic 90s space opera starter; it is a thoughtful exploration of how empathy can become a weapon against tyranny. Lisanne Norman successfully lays the groundwork for a sweeping epic by reminding us that massive alliances are ultimately forged one fragile, trusting connection at a time. For fans of character-focused science fiction, alien cultures, and tales of underground resistance, this book is an essential gateway.
