# Keeping Secrets (Sam Mason Mystery #2)

**Author:** L.A. Dobbs  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2008

**Keeping Secrets: A Sharp, Suspenseful Dive into Corporate Deception**

L.A. Dobbs’ *Keeping Secrets*—the second installment in the Sam Mason mystery series—is a razor-sharp thriller that blends the cutthroat world of corporate accounting with the gritty, morally ambiguous terrain of investigative journalism. Set against the backdrop of a city teeming with towering law firms, accounting behemoths, and shadowy financial dealings, this novel doesn’t just follow a detective; it immerses readers in a high-stakes game where the real crime isn’t just murder—it’s the systematic erosion of trust. Dobbs, known for her meticulous research and knack for crafting morally complex protagonists, delivers a story that feels both urgent and eerily plausible, a testament to her ability to turn the mundane into the menacing.

At its core, *Keeping Secrets* is a tale of secrets—some buried, some whispered, and others screamed from the rooftops. The novel opens with a body, a suicide note, and a financial ledger that doesn’t add up. Enter Sam Mason, a journalist with a nose for fraud and a reputation for digging where others fear to tread. But Mason isn’t just chasing a killer; she’s unraveling a web of embezzlement, forgery, and corporate cover-ups that stretches across some of the most powerful firms in the city. The list of accounting and legal firms provided in the synopsis isn’t just window dressing—it’s a deliberate nod to the novel’s setting, a world where numbers are power, and the line between ethics and exploitation is often blurred. Dobbs doesn’t shy away from the bureaucratic jargon or the cold, clinical language of finance, using it as both a tool and a weapon. The reader isn’t just following a story; they’re being pulled into the mechanics of deception, where every receipt, every spreadsheet, and every signed contract could be a clue—or a lie.

What sets *Keeping Secrets* apart isn’t just its intricate plotting but Dobbs’ unflinching portrayal of her characters. Sam Mason is no lone wolf with a badge; she’s a woman who’s been burned before, who trusts no one, and who operates on the fringes of the law. Her journey is as much about survival as it is about justice, and Dobbs crafts her with a depth that makes her feel real—flawed, resilient, and deeply human. The supporting cast is equally compelling, from the slick, morally flexible corporate types to the desperate individuals caught in the crossfire. There’s no clear-cut heroism here; the novel thrives on moral ambiguity, where the "good guys" are often as compromised as the "bad guys."

Dobbs’ writing style is precise, almost surgical, with a knack for delivering tension in tight, economical prose. She doesn’t waste words on unnecessary exposition; instead, she drops readers into the action, letting the stakes build organically. The financial intricacies are never dumbed down, but they’re never overwhelming either. Dobbs has a talent for making the dry world of accounting feel as visceral as a knife fight, and her ability to weave financial fraud into a gripping narrative is a hallmark of her craft. By the time the final pages unfold, the reader isn’t just satisfied—they’re left with a lingering sense of unease, as if the real crime was never solved, but merely buried deeper.

The novel’s pacing is relentless, with each chapter peeling back another layer of the conspiracy. Dobbs excels at creating red herrings that feel genuine, at planting clues that mislead just enough to keep the reader guessing. The twist at the end isn’t just a shock—it’s a revelation that forces a reevaluation of everything that came before, a testament to Dobbs’ skill in subverting expectations without sacrificing narrative cohesion.

*Keeping Secrets* is more than just a mystery; it’s a dissection of power, greed, and the ways in which institutions protect their own. Dobbs doesn’t offer easy answers, and that’s what makes the book so compelling. In a world where trust is a commodity and the truth is often the first casualty, Sam Mason’s relentless pursuit of justice feels both necessary and futile—just like the real world she inhabits.

For fans of financial thrillers like *The Firm* or *The Big Short*, or for readers who enjoy morally complex protagonists in the vein of *True Detective*, *Keeping Secrets* is a must-read. It’s a book that lingers, not just because of its twists, but because of the way it makes the reader question the very systems it critiques. Dobbs has crafted a story that’s as sharp as the ledgers it dissects, and one that will leave readers questioning what they see—and what they’re willing to believe.
