# Habibi

**Author:** Craig Thompson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

In the realm of alternative comics, few creators have made a name for themselves like Craig Thompson, the critically acclaimed author of the poignant "Blankets" and the majestic "Habibi". The latter, published in 2011 but featured in the 2013 Alternative Comics anthology, is a sprawling, 672-page epic that defies easy categorization. Part fable, part romance, part exploration of the human condition, "Habibi" is a sweeping narrative that cements Thompson's reputation as one of the most innovative and daring cartoonists of his generation.

Set in a fantastical, unnamed Middle Eastern city, "Habibi" tells the story of Dodola and Zam, two escaped slaves who find refuge in a mystical garden. As their relationship blossoms, the narrative expands to encompass a vast array of themes and motifs, from the power of storytelling to the fragility of human connection. Thompson's prose is lyrical and expressive, his artwork a stunning blend of intricate patterns, sweeping vistas, and tender character studies.

One of the most striking aspects of "Habibi" is its use of visual metaphor. Thompson's pages are a riot of color and texture, with intricate Islamic geometric patterns and lush, organic forms that evoke the beauty and mystery of the natural world. At the same time, his characters are drawn with a simplicity and directness that makes them feel both timeless and utterly contemporary.

Thompson's style is both deeply personal and profoundly influenced by the cultural and artistic traditions that he draws upon. His use of Arabic calligraphy and geometric patterns is not merely decorative, but rather a way of tapping into the rich cultural heritage of the Middle East, while his narrative is informed by a deep sense of empathy and understanding for the people and places that he depicts.

The Alternative Comics anthology, which features "Habibi" alongside work by other innovative creators like Theo Ellsworth and James Kochalka, is a testament to the diversity and creativity of the indie comics scene. With its mix of established artists and newcomers, the anthology is a thrilling snapshot of the medium's current state, and a reminder that comics can be both a powerful tool for storytelling and a vibrant, expressive art form in their own right.

In short, "Habibi" is a masterpiece, a work of profound beauty and emotional resonance that will leave readers breathless and eager for more. If you're new to Thompson's work, this anthology is the perfect place to start; if you're already a fan, then you'll be thrilled to see his work alongside that of so many other talented creators.
