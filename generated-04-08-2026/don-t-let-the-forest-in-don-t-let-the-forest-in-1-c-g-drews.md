# Don't Let the Forest In (Don't Let The Forest In, #1)

**Author:** C.G. Drews  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2024

In the haunting and mesmerizing "Don't Let the Forest In," C.G. Drews masterfully weaves a dark and twisted tale of obsession, loyalty, and the blurred lines between reality and fantasy. This gripping YA psychological horror novel delves into the complex and often toxic relationships that can both destroy and redeem us.

At its core, the story revolves around the intense and all-consuming bond between Andrew Perrault and Thomas Rye, two high school seniors bound together by their shared love of twisted fairytales and their own dark pasts. As the narrative unfolds, Drews expertly explores the theme of codependency, raising questions about the true nature of love, loyalty, and sacrifice. Andrew's willingness to do anything for Thomas, including kill, is both captivating and unsettling, forcing the reader to confront the depths of their own emotions and the lengths to which they would go for those they care about.

Drews' writing style is a character in its own right, weaving a spell of atmospheric tension and foreboding that draws the reader inexorably into the dark world of Wickwood Academy. The author's use of language is both poetic and visceral, conjuring vivid images of the twisted monsters that haunt Thomas's drawings and the eerie, off-limits forest that seems to pulse with a malevolent life of its own.

One of the most striking aspects of "Don't Let the Forest In" is its exploration of the power of storytelling and the blurring of reality and fantasy. Andrew's fairytales, which initially serve as a source of comfort and escapism, gradually take on a life of their own, becoming a manifestation of the darkness that lurks within both him and Thomas. This raises important questions about the role of creativity in shaping our perceptions of the world and the dangers of becoming too deeply embroiled in our own narratives.

The characters themselves are multidimensional and complex, with Andrew and Thomas being expertly drawn as flawed, vulnerable, and deeply human. The supporting cast, including Andrew's twin sister Dove and Thomas's abusive parents, add depth and nuance to the narrative, highlighting the ways in which our relationships with others can both heal and harm us.

Overall, "Don't Let the Forest In" is a gripping and thought-provoking read that will appeal to fans of psychological horror and dark fantasy. Drews' masterful storytelling and vivid world-building make for a compelling narrative that will keep readers on the edge of their seats until the very end.
