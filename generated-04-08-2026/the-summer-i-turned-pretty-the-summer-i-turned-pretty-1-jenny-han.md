# The Summer I Turned Pretty (The Summer I Turned Pretty, #1)

**Author:** Jenny Han  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

**A Summer of Longing, Loss, and the Unavoidable Beauty of Change**

Jenny Han’s *The Summer I Turned Pretty* is more than a coming-of-age story—it’s a tender, sun-drenched meditation on love, family, and the quiet ache of growing up. Set against the golden backdrop of a New England beach house, the novel follows Belly Conway, a girl who measures her life in summers, where every year brings new layers of emotion, new heartbreaks, and the inescapable pull of two brothers: Jeremiah and Conrad. Han’s prose is warm, lyrical, and effortlessly immersive, weaving together nostalgia, longing, and the kind of emotional truth that lingers long after the last page.

At its core, *The Summer I Turned Pretty* explores the bittersweet nature of first love and the way it reshapes everything in its wake. Belly’s summers are sacred—her family’s annual retreat to the beach house is where she feels most alive, where she dreams of love and belonging. But this summer, everything is different. Jeremiah, the older brother she’s always adored, is back, and Conrad, the younger one she’s never quite seen beyond her brotherly affection, is there too. As the weeks unfold, Belly finds herself caught between two worlds: the comfort of her childhood crush on Jeremiah and the intoxicating newness of Conrad’s presence. Han doesn’t shy away from the complexity of these feelings, portraying Belly’s internal conflict with raw honesty. There’s no neat resolution, no easy choice—just the messy, beautiful reality of wanting two things that can’t coexist.

What makes the novel so compelling is Han’s ability to balance heartache with hope. The story isn’t just about the pain of growing up; it’s about the ways love, even in its most complicated forms, can feel like a second home. The beach house itself becomes a character, its salty air and flickering fireflies a constant reminder of the summers that shaped Belly. Han’s writing is rich with sensory details—the sound of waves, the scent of sunscreen, the way the sun sets over the water—all of which ground the emotional turmoil in something tangible. It’s a love letter to the places and people that define us, even when they change us in ways we never expected.

The novel also shines in its portrayal of family and the bonds that transcend time. Belly’s relationship with her mother, Susannah, is particularly poignant, a quiet but powerful dynamic that adds depth to the story. Susannah’s own history with Jeremiah and Conrad is hinted at in ways that make their presence feel like a living, breathing part of Belly’s world. Han doesn’t romanticize family—there are tensions, unspoken rules, and moments of frustration—but she also doesn’t underestimate the power of love to endure, even when it’s imperfect.

One of the most striking aspects of *The Summer I Turned Pretty* is its refusal to offer easy answers. Belly’s journey isn’t about finding a perfect love or a neat ending; it’s about accepting that some things are meant to be, even if they hurt. The novel’s emotional payoff isn’t in a grand revelation but in the quiet acceptance of change. By the time the final pages turn, readers will feel the weight of Belly’s choices, the ache of her heart, and the undeniable truth that some summers—some loves—are simply meant to be pretty, even if they leave you changed forever.

For fans of Jenny Han’s signature blend of romance, family drama, and emotional depth, this book is a must-read. It’s a story that lingers, a summer you’ll want to revisit, and a reminder that sometimes, the most beautiful things in life are the ones that leave you a little bit broken—and a lot more alive.
