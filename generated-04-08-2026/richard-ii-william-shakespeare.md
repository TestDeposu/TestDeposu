# Richard II

**Author:** William Shakespeare  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

Title: The Tragedy of the Gilded Crown: Shakespeare’s Masterclass in Divine Right and Human Folly

In Richard II, William Shakespeare delivers a profound and arresting meditation on the fragility of power, the dangerous illusion of divine right, and the heavy cost of political vanity. Long overshadowed by its louder, more bombastic successor plays in the Henriad cycle, this 1595 masterpiece stands alone as one of the Elizabethan stage's most lyrical and psychologically nuanced explorations of kingship. 

At the heart of the drama is King Richard himself, a monarch more captivated by the theater of rule than its heavy labor. Against the urgent warnings of his counselors, Richard drains England’s treasury to fuel his extravagant tastes in fashion and to lavishly reward his inner circle. To sustain this cycle of wasteful spending, he resorts to predatory taxation of the commoners and the leasing of royal lands to wealthy noblemen. When the wealthy and formidable John of Gaunt falls gravely ill, Richard’s predatory instincts flare anew; he eyes Gaunt’s vast estates as a convenient funding source for his military campaigns in Ireland, proving utterly blind to the looming precipice of rebellion.

Shakespeare wastes no time setting the wheels of tragedy in motion. The play opens with a bitter aristocratic feud at Windsor Castle, where Henry Bolingbroke—Gaunt’s fierce and capable son—accuses Thomas Mowbray of high treason, including the murder of the Duke of Gloucester and the embezzlement of royal funds. As Gaunt’s attempts at reconciliation fail, Richard steps in to decree a trial by combat. Yet, in a characteristic display of capricious authority, Richard halts the duel at the very last second, banishing both men from the realm. This fateful act sets off a chain reaction that ultimately strips Richard of his crown and hands it to the rising, pragmatic force of Bolingbroke.

What elevates Richard II above a mere historical chronicle is Shakespeare’s breathtaking mastery of language and style. Uniquely among Shakespeare’s history plays, Richard II is written almost entirely in verse. The stylistic contrast between characters is striking: Bolingbroke speaks with the calculated, sparse prose and pragmatic verse of a man of action, while Richard speaks in the elevated, metaphorical poetry of a man who views his life as a grand theatrical tragedy. Richard is a poet-king, more concerned with the divine symbolism of his crown than the administrative reality of wielding it. As his fortunes wane, his language becomes an exquisite, melancholic aria of suffering. Shakespeare masterfully uses this poetic interiority to transform a historically controversial monarch into a deeply sympathetic, tragic figure. 

The central theme of the play—the tension between the divine right of kings and the practical necessity of good governance—remains startlingly relevant. Shakespeare exposes the terrifying vulnerability of a nation led by a ruler detached from reality. Through Richard, we witness the agonizing de-crowning of a man who slowly realizes that sacred oil cannot protect a monarch from his own poor choices. Through Bolingbroke, we see the grim rise of utilitarian politics, where efficiency trumps legitimacy. 

This Standard Ebooks edition, drawing upon William George Clark and William Aldis Wright’s esteemed 1887 Victoria text, preserves the pristine rhythm and poetic brilliance of Shakespeare’s original vision. It invites modern readers to bear witness to a master dramatist dissecting the psychology of power with unmatched eloquence. 

Richard II is not just a study of medieval English history; it is a timeless theatrical autopsy of leadership, vanity, and the inevitable fall of those who confuse the trappings of power with its responsibilities. Striking, deeply moving, and intellectually rigorous, it remains an essential cornerstone of the English literary canon.
