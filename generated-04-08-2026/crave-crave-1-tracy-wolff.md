# Crave (Crave, #1)

**Author:** Tracy Wolff  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

**Crave by Tracy Wolff: A Dark, Addictive Love Story That Will Haunt You**

Tracy Wolff’s *Crave*—the first installment in the wildly popular *Crave* series—is a gothic romance so intoxicating, so steeped in danger and desire, that it feels like stepping into a fever dream. At its core, this isn’t just a paranormal love story; it’s a psychological unraveling, a battle between self-destruction and salvation, wrapped in the kind of lush, atmospheric prose that makes you forget you’re even reading. Wolff masterfully blends the tropes of vampire romance with the raw, messy reality of trauma, identity, and the terrifying allure of the unknown.

The novel follows an unnamed protagonist—a mortal girl thrust into the brutal, glamorous world of the *Academy*, a place where gods and monsters coexist in a fragile, violent balance. From the moment she steps inside, she’s an outsider, a target, a pawn in a game she doesn’t understand. The Academy isn’t just a school; it’s a battleground, a prison, a place where power is currency and loyalty is a liability. Wolff’s world-building is immersive, painting a vivid picture of a society where beauty is weaponized, where status is earned through blood and betrayal. The tension is palpable, the stakes impossibly high, and the protagonist’s isolation is so well-crafted that you’ll ache for her before you even meet Jaxon Vega.

And then there’s Jaxon—a vampire so emotionally dead that he’s become a weapon, a hollow shell with a century of pain buried beneath his icy exterior. His arrival is like a knife twist: here’s a character who’s spent a hundred years feeling nothing, and now he’s forced to confront something—*her*. Wolff’s writing shines in the way she explores Jaxon’s fractured psyche, his love-hate relationship with his own monstrosity, and the slow, agonizing process of him remembering how to feel. Their chemistry is electric, not just because of the undeniable attraction, but because of the way Wolff makes their connection feel like a collision of two broken things trying to become whole. It’s not just romance; it’s survival.

What makes *Crave* truly gripping is its themes. Wolff doesn’t shy away from the darker aspects of love—possession, obsession, the way desire can blur into danger. The protagonist isn’t just fighting for her life; she’s fighting for her sanity, her identity, and her right to exist in a world that wants to consume her. Jaxon, meanwhile, is trapped in his own cycle of violence, and the question isn’t just whether they can love each other, but whether they can love *themselves* enough to break free. Wolff’s exploration of trauma and redemption is raw and unflinching, making the emotional payoff all the more powerful.

The prose itself is a standout. Wolff’s descriptions are vivid, her dialogue sharp, and her pacing relentless. She has a knack for making the supernatural feel visceral—blood isn’t just red; it’s a force, a currency, a curse. The Academy isn’t just a setting; it’s a character in its own right, with its opulent halls, its hidden dangers, and its rules that shift like sand. And the bonus scenes from Jaxon’s POV? A masterstroke. They deepen his character, give us insight into his world, and make the reader feel like they’re peering into a secret, forbidden place.

Of course, *Crave* isn’t without its flaws. Some readers might find the pacing uneven at times, particularly in the buildup to the climax, and the sheer volume of characters can be overwhelming. But these are minor quibbles in an otherwise flawless debut. The series has a way of pulling you in so deeply that you’ll devour *Crush* and *Covet* without a second thought, eager to see where Wolff takes her characters next.

In the end, *Crave* is more than just a vampire romance—it’s a story about finding yourself in the ruins of what you were meant to be. It’s about love as both salvation and destruction, about the monsters we carry inside us, and the courage it takes to face them. Wolff’s writing is lush, her characters are unforgettable, and her world is so richly imagined that you’ll be obsessed long after the last page. If you love dark, addictive romance with a side of psychological depth, this is a series you won’t want to miss.
