# The Magic Goes Away

**Author:** Larry Niven  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

"The Magic Goes Away" by Larry Niven is a captivating tale of magic, destruction, and self-discovery, set in the enchanted ancient city of Tep's Town. The story revolves around Whandall Placehold, a young man who has grown up in the city's most impoverished neighborhoods, where the volatile god of fire, Yagen-Atep, holds sway. The city is plagued by the Burning, a mysterious phenomenon in which the inhabitants, possessed by Yagen-Atep, set their own town ablaze, often without warning.

Niven masterfully weaves a complex narrative, rich in imagery and symbolism, that explores the human condition in the face of chaos and destruction. Through Whandall's journey, the author delves into themes of hope, redemption, and the search for meaning in a world where the rules of reality are constantly shifting. The city of Tep's Town, with its stark contrasts of beauty and ugliness, serves as a powerful metaphor for our own world, where the line between creation and destruction is often blurred.

One of the most striking aspects of Niven's style is his ability to craft a sense of atmosphere and mood. The city of Tep's Town is vividly brought to life, with its narrow streets, bustling markets, and ancient magic. The author's use of language is evocative and immersive, drawing the reader into the world of the story and refusing to let go. The characters, too, are well-developed and complex, with Whandall being a particularly compelling protagonist.

Throughout the book, Niven's signature blend of science fiction and fantasy elements is on full display. The magical system, based on the concept of "mana," is cleverly thought out, and the author's use of scientific principles to explain the workings of magic adds a layer of depth and complexity to the narrative. The world-building is meticulous and detailed, with a rich history and mythology that underpins the story.

If there is one criticism to be made, it is that the pacing of the book can be somewhat uneven. At times, the narrative feels a bit slow, and some readers may find themselves growing impatient with the lack of action. However, for the most part, the book is engaging and well-paced, with a narrative that is both propulsive and thought-provoking.

Overall, "The Magic Goes Away" is a gripping and thought-provoking read that is sure to appeal to fans of science fiction, fantasy, and adventure. Niven's unique blend of magic and science, combined with his vivid world-building and compelling characters, makes for a truly unforgettable reading experience.
