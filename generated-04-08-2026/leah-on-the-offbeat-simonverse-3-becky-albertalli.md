# Leah on the Offbeat (Simonverse, #3)

**Author:** Becky Albertalli  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

In the delightful and authentic novel "Leah on the Offbeat" by Becky Albertalli, readers are treated to a refreshing and heartfelt exploration of self-discovery, friendship, and love. This sequel to the acclaimed "Simon vs. the Homo Sapiens Agenda" shifts the focus to Leah Burke, Simon's loyal and creative best friend, as she navigates the complexities of senior year, relationships, and her own identity.

One of the standout aspects of Albertalli's writing is her ability to craft relatable and endearing characters. Leah, with her sharp wit, passion for drumming, and love for drawing, is a protagonist that readers can't help but root for. Her struggles to balance her relationships, academic pressures, and personal aspirations are deeply resonant, making her an excellent example of a well-rounded and authentic character.

A significant theme of the novel is Leah's journey of self-acceptance and discovery. As a bisexual teenager who has yet to come out to her friends, Leah grapples with the fear of rejection and the uncertainty of her relationships. Albertalli handles this narrative thread with sensitivity and care, never shying away from the complexities and nuances of Leah's experiences. The portrayal of Leah's bisexuality is particularly noteworthy, as it is depicted as a natural and integral part of her identity, rather than a plot device or a source of angst.

The novel is also notable for its unapologetic representation of fat characters. Leah is a confident and self-assured protagonist who refuses to let societal beauty standards define her worth. Albertalli's portrayal of Leah's body image is refreshing and empowering, offering a much-needed counterpoint to the usual narratives surrounding weight and self-esteem.

Albertalli's writing style is, as always, engaging and accessible. Her use of language is witty and insightful, capturing the humor and pathos of Leah's experiences with ease. The supporting cast of characters is equally well-developed, with familiar faces from the "Simon" universe making welcome appearances.

If there is one criticism to be made, it is that the novel occasionally relies on familiar tropes and plot devices. However, Albertalli's execution is so skilled and heartfelt that these moments never feel like clichés. Instead, they serve to underscore the universality of Leah's experiences and the timelessness of her story.

In conclusion, "Leah on the Offbeat" is a triumphant and uplifting novel that cements Becky Albertalli's status as a leading voice in young adult literature. With its thoughtful exploration of identity, relationships, and self-acceptance, this novel is sure to resonate with readers of all ages.
