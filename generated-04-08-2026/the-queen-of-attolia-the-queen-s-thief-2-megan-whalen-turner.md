# The Queen of Attolia (The Queen's Thief, #2)

**Author:** Megan Whalen Turner  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

The Queen of Attolia, the second installment in Megan Whalen Turner's acclaimed Queen's Thief series, is a masterful tale of intrigue, deception, and power. This New York Times bestseller has captivated readers and critics alike with its richly detailed world, complex characters, and gripping storyline.

At the heart of the novel is Eugenides, the charismatic and cunning thief, who finds himself in the precarious position of being captured by the ruthless Queen of Attolia. As Eugenides navigates the treacherous landscape of the palace, he must use all his wit and guile to survive and outmaneuver the queen's plans. The queen, a formidable and intelligent adversary, is determined to break Eugenides and use him for her own purposes.

Throughout the novel, Turner expertly weaves together themes of power, loyalty, and identity. The queen's character, in particular, is a fascinating exploration of the complexities of female power and the moral compromises that come with it. Her relationship with Eugenides is a delicate dance of cat and mouse, with each character trying to outmaneuver the other.

Turner's writing style is a key aspect of the novel's success. Her prose is elegant and evocative, conjuring up the vivid world of the Queen's Thief series with ease. The pacing is expertly managed, with a narrative that twists and turns in unexpected ways, keeping the reader on the edge of their seat. The characters are multidimensional and richly drawn, with Eugenides being a particular standout. His voice is distinctive and compelling, and his character development throughout the novel is both nuanced and satisfying.

One of the most striking aspects of the novel is its exploration of the gray areas between good and evil. The Queen of Attolia is a morally complex character, driven by a desire to protect her country and her people, even if it means making difficult choices. Eugenides, too, exists in a gray area, using his thieving skills for both personal gain and to aid those he cares about. This ambiguity adds depth and richness to the narrative, making it impossible to categorize the characters as simply good or evil.

The Queen of Attolia has received widespread critical acclaim, with praise from authors such as Laini Taylor, Leigh Bardugo, and Lloyd Alexander. It has also won numerous awards, including the Los Angeles Times Book Prize and the Newbery Honor.
