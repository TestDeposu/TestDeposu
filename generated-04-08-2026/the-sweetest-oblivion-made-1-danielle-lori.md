# The Sweetest Oblivion (Made, #1)

**Author:** Danielle Lori  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

Dangerous, magnetic, and dripping with forbidden tension, Danielle Lori’s 2020 romance novel The Sweetest Oblivion delivers an intoxicating dive into the shadows of the mafia underworld. Known to fans of dark romance as the ultimate hate-to-love indulgence, this novel successfully breathes new life into well-worn tropes through razor-sharp dialogue, palpable chemistry, and characters who command the page. 

At the center of the narrative is Elena Abelli, a woman whose sunny disposition earned her the deceptively innocent moniker of the sweet Abelli. Yet, beneath her compliant facade and reputation for being the well-behaved sister, lies a woman of fierce loyalty and hidden depths. Enter Nicolas Le Rosso—a ruthless, tattooed mafia boss known simply as "Ace," a man whose very presence commands absolute obedience and fear. The dramatic core of the novel hinges on a deliciously agonizing complication: Nicolas is slated to marry Elena’s younger sister in an arranged union meant to bind rival factions, making him not only untouchable, but her future brother-in-law. 

Lori’s primary thematic exploration centers on the friction between duty and desire. The book thrives on the agonizing slow-burn of a forbidden attraction where every shared glance, every sharp verbal sparring match, and every accidental brush of skin carries the weight of impending ruin. Elena and Nicolas are a masterclass in dynamic character pairing. Nicolas is the quintessential brooding alpha, fiercely protective yet terrifyingly lethal, while Elena refuses to cower, matching his dangerous intensity with her own quiet resilience. Their evolving dynamic avoids the pitfalls of passive heroines and one-dimensional villains; instead, it offers a sophisticated chess match of wills where vulnerability is weaponized and passion feels earned.

From a stylistic standpoint, Lori’s writing is punchy, cinematic, and effortlessly addictive. She avoids excessive purple prose, opting instead for snappy banter and internal monologues that heighten the claustrophobic tension of their impossible situation. The pacing moves at a breakneck speed, keeping readers perpetually on the hook as the boundaries between hate and love begin to dangerously blur. For readers who revel in high-stakes romance, brooding antiheroes, and the exquisite agony of wanting what is strictly forbidden, The Sweetest Oblivion is an absolute triumph of the genre.
