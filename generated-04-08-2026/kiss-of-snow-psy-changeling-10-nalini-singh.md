# Kiss of Snow (Psy-Changeling, #10)

**Author:** Nalini Singh  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

In "Kiss of Snow," Nalini Singh masterfully weaves a tale of forbidden love, primal passion, and the unbreakable bonds of loyalty within the SnowDancer wolf pack. The tenth installment in the Psy-Changeling series, this novel is a testament to Singh's skill in crafting complex characters, intricate world-building, and pulse-pounding action sequences.

At the heart of the story lies the tumultuous relationship between Sienna Lauren, a young and powerful Psy defector, and Hawke, the charismatic alpha of the SnowDancer pack. Theirs is a bond forged in the fires of mutual attraction, yet threatened by the very secrets that Sienna keeps hidden. As they navigate the treacherous landscape of their emotions, Singh expertly ratchets up the tension, creating a palpable sense of anticipation that propels the reader forward.

One of the standout aspects of Singh's writing is her ability to balance action, romance, and drama with ease. The world of the Psy-Changeling series is richly detailed, with a complex hierarchy of characters and a deep history that underpins the narrative. In "Kiss of Snow," Singh deftly expands on this world, introducing new characters and plot threads while deepening our understanding of the existing cast.

Hawke, in particular, is a fascinating character, his gruff exterior and dry wit masking a deep well of pain and vulnerability. His relationship with Sienna is a slow-burning fire, one that simmers with intensity as the story unfolds. Singh's portrayal of their romance is both tender and passionate, capturing the all-consuming nature of their desire for one another.

Meanwhile, the external threat to the SnowDancer pack provides a sense of urgency, raising the stakes and testing the bonds of loyalty and trust within the pack. Singh's pacing is expertly handled, with a narrative that hurtles forward at breakneck speed. The action sequences are intense and suspenseful, with a keen sense of tension that keeps the reader on the edge of their seat.

If there's one criticism to be made, it's that the supporting cast occasionally feels a little shortchanged. Some characters, like the enigmatic Kaleb Krychek, are tantalizingly introduced, only to disappear from view for extended periods. However, this is a minor quibble in an otherwise excellent novel.

In conclusion, "Kiss of Snow" is a gripping and emotionally resonant addition to the Psy-Changeling series. With its lush world-building, complex characters, and pulse-pounding action sequences, it's a must-read for fans of paranormal romance. Singh's writing is, as ever, engaging and evocative, making this novel a compelling page-turner from start to finish.
