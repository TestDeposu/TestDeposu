# Wrath (New Species, #6)

**Author:** Laurann Dohner  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

In Wrath, the sixth installment of Laurann Dohner’s wildly popular New Species series, the author once again proves her mastery of the alpha-male romance genre by diving deep into the psychological scars of systemic abuse and the redemptive power of unapologetic, fierce passion. Published in 2016, this novel reinforces Dohner’s signature style: fast-paced, high-stakes storytelling driven by visceral emotion, intense physical attraction, and characters larger than life.

The central theme of Wrath is trauma recovery through intimacy, exploring how love can act as a catalyst for healing the most fractured souls. Unlike some romance novels that breeze past the psychological damage of their protagonists, Dohner places Wrath’s profound trauma front and center. Having endured horrific abuse, conditioning, and psychological torture at the hands of Mercile—where he was chained and forced to watch endless loops of human females while subjected to perverted machinery—Wrath is a man existing on the precipice of his own internal chaos. He is convinced that he is too dangerous, too broken, and too emotionally scarred to ever deserve or safely handle a female. This makes his internal conflict genuinely compelling. He isn't just fighting external enemies; he is fighting the ghosts of his past captivity.

Enter Lauren, a stark contrast to Wrath’s dark, brooding, and guarded demeanor. Detained by New Species and unexpectedly thrust into the role of Wrath’s roommate, Lauren is vibrant, confident, and delightfully proactive. Dohner brilliantly flips the traditional romance script by making Lauren the pursuer. When she sets her sights on the tall, imposing, and undeniably spectacular New Species male, she resolves to taste every inch of his body. Lauren’s boldness serves as the perfect antidote to Wrath’s paralyzing self-doubt. She doesn't cower from his size or his brooding nature; instead, she leans in, offering him a sensual lifeline that he desperately craves, even if he is terrified to accept it.

Dohner’s writing style in Wrath is direct, emotionally charged, and unpretentious. She doesn't waste time with flowery prose; instead, she delivers punchy dialogue, raw internal monologues, and steam-laden encounters that keep the pages turning. The chemistry between Wrath and Lauren is palpable from the moment they meet. The sex scenes are scorching, written with Dohner’s trademark intensity, but what elevates them beyond mere erotica is the emotional vulnerability woven into the physical acts. Wrath gives Lauren everything she needs in the bedroom, yet the lingering suspense stems from the invisible wall he keeps erected around his heart. The narrative tension relies on Lauren’s mission to break down those final barriers and Wrath’s terrifying journey toward trusting himself and another person.

For fans of the New Species universe, Wrath delivers everything expected from the series while offering a particularly poignant look at a hero battling his own mind. It is a story about shedding the shackles of the past, embracing bodily and emotional autonomy, and realizing that love doesn't require perfection—just a willingness to fight for a future together. Dohner successfully balances the gritty, dark undertones of the New Species lore with a deeply satisfying, passionate romance that leaves a lasting impact.
