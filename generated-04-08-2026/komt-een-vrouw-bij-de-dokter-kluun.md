# Komt een vrouw bij de dokter

**Author:** Kluun  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

Love, grief, and raw, unfiltered human frailty collide in Kluun’s explosive literary debut, Komt een vrouw bij de dokter (Public Access: Love and Death in Amsterdam). Published as a semi-autobiographical phenomenon, this novel breaks away from the traditional, sanitized narratives of terminal illness to deliver a punch to the gut of contemporary fiction. At its core, the book explores the ultimate test of devotion: how does a couple in the prime of their vibrant, hedonistic lives cope when a devastating breast cancer diagnosis shatters their invincibility?

The narrative centers on Stijn and Carmen, a glamorous Amsterdam couple who seemingly have it all. They are young, successful, deeply in love, and accustomed to a fast-paced lifestyle fueled by nightlife, infidelity, and ambition. When Carmen is diagnosed with terminal breast cancer, their world implodes. Yet, Kluun refuses to paint them as tragic saints. Instead, he presents them as painfully, frustratingly real. 

The central theme of the novel is not merely grief, but the chaotic, often ugly dissonance between impending death and the irrepressible urge to keep living. As Carmen endures brutal medical treatments, her body wasting away while her spirit fights fiercely for survival, Stijn copes in the most self-destructive ways imaginable. Rather than retreating into quiet sorrow, he plunges deeper into the nightlife, seeking solace, distraction, and escape in casual affairs. This unapologetic portrayal of infidelity in the shadow of death is what makes the book so dangerously compelling. Kluun does not ask the reader to forgive Stijn, but rather to understand the sheer panic of a man watching his universe burn down around him.

Kluun’s authorial style is the true engine of the novel. Written with a staggering blend of biting dark humor, cynicism, and heart-wrenching tenderness, the prose moves at a breathless pace. There is no flowery, romanticized language here; Kluun writes with the raw urgency of a man exorcising his own demons. The dialogue is snappy, contemporary, and often brutally honest. By injecting humor into the darkest of circumstances, the author captures the bizarre surrealism of facing mortality in the modern age. The juxtaposition of shallow luxury with the ultimate depth of human suffering creates an emotional whiplash that leaves readers reeling.

Ultimately, Komt een vrouw bij de dokter is not a comfortable read, nor is it meant to be. It is a fierce, visceral testament to the fact that love is rarely pure or simple, and that letting go is the hardest thing a human being will ever have to do. Kluun strips away the taboos surrounding illness, grief, and marital betrayal, leaving behind a bleeding, beating heart of a story that stays with you long after you turn the final page.
