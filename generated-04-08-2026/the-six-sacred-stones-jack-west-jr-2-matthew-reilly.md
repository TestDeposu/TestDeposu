# The Six Sacred Stones (Jack West Jr, #2)

**Author:** Matthew Reilly  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2008

In Matthew Reilly's electrifying sequel to 'Seven Deadly Wonders', the stakes are raised, and the adventure becomes even more unforgiving. 'The Six Sacred Stones' takes readers on a relentless journey through time, blending historical mythology with cutting-edge military hardware in a thrilling tale that threatens to upstage its predecessor.

At its core, the story revolves around Jack West Jr., a former special forces operative transformed into a supersoldier after a catastrophic event that wiped out most of humanity. West's unwavering determination and unshakeable loyalty to his team make him an instantly relatable and heroic presence. The mysterious 'Six Sacred Stones' he's now determined to find and rebuild are central to the narrative, as are the ancient devices and historical landmarks he encounters along the way.

Throughout the book, Reilly masterfully weaves historical accuracy into a gripping narrative that propels the reader forward at a breakneck pace. While his descriptions of battles, high-tech military innovations, and exotic locations can feel somewhat reminiscent of Dan Brown's works, Reilly has developed a unique voice that is distinctively action-oriented and immersive. It is not unusual to find yourself caught up in the world Reilly has created, racing alongside West's team through ancient sites such as Stonehenge and the Three Gorges region, each step of the way leading to the impending countdown that could signal the end of all life on Earth.

One of the standout aspects of 'The Six Sacred Stones' is its ability to seamlessly switch between various action-packed set pieces and quieter moments of introspection. West's character development is impressive, as he confronts the weight of his responsibilities, and grapples with the consequences of his own morality. Supporting characters, including his loyal team members and new allies, also benefit from nuanced descriptions that add depth to the story.

It is worth noting that some readers may find Reilly's prose overly simplistic, lacking in subtlety or complexity at certain points. While a large component of his audience will likely welcome this accessibility, some readers who value layered storytelling may feel that the pacing suffers as a result.

Overall, 'The Six Sacred Stones' is a thrilling page-turner that delivers what its predecessor promised: an adventure-filled ride that will push readers to the edge of their seats. As the stakes escalate, West's battle-hardened presence and unwavering resolve make for an unforgettable experience. This is a book that combines the fast-paced action of military thrillers, with the historical and intellectual curiosity of 'Indiana Jones' and other legendary adventures.
