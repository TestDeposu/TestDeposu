# The Halfling's Gem (Forgotten Realms: The Icewind Dale, #3; The Legend of Drizzt, #6)

**Author:** R.A. Salvatore  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

R.A. Salvatore’s *The Halfling’s Gem* barrels forward with the kinetic energy that has become his trademark, turning a straightforward rescue mission into a high‑octane trek across the sword‑and‑sorcery landscape of the Forgotten Realms. At its core, the novel is a meditation on loyalty and the masks we wear—both literal and figurative—to protect those we love. When Artemis Entreri spirals Regis back into the clutches of Pasha Pook, Drizzt Do’Urden and Wulfgar are forced to chase not just a friend but the very idea of steadfast camaraderie that has defined their bond since the icy wastes of Icewind Dale. The titular gem, a relic that can shroud its bearer’s true identity, becomes a powerful symbol: it lets Drizzt walk among ordinary elves, stripping away the fearsome reputation of the drow and forcing him to confront what it means to be seen—and to see himself—without the legend that precedes him.

Salvatore’s style shines brightest in the novel’s relentless pacing and visceral combat scenes. He writes swordplay with a choreographer’s precision, each parry and thrust rendered in crisp, kinetic prose that makes the reader feel the sting of steel and the rush of adrenaline. Yet he never lets the action overshadow character; quieter moments—Drizzt’s internal dialogue as he dons the gem’s disguise, Wulfgar’s gruff optimism, Regis’s quick‑witted banter—are woven in with the same care, giving the tale emotional depth amid the chaos. The desert crossing of Calimshan, the pirate‑infested waters of the Sword Coast, and the eerie encounter with a banshee are all rendered with Salvatore’s trademark blend of gritty realism and high fantasy wonder, each setting feeling both exotic and intimately familiar.

What elevates *The Halfling’s Gem* beyond a simple chase narrative is its exploration of identity. The magical gem’s power to mask its wearer forces Drizzt to step out of the shadow of his infamous lineage and experience the world as an “ordinary” elf—a perspective that highlights the prejudice he constantly battles and the yearning for acceptance that drives many of his choices. Simultaneously, Regis’s plight underscores the theme of trust: even the smallest, most unassuming friend can become the linchpin of a hero’s quest when loyalty is tested. Salvatore balances these introspective threads with his trademark humor—Regis’s clever quips and the occasional absurdity of a banshee’s lament—keeping the tone from becoming overly solemn while still honoring the stakes.

In the end, the novel delivers a satisfying crescendo that ties up the Icewind Dale Trilogy’s central conflict while leaving room for the larger Legend of Drizzt saga to unfold. Salvatore proves once again that he can marry breakneck action with thoughtful character study, making *The Halfling’s Gem* not just a thrilling adventure but a resonant story about the lengths we will go for friendship and the identities we forge—or hide—along the way.
