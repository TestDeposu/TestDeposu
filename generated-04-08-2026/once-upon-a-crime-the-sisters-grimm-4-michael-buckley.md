# Once Upon a Crime (The Sisters Grimm, #4)

**Author:** Michael Buckley  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

"Once Upon a Crime" – The Sisters Grimm, a delightful quartet by Michael Buckley, whisks readers away to a world where fairy-tale legends collide with the concrete jungle of New York City. Book four of the series, published in 2017, is a gripping tale of sibling love, mystery, and the struggle between good and evil.

In this installment, Sabrina and Daphne Grimm navigate the treacherous streets of their hometown, searching for Puck's family amidst an urban landscape overrun by fairy-tale characters. Gone are the halcyon days of their childhood; now, the city's streets buzz with the presence of wand-wielding fairy godfathers, swashbuckling Wall Street pirates, and even a murderer on the loose. As the sisters delve deeper into the heart of New York City, they begin to unravel secrets about their own family's past, particularly the enigmatic secret their mother, Relda, kept from them.

The narrative deftly weaves together an intricate web of mysteries and plot twists, keeping the reader engaged from the very first page. Buckley masterfully captures the dynamic relationship between Sabrina and Daphne – their banter, trust, and deep-rooted loyalty to one another shining through in every scene. The author's flair for the absurd and his ability to craft characters that blend seamlessly into the fairy-tale world, infusing them with depth and complexity, is impressive.

What sets Buckley apart from other authors in the fantasy genre is his unique ability to balance humor, suspense, and nostalgia. His descriptions of the urban Everafters are at once poignant and hilarious, making it easy to become completely enthralled in the world of the series. His writing is vivid, evocative, and precise – the world of New York City, now inhabited by fairy tale characters, springs to life in the reader's imagination with each turn of the page.

Moreover, the themes explored in "Once Upon a Crime" are just as compelling as the plot itself. The novel delves into the complex dynamics of family, the power of secrets, and the nature of evil – all of which are skillfully woven into the narrative without feeling forced or didactic. The author cleverly uses the fairy-tale world as a metaphor for the real-world struggles faced by children and adolescents, making the story relatable and endearing to readers of all ages.

However, what's perhaps most remarkable about Buckley's work is the way he subverts expectations and creates a world that feels both familiar and new, even to readers who have devoured every word of the series. With each instalment, he raises the stakes and deepens the mythology of this captivating world, making it increasingly hard to resist the allure of the Sisters Grimm's adventures.

In the end, "Once Upon a Crime" is an enthralling tale that combines wit, suspense, and heart – a testament to the enduring power of Michael Buckley's storytelling. Whether you're a seasoned fan of the series or discovering the Grimm family for the first time, this book is sure to capture your imagination and leave you eagerly anticipating the next installment in the Sisters Grimm saga.
