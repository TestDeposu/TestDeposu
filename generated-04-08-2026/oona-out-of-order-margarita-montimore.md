# Oona Out of Order

**Author:** Margarita Montimore  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

Margarita Montimore’s 2020 national bestseller, Oona Out of Order, takes a high-concept premise reminiscent of The Time Traveler’s Wife and grounds it in a deeply emotional, strikingly intimate exploration of human identity. Imagine standing on the precipice of adulthood on New Year’s Eve, 1982, poised to choose between London and Brooklyn, love and career, only to wake up thirty-two years later in a stranger’s sprawling estate, staring at the reflection of a fifty-one-year-old woman. This is the dizzying reality for Oona Lockhart, who, from that fateful midnight onward, begins experiencing her life entirely out of chronological sequence. One year she is a wealthy philanthropist in her forties; the next, a struggling twenty-something; and later, a fragile senior citizen navigating the twilight of her fragmented existence. 

At its core, Montimore’s novel is anchored by a poignant and urgent central theme: the relentless, often disorienting nature of time, and the radical grace required to live fully in the present when the past and future are moving targets. By stripping away the linear progression of aging, Montimore forces both Oona and the reader to confront the existential weight of our choices. Without a steady timeline, Oona cannot rely on the comforting narrative arc of cause and effect. Instead, she must learn to piece together her life like a mosaic, gleaning wisdom from future memories she has yet to live and mourning losses from years she has already left behind. It is a brilliant structural metaphor for the way humans actually experience memory and regret—never neatly, but rather in emotional bursts and sudden leaps. 

Montimore’s literary style is breezy, highly engaging, and deceptively profound. Translating a narrative that hops randomly across decades could easily descend into chaotic confusion, yet the author maintains a steady, magnetic narrative pulse. The prose is peppered with vivid nostalgia, utilizing pop culture touchstones, shifting fashion trends, and technological evolutions as emotional anchors that ground Oona’s jarring transitions. Yet, beneath the sparkling surface of MTV-era nostalgia and clever stock-market foresight lies a deeply empathetic character study. Montimore balances the whimsical sci-fi mechanics of time-jumping with raw, aching human vulnerability. Oona’s journey is not merely about adapting to different physical ages or enjoying sudden financial windfalls; it is about the endurance of love, the complexities of familial bonds, and the fierce independence of a woman trying to anchor her soul in a shifting world.

What makes Oona Out of Order such a triumphant read is its ultimate refusal to lean entirely into tragedy. While the premise inherently carries a sense of profound displacement, Montimore infuses the narrative with joy, resilience, and an uplifting redemptive spirit. Oona’s haphazard timeline teaches her—and us—that every phase of life, no matter how unexpected or disorderly, holds its own distinct beauty and wisdom. Striking, inventive, and deeply moving, this novel proves that living a life fully in the moment has nothing to do with time, and everything to do with presence.
