# Glass (Crank, #2)

**Author:** Ellen Hopkins  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2008

In the haunting and evocative novel "Glass," Ellen Hopkins masterfully weaves a poignant tale of addiction, identity, and the blurring of lines between reality and fantasy. As the second installment in the Crank trilogy, this book is a powerful exploration of the destructive nature of crystal meth and its devastating effects on a young life.

Through the eyes of Kristina Snow, a gifted high school junior, Hopkins crafts a narrative that is both deeply personal and universally relatable. Kristina's transformation from a quiet, obedient daughter to a reckless, meth-addicted teenager is a heart-wrenching journey that is as captivating as it is disturbing. With each turn of the page, the reader is drawn into Kristina's world, a world that is both fragile and volatile.

Hopkins' use of verse is a stroke of genius, allowing the reader to experience Kristina's emotions and thoughts in a raw, unfiltered way. The poetry is lyrical, yet brutal, conveying the intensity of Kristina's feelings and the chaos that ensues as she succumbs to the monster of addiction. The author's writing is akin to a punch to the gut, leaving the reader breathless and reeling.

One of the most striking aspects of "Glass" is its unflinching portrayal of addiction. Hopkins pulls no punches, presenting a stark and unromanticized view of the devastating consequences of meth use. The novel is a powerful cautionary tale, one that highlights the dangers of experimentation and the ease with which addiction can take hold.

Through Kristina's story, Hopkins also explores themes of identity, family dynamics, and the fragility of the human psyche. Kristina's struggles to reconcile her old life with her new reality are deeply relatable, and her desperate attempts to hold on to her sense of self are both poignant and heartbreaking.

What sets "Glass" apart from other novels about addiction is its refusal to offer easy answers or solutions. Hopkins presents a complex, multifaceted portrayal of addiction, one that acknowledges the nuances and contradictions of the human experience. The novel is a powerful reminder that addiction is a disease, one that requires compassion, understanding, and support.

In conclusion, "Glass" is a masterpiece of contemporary young adult literature. Ellen Hopkins' writing is a tour de force, a powerful and evocative exploration of the human condition. This novel is a must-read for anyone who has ever struggled with addiction, or for those who simply want to understand the complexities of the human experience.
