# I Kill Giants

**Author:** Joe Kelly  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

In the realm of graphic novels, few stories have managed to capture the essence of childhood struggles and the power of imagination as poignantly as Joe Kelly's 'I Kill Giants'. This critically acclaimed book, brought to life by the talented J.M. Ken Niimura, delves into the complex world of Barbara Thorson, a fifth-grader with a penchant for Norse mythology and a penchant for battling giants.

At its core, 'I Kill Giants' is a poignant exploration of the human condition, tackling themes of trauma, loss, and the blurred lines between reality and fantasy. Barbara's world is one of giants, mythical creatures that she battles with the aid of her trusty war hammer, Coveleski. But as the story progresses, it becomes increasingly clear that these giants may not be just the product of Barbara's vivid imagination. They may, in fact, be a manifestation of the very real struggles she faces in her everyday life.

Kelly's writing is a masterclass in subtlety, weaving together a narrative that is both heartbreaking and uplifting. His portrayal of Barbara is nothing short of remarkable, capturing the nuances of childhood angst and the struggles of growing up. The character's sharp wit and sarcasm serve as a clever facade, hiding a deep well of pain and vulnerability.

Niimura's artwork is the perfect complement to Kelly's writing, bringing a level of depth and emotion to the story that is nothing short of remarkable. His illustrations are both beautiful and haunting, capturing the fantastical world of giants and monsters that Barbara inhabits.

One of the most striking aspects of 'I Kill Giants' is its ability to balance the fantastical with the mundane. Barbara's battles with giants are juxtaposed with her everyday struggles in school and at home, creating a sense of tension that is both captivating and heartbreaking. This balance is a testament to Kelly's skill as a storyteller, able to weave together disparate threads into a narrative that is both cohesive and compelling.

Ultimately, 'I Kill Giants' is a story about the power of imagination and the resilience of the human spirit. It is a testament to the idea that even in the darkest of times, there is always hope. Barbara's story is one that will resonate with readers of all ages, a poignant reminder of the struggles we face and the power of our own imagination.
