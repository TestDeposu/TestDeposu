# When Darkness Falls (Obsidian Mountain, #3)

**Author:** Mercedes Lackey  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

Epic fantasy is a genre often crowded with tired tropes—chosen ones, ancient evils, and predictable quests—yet the collaborative mastery of Mercedes Lackey and James Mallory breathes vibrant, urgent life into the traditional formula. When Darkness Falls, the climactic third installment of The Obsidian Trilogy, stands as a testament to world-building depth and emotional resonance. Following The Outpost and To Light a Flame, this 2007 release does not merely wrap up a sprawling conflict; it plunges the realms of Elves, humans, and demons into a breathtaking crucible where survival demands the ultimate price.

At the narrative’s core lies a profound and sophisticated theme: the crippling cost of prejudice in the face of annihilation. Even as the Demon Queen Savilla gathers her forces for a catastrophic ritual to summon the dark entity known as He Who Is, the human Mage Council remains stubbornly blind, viewing Knight-Mage Kellen and his Elven allies as enemies rather than saviors. Lackey and Mallory use this institutional stubbornness as a mirror for real-world divisiveness, illustrating how tribalism and systemic distrust can sabotage a society on the brink of ruin. The desperate quest to shatter the Demons' hold on the human Mages becomes more than a tactical necessity; it is a psychological battle to heal centuries of cultural hatred before darkness consumes them all.

The authors' signature style shines through their intricate plotting and deeply empathetic character work. Lackey’s gift for emotional intimacy merges seamlessly with Mallory’s knack for grand-scale world-building. We see this dual mastery brilliantly displayed through Kellen, whose engineering assignment to fortify an Elven mine turns into a chilling encounter with the long-lost Shadowed Elves. Meanwhile, the perspective of Vestakia—the half-human, half-Demon healer burdened with the ability to read the mind of her demonic father, Crown Prince Zyperis—adds a haunting layer of internal conflict. Vestakia embodies the trilogy’s exploration of identity and redemption, walking a razor-thin edge between her monstrous heritage and her compassionate soul.

The pacing in When Darkness Falls is relentless, quickening as Wild Mage Idalia uncovers Savilla’s horrific plans for a massive sacrifice. The narrative tension reaches a fever pitch as the characters realize that defeating ultimate evil requires an inversion of sacrifice itself. Rather than relying solely on magical Deus ex machina solutions, the authors ground their climax in agonizing moral choices and personal losses. The prose is vivid and immersive, painting the bleakness of war and the blazing resilience of hope with equal strokes of poetic grace and visceral realism.

Ultimately, When Darkness Falls is a masterclass in epic fantasy concluding acts. It rewards longtime fans with emotional pay-offs for character arcs years in the making, while delivering enough high-stakes magic, political intrigue, and shadowy peril to thrill any newcomer to Obsidian Mountain. Lackey and Mallory prove that even when the night is at its blackest, the human (and Elven) spirit possesses a fierce, unyielding light. This is a striking, emotionally charged triumph that lingers long after the final page is turned.
