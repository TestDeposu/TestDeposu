# Dairy Queen (Dairy Queen, #1)

**Author:** Catherine Gilbert Murdock  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

Dairy Queen, the debut novel by Catherine Gilbert Murdock, is a poignant and thought-provoking exploration of the power of silence and the liberating force of self-expression. Set in the small town of Red Bend, Wisconsin, the story revolves around 15-year-old D.J. Schwenk, a complex and multi-faceted protagonist who embodies the struggles of adolescence.

Through D.J.'s narrative, Murdock masterfully weaves a tale of family dynamics, friendship, and first love, all of which are expertly intertwined with the themes of silence, communication, and the weight of unspoken words. The author's writing style is characterized by a unique blend of humor, wit, and sensitivity, making the characters feel remarkably real and relatable.

One of the most striking aspects of Dairy Queen is its thoughtful portrayal of the consequences of not talking. D.J.'s family, in particular, is plagued by unspoken secrets and unresolved tensions, which threaten to tear them apart. As the protagonist navigates her relationships with her parents, siblings, and friends, she begins to realize the importance of open communication in healing emotional wounds and bridging gaps.

Murdock's use of D.J.'s inner monologue provides a fascinating glimpse into the mind of a teenager struggling to find her voice. The protagonist's thoughts are a candid reflection of her fears, desires, and insecurities, making her an instantly likable and sympathetic character. The author's writing is also notable for its subtlety, as she skillfully conveys the complexities of D.J.'s emotions through nuanced descriptions and clever dialogue.

The character of Brian Nelson, the boy who awakens D.J.'s interest in football and, more importantly, in herself, is a welcome addition to the narrative. Their romance is sweet and tender, but it's also fraught with the uncertainty and awkwardness that often accompanies first love.

Throughout the book, Murdock tackles a range of issues, from family conflicts and peer pressure to identity formation and self-acceptance. Her approach is refreshingly honest and non-judgmental, making Dairy Queen an excellent choice for readers of all ages.

In conclusion, Dairy Queen is a captivating and insightful novel that explores the transformative power of self-expression and open communication. Catherine Gilbert Murdock's engaging writing style, coupled with her well-crafted characters and thought-provoking themes, make this book a must-read for anyone looking for a compelling and relatable story.
