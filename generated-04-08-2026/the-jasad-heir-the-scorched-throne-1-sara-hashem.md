# The Jasad Heir (The Scorched Throne, #1)

**Author:** Sara Hashem  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

Ten years after the ash settled over a fallen kingdom, Sara Hashem’s striking debut, The Jasad Heir, steps boldly into the scorched wreckage to ask a dangerous question: what does it truly cost to survive a slaughter? Infused with rich Egyptian-inspired mythology and a razor-sharp narrative voice, this slow-burn epic fantasy wastes no time sinking its claws into the reader. Hashem crafts a world where magic is not merely a tool, but an outlawed, volatile inheritance that clings to the skin like soot—a brilliant central metaphor for generational trauma and the desperate necessity of concealment.

At the heart of the novel is Sylvia, a fugitive princess who has learned the art of invisibility all too well. Having narrowly escaped the massacre that claimed her family and scorched Jasad to the bone, she spends her days dodging the oppressive shadow of the Nizahl empire. Hashem excels at internal pacing, anchoring the high-stakes political machinations in the raw, aching reality of a survivor forced to swallow her own pride day after day. Sylvia is no pristine, ready-made savior; she is hardened by paranoia, sharp-tongued, and fiercely defensive of a life built on a foundation of lies. 

The narrative’s engine truly ignites when Sylvia’s carefully constructed anonymity is shattered in a single, careless flash of magic. Enter Arin, the Nizahl Heir—a formidable tracker whose pursuit of Jasadi rebels inadvertently corners Sylvia. Rather than executing her on the spot, Hashem sets the stage for a deliciously tense, high-stakes game of cat and mouse. The enforced partnership between Sylvia and her greatest enemy forms the emotional core of the book. As they are thrown together into the viper’s nest of imperial politics and rebel factions, Hashem masterfully navigates the treacherous waters between hatred and attraction. The romance is a masterclass in slow-burn tension, built on mutual suspicion, intellectual sparring, and the terrifying vulnerability of being truly seen by the person sworn to destroy you.

Hashem’s style is atmospheric and incisive. She paints her desert-infused fantasy landscape with vivid sensory details—the taste of ash, the hum of suppressed magic, the suffocating luxury of the Nizahl court contrasted against the ragged resilience of the Jasadi underground. There is a cinematic quality to her action sequences, yet she never sacrifices character development for spectacle. Every maneuver in this deadly game feels earned, carrying heavy emotional and political consequences. 

The Jasad Heir is ultimately a story about identity and reclamation. Sylvia’s journey is not just about dodging executioners or outsmarting a prince; it is about confronting the ghost of a crown she tried to bury. Hashem handles the themes of cultural erasure and resistance with a maturity that elevates the book above standard YA or crossover fantasy tropes. The world is morally gray, the stakes feel perpetually apocalyptic, and the characters are wonderfully, dangerously messy. 

For readers hungry for intricate political maneuvering, enemies-to-lovers chemistry that practically crackles off the page, and a protagonist who has to claw her way toward her own power, Hashem’s debut is an absolute triumph. The scorched kingdom is rising indeed, and once you step into Hashem's serpentine grasp, you will gladly follow Sylvia down into the ashes.
