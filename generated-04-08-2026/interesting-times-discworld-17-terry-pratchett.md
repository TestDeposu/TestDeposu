# Interesting Times (Discworld, #17)

**Author:** Terry Pratchett  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

To wish that someone live in interesting times is, by all accounts on the Discworld, a malicious curse of the highest order. It promises upheaval, political instability, and a staggering amount of bloodshed. Yet, for the devoted reader of Sir Terry Pratchett, interesting times are precisely what we line up for. In the 1995 masterpiece *Interesting Times*, Pratchett takes his razor-sharp satirical pen to the Far East, military strategy, and the absurdity of revolution, resulting in one of the most intellectually stimulating and genuinely hilarious installments in the sprawling Discworld canon.

At the center of this storm is Rincewind, the Discworld’s premier cowardly wizard whose only magical talent is a miraculous, self-preserving aptitude for running away very fast. Having been unceremoniously drafted as a Great Wizzard and shipped off to the distant Agatean Empire via carrier albatross, Rincewind wants nothing to do with the impending civil war. True to Pratchett’s style, the novel thrives on this magnificent mismatch between grandiose epic fantasy tropes and utter mundane cowardice. While great armies clash and empires tremble, Rincewind is simply trying to find a high wall to hide behind. 

Pratchett’s primary theme in *Interesting Times* is the dangerous weight of narratives and the terrifying reality of mass movements. The Agatean Empire is a thinly veiled, wonderfully distorted mirror of imperial China, complete with bureaucracy, secret societies, and ancient traditions. Into this powder keg steps Cohen the Barbarian alongside his Silver Horde—a band of geriatric warriors whose joints creak louder than their broadswords. Through the Silver Horde, Pratchett explores the passage of time and the obsolescence of the classic barbarian hero. These characters are not fighting for glory; they are fighting because it beats sitting around complaining about their backs. They represent the literal embodiment of the old order crashing down on the new, subverting heroic tropes with a wink and a heavy dose of melancholy.

What elevates *Interesting Times* above a standard comedic romp is Pratchett’s peerless prose style. He possesses the rare gift of making you laugh out loud on one page while quietly pondering the deep-seated flaws of human civilization on the next. His observations on politics, propaganda, and the sheer momentum of history are as piercing as anything written by Swift or Voltaire. Pratchett uses fantasy not as an escape from reality, but as a prism to refract it, showing us the ridiculousness of our own world through the lens of a flat disc riding on the back of four giant elephants.

Whether you are a lifelong citizen of Ankh-Morpork or a newcomer stepping onto the Disc for the first time, *Interesting Times* delivers a masterclass in comic world-building. It is fast-paced, deeply clever, and anchored by a protagonist who proves that survival is the ultimate form of heroism. Pratchett reminds us that while interesting times are a curse, reading about them is an absolute blessing.
