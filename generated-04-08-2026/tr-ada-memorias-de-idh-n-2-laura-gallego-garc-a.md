# Tríada (Memorias de Idhún, #2)

**Author:** Laura Gallego García  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

The weight of prophecy and the volatile nature of choice take center stage in Tríada, the compelling second installment of Laura Gallego’s celebrated fantasy series, Memorias de Idhún. Picking up the threads from La Resistencia, this volume shifts the momentum from underground rebellion to open, desperate hope as Jack and Victoria finally set foot on the soil of Idhún. Gallego wastes no time plunging her characters into a world teetering on the edge of annihilation under the iron-fisted tyranny of Ashran the Nigromante and the terrifying sheks.

At its core, Tríada is a profound exploration of destiny versus free will. The narrative thrives on the friction between what the ancient prophecy demands and what the characters actually desire. Gallego masterfully deconstructs her young heroes, refusing to let them remain simple archetypes of light and dark. Jack’s unwavering righteousness is tested by the harsh realities of war and jealousy, while Victoria finds herself torn between her loyalties and her burgeoning, inexplicable connection to Kirtash. This enigmatic assassin remains one of the trilogy’s most fascinating creations; his ambiguous allegiance injects a constant undercurrent of suspense, keeping both the characters and the reader perpetually off-balance.

Gallego’s literary style continues to shine through its brisk pacing, accessible yet evocative prose, and deeply emotional character dynamics. She excels at writing YA fantasy that respects its audience, tackling complex themes of sacrifice, identity, and the intoxicating danger of first love without ever losing the sense of epic wonder. The world-building of Idhún expands organically in this volume, moving beyond the shadows of exile into a vibrant, albeit bleeding, landscape waiting to be liberated. 

Tríada is not merely a bridge between the beginning and the end of a journey; it is a turbulent, heart-wrenching crucible that forces its protagonists to grow up fast. Gallego delivers a striking critique of blind faith, arguing that true heroism is forged not in fulfilling a predetermined script, but in the agonizing choices one makes when the path ahead is shrouded in darkness. For fans of immersive fantasy and intricate interpersonal triangles, this middle chapter remains an unforgettable, emotionally resonant triumph.
