# Sing, Unburied, Sing

**Author:** Jesmyn Ward  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

**A Haunting Odyssey Through Family, History, and the American South**

In "Sing, Unburied, Sing", Jesmyn Ward weaves a rich and deeply unsettling tale of a family's journey through rural Mississippi, exposing the brutal realities of racism, poverty, and the unyielding struggles of the human spirit. This majestic novel, a "tour de force" according to Oprah Daily, follows the complex lives of three generations of a family, as they confront the legacies of slavery, the long shadow of lynching, and the disintegration of the nuclear family.

At its core, "Sing, Unburied, Sing" is a deeply moving exploration of masculinity, love, and the quest for identity in a world where these values are constantly being defined and redefined. Thirteen-year-old Jojo, the novel's de facto narrator, is a poignant representation of a generation's search for meaning, as he navigates the expectations set upon him by his family, community, and heritage. His journey is inextricably linked to that of his mother, Leonie, a flawed and vulnerable woman who embodies the very definition of imperfection. Ward's vivid portrayal of Leonie, a complex character trapped by her circumstances, makes for a searing and heart-wrenching portrayal of addiction, grief, and redemption.

Through Ward's masterful writing, the past and present collide, as the brutal realities of the American South's racial legacy unfold before us. The family's perilous journey to Parchman State Penitentiary, to visit the imprisoned father and the ancestral homeland, becomes an odyssey through the region's troubled past. The penitentiary itself serves as a threshold to the past, where the ghost of a dead inmate carries the weight of history, forcing Jojo to confront the unspoken truths about his family's past, present, and future.

One of the most remarkable aspects of "Sing, Unburied, Sing" is Ward's prose, which is both lyrically beautiful and uncompromisingly brutal. Her writing throbs with life, grief, and love, imbuing the narrative with a sense of urgency and desperation. Ward's unique style weaves together elements of magical realism, folk lore, and personal history, crafting a narrative that is both deeply intimate and epic in scope.

What makes "Sing, Unburied, Sing" a true tour de force, however, is its unwavering commitment to truth-telling. Ward's writing is unflinching, unromanticized, and ultimately, unapologetic. The novel confronts us with the stark realities of poverty, racism, and inequality in the American South, refusing to shy away from the brutalities that have defined the region's history.

In "Sing, Unburied, Sing", Ward creates a timeless and searing work of fiction that is both a tribute to the enduring power of family and a scathing indictment of the injustices that have besmirched American history. This National Book Award-winning novel is an odyssey through the heart of darkness, a powerful testament to the human spirit's capacity for survival, resilience, and love.
