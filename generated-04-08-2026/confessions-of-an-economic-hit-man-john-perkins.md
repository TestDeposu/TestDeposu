# Confessions of an Economic Hit Man

**Author:** John Perkins  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

In the latest edition of his New York Times bestseller, "Confessions of an Economic Hit Man," John Perkins delivers a scathing critique of the economic hit man (EHM) strategy that has been exploited by the United States and, more recently, China. This third edition is a riveting exposé that sheds light on the corrupt system that has been cheating countries out of trillions of dollars, causing staggering income inequality and ecological devastation.

As a former EHM himself, Perkins offers a unique insider perspective on the highly paid professionals who use development loans to saddle countries with huge debts, forcing them to serve the interests of the United States or China. The book is a shocking account of the ways in which these EHMs have wreaked havoc on countries around the globe, from Latin America to Asia, Africa, the Middle East, and Europe.

Perkins' writing style is engaging and accessible, making the book an easy read despite the complexity of the subject matter. He weaves together a narrative that is both personal and informative, drawing on his own experiences as an EHM to illustrate the ways in which the system works. The book is also thoroughly researched, with Perkins drawing on a wide range of sources to support his claims.

One of the most striking aspects of the book is its critique of China's EHM strategy. Perkins argues that China has taken the US EHM blueprint and adapted it to its own purposes, using its economic might to strong-arm countries into serving its interests. The book includes twelve new chapters that detail the ways in which China's EHM strategy is playing out in different regions of the world.

Despite the bleak picture that Perkins paints, the book is not without hope. He offers a plan for transforming the current system, which prioritizes profits above all else, into a "Life Economy" that restores the earth. This vision is rooted in a commitment to global cooperation and a recognition of the need for a new era of international relations.

Throughout the book, Perkins' passion and conviction shine through. He is a compelling storyteller, and his narrative is both engaging and thought-provoking. The book is a call to action, urging readers to take steps towards creating a more just and equitable world.

In short, "Confessions of an Economic Hit Man" is a must-read for anyone interested in economics, politics, or international relations. Perkins' critique of the EHM strategy is both timely and urgent, and his vision for a more sustainable future is inspiring.
