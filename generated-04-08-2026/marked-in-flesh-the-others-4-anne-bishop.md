# Marked in Flesh (The Others, #4)

**Author:** Anne Bishop  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

In Marked in Flesh, the fourth installment of Anne Bishop’s captivating The Others series, the delicate equilibrium between humanity and the supernatural becomes increasingly fraught with tension. Bishop, a master of world-building, continues to refine her portrait of a world where humanity exists only at the sufferance of the Others—the ancient, primal predators who view us as little more than clever, albeit dangerous, livestock.

The narrative momentum picks up following the pivotal alliance between the Lakeside Courtyard’s Simon Wolfgard and the blood prophet Meg Corbyn. Their deepening, unconventional bond serves as the emotional anchor of the novel, offering a glimpse into a potential future where empathy, rather than just survival, dictates the relationship between species. However, Bishop excels at reminding her readers that evolution is a slow, often painful process. While Simon and Meg navigate the burgeoning nuances of their partnership, the external world is rapidly deteriorating.

The central conflict of this entry—a rise in radical human insurgencies—provides the perfect catalyst for Bishop to flex her thematic muscles. She poses a chilling, existential question: How much humanity is the world willing to tolerate when humanity itself has become a contagion of violence? The radicals seeking to claim territory through bloodshed are acting on a fundamental misunderstanding of the food chain. Bishop’s greatest strength here is her ability to shift the narrative lens from the known threats (the wolf-shifters and vampires) to the truly eldritch, ancient entities that inhabit the land. These forces, indifferent to human politics and morality, remind the reader that the Others are not merely heroes; they are the natural law, and they are becoming impatient.

Bishop’s prose remains characteristically lean and atmospheric. She has a unique talent for writing dialogue that feels distinctly non-human without becoming unreadable. When the Others speak of "meat" or "prey," the casual detachment sends a shiver down the spine that effectively grounds the stakes of the series. She avoids the pitfalls of generic urban fantasy by refusing to humanize her monsters too quickly; instead, she forces the human characters to adapt to the monsters' reality.

Marked in Flesh is a masterclass in escalating tension. It is less a story about a specific battle and more a study of cultural collision. By the time the final pages turn, the reader is left with the unsettling realization that the peace at Lakeside is fragile, held together by the thin thread of a few individuals who are willing to bridge a divide that most of the world wants to burn. For fans who have followed Meg and Simon from the start, this book is an essential bridge, expanding the scope of the world while deepening the internal character arcs that make this series so difficult to put down. It is a haunting, evocative, and striking addition to one of the most original series in modern speculative fiction.
