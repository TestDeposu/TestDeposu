# Vampireville (Vampire Kisses, #3)

**Author:** Ellen Schreiber  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Darkness falls once again over the deceptively named Dullsville in Ellen Schreiber’s third installment of her cult-favorite gothic romance series, Vampireville. Released in 2009, this brisk and atmospheric continuation deepens the stakes for our favorite mortal-immortal couple, raven-haired goth devotee Raven Madison and her brooding vampire boyfriend, Alexander Sterling. Schreiber returns to the page with her signature blend of teenage angst, small-town claustrophobia, and supernatural suspense, delivering a breezy read that remains effortlessly charming for young adult audiences.

At the heart of Vampireville lies the enduring theme of outsider identity and the complications of teenage love pushed to dramatic extremes. Raven has always worn her goth aesthetic like armor, proudly standing apart from the mundane conformity of her peers. Yet, loving a creature of the night tests even her unwavering dedication. The nocturnal nature of their romance isolates them, symbolizing the universal adolescent feeling of being misunderstood by the surrounding world. Schreiber explores how young love requires sacrifice, trust, and resilience, especially when external forces threaten to tear a fledgling relationship apart.

The narrative gains fresh momentum with the arrival of Alexander's dangerous archrival, Jagger, and his pale, enigmatic sister, Luna. The introduction of these new antagonists injects a palpable sense of peril into Dullsville, raising the question of whether Raven and Alexander's secluded haven can withstand the rising tide of dark forces. Schreiber skillfully weaves subplot into main plot, notably pairing the chilling Luna with Raven's longtime mortal nemesis, Trevor, creating an amusing yet tense dynamic that adds layers to the story. The race to find Jagger and Luna's hideout before Dullsville is permanently transformed into a literal Vampireville transforms the novel into a fast-paced hunt against the clock.

Stylistically, Schreiber’s writing is accessible, visual, and dripping with early-millennium goth-pop culture flavor. Her prose is unpretentious, prioritizing swift pacing, emotional resonance, and playful melodrama over dense world-building. She captures the heightened emotional landscape of adolescence where every glance, slight, and romance feels like a matter of life and death. The contrast between sleepy suburban life and the secretive, shadowy Underworld remains one of the series' greatest strengths, providing a fun, escapist backdrop for readers who relish light supernatural thrills. 

Vampireville does not reinvent the vampire genre, nor does it attempt to. Instead, it delivers precisely what its devoted readership craves: a stylish, romantic, and entertaining chapter in a beloved saga. Schreiber successfully maintains the whimsical, spooky charm that defined the earlier books, ensuring that Raven and Alexander's gothic fairy tale continues to captivate the imagination.
