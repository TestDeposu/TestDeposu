# Something Blue (Darcy & Rachel, #2)

**Author:** Emily Giffin  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

**Something Blue by Emily Giffin: A Glamorous Crash Course in Self-Discovery**

Emily Giffin’s *Something Blue* is the kind of novel that lingers long after the last page—less a story and more a mirror held up to the messy, beautiful chaos of human ambition, betrayal, and redemption. A sequel to *Something Borrowed*, this book dives deeper into the life of Darcy Rhone, a woman who once believed beauty and charm were currency enough to buy happiness. But when her carefully constructed world collapses—thanks to a betrayal by her best friend and the revelation of an unexpected pregnancy—Darcy is forced to confront the hollow truth behind her own rules.

At its core, *Something Blue* is a story about the cost of perfection. Darcy, the "perfect" girl—glamorous, ruthless, and unapologetically self-serving—has spent her life playing by her own ruthless logic: the prettier the girl, the luckier she is. But when Rachel, her plain-Jane best friend, steals her fiancé and leaves her stranded in London with a baby on the way, Darcy’s carefully curated life unravels. What follows is a brutal, unflinching journey of self-examination, where Giffin strips away Darcy’s veneer to reveal a woman who is far more complicated—and far more human—than she ever imagined.

Giffin’s prose is sharp, witty, and effortlessly engaging, blending the razor-sharp social commentary of a modern Jane Austen with the emotional rawness of a contemporary drama. Her dialogue crackles with authenticity, and her characters—especially Darcy—are so vividly flawed that you can’t help but root for them, even when they’re at their most infuriating. The novel’s strength lies in its refusal to offer easy answers. Darcy’s transformation isn’t a neat, Hollywood-style redemption arc; it’s messy, painful, and sometimes humiliating. She makes mistakes, clings to old habits, and only gradually learns that true happiness isn’t about having it all—it’s about wanting the right things.

The book’s exploration of friendship, betrayal, and motherhood is both poignant and timely. Darcy’s relationship with Rachel is one of the most compelling dynamics in the novel, forcing readers to question what it means to be loyal, to forgive, and to grow. Meanwhile, the arrival of a child thrusts Darcy into a role she never saw herself in, and Giffin handles this transition with a mix of humor, heartbreak, and quiet wisdom. The result is a story that feels both intimate and universally relatable—a reminder that even the most confident among us are capable of stumbling, falling, and finding their way back to something real.

What makes *Something Blue* truly special is its refusal to romanticize Darcy’s flaws. She is selfish, manipulative, and often cruel—but she’s also deeply human. Giffin doesn’t let her off the hook, and neither does the reader. Instead, the novel becomes a cathartic exploration of how people change, how love can be both a weapon and a healing force, and how the things we think we want might not be the things we truly need.

For fans of emotional, character-driven fiction with a dash of wit and a whole lot of heart, *Something Blue* is a must-read. It’s a novel that will leave you laughing, crying, and questioning everything you thought you knew about happiness, success, and the people who shape us. In the end, it’s less about the blue dress Darcy buys (a symbol of her attempt to reclaim her old life) and more about the blue sky she eventually learns to see—one filled with possibilities she never dared imagine.
