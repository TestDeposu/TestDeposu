# Jacob (Nightwalkers, #1)

**Author:** Jacquelyn Frank  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2024

**A Love Written in Blood and Moonlight: *Jacob* by Jacquelyn Frank**

Jacquelyn Frank’s *Jacob*—the first installment in the *Nightwalkers* series—is a masterclass in the alchemy of forbidden desire, where centuries-old immortals and mortal hearts collide in a storm of passion, danger, and destiny. At its core, this is a story about the chaos of love when it defies logic, tradition, and even survival. Bella Ross, a quiet librarian with a life as ordinary as her name, never expected to meet her fate by plummeting from a window. But when she lands in the arms of Jacob—a Demon Enforcer bound by an ancient curse—she doesn’t just find a protector; she becomes the catalyst for a fire that could burn them both alive.

Frank’s prose is a seductive blend of lyrical beauty and raw intensity, painting a world where immortals walk among us, their powers as fluid as the forests they command. Jacob, a Demon with the gift of nature’s whispers and the duty to suppress his species’ primal urge to claim human mates during the full moon, is a hero who carries the weight of his kind’s curse like a second skin. His restraint is his armor, but Bella—with her sharp wit, quiet strength, and the unshakable pull of her humanity—is the one thing that could shatter it. The tension between them isn’t just physical; it’s existential. Jacob has spent four hundred years denying the madness that gnaws at his brothers, yet Bella stirs something in him that even time couldn’t silence. The result is a romance that’s as much about control and surrender as it is about love.

What makes *Jacob* so compelling is Frank’s ability to weave romance with stakes that feel visceral and immediate. The Demon world isn’t just a backdrop; it’s a living, breathing entity with its own politics, dangers, and moral ambiguities. Jacob’s role as an Enforcer isn’t just about stopping his kind from losing control—it’s about preserving a fragile balance between their immortality and their humanity. And Bella? She’s not some passive damsel. She’s a woman who sees the world clearly, who questions, who fights, and who refuses to be anyone’s prey. Their dynamic is electric, a dance of trust and tension where every touch could be the last before Jacob’s madness takes over.

The bonus novella, *Kane*, hints at the broader world of the *Nightwalkers* series, introducing another Demon brother and his own mortal mate, Corinne. It’s a promise of more—more brothers, more mates, more stories of love that could either save or destroy them. But *Jacob* stands alone as a perfect storm of passion and peril, a tale where the line between predator and protector blurs, and where love is both the greatest gift and the most dangerous temptation.

Frank’s writing is immersive, her descriptions rich with sensory detail—from the scent of rain on bark to the way Jacob’s touch feels like a brand. She doesn’t shy away from the darker aspects of her world, either. The stakes are high, the consequences brutal, and the emotional payoff is everything. If you love romance that’s as fierce as it is tender, where the heroes are as flawed as they are compelling, and where every page feels like a heartbeat—then *Jacob* is your kind of story.

In a genre often criticized for its clichés, Frank delivers something rare: a love story that feels earned, a world that feels alive, and characters who feel like they’ve been waiting for you all along. *Jacob* isn’t just a book about immortals and mates—it’s about the fire that starts when two souls, one mortal and one eternal, finally dare to meet.
