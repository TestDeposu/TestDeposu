# The 5000 Year Leap

**Author:** W. Cleon Skousen  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1981

"The 5000 Year Leap" by W. Cleon Skousen is a thought-provoking and insightful exploration of the fundamental principles that have shaped the United States of America into the powerful and prosperous nation it is today. Written in 1981, this book remains a timeless and relevant treatise on the essential elements of a free and just society.

Skousen, a renowned scholar and historian, delves into the world of the Founding Fathers, uncovering the 28 basic principles that guided their vision for a new kind of civilization. With meticulous research and a clear, concise writing style, Skousen masterfully weaves together the historical narrative of America's founding, revealing the deliberate and calculated efforts of the Founders to create a society that would guarantee freedom, equality, and justice to all.

One of the most striking aspects of Skousen's work is his ability to distill complex historical and philosophical concepts into accessible and engaging language. His writing is characterized by a clarity and precision that makes the book an enjoyable and enlightening read, even for those without a background in history or politics.

Throughout the book, Skousen emphasizes the significance of the Founders' vision and the enduring relevance of their principles. He argues that the United States has made tremendous progress over the past two centuries, far surpassing the achievements of previous civilizations, precisely because of its adherence to these fundamental principles. Skousen's thesis is both compelling and convincing, offering a refreshing alternative to the prevailing narratives of American history.

What sets "The 5000 Year Leap" apart from other historical and philosophical treatises is its unique blend of scholarship and accessibility. Skousen's use of illustrations, footnotes, and a detailed table of contents adds depth and texture to the book, making it a valuable resource for scholars and general readers alike.

If there is a criticism to be made, it is that Skousen's enthusiasm for the Founders' vision sometimes leads him to gloss over the complexities and controversies of American history. However, this is a minor quibble, and Skousen's overall thesis remains persuasive and well-supported.

In conclusion, "The 5000 Year Leap" is a remarkable book that offers a fresh and inspiring perspective on the founding of the United States. Skousen's masterful storytelling and clear, concise writing style make the book an absolute delight to read. Whether you are a historian, a philosopher, or simply someone interested in understanding the fundamental principles of a free and just society, this book is an absolute must-read.
