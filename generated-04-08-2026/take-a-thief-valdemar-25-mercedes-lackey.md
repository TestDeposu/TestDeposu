# Take a Thief (Valdemar #25)

**Author:** Mercedes Lackey  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2001

In "Take a Thief", Mercedes Lackey weaves a captivating narrative that delves into the untold story of Skif, a beloved character from her debut novel, "Arrows of the Queen". This installment in the Valdemar series is a triumphant return to the world that has enthralled readers for decades. Lackey's masterful storytelling brings to life the journey of Skif, a young orphan who finds himself entangled in a world of thievery and deception.

From the onset, Lackey's vivid descriptions transport readers to the streets of Valdemar, where Skif's tale of survival and self-discovery unfolds. The author's portrayal of Skif's early life, marked by malnutrition and exposure, is both poignant and thought-provoking. It is here that Lackey showcases her ability to craft relatable characters, imbuing Skif with a depth that makes his subsequent transformation all the more compelling.

As Skif navigates the complexities of his newfound profession under the guidance of Deke the pickpocket, Lackey skillfully explores the themes of loyalty, trust, and redemption. The author's writing is characterized by a unique blend of wit, humor, and pathos, making "Take a Thief" an engaging and emotional read.

One of the standout aspects of Lackey's writing style is her ability to balance action, suspense, and character development. The pacing of the narrative is well-balanced, with moments of high tension expertly juxtaposed with periods of introspection and quiet contemplation. This masterful control over the narrative ensures that readers remain invested in Skif's journey, eagerly anticipating the twists and turns that lie ahead.

The world-building in "Take a Thief" is equally impressive, with Lackey drawing upon her extensive knowledge of the Valdemar universe to craft a richly detailed and immersive environment. The author's use of descriptive language effectively transports readers to the streets, taverns, and marketplaces of Valdemar, making it easy to become fully immersed in Skif's world.

Throughout the novel, Lackey explores the complexities of Skif's character, gradually revealing the nuances of his personality and the motivations that drive him. The author's portrayal of Skif's relationships with other characters, particularly Deke and the enigmatic white horse, is both authentic and emotionally resonant.

In conclusion, "Take a Thief" is a triumphant return to the world of Valdemar, and a testament to Mercedes Lackey's skill as a storyteller. This captivating narrative is a must-read for fans of the series, and a compelling introduction for new readers. With its richly detailed world-building, memorable characters, and engaging narrative, "Take a Thief" is an unforgettable reading experience.
