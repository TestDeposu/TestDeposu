# Hurt Go Happy

**Author:** Ginny Rorby  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

"Hurt Go Happy" by Ginny Rorby is a poignant and thought-provoking novel that delves into the world of a deaf teenager, Joey Willis, and her journey towards self-discovery and empowerment. The story is a powerful exploration of the themes of identity, communication, and the unbreakable bonds of friendship.

Through Joey's eyes, Rorby masterfully weaves a narrative that is both heartbreaking and uplifting. The author's vivid descriptions of Joey's struggles to navigate a world that is often hostile to her deafness are deeply moving, and her portrayal of the protagonist's inner world is both authentic and relatable. Joey's frustration, isolation, and longing for connection are palpable, making her a deeply sympathetic character.

The introduction of Dr. Charles Mansell and his baby chimpanzee, Sukari, marks a turning point in the story, as Joey is finally able to find a sense of belonging and purpose. The author's use of sign language as a means of communication between Joey and her new friends is a powerful symbol of connection and understanding. Through this, Rorby highlights the importance of accessibility and inclusivity, and challenges the reader to rethink their assumptions about communication and identity.

One of the strengths of Rorby's writing is her ability to balance lightness and darkness. The novel is peppered with moments of humor and joy, particularly in the scenes between Joey and Sukari, which are both delightful and poignant. However, the author does not shy away from exploring the more difficult themes, such as the exploitation of animals and the challenges faced by individuals with disabilities.

The character of Sukari is particularly noteworthy, as Rorby uses the chimpanzee as a symbol of the power of connection and empathy. Sukari's bond with Joey is deeply moving, and serves as a powerful reminder of the importance of compassion and understanding.

If there is a criticism to be made, it is that the supporting characters are somewhat one-dimensional. Joey's mother, in particular, is portrayed as a rather unsympathetic figure, and her motivations are not fully explored. However, this is a minor quibble in what is otherwise a compelling and thought-provoking novel.

Overall, "Hurt Go Happy" is a beautifully written and deeply moving novel that explores themes of identity, communication, and connection. Rorby's writing is both accessible and engaging, making this a great choice for readers of all ages. The novel is a powerful reminder of the importance of empathy, compassion, and understanding, and is a testament to the transformative power of human connection.
