# End of Watch (Bill Hodges Trilogy, #3)

**Author:** Stephen King  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

Stephen King’s 2016 thriller End of Watch marks the thrilling and unexpected conclusion to the acclaimed Bill Hodges trilogy, which began with Mr. Mercedes and continued with Finders Keepers. In this final installment, King pivots away from traditional hard-boiled detective fiction and plunges headfirst into the realm of the supernatural, delivering a narrative that is as chilling as it is unconventional. 

The story reunites readers with retired detective Bill Hodges and his quirky partner Holly Gibney as they face their most insidious nemesis yet: Brady Hartsfield, the notorious "Mercedes Massacre" killer. Confined to a brain injury clinic in a vegetative state for six years, Brady is seemingly trapped in his own broken body. However, a cocktail of experimental drugs has unlocked a terrifying new dimension within him. Brady now possesses deadly psychic abilities, granting him the power to manipulate minds, induce suicide, and wreak mass havoc—all without ever leaving his hospital room. With thousands of innocent lives at stake and a personal vendetta against Hodges, Brady embarks on a final, high-stakes reign of terror.

At its core, the main theme of End of Watch is the intersection of technology, human consciousness, and malevolence. King explores how modern obsession with screens, video games, and digital connectivity can be weaponized against a vulnerable society. Brady does not need a gun or a stolen car this time; he uses the omnipresent tools of the modern age to infiltrate the minds of teenagers and desperate people, turning them into unwitting instruments of destruction. Furthermore, the novel deeply explores the themes of mortality, legacy, and the heavy toll of fighting evil. Bill Hodges is older, weary, and facing his own physical mortality, adding a poignant, emotional weight to the cat-and-mouse game. 

True to King's signature style, End of Watch blends gritty realism with macabre fantasy. While the first two books adhered strictly to the crime thriller genre, this finale boldly embraces Stephen King's roots in supernatural horror. The author maintains his trademark pacing—relentless, cinematic, and deeply suspenseful—keeping readers on edge with short, punchy chapters that alternate between the perspectives of the dying detective and the awakening monster. King’s dialogue remains sharp, authentic, and laced with dark humor, brilliantly balancing the bleakness of the plot with the heartwarming, albeit eccentric, chemistry between Hodges and Holly. 

While the shift toward science-fiction and telekinesis might surprise purists who expected a grounded police procedural, King executes the transition with his usual storytelling confidence. He reminds us that true evil is not just about physical violence, but about the corrupting influence of a brilliant, unhinged mind. 

End of Watch is a fitting, emotional, and pulse-pounding curtain call for Bill Hodges. It proves that even after decades of writing, Stephen King can still reinvent his own characters and take his readers on a dark, unpredictable ride until the very last page.
