# The History of Joseph Smith by His Mother

**Author:** Lucy Mack Smith  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

"The History of Joseph Smith by His Mother" is a biography that has been shrouded in controversy due to the various versions of Lucy Mack Smith's account of her son's life. However, this 2023 publication, based on the 1996 restoration by Scot and Maurine Proctor, seeks to set the record straight. As a reviewer, I was eager to dive into this reaffirmed version and explore the life of the Mormon prophet through the eyes of his mother.

From the outset, it is clear that Lucy Mack Smith's writing style is distinct and personal. Her narrative is infused with a deep sense of love and devotion to her son, which at times can feel overly sentimental. However, this also lends an air of authenticity to the account, as it is clear that she is writing from a place of deep emotional investment. The author's style is also notable for its folksy, conversational tone, which makes the narrative feel more like a series of oral traditions than a formal biography.

One of the main themes that emerges from this biography is the notion of Joseph Smith as a chosen vessel for divine revelation. Lucy Mack Smith presents her son as a spiritual prodigy, blessed with visions and visitations from a young age. This portrayal is deeply rooted in the Mormon faith, and it is clear that the author sees her son's life as a testament to the power of faith and spiritual guidance.

Throughout the book, Lucy Mack Smith also grapples with the challenges and hardships that her son faced, from the early days of persecution to the eventual founding of the Mormon church. Her account is marked by a sense of resilience and determination, as she recounts the ways in which her son and his followers persevered in the face of adversity.

One of the strengths of this biography is its ability to humanize Joseph Smith, presenting him as a complex and multifaceted individual rather than a mere icon or symbol. Lucy Mack Smith's account is full of anecdotes and personal details that bring her son to life, from his childhood antics to his later struggles with leadership and responsibility.

If there is a weakness to the book, it is the author's tendency to gloss over certain aspects of her son's life, particularly those that might be seen as contentious or problematic. For example, the account of Joseph Smith's multiple marriages is handled somewhat cursorily, and the author's treatment of his relationships with other women feels somewhat sanitized.

Despite this, "The History of Joseph Smith by His Mother" remains a compelling and deeply personal account of the Mormon prophet's life. Lucy Mack Smith's writing style may be somewhat idiosyncratic, but it is also deeply engaging, and her love for her son shines through on every page. This biography is a must-read for anyone interested in the life and times of Joseph Smith, and it offers a unique and fascinating perspective on the early days of the Mormon faith.
