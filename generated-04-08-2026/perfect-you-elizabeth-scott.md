The novel’s core theme is the painful journey from self‑isolation to self‑acceptance. Yet Scott refuses to let her protagonist stay trapped in that mindset. Through small, honest moments—a misstep at the mall, an awkward conversation with Will, a fleeting glimpse of her father’s vulnerability—Kate learns that perfection is not a static ideal but a messy, ongoing process of letting others in. The result is a narrative that feels both urgent and contemplative, never slipping into melodrama while still honoring the intensity of teen emotion.

Scott’s handling of secondary characters also deserves note. Kate’s best friend, though seemingly aloof, embodies the shifting loyalties that often accompany adolescence, and her father’s career change serves as a metaphor for the unpredictability of adult life that teens must navigate.
