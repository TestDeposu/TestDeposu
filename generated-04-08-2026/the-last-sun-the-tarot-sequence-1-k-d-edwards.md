# The Last Sun (The Tarot Sequence, #1)

**Author:** K.D. Edwards  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

Urban fantasy often drowns in a sea of interchangeable tropes, recycled mythologies, and brooding protagonists who blur into a monotonous gray. Enter K.D. Edwards and his blazing 2018 debut, The Last Sun, the explosive opening salvo of The Tarot Sequence. Edwards takes the familiar bones of contemporary magical noir and infuses them with a vibrant, high-stakes mythology, creating a world that feels simultaneously ancient and aggressively modern. At once a pulse-pounding detective story and a deeply emotional trauma narrative, this novel carves out a distinct and unforgettable space in modern speculative fiction.

The beating heart of the book is Rune Saint John, the last surviving member of the disgraced and brutally murdered Sun Court. Living in the neon-drenched, high-tech ruins of New Atlantis—an island refuge where the survivors of a shattered magical race dwell alongside ordinary humanity—Rune operates as a reluctant private investigator. When he is hired to find the missing son of Lady Judgment, the case pulls him out of his self-imposed isolation and hurls him back into the gilded, treacherous cage of Atlantean high society. Alongside his steadfast companion and bodyguard, Brand, Rune must navigate a labyrinth of political posturing, ancient vendettas, and lethal magic. 

The primary theme coursing through the veins of The Last Sun is the agonizing, nonlinear journey of survival and recovery after unimaginable trauma. Unlike many fantasy heroes who bounce back from tragedy with a sarcastic quip and a sharpened blade, Rune wears his scars visibly and internally. His past is not merely a backstory for edgy motivation; it is a heavy, suffocating anchor. Edwards explores how abuse, grief, and survivor’s guilt shape a person’s identity. Rune’s quest to find Addam is inextricably tied to his desperate, agonizing need to understand the massacre that stole his family and subjected him to unspeakable horrors. The mystery of the missing nobleman cleverly mirrors Rune's internal investigation into his own shattered psyche, blending external peril with profound emotional resonance.

Stylistically, Edwards writes with a kinetic, cinematic flair that refuses to let the reader look away. His prose is punchy, deeply atmospheric, and laced with a sharp, defensive wit that serves as Rune's primary armor against the world. New Atlantis springs to life through sensory overload: towering glass spires, underground black-market fighting rings, opulent high-society galas, and the crackle of volatile Arcana. The world-building is dense, drawing heavily upon the rich symbolism of the Tarot deck, yet Edwards parcels out the lore organically, ensuring the reader is never bogged down by exposition. The action sequences are choreographed with precision, moving at a breakneck speed that matches the relentless urgency of the plot.

Furthermore, the relationship between Rune and Brand is a masterclass in dynamic character writing. Their bond—rooted in fierce loyalty, shared pain, and a profound, unspoken devotion—anchors the high-concept magical chaos in genuine human emotion. In a genre often saturated with grim-dark cynicism, the fierce tenderness and protective love between these characters provide a glowing, defiant ember of hope.

The Last Sun is an electrifying debut that successfully balances heartbreak with high adventure. K.D. Edwards has constructed a richly layered urban fantasy that respects its genre roots while vigorously subverting them. For readers craving a protagonist they can genuinely root for, a world brimming with secrets, and a mystery that cuts dangerously close to the bone, this novel is an absolute must-read.
