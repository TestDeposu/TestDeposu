# Wicked Appetite (Lizzy & Diesel, #1)

**Author:** Janet Evanovich  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

**Wicked Appetite: A Feast for the Senses—Where Sin, Steak, and Stealth Collide**

Janet Evanovich’s *Wicked Appetite* isn’t just another romp through the Seven Deadly Sins—it’s a high-stakes, fast-talking, pie-in-the-face celebration of chaos, charm, and the kind of trouble that only a man who eats like a linebacker and thinks like a criminal mastermind can get into. This first installment in the *Lizzy & Diesel* series (a spin-off of Evanovich’s beloved *Stephanie Plum* universe) delivers the same razor-sharp wit, relentless energy, and darkly comedic tension that fans adore, but with a fresh twist: a culinary detective duo on the hunt for a cursed artifact—and a villain who’s *literally* starving for power.

At its core, *Wicked Appetite* is a story about the seductive allure of excess. The relic in question? A golden chalice said to embody gluttony, a sin Diesel—Evanovich’s everyman antihero—finds as tempting as the steaks he devours between missions. But gluttony here isn’t just about overindulgence; it’s about the hunger for more—more power, more control, more *everything*—and the way it corrupts. Gerewulf Grimorie, Diesel’s cousin and the book’s villain, embodies this perfectly: a man so consumed by his own ambition that he’ll burn down a town (or a bakery) to get what he wants. His obsession with the chalice mirrors the real-world dangers of unchecked desire, a theme Evanovich explores with her signature blend of satire and suspense.

The real star, though, is the partnership between Diesel and Lizzie Tucker, a pastry chef with a sharp tongue and a sharper eye for trouble. Where Diesel is brute force wrapped in a charming, slightly dim-witted exterior, Lizzie is the brains—and the one who keeps him from getting himself (or her) killed. Their dynamic is electric, a mix of bickering, reluctant respect, and the kind of chemistry that makes you root for them even when they’re being idiots. Lizzie’s world—Marblehead’s cozy, historic streets, her bustling bakery, the small-town gossip that fuels the plot—grounds the absurdity of the supernatural stakes, making the chase for the relics feel both grand and intimate. Evanovich’s knack for blending the mundane with the mythic is on full display here, whether it’s Diesel accidentally triggering a curse by eating too much pie or Lizzie outsmarting a villain with nothing but a well-timed croissant.

Evanovich’s prose is as delectable as the pastries Lizzie bakes. Her dialogue crackles with humor, her descriptions are vivid and often absurd (imagine a ghostly apparition that’s *literally* a walking pile of sins), and her pacing is relentless. There’s no time to catch your breath—just one set piece after another, from a high-speed chase through Salem’s historic streets to a showdown in a bakery that’s been turned into a battleground. The book’s energy is infectious, a perfect blend of *Indiana Jones* meets *The Simpsons*, with a dash of *True Detective*’s darker undertones.

But *Wicked Appetite* isn’t just about thrills—it’s a meditation on the sins themselves, and how they manifest in real life. Gluttony isn’t just about food; it’s about greed, about taking more than you need, about letting desire rule you. And Evanovich doesn’t shy away from the consequences. Gerewulf’s downfall isn’t just because he’s a bad guy—it’s because he *chose* to be. The same could be said for Diesel, who spends most of the book trying to out-eat, out-drink, and out-sneak his way to victory. The message? You can’t outrun your nature, but you can outsmart it. And sometimes, the best way to defeat a sin is to indulge in it—just a little less.

For fans of Evanovich’s work, *Wicked Appetite* is a love letter to the chaos they know and love, but with a fresh cast of characters and a setting that feels like a cross between *Stephanie Plum*’s small-town charm and *Stacks*’ supernatural intrigue. It’s fast, funny, and occasionally frightening, with a villain who’s one of Evanovich’s most compelling yet—a man so consumed by his own hunger that he becomes a monster. And Diesel? Well, he’s still Diesel: a man who can’t resist a challenge, a steak, or a good pie, even when it’s about to get him killed.

If you’ve ever wanted a book that’s equal parts *Breakfast Club* and *Game of Thrones*—but with more pie and less blood—*Wicked Appetite* is your ticket. It’s a feast for the senses, a race against time, and a reminder that sometimes, the best way to survive is to eat, drink, and be merry… before the sins catch up to you.
