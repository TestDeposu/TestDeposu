# A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms (The Tales of Dunk and Egg, #1-3)

**Author:** George R.R. Martin  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

**A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms: Where Chivalry Meets the Shadows of Westeros**

George R.R. Martin’s *A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms* isn’t just a collection—it’s a time machine. Set nearly a century before the brutal, dragon-fueled chaos of *A Game of Thrones*, this trio of novellas (*The Hedge Knight*, *The Sworn Sword*, and *The Mystery Knight*) reintroduces us to a Westeros still steeped in the fading glory of the Targaryens, where knights still ride for honor, and dragons are more myth than memory. At its heart, this is a story about two unlikely heroes: Ser Duncan the Tall, a broad-shouldered hedge knight with a heart as noble as his sword arm, and his diminutive squire, Egg (whose true name remains a mystery even to the reader). Together, they navigate a world where loyalty is tested, kingship is a game of thrones, and the line between heroism and folly is as thin as a blade’s edge.

What makes *A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms* so compelling isn’t just its rich worldbuilding—though Martin’s ability to weave Westeros’ history into vivid, living detail is unmatched—but its exploration of the tension between idealism and reality. Dunk is a knight of the old school, bound by codes of chivalry that seem increasingly anachronistic in an age of shifting alliances and ruthless ambition. His squire, Egg, is the everyman, a boy who stumbles into greatness through sheer persistence and a knack for survival. Their adventures—from the tournament halls of King’s Landing to the war-torn streets of the Riverlands—serve as a poignant meditation on what it means to be a hero in a world that no longer rewards virtue as it once did.

Martin’s prose, as always, is sharp and immersive, balancing wit with gravitas. His dialogue crackles with the kind of banter that makes characters feel real, whether it’s Dunk’s dry humor or Egg’s wide-eyed naivety. The novellas are short but dense, packed with the kind of intricate plotting and political maneuvering that fans of *A Song of Ice and Fire* will recognize. Yet, unlike the sprawling, often bleak epic of the main series, these stories feel lighter, more focused on the personal stakes of honor, friendship, and the cost of ambition. There’s a sense of nostalgia here, a longing for a time when knights still believed in the ideals they swore to uphold, even as the world around them crumbles.

The illustrations by Gary Gianni are a delightful addition, bringing the world to life with a mix of whimsy and grit. Whether it’s the towering figure of Dunk or the mischievous grin of Egg, the art captures the essence of these characters and their world, making the reading experience even more vivid.

For fans of *A Song of Ice and Fire*, this collection is a treasure trove—it deepens the lore, introduces beloved characters in their youth, and offers a glimpse of the world before the dragons’ return. But it’s also a stand-alone adventure, a tale of camaraderie and courage that stands on its own merits. If you’ve ever wondered what Westeros was like before the war, before the madness, before the dragons—this is your answer.

*A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms* isn’t just a prelude; it’s a celebration of the stories that shaped the world we know. And in a universe where heroes are often few and far between, Dunk and Egg prove that even the most unlikely of men can carve out their own legend.
