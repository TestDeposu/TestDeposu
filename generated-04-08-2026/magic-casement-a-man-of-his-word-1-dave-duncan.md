# Magic Casement (A Man of His Word, #1)

**Author:** Dave Duncan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Classic high fantasy often relies on towering castles, ancient prophecies, and sprawling maps, but the true masters of the genre know that enduring stories are built on the fragile bonds between ordinary hearts caught in extraordinary storms. With Magic Casement, the inaugural volume of his beloved A Man of His Word series, the late Dave Duncan established himself as just such a master. Originally introduced to readers in the late twentieth century and re-released to captivate a new generation, this novel serves as a masterclass in blending whimsical wonder with high-stakes peril, anchored firmly by a timeless romance.

At the narrative heart of Magic Casement is a theme as old as storytelling itself: the gulf between social strata and the unwavering power of devotion to bridge it. Inos, a spirited young princess, and Rap, a resourceful stable boy, share an idyllic childhood in the remote mountain kingdom of Krasnegar. Yet, the realities of their world dictate that a princess and a stable boy can never truly belong together. Duncan uses this classic setup not as a tired trope, but as the emotional anchor for a narrative that rapidly spirals outward into a kaleidoscopic universe of magic, abduction, and imperial ambition. When sudden maturity and dark forces rupture their peaceful existence, the true journey begins. 

What elevates Duncan’s work above standard genre fare is his distinctive stylistic approach. He writes with a deceptive lightness, infusing his prose with a conversational wit and a brisk, episodic momentum that makes turning the pages effortless. Yet, beneath the charming surface lies a remarkably ruthless world-building philosophy. Duncan does not coddle his characters. As Inos and Rap are violently separated and thrust into bewildering new realms—with Inos cast into the treacherous lands of Zark and Rap navigating the bizarre wonders of Faerie—the narrative takes on the breathless urgency of a desperate odyssey. 

The author’s magic system and geographical scope are wonderfully imaginative, operating under strict, whimsical rules that nevertheless carry lethal consequences. Danger lurks behind every magical casement, and the looming threat of the fearsome warlock wardens ensures that the stakes remain agonizingly high. Furthermore, Duncan’s characterization is a triumph. While Inos and Rap provide the romantic engine of the tale, the inclusion of formidable supporting figures—most notably the pragmatic and indomitable Aunt Kade—adds layers of grit, tactical brilliance, and sharp humor that prevent the narrative from ever descending into pure melodrama.

Magic Casement is a portal fantasy in the truest sense, inviting readers through a window into a universe where love is pitted against empires, and where a humble stable boy must achieve the impossible to reclaim his world and his queen. Dave Duncan crafts an intoxicating blend of swashbuckling adventure, coming-of-age trials, and high-stakes political machination. For both devoted fantasy aficionados and newcomers looking for a rich, emotionally resonant epic, this novel proves that a steadfast heart is the most potent magic of all.
