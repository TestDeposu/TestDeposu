# Red Sister (Book of the Ancestor, #1)

**Author:** Mark Lawrence  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Mark Lawrence has carved a formidable reputation out of the ashes of grimdark fantasy, known for constructing worlds where hope is a rare currency and protagonists are forged in the fires of trauma. With *Red Sister*, the explosive first entry in his 2017 *Book of the Ancestor* series, Lawrence pivots slightly from the cynical brutality of his earlier *Broken Empire* trilogy, offering instead a narrative that is equally dark yet strangely illuminated by the fierce, unyielding bonds of sisterhood. At its core, *Red Sister* is a brutal coming-of-age story that asks a haunting question: how do you teach children to become holy warriors in a dying world without losing their humanity entirely?

The novel opens with one of the most arresting hooks in modern fantasy: a nine-year-old girl named Nona Grey standing on the gallows, a noose tightening around her neck. Saved from the executioner at the eleventh hour, Nona is inducted into the Convent of Sweet Mercy, a secretive institution nestled beneath a dying sun where the moons are shattered and the world is slowly freezing to death. Here, girls possessing the rare, ancient bloodlines of the world are trained in the deadly arts of blade, fist, poison, and shadow. 

Lawrence’s thematic exploration centers on agency, belonging, and the duality of violence. The Convent of Sweet Mercy is a paradox—a sanctuary of holy women who are simultaneously lethal assassins. Nona herself is a masterpiece of character design. She is feral, driven by a deep well of rage and a protective instinct that burns like a hearth fire. Yet, her journey at the convent is not merely about learning how to kill more efficiently; it is about finding a place where she belongs. Through Nona, Lawrence examines how society shapes the marginalized, weaponizing the unwanted and dressing their survival in the language of divine duty. 

The author’s signature style is immediately recognizable in the prose, which is razor-sharp, atmospheric, and relentlessly paced. Lawrence possesses a rare talent for sensory world-building. The Convent feels claustrophobic, cold, and ancient, smelling of candle wax, stone dust, and iron. He avoids bloated exposition, preferring instead to drop the reader directly into the brutal reality of Nona’s daily life. The training scenes—meticulously detailed lessons in anatomy, combat, and mysticism—are thrilling, driven by a kinetic energy that makes it nearly impossible to put the book down. 

Furthermore, Lawrence excels at crafting complex, morally grey interpersonal dynamics. Nona’s friendships with her fellow novices provide the emotional anchor of the novel. In a world defined by political machinations, imperial decay, and impending doom, these relationships are the beating heart of the story, offering warmth against the encroaching literal and figurative cold. 

*Red Sister* is a triumph of epic fantasy. It takes familiar tropes—the magic school, the chosen-one narrative, the assassin convent—and subverts them through brilliant prose, unforgettable characters, and a relentless thematic focus on the cost of power. Lawrence has created a masterpiece of dark fantasy that is as emotionally resonant as it is viscerally exciting, proving once again that he is one of the most vital voices in the genre today.
