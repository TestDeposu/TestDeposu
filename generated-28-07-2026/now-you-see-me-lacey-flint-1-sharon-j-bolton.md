# Now You See Me (Lacey Flint, #1)

**Author:** Sharon J. Bolton  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

In the gripping world of crime fiction, few authors have managed to craft a tale as masterfully woven as Sharon J. Bolton's "Now You See Me". This debut novel in the Lacey Flint series is a modern gothic masterpiece that delves into the darkest corners of human psychology, weaving a complex web of suspense that will leave readers on the edge of their seats.

At its core, "Now You See Me" is a story about the blurred lines between reality and obsession. When a young detective constable, Lacey Flint, stumbles upon a brutal murder in a London parking lot, she unwittingly becomes the focal point of a sinister game. The killer, seemingly inspired by the infamous Jack the Ripper, sends an anonymous letter to a reporter, taunting Lacey and drawing her into a cat-and-mouse chase that will push her to the limits of her sanity.

Bolton's writing style is akin to a masterful puppeteer, expertly manipulating the strings of tension and suspense to create a narrative that is both fast-paced and deeply unsettling. Her prose is razor-sharp, with a keen eye for detail that brings the dark, gritty world of London's streets to vivid life. The characters, too, are multidimensional and complex, with Lacey Flint being a particularly compelling protagonist. Her troubled past and inner demons make her a deeply human and relatable character, and her determination to catch the killer is both admirable and heartbreaking.

One of the most striking aspects of "Now You See Me" is its exploration of the theme of obsession. The killer's fixation on Lacey and the Ripper's crimes is a twisted manifestation of this, but it's also a reflection of Lacey's own obsession with the case. As the investigation unfolds, Lacey becomes increasingly entangled in the killer's web, and the lines between hunter and prey begin to blur. This blurring of boundaries is a hallmark of Bolton's writing style, and it adds a layer of complexity to the narrative that is both thought-provoking and deeply unsettling.

The pacing of the novel is relentless, with Bolton expertly ratcheting up the tension as the story hurtles towards its climax. The twists and turns are expertly handled, with a few genuinely shocking moments that will leave even the most seasoned thriller readers gasping in surprise. The supporting cast is equally well-drawn, with a particular standout being the enigmatic and charismatic DI Dana Tulloch, who becomes Lacey's unlikely ally in the hunt for the killer.

Overall, "Now You See Me" is a triumph of modern crime fiction. Bolton's writing is masterful, her characters are complex and compelling, and the narrative is a gripping, suspenseful ride that will keep readers on the edge of their seats. If you're a fan of crime fiction, or just looking for a gripping thriller to keep you up late at night, then "Now You See Me" is an absolute must-read.
