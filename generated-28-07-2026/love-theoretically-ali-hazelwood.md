# Love, Theoretically

**Author:** Ali Hazelwood  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

**Love, Theoretically: A Quantum Leap in Rom-Com Magic**

Ali Hazelwood, the reigning queen of STEM romance, is back with 'Love, Theoretically', a delightful concoction of academic rivalry, fake dating, and heartwarming realness. Hazelwood's signature wit and unapologetic nerdiness shine brightly in this tale of two physicists locked in a dance of attraction and animosity.

Elsie Hannaway, our heroine, is a theoretical physicist with a side hustle as a fake girlfriend. Her life is a carefully orchestrated symphony of personas, each tailored to please her clients. But when Jack Smith, experimental physicist and Elsie's nemesis, enters the scene, her perfectly calibrated existence starts to unravel. Jack, with his arrogance and undeniable charm, is the antithesis of everything Elsie believes in, yet she finds herself drawn to him, like protons and electrons in a dance of attraction and repulsion.

Hazelwood masterfully weaves the intricacies of physics into the narrative, making it not just a rom-com, but a love letter to the world of academia. The banter between Elsie and Jack is as sparkling as the periodic table, and their intellectual sparring matches are as exhilarating as a high-stakes physics experiment. Yet, beneath the wit and the rivalry, there's a depth of emotion that Hazelwood handles with her signature grace. Elsie's struggle with her multiple personas, her fear of commitment, and her desire for acceptance are all relatable, making her a heroine worth rooting for.

'Love, Theoretically' is more than just a rom-com; it's a testament to the power of being true to oneself, the beauty of intellectual pursuits, and the transformative nature of love. Hazelwood's style is as engaging as ever, her characters as endearing, and her plot as unpredictable. She continues to prove that love, like physics, is a complex, beautiful, and sometimes messy dance.
