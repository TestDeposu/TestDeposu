# The Crown (The Selection, #5)

**Author:** Kiera Cass  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

**A Royal Finale: "The Crown" Delights and Disappoints in Equal Measure**

Kiera Cass's The Selection series comes to a close with "The Crown," the fifth and final installment of the beloved series. Since its debut, Cass's world of royals, romance, and politics has captivated readers worldwide, and it's no surprise that fans eagerly await the conclusion of America Singer and Prince Maxon's story. With Eadlyn, their daughter, taking center stage, Cass delivers a satisfying (if predictable) finale that explores the complexities of duty, love, and leadership.

One of Cass's greatest strengths as an author is her ability to craft relatable protagonists that resonate deeply with readers. Eadlyn, as the protagonist, is a worthy successor to her mother's story, bringing her own set of anxieties, insecurities, and strengths to the table. As the first princess to hold her own Selection, Eadlyn is under immense scrutiny, not only from the court but also from the public. Cass astutely examines the weight of expectation and the fear of living up to one's family's legacy, making Eadlyn's struggles feel authentic and heart-wrenching.

Cass's writing style, which often relies on convenient plot devices and predictable love interests, is still an issue in "The Crown." At times, the story feels like a series of events strung together rather than a cohesive narrative. However, Cass's prose is engaging and easy to follow, making the book a quick and enjoyable read. Additionally, her exploration of the Selection's inner workings and the intricacies of royal politics are fascinating and well-researched.

The real highlight of "The Crown" lies in its portrayal of Eadlyn's inner conflict as she navigates her duty to her country and her burgeoning feelings for a mysterious suitor. Cass does a commendable job of making the reader question the nature of duty versus heart, leaving one to wonder whether a ruler can truly have it all. The supporting cast, though largely forgettable, serves its purpose, adding some much-needed depth to the story.

Ultimately, "The Crown" is a satisfying conclusion to the Selection series, if not an overly surprising one. While some fans may be disappointed by the predictability of the love interests or the convenient resolutions to various plot threads, others will appreciate the character development and the sense of closure that comes with the finale. For those who have grown attached to the world of Illea and its inhabitants, "The Crown" is a worthy conclusion that will leave you feeling hopeful and optimistic about the future of the royal family.

If you're looking for a lighthearted, feel-good romance with a touch of royal intrigue, "The Crown" is an excellent choice. However, for those seeking something with more depth and complexity, you may be better off looking elsewhere.
