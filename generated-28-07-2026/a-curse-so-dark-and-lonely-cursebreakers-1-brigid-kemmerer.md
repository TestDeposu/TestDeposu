# A Curse So Dark and Lonely (Cursebreakers, #1)

**Author:** Brigid Kemmerer  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

In "A Curse So Dark and Lonely", Brigid Kemmerer masterfully weaves a captivating tale that breathes new life into the classic Beauty and the Beast narrative. This lush, contemporary fantasy retelling is a beautifully crafted exploration of hope, redemption, and the transformative power of love.

At its core, the story revolves around the poignant struggle of Prince Rhen, the heir to Emberfall, who is cursed to relive the autumn of his eighteenth year ad infinitum. With each passing cycle, he becomes increasingly desperate to break the curse, which ultimately leads to his transformation into a monstrous beast. As Rhen's world begins to crumble, he finds himself losing all sense of hope.

Enter Harper, a fiercely determined and strong-willed young woman from Washington, DC, who is struggling to cope with her own set of challenges. With her father absent, her mother dying, and her brother underestimating her due to her cerebral palsy, Harper has learned to rely solely on herself. When she is suddenly transported to Rhen's cursed world, she finds herself entangled in a complex web of magic, politics, and ancient secrets.

Kemmerer's writing style is nothing short of enchanting. Her prose is rich, evocative, and masterfully paced, effortlessly drawing the reader into the world of Emberfall. The characters are multidimensional and relatable, with Harper's sharp wit and Rhen's brooding intensity making for a compelling contrast.

One of the most striking aspects of "A Curse So Dark and Lonely" is its thoughtful exploration of disability and representation. Harper's cerebral palsy is not portrayed as a weakness, but rather as an integral part of her identity. Kemmerer handles this aspect with sensitivity and nuance, avoiding stereotypes and instead crafting a powerful narrative that celebrates Harper's agency and strength.

The romance that blossoms between Harper and Rhen is sweet and tender, but it is not without its challenges. Kemmerer skillfully navigates the complexities of their relationship, avoiding clichés and instead opting for a nuanced portrayal of two damaged individuals finding solace in each other.

As the story unfolds, Kemmerer masterfully ratchets up the tension, introducing a cast of complex characters and unexpected plot twists. The world-building is meticulous, with a richly detailed history that adds depth and texture to the narrative.

Ultimately, "A Curse So Dark and Lonely" is a beautifully crafted tale that will captivate fans of fantasy, romance, and fairy tale retellings. With its lush prose, memorable characters, and thought-provoking themes, this book is a must-read for anyone looking to escape into a richly imagined world.
