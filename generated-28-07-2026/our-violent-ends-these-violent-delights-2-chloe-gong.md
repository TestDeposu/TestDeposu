# Our Violent Ends (These Violent Delights, #2)

**Author:** Chloe Gong  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

Amid the neon-soaked shadows and blood-stained gutters of 1927 Shanghai, Chloe Gong returns with Our Violent Ends, a searing and breathless conclusion to the duology that began with These Violent Delights. If the first installment was an intoxicating introduction to a fractured city, this sequel is a full-scale detonation. Gong takes the timeless, star-crossed tragedy of Romeo and Juliet and plunges it headfirst into the tumultuous machinery of political revolution, gang warfare, and body horror, crafting a historical fantasy that is as intellectually ambitious as it is emotionally devastating.

At the heart of Our Violent Ends lies the toxic, pulsating theme of sacrifice born from profound loyalty. Juliette Cai and Roma Montagov are no longer just warring heirs; they are victims of a brutal geopolitical and familial chess game. Juliette wears the mask of a remorseless cold-blooded killer—a heavy crown worn to shield Roma from the lethal wrath of the Scarlet Gang. Roma, meanwhile, drowns in grief and the intoxicating dissonance of hating the girl who murdered his best friend, even as every beat of his heart betrays him by continuing to love her. Gong explores the agony of self-sabotage and emotional martyrdom, exposing how love, in times of war and political upheaval, is not a sanctuary, but a dangerous liability. The novel asks a haunting question: how much of yourself can you tear away to protect the person you adore before there is nothing left of either of you?

Against this backdrop of personal ruin, Shanghai itself becomes a living, breathing character teetering on the precipice of annihilation. The backdrop of 1927—fraught with marching Nationalists, communist uprisings, and the twilight of gangster dominance—is not merely decorative. Gong masterfully weaves historical tension with speculative fiction, integrating a terrifying, parasitic monster threat that mirrors the internal rot of a city tearing itself apart from the inside out. The monsters in the Huangpu River are horrifying, yes, but they are ultimately symptoms of a deeper societal disease: imperialism, betrayal, and greed.

Stylistically, Gong’s prose is lush, sensory, and relentlessly paced. She has a rare gift for atmospheric world-building; readers can practically taste the bitter tea, smell the ozone and copper of the streets, and feel the suffocating humidity of the Chinese summer. Her dialogue snaps with sharp wit and underlying vulnerability, while her action sequences are choreographed with cinematic precision. Yet, it is her psychological acuity that truly elevates the narrative. Gong lingers in the quiet, agonizing spaces between her characters, capturing the unbearable weight of unspoken truths and lingering glances. The romantic tension is exquisitely painful, built on a foundation of mutual survival and inevitable doom.

Our Violent Ends is a triumph of YA historical fantasy. It delivers a masterclass in pacing, high-stakes drama, and emotional payoff, refusing to offer easy resolutions in a world that is fundamentally broken. Chloe Gong cements her reputation not just as a gifted reinterpreter of classics, but as a formidable literary voice capable of breaking her readers' hearts and beautifully piecing them back together again.
