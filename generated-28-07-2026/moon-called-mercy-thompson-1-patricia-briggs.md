# Moon Called (Mercy Thompson, #1)

**Author:** Patricia Briggs  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2006

When Patricia Briggs introduced Mercedes Thompson to the urban fantasy landscape in 2006 with Moon Called, she did not merely launch a bestselling series; she quietly redefined what a supernatural heroine could be. In a genre increasingly saturated with leather-clad badasses relying solely on inherited magical bloodlines or overt physical dominance, Mercy stands out precisely because of her quiet resilience, vulnerability, and utter lack of pretense.

The central theme of Moon Called is the delicate, often painful negotiation of belonging. Mercy is an outsider by nature and by circumstance. As a walker—a Native American shapeshifter capable of taking the form of a coyote—she exists in the liminal spaces between human society and the nocturnal underworld of werewolves and vampires. Raised by a werewolf pack after the death of her father, she was ultimately cast out for a forbidden romance. Yet, despite the betrayal, she cannot escape the pull of pack politics. Briggs brilliantly uses Mercy's mechanical trade as a metaphor for her life: she spends her days covered in grease, fixing broken things that others have discarded, trying to keep the fractured pieces of her existence running smoothly. When a terrified, newly turned teenage werewolf stumbles into her garage seeking refuge, Mercy's instinctual need to protect the broken shatters the fragile boundary she has built between her past and present.

Briggs’s authorial style is defined by its grounded, unpretentious realism. Even though the novel is steeped in myth, folklore, and blood-soaked pack dynamics, the Tri-Cities of Washington State feel vividly lived-in. Briggs avoids the purple prose and heavy-handed exposition that often plague first-person urban fantasy narratives. Instead, Mercy’s voice is sharp, deeply observant, and laced with a dry, self-deprecating humor that keeps the high-stakes drama tethered to earth. The pacing is relentless, moving seamlessly from atmospheric tension-building to kinetic, brutal action sequences. 

What elevates Moon Called above standard paranormal fluff is its complex exploration of power dynamics. Werewolf packs are hierarchical, rigid, and inherently patriarchal, contrasted sharply with the coyote's nature as a trickster—clever, adaptable, and deceptively resilient. Mercy is physically outmatched by almost every supernatural creature she encounters, yet she survives and protects those she loves through sheer wit, loyalty, and emotional intelligence. Her interactions with Adam Hauptman, the local Alpha who is both intimidating neighbor and potential threat, crackle with sharp dialogue and a slow-burn tension that avoids tired romance tropes.

Moon Called is a triumph of urban fantasy world-building and character design. Patricia Briggs crafted a protagonist who is tough without being callous, independent without being isolated, and deeply human despite her non-human nature. For readers looking for a smart, fast-paced supernatural mystery with a genuinely refreshing heroine, Mercy Thompson’s debut remains an absolute must-read.
