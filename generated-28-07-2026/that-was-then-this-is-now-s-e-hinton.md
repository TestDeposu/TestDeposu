# That Was Then, This Is Now

**Author:** S.E. Hinton  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1971

In the early 1970s, youth culture was a powder keg, and S.E. Hinton's "That Was Then, This Is Now" captured the raw energy and desperation of that era in a compelling narrative. Written in the same raw and honest voice as her breakout novel "The Outsiders," this book delves deeper into the complexities of male friendship and the fragility of youthful innocence.

The storyline is centered around Mark and Bryon, two close friends navigating the treacherous landscape of 1960s California. They're at a crossroads, unsure of what the future holds or what they truly want from life. As they face the harsh realities of growing up, they begin to drift apart, and Bryon becomes entangled in a relationship with Cathy, a more mature and emotionally stable girl. This change in Bryon's life only adds fuel to the already volatile atmosphere, and eventually, tragedy sets in.

Hinton's mastery in portraying teenage angst and rebellion shines in this book, particularly in her depiction of Bryon's grappling with identity. His desire to escape the violent and troubled world around him, in part by embracing the comfort and security that Cathy brings, resonates deeply with the adolescent reader. Conversely, Mark remains stuck in the cycle of petty crime and delinquency, a stark contrast to Bryon's increasing maturity, highlighting the tension between two individuals who were once as close as brothers.

One of the most striking aspects of "That Was Then, This Is Now" is its gripping portrayal of the destructive power of masculinity. The men in this narrative are not romanticized or reduced to simplistic archetypes; instead, they are multidimensional characters, each struggling to come to terms with their own limitations, fears, and desires. Charlie's tragic death serves as a catalyst for change, propelling Bryon toward a confrontation with the harsh realities he had previously avoided.

Hinton's writing is as unflinching as it is compassionate, capturing the raw emotions and inner turmoil of the characters. Her use of vivid imagery and poetic prose immerses the reader in a world that feels both authentic and eerily familiar. The book is a poignant exploration of the human condition, laying bare the vulnerability that lies beneath even the toughest facades.

Ultimately, "That Was Then, This Is Now" is a testament to S.E. Hinton's skill as a storyteller and her ability to craft relatable characters that linger long after the story concludes. It presents a haunting and thought-provoking portrayal of adolescence and the enduring power of male friendships. This book serves as a powerful reminder that our relationships and experiences are what shape us – and ultimately, make us who we are.
