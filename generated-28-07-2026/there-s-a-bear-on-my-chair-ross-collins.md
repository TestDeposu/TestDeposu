# There's A Bear On My Chair

**Author:** Ross Collins  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

In the delightful world of contemporary children's literature, few books manage to marry visual comedy with lyrical brilliance quite as seamlessly as Ross Collins’s 2017 triumph, There’s A Bear On My Chair. At first glance, the premise is deceptively simple: a fastidious, pint-sized mouse finds his favorite chair occupied by a remarkably stubborn and oblivious giant bear. What follows is a masterclass in pacing, tone, and comedic frustration that appeals just as much to the adults reading aloud as it does to the wide-eyed listeners on their laps. 

At the heart of the book lies the universal theme of personal space and the humorous futility of trying to control the uncontrollable. Mouse’s struggle is deeply existential yet utterly relatable. He attempts every tactic in the book—from polite requests to outright bribery and aggressive posturing—to evict the massive interloper. Yet, Bear remains entirely unfazed, an immovable mountain of fur and nonchalance. Collins uses this dynamic to explore the friction between expectation and reality, teaching young readers a subtle lesson in perseverance, frustration, and the eventual irony that comes when life turns the tables on us. 

Stylistically, Collins channels the whimsical, anarchic spirit of Dr. Seuss, wrapped in a thoroughly modern aesthetic. The text bounces along on a buoyant, scanning rhyme scheme that begs to be read aloud. The rhymes are tight, clever, and endlessly rhythmic, creating an acoustic momentum that carries the reader effortlessly from page to page. This lyrical musicality is further amplified in the audio-enhanced editions, where sound and text synchronization invite even the youngest pre-readers to engage with the rhythm of the words. 

Visually, Collins’s draughtsmanship is nothing short of brilliant. The character design relies heavily on contrasting proportions—tiny, exasperated Mouse versus enormous, serene Bear—which instantly generates visual gags before a single word is read. The minimalist backgrounds keep the focus razor-sharp on the emotional states of the characters. Mouse’s escalating fury is communicated through brilliant micro-expressions, wildly expressive eyebrows, and frantic body language, while Bear’s placid, blank stare serves as the ultimate comedic foil. 

The book culminates in a deliciously satisfying twist ending that delivers a punchline without needing a single extra syllable. It is a brilliant button on a tightly wound narrative, ensuring giggles and immediate demands for a reread. There’s A Bear On My Chair is more than just a clever bedtime story; it is a stylish, impeccably crafted picture book that proves great things—and great laughs—often come in deceptively simple packages.
