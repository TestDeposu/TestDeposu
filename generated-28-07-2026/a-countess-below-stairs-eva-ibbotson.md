# A Countess Below Stairs

**Author:** Eva Ibbotson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

Step into the enchanting, gilt-edged world of 1922 Vienna with Eva Ibbotson’s timeless historical romance, A Countess Below Stairs. Originally published under other cherished titles, this sparkling novel is a masterclass in the romantic comedy of manners, seamlessly blending aristocratic nostalgia with the vibrant, greasepaint-scented chaos of the operatic stage. 

At its heart, the novel explores the enduring themes of identity, class transgression, and the quiet courage of reinvention. The narrative follows young Austrian Princess Theresa-Maria—affectionately known as 'Putzerl' to her eccentric, impoverished aunts—who makes the radical decision to shed the suffocating weight of her royal duties. Leaving behind her crumbling ancestral castle, she adopts the unassuming moniker of Tessa and secures a humble position as an under-wardrobe mistress for the International Opera Company. 

Ibbotson uses this premise to construct a delightful Cinderella story turned on its head. Tessa’s descent from the dizzying heights of the nobility to the bustling, soot-and-velvet depths of the costume department is more than just a plot device; it is a profound journey of self-discovery. Working alongside seamstresses, divas, and musicians, Tessa finds a rich, authentic sense of community that her gilded cage could never provide. Yet, the past has a way of catching up. When the wealthy, enigmatic Englishman Guy Farne arrives in Austria to commission the company for a grand performance at his newly acquired estate, the worlds of high society and backstage labor violently—and deliciously—collide. 

What elevates A Countess Below Stairs far above the standard genre fare is Ibbotson’s signature literary style. As celebrated by critics and fellow writers like Nigella Lawson, Ibbotson’s prose is elegantly written, effortlessly witty, and acutely observant. She possesses a rare gift for breathing vivid life into the margins of her stories, populating her pages not just with charming leads, but with unforgettable supporting characters, vivid settings, and a palpable sensory atmosphere. You can smell the greasepaint, hear the soaring notes of the orchestra, and feel the frayed velvet of the opera house curtains. 

The romance between Tessa and Guy simmers with delightful tension, fueled by mistaken impressions, social barriers, and the agonizingly sweet friction of proximity. Guy, seeking to impress his sophisticated new fiancée, finds himself utterly captivated by the grace and spirit of the mysterious under-wardrobe mistress, entirely unaware of her aristocratic pedigree. Ibbotson handles this dynamic with a light, sparkling touch, ensuring the humor is as sharp as the emotional stakes are high.

Ultimately, A Countess Below Stairs is a triumphant, life-affirming fable about the music that plays when love transcends social divides. It is a comforting, brilliantly crafted escape for anyone who believes in happily-ever-afters, delivered by a master storyteller at the absolute height of her powers.
