# Finding Chika: A Little Girl, an Earthquake, and the Making of a Family

**Author:** Mitch Albom  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

Mitch Albom has long established himself as a master storyteller of the human condition, a writer uniquely gifted at finding profound philosophical truths in the most heartrending of circumstances. With his 2019 memoir, Finding Chika: A Little Girl, an Earthquake, and the Making of a Family, Albom returns to the emotional terrain that made *Tuesdays with Morrie* a global phenomenon. Yet, this time, the reflection is born not from an aging professor, but from a three-year-old Haitian girl whose life was tragically cut short, leaving an indelible imprint on everyone she touched.

At its core, the book explores the transcendent theme of unexpected love and the ultimate definition of family. Albom and his wife, Janine, operate the Have Faith Haiti orphanage in Port-au-Prince, a sanctuary established in the wake of the devastating 2010 earthquake. Into this world walks Chika, a spirited, sharp-witted toddler who enters the orphanage after losing her mother and her home in rapid succession. What begins as a refuge for an orphaned child quickly evolves into something much deeper. When Chika is diagnosed with an inoperable, terminal brain tumor, Albom and Janine make the courageous choice to bring her to the United States, embarking on a grueling, globe-spanning quest for a medical miracle that they know, in their quietest moments, may never materialize.

What makes this narrative so compelling is Albom’s signature literary style. Eschewing overly sentimental melodrama, Albom writes with a raw, disarming vulnerability. His prose is conversational, deeply reflective, and remarkably unpretentious. He balances the harrowing reality of pediatric cancer with the vibrant, joyful, and often humorous personality of Chika herself. Through a series of poignant vignettes, Albom captures the duality of grief and gratitude. He does not ask the reader to pity Chika; instead, he invites us to admire her staggering resilience, her fierce independence, and the profound wisdom she possesses despite her tender age.

The memoir also serves as an unspoken exploration of parenthood. For a couple grappling with the quiet sorrow of childlessness, Chika arrives as a seismic shift—a daughter who anchors their lives and teaches them that love is not measured by the length of time we share with someone, but by the depth of the connection forged. Albom reflects on the helplessness of being a parent who cannot protect a child from the inevitable, transforming a personal tragedy into a universal meditation on mortality, faith, and the enduring nature of affection.

Finding Chika is not an easy read, but it is an essential one. It challenges us to look beyond our own tribulations and recognize the fragile, beautiful gift of the present moment. Albom’s ability to extract light from profound darkness ensures that Chika’s story is not merely one of loss, but a celebration of a life that burned brightly and left the world permanently altered.
