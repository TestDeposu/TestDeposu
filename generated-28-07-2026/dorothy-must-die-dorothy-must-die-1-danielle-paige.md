# Dorothy Must Die (Dorothy Must Die, #1)

**Author:** Danielle Paige  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

In the midst of a tornado of twisted fairy tales, Danielle Paige's "Dorothy Must Die" stands out as a refreshingly dark and bold reimagining of the classic Oz saga. This New York Times bestselling novel is not for the faint of heart, as it plunges readers into a gritty, corrupted world where Good Witches are not what they seem, and the Wicked may be the only hope for salvation.

At the center of this maelstrom is Amy Gumm, the "other girl from Kansas," a feisty and sarcastic heroine with a penchant for pink hair and a rebellious spirit. Recruited by the Revolutionary Order of the Wicked, Amy is trained to undertake a perilous mission: to remove the Tin Woodman's heart, steal the Scarecrow's brain, take the Lion's courage, and ultimately, to kill Dorothy. Yes, that Dorothy – the same sweet, innocent girl from the classic tale, now transformed into a ruthless tyrant who has seized power and is terrorizing the land of Oz.

Paige's masterful storytelling weaves a complex web of moral ambiguity, where the lines between good and evil are constantly blurred. In this Oz, the yellow brick road is crumbling, winged monkeys are executed for rebellion, and the very fabric of the magical land is torn apart by Dorothy's tyranny. As Amy navigates this treacherous landscape, she must confront her own demons and question everything she thought she knew about the world of Oz.

One of the most striking aspects of Paige's writing style is her ability to balance dark humor with intense action sequences. Amy's witty one-liners and sarcastic observations provide a welcome respite from the otherwise intense and suspenseful plot. At the same time, Paige's vivid descriptions of the twisted Oz landscape and its inhabitants will leave readers on the edge of their seats, eager to discover what horrors or wonders lie around the next corner.

Throughout the book, Paige also explores themes of identity, power, and the corrupting influence of both. Amy's journey is not just about completing her mission, but also about discovering her own place in the world and confronting the darkness within herself. As she grapples with the moral complexities of her task, Amy must also confront the possibility that she may not be the hero she thought she was.

In short, "Dorothy Must Die" is a gripping, thought-provoking, and deliciously dark fairy tale retelling that will appeal to fans of "The Hunger Games" and "The Wizard of Oz" alike. With its strong female protagonist, morally complex themes, and pulse-pounding action sequences, this book is sure to captivate readers and leave them eagerly anticipating the next installment in the series.
