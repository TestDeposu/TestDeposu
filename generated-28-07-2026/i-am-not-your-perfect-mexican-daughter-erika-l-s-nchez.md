# I Am Not Your Perfect Mexican Daughter

**Author:** Erika L. Sánchez  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

"I Am Not Your Perfect Mexican Daughter" by Erika L. Sánchez is a raw, unapologetic exploration of identity, grief, and the complexities of family dynamics, wrapped in a gripping narrative that keeps you turning pages until the very end. This #1 New York Times bestseller is a powerful testament to the author's ability to craft authentic, relatable characters and tackle heavy themes with a deft, yet delicate touch.

At its core, this novel is about Julia, a Mexican-American teenager grappling with the aftermath of her sister Olga's sudden death. While Olga was the epitome of their mother's expectations - the 'perfect Mexican daughter' - Julia feels like a constant disappointment. As she navigates her grief and her mother's increasing criticism, Julia begins to question the narrative of her sister's life and the expectations placed upon her own.

Sánchez's prose is alive and crackling, much like the city of Chicago that serves as the novel's backdrop. She masterfully weaves together Julia's first-person perspective with flashbacks of Olga's life, creating a rich, multi-layered narrative that explores the intricacies of sisterhood, cultural identity, and the pressure to conform to societal expectations.

One of the novel's standout features is its unflinching portrayal of mental health. Julia's grief is messy and raw, often manifesting in ways that are uncomfortable and difficult to watch. Yet, Sánchez handles this topic with sensitivity and authenticity, providing a much-needed representation of mental health struggles in young adult literature.

Another strength of the novel is its exploration of cultural identity and the pressure to conform to stereotypes. Through Julia's journey, Sánchez challenges the notion of the 'perfect Mexican daughter' and explores the complexities of navigating a bicultural identity. She also shines a light on the often overlooked experiences of Mexican-American women, providing a nuanced and authentic portrayal of their struggles and triumphs.

However, the novel is not without its flaws. Some readers may find the pacing uneven, with certain sections feeling rushed while others linger too long on mundane details. Additionally, some of the secondary characters, particularly Julia's love interest Connor, feel underdeveloped and one-dimensional.

Despite these minor shortcomings, "I Am Not Your Perfect Mexican Daughter" is a compelling and thought-provoking read that will resonate with anyone who has ever felt like they don't fit the mold. Sánchez's writing is bold, honest, and unapologetic, making this novel a standout in the young adult genre.

In conclusion, "I Am Not Your Perfect Mexican Daughter" is a powerful exploration of identity, grief, and cultural expectations. With its authentic characters, gripping narrative, and unflinching portrayal of mental health, this novel is a must-read for anyone looking for a raw, emotional journey that challenges societal norms and expectations.
