# The Fan Man

**Author:** William Kotzwinkle  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

"The Fan Man" invites readers into the lily‑pad world of Bubbles, a frog whose incessant, unintentional bubbling has earned him the nickname “the fan.” From the first splash, William Kotzwinkle captures the gentle absurdity of childhood embarrassment with a voice that feels both whimsical and warmly familiar—think the earnest goofiness of *Walter the Farting Dog* blended with the earnest self‑acceptance of *I Don’t Want to Be a Frog*. Kotzwinkle’s prose is breezy and rhythmic, peppered with onomatopoeic bubbles and splashes that make the narrative almost tactile; you can practically hear the gurgling as Bubbles navigates the pond’s social currents.

At its heart, the story tackles the universal fear of being singled out for something you can’t control. Bubbles’ bubbling isn’t a mischievous prank but a biological quirk, and Kotzwinkle treats it with compassion rather than mockery. The pond’s inhabitants—some supportive, some skeptical—serve as a microcosm of any schoolyard or playground, allowing young readers to see both the sting of bullying and the healing power of empathy. When a looming threat (a hungry heron, perhaps, or a sudden drought) forces the frogs to unite, Bubbles’ perceived “flaw” becomes the very trait that saves the day, reinforcing the book’s central message: differences can be strengths when the community embraces them.

Kotzwinkle’s style shines in his ability to balance humor with heartfelt moments without tipping into saccharine territory. His dialogue snaps with the quick‑witted banter of friends who tease each other affectionately, while his descriptive passages linger just long enough to paint a vivid pond scene without slowing the pace. The result is a story that reads aloud like a joyful chant, inviting kids to join in the bubbling chorus while subtly nudging them toward kindness and self‑acceptance.

For parents and educators seeking a light‑hearted yet meaningful read to spark conversations about inclusion, *The Fan Man* delivers a splash of fun that lingers long after the last page is turned. It’s a reminder that even the most embarrassing quirks can ripple outward, turning a solitary tadpole into a hero whose bubbles lift everyone up.
