# Theft of Swords (The Riyria Revelations, #1-2)

**Author:** Michael J. Sullivan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

In "Theft of Swords," Michael J. Sullivan masterfully weaves a captivating tale of adventure, magic, and myth, set against the backdrop of a treacherous world where loyalty is a luxury and survival is a constant struggle. This epic fantasy novel, comprising the first two installments of The Riyria Revelations series, introduces readers to the unlikely duo of Royce Melborn, a cunning thief, and Hadrian Blackwood, a skilled mercenary, as they navigate the complex web of intrigue and deception that permeates their world.

One of the most striking aspects of Sullivan's writing style is his ability to balance action, humor, and drama, creating a narrative that is both engaging and unpredictable. The characters of Royce and Hadrian are expertly crafted, with distinct personalities that complement each other perfectly. Royce's cynical wit and Hadrian's idealistic nature create a compelling dynamic, as they navigate the moral gray areas of their profession and confront the harsh realities of their world.

Throughout the novel, Sullivan explores the theme of identity and the blurred lines between good and evil. Royce and Hadrian are not traditional heroes, but rather complex, multidimensional characters with their own motivations and flaws. As they become embroiled in a conspiracy that threatens the very fabric of their world, they are forced to confront their own identities and the true nature of their actions.

The world-building in "Theft of Swords" is equally impressive, with Sullivan creating a richly detailed and immersive environment that is steeped in history and mythology. The magical system is cleverly designed, with a unique blend of mysticism and science that adds depth and complexity to the narrative.

One of the most impressive aspects of Sullivan's writing is his ability to craft a compelling story that is both self-contained and part of a larger narrative. "Theft of Swords" is a complete story in its own right, with a satisfying conclusion that sets the stage for the rest of the series. At the same time, it is clear that this is just the beginning of a much larger tale, with hints of a deeper conspiracy and a world-spanning epic that will unfold in future volumes.

In conclusion, "Theft of Swords" is a gripping and immersive fantasy novel that will appeal to fans of adventure, magic, and mythology. With its complex characters, rich world-building, and engaging narrative, it is a must-read for anyone looking to escape into a richly detailed and immersive world. Sullivan's writing style is engaging, witty, and unpredictable, making this a compelling read from start to finish.
