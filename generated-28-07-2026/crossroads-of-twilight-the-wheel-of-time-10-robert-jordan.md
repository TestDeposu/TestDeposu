# Crossroads of Twilight (The Wheel of Time, #10)

**Author:** Robert Jordan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

**Crossroads of Twilight: A Visual Feast for Wheel of Time Enthusiasts**

Robert Jordan's expansive and intricate world of The Wheel of Time has long captivated readers with its rich tapestry of cultures, history, and magic. Now, fans can delve even deeper into this captivating universe with "The World of Robert Jordan's The Wheel of Time," a stunning visual companion to the epic series.

This beautifully crafted volume is a treasure trove for devoted fans, offering over eighty full-color paintings that bring Jordan's imaginative world to life. From detailed maps of the world, to portraits of beloved characters, landscapes, and artifacts of Power, each illustration is a testament to the series' depth and complexity.

The book is not just a collection of artwork, but a comprehensive guide to the Wheel of Time universe. It explores the history and legends of the world, from the rise and fall of Artur Hawking to the creation of the Ajahs in the White Tower. It even reveals previously unknown continents and the inner workings of the mysterious country, Shara.

One of the standout features is the double-page spreads of the first seven book jackets by Darrell Sweet. These iconic images have long been associated with the series, and seeing them in all their glory, without text, is a delight for fans.

Robert Jordan's style is evident throughout the book. His meticulous world-building, which has always been a hallmark of the series, is on full display. Each illustration feels like a window into a fully realized world, with its own history, cultures, and politics. The attention to detail is astonishing, and it's clear that Jordan had a vast, intricate universe in mind when he began writing the series.

The artwork, by a variety of talented artists, captures the essence of Jordan's world. From the sweeping landscapes to the intricate details of objects of Power, each piece feels true to the spirit of the series. The illustrations of the Seanchan's exotic beasts are particularly noteworthy, bringing to life creatures that have long been described but never fully visualized.

However, the book is not without its criticisms. Some fans might find the lack of text disappointing, as the artwork is not accompanied by extensive explanations. While this allows the illustrations to speak for themselves, it also means that some of the more subtle details of the world may be lost on readers who are not already familiar with the series.

Moreover, the book's focus on visuals means that it may not appeal to readers who are looking for more in-depth analysis or new information about the series. It's a companion book, not a new addition to the series, and should be approached as such.

In conclusion, "The World of Robert Jordan's The Wheel of Time" is a must-have for fans of the series. It's a beautiful, immersive experience that offers a new way to engage with Jordan's complex and captivating world. Whether you're a long-time fan or a newcomer to the series, this book is a visual feast that will deepen your appreciation for the Wheel of Time universe.
