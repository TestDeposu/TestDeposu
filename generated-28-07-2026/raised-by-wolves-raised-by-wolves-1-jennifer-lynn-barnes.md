# Raised by Wolves (Raised by Wolves, #1)

**Author:** Jennifer Lynn Barnes  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

Before crafting masterclasses in high-society deception like The Inheritance Games, Jennifer Lynn Barnes staked her claim in the YA supernatural arena with Raised by Wolves. Published in 2010, this gritty, pulse-pounding paranormal adventure introduces readers to a world where myth and modern adolescence collide, proving that Barnes has always possessed an innate talent for pacing, psychological tension, and fierce, resilient heroines. 

At the heart of the novel lies the compelling exploration of identity versus instinct. The story follows fifteen-year-old Bryn, a girl physically and emotionally scarred by tragedy, having witnessed her parents' brutal murder by a rogue wolf before being adopted by the alpha of a local werewolf pack. Bryn’s entire existence is defined by the rigid, patriarchal social hierarchy of pack life—a system built on dominance, submission, and survival. Yet, Barnes’s central theme transcends standard monster lore. It examines what it truly means to be domesticated. Bryn knows how to survive within the cage of pack rules, but her relentless curiosity and deeply ingrained need for truth force her to question the very structure that protects her. When she discovers Chase, a captive teenage boy locked in her guardian's basement, the boundaries of her world begin to fracture. Chase represents the wild unknown, a catalyst who forces Bryn to confront not only the horrific ghosts of her past but also the suffocating constraints of her present.

Barnes’s authorial style is sharp, atmospheric, and relentlessly fast-paced. She writes with an economy of language that prioritizes visceral emotion and high-stakes momentum over flowery exposition. The dialogue is snappy and laced with the authentic angst and defiance of a teenage protagonist who refuses to be sidelined. What sets Barnes apart from her contemporaries in the 2010 paranormal boom is her ability to blend complex world-building with deeply personal character arcs. The werewolf mythology here is not just a vehicle for romance; it is a rigid political landscape fraught with danger, where every social misstep carries deadly consequences. Barnes crafts a heroine who rivals television icons like Buffy Summers—Bryn is physically capable, emotionally vulnerable, stubbornly inquisitive, and entirely unwilling to wait for someone else to save her.

Raised by Wolves is a sharp, gripping gateway into a dark and intricate universe. It balances visceral action with sharp thematic questions about loyalty, freedom, and the cages we willingly inhabit. For readers who love high-stakes YA drama driven by a fierce female protagonist and a relentless plot, this reissue is an absolute must-read that delivers a fierce bite and leaves you howling for more.
