# Playing to Win: How Strategy Really Works

**Author:** A.G. Lafley  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

Strategy is not complex, but it is brutally hard. In Playing to Win: How Strategy Really Works, A.G. Lafley, the visionary former CEO of Procter & Gamble, alongside strategic luminary Roger Martin, strips away the suffocating corporate jargon that usually surrounds business planning and delivers a masterclass in pragmatic decision-making. Far from a theoretical academic treatise, this contemporary business classic is forged in the trenches of real-world corporate warfare. It is the definitive playbook behind one of the most astonishing turnarounds in modern commercial history—P&G’s decade-long triumph that doubled sales, quadrupled profits, and added over $100 billion in market value. 

At the absolute core of Lafley and Martin’s thesis is a refreshingly direct premise: strategy is about making hard choices. Most failing organizations mistake wishful thinking, grand mission statements, or endless operational efficiency for strategy. True strategy, however, is an integrated framework of five fundamental choices designed to guide every single action within an organization. 

The authors break this down into a brilliant, highly actionable cascading framework: What is our winning aspiration? Where will we play? How will we win? What capabilities must we have in place to win? And what management systems are required to support our choices? 

Throughout the book, the authors deconstruct these choices using rich, behind-the-scenes case studies of iconic consumer brands like Olay, Bounty, and Gillette. They demonstrate how P&G transformed struggling product lines into dominant market leaders not by trying to be everything to everyone, but by having the courage to say no to marginal opportunities. By ruthlessly defining target demographics, geographic regions, and product channels, and then aligning internal capabilities—such as supply chain mastery, cutting-edge R&D, and brilliant marketing—to support those choices, they turned everyday household items into unstoppable growth engines.

Lafley’s authorial style is remarkably grounded, authoritative, and deeply collaborative. Written from the dual perspectives of a battle-tested CEO who had to live with the consequences of every decision and a sharp external strategist who provided the conceptual architecture, the book bridges the often-chasm-like gap between executive theory and frontline execution. The narrative avoids overly dense economic modeling, favoring instead clear storytelling, transparent admissions of past missteps, and logical step-by-step reasoning. 

This updated edition further elevates the text with a sharp new foreword by former Harvard Business Review editor-in-chief Adi Ignatius, alongside two bonus HBR articles that contextualize the framework's enduring relevance in a rapidly shifting economic landscape.

Playing to Win is an indispensable read for entrepreneurs, managers, and executives at any level. It challenges leaders to stop hiding behind vague ambitions and start making the difficult, defining choices that separate market leaders from the rest of the pack. If you want to move beyond merely participating in your industry and actually learn how to dominate it, this book is your mandatory first step.
