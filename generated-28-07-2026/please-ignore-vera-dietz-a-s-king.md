# Please Ignore Vera Dietz

**Author:** A.S. King  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

To read A.S. King is to step onto a high-wire without a safety net. In her remarkable 2010 novel, Please Ignore Vera Dietz, King proves once again that she is a master of the messy, beautifully complicated adolescent psyche. This is not your polished, predictable coming-of-age story; it is a raw, jagged, and profoundly empathetic exploration of grief, guilt, and the heavy burden of secrets. 

At the narrative’s thumping, bruised heart is Vera Dietz, a cynical eighteen-year-old working-class girl whose life has been upended by the sudden, suspicious death of her former best friend, Charlie Kahn. Vera and Charlie's relationship is the axis upon which the entire book spins. They were two outcasts bound by unspoken affection, shared trauma, and a constellation of dangerous secrets. When Charlie dies under dark circumstances, Vera is left holding the bag of a truth that could either destroy his memory or salvage it. But the central tension of the novel is far more compelling than a standard whodunit: does Charlie even deserve to be saved?

King’s structural approach is nothing short of brilliant. The narrative unfolds through Vera’s biting, defensive first-person perspective, punctuated by the heartbreakingly direct ghosts and echoes of the recently deceased Charlie himself. Through this dual lens, King explores the book’s central theme: the fiction of reputation versus the devastating reality of private pain. Vera spends her days ignoring the whispers at school, hiding behind her dead-end job, and listening to her recovering alcoholic father preach about machinery and life. Yet, as the layers are peeled back, King reveals how deeply our past attachments define us, even when those attachments threaten to pull us under. 

The author’s style is electrifyingly distinct. King writes with a rhythmic, poetic edge that captures the chaotic cadence of teenage thought. She treats her readers with immense respect, refusing to spoon-feed morals or offer neat, bow-tied resolutions. Instead, she leans into the discomfort of ambiguity. Vera is prickly, sarcastic, and deeply flawed, making her one of the most authentic female protagonists in contemporary young adult literature. 

Please Ignore Vera Dietz is a striking achievement. It is a book that vibrates with emotional intelligence, sharply observing how teenagers construct armor to survive a world that often misunderstands them. King doesn’t just tell a story about a dead boy and the girl who loved him; she crafts an urgent anthem for anyone who has ever had to carry a secret too heavy for their years. Edgy, darkly funny, and ultimately transcendent, this novel demands to be read, absorbed, and remembered long after the final page.
