# In a Dark, Dark Wood

**Author:** Ruth Ware  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

**In a Dark, Dark Wood: A Psychological Thriller that Keeps You Guessing**

Ruth Ware, the master of suspense who brought us "The Woman in Cabin 10" and "The Lying Game," returns with another chilling tale in "In a Dark, Dark Wood." This time, Ware delves into the eerie depths of the English countryside, where a seemingly innocuous weekend getaway takes a sinister turn.

The story revolves around Leonora, a reclusive writer, who finds herself invited to a glass house deep in the woods for a hen party. As the first night descends, secrets begin to unravel, and Leonora's carefully constructed walls start to crumble. Ware's prowess lies in her ability to create an atmosphere so tense, you can almost hear the creaking floorboards and feel the cold draft seeping in.

Ware's style is reminiscent of Agatha Christie, with a modern twist. She weaves a complex web of characters, each with their own motives and secrets, leaving the reader questioning who to trust. The narrative is told from Leonora's perspective, her voice raw and vulnerable, yet laced with an undercurrent of unease that keeps you on the edge of your seat.

The main theme of "In a Dark, Dark Wood" is the dark side of human nature and the secrets we keep. Ware explores how our past can haunt us, and how a single memory can shatter our carefully constructed facades. She delves into the psychology of her characters, making them relatable yet unpredictable, blurring the lines between friend and foe.

Ware's use of the glass house as a setting is genius. It's a character in itself, its transparency reflecting the characters' vulnerabilities, while its isolation amplifies the sense of danger. The woods surrounding it add to the eerie atmosphere, their darkness hiding God knows what.

"In a Dark, Dark Wood" is a psychological thriller that keeps you guessing until the very end. Ware masterfully builds suspense, her prose sharp and engaging. It's a book that demands to be read in one sitting, preferably with all the lights on.
