# Thud! (Discworld, #34; City Watch, #7)

**Author:** Terry Pratchett  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Terry Pratchett's Thud! is a masterful blend of fantasy, mystery, and satire that delves into the complexities of prejudice, ancient feuds, and the unyielding power of hatred. As the 34th installment in the Discworld series and the 7th book in the City Watch series, Thud! is a testament to Pratchett's unwavering ability to craft compelling stories that are both entertaining and thought-provoking.

At the heart of the novel is the murder of Grag Hamcrusher, a prominent dwarf who has been fueling unrest among the citizens of Ankh-Morpork. As City Watch Commander Sam Vimes navigates the intricate web of clues and suspects, he must also contend with the simmering animosity between trolls and dwarfs, a centuries-old feud that threatens to plunge the city into chaos. Pratchett's depiction of this fragile social landscape is both poignant and unsettling, highlighting the ways in which deep-seated prejudices can be exploited and manipulated by those seeking to ignite conflict.

One of the most striking aspects of Thud! is its exploration of the complexities of fatherhood and the human condition. Through Vimes's daily ritual of reading Where's My Cow? to his young son, Pratchett offers a powerful counterpoint to the darkness and violence that pervades the novel. This tender portrayal of paternal love and devotion serves as a reminder that even in the most trying of times, there are moments of beauty and joy to be found. It is this nuanced characterization that has come to define Pratchett's writing style, as he effortlessly balances humor and pathos to create a narrative that is both captivating and emotionally resonant.

Pratchett's writing is, as always, a joy to behold. His prose is witty and engaging, filled with clever wordplay and satire that cuts to the very heart of modern society. The Discworld series has long been renowned for its clever subversions of fantasy tropes, and Thud! is no exception. Pratchett's use of humor and irony serves to underscore the gravity of the themes he is exploring, creating a narrative that is both entertaining and thought-provoking.

Throughout the novel, Pratchett's satire is biting and unflinching, targeting everything from racism and xenophobia to the dangers of unchecked nationalism. His depiction of the trolls and dwarfs, with their centuries-old feud and deeply ingrained prejudices, serves as a powerful commentary on the ways in which hatred and intolerance can be perpetuated and exploited. And yet, despite the gravity of these themes, Thud! is ultimately a hopeful novel, one that suggests that even in the darkest of times, there is always the possibility for redemption and forgiveness.

In short, Thud! is a triumph, a novel that showcases Pratchett's mastery of the fantasy genre while also offering a powerful commentary on the human condition. With its richly detailed world-building, compelling characters, and nuanced exploration of complex themes, Thud! is a must-read for fans of the Discworld series and fantasy enthusiasts alike.
