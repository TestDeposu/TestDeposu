# Things We Never Got Over (Knockemout, #1)

**Page Count:** Unknown

Book Review: Things We Never Got Over by Lucy Score

In the vast, often formulaic landscape of contemporary romance, few novels manage to capture the zeitgeist quite like Lucy Score’s blockbuster hit, Things We Never Got Over. Erupting as a viral sensation on BookTok before cementing its status as a New York Times bestseller, this hefty small-town romance delivers an intoxicating blend of emotional depth, high stakes, and blistering chemistry. Score takes the classic grumpy-meets-sunshine trope and injects it with a shot of adrenaline, crafting a narrative that is as hilarious as it is heartbreaking.

At the heart of the novel is Naomi Witt, a runaway bride who arrives in the sleepy, punch-first-ask-questions-later town of Knockemout, Virginia, under the worst possible circumstances. Abandoned by her chaotic twin sister, stranded without a penny or a car, and suddenly tasked with caring for an eleven-year-old niece she never knew existed, Naomi is thrust straight into a domestic nightmare. Yet, Score refuses to let her protagonist remain a helpless damsel. Naomi is remarkably resilient, wielding an infectious optimism and a fierce protective streak that slowly chips away at the cynicism of her new surroundings. 

Enter Knox Morgan, a local barber who embodies the quintessential brooding bad boy. Armed with a grumpy disposition, a chiseled physique, and a strict aversion to complications, Knox prefers his life just like his morning coffee: dark, bitter, and entirely left alone. He is a man who builds walls, not bridges. Naturally, the arrival of Naomi—a walking, talking natural disaster—threatens to dismantle the carefully constructed solitude he holds so dear. 

Score’s greatest triumph in this novel lies in her thematic exploration of trauma, healing, and found family. Beneath the snappy banter and the undeniable romantic tension, Things We Never Got Over grapples with the heavy baggage of the past. Both Naomi and Knox are carrying invisible scars—unresolved familial betrayals, expectations that stifle authenticity, and the fear of truly being seen. The author uses the eccentric backdrop of Knockemout not merely as a quaint setting, but as a crucible where these broken characters are forced to confront what they have been running from. The romance works so effectively because it is hard-won; it grows out of shared vulnerabilities and mutual rescue rather than mere physical attraction.

From a stylistic standpoint, Score writes with an effortless, binge-able cadence. Her prose is sharp, laced with self-aware humor and snappy dialogue that keeps the pages turning at a breakneck speed. While the book leans heavily into romance tropes—including a generous helping of spice that has fueled its online infamy—it never loses sight of its emotional core. Score balances the laugh-out-loud comedy with poignant moments of introspection, ensuring that the characters feel grounded, flawed, and deeply human. 

Things We Never Got Over is a testament to the power of resilience and the messy, beautiful reality of second chances. It reminds readers that sometimes the things we never got over are precisely what lead us to where we are supposed to be. For fans of slow-burn tension, protective alpha heroes with hidden hearts of gold, and found family tropes that squeeze the heart, Score’s viral phenomenon is an absolute must-read.
