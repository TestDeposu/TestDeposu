# Last Chance to See

**Author:** Douglas Adams  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1991

**Last Chance to See: A Hilarious and Heartrending Journey Through Earth's Wildlife Galaxy**

In "Last Chance to See," the inimitable Douglas Adams, creator of "The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy," teams up with zoologist Mark Carwardine for a globe-trotting adventure that is as entertaining as it is alarming. Their mission? To seek out and encounter some of the world's most exotic and endangered creatures before they disappear forever.

Adams, known for his witty and irreverent humor, brings his unique perspective to the sobering topic of species extinction. He doesn't shy away from the harsh realities, but instead uses his comedic prowess to shine a spotlight on the plight of these remarkable animals. From the deadly Komodo dragon to the lovable but hapless Kakapo parrot, each creature is brought to life with vivid, often hilarious descriptions that make you feel like you're right there with them.

But "Last Chance to See" is more than just a funny travelogue. It's a wake-up call, a stark reminder of the consequences of human carelessness. Adams and Carwardine don't just show us these incredible animals; they tell us their stories, explain why they're disappearing, and introduce us to the dedicated people working tirelessly to save them. It's a powerful combination of humor, information, and emotion that makes the book impossible to put down.

Adams' style is as engaging as ever, his words painting pictures so vivid you can almost hear the squawks and roars of the animals, feel the heat of the Indonesian sun, and taste the dust of the African savannah. Yet, beneath the laughter, there's a profound sadness, a sense of loss for the world we're losing, and a call to action.

"Last Chance to See" is more than just a book; it's a call to arms, a plea for us to open our eyes, our hearts, and our wallets to the plight of our planet's most vulnerable inhabitants. It's a reminder that we're not just visitors to this world, but custodians, and it's high time we started acting like it.
