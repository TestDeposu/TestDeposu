# P.S. I Still Love You (To All the Boys I've Loved Before, #2)

**Author:** Jenny Han  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

P.S. I Still Love You, the second installment in Jenny Han's To All the Boys I've Loved Before series, is a captivating and charming novel that delves into the complexities of love, identity, and relationships. The story picks up where the first book left off, with Lara Jean Song navigating the aftermath of her secret love letters being mailed to all the boys she has ever had a crush on.

Jenny Han's writing style is as enchanting as ever, weaving a narrative that is both lighthearted and profound. Her use of language is effortless, making it easy to become fully immersed in Lara Jean's world. The characters are well-developed and relatable, with Lara Jean being a particularly strong protagonist. Her vulnerabilities, insecurities, and passions make her a character that readers can't help but root for.

The main theme of the book explores the idea of love and relationships in all their forms. Lara Jean finds herself torn between her feelings for two different boys, Peter and John Ambrose, each representing a different aspect of her personality and desires. As she navigates these complex emotions, she must also confront her own sense of identity and what she wants from life. Han handles these themes with sensitivity and nuance, creating a narrative that is both authentic and engaging.

One of the strengths of the book is its ability to balance humor and heart. Han's writing is infused with a witty humor that makes the characters' interactions feel natural and enjoyable. At the same time, the novel tackles serious issues such as family, friendship, and first love, making it a relatable and impactful read.

The supporting characters in the book are equally well-crafted, adding depth and richness to the story. Lara Jean's family, in particular, is a highlight of the novel, with her father and sisters providing a loving and supportive backdrop to her journey. The character of Peter Kavinsky is also well-developed, with his complexities and flaws making him a more believable and endearing love interest.

Overall, P.S. I Still Love You is a delightful and engaging read that will appeal to fans of romance, comedy, and coming-of-age stories. Jenny Han's writing is captivating, and her characters are relatable and well-developed. The novel's exploration of love, identity, and relationships makes it a must-read for anyone looking for a story that will make them laugh, cry, and reflect on their own experiences.
