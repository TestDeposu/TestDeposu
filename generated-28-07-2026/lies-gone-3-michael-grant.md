# Lies (Gone, #3)

**Author:** Michael Grant  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Lies, the third installment in Michael Grant's bestselling Gone series, plunges readers back into the dark and twisted world of the FAYZ, a mysterious quarantine zone where children under the age of 15 are trapped, and supernatural powers are on the rise. As the stakes grow higher, the lines between good and evil are constantly blurred, forcing the characters to confront the ultimate question: when chaos rules, which side will you choose?

Grant's writing style is a masterclass in suspense and tension, weaving a complex narrative that's both breathtaking and terrifying. With each turn of the page, the reader is thrust into a world of moral dilemmas, where the characters are forced to make impossible choices that will haunt them forever. The author's ability to craft relatable and multidimensional characters is a testament to his skill as a storyteller, making it impossible not to become invested in their struggles and root for their survival.

One of the most striking aspects of the Gone series is its thought-provoking exploration of human nature. In the absence of adult supervision, the children of Perdido Beach are left to their own devices, and the results are both fascinating and disturbing. Grant raises important questions about the effects of trauma, the dangers of unchecked power, and the true meaning of leadership, all while maintaining a breakneck pace that will keep readers on the edge of their seats.

The world-building in Lies is equally impressive, with Grant expanding on the mythology of the FAYZ and introducing new characters with extraordinary abilities. The supernatural elements are expertly woven into the narrative, adding an extra layer of tension and complexity to the story. As the characters navigate this treacherous new world, they must confront the ultimate question: what does it mean to be human?

Lies is a gripping and unsettling read that will appeal to fans of The Hunger Games, Divergent, and The Maze Runner. Grant's writing is visceral and unflinching, tackling tough subjects with unapologetic honesty. This is not a series for the faint of heart, but for those who dare to confront the darkness within, the rewards are well worth it.

In short, Lies is a must-read for anyone who enjoys dystopian fiction, complex characters, and moral dilemmas. With its breakneck pace, thought-provoking themes, and supernatural twists, this book will keep you up all night, wondering what's next for the children of Perdido Beach.
