# Sold

**Author:** Patricia McCormick  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

To read Patricia McCormick’s *Sold* is to step into a nightmare that is happening right now, in our world, to thousands of children. Published in 2010, this National Book Award finalist is not an easy book to digest, but it is an essential one. Through the rhythmic, unflinching power of verse, McCormick tells the story of Lakshmi, a thirteen-year-old girl from a remote mountain village in Nepal whose life is upended by poverty, monsoon rains, and the devastating betrayal of a stepfather who sells her into the horrors of a sex-trafficking ring in India.

What makes *Sold* a masterclass in contemporary young adult literature is not merely the gravity of its subject matter, but the exquisite restraint of the author’s style. McCormick chooses to write in spare, evocative vignettes—short, poetic bursts that capture the sensory details of Lakshmi’s existence. By stripping away excess narrative, the verse amplifies the emotional resonance of every single word. The reader feels the warmth of the oil lamp as Lakshmi’s mother brushes her hair, the bitter cold of the Himalayan winds, and eventually, the suffocating claustrophobia and terror of "Happiness House," the brothel where Lakshmi is imprisoned under the cruel thumb of a woman named Mumtaz.

The central theme of the novel is survival against insurmountable odds, anchored by a poignant maternal mantra: "Simply to endure is to triumph." McCormick navigates the harrowing reality of human trafficking without ever resorting to gratuitous exploitation. Instead, she focuses the lens sharply on the interior life, resilience, and humanity of her protagonist. Even within the bleakest confines of the brothel, the narrative blooms with moments of profound grace, forged through the fragile, life-saving friendships Lakshmi builds with the other captive girls. 

McCormick balances the harrowing darkness of the environment with the fierce, unbreakable light of the human spirit. The prose is clean, poetic, and devastatingly effective, making complex and dark realities accessible without diluting their urgency. *Sold* is a testament to the power of storytelling to give voice to the voiceless. It is a stunning, unforgettable, and deeply striking achievement that demands to be read, remembered, and acted upon.
