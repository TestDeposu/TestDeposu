# Finders Keepers (Bill Hodges Trilogy, #2)

**Author:** Stephen King  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

Stephen King’s 2015 thriller *Finders Keepers*, the second installment in the acclaimed Bill Hodges trilogy following *Mr. Mercedes*, is a taut, electrifying exploration of the dangerous, intoxicating power of art. While the first novel leaned heavily into the classic cat-and-mouse dynamic of detective fiction blended with a cyber-stalker menace, *Finders Keepers* pivots toward a different kind of darkness. It investigates the sacred, sometimes terrifying bond between a creator and an audience—a thematic territory King famously mapped out in his 1987 masterpiece, *Misery*. 

The narrative opens with a brutal crime of passion driven by literary betrayal. Morris Bellamy, a deranged, obsessive fan, murders his reclusive idol, the legendary author John Rothstein. Rothstein’s sin in Morris's eyes is not just halting publication, but allowing his iconic protagonist, Jimmy Gold, to sell out and embrace the commercialism of middle-class America. While the stolen cash from Rothstein’s safe is a handsome bonus, Morris’s true prize is a trunk full of unpublished notebooks, containing at least one more manuscript featuring the legendary Jimmy Gold. Before Morris can devour this holy grail of fiction, fate intervenes, and he is imprisoned for an unrelated, decades-long crime. 

Thirty-five years later, the trunk is discovered by a desperate teenager named Pete Saubers, whose family was financially shattered in the very Mercedes-massacre that launched this trilogy. Pete uses the stolen cash to save his family from ruin, while hoarding the priceless manuscripts like a dragon guarding gold. But when a now-paroled Morris reclaims his freedom, a terrifying collision course is set. Standing between the homicidal bibliophile and the innocent teen is the aging, relentless retired detective Bill Hodges, alongside his brilliant, unorthodox associates Holly Gibney and Jerome Robinson.

What makes *Finders Keepers* so striking is King’s astute dissection of reading as a transformative, all-consuming act. Morris Bellamy is not merely a common thief; he is the dark mirror of every passionate reader who has ever felt personally betrayed by an author's narrative choices. King paints Morris with a unsettling psychological realism. He is articulate, passionate about literature, and utterly unhinged—a man who believes that the fictional world created by Rothstein belongs more to the reader than to the writer who birthed it. This elevates the thriller from a standard procedural into a profound meditation on ownership, inspiration, and the psychological weight of words.

King’s signature style shines through its relentless pacing and intimate character development. He alternates effortlessly between the claustrophobic, feverish desperation of Morris and the warm, grounded humanity of Bill Hodges’ makeshift detective agency. Hodges, Holly, and Jerome provide a vital emotional anchor against the creeping dread of Morris’s vengeance. Holly Gibney, in particular, continues her trajectory as one of King’s most compelling creations—neurotic, hyper-intelligent, and fiercely loyal. 

The author’s prose is as sharp and conversational as ever, pulling the reader relentlessly forward through short chapters and escalating tension. King seamlessly weaves social commentary about the post-2008 economic downturn into the plot via the struggling Saubers family, grounding the high-stakes thriller in relatable domestic realism. 

Ultimately, *Finders Keepers* succeeds because it taps into a universal truth known to anyone who has ever loved a book: stories matter. They can heal, they can save, and, in the hands of the wrong people, they can kill. King has crafted a masterclass in suspense that honors the obsessive devotion of the written word while delivering the high-octane thrills his readers crave.
