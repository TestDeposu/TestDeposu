# Inside Out (Insider, #1)

**Author:** Maria V. Snyder  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2026

Inside Out, the debut novel in the Insider series by Maria V. Snyder, is a gripping and thought-provoking dystopian tale that delves into the themes of oppression, rebellion, and self-discovery. Set in a world where the haves and have-nots are starkly divided, the story follows Trella, a young "scrub" who toils in the lower levels of Inside, keeping the upper echelons spotless and pristine.

From the outset, Snyder's masterful world-building draws the reader into the bleak and claustrophobic environment of Inside, where the air is thick with tension and the divide between the upper and lower levels is palpable. The author's vivid descriptions of the labyrinthine pipes, cramped living quarters, and endless drudgery of the scrubs' existence are both captivating and unsettling, making it easy to become fully immersed in Trella's world.

Trella herself is a compelling and relatable protagonist, whose initial desire to keep a low profile and avoid the ever-watchful Pop Cops slowly gives way to a growing sense of discontent and, ultimately, defiance. As she navigates the treacherous landscape of Inside, Trella's voice rings out with a perfect blend of sarcasm, humor, and vulnerability, making her an endearing and rootable heroine.

One of the most striking aspects of Inside Out is its exploration of the human condition, particularly in the face of oppression. Snyder skillfully weaves together themes of trust, loyalty, and the dangers of blind obedience, raising important questions about the consequences of a society that values conformity above all else. Through Trella's journey, the author shows how even the smallest acts of resistance can have far-reaching consequences, and how the power of individual agency can spark a revolution.

Snyder's writing style is engaging and accessible, with a keen sense of pacing that propels the reader through the twists and turns of the narrative. Her characters are multidimensional and complex, with rich backstories that add depth and nuance to the story. The author's use of language is evocative and concise, conjuring vivid images of the world of Inside and its inhabitants.

If there's one criticism to be made, it's that the supporting cast, while well-developed, occasionally feels slightly overshadowed by Trella's dominant narrative voice. However, this is a minor quibble in an otherwise gripping and immersive read.

Overall, Inside Out is a riveting and thought-provoking novel that will appeal to fans of dystopian fiction, particularly those who enjoy strong female protagonists and stories of rebellion and resistance. With its vivid world-building, compelling characters, and timely themes, this book is sure to resonate with readers long after the final page has been turned.
