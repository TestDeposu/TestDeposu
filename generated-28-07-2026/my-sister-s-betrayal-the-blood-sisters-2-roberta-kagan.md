# My Sister's Betrayal (The Blood Sisters, #2)

**Author:** Roberta Kagan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

Title: Fractured Bonds in the Shadow of the Swastika: A Review of Roberta Kagan's My Sister’s Betrayal

In historical fiction, the Second World War has been examined through countless lenses, yet few authors manage to dissect the anatomy of survival with the raw intimacy found in Roberta Kagan’s work. With My Sister’s Betrayal, the second harrowing installment in The Blood Sisters series, Kagan returns to the darkest chapters of the twentieth century to ask an agonizing question: when tyranny reduces humanity to ash, what happens when those closest to us strike the match?

Building upon the emotional architecture of the first book, this 2025 release tightens the noose of Nazi oppression around four women whose bond was once thought unbreakable. Anna, Bernie, Elica, and Dagna are no longer merely enduring the war; they are being actively fractured by it, propelled into divergent, high-stakes trajectories where every decision carries the weight of life and death. 

Kagan’s greatest strength lies in her mastery of character-driven tension. She avoids the trap of portraying her protagonists as flat symbols of wartime resilience. Instead, they are brilliantly, messily human. Elica’s desperate infiltration of the Gestapo headquarters to find her husband, Daniel, is a masterclass in heart-stopping suspense. It is an act driven by a fierce, blinding love that borders on self-destruction. Similarly, Bernie’s perilous journey to rescue Elica’s infant, Theo, tests the boundaries of ordinary courage, turning a simple rescue mission into an odyssey of nerve-shredding peril.

Yet, it is the moral complexity of the supporting cast and antagonists that elevates the narrative. The return of Ulf—Anna’s former crush turned Gestapo-adjacent authority figure—introduces a chillingly compelling dynamic. Consumed by an illicit obsession with Anna, Ulf embodies the moral rot of the regime. He is a man teetering on a razor’s edge, where his dark desires clash against his indoctrinated loyalty to the Führer. Through Ulf, Kagan explores a terrifying psychological landscape: the notion that a monster’s fixation might paradoxically become a captive’s only lifeline.

Perhaps the most striking and tragic arc belongs to Dagna. While her sisters fight for love, family, and innocence, Dagna hungers for power. In a world stripped of agency, she seeks to seize it by any means necessary, willingly paying the ultimate toll of betrayal to climb the Nazi ranks. Dagna is not merely a villain; she is a cautionary tale of how absolute corruption can seduce the desperate, turning sister against sister in the ultimate act of self-preservation.

Kagan’s writing style is unapologetically visceral and emotionally immersive. She does not shy away from the brutal realities of the Holocaust and the omnipresent paranoia of the Third Reich. Her prose is swift, cinematic, and dripping with atmosphere, ensuring that readers cannot simply observe the narrative—they are forced to inhabit it. The pacing is relentless, moving seamlessly between the claustrophobic corridors of Nazi headquarters, the bitter cold of escape routes, and the suffocating tension of forced compromises.

My Sister’s Betrayal is more than a wartime thriller; it is a profound exploration of sacrifice, the corrupting nature of power, and the devastating cost of survival. Kagan reminds us that while bombs and bullets destroy cities, it is betrayal from within that truly shatters the soul. For fans of gripping World War II historical fiction who crave deep emotional resonance, complex female protagonists, and relentless suspense, this novel is an absolute must-read.
