# Picking Daisies on Sundays (Picking Daisies on Sundays, #1)

**Author:** Liana Cincotti  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

If you have ever memorized the iconic monologue from 10 Things I Hate About You, spent your Friday nights wrapped in a fleece blanket rewatching To All the Boys I’ve Loved Before, or believed in your bones that love is a grand, cinematic gesture, Liana Cincotti has written your next literary obsession. Picking Daisies on Sundays, the sparkling first installment in a promising new contemporary romance series, takes two of the genre’s most delicious tropes—friends-to-enemies-to-lovers and the absolute classic fake-dating setup—and infuses them with a sharp, modern vulnerability that elevates the novel far above standard genre fare.

At the beating heart of Cincotti’s narrative is Daniella Daisy Maria, a self-proclaimed hopeless romantic navigating the terrifying precipice of finishing college. Daniella is a character built out of warmth, slightly bruised nostalgia, and an unshakeable devotion to the rom-com blueprint. Her internal monologue is delightfully witty, laced with pop-culture observations and the breathless anxiety of a young woman whose heart is entirely too big for her own safety. When a chance encounter in a sweaty, dimly lit West Village bar throws her back into the orbit of Levi—her childhood best friend, first love, and the boy who shattered her four years prior—Cincotti captures the exact, breath-catching panic of seeing a ghost who still has the keys to your soul.

The stylistic brilliance of Picking Daisies on Sundays lies in Cincotti’s pacing and her ear for dialogue. The author does not rush the reconciliation; instead, she weaponizes the fake-dating trope to create delicious, slow-burn psychological torture for both her protagonists and her readers. When Levi asks Daniella to play the role of his devoted girlfriend for his sister’s wedding, it is a setup dripping with manufactured domesticity. Every staged hand-hold, every lingering glance across a crowded room, and every late-night whispered conversation forces Daniella to confront the ghost of a rejection she has spent years trying to bury. Cincotti writes these forced-proximity scenes with a palpable, tactile tension. You can practically smell the wedding spray roses and feel the static electricity crackling between characters who know each other’s secrets, yet are currently strangers navigating new emotional territory.

Beyond the romantic friction, Cincotti anchors her story in the very real, paralyzing anxieties of early adulthood. As Daniella waits on pins and needles for the verdict from her dream grad school, the existential dread of the future casts a long shadow over her romantic entanglement. The weight of potential academic rejection mirrors her romantic fears: what if she tries her absolute hardest, gives everything she has, and still ends up unworthy? This thematic parallel gives Picking Daisies on Sundays a surprising emotional depth. It is not just about getting the boy; it is about Daniella learning how to stand firmly in her own confidence, shed the lingering insecurities of her college years, and risk her heart a second time without the safety net of denial.

Cincotti’s prose is breezy, deeply visual, and effortlessly engaging, making it nearly impossible to put down once the setup takes flight. She balances laugh-out-loud banter with quiet, chest-aching moments of emotional realization. Fans of contemporary romance who crave emotional stakes, undeniable chemistry, and a heroine who feels like a mirror to their own romantic daydreams will find an absolute treat in these pages. Picking Daisies on Sundays is a triumphant, swoon-worthy debut that proves the best kind of love stories are the ones where you have to be brave enough to pick the daisies yourself.
