# The City of Brass (The Daevabad Trilogy, #1)

**Author:** S.A. Chakraborty  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

The City of Brass, S.A. Chakraborty's debut novel, is a masterful blend of Middle Eastern mythology, politics, and fantasy that will captivate readers from the very first page. Set in 18th century Cairo, the story follows Nahri, a young con artist with a mysterious past and an extraordinary gift for healing. When Nahri accidentally summons a powerful djinn warrior, Dara, she is thrust into a world of magic and politics that she never knew existed.

Chakraborty's writing is rich and immersive, drawing heavily from Middle Eastern mythology and folklore. The world-building is meticulous and detailed, with a focus on the intricacies of djinn politics and the complex web of alliances and rivalries between the different tribes. The author's use of descriptive language is stunning, transporting readers to the vibrant streets of Cairo and the majestic city of Daevabad.

At its core, The City of Brass is a story about identity, power, and the complexities of politics. Nahri, a strong-willed and determined protagonist, must navigate the treacherous world of Daevabad, where old resentments and rivalries simmer just below the surface. As she becomes embroiled in the intrigue of the royal court, Nahri must confront the harsh realities of power and the true cost of her own ambition.

One of the most striking aspects of Chakraborty's writing is her ability to craft complex, multi-dimensional characters. Nahri is a brilliant con artist, but she is also vulnerable and fiercely independent. Dara, the djinn warrior, is a brooding and enigmatic figure, driven by a deep sense of loyalty and duty. And Alizayd, the idealistic prince, is a charismatic and passionate leader, determined to bring about change in a world that seems determined to resist it.

The City of Brass is also a story about the complexities of cultural identity and the tensions between tradition and modernity. Nahri, a young woman from a marginalized community, must navigate the complexities of her own identity and the expectations placed upon her by society. As she becomes embroiled in the world of Daevabad, Nahri must confront the harsh realities of prejudice and racism, and the ways in which power is wielded and maintained.

Overall, The City of Brass is a stunning debut novel that will appeal to fans of fantasy, politics, and adventure. Chakraborty's writing is masterful, her world-building is meticulous, and her characters are complex and multi-dimensional. This is a story that will keep you on the edge of your seat, eager to find out what happens next.
