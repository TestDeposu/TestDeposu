# Princess: A True Story of Life Behind the Veil in Saudi Arabia

**Author:** Jean Sasson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

**Preview/Critique: "Princess: A True Story of Life Behind the Veil in Saudi Arabia" by Jean Sasson**

In the heart of the Middle East, shrouded in mystery and tradition, lies Saudi Arabia, a kingdom where the lives of its women remain largely veiled from the world's gaze. Jean Sasson's "Princess: A True Story of Life Behind the Veil in Saudi Arabia" (2011) is a compelling memoir that lifts this veil, offering a rare and intimate glimpse into the lives of Saudi women, as told through the eyes of Princess Sultana al-Saud.

Sasson, an American journalist, spent years befriending Princess Sultana, who, despite her royal status, was not immune to the strict patriarchal rules governing Saudi women. The book is a testament to their friendship and a courageous act of defiance, as Princess Sultana shares her story, risking her safety and the disapproval of her family and society.

The narrative is a stark portrayal of life under the Saudi Arabian regime, where women are subject to oppressive laws and traditions. From the arranged marriages and forced veiling to the lack of basic rights and freedoms, Sasson paints a vivid picture of a world where women are often treated as second-class citizens. Yet, it is not just a story of oppression; it is also a tale of resilience, courage, and sisterhood.

Sasson's style is engaging and accessible, making complex cultural and political issues understandable to a Western audience. She masterfully weaves Princess Sultana's personal story with broader societal issues, providing a nuanced understanding of the challenges faced by Saudi women. The author's journalistic background is evident in her thorough research and commitment to truth-telling, yet she never loses sight of the human story at the heart of the book.

"Princess" is not just a book; it is a call to action, a plea for understanding, and a testament to the power of women's voices. It is a must-read for anyone interested in Middle Eastern culture, women's rights, or the human condition. Sasson's work is a powerful reminder that behind every veil, there is a story waiting to be told.
