# Waterfall (River of Time, #1)

**Author:** Lisa Tawn Bergren  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

Time travel has always been the ultimate narrative playground, offering authors the infinite canvas of history mixed with speculative wonder. In her novel Waterfall, part of the River of Time series, Lisa Tawn Bergren steps boldly into this arena, crafting a high-stakes adventure that blends historical romance with the breathless urgency of young adult fantasy. Though the provided text highlights a re-emergence within the franchise under the compelling framing of the Vestige Society and protagonist Lily Destin, Bergren’s signature storytelling DNA remains unmistakable: she specializes in placing modern sensibilities directly into the turbulent, atmospheric currents of the past.

At the core of Waterfall lies the profound theme of identity versus legacy. Lily Destin is not merely a traveler bouncing through centuries for the thrill of it; she is burdened by the heavy armor of familial expectation. As she attempts to prove herself a worthy agent—navigating the looming threat of a memory wipe and expulsion—the narrative explores how young adults struggle to break free from the shadows of their parents. The time-travel element acts as a brilliant metaphor for this internal conflict. By physically removing Lily from her own era and dropping her into an alternate seventeenth-century reality, Bergren forces her protagonist to strip away the superficial pressures of modern-day social hierarchies, high school rivalries, and institutional demands. In the past, stripped of her familiar safety nets, Lily must confront her deepest self-doubts and decide whether she is merely a pawn for the Vestige Society or the author of her own destiny. 

Bergren’s authorial style is defined by its kinetic energy and emotional immediacy. She writes with a cinematic flair that makes the transition between historical detail and fast-paced action feel seamless. One of the greatest challenges of writing time-travel romance is balancing the world-building weight of a historical setting with the contemporary voice of the protagonist. Bergren handles this delicate tightrope walk with commendable grace. Her prose is clean, accessible, and sharply paced, designed to pull readers relentlessly through the chapters. Yet, she never sacrifices emotional resonance for plot momentum. The danger feels palpable, the stakes feel terminal, and the romantic tension—heightened by a rival agent playing a dangerous game of seduction—is layered with genuine vulnerability. 

Furthermore, Bergren understands her demographic intimately. She weaves in just enough modern wit and situational humor to anchor the narrative, ensuring that the heavy themes of duty, deception, and survival are balanced with relatable teenage snark and charm. The inclusion of modern-day bullying contrasted against the starker, cruder dangers of a seventeenth-century alternate world creates a jarring, effective juxtaposition. It reminds the reader that cruelty and the quest for power are timeless human afflictions, transcending whatever century one happens to inhabit.

Waterfall stands as an inviting portal for fans of clean YA fantasy romance who crave a narrative with intellectual and emotional teeth. It speaks directly to readers who love the atmospheric romance of Cassandra Clare’s historical fantasies or the deft time-travel mechanics of Kerstin Gier. Bergren proves that history is not a rigid museum exhibit, but a living, breathing stage where personal transformation can occur. For anyone willing to take the leap into the river of time alongside Lily Destin, the journey promises not only danger and romance, but a hard-earned triumph of self-discovery.
