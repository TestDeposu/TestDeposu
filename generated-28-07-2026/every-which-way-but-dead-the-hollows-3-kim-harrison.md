# Every Which Way But Dead (The Hollows #3)

**Author:** Kim Harrison  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Every Which Way But Dead, the third installment in Kim Harrison's The Hollows series, is a captivating and action-packed ride that delves deeper into the life of witch and former bounty hunter Rachel Morgan. With its richly detailed world-building and complex characters, this novel is a must-read for fans of urban fantasy.

At its core, Every Which Way But Dead is a story about the consequences of one's actions. Rachel, having made a desperate bargain with the archdemon Algaliarept to put a vampire master behind bars, must now face the repercussions of her decision. As she navigates the treacherous landscape of Cincinnati's underworld, she must also contend with the possibility of losing her soul to the ever-after.

Harrison's writing style is one of the standout features of this novel. Her use of vivid descriptions and witty dialogue brings the world of The Hollows to life, making it easy to become fully immersed in the story. The characters are multidimensional and relatable, with Rachel being a particularly strong protagonist. Her struggles with her own morality and the consequences of her actions make her a compelling and sympathetic character.

One of the most impressive aspects of Every Which Way But Dead is its ability to balance action, suspense, and humor. Harrison seamlessly weaves together intense fight scenes, heart-pumping suspense, and laugh-out-loud moments, creating a narrative that is both thrilling and entertaining.

The world-building in this novel is also noteworthy. Harrison's vision of a world where supernatural creatures and humans coexist is richly detailed and immersive. The politics and social structures of the vampire and werewolf communities are particularly well-developed, adding depth and complexity to the story.

The themes of loyalty, friendship, and redemption are also prominent in this novel. Rachel's relationships with her roommate Ivy and her boyfriend Nick are particularly well-explored, and the tensions that arise between them are palpable. The character of Kisten, the seductive vampire, adds an extra layer of complexity to the story, and his interactions with Rachel are both captivating and unsettling.

In conclusion, Every Which Way But Dead is a gripping and engaging read that will appeal to fans of urban fantasy and supernatural fiction. With its richly detailed world-building, complex characters, and suspenseful plot, this novel is a must-read for anyone looking for a compelling and entertaining story.
