# Financial Feminist: Overcome the Patriarchy's Bullsh*t to Master Your Money and Build a Life You Love

**Author:** Tori Dunlap  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

In "Financial Feminist: Overcome the Patriarchy's Bullsh*t to Master Your Money and Build a Life You Love", Tori Dunlap delivers a searing critique of the patriarchal systems that have long hindered women's financial empowerment. With unflinching candor and a healthy dose of humor, Dunlap exposes the deeply ingrained biases that have led to a staggering wealth gap between men and women. This book is not just a personal finance guide; it's a battle cry for women to reclaim their financial autonomy and forge a new path to economic freedom.

Dunlap's writing style is engaging, accessible, and decidedly unapologetic. She weaves together personal anecdotes, expert insights, and hard-hitting data to create a narrative that is both deeply relatable and profoundly informative. The author's commitment to inclusivity and social justice shines through on every page, as she tackles topics such as the racial wealth gap, predatory credit card companies, and the importance of voting with your dollars.

One of the most significant strengths of "Financial Feminist" is its ability to balance practical advice with broader social commentary. Dunlap offers actionable tips on everything from budgeting and investing to salary negotiation and debt management, all while situating these practices within a larger context of systemic inequality. The result is a book that is both a useful guide and a call to action, urging readers to think critically about the ways in which money intersects with power, privilege, and social justice.

Throughout the book, Dunlap draws on her own experiences as a woman who has navigated the complexities of personal finance and emerged as a successful entrepreneur and educator. Her story is inspiring, but it's also deeply nuanced, acknowledging the ways in which privilege and luck have contributed to her success. This willingness to confront her own biases and limitations makes Dunlap a trustworthy and empathetic guide, and her writing is infused with a sense of solidarity and shared purpose.

If there's a criticism to be made, it's that the book sometimes feels a bit disjointed, leaping between different topics and themes without always providing a clear through-line. However, this is a minor quibble, and the book's overall impact is undiminished by this slight lack of cohesion.

Ultimately, "Financial Feminist" is a book that will resonate deeply with anyone who has ever felt marginalized, excluded, or frustrated by the patriarchal systems that govern our financial lives. It's a book that will make you angry, but it will also make you hopeful – hopeful that a different future is possible, one in which women have the economic power and autonomy to create the lives they deserve. As Dunlap so eloquently puts it, "Money doesn't have to mean restriction; it can mean choice." This book is a powerful tool for anyone seeking to exercise that choice and build a more just, equitable world.
