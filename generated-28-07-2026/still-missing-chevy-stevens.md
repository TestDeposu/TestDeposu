# Still Missing

**Author:** Chevy Stevens  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

In the gripping and visceral debut novel "Still Missing" by Chevy Stevens, the author masterfully weaves together a tale of abduction, captivity, and the long, arduous journey towards healing and recovery. Through the eyes of Annie O'Sullivan, a thirty-two-year-old realtor, Stevens delves into the darkest recesses of the human psyche, laying bare the horrors of being held captive by a psychopath and the struggle to rebuild a shattered life.

The narrative is presented in a non-linear fashion, alternating between Annie's sessions with her psychiatrist and the events that unfold after her escape. This clever narrative device allows Stevens to slowly unravel the mystery of Annie's captivity, while simultaneously exploring the complexities of her recovery. The author's use of this dual narrative structure not only adds depth to the story but also serves to underscore the theme of the novel: the truth, though painful, is a necessary step towards healing.

Stevens's writing style is both brutal and beautiful, with a keen eye for detail that makes the reader feel like they are experiencing Annie's ordeal firsthand. The author's use of vivid imagery and unflinching descriptions of Annie's captivity is not for the faint of heart, but it is a testament to Stevens's skill as a storyteller that she is able to convey the horrors of Annie's experience without resorting to gratuitous violence or exploitation.

One of the most striking aspects of "Still Missing" is its thoughtful exploration of the psychological aftermath of trauma. Stevens is unflinching in her portrayal of Annie's struggles, from her feelings of guilt and shame to her struggles with anxiety and PTSD. The author's nuanced understanding of the human psyche is evident in her depiction of Annie's complex emotions, and her refusal to offer easy answers or tidy resolutions.

The supporting cast of characters is equally well-drawn, from Annie's ever-patient boyfriend to her psychiatrist, who serves as a steady source of support and guidance. The police investigation into Annie's captivity is also well-handled, with Stevens avoiding the usual cliches and instead opting for a more realistic portrayal of the challenges and frustrations of investigating a crime.

In short, "Still Missing" is a gripping and thought-provoking debut novel that will appeal to fans of psychological thrillers and literary fiction alike. Stevens's masterful storytelling and nuanced characterization make for a compelling read, and her thoughtful exploration of the psychological aftermath of trauma is both timely and necessary.
