# Death at a Highland Wedding (A Rip Through Time, #4)

**Author:** Kelley Armstrong  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

Title: Murder Amid the Mist: Kelley Armstrong’s Rip Through Time Returns with Victorian Drama and Highland Danger

When Kelley Armstrong introduced readers to Mallory Atkinson—a modern-day homicide detective whose consciousness was violently thrust 150 years into the past—the genre of historical mystery gained a sharp, refreshing edge. Now in the fourth installment of the gripping Rip Through Time series, Death at a Highland Wedding, Armstrong transports her beloved trio far from the grimy cobblestones of Edinburgh and straight into the dramatic, mist-shrouded peaks of the Scottish Highlands. The result is a delightfully atmospheric, tightly plotted locked-room-style mystery that proves this time-travel procedural is only growing stronger with age.

The core theme of the series has always been the friction between eras, the push and pull of modern forensic acumen crashing against Victorian sensibilities and rigid social strata. In this novel, Armstrong brilliantly weaponizes that theme through setting. Mallory, now seamlessly embedded in 1870s life as housemaid Catriona Mitchel, travels alongside her employer, undertaker Dr. Duncan Gray, and Detective Hugh McCreadie to attend McCreadie’s sister’s wedding. Instead of a joyous celebration, they walk directly into a social minefield. The wedding unites the McCreadies and the Cranstons, two families bound by a deeply complicated and deeply uncomfortable history. 

It is within this pressure cooker of familial resentment and clashing Victorian egos that Armstrong’s greatest stylistic strength shines: her ability to weave social commentary effortlessly into a compelling whodunit. The opulence of the Cranston estate serves as a facade for rot. When Mallory and Duncan escape the stifling atmosphere to explore the surrounding wilderness, the novel’s tone shifts seamlessly from drawing-room drama to ecological and physical peril. Discovering deadly traps set by the pompous groom, Archie Cranston, ostensibly for endangered Scottish wildcats, Mallory’s modern detective instincts immediately fire. When she notices that a trapped animal's injuries do not align with the mechanics of the snare, Armstrong signals to the reader that the Cranston estate holds darker secrets than mere family feuds. 

True to form, it is not long before the festive weekend is upended by murder. When one of the wedding guests turns up dead, Death at a Highland Wedding shifts into high gear, transforming the sprawling castle and its treacherous woods into a classic hunting ground for a killer. 

Armstrong’s writing style remains a masterclass in balance. She grounds the narrative in rich historical texture—making the 1870s feel tangible, smelling faintly of peat smoke, damp wool, and Victorian pomade—while maintaining the breathless pacing of a contemporary thriller. Her protagonist, Mallory, is as engaging as ever, refusing to let the repressive gender norms of the era stifle her sharp intellect or her fierce sense of justice. Furthermore, the slow-burn dynamic between Mallory and Dr. Duncan Gray continues to provide a deeply satisfying emotional anchor, offering moments of genuine vulnerability and shared intellect amidst the chaos of a murder investigation.

Fans of the series will find everything they love here: clever deductions, sharp banter, immersive historical world-building, and a mystery that keeps you guessing until the final pages. For newcomers, Death at a Highland Wedding stands as a testament to Kelley Armstrong’s skill at blending genres, proving that time travel, historical fiction, and classic whodunits can merge into something entirely unique. Pack your warmest woolen shawl and prepare for a perilous journey into the Scottish Highlands; this is one wedding reception you definitely cannot afford to miss.
