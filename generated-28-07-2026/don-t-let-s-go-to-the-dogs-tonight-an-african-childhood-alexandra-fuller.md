# Don't Let's Go to the Dogs Tonight: An African Childhood

**Author:** Alexandra Fuller  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2001

Don't Let's Go to the Dogs Tonight: An African Childhood by Alexandra Fuller is a mesmerizing memoir that whisks readers away to the untamed landscapes of southern and central Africa in the 1970s and 80s. This New York Times bestseller is a poignant and unflinching portrayal of the author's unconventional childhood, marked by war, loss, and resilience.

At the heart of the book is the complex and fascinating figure of the author's mother, Nicola Fuller, a force of nature who embodies the contradictions of colonial Africa. Nicola is a woman who flings herself at life with manic energy, teaching her daughters to be self-sufficient, resilient, and strong-willed. She is a nurturer, but not in the classical sense – her love is tough, unyielding, and often brutal. Through her mother's eyes, Fuller recounts a childhood marked by adventure, danger, and uncertainty, as the family navigates the treacherous terrain of war-torn Rhodesia.

Fuller's prose is a marvel, weaving together the threads of her childhood with a narrative voice that is both wry and vulnerable. Her writing is unsentimental, yet deeply moving, capturing the beauty and brutality of Africa with a keen eye for detail. The book is a testament to the power of storytelling, as Fuller recounts her mother's tales of love, loss, and survival, which become her own salvation in the face of adversity.

One of the most striking aspects of the book is its exploration of the complex relationships between mothers and daughters, particularly in the context of colonial Africa. Fuller's portrayal of her mother is multifaceted, revealing a woman who is both fiercely loving and recklessly destructive. This nuanced exploration of motherhood is a highlight of the book, offering a fresh perspective on the traditional narratives of family and identity.

The book is also a powerful exploration of the author's own identity, forged in the crucible of war, displacement, and cultural upheaval. Fuller's narrative is a deeply personal one, yet it speaks to universal themes of belonging, identity, and the search for meaning in a chaotic world. Through her story, she offers a unique perspective on the complexities of colonialism, racism, and the human cost of war.

In Don't Let's Go to the Dogs Tonight, Alexandra Fuller has crafted a memoir that is both a tribute to her mother's indomitable spirit and a testament to the power of storytelling. This book is a must-read for anyone who loves Africa, memoirs, or simply great storytelling. It is a riveting, humorous, and compassionate account of a life lived on the edge, and a powerful exploration of the human condition.
