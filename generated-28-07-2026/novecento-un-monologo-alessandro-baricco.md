# Novecento. Un monologo

**Author:** Alessandro Baricco  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2006

To read Alessandro Baricco’s Novecento is to step onto the shifting, rhythmic decks of the Virginian, an ocean liner cutting through the grey Atlantic between the wars, carrying the weight of a fractured world in its belly. Published in 2006, this short masterpiece—though technically a theatrical monologue designed to be spoken aloud—operates with the literary grace of a novel and the haunting resonance of a modern myth. 

At the heart of the story lies Danny Boodt T.D. Lemon Novecento, an orphan born on the ship, abandoned in a box of lemons on a first-class piano, and destined to never touch solid land. He grows up to become the greatest pianist the ocean has ever known, a man whose fingers do not merely play jazz and ragtime; they invent them, bend them, and release them into the salt air. Through the eyes of his lifelong friend, a trumpet player who narrates the tale, Baricco constructs a fable about art, freedom, and the agonizing boundaries of human existence.

Baricco’s stylistic brilliance shines through his mastery of rhythm. The prose reads like a musical score, moving seamlessly from the frantic, syncopated beats of a piano duel with Jelly Roll Morton to the quiet, melancholic hum of a lonely night on the open sea. He avoids heavy theatrical staging in favor of pure, breathless storytelling. The language is lyrical yet accessible, carrying the reader forward with the momentum of a rolling wave. It is a style that demands to be read in a single sitting, preferably in one breath, mimicking the endless horizon that defines Novecento's entire universe.

The central theme of the book is as profound as it is poetic: the paradox of infinite choice versus absolute limitation. The mainland represents the chaotic, terrifying multitude of options that paralyze the modern soul—thousands of streets, countless women, endless destinies. For Novecento, the piano keyboard is his world. With eighty-eight keys, finite and bounded, he can create infinity. But when the time finally comes to step off the gangplank and face the terra firma of New York City, Novecento freezes. He realizes that a life without borders is a life without music, a canvas too vast to paint upon.

Novecento is a striking, bittersweet jewel of contemporary Italian literature. It asks uncomfortable questions about where we truly belong and whether the cages we build for ourselves are actually sanctuaries. Baricco has crafted a timeless vignette about a man who chose to remain a ghost of the ocean, leaving behind an unforgettable melody that echoes long after the final page is turned.
