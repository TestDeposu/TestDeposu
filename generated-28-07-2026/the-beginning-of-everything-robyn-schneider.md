# The Beginning of Everything

**Author:** Robyn Schneider  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

How do you start over when youve lost everything you thought you were? In The Beginning of Everything, author Robyn Schneider tackles this profound existential question with a sharp wit, an aching vulnerability, and a refreshing literary sensibility that elevates the traditional high school drama into something truly memorable. 

At the center of the story is Ezra Faulkner, a boy who once had it all. As the varsity tennis captain and a shoe-in for homecoming king, Ezra’s social currency was unassailable. That is, until a devastating car accident shatters his knee, abruptly ending his athletic career, coupled with the soul-crushing betrayal of his cheating girlfriend. Overnight, the golden boy is forced off the tennis court and into the debate team, stripped of the superficial identity he had so carefully constructed. 

Enter Cassidy Thorpe: an enigmatic, unpredictable new girl who operates entirely outside the rigid social hierarchy of high school. Armed with secrets, flash mobs, blanket forts, and a peculiar poodle who just might be the reincarnation of Jay Gatsby, Cassidy upends Ezra's world. Their dynamic is the beating heart of the novel. Schneider crafts a romance fueled by snappy banter, intellectual sparring, and deeply human flaws. As Kirkus aptly noted in a starred review, Schneider takes familiar teen tropes and infuses them with profound depth, giving us characters sharp enough to trade barbs with the finest creations of John Green or Ned Vizzini.

Thematically, The Beginning of Everything is an exploration of reinvention and resilience. Schneider’s style shines brightest when she examines the heavy burden of expectations. Ezra’s journey is not just about adapting to a new physical disability; it is a poignant, honest look at the psychological wreckage of losing one's perceived status and the terrifying, liberating process of discovering who you are underneath the applause. It echoes the disillusioned longing of The Great Gatsby and the alienated introspection of The Catcher in the Rye, yet it remains distinctly modern, vibrant, and hopeful. 

Fans of contemporary coming-of-age stories will find themselves thoroughly captivated by Schneider’s ability to balance heartbreak with genuine humor. The narrative reminds us that sometimes, the most catastrophic endings are merely the prerequisites for a new beginning. Intelligent, emotionally resonant, and beautifully paced, this is a contemporary twist on the classic teen novel that lingers long after you turn the final page.
