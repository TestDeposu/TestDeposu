# Sempre (Sempre, #1)

**Author:** J.M. Darhower  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

Sempre opens with a punch that reverberates long after the final page: the Kevlar Killer, a name whispered in back‑alley gossip, is revealed not as a myth but as Corrado Moretti—a boy forged in neglect, a teenager taught that affection is a liability, and a man whose every breath is a calculation of survival. J.M. Darhower does not merely introduce a protagonist; she dismantles the armor that surrounds him, exposing the raw nerve of a soul that has learned to love only in the shadows of violence.

The novel’s central theme orbits the tension between self‑preservation and the irresistible pull of family. From Corrado’s earliest memories—beatings whispered behind closed doors, the hollow echo of a mother’s absence—to his later entanglement with the DeMarco clan, Darhower shows how loyalty can become both shackle and salvation. The mantra “Never get close. Never get attached.” is repeated like a prayer, yet each encounter with the DeMarcos chips away at that creed, forcing Corrado to confront a truth he has spent years denying: that love, however dangerous, can also be the very thing that makes life worth risking. This push‑pull creates a moral gray area that feels authentic rather than contrived; the reader is never allowed to simply label Corrado a hero or a villain. Instead, we witness a man constantly negotiating the cost of his choices, weighing the blood on his hands against the warmth of a fleeting embrace.

Darhower’s style is as gritty as the world she inhabits. Her prose leans into short, staccato sentences during moments of tension, mirroring the rapid heartbeat of a character perpetually on alert. When the narrative slows to explore Corrado’s interior life, the language becomes almost lyrical—metaphors of shattered glass, lingering smoke, and the cold kiss of steel serve to externalize his internal scars. This shift in rhythm does more than vary pacing; it embodies the duality of Corrado’s existence: the brutal exterior of the killer versus the fragile, yearning interior of the boy who still hopes for a semblance of normalcy. The author’s attention to sensory detail— the metallic tang of blood, the oppressive heat of a Sicilian summer, the hushed murmurs of a mafia meeting—grounds the story in a tangible reality that makes the occasional flights of poetic introspection feel earned rather than indulgent.

What truly sets Sempre apart is Darhower’s willingness to let love develop amid brutality without sanitizing either element. The romance that blooms between Corrado and his DeMarco counterpart is tender, yet it never loses the edge of danger that surrounds it. Their interactions are charged with a palpable electricity: a stolen glance across a crowded room, a hesitant touch that could just as easily be a threat as a promise. This careful balance keeps the reader constantly aware that any moment of tenderness could be shattered by the next act of violence, reinforcing the novel’s central lesson that safety is an illusion in a world ruled by honor codes and blood feuds.

Critically, the novel does not shy away from the consequences of a life lived in the shadows. Corrado’s trauma is portrayed with unflinching honesty; nightmares, flashbacks, and the ever‑present fear of becoming the very monster he despises are rendered with psychological depth. Darhower avoids glorifying the mafia lifestyle, instead presenting it as a corrupting force that offers both protection and imprisonment. The result is a narrative that feels both expansive in its scope—spanning years of Corrado’s life—and intimate in its focus on the minutiae of his emotional landscape.

In sum, Sempre is a compelling study of how love can survive—and sometimes thrive—within the darkest of circumstances. Darhower’s style, marked by its rhythmic shifts and vivid imagery, serves the story’s thematic core perfectly, allowing readers to feel every pulse of Corrado’s conflicted heart. The novel does not offer easy redemption; instead, it asks whether the scars we carry can ever truly heal when the world demands we keep fighting to stay alive.
