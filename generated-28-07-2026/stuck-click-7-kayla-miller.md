# Stuck (Click #7)

**Author:** Kayla Miller  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

Navigating the shifting tides of middle-grade friendship has always been Kayla Miller’s superpower, and in *Stuck*, the seventh installment of her beloved *Click* series, she proves once again why she is a definitive voice in contemporary graphic novels. Set against the nostalgic, pine-scented backdrop of Camp Acorn Lake, this 2025 release takes a universally dreaded social scenario—being caught in the crossfire of a bitter feud between your closest friends—and transforms it into a masterclass on emotional maturity, boundaries, and self-awareness.

The story reunites readers with Olive, who arrives at camp anticipating two idyllic, stress-free weeks of swimming, arts and crafts, and bonding with her cherished camp friends, Max and Bree. However, Miller wastes no time subverting those expectations. Before the campers have even unpacked, Max and Bree are locked in a silent, icy war of attrition, immediately attempting to draft Olive into their respective corners. Every diplomatic overture Olive makes backfires, leaving her isolated and utterly paralyzed by the social friction. 

What elevates *Stuck* above a standard camp comedy is Miller’s deeply empathetic exploration of the theme of emotional control and interdependence. Olive’s dilemma isn't just about managing her friends' bad moods; it is about learning a hard truth of growing up: you cannot manage, fix, or control anyone else's feelings. When her attempts to play peacemaker only amplify the tension—and with her other friend Willow happily absorbed in a bustling social schedule—Olive finds herself saddled with Nat, a homesick and unhappy first-time camper. 

Rather than wallowing in her ruined summer, Olive channels her frustration into a constructive camp-wide improvement project. This subplot serves as the narrative engine of the book, brilliantly mirroring the internal struggles of the characters. While Willow, Nat, and even the warring Max and Bree eventually want to contribute to Olive’s winning camp plan, their inability to cooperate exposes the rot of their interpersonal drama. Miller uses the architecture of the camp project to pose a poignant question to her young readers: How do you build something meaningful together when the foundation of your group is cracking under the weight of stubbornness and pride?

Stylistically, Miller’s signature vibrant artwork shines brightly in *Stuck*. Her expressive character designs capture micro-expressions with uncanny accuracy—the tight-lipped glare of a grudge, the heavy shoulders of exhaustion, and the bright, tentative smile of a new friendship forming. The visual pacing mirrors the weather in the story; just as a literal storm brews over the lake, the emotional climate within Olive's circle shifts dynamically through panel layouts and a warm, evocative color palette. The cartooning is clean, accessible, and bursting with personality, making the emotional stakes feel entirely authentic and high-definition.

Ultimately, *Stuck* is a triumph for the *Click* series because it refuses to offer easy, magical resolutions. Friendships aren't mended with the wave of a wand; they require grace, space, and individual accountability. Miller delivers a narrative that respects the emotional intelligence of her middle-grade audience, offering a reassuring hug wrapped in an engaging, fast-paced graphic novel. For anyone who has ever felt trapped between the people they love, Olive’s latest adventure is an essential, heartwarming reminder that sometimes the only way out of the mud is through honest communication and letting go of what you cannot change.
