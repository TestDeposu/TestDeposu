# Chain of Iron (The Last Hours, #2)

**Author:** Cassandra Clare  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

Shadowhunter Chronicles architect Cassandra Clare returns to her beloved Edwardian London with Chain of Iron, the breathless, tension-fueled second installment in The Last Hours trilogy. Following the glittering yet perilous events of Chain of Gold, Clare pivots from establishing her sprawling, diverse cast to plunging them headfirst into psychological turmoil, romantic deception, and visceral terror. In this installment, the author trades some of the youthful optimism of her earlier series for a darker, more atmospheric tone, proving that growing up in the Shadowhunter world often means learning to bear unbearable secrets.

At the heart of Chain of Iron lies the central theme of duplicity and the agonizing weight of performative perfection. Clare masterfully deconstructs the facade of the Edwardian era, contrasting the glittering ballrooms and polite society of London with the blood-soaked alleys hunted by a ruthless serial killer. This dichotomy is mirrored perfectly in the inner lives of her protagonists. Cordelia Carstairs, James Herondale, and Lucie Herondale are all trapped in webs of their own making, bound by noble intentions that have curdled into dangerous liabilities. Cordelia wears the ring of an engaged woman, yet her marriage is a hollow arrangement masking unrequited devotion; James harbors a terrifying suspicion that his own demonic lineage makes him the very monster they hunt; and Lucie plays with forbidden necromancy in a desperate bid to alter fate. Clare uses these intertwined deceptions to explore how love, when tangled with sacrifice and silence, can become as much a prison as a sanctuary. 

Clare’s signature stylistic flourishes are on full display here, blending high-stakes urban fantasy with the sweeping, emotional melodrama of Victorian and Edwardian romance. Her prose is lush, deeply sensory, and relentlessly paced. She excels at world-building that feels both historically grounded and wonderfully fantastical, painting Edwardian London with gaslit streets, fog-shrouded bridges, and hidden magical underworlds. Furthermore, Clare’s dialogue remains a major strength—snappy, emotionally resonant, and occasionally devastating. 

What elevates Chain of Iron above a standard middle-book syndrome is Clare’s refusal to give her characters easy outs. The stakes feel genuinely high because the threats are as internal as they are external. The physical toll of Cortana burning Cordelia’s hands is a brilliant metaphor for her spiritual and emotional anguish, a motif Clare handles with nuance. 

Ultimately, Chain of Iron is a gripping, emotionally exhausting, and thoroughly satisfying chapter in the Shadowhunter saga. Clare expertly balances a sprawling ensemble cast, keeping readers guessing about the identity of the killer while driving a stake straight through their hearts regarding the romantic entanglements. It is a story about the monsters we fight in the dark, and the even scarier ones we harbor within ourselves.
