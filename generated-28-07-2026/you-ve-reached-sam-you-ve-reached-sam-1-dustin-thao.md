# You've Reached Sam (You've Reached Sam, #1)

**Author:** Dustin Thao  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

If I Stay meets Your Name in Dustin Thao’s sensational 2021 debut, You’ve Reached Sam, a novel that captures the excruciating beauty of first love and the unbearable weight of sudden loss. At its core, Thao’s story is not merely a supernatural romance, but a profound exploration of grief, survivor's guilt, and the agonizing process of learning how to say goodbye when the universe has denied you closure. 

Seventeen-year-old Julie Clarke is a girl with a blueprint for tomorrow. She and her boyfriend, Sam, have their futures meticulously mapped out: escaping their small town, heading to college in the city, and spending a dream summer in Japan. But life’s carefully constructed architecture collapses in a single, devastating moment when Sam dies. Shattered by the abruptness of her reality, Julie retreats into a fortress of avoidance. She skips his funeral, purges his belongings, and desperately attempts to outrun the ghost of a shared future. 

Yet grief is a persistent shadow. When Julie discovers a forgotten message from Sam written in the back of her high school yearbook, the dam breaks. In a moment of pure, desperate longing, she dials his old cell phone number just to listen to the familiar cadence of his voice on the voicemail greeting. Then, impossibly, the line connects. Sam picks up.

What follows is the emotional anchor of Thao’s narrative. The phone calls offer a fragile, temporary bridge between the living and the lost. With every conversation, Julie falls for Sam all over again, reviving a romance that was cut tragically short. But this miraculous second chance comes with a devastating psychological toll. As Julie pours her days into waiting for the phone to ring, she becomes increasingly untethered from the present, turning the impossible connection into an emotional anchor that threatens to drag her under. 

Thao’s writing style is defined by its aching vulnerability and cinematic accessibility. He employs a clean, evocative prose style that strips away unnecessary literary embellishment, allowing the raw emotional landscape of adolescence to take center stage. The pacing mirrors the erratic nature of grief itself—oscillating between quiet, devastating moments of introspection and frantic, heartbeat-skipping urgency during their phone conversations. Thao has a remarkable ear for contemporary teen dialogue, capturing the breathless intensity of young love without losing the grounded reality of teenage sorrow. 

The central theme of the novel examines the difference between moving on and letting go. Thao challenges the reader to consider what closure truly means. Is it forgetting, or is it integrating the memory of the lost into a changed existence? Through Julie’s journey, the author portrays grief not as a linear path, but as a labyrinth of denial, longing, and eventual acceptance. The supernatural element of the phone calls serves as an external manifestation of Julie’s internal resistance to letting go, making her journey deeply relatable to anyone who has ever wished for just five more minutes with someone they lost.

While the premise flirts with magical realism, Thao keeps the emotional stakes painfully anchored in reality. He does not offer easy answers or cheap miracles. Instead, he crafts a bittersweet meditation on mortality and memory, reminding us that love leaves an indelible mark long after the physical presence is gone. 

You’ve Reached Sam is a poignant, fast-paced tearjerker that will resonate deeply with fans of contemporary young adult fiction. Dustin Thao has crafted an unforgettable debut about the courage it takes to live fully in a world where everything can change in a heartbeat. It is a striking reminder that sometimes, the hardest part of love isn't holding on, but finding the strength to let go.
