# Spells (Wings, #2)

**Author:** Aprilynne Pike  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

In Spells, the highly anticipated second installment of Aprilynne Pike’s bestselling Wings series, the author returns to the lush, floral-infused mythology that enchanted readers worldwide. Picking up not long after the revelations of the first novel, Spells dives deeper into the delicate, often agonizing dichotomy of a protagonist caught between two entirely different worlds. Pike’s signature style—characterized by vivid sensory descriptions, botanical wonder, and high-stakes adolescent emotion—takes center stage, cementing her place as a distinct voice in young adult fantasy. 

At the heart of Spells lies the timeless and universal theme of identity and the agonizing burden of choice. Laurel Sewell is no longer the oblivious human teenager she once believed herself to be; she has accepted her true nature as an autumn-blooming faerie. Yet, this acceptance brings a profound crisis of belonging. Avalon calls to her with its ethereal beauty, ancient secrets, and the magnetic pull of her own kind. More importantly, it brings her face-to-face with Tamani, the charismatic faerie sentry whose devotion spans lifetimes. But Laurel’s roots in the human world run deep. She cannot simply erase her mortal upbringing, nor can she casually discard the genuine affection she holds for her human boyfriend, David. 

Pike brilliantly uses this internal conflict to explore what it truly means to belong. Laurel is torn between the comforting familiarity of human suburban life—represented by David’s steadfast, gentle support—and the intoxicating, destiny-bound allure of her faerie heritage, embodied by Tamani’s quiet, eternal patience. The author avoids making this a simple black-and-white romance dilemma. Instead, she portrays Laurel’s indecision not as cruelty, but as the authentic paralysis of a young woman forced to choose between two futures that demand the sacrifice of her own heart. 

Stylistically, Pike excels in world-building through botany. The faerie realm of Avalon is not depicted through cold, metallic castles or generic medieval tropes, but through living, breathing nature. Avalon is a sensory masterpiece of vibrant petals, crisp autumn air, and magical flora that react to the emotional states of its inhabitants. This floral motif extends to Laurel herself, grounding her supernatural identity in the natural cycles of the earth. Pike’s prose is fluid, accessible, and deeply atmospheric, effortlessly drawing the reader into the scent of pine, blooming blossoms, and the charged tension of clandestine reunions. 

Furthermore, the author effectively raises the stakes in this sequel. While the first book focused largely on awakening and discovery, Spells introduces real peril. The threat of trolls—the ancient enemies of the fae—looms larger, reminding Laurel that her world is not just one of romantic triangles and poetic longing, but of genuine danger. This external threat pushes the narrative forward, ensuring that the emotional introspection never bogs down the pacing. 

Spells is a compelling, tender, and enchanting read that successfully captures the bittersweet ache of first love and the complicated journey of self-discovery. Aprilynne Pike weaves a sophomore novel that honors its predecessor while expanding its thematic horizons, leaving readers eagerly turning pages to see how the petals will fall.
