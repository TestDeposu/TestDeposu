# Uncommon Criminals (Heist Society, #2)

**Author:** Ally Carter  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

In the high-stakes, glittering world of international art theft, staying out of trouble is far more difficult than breaking into a heavily guarded vault. Ally Carter returns with Uncommon Criminals, the dazzling second installment in her beloved Heist Society series, proving once again that she is a master of young adult adventure. Following the breathless success of her debut outing, Carter crafts a sophomore novel that is sharper, funnier, and infinitely more charming than its predecessor, blending clever capers with the irresistible thrill of teenage espionage.

At the heart of the novel is Katarina Bishop, a young woman whose lineage is practically mapped out in stolen masterpieces. After pulling off the impossible and robbing the greatest museum in the world, Kat just wants a moment to breathe. Instead, she finds herself thrust back into the criminal underworld with a mandate that sounds less like a job and more like a death sentence: steal the infamous Cleopatra Emerald and return it to its rightful owners. 

The central theme of Uncommon Criminals explores the friction between inherited destiny and self-determination. Kat’s family has operated by a strict set of rules, cons, and traditions for centuries. Yet, as she stares down a target protected by the world’s most secure auction house, she quickly realizes that her family’s legacy playbook is useless. Compounding the physical danger is a psychological one—the legend of the emerald dictates that no one who holds it keeps it for long, fueling whispers that the gem is cursed. Carter uses this curse not merely as a supernatural trope, but as a brilliant thematic device to question whether Kat can outrun the history written for her, or if she must forge an entirely new path. By forcing her protagonist to invent her own rules, Carter elevates the narrative from a simple puzzle-box mystery into a compelling coming-of-age story about agency and identity.

Stylistically, Carter’s prose sparkles with wit, cinematic pacing, and effortless cool. Her writing style is tailor-made for readers who crave fast-paced escapism without sacrificing character depth. The dialogue snaps with rapid-fire banter, particularly between Kat and her endlessly resourceful, devastatingly charming best friend-turned-accomplice, W.W. Hale. Their simmering romantic tension provides a grounding emotional anchor amidst the globe-trotting chaos. Carter excels at writing ensemble dynamics; every member of Kat’s crew—from tech geniuses to master-of-disguise con artists—gets a moment to shine, contributing to a vibrant sense of camaraderie that makes the reader feel like an honorary member of the heist team.

The book’s structure mimics a well-orchestrated magic trick. Carter drops clues with casual sleight of hand, leading the audience down one path before executing a dizzying, thoroughly satisfying twist. There are no wasted motions in her storytelling. Every exotic locale, from glittering European auction houses to shadowed alleyways, is painted with vivid, sensory-rich details that immerse the reader completely in the jet-setting lifestyle of elite thieves. 

Uncommon Criminals is a triumph of YA contemporary caper fiction. It delivers everything fans could possibly want: high-octane suspense, sparkling humor, clever plotting, and a slow-burn romance that satisfies the soul. Ally Carter has engineered a seamless blend of mystery and heart, ensuring that Katarina Bishop remains one of the most memorable and captivating heroines in modern adventure literature. This is a journey well worth taking, even if the treasure at the end comes with a curse.
