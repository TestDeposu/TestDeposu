# Just One Damned Thing After Another (The Chronicles of St Mary's, #1)

**Author:** Jodi Taylor  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

History, as the cynical adage goes, is just one damned thing after another. But at the St Mary’s Institute of Historical Research, that phrase is not a passive observation—it is an active, adrenaline-fueled, and frequently explosive way of life. In her bestselling and wildly inventive debut novel, *Just One Damned Thing After Another*, author Jodi Taylor takes the well-worn sci-fi trope of time travel and gleefully turns it on its head, injecting it with British wit, academic chaos, and an irrepressible sense of adventure. 

At the pulsing, bruised heart of the narrative is Dr. Madeleine “Max” Maxwell. Newly recruited into the fold, Max quickly discovers that St Mary’s is far from a dusty, quiet academic institution. Here, historians do not merely read about the past; they pack a lunch, strap themselves into time-traveling pods, and go witness it firsthand. Yet, as Max and her eccentric colleagues rapidly learn, stepping into the timestream is a perilous business governed by one absolute, unforgiving rule: try not to die. 

Taylor’s narrative style is the true engine of the novel. It is delightfully unconventional, swinging wildly between laugh-out-loud comedy and genuine, breath-hitching peril. Fans of Terry Pratchett’s satirical world-building or the manic, universe-saving energy of *Doctor Who* will find themselves immediately at home. Taylor possesses a rare gift for pacing, sweeping the reader along at a breakneck speed that mirrors the breathless panic of her characters as they dodge historical catastrophes by the skin of their teeth. 

The central theme of the novel explores the stubborn, self-correcting resilience of time itself. History, in Taylor’s universe, has a personality. It is a living, breathing entity that simply does not like to be messed with. No matter how meticulously the historians plan their observations—whether they are cataloging the Cretaceous period or witnessing the destruction of the Library of Alexandria—chaos invariably ensues. The universe has a way of course-correcting, usually at the expense of St Mary’s battered equipment and long-suffering staff. 

Beyond the time-jumping mayhem, what truly elevates the book is its profound affection for humanity and history itself. St Mary's is a dysfunctional, tea-drinking, fiercely loyal family bound together by shared trauma and gallows humor. Max’s transformation from an outsider into a hardened, dedicated member of this eccentric clan grounds the high-concept sci-fi in real emotional stakes. 

*Just One Damned Thing After Another* is a swashbuckling joyride that defies easy genre categorization. It is science fiction, historical fantasy, and a workplace comedy all wrapped into one addictive package. Taylor has crafted a universe so vibrant and chaotic that readers will finish the final page desperately wishing St Mary’s was real and that they, too, were brave enough to sign up.
