# As Long as the Lemon Trees Grow

**Author:** Zoulfa Katouh  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

In the midst of war and chaos, Zoulfa Katouh's "As Long as the Lemon Trees Grow" blossoms as a powerful love letter to Syria and its people. Set against the backdrop of the Syrian Revolution, this speculative novel masterfully weaves together threads of hope, love, and resilience, crafting a narrative that is both poignant and thought-provoking.

At the heart of the story is Salama Kassab, a pharmacy student turned volunteer at a hospital in Homs, where the wounded pour in daily. As she navigates the treacherous landscape of war, Salama is torn between her loyalty to her country and her desperate desire to escape. Her inner turmoil is manifested in the form of Khawf, a physical embodiment of her fear that haunts her every move. This clever narrative device allows Katouh to explore the complexities of Salama's emotions, laying bare the psychological toll of living in a war zone.

Katouh's writing is evocative and immersive, conjuring the sights, sounds, and smells of a war-torn city. Her prose is lyrical and expressive, imbuing even the most mundane details with a sense of urgency and desperation. The author's use of magical realism adds a layer of depth to the narrative, allowing her to explore themes that might otherwise be too difficult to confront.

One of the most striking aspects of the novel is its portrayal of the Syrian Revolution. Katouh eschews simplistic or binary representations of the conflict, instead opting for a nuanced and multifaceted exploration of the complexities involved. Through Salama's experiences, the author sheds light on the human cost of war, highlighting the ways in which it can both unite and divide communities.

The characters in the novel are richly drawn and multidimensional, with Salama being a particularly compelling protagonist. Her struggles to reconcile her desire for freedom with her loyalty to her country are deeply relatable, and her relationships with those around her are authentic and heartfelt. The supporting cast is equally well-developed, from the enigmatic Khawf to the charming and charismatic boy Salama meets in the midst of the chaos.

If there is a criticism to be made, it is that the pacing of the novel can be uneven at times. Some sections feel a little rushed, while others are more languidly paced. However, this is a minor quibble in an otherwise excellent novel.

Overall, "As Long as the Lemon Trees Grow" is a powerful and moving novel that will resonate with fans of "The Book Thief" and "Salt to the Sea". Katouh's writing is masterful, and her portrayal of the Syrian Revolution is both nuanced and thought-provoking. This is a novel that will linger in the reader's mind long after the final page has been turned.
