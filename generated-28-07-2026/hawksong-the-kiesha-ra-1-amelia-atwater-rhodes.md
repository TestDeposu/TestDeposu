# Hawksong (The Kiesha'ra, #1)

**Author:** Amelia Atwater-Rhodes  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2003

**Hawksong: A Feathers-and-Fangs Tale of Trust and Royalty**

Amelia Atwater-Rhodes' "Hawksong," the inaugural novel of her captivating Kiesha'ra series, is a breath of fresh air in the realm of young adult fantasy. Published in 2003, it weaves a tale of ancient enmity, shapeshifting royalty, and an unexpected bond that challenges the very foundations of war.

At the heart of "Hawksong" lies Danica Shardae, an avian shapeshifter and heir to her people's throne. She's a golden hawk in the sky and a human on land, but it's her role as a peacemaker that truly sets her apart. The war between her people and the serpiente has raged for generations, and Danica is determined to end it, even if it means accepting Zane Cobriana, the serpiente leader, as her pair bond.

Atwater-Rhodes' prose is as smooth as a hawk's flight, effortlessly carrying readers through a world that's both familiar and fantastical. Her characters are complex, their motivations nuanced, and their struggles relatable. Danica, in particular, is a heroine worth rooting for, her strength tempered by vulnerability and her courage tested by the weight of her responsibilities.

The author masterfully explores the theme of trust, the cornerstone of any relationship, let alone a political alliance. Danica's struggle to trust Zane, and his struggle to earn that trust, is the engine that drives the plot. It's a slow burn, a dance of wills and words, that keeps readers on the edge of their seats.

Atwater-Rhodes also excels in world-building. The avian and serpiente cultures are distinct, their histories intertwined, and their enmity palpable. The shapeshifting aspect adds a layer of intrigue, as characters navigate their dual existences and the challenges they present.

"Hawksong" is more than just a story of love conquering war. It's a tale of trust, understanding, and the power of communication. It's a reminder that even in the heat of conflict, there's always room for dialogue, for understanding, for change.

In a nutshell, "Hawksong" is a must-read for fans of young adult fantasy, shapeshifter lore, and stories that challenge the status quo. It's a book that lingers in the mind long after the last page is turned.
