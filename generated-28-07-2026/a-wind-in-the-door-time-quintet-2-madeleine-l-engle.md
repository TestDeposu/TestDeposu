# A Wind in the Door (Time Quintet, #2)

**Author:** Madeleine L'Engle  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

In A Wind in the Door, the second installment of Madeleine L'Engle's iconic Time Quintet, the author masterfully weaves a tale that defies the boundaries of time and space, inviting readers to embark on a thrilling adventure that delves into the mysteries of the universe. This enchanting story continues to explore the intricate lives of Meg Murry, her brother Charles Wallace, and their friend Calvin as they navigate the complexities of friendship, faith, and the interconnected nature of all things.

One of the standout features of L'Engle's writing style is her ability to craft worlds that are both fantastical and eerily familiar. As the story begins to unfold, we find ourselves drawn into the everyday lives of the Murry family, only to be propelled into a realm where dragons and cherubim roam free. The world-building in A Wind in the Door is nothing short of breathtaking, with the author seamlessly merging the rational and the mystical, giving readers a glimpse into a realm where science and spirituality coexist in perfect harmony.

At the heart of this captivating narrative lies the central theme of the book: the battle between good and evil. Echthros, the malevolent force threatening to disrupt the balance of the universe, serves as a powerful metaphor for the internal struggles we all face. Charles Wallace's illness becomes a catalyst for this battle, and Meg, Calvin, and Mr. Jenkins must join forces to travel inside his consciousness and confront the shadows within. This inner journey is both a poignant exploration of the human condition and a testament to the transformative power of love and friendship.

L'Engle's prose is characterized by a unique blend of lyricism and simplicity, making her writing accessible to readers of all ages. Her use of language is reminiscent of Tolkien's epic fantasy, but with a distinctly modern touch. The author's ability to convey complex ideas and emotions through simple, yet evocative language is a hallmark of her craft.

One of the most striking aspects of A Wind in the Door is its exploration of the interconnectedness of all things. Through the eyes of Proginoskes, the cherubim, we are given a glimpse into a realm where every action, every thought, and every emotion has a ripple effect on the universe. This idea is not only a central tenet of the story but also a powerful reminder of our responsibility to the world around us.

In A Wind in the Door, Madeleine L'Engle has crafted a narrative that is both a thrilling adventure and a profound exploration of the human experience. This enchanting tale is a powerful reminder of the transformative power of love, friendship, and the interconnectedness of all things. As we journey alongside Meg, Calvin, and Charles Wallace, we are invited to consider the deeper implications of our actions and the importance of living in harmony with the world around us.
