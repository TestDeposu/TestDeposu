# The Last Ever After (The School for Good and Evil, #3)

**Author:** Soman Chainani  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

In "The Last Ever After", the third installment of Soman Chainani's captivating School for Good and Evil series, the author masterfully weaves a tale of friendship, love, and the eternal struggle between good and evil. This book is a testament to Chainani's skill in crafting a narrative that is both a nostalgic throwback to classic fairy tales and a fresh, modern take on the genre.

At the heart of the story is the complicated relationship between Sophie and Agatha, former best friends who have found themselves on opposite sides of the battle between good and evil. As the forces of evil gain momentum, the two girls must navigate their complicated past and confront the darkness that threatens to destroy their world. Chainani expertly explores the complexities of female friendship, delving into the ways in which relationships can be both a source of strength and a catalyst for conflict.

One of the most striking aspects of Chainani's writing is his ability to balance lighthearted humor with darker, more serious themes. The book is full of clever wit and comedic moments, but it also tackles complex issues like the nature of evil, the power of love, and the difficulty of finding one's place in the world. This blend of tone is a hallmark of Chainani's style, and it makes for a reading experience that is both entertaining and emotionally resonant.

The world-building in "The Last Ever After" is also noteworthy. Chainani's creation of the School for Good and Evil is a masterclass in fantasy world-building, with a richly detailed landscape that is full of magic, adventure, and hidden dangers. The author's use of fairy tale tropes and conventions is clever and subversive, often turning familiar stories on their head in unexpected ways.

The romance in the book is another highlight, with Sophie's complicated relationship with the young School Master adding an extra layer of tension to the story. Chainani handles the romance with sensitivity and nuance, exploring the ways in which love can be both redemptive and destructive.

If there is a criticism to be made, it is that the book can feel a bit overstuffed at times, with multiple plot threads and characters vying for attention. However, Chainani's writing is so engaging and the story is so compelling that this is a minor quibble.

Overall, "The Last Ever After" is a captivating and emotionally resonant read that will appeal to fans of fantasy, adventure, and fairy tales. With its rich world-building, complex characters, and nuanced exploration of themes, it is a standout in the series and a testament to Chainani's skill as a storyteller.
