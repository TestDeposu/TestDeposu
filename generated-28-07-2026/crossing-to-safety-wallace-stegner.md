# Crossing to Safety

**Author:** Wallace Stegner  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1987

In the realm of American literature, few novels have managed to capture the essence of human relationships with the same level of depth and nuance as Wallace Stegner's "Crossing to Safety". Published in 1987, this masterpiece has stood the test of time, and its beauty and significance continue to resonate with readers to this day. This is a novel that will appeal to anyone who has ever experienced the joy and pain of deep friendships and marriages, and the ways in which these relationships can shape and define us.

At its core, "Crossing to Safety" is a novel about the power of relationships to transform and transcend our lives. The story revolves around two couples, Larry and Sally Morgan, and Charity and Sid Lang, who meet while teaching at a university in Wisconsin and form a lifelong bond. As the novel unfolds, we follow these characters as they navigate the complexities of marriage, friendship, and personal aspiration, moving back and forth between Vermont and Wisconsin.

Stegner's prose is characterized by its simplicity, elegance, and precision. He has a remarkable ability to capture the subtleties of human emotion and the ways in which our relationships with others can both uplift and devastate us. His writing is also infused with a deep sense of compassion and empathy, which allows him to portray his characters in all their complexity and vulnerability.

One of the most striking aspects of "Crossing to Safety" is its portrayal of marriage as a dynamic and constantly evolving process. Stegner shows us that marriage is not a static state, but rather a journey that requires effort, commitment, and compromise. Through the Morgans and the Langs, we see the ways in which marriage can be both a source of great joy and a catalyst for personal growth, as well as a potential source of conflict and heartache.

The novel also explores the theme of friendship and the ways in which our relationships with others can provide us with a sense of belonging and connection. The bond between the two couples is portrayed as a source of strength and comfort, a reminder that we are not alone in our struggles and aspirations. At the same time, Stegner also shows us that friendships can be complicated and multifaceted, requiring effort and dedication to maintain.

Throughout the novel, Stegner's use of landscape and setting is also noteworthy. The contrast between the rugged beauty of Vermont and the more subdued landscape of Wisconsin serves as a powerful backdrop to the story, highlighting the ways in which our surroundings can shape our experiences and perceptions.

In short, "Crossing to Safety" is a masterpiece of American literature that deserves to be read and cherished. It is a novel that will resonate with anyone who has ever experienced the beauty and complexity of human relationships. With its elegant prose, nuanced characterization, and profound insights into the human condition, this is a book that will stay with you long after you finish reading it.
