# Moxie

**Author:** Jennifer Mathieu  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

"Moxie" by Jennifer Mathieu is a fiery, unapologetic exploration of feminism and teenage rebellion, wrapped in a compelling coming-of-age narrative. The story follows Vivian Carter, a high school student who, inspired by her mother's punk rock past, creates a feminist zine to challenge the sexist norms of her Texas high school. Mathieu's prose is sharp and engaging, capturing the raw emotion and frustration of adolescence with authenticity.

At its core, "Moxie" is a love letter to female empowerment and the power of collective action. Mathieu masterfully weaves together a diverse cast of characters, each grappling with their own struggles against patriarchal systems. From the popular cheerleader to the quiet, nerdy girl, each character's journey is a testament to the strength and resilience of young women. The novel's exploration of intersectionality is particularly commendable, as Mathieu acknowledges the unique challenges faced by women of color, LGBTQ+ women, and women with disabilities.

Mathieu's style is straightforward and accessible, making "Moxie" an easy yet powerful read. She expertly balances moments of humor and heartache, creating a narrative that is both entertaining and thought-provoking. The zine entries interspersed throughout the novel serve as a poignant reminder of the issues at stake, providing a unique and engaging way to explore the themes of the story.

"Moxie" is more than just a story about a girl starting a revolution; it's a call to action. It's a reminder that change is possible, even in the face of adversity, and that our voices matter. Mathieu's novel is a must-read for anyone who has ever felt marginalized, silenced, or underestimated. It's a testament to the power of standing up, speaking out, and fighting back.
