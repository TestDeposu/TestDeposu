# Ten Tiny Breaths (Ten Tiny Breaths, #1)

**Author:** K.A. Tucker  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

Grief is rarely pretty. In contemporary romance, trauma is often treated as a convenient backstory—a dark shadow easily dispelled by the arrival of a handsome, attentive love interest. K.A. Tucker shatters this comforting illusion in her 2013 breakthrough novel, Ten Tiny Breaths. Instead of writing a standard, breezy romance, Tucker delivers a raw, unflinching exploration of survival, guilt, and the agonizing process of piecing together a shattered existence.

The emotional core of the novel rests on its visceral central theme: the suffocating weight of survivor's guilt and the fierce, desperate instinct to protect what little you have left. Twenty-year-old Kacey Cleary is not your typical romance heroine. When a drunk driver obliterates her family, her boyfriend, and her best friend, Kacey is left standing amidst the wreckage, listening in her nightmares to the final, echoing breaths of her mother. Instead of crumbling completely, she morphs into a hardened protector. Armed with two bus tickets, she flees Michigan for Miami, dragging her fifteen-year-old sister, Livie, into a gritty, hand-to-mouth existence where every dollar counts and trust is an unaffordable luxury. 

Tucker’s writing style in Ten Tiny Breaths is defined by its emotional honesty and pacing. She avoids overly flowery prose, choosing instead a sharp, first-person narrative voice that mirrors Kacey’s defensive armor. Kacey is cynical, fiercely protective, and deeply flawed. She swears, she builds walls, and she pushes people away because loving someone means risking the agony of losing them all over again. Yet, Tucker balances this heaviness with moments of fierce sibling loyalty and gritty realism. The struggle to pay rent, keep food on the table, and parent a rebellious teenager grounds the novel, preventing it from floating away into melodrama.

Enter Trent Emerson, the quintessential brooding neighbor in apartment 1D. On paper, Trent checks every box of the alpha-male romance trope: smoldering blue eyes, deep dimples, and a tantalizing blend of bad-boy edge and heroic sensitivity. However, Tucker uses Trent not just as a romantic catalyst, but as a mirror for Kacey’s healing. Their dynamic crackles with palpable tension, but the romance never feels rushed or superficial. Trent’s determination to chip away at Kacey’s armor is met with genuine resistance, forcing the narrative to earn every emotional breakthrough. 

What elevates Ten Tiny Breaths above standard New Adult fare is the explosive secret Trent carries—a revelation that threatens to upend the fragile sanctuary Kacey has built in Miami. When the truth finally surfaces, Tucker handles the fallout with dramatic flair, testing the limits of forgiveness and resilience. The climax forces Kacey to confront the very past she ran halfway across the country to escape, proving that true survival isn't just about breathing—it's about learning how to live again.

Ultimately, K.A. Tucker’s novel is a testament to the fact that healing is not linear. It is messy, painful, and punctuated by setbacks. By anchoring a deeply passionate romance to a harrowing journey of trauma recovery, Tucker crafts a striking, unforgettable story that lingers long after the final page. For readers seeking a romance with teeth, heart, and emotional depth, Ten Tiny Breaths is an absolute must-read.
