# Fade (Wake, #2)

**Author:** Lisa McMann  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Dreams and reality blur with terrifying precision in Fade, Lisa McMann’s gripping 2009 sequel to her breakout novel Wake. Where the first installment introduced us to the harrowing burden of Janie Hannagan—a seventeen-year-old involuntarily pulled into the nightmares of those around her—this second chapter deepens the mythology, raising the emotional and psychological stakes to a fever pitch. McMann once again proves that less is profoundly more, delivering a lean, atmospheric narrative that packs the emotional punch of books twice its length.

The central theme of Fade revolves around the inescapable weight of destiny versus the desperate human desire for normalcy. Janie and Cabel are just trying to carve out a quiet, secret space for their burgeoning romance, but the universe has other, much darker plans. McMann expertly uses the dreamscape not just as a paranormal gimmick, but as a stark metaphor for trauma, hidden violence, and the ugly truths people try to bury in the waking world. When Janie taps into a violent classmate’s nightmare at Fieldridge High, the case breaks open, dragging her into a web of dark secrets that no high schooler is equipped to handle. 

What makes Fade stand out is McMann’s distinct literary style. Her prose is sparse, punchy, and cinematic, mirroring the breathless panic of a nightmare. There is no narrative fat here; every sentence feels charged with urgency, perfectly capturing Janie’s exhausted, hyper-vigilant state of mind. McMann writes with a rhythmic cadence that makes the pages turn themselves, yet she never sacrifices emotional depth for pacing. Through brief, sharp descriptions, she builds a heavy, moody atmosphere where safety is an illusion and shadows always hide something sinister.

As Janie uncovers the truth about her ability as a dream catcher, the narrative takes a chilling turn. The bleak revelation of her future shifts the series from a clever supernatural thriller into something much more tragic and profound. Cabel’s shocking behavior adds another layer of tension, forcing both characters to realize that protecting each other might require sacrifices they aren't ready to make. 

Fade is a masterclass in YA suspense. It is dark, fast-paced, and unflinchingly honest about the bleakness of its protagonist's fate. Lisa McMann doesn't offer easy fairy-tale resolutions; instead, she invites readers into a terrifyingly vivid world where the monsters aren't hiding under the bed—they are walking the school halls, waiting for sleep to fall.
