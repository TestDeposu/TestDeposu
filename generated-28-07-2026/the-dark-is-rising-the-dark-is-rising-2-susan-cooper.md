# The Dark Is Rising (The Dark is Rising, #2)

**Author:** Susan Cooper  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2001

In Susan Cooper's enchanting fantasy series, The Dark Is Rising, Will Stanton embarks on a remarkable journey that delves into the mystical realms of good and evil. Published in 1965, the second installment, originally titled as The Dark Is Rising, is a masterclass in weaving intricate mythology, rich imagination, and captivating storytelling.

The Dark Is Rising follows the eleven-year-old Will Stanton as he discovers his true nature - the last of the Old Ones, immortal beings tasked with protecting the world from the Dark, forces of malevolence that seek to dominate and destroy. With his newfound powers and sense of destiny, Will sets out to find the six magical Signs that hold the key to the ultimate battle between the Dark and the Light. This epic quest is set against the backdrop of the Midwinter festivities, as the twelve days of Christmas unfold with all their attendant joys and terrors.

Cooper's prose is a symphony of wonder, deftly weaving together elements of folklore, mythology, and fairy tale to create a narrative that is by turns mystical, magical, and profoundly moving. Her writing is characterized by a deep appreciation for the richness of traditional British folklore, and The Dark Is Rising is a testament to her skill in bringing these stories to life in a way that feels both nostalgic and freshly minted.

One of the most striking aspects of Cooper's storytelling is her ability to balance tension and release with consummate skill. As Will navigates the dangers that lie in wait for him, the reader is swept up in a tide of excitement, fear, and anticipation, only to be calmed by moments of warmth and tenderness. This is a masterful technique, one that not only keeps the reader's heart pounding but also deepens their emotional investment in Will's quest.

The characters that populate Cooper's world are equally well-crafted, with Will himself emerging as a fully formed, vulnerable, and courageous hero. His relationships with the other Old Ones, particularly Merriman and Bran, are a highlight of the book, offering a glimpse into a richly textured world of ancient lore and traditions. Meanwhile, the Dark is a suitably chilling presence, embodied in various forms throughout the story, from the malevolent forces of nature to the dark magic that threatens to consume everything in its path.

Through The Dark Is Rising, Cooper explores themes of identity, power, and the struggle between light and darkness that are both timeless and universally relatable. Her writing is imbued with a deep sense of wonder, and an abiding commitment to the human values of courage, loyalty, and resilience. This is a book that will resonate with readers of all ages, and one that will stay with them long after the final page has been turned.

For those who have not yet experienced the magic of The Dark Is Rising, this book is a must-read. It is a testament to Susan Cooper's enduring legacy as a writer of fantasy, and a reminder that even in the darkest of times, there is always hope and a chance to rise above the shadows.
