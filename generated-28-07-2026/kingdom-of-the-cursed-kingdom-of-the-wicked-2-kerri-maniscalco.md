# Kingdom of the Cursed (Kingdom of the Wicked, #2)

**Author:** Kerri Maniscalco  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

**A Dance with Demons: Kerri Maniscalco's "Kingdom of the Cursed"**

In the enthralling sequel to "Kingdom of the Wicked," Kerri Maniscalco weaves a tale that is as darkly seductive as it is emotionally charged. "Kingdom of the Cursed" is a mesmerizing journey into the Seven Circles of Hell, where the line between love and betrayal, power and vulnerability, is as thin as a razor's edge.

Emilia, our protagonist, is no longer the naive girl she once was. Having sold her soul to become Queen of the Wicked, she now navigates a world of vice and sin with a fierce determination to avenge her sister. Maniscalco's prowess lies in her ability to create a heroine who is both strong and vulnerable, a woman who can wield power but is not afraid to show her pain. Emilia's growth is a testament to Maniscalco's skill in character development, as we watch her evolve from a grieving sister to a queen who commands respect.

The world-building in "Kingdom of the Cursed" is nothing short of stunning. Maniscalco's Hell is a place of opulence and danger, where palaces shimmer like mirages and demons plot in the shadows. Each of the Seven Circles is distinct, from the gluttonous feasts of the Gourmands to the frozen wasteland of the Usurers, creating a rich tapestry of sin that is as fascinating as it is terrifying.

At the heart of this story are the princes of Hell, each a sin incarnate. Maniscalco masterfully explores the complexities of their relationships with Emilia, delving into themes of trust, loyalty, and the destructive power of pride. The dynamic between Emilia and the enigmatic Prince of Wrath is particularly compelling, their dance of deception and desire a masterclass in slow-burn tension.

Maniscalco's writing style is as intoxicating as the world she creates. Her prose is lush and evocative, painting vivid images that linger in the mind long after the last page is turned. She has a knack for balancing suspense with moments of tender intimacy, creating a narrative that is as emotionally resonant as it is thrilling.

However, "Kingdom of the Cursed" is not without its flaws. The pacing can feel uneven at times, with some sections dragging while others race forward at breakneck speed. Additionally, the plot can be convoluted, with multiple threads that sometimes feel like they're leading nowhere. Yet, these are minor quibbles in an otherwise outstanding novel.

In conclusion, "Kingdom of the Cursed" is a captivating tale of love, loss, and revenge set against the backdrop of a richly imagined Hell. Maniscalco's signature blend of history, mythology, and romance makes this a standout addition to the paranormal romance genre. Fans of the first book will not be disappointed, and new readers will find themselves eager to explore this dark and seductive world.
