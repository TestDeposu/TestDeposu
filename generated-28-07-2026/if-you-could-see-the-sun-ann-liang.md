# If You Could See the Sun

**Author:** Ann Liang  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

Title: The Price of Being Seen: Ambition and Morality in Ann Liang’s *If You Could See the Sun*

In a literary landscape frequently saturated with familiar YA tropes, Ann Liang’s 2022 speculative debut, *If You Could See the Sun*, arrives as a sharp, refreshing, and deeply relevant pulse of modern young adult fiction. Seamlessly blending contemporary boarding school drama with magical realism, Liang crafts a compelling narrative that uses a supernatural premise to dissect very real, grinding systemic pressures. At once a high-stakes thriller, a witty enemies-to-lovers romance, and a poignant coming-of-age story, the novel asks a piercing question: How much of yourself are you willing to erase just to secure a place in a world that refuses to look at you?

The story centers on Alice Sun, an overachieving Chinese American scholarship student navigating the hyper-exclusive corridors of an elite Beijing international boarding school. Surrounded by ultra-wealthy peers who treat luxury as an afterthought, Alice is acutely aware of her financial precarity. She is the ultimate outsider, literally and metaphorically invisible to the elite ecosystem she inhabits. But when physical invisibility strikes—a literal manifestation of her marginalization—Alice seizes the opportunity. Teaming up with her infuriating academic rival, Henry Li, she transforms her bizarre affliction into a lucrative underground business, blackmailing and uncovering the scandalous secrets of her affluent classmates to pay her looming tuition fees.

What makes Liang’s novel truly stand out is its incisive exploration of its primary theme: the commodification of identity under late-stage capitalism. Alice’s invisibility is not just a quirky superpower; it is a brilliant metaphor for the immigrant and scholarship experience, where individuals from working-class backgrounds are often expected to work twice as hard while remaining unseen, valued only for their output rather than their humanity. By monetizing her ability to spy, Alice initially feels empowered, turning the tables on the privileged elite who ignore her. However, Liang deftly escalates the stakes, moving Alice from petty gossip to dangerous criminal enterprises. In doing so, the narrative tackles the slippery slope of ambition, forcing Alice—and the reader—to confront the moral compromise required to achieve the elusive American (or global elite) dream.

Liang’s authorial style is brisk, razor-sharp, and effortlessly engaging. Her prose crackles with kinetic energy, balancing cynical humor with genuine emotional vulnerability. The pacing mirrors a breathless thriller, keeping the pages turning as the assignments become more perilous. Yet, beneath the high-concept premise and the palpable chemistry of the academic-rivals-to-lovers dynamic, Liang anchors the story in authentic emotional truth. Alice is not a flawless heroine; she is desperate, proud, flawed, and fiercely resilient, making her journey all the more compelling. 

*If You Could See the Sun* is a dazzling, thought-provoking debut that cement Ann Liang as a formidable new voice in YA fiction. It captures the intoxicating rush of teenage ambition and the sobering realization that some victories cost too much. For readers who crave sharp wit, high-tension plotting, and a protagonist with both a formidable intellect and a fiercely beating heart, this novel is an absolute must-read.
