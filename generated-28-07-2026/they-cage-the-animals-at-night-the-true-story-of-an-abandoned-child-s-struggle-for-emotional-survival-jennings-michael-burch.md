# They Cage the Animals at Night: The True Story of an Abandoned Child's Struggle for Emotional Survival

**Author:** Jennings Michael Burch  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1984

**They Cage the Animals at Night: A Harrowing Journey Through the Foster Care System**

In the chilling opening of Jennings Michael Burch's memoir, "They Cage the Animals at Night," we are introduced to an eight-year-old boy left alone in an orphanage, his mother's promise of return echoing in the misty Brooklyn air. This heart-wrenching beginning sets the tone for Burch's raw and unflinching account of his journey through the American foster care system.

Burch's prose is stark and unadorned, much like the stark reality he faced as a child. He doesn't shy away from the brutal truths of his experiences, detailing the physical and emotional abuse he endured in a series of foster homes, orphanages, and institutions. The narrative is punctuated by moments of profound loneliness and despair, as Burch struggles to find a sense of belonging and love in a world that often seemed intent on breaking him.

Yet, amidst the darkness, there are glimmers of hope. Burch's resilience is astonishing, his spirit unbreakable. He finds solace in the most unlikely of places - a tattered stuffed animal named Doggie, a brief moment of kindness from a stranger, or the fleeting friendship with another child. These moments, though few and far between, are what keep him going, what allow him to hold onto the belief that he is worthy of love.

Burch's story is not just a personal testament; it's a searing indictment of a system that failed him and countless others like him. He exposes the cracks in the foster care system, the lack of oversight, and the devastating impact of constant upheaval on a child's psyche. His memoir serves as a stark reminder of the urgent need for reform, for better care, and for a system that truly prioritizes the well-being of its most vulnerable members.

Despite the heavy subject matter, "They Cage the Animals at Night" is not without its moments of levity. Burch's dry wit and dark humor shine through, providing a much-needed respite from the heaviness of his experiences. He has a unique ability to find the absurd in the tragic, to turn the mundane into the profound.

In the end, Burch's story is one of survival, of triumph over adversity. It's a testament to the human spirit's incredible capacity to endure, to heal, and to love. It's a story that will stay with you long after you've turned the last page, a story that demands to be told, and demands to be heard.
