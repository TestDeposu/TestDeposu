# We Are All Completely Beside Ourselves

**Author:** Karen Joy Fowler  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

In "We Are All Completely Beside Ourselves," Karen Joy Fowler delves into the complexities of family dynamics, identity, and the blurred lines between humans and animals. This poignant novel tells the story of the Cooke family, whose ordinary facade conceals a remarkable secret: they were once a family of four, including a chimpanzee named Fern.

Through the voice of Rosemary, the protagonist, Fowler masterfully weaves a narrative that is both deeply personal and universally relatable. Rosemary's story is a powerful exploration of the human condition, delving into themes of love, loss, and the search for identity. Her relationship with Fern, her chimpanzee sister, is particularly striking, raising questions about what it means to be human and the boundaries between species.

Fowler's writing style is both elegant and unflinching, tackling difficult topics with sensitivity and nuance. Her prose is evocative and engaging, drawing the reader into the world of the Cooke family with ease. The characters are multidimensional and complex, with flaws and strengths that make them feel remarkably real.

One of the most striking aspects of the novel is its exploration of the consequences of playing God. The Cooke family's decision to raise Fern as one of their own has far-reaching and devastating consequences, highlighting the dangers of scientific experimentation and the exploitation of animals. Through Rosemary's narrative, Fowler raises important questions about the ethics of such experiments and the impact they have on both humans and animals.

The novel is also a powerful exploration of the human need for connection and belonging. Rosemary's relationship with Fern is deeply moving, highlighting the ways in which we form bonds with others, regardless of species. The novel shows how these bonds can be both a source of strength and a source of pain, as Rosemary struggles to come to terms with the loss of her sister.

Throughout the novel, Fowler's use of language is precise and evocative, conjuring vivid images of the Cooke family's world. Her writing is both poetic and accessible, making the novel a joy to read. The characters are richly drawn, with distinct voices and perspectives that add depth and complexity to the narrative.

In short, "We Are All Completely Beside Ourselves" is a masterpiece of contemporary literature. It is a novel that will make you laugh and cry, think and feel. Fowler's writing is both beautiful and unflinching, tackling difficult topics with sensitivity and nuance. This is a novel that will stay with you long after you finish reading it, haunting your thoughts and challenging your assumptions.
