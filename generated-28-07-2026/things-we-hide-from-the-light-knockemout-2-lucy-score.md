# Things We Hide from the Light (Knockemout, #2)

**Author:** Lucy Score  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

In the bustling landscape of contemporary romance, few phenomena match the viral juggernaut that is Lucy Score’s Knockemout series. Following the massive success of Things We Never Got Over, the author returns to the quirky, tight-knit Virginia town with Things We Hide from the Light, shifting the spotlight from Knox and Naomi to a new, equally magnetic pairing. Striking a delicate balance between small-town charm, high-stakes suspense, and raw emotional vulnerability, Score delivers a poignant narrative that proves why she has captured the hearts of millions of readers across the globe.

At the heart of this second installment is a compelling exploration of hidden scars and the courage it takes to let someone in. The novel pairs Nash Morgan—the town’s golden-boy police chief, now reeling physically and psychologically from a near-fatal shooting—with Lina Solavita, a sharp-tongued, no-nonsense stranger carrying secrets of her own. Nash is a man defined by his duty, yet behind his heroic facade, he is quietly drowning in panic attacks, nightmares, and a profound sense of isolation. Lina, fiercely independent and allergic to traditional definitions of a good girl, is hiding behind a wall of sarcasm and an exit strategy. When these two opposing forces collide on a shared apartment landing, the result is a slow-burn collision of vulnerability and undeniable chemistry.

Score’s greatest strength in this novel lies in her handling of mental health and trauma. Romance novels often rush characters through their pain for the sake of the plot, but Score treats Nash’s PTSD with surprising gravity and tenderness. Watching a traditional "alpha hero" unravel, struggle, and ultimately accept help subverts romantic tropes in a refreshing way. Nash’s journey is not about being "fixed" by a woman, but rather about learning that sharing a burden doesn't make him weak. Similarly, Lina’s guarded nature is peeled back layer by layer, revealing a woman of fierce loyalty and deep empathy. Their dynamic crackles with witty banter while remaining anchored by a profound, mutual healing process.

True to the DNA of the Knockemout series, Score masterfully blends steamy romance with eccentric small-town comedy and a simmering subplot of lingering danger. The town of Knockemout functions almost as a character itself—meddling, fiercely protective, and overflowing with unforgettable eccentric personalities. The supporting cast steals scenes with effortless charm, ensuring that the pages turn rapidly. Score’s signature writing style is punchy, conversational, and deeply engaging, effortlessly oscillating between laugh-out-loud humor and heart-wrenching emotional beats. 

Things We Hide from the Light is more than just a satisfying sequel; it is a testament to the fact that true strength often lies in admitting our weaknesses. Lucy Score has crafted a comforting yet gripping romantic escape that delivers on every front, offering readers a potent mixture of danger, laughter, and deeply earned love.
