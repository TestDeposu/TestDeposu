# The Last Murder at the End of the World

**Author:** Stuart Turton  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2024

The Last Murder at the End of the World by Stuart Turton is an inventive and high-concept murder mystery that will keep you on the edge of your seat. The story takes place in a post-apocalyptic world where a deadly fog has destroyed everything outside a small island, leaving 122 villagers and three scientists to live in a peaceful yet controlled environment. However, when one of the scientists is found brutally stabbed to death, the island's fragile harmony is disrupted, and the security system that protects them from the fog is compromised.

What follows is a gripping tale of survival, memory, and detection, as the islanders must work together to solve the murder before the security system fails and the fog engulfs the island. The twist? Everyone's memories of the night before the murder have been wiped, making it impossible for the murderer to know their own guilt. The clock is ticking, and the islanders have only 107 hours to unravel the mystery and save themselves.

Turton's writing style is reminiscent of Sherlock Holmes, with its clever plot twists, ingenious puzzles, and an extraordinary backdrop. His use of language is vivid and immersive, drawing the reader into the world of the island and its inhabitants. The characters are well-developed and complex, with each one bringing their own unique perspective to the investigation.

One of the main themes of the book is the importance of memory and how it shapes our identity. The islanders' memories are wiped, leaving them with no recollection of their past or their actions. This raises questions about the nature of self and how we define ourselves. Are we our memories, or are we something more?

The book also explores the theme of control and power. The scientists on the island have complete control over the villagers, dictating their every move and decision. However, when the murder occurs, this control is disrupted, and the villagers are forced to take matters into their own hands. This shift in power dynamics creates tension and conflict, as the villagers struggle to assert their independence.

Turton's style is audacious and inventive, pushing the boundaries of the murder mystery genre. He weaves together multiple plot threads, creating a complex and engaging narrative that keeps the reader guessing until the very end. The solution to the mystery is both surprising and satisfying, making the book a must-read for fans of the genre.

In conclusion, The Last Murder at the End of the World is a gripping and inventive murder mystery that will appeal to fans of Sherlock Holmes and Agatha Christie. With its clever plot twists, complex characters, and thought-provoking themes, this book is a must-read for anyone who loves a good puzzle. So, if you're looking for a book that will challenge your mind and keep you on the edge of your seat, look no further.
