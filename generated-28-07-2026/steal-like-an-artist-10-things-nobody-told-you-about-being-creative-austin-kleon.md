# Steal Like an Artist: 10 Things Nobody Told You About Being Creative

**Author:** Austin Kleon  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

In a culture obsessed with the impossible myth of pure originality, Austin Kleon’s 2012 manifesto, Steal Like an Artist, arrives like a refreshing dash of cold water to the face of every aspiring creator. Far from a dense, academic treatise on artistic theory, Kleon’s breakthrough book operates as a punchy, highly visual, and fiercely pragmatic manual for making things in the digital age. Across ten deceptively simple principles, Kleon dismantles the paralyzing pressure of total invention and replaces it with a liberating truth: nothing is original, so you might as well embrace the influences that shape you.

The core theme of the book hinges on a re-framing of the word "steal." Rather than advocating for plagiarism, Kleon uses the term in the tradition of Pablo Picasso and T.S. Eliot, suggesting that artists are merely collectors of ideas, filters through which the world is re-imagined. He invites readers to study their heroes, reverse-engineer their work, and openly acknowledge their lineage. By looking at creativity not as a magical lightning strike, but as a remix of existing cultural DNA, Kleon successfully lowers the barrier to entry for anyone who has ever felt too ordinary to be an artist. 

Kleon’s authorial style is arguably his greatest creation. The book reads less like a traditional paperback and more like a zine—a chaotic, beautiful collage of bold typography, handwritten notes, striking bullet points, and minimalist illustrations. It is a text designed to be consumed in a single sitting, yet pondered over a lifetime. His prose is brisk, aphoristic, and relentlessly encouraging. He speaks directly to the reader with the tough-love warmth of a seasoned mentor, cutting through self-doubt with memorable maxims like "write the book you want to read" and "school yourself." 

Beyond the philosophy of borrowing, Kleon roots his advice in the unglamorous realities of daily life. He tackles subjects that most creative guides conveniently ignore: the necessity of maintaining a day job, the importance of staying out of debt to protect your artistic freedom, and the surprising power of embracing a boring, routine everyday existence so that your imagination can run wild on the page or canvas. He reminds us that hobbies aren’t distractions; they are often the secret laboratory where your life's true work is quietly formulated.

Ultimately, Steal Like an Artist is not just a book about art; it is a book about permission. It grants the reader permission to love what others dismiss, permission to copy until they find their own voice, and permission to start before they feel ready. In an era paralyzed by perfectionism and digital comparison, Kleon’s energetic manifesto remains an essential, bite-sized compass for anyone looking to unlock their inner creator and finally put something meaningful out into the world.
