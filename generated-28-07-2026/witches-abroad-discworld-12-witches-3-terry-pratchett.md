# Witches Abroad (Discworld, #12; Witches, #3)

**Author:** Terry Pratchett  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

In the enchanting realm of Discworld, Terry Pratchett weaves a captivating tale of magic, mayhem, and the unlikeliest of heroes in Witches Abroad. This 12th installment of the beloved series whisks readers away on a thrilling adventure, as three inimitable witches – Magrat Garlick, Granny Weatherwax, and Nanny Ogg – embark on a perilous quest to ensure the happiness of a young servant girl, Emberella, in the far-off land of Genua.

At its core, Witches Abroad is a scathing satire of traditional fairy tales, cleverly subverting the notion of a 'happy ending' and the often-damaging expectations that accompany it. Pratchett masterfully exposes the darker side of these ostensibly idyllic stories, revealing the sinister forces that can drive the pursuit of a 'perfect' conclusion. Through the witches' journey, he highlights the importance of individual agency, self-determination, and the dangers of meddling with the fate of others.

One of Pratchett's greatest strengths as a writer lies in his ability to craft characters that are at once relatable, flawed, and utterly fascinating. Magrat, Granny, and Nanny are a triumvirate of distinct personalities, each bringing their unique brand of wit, wisdom, and witchcraft to the table. As they navigate the treacherous landscape of Genua, they must confront their own biases, prejudices, and limitations, making for a richly nuanced and humorous exploration of female friendship and empowerment.

Pratchett's writing is, as ever, a marvel of clever wordplay, absurd humor, and ingenious plotting. His prose is akin to a rich tapestry, woven from threads of clever satire, social commentary, and fantastical invention. The world of Discworld is a place where vampires, werewolves, and witches coexist with humans, and Pratchett's masterful handling of these disparate elements creates a sense of depth and history that is nothing short of remarkable.

Throughout Witches Abroad, Pratchett's signature wit and irreverence are on full display, as he lampoons everything from fairy tale tropes to the conventions of storytelling itself. His use of metafictional devices – such as the witches' self-aware commentary on the nature of their quest – adds an extra layer of cleverness to the narrative, blurring the lines between reality and fiction.

In conclusion, Witches Abroad is a sidesplitting, thought-provoking, and deeply satisfying addition to the Discworld canon. With its clever subversion of fairy tale conventions, memorable characters, and trademark Pratchett wit, this novel is sure to delight both longtime fans of the series and newcomers alike. So, saddle up your broomstick and join the witches on their unforgettable journey to Genua – but be warned: the road to 'happily ever after' is rarely straightforward, and the consequences of meddling with fate can be disastrous.
