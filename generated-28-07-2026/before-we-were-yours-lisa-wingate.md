# Before We Were Yours

**Author:** Lisa Wingate  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Unearthing the darkest corners of American history requires a delicate touch, a fierce sense of justice, and a storyteller capable of breathing soul into forgotten tragedies. In her blockbuster bestseller, *Before We Were Yours*, Lisa Wingate achieves precisely this, delivering a riveting, emotional powerhouse that exposes one of the most depraved adoption scandals of the twentieth century. Based on the horrifying real-life crimes of Georgia Tann and the Tennessee Children’s Home Society, Wingate’s 2017 novel is not merely a historical excavation; it is a testament to the unbreakable bonds of family and the haunting endurance of the human spirit.

Wingate structures her narrative using a dual-timeline approach, shifting effortlessly between Memphis in 1939 and the present day in Aiken, South Carolina. In the historical thread, we meet twelve-year-old Rill Foss, whose idyllic childhood aboard her family’s Mississippi River shantyboat is shattered in a single night. Left temporarily in charge of her four younger siblings while her parents seek emergency medical care, Rill is targeted by predatory authorities. Wrenched from everything she knows, she and her siblings are plunged into the terrifying, sterile reality of the orphanage, where children of the destitute are systematically commodified, stripped of their identities, and sold to the highest-bidding elite.

Parallel to Rill’s harrowing survival is the modern-day journey of Avery Stafford. Born into a dynasty of wealth and political privilege, Avery seemingly has the world at his or her feet—a thriving career as a federal prosecutor, an enviable social standing, and a storybook wedding ahead. Yet, returning home to assist her ailing father, a chance encounter with an elderly stranger plants a seed of doubt. This catalyst propels Avery down a rabbit hole of long-buried family secrets, bridging the chasm between two seemingly unrelated worlds and forcing a reckoning with a past built on stolen lives.

Wingate’s greatest triumph lies in her masterful handling of theme and style. The central theme of the novel pivots on the contrast between institutional cruelty and fierce, familial loyalty. Throughout the narrative, Wingate explores how identity is forged not by wealth, status, or legal decree, but by the indelible connections of the heart. Rill’s fierce, desperate protection of her younger siblings forms the emotional anchor of the book. Even as the orphanage seeks to break their spirits through isolation, abuse, and psychological manipulation, the children cling to the memory of the river, their parents, and each other. Wingate contrasts this with Avery’s trajectory, exploring how privilege can sometimes blind us to systemic injustice, and how confronting uncomfortable truths is the only true path to redemption.

Stylistically, Wingate writes with a lyrical urgency that makes the pages turn themselves. Her prose is deeply atmospheric, capturing the sensory details of the shantyboat life—the gentle lapping of the river, the scent of pine and river mud—only to sharply pivot to the chilling, antiseptic cruelty of the orphanage corridors. This sharp tonal contrast mirrors the disruption in the characters' lives. Wingate does not shy away from the grim realities of Tann’s operation, yet she refuses to let despair have the final word. Instead, she infuses the narrative with a profound sense of resilience, ensuring that the victims are remembered not merely as casualties of a corrupt system, but as survivors whose stories demanded to be told.

*Before We Were Yours* is more than a historical fiction page-turner; it is an emotional reckoning that exposes how easily the vulnerable can be exploited when unchecked power meets systemic apathy. Wingate has crafted a timeless, poignant masterpiece that lingers in the mind long after the final page is turned, reminding us that no matter how deeply the past is buried, the heart never truly forgets where it belongs.
