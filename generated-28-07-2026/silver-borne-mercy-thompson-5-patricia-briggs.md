# Silver Borne (Mercy Thompson, #5)

**Author:** Patricia Briggs  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

In the fifth installment of Patricia Briggs' Mercy Thompson series, "Silver Borne" delves into the world of magic, mystery, and mayhem, as the protagonist navigates a complex web of Fae politics and ancient secrets. The story is a gripping blend of action, suspense, and humor, showcasing Briggs' masterful storytelling and character development.

The novel revolves around Mercy's attempt to return a powerful Fae book, which she had borrowed in a moment of desperation. However, the bookstore is closed, and the Fae are determined to keep the book's secrets buried. As Mercy becomes embroiled in a deadly game of cat and mouse, she must also contend with her friend Samuel's struggles with his werewolf side. The stakes are high, and Mercy's life hangs in the balance.

One of the standout features of "Silver Borne" is its exploration of the Fae world, a realm that is both fascinating and treacherous. Briggs' depiction of the Fae is nuanced and multifaceted, revealing the intricate hierarchies and motivations that drive these enigmatic creatures. The world-building is meticulous, with a richly detailed landscape that draws the reader in.

Mercy, as always, is a compelling protagonist, with her quick wit, sharp instincts, and unwavering loyalty. Her character continues to evolve, as she faces new challenges and confronts her own vulnerabilities. The supporting cast is equally well-crafted, with Samuel's struggles adding an emotional depth to the narrative.

Briggs' writing style is engaging and accessible, with a keen sense of pacing and tension. The action sequences are well-choreographed, and the dialogue is snappy and realistic. The author's use of humor is also noteworthy, as she deftly injects moments of levity into the narrative, often at the most unexpected times.

The themes of "Silver Borne" are equally impressive, as Briggs explores the complexities of loyalty, duty, and the blurred lines between right and wrong. The novel raises important questions about the nature of power and the consequences of playing with forces beyond one's control.

In conclusion, "Silver Borne" is a captivating addition to the Mercy Thompson series, with its rich world-building, compelling characters, and breakneck pacing. Briggs' masterful storytelling and nuanced exploration of the Fae world make this novel a must-read for fans of urban fantasy and paranormal fiction.
