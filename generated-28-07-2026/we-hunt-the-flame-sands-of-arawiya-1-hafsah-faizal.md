# We Hunt the Flame (Sands of Arawiya, #1)

**Author:** Hafsah Faizal  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

In her dazzling 2019 debut, We Hunt the Flame, Hafsah Faizal invites readers into the shimmering, perilous dunes of Arawiya, a fantasy realm breathing with ancient Arabian-inspired myth and modern emotional resonance. As the first installment in the Sands of Arawiya duology, this critically acclaimed novel—lauded by TIME Magazine and prominent YA book clubs alike—distinguishes itself not merely through its atmospheric world-building, but through a deeply introspective exploration of identity, expectation, and the courage it takes to unearth one's true self beneath layers of imposed legend.

At the heart of Faizal’s narrative lies a profound exploration of duality and disguise. The story introduces us to two formidable figures whose very lives are defined by what they must hide. Zafira, known to her people as the Hunter, braves the encroaching, soul-consuming shadows of the cursed Arz forest disguised as a man, providing sustenance for her village while suppressing her true identity in a patriarchal society that would weaponize her gender against her. Conversely, Nasir, the feared Prince of Death, serves as the brutal executioner for his tyrannical father, burying his innate compassion beneath a shroud of blood and terror to survive his father's sadistic whims. 

Faizal uses these dual protagonists to construct a brilliant thematic contrast between survival and living. Both characters are trapped by the weight of their reputations; Zafira and Nasir are living myths, yet both despise the legends that swallow them whole. The central thesis of the novel hinges on the dangerous intersection of duty and autonomy. When Zafira embarks on a desperate quest to restore lost magic to Arawiya, and Nasir is dispatched by the sultan to retrieve the same artifact and eliminate the Hunter, their collision course becomes a metaphorical shedding of armor. Their journey into the unknown is not just about saving a dying world, but about stripping away the expectations of tyrants, crowds, and cultures to discover who they are when no one is watching.

Stylistically, Faizal’s prose is lush, sensory, and immersive. She writes with a cinematic eye, crafting a landscape where the heat of the desert sands, the chill of the creeping Arz, and the shimmer of ancient artifacts feel viscerally alive. The pacing mirrors the treacherous terrain of Arawiya—shifting between breathless tension, political intrigue, and quiet, agonizing moments of vulnerability. Faizal excels at atmosphere; the world of Arawiya is not a static backdrop, but an active, breathing entity that mirrors the internal corruption and magical decay of its inhabitants. Furthermore, the slow-burn dynamic between Zafira and Nasir is handled with exquisite emotional intelligence, anchored by biting banter and a mutual recognition of shared burdens. 

Ultimately, We Hunt the Flame succeeds because it balances grand, sweeping fantasy stakes with deeply intimate character arcs. Faizal crafts a narrative that asks what it truly means to be brave in a world that demands conformity and cruelty. For readers seeking an intoxicating blend of rich cultural mythology, high-stakes adventure, and a masterclass in enemies-to-lovers tension, Faizal’s debut is an unputdownable triumph that cements her as a vital voice in modern fantasy.
