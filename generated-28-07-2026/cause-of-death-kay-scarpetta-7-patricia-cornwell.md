# Cause of Death (Kay Scarpetta, #7)

**Author:** Patricia Cornwell  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1996

In the seventh installment of Patricia Cornwell's iconic Kay Scarpetta series, Cause of Death is a gripping thriller that masterfully weaves together a complex web of danger, deception, and death. Set against the ominous backdrop of Virginia's bloodiest year since the Civil War, the story unfolds with a sense of foreboding and tension that is quintessentially Cornwell.

At the heart of the novel is Dr. Kay Scarpetta, the brilliant and unflappable forensic pathologist who has become synonymous with the genre. Her latest case begins on New Year's Eve, with the recovery of investigative reporter Ted Eddings' remains from the Elizabeth River. As Scarpetta delves deeper into the mystery, she is confronted with a labyrinthine plot that threatens to destroy not only her own life but also those of her loved ones.

Cornwell's writing is, as always, meticulous and evocative, conjuring the dark and foreboding atmosphere of the Elizabeth River with ease. Her use of language is concise and potent, making the gruesome details of the crime scene both chilling and mesmerizing. The author's trademark attention to detail is evident throughout the novel, from the intricate descriptions of forensic procedures to the nuanced portrayals of her characters.

One of the standout aspects of Cause of Death is its exploration of the intersection of technology and old-fashioned detective work. As Scarpetta navigates the treacherous world of cutting-edge science and ancient human nature, she must rely on her instincts and expertise to unravel the tangled threads of the case. Cornwell's portrayal of this fusion of old and new is both thought-provoking and fascinating, highlighting the tension between progress and tradition.

The characters in Cause of Death are, as always, multidimensional and richly drawn. Scarpetta's relationships with her niece Lucy and police captain Pete Marino are particularly compelling, offering a glimpse into the personal lives of these complex characters. The dynamic between Scarpetta and Marino is especially noteworthy, their banter and repartee providing a welcome respite from the darkness of the plot.

Throughout the novel, Cornwell expertly ratchets up the tension, culminating in a shocking and suspenseful climax. The author's ability to craft a narrative that is both propulsive and intelligent is a hallmark of her writing style, making Cause of Death a true page-turner.

In conclusion, Cause of Death is a gripping and unsettling thriller that showcases Patricia Cornwell's mastery of the genre. With its intricate plot, well-developed characters, and atmospheric setting, this novel is a must-read for fans of crime fiction. As Jamie Lee Curtis so aptly puts it, "there WILL be BLOOD" – and plenty of it.
