# Smoke Gets in Your Eyes & Other Lessons from the Crematory

**Author:** Caitlin Doughty  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

Death is the ultimate taboo in modern Western society. We sanitize it, hide it behind hospital curtains, and outsource the remnants of our mortality to strangers in matching suits. But what happens when someone decides to pull back the velvet curtain and stare directly into the furnace? Enter Caitlin Doughty and her brilliant, groundbreaking 2014 memoir, Smoke Gets in Your Eyes & Other Lessons from the Crematory. 

Armed with a degree in medieval history and an unapologetic fascination with the macabre, a young Doughty stepped straight out of college and into the belly of the beast: a crematory. Far from the solemn, sterile environments depicted in television dramas, her workplace was a strange, industrial purgatory where human remains were processed with a mix of mechanical grit and profound humanity. 

The central theme of Doughty’s debut is not merely about the mechanics of death, but rather how our cultural phobia of mortality warps our society. By shielding ourselves from the reality of corpses, grief, and decay, we have surrendered our dead to a multi-billion-dollar death industry that prioritizes expensive cosmetic preservation over emotional acceptance. Doughty argues passionately and persuasively for a return to a death-positive culture—one where we reclaim our dead, accept our physical impermanence, and shed the sanitized lies we tell ourselves about the end of life.

What makes Smoke Gets in Your Eyes an unforgettable read, however, is not just its polemical stance, but the singular voice of its author. Doughty writes with a razor-sharp, gallows humor that acts as a protective shield against the grim realities of her daily grind. She recounts bizarre workplace anecdotes, gruesome historical practices of the undertaking profession, and her own awkward coming-of-age moments with equal parts mordant wit and tender empathy. Her prose is vividly cinematic, pulling the reader through the crematory doors and forcing them to confront the exact things they have been conditioned to avoid. 

Yet, beneath the dark comedy and fascinating historical digressions lies a deeply uplifting human story. Doughty’s journey from wide-eyed novice to fierce advocate is transformative. She learns to view the deceased not as grotesque objects of horror, but as vessels of human history deserving of dignity and respect. In doing so, she discovers a profound sense of purpose.

Smoke Gets in Your Eyes is a triumphant, illuminating, and wildly entertaining wake-up call. It challenges our deepest discomforts and demands that we rethink how our culture treats the dead, and by extension, how we choose to live. Doughty has written an essential manual for the mortal coil, proving that the best way to conquer our fear of dying is to simply look it in the eye.
