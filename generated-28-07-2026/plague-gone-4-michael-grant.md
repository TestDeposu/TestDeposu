# Plague (Gone, #4)

**Author:** Michael Grant  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

**Plague (Gone, #4) by Michael Grant: A Chilling Tale of Survival and Decay**

In the gripping fourth installment of Michael Grant's acclaimed Gone series, 'Plague', the author masterfully weaves a tale of desperation, power, and the insidious nature of disease. Set in the eerie FAYZ, a dome-like barrier that has isolated the town of Perdido Beach from the rest of the world, 'Plague' delves into the heart of darkness that has taken root in this once-idyllic community.

The story opens with a terrifying new threat: a virulent cough that reduces its victims to a grotesque parody of life, choking them from the inside out. As the illness sweeps through the town, panic and chaos ensue, and the fragile society that has emerged within the FAYZ begins to crumble. Meanwhile, Little Pete, the most powerful mutant in the FAYZ, lies unconscious, his fate uncertain. With Pete out of action, the future of the world hangs in the balance, and the power vacuum left in his wake draws old enemies and new threats from the shadows.

At the heart of 'Plague' is the theme of decay, both physical and moral. Grant paints a vivid and unsettling picture of a town ravaged by disease, where the once-vibrant streets now echo with the hacking coughs of the infected. But the decay goes deeper than the physical; it seeps into the very soul of the FAYZ, corrupting the ideals and values of its inhabitants. As the society fragments, we are reminded of the darkest aspects of human nature, and the lengths to which people will go to survive.

Grant's writing style is as infectious as the plague that ravages Perdido Beach. His prose is lean and muscular, driving the narrative forward with a relentless energy that is as exhilarating as it is exhausting. He has a knack for creating vivid, unforgettable characters, each with their own unique voice and perspective. From the stoic and steadfast Sam Temple to the cunning and ruthless Caine, Grant's characters are complex and compelling, their actions and motivations often morally ambiguous.

One of the most striking aspects of 'Plague' is Grant's exploration of power and its corrupting influence. As the illness sweeps through the town, so too does the power vacuum left by Little Pete's incapacitation. Old enemies and new threats emerge from the shadows, each vying for control of the FAYZ. Grant masterfully explores the ways in which power can corrupt, and the lengths to which people will go to maintain it.

'Plague' is not for the faint of heart. It is a brutal, unflinching look at the darkest aspects of human nature, and the lengths to which we will go to survive. It is a testament to Grant's skill as a storyteller that he can make such a grim tale so compelling, so impossible to put down. 'Plague' is a chilling reminder that in the face of adversity, we are all capable of both great heroism and great cruelty.
