# The Fairy-Tale Detectives (The Sisters Grimm, #1)

**Author:** Michael Buckley  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

What if the fairy tales we grew up reading were not mere flights of fancy, but documented history? In Michael Buckley’s captivating 2017 anniversary edition of The Fairy-Tale Detectives, the inaugural installment of the New York Times bestselling Sisters Grimm series, this tantalizing premise transforms into a breathless, madcap adventure that breathes entirely new life into classic folklore. 

At its core, the novel explores the theme of legacy and the reimagining of identity. The story follows orphaned sisters Sabrina and Daphne, who are abruptly uprooted and sent to live with their eccentric, long-lost grandmother, Relda Grimm. They arrive in the seemingly quaint town of Ferryport Landing, only to discover a staggering family secret: they are direct descendants of the Brothers Grimm. More astonishingly, the famous book of fairy tales is no collection of children’s stories; it is a meticulously recorded history book, and its legendary inhabitants—ranging from reformed villains to bitter former royals—are very much alive, hiding in plain sight among unsuspecting humans. 

Buckley’s thematic brilliance lies in how he fractures these familiar archetypes, taking beloved childhood figures and viewing them through a gritty, modern lens. Prince Charming is no longer a shimmering symbol of romantic idealism; instead, he is a corrupt, power-hungry mayor desperate to reclaim his lost kingdom. The Three Little Pigs have traded in their straw and brick houses for police badges, operating as shifty town cops. Snow White and Jack the Giant Killer lurk in the shadows with hidden motives. By subverting these iconic characters, Buckley crafts a rich narrative about the complexities of morality. Heroes can harbor dark resentments, and monsters can possess unexpected depth, forcing young protagonists—and readers—to question the absolute binaries of good and evil. 

Stylistically, Buckley writes with an infectious energy that is tailor-made for middle-grade readers, yet sophisticated enough to hook adults. His narrative style is fast-paced, propulsive, and cinematic. He balances moments of genuine peril with sharp, situational humor, ensuring that the tension never becomes too heavy for younger audiences while maintaining high stakes. When a terrifying giant goes on a rampage through Ferryport Landing, the investigation transforms into a high-stakes whodunit. Buckley excels at pacing out clues, turning the sleepy town into a labyrinth of magical suspects and municipal corruption. 

Furthermore, the author’s stylistic voice shines through the clever integration of vocabulary lessons woven seamlessly into the dialogue and narration. Rather than feeling like a chore, these educational elements enhance the book’s charm, highlighting Sabrina’s sharp intellect and the sisters' determination to survive in a world far more dangerous than any textbook could prepare them for. The dynamic between the sisters—the cynical, protective Sabrina and the delightfully fearless, eccentric Daphne—anchors the chaotic magical world with emotional resonance and sisterly warmth. 

The 2017 paperback repackaging serves as a brilliant gateway. With fresh cover art, it invites both nostalgic fans to revisit the cobblestone streets of Ferryport Landing and a brand-new generation to step into a universe where happily ever after is hard-won and endlessly complicated. 

The Fairy-Tale Detectives is a triumph of modern fantasy, blending mystery, folklore, and heartfelt family dynamics into a mesmerizing page-turner. Buckley proves that the best way to honor a classic tale is to turn it upside down, shake it out, and watch the magic fall where it may.
