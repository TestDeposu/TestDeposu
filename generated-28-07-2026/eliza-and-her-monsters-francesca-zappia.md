# Eliza and Her Monsters

**Author:** Francesca Zappia  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Eliza and Her Monsters, written by Francesca Zappia, is a captivating novel that delves into the intricate world of online fandom, creativity, and the struggles of adolescence. This book is a must-read for anyone who has ever felt like they don't quite fit in, and for those who have found solace in the world of art and storytelling.

The story revolves around Eliza Mirk, a shy and introverted eighteen-year-old who is the anonymous creator of the wildly popular webcomic Monstrous Sea. Online, Eliza is known as LadyConstellation, and she has built a massive following and a sense of community that she can't find in her real life. However, when Wallace Warland transfers to her school, Eliza begins to question whether a life offline might be worthwhile. As their relationship blossoms, Eliza's secret is accidentally shared with the world, and everything she's built begins to crumble.

One of the most striking aspects of this novel is its exploration of the tension between online and offline identities. Eliza's struggles to reconcile her two personas are deeply relatable, and Zappia's portrayal of the complexities of online fame is both nuanced and thought-provoking. The inclusion of pages from Eliza's webcomic and screenshots from her online forums adds a unique and engaging layer to the narrative, making the reader feel like they are experiencing the story firsthand.

Zappia's writing style is witty, insightful, and engaging, making it easy to become fully immersed in Eliza's world. The characters are well-developed and complex, with Eliza being a particularly compelling protagonist. Her struggles to find her voice and assert her identity are deeply moving, and her relationships with those around her are authentic and relatable.

The themes of art, fandom, and creativity are woven throughout the narrative, and Zappia's love letter to the world of storytelling is evident on every page. The novel is a celebration of the power of art to bring people together and to provide a sense of community and belonging. It's also a thoughtful exploration of the complexities of online fandom, and the ways in which it can both unite and isolate people.

Overall, Eliza and Her Monsters is a captivating and thought-provoking novel that will resonate with anyone who has ever felt like they don't quite fit in. It's a must-read for fans of Rainbow Rowell's Fangirl and Noelle Stevenson's Nimona, and for anyone who has ever found solace in the world of art and storytelling.
