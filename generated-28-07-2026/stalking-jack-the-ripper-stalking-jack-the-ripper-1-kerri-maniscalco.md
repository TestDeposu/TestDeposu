# Stalking Jack the Ripper (Stalking Jack the Ripper, #1)

**Author:** Kerri Maniscalco  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

Step into the fog-shrouded, gaslit streets of Victorian London, where the cobblestones run slick with secrets and the shadow of history’s most infamous monster looms large. In her dazzling #1 New York Times bestselling debut, Stalking Jack the Ripper, author Kerri Maniscalco breathes terrifying new life into a century-old mystery. This deliciously haunting and atmospheric novel is not merely a retelling of a gruesome true-crime horror story; it is a masterclass in subverting expectations, blending historical fiction, forensics, and a pulse-pounding gothic romance into an unforgettable literary cocktail.

At the beating heart of Maniscalco’s narrative is Audrey Rose Wadsworth, a seventeen-year-old protagonist who immediately shatters the rigid constraints of her aristocratic upbringing. Born a lord's daughter with a gilded, predictable future of social teas and silk dress fittings mapped out before her, Audrey possesses a mind far too sharp and an ambition far too dark for polite society. Against her stern father's strict wishes and the suffocating expectations of Victorian propriety, she leads a thrilling, forbidden secret life, slipping away to her uncle’s laboratory to study the gruesome, fascinating practice of forensic medicine. Maniscalco uses Audrey Rose to explore the central theme of female autonomy and rebellion in a patriarchal world. While high-society women of the era were expected to be delicate ornaments, Audrey wields a bone saw with precision, proving that intellect and blood have no gender. Her struggle to bridge the gap between the corseted expectations of the upper crust and the unvarnished brutality of the morgue forms the emotional anchor of the book.

The author’s style is a sumptuous feast of sensory details, rich with period-accurate dread. Maniscalco does not shy away from the macabre; instead, she leans into it, capturing the stench of Victorian slums, the cold metallic gleam of dissection tools, and the oppressive, damp London fog that seems to swallow truth and justice whole. What elevates the novel from a standard historical thriller is the inclusion of real, sinister period photographs peppered throughout the text. This brilliant stylistic choice blurs the line between fiction and historical reality, plunging the reader deeper into the unsettling atmosphere of 1888 Whitechapel. 

When Audrey Rose's forensic work on a string of savagely killed corpses drags her directly into the police investigation of the Ripper murders, the narrative shifts into overdrive. The hunt for the killer is not just an intellectual puzzle; it becomes a deeply personal nightmare that threatens to tear apart Audrey’s sheltered world and expose the dark underbelly of the British aristocracy. Along the way, Maniscalco crafts a delicious intellectual and romantic sparring partner for her protagonist—a cynical, arrogant, yet utterly charming fellow apprentice whose sharp tongue matches Audrey's own. Their sizzling chemistry adds a layer of sharp wit and emotional tension that balances the pervasive gloom of the murders.

Stalking Jack the Ripper succeeds because it respects its readers' intelligence while delivering pure, unadulterated genre thrills. Maniscalco expertly paces the investigation, planting clever red herrings and building a mounting sense of claustrophobia that culminates in an unexpected, blood-chilling conclusion. It is a stunning debut that proves horror and history can dance together in the most mesmerizing of ways, leaving readers desperate to turn the page while glancing nervously over their shoulders into the dark.
