# She Gets the Girl

**Author:** Rachael Lippincott  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

If you mix the classic, high-stakes matchmaking energy of She is All That with the sweet, contemporary awkwardness of What If It is Us, you get Rachael Lippincott and Alyson Derrick’s magnetic 2022 young adult rom-com, She Gets the Girl. Co-authored by Lippincott alongside debut writer Derrick, the novel delivers a vibrant, fast-paced exploration of young love, identity, and unexpected connection. At its core, the book tackles the timeless theme of vulnerability—specifically, how terrifying it is to drop our carefully constructed armor and let someone see the messy truth underneath. 

The story hinges on a classic romance trope: the collision of polar opposites. On one side, we have Alex Blackwood—a chaotic, unapologetically flirtatious whirlwind who excels at the thrill of the chase but stumbles miserably when it comes to long-term commitment. On the other side is Molly Parker, a meticulously organized introvert whose life is entirely under control, save for her debilitating inability to speak to her long-time crush, the effortlessly cool Cora Myers. When fate forces these two vastly different college freshmen into an unlikely alliance, they forge a pact. Alex will teach Molly how to win over Cora, thereby proving to her own recent ex that she is capable of real romance. Molly, desperate and hopelessly inept when it comes to romance, reluctantly agrees. 

What follows is a delightful five-step plan that inevitably derails in the most satisfying ways possible. The primary theme of the book anchors itself on the juxtaposition of public personas versus private realities. Both Alex and Molly are hiding behind masks: Alex uses charm and surface-level confidence to avoid the pain of abandonment, while Molly uses rigid control and isolation to protect herself from rejection. As they coach each other through their respective romantic pursuits, Lippincott and Derrick brilliantly deconstruct these defenses. The narrative gently argues that true intimacy cannot begin until we stop performing and start being honest—not just with the objects of our affection, but with ourselves. 

The authors stylistic approach is one of the book's greatest strengths. Told through dual perspectives, the narrative vibrates with authentic teenage angst, sharp wit, and palpable chemistry. Lippincott and Derrick employ a snappy, conversational prose style that makes the pages fly by. The alternating viewpoints between Alex and Molly allow readers to intimately experience the contrast between Alex is loud, defensive bravado and Molly is quiet, overthinking internal landscape. This dual lens not only deepens character empathy but also creates a delicious dramatic irony; readers will find themselves yelling at the page as the protagonists meticulously execute plans to win over the wrong people, all while accidentally falling for the right one. 

Furthermore, the pacing is expertly tuned to the rhythm of the modern college experience. The setting of a sprawling university campus serves as a crucible for reinvention, making it the ideal backdrop for characters trying to figure out who they are outside of the confines of high school. The dialogue crackles with realistic banter, and the emotional beats—ranging from laugh-out-loud awkward encounters to tender moments of self-realization—land with genuine impact. 

She Gets the Girl is more than just a sweet, predictable queer rom-com. It is a striking critique of the ways we sabotage our own happiness out of fear, wrapped in an irresistibly charming package. Lippincott and Derrick have crafted a story that honors the messy, confusing, and ultimately beautiful journey of first love. For anyone who has ever felt too much or too little, who has fumbled for words in front of a crush, or who has used a wall of jokes or schedules to keep the world at bay, this novel offers both a mirror and a warm embrace. It is a triumphant, feel-good read that proves sometimes the person you need was right in front of you the entire time, even if you started out hating them.
