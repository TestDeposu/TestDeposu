# Crier's War (Crier's War, #1)

**Author:** Nina Varela  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

**Crier's War: A Symphony of Love, Power, and Revolution**

In her debut novel, Nina Varela weaves a captivating tale of love, power, and rebellion in a world where the boundaries between humanity and creation blur. "Crier's War" is a mesmerizing blend of political intrigue, forbidden love, and a thought-provoking exploration of identity and freedom, all set against a richly imagined backdrop reminiscent of Marie Rutkoski's "The Winner's Curse" and the intricate world-building of "Game of Thrones."

At the heart of the story lies a forbidden love between Ayla, a human servant with a heart full of vengeance, and Crier, a Made—an Automae designed to serve the royals, but now ruling over humans. Varela masterfully crafts these two characters, each grappling with their own demons and societal expectations. Ayla, driven by a thirst for revenge, is a complex heroine whose moral compass is not always steadfast, making her relatable and intriguing. Crier, meanwhile, is a refreshing take on the 'princess in distress' trope, her journey from naive to empowered a compelling narrative arc.

Varela's prose is elegant and evocative, painting vivid images of the kingdom of Rabu and its inhabitants. She deftly balances the intricate political machinations with tender, intimate moments between Ayla and Crier, creating a tension that keeps the reader on the edge of their seat. The author's ability to weave together multiple plot threads—from Ayla's quest for vengeance, to Crier's discovery of her father's true nature, to the brewing human unrest—is a testament to her skill as a storyteller.

"Crier's War" is not just a love story; it's a commentary on power dynamics, societal norms, and the human condition. Varela challenges the reader to question what it means to be human, to be free, and to love. She subverts traditional tropes, offering a refreshing take on the classic 'enemies to lovers' trope, and explores the complexities of love in a world where one's very existence is a political statement.

The novel's pacing is near-perfect, building steadily towards a climax that is both satisfying and heart-wrenching. Varela leaves enough threads hanging to ensure readers are eager for the sequel, but each chapter feels complete in itself, making "Crier's War" a standalone story that stands out in the epic fantasy genre.

In a world filled with clichés, "Crier's War" is a breath of fresh air. It's a story that will captivate you, make you question your beliefs, and leave you yearning for more. Nina Varela is a voice to watch in the world of epic fantasy, and "Crier's War" is a remarkable debut that promises great things to come.
