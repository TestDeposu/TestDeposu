# Unoffendable: How Just One Change Can Make All of Life Better

**Author:** Brant Hansen  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

Unoffendable: How Just One Change Can Make All of Life Better by Brant Hansen arrives at a moment when the cultural temperature feels perpetually high—social media outrage, political polarization, and personal slights seem to fuel a never‑ending cycle of anger. Hansen’s central proposition is startlingly simple yet profoundly disruptive: the belief that we have a “right” to be offended is a myth, and clinging to that supposed righteous anger is the primary source of stress, division, and spiritual fatigue in modern life. Drawing on the wisdom of Dallas Willard and a solid grounding in Christian theology, Hansen argues that true freedom—and the flourishing God intends for us—comes from relinquishing our offense‑taking habit altogether.

The book’s strength lies in its ability to translate a lofty spiritual ideal into everyday, actionable practices. Hansen walks readers through a three‑step process: first, recognizing that our expectations of others are often unrealistic and rooted in a desire for control; second, replacing the habit of nursing grudges with humility and gratitude; and third, actively cultivating forgiveness that manifests in concrete, loving actions toward those who have wronged us. Each chapter is peppered with brief anecdotes—sometimes self‑deprecating, sometimes drawn from Hansen’s own radio‑show experiences—that illustrate how a shift in mindset can defuse tense situations, from traffic jams to family disagreements. The tone is consistently conversational, as if Hansen is leaning across a coffee shop table, inviting the reader to consider a perspective that feels both challenging and oddly liberating.

Hansen’s style is one of the book’s most appealing features. He avoids the dense, jargon‑laden prose that can alienate readers from theological discussions, opting instead for a witty, accessible voice that feels like a blend of pastoral counsel and stand‑up comedy. His humor is never merely decorative; it serves to disarm defensiveness, making the uncomfortable truth—that our anger often masks pride—easier to swallow. This approachable manner allows the book to reach a broad audience: longtime believers seeking a fresh spiritual discipline, skeptics curious about Christian ethics, and anyone weary of the emotional toll of perpetual offense.

Nevertheless, the work is not without its limitations. The central thesis, while compelling, is presented with a degree of repetition that can feel redundant after the first few chapters. Readers looking for a deep, scholarly engagement with the philosophical nuances of anger and forgiveness may find the treatment somewhat surface‑level. Additionally, Hansen’s reliance on personal anecdotes, while endearing, sometimes substitutes for broader empirical evidence or historical examples that could strengthen his argument about societal benefits. A more explicit engagement with contemporary psychology or sociology could bridge the gap between spiritual insight and practical, evidence‑based application.

Despite these minor shortcomings, Unoffendable succeeds in its primary goal: offering a concrete pathway to a less stressful, more joyful life rooted in a radical reorientation of the heart. Hansen’s invitation to “become the most unoffendable people on the planet” is not a call to passive tolerance but an active, grace‑filled stance that transforms how we engage with conflict, disappointment, and even injustice. By reframing offense as a choice rather than an entitlement, he provides a refreshing antidote to the burnout that plagues so many of today’s conversations—both online and off.

In a world that rewards outrage, Hansen’s book feels like a countercultural breath of fresh air. It challenges readers to examine the hidden cost of their anger, to replace it with gratitude, and to discover a freedom that is both deeply spiritual and markedly practical. For anyone weary of the endless cycle of being offended—and seeking a tangible way to live with greater peace—Unoffendable offers a hopeful, humor‑laced roadmap worth walking.
