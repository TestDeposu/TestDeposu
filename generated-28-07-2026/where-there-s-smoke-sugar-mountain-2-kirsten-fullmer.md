# Where There's Smoke (Sugar Mountain #2)

**Author:** Kirsten Fullmer  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Unknown

In the second installment of her beloved Sugar Mountain series, Where There’s Smoke, Kirsten Fullmer delivers a masterclass in small-town romance, proving that the hottest flames are often those kindled by unexpected second chances. While the exact publication date and page count remain footnotes to the sheer experiential weight of the novel, Fullmer’s narrative footprint is undeniably vast. She invites readers back into a tight-knit community where gossip travels faster than wildfire, but where loyalty, warmth, and redemption ultimately run deeper than the local coal seams.

At the heart of Where There’s Smoke lies the timeless theme of emotional combustion and renewal. Fullmer explores the fragile space between who we used to be and who we are forced to become when life completely unravels. The overarching metaphor of fire—both destructive and purifying—serves as the emotional anchor for the entire book. The author examines how past traumas and unhealed wounds can smolder beneath the surface for years, waiting for the right oxygen to reignite into a full-blown crisis. Yet, rather than treating these trials with melodramatic despair, Fullmer infuses the narrative with a gritty resilience. The central thesis of the book is clear: you cannot fear the burn if you ever hope to feel the warmth of true connection. 

Fullmer’s writing style is the true engine driving this emotional vehicle. She possesses a rare talent for balancing high-stakes emotional vulnerability with sharp, self-aware wit. Her prose is conversational yet evocative, stripping away the pretension often found in contemporary romance and replacing it with raw, unvarnished authenticity. Where lesser writers might rely on cliches to bridge the gaps in a small-town narrative, Fullmer leans heavily into atmospheric world-building. You can practically smell the pine trees, taste the strong diner coffee, and hear the creak of floorboards in her meticulously crafted setting. 

Moreover, her character-driven approach ensures that the plot serves the people, rather than the other way around. The dialogue snaps with realistic tension, laced with unspoken histories and subtle undercurrents of desire and fear. Fullmer writes with an empathy that makes even the most stubborn, guarded characters profoundly relatable. She understands that healing is not a linear path, and she grants her protagonists the grace to stumble, regress, and ultimately choose love over fear. 

Where There’s Smoke is a compelling, heart-pounding, and deeply comforting read that cements Kirsten Fullmer’s status as a formidable voice in contemporary romance. It is a story about clearing away the ash to reveal what truly matters, leaving readers thoroughly warmed and eagerly anticipating the next chapter in the Sugar Mountain saga.
