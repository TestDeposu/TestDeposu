# This Time It's Real

**Author:** Ann Liang  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

In This Time It's Real, bestselling author Ann Liang delivers a sparkling, deeply charming addition to the contemporary young adult romance canon. Published in 2023, the novel captures the intoxicating rush of first love while deftly navigating the modern pressures of identity, ambition, and digital exposure. Reminiscent of the classic, swoon-worthy mechanics of Jenny Han and Meg Cabot, Liang’s story stands out through its distinct contemporary lens, blending international settings with the hyper-connected reality of today's teenagers.

At the heart of the novel is seventeen-year-old Eliza Lin, a chronic outsider navigating the daunting hallways of a new international school in Beijing. When an essay detailing a breathtaking romance unexpectedly goes viral, Eliza’s social standing transforms overnight. The catch? The essay is entirely fictional. Never having been in a relationship, Eliza finds herself trapped in a web of her own making, desperately needing to produce a real-life romance before her fabricated narrative catches up with her. Enter Caz Song—a charismatic, perpetually guarded classmate and a famous actor. A transactional agreement is struck: Eliza helps Caz with his college application essays, and in exchange, he plays the role of her devoted boyfriend. 

Liang uses this classic fake-dating trope not merely as a vehicle for easy laughs and heart-fluttering moments, but as a thematic exploration of authenticity. The central paradox of the book is brilliantly encapsulated by Eliza’s justification that "all good writing is lying." Throughout the narrative, Liang questions where the boundary lies between the stories we tell the world to make ourselves lovable and the messy, unpolished truths of who we actually are. Eliza is a writer trying to script her own life, but Liang’s sharp prose reminds us that genuine human connection cannot be meticulously edited or previewed. 

The author’s writing style is effortlessly witty, brisk, and deeply observant. Beijing is rendered not just as a backdrop, but as a vibrant, sensory-rich character in its own right. From late-night motorcycle rides to local snack stalls to the quiet intimacy of handwritten notes passed in class, Liang crafts scenes that feel tactile and achingly romantic. The chemistry between Eliza and Caz crackles with electric banter, yet the narrative never loses sight of their individual vulnerabilities. Caz is more than just a brooding teen heartthrob; his struggles with fame and academic expectations mirror Eliza's insecurities about belonging and artistic validation. 

Ultimately, This Time It's Real is a triumph of pacing and emotional resonance. Ann Liang balances humor and heartbreak with a deft hand, ensuring that the story’s predictable beats are elevated by fresh insights into modern youth culture. It is a love letter to storytelling, the fear of vulnerability, and the terrifying beauty of letting someone see the unedited version of your life.
