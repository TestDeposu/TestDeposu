# The Blue Sword (Damar, #1)

**Author:** Robin McKinley  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

The Blue Sword, penned by the inimitable Robin McKinley, is a timeless masterpiece of young adult fantasy that has captivated readers for generations. This Newbery Honor Book is a testament to McKinley's skill in crafting rich, immersive worlds and complex, compelling heroines. Set in the arid yet majestic landscape of Damar, The Blue Sword is a sweeping epic that delves into the intricate dance of destiny, magic, and the unbreakable bonds of legend.

At the heart of this mesmerizing tale is Harry Crewe, a Homelander orphan girl who finds herself inexplicably drawn to the harsh beauty of Damar. Her sense of disconnection from the lush green hills of her Homeland is palpable, and her longing to explore the unforgiving yet majestic terrain of Damar is a siren's call that cannot be ignored. It is here, in this unforgiving landscape, that Harry's destiny becomes inextricably linked with that of Corlath, the enigmatic golden-eyed king of the Free Hillfolk.

Corlath, the scion of the legendary Lady Aerin, is a masterfully crafted character whose presence is as commanding as the mountains he calls home. His arrival in Harry's town sets in motion a chain of events that will forever alter the course of her life, as she finds herself chosen to wield the fabled Blue Sword, a treasure of untold power that has lain dormant since the days of Lady Aerin. As Harry embarks on her perilous journey, she must confront the darkness that threatens to consume Damar and, in doing so, discovers the unyielding strength that lies within herself.

One of McKinley's greatest strengths as a storyteller is her ability to weave together the threads of myth and legend, creating a rich tapestry that is at once both epic in scope and intimately personal. Her prose is as effortless as it is evocative, conjuring the very essence of Damar's stark beauty and the fierce, unyielding spirit of its people. The world-building in The Blue Sword is nothing short of masterful, with McKinley's vivid descriptions transporting readers to a realm that is at once both familiar and yet, utterly alien.

Throughout the narrative, McKinley explores the complex interplay between fate and free will, raising questions about the nature of destiny and the role that individual agency plays in shaping the course of our lives. Harry's journey is a powerful exploration of self-discovery, as she navigates the treacherous landscape of her own identity and the expectations that are placed upon her. Her growth from a tentative, uncertain girl to a confident, self-assured young woman is a true marvel, and a testament to McKinley's skill in crafting compelling, relatable heroines.

In short, The Blue Sword is a triumph of epic fantasy, a sweeping narrative that will captivate fans of Sarah J. Maas, Leigh Bardugo, and Rae Carson. With its lush world-building, complex characters, and timeless themes, this modern classic is a must-read for anyone who has ever been enchanted by the magic of fantasy.
