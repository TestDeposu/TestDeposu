# If Tomorrow Comes (Tracy Whitney, #1)

**Author:** Sidney Sheldon  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

Sidney Sheldon’s *If Tomorrow Comes* is a masterclass in high-stakes drama, where revenge and redemption collide in a whirlwind of glamour, deception, and relentless pursuit. At its core, this novel is a feminist revenge fantasy dressed in the sleek, seductive armor of a thriller, where a wronged woman transforms from victim to predator with breathtaking precision. Tracy Whitney, the protagonist, is no damsel in distress—she’s a woman scorned who weaponizes her intelligence, charm, and sheer audacity to dismantle the corrupt men who destroyed her life. Sheldon crafts a narrative that is as much about survival as it is about vengeance, making *If Tomorrow Comes* a gripping exploration of power, resilience, and the lengths one will go to reclaim what was stolen.

From the very first page, Sheldon establishes a tone that is both polished and pulse-pounding. His prose is sleek, almost cinematic, with a rhythm that propels the reader forward like a runaway train. There’s a deliberate economy in his storytelling—no word is wasted, no scene overstayed—yet the emotional beats land with the force of a hammer blow. Tracy’s journey begins in a prison cell, a place of despair that could have been a narrative dead end, but Sheldon refuses to let his heroine wallow. Instead, he turns her incarceration into a crucible of transformation. The prison scenes are claustrophobic yet vivid, with Sheldon’s knack for detail making the oppressive atmosphere tangible. When Tracy escapes, it’s not just a physical breakout but a symbolic one—a rejection of the system that tried to break her.

The novel’s central theme is the reclaiming of agency. Tracy is stripped of her freedom, her reputation, and her future, but Sheldon ensures that she never loses her agency. Her intelligence becomes her greatest weapon, and her beauty, a tool to disarm her enemies. The way she outsmarts the crime lords who framed her is nothing short of exhilarating. There’s a delicious irony in watching a woman who was once powerless wield power with such precision, turning the tables on those who underestimated her. Sheldon doesn’t shy away from the moral ambiguity of Tracy’s actions—she’s not a heroine in the traditional sense, but a woman who has been pushed to the edge and chooses to fight fire with fire. It’s a theme that resonates deeply in a world where women are often told to be "nice" even when they’re being wronged.

Sheldon’s style is unmistakable: he blends the grit of a crime thriller with the gloss of a soap opera, creating a world where wealth, danger, and seduction are inextricably linked. His characters are larger than life, but they’re also deeply flawed, which makes them compelling. Tracy is a woman of contradictions—vulnerable yet formidable, tender yet ruthless. Her counterpart, Jeff Stevens, is a rogue with a heart of gold (or so it seems), and their dynamic crackles with chemistry. Sheldon doesn’t waste time on drawn-out romantic subplots; instead, their relationship unfolds naturally, fueled by mutual respect and a shared history of survival. The chemistry between them adds a layer of emotional depth to the story, reminding us that even in the darkest moments, human connection can be a source of strength.

One of the novel’s greatest strengths is its global scope. Tracy’s escapades take her from the gilded cages of European high society to the neon-lit underbelly of America, from the opulence of Monte Carlo to the gritty streets of New York. Sheldon’s ability to paint these diverse settings with vivid strokes makes the story feel expansive and immersive. Whether it’s the high-stakes world of art forgery, the cutthroat realm of international finance, or the shadowy deals of the criminal underworld, Sheldon makes each setting feel real and dangerous. The pacing never flags, and the twists come fast and furious, keeping the reader guessing until the very last page.

What makes *If Tomorrow Comes* stand out, however, is its unapologetic embrace of female empowerment. Tracy Whitney is a heroine for the ages—a woman who refuses to be a victim, who turns her pain into power, and who proves that intelligence and cunning are just as formidable as brute force. In a genre often dominated by male protagonists, Sheldon gives us a woman who is equal parts victim and victor, and who commands the story with an iron will. It’s a refreshing and empowering take on the revenge narrative, one that doesn’t rely on male saviors or passive suffering.

Sheldon’s writing is also notable for its wit and sharp dialogue. The banter between Tracy and Jeff is electric, filled with sarcasm and innuendo that add a layer of humor to the otherwise intense story. The dialogue crackles with tension, whether the characters are flirting, scheming, or outright threatening each other. It’s a testament to Sheldon’s skill that even in the most high-stakes moments, the writing never feels heavy-handed or melodramatic.

Ultimately, *If Tomorrow Comes* is a novel that thrills, surprises, and lingers in the mind long after the last page is turned. It’s a story about justice—albeit a justice that is messy, morally complex, and entirely satisfying. Tracy Whitney is a character who demands to be remembered, a woman who proves that even in a world stacked against her, she will not be defeated. Sidney Sheldon may have written this book decades ago, but its themes of resilience, cunning, and the pursuit of vengeance are timeless.

For readers who love a story where the underdog triumphs, where the stakes are high and the rewards even higher, *If Tomorrow Comes* is a must-read. It’s a novel that reminds us that sometimes, the most powerful weapon a woman can wield is her own mind.
