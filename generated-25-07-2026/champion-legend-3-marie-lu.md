# Champion (Legend, #3)

**Author:** Marie Lu  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

**Champion: A Heart-Wrenching Finale to a Legendary Trilogy**

Marie Lu’s *Champion* is the electrifying conclusion to the *Legend* trilogy, a dystopian saga that has captivated readers with its relentless pacing, moral complexity, and unforgettable characters. As the final installment, *Champion* delivers everything fans have come to expect—high-stakes drama, heartbreaking sacrifices, and a conclusion that lingers long after the last page. But what makes this book truly stand out is its unflinching exploration of love, duty, and the cost of power, all wrapped in Lu’s signature blend of adrenaline-fueled action and raw emotional depth.

At its core, *Champion* is a story about the impossible choices we make when love and duty collide. June and Day, the trilogy’s iconic duo, are now on opposite sides of the Republic’s power structure—June as the rising star of the government, Day as a high-ranking military officer. Their reunion is not one of joy but of necessity, as a deadly new plague threatens to tear the fragile peace between the Republic and the Colonies apart. June, armed with a secret that could save millions, must confront the ultimate question: Can she save her country without losing the man she loves?

Lu masterfully builds tension from the very first page, blending political intrigue with personal stakes. The plague outbreak isn’t just a plot device—it’s a metaphor for the corruption and decay that has festered within the Republic for decades. The disease spreads like a silent enemy, mirroring the way oppression and injustice have quietly eroded the foundations of society. June, who has spent her life believing in the Republic’s ideals, is forced to question everything she’s fought for. Her journey from blind loyalty to reluctant rebellion is one of the novel’s most compelling arcs.

Day, too, undergoes a profound transformation. Once the Republic’s most wanted criminal, he now wears a uniform, his past sins haunting him as he navigates the moral gray areas of war. His relationship with June is both tender and fraught with tension—two people who love each other but are trapped by the roles they’ve been forced into. Their dynamic is the emotional heartbeat of the book, and Lu doesn’t shy away from making their choices devastatingly difficult. The scene where June must ask Day to sacrifice everything for the greater good is a masterclass in heartbreak, leaving readers breathless and aching.

Lu’s prose is sharp and immersive, with a cinematic quality that makes the action sequences pulse with urgency. The fight scenes are visceral, the political maneuvering intricate, and the emotional beats devastatingly real. She doesn’t just tell a story—she makes you *feel* it. The world-building, too, is rich and immersive, with the plague serving as a chilling reminder of how easily society can unravel when fear takes hold.

But what truly elevates *Champion* is its refusal to offer easy answers. There are no clean victories here, no perfect resolutions. The ending is bittersweet, a testament to the fact that in a world as broken as the one Lu has created, true peace often comes at a terrible cost. It’s a fitting conclusion to a trilogy that has always challenged its readers to question authority, to see the humanity in their enemies, and to recognize that the line between hero and villain is often thinner than we’d like to admit.

For fans of dystopian fiction, *Champion* is a must-read—a thrilling, emotional, and thought-provoking finale that cements *Legend* as one of the genre’s most compelling trilogies. And for those who haven’t yet experienced June and Day’s story, now is the time to dive in. You won’t regret it.
