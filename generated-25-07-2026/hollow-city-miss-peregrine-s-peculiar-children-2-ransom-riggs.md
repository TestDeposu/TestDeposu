# Hollow City (Miss Peregrine's Peculiar Children, #2)

**Author:** Ransom Riggs  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

Hollow City, the second installment in Ransom Riggs's captivating series Miss Peregrine's Peculiar Children, is a masterful blend of dark fantasy, supernatural mystery, and gothic adventure. Building upon the eerie and enchanting world introduced in the first book, Riggs delves deeper into the complexities of his peculiar characters, crafting a narrative that is both haunting and mesmerizing.

At its core, Hollow City is a story about the struggle to find one's place in the world, to belong, and to be accepted for who one is. Jacob, the protagonist, is a peculiar individual himself, having discovered his ability to see and communicate with the supernatural entities known as the Hollows and the Wights. As he navigates the treacherous landscape of wartime London, Jacob must confront the darkness within himself and the world around him, all while trying to protect his newfound friends and allies.

Riggs's writing style is a perfect complement to the story's themes and atmosphere. His prose is rich and evocative, conjuring vivid images of the crumbling landscapes and decaying architecture that serve as the backdrop for the narrative. The inclusion of vintage photographs, which has become a hallmark of the series, adds an extra layer of depth and authenticity to the story, blurring the lines between reality and fantasy.

One of the most striking aspects of Hollow City is its exploration of the human condition. Riggs's characters are multidimensional and flawed, each struggling with their own demons and insecurities. The peculiar children, in particular, are a fascinating and diverse group, each with their unique abilities and strengths. The author's portrayal of their struggles and triumphs is both poignant and thought-provoking, inviting readers to reflect on their own experiences and emotions.

The world-building in Hollow City is also noteworthy, with Riggs expanding the series' mythology and introducing new concepts and creatures. The hollows and the wights are terrifying entities, embodying the fears and anxieties of the characters and the reader alike. The author's use of historical events and settings adds an extra layer of depth to the narrative, grounding the fantastical elements in a sense of reality.

In conclusion, Hollow City is a captivating and unsettling read, perfect for fans of dark fantasy and supernatural mystery. Ransom Riggs's unique blend of storytelling, atmospheric setting, and vintage photography makes for a compelling narrative that will keep readers on the edge of their seats. With its thought-provoking themes and complex characters, this book is a must-read for anyone looking for a story that will haunt them long after the final page has been turned.
