# Crooked Kingdom (Six of Crows, #2)

**Author:** Leigh Bardugo  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

**Crooked Kingdom: A Heist of Hearts, Lies, and Redemption**

Leigh Bardugo’s *Crooked Kingdom* is a masterclass in tension, character depth, and the messy, exhilarating chaos of found family. As the explosive sequel to *Six of Crows*, it doesn’t just deliver on the promise of its predecessor—it surpasses it, diving deeper into the morally gray world of Ketterdam where every deal is a gamble and every loyalty is tested. This isn’t just a heist story; it’s a story about what happens when the crew you’d die for becomes the reason you might.

At its core, *Crooked Kingdom* is a story about power—who wields it, who craves it, and who gets crushed beneath it. The book’s central conflict revolves around the crew’s attempt to outmaneuver Pekka Rollins, the ruthless crime lord who has betrayed them time and again. But Bardugo isn’t interested in a simple revenge plot. Instead, she explores the cost of power: the way it corrupts, the way it isolates, and the way it forces people to make impossible choices. Kaz Brekker, the icy, calculating leader of the Dregs, is the embodiment of this theme. His genius is matched only by his paranoia, and as the story unfolds, we see how his obsession with control has warped him. His relationship with Inej—fraught with unspoken love and mutual destruction—is the heart of the book, a raw, painful push-and-pull that forces both characters to confront their own demons.

Bardugo’s prose is as sharp as a knife’s edge, cutting through the glamour of Ketterdam to reveal the rot beneath. Her world-building is meticulous, painting a city where beauty and brutality coexist in uneasy harmony. The magic system, rooted in Grisha lore, feels both familiar and fresh, with Heartrender Nina’s struggles to control her power adding a layer of emotional depth to the heist’s high-stakes action. The prose is lush but never overwrought, balancing gritty realism with moments of poetic beauty—especially in Inej’s internal monologues, which are as lyrical as they are heartbreaking.

What truly sets *Crooked Kingdom* apart, however, is its characters. Bardugo doesn’t just write a crew; she writes a family, one that bickers, betrays, and ultimately loves each other in ways that are messy and real. Jesper’s gambling addiction, Wylan’s quiet resilience, Matthias’s struggle between duty and desire—each character is given room to grow, to fail, and to redeem themselves in ways that feel earned. The banter is razor-sharp, the emotional beats land with devastating precision, and the action sequences are so vivid you’ll feel the adrenaline coursing through your veins.

The book’s structure is another triumph. Bardugo weaves multiple POVs seamlessly, each chapter pulling the reader deeper into the crew’s web of lies and alliances. The tension is relentless, with betrayals lurking around every corner and no character safe from the fallout. Even the smallest details—a throwaway line about a debt, a flicker of emotion in a character’s eyes—pay off in ways that are both surprising and inevitable.

But perhaps the most compelling aspect of *Crooked Kingdom* is its exploration of redemption. No one in this story is purely good or purely evil. Kaz is a villain in his own right, Inej is a survivor who has done terrible things, and even Matthias, with his rigid moral code, is forced to confront the limits of his beliefs. Bardugo doesn’t offer easy answers, but she does offer hope—a fragile, hard-won hope that comes from choosing your family, even when it costs you everything.

*Crooked Kingdom* is more than a sequel; it’s a culmination of everything that makes the *Six of Crows* duology so special. It’s a story about love and loyalty, about the lies we tell ourselves and the truths we can’t escape. It’s a heist where the real treasure isn’t gold or power, but the people who would burn the world down for you—and the ones you’d do the same for.

If *Six of Crows* was a spark, *Crooked Kingdom* is the inferno. And like any great fire, it leaves you scorched, breathless, and desperate for more.
