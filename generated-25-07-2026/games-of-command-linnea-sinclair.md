# Games of Command

**Author:** Linnea Sinclair  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

Games of Command, penned by Linnea Sinclair, is a gripping space opera that delves into the complexities of war, loyalty, and the human heart. Set in a universe where the oppressive Empire reigns supreme, the story follows Admiral Philip Guthrie, a rebel leader with a price on his head, as he navigates the treacherous landscape of resistance and personal redemption.

Sinclair's writing style is a masterclass in balancing action, suspense, and emotional depth. Her prose is crisp and concise, propelling the reader through the narrative with a sense of urgency that's impossible to ignore. The world-building is meticulous, with a richly detailed universe that's both immersive and believable. From the cramped corridors of the derelict cruiser, Hope's Folly, to the high-stakes battles between rebels and Imperial forces, Sinclair's vision is vivid and unapologetic.

At the heart of the story is the complicated relationship between Philip Guthrie and Rya Bennton, his assigned bodyguard and the daughter of his former commanding officer. Rya's unrequited love for Philip, which began when she was a girl, is a poignant counterpoint to the brutal realities of war. As they navigate the dangers of their mission, their mutual attraction simmers just below the surface, threatening to upend their carefully constructed defenses.

Sinclair's characterization is superb, with Philip and Rya emerging as fully realized, multidimensional characters. Philip's guilt and sense of responsibility for his friend's death are palpable, making his gradual thawing a compelling arc. Rya, meanwhile, is a force to be reckoned with – a skilled assassin with a deep sense of loyalty and a desperate need for connection.

The themes of Games of Command are timely and thought-provoking. Sinclair explores the nature of command, the weight of responsibility, and the true cost of rebellion. Through Philip and Rya's story, she raises questions about the morality of war, the blurred lines between loyalty and duty, and the human cost of sacrifice.

If there's a criticism to be made, it's that the supporting cast feels somewhat one-dimensional at times. The Imperial forces, in particular, are portrayed as cartoonishly evil, which detracts from the overall sense of nuance. However, this is a minor quibble in an otherwise engaging narrative.

In conclusion, Games of Command is a gripping, emotionally resonant space opera that will appeal to fans of Lois McMaster Bujold and Catherine Asaro. With its breakneck pacing, memorable characters, and thought-provoking themes, it's a must-read for anyone looking for a compelling, action-packed romance.
