# Lessons In Faking (Hall Beck University, #1)

**Author:** Selina Mae  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

Selina Mae’s *Lessons in Faking* is a dazzling debut that blends the sharp wit of enemies-to-lovers romance with the emotional depth of sibling bonds, all wrapped in the irresistible charm of a fake-dating trope. Set against the backdrop of Hall Beck University, this Wattpad sensation—now polished for a wider audience—delivers a story that’s as much about self-discovery as it is about love. With its promise of gorgeous stenciled edges, artful interior designs, and an exclusive bonus chapter, the book isn’t just a feast for the heart; it’s a visual and emotional experience.

At its core, *Lessons in Faking* explores the tension between authenticity and performance, both in relationships and within oneself. Athalia Pressley, our fiercely independent heroine, is trapped in a cycle of grief and guilt after her twin brother Henry withdraws from her life following their parents’ divorce. Her desperation to reconnect with him leads her to an audacious plan: fake a relationship with Dylan McCarthy, the golden boy Henry despises, to regain his attention. What starts as a calculated scheme quickly spirals into something far more dangerous—emotions that refuse to stay neatly compartmentalized.

Mae’s writing is a masterclass in balancing humor and heartache. Athalia’s voice is sharp, self-aware, and laced with sarcasm, making her an instantly relatable protagonist. Her internal monologues crackle with wit, but beneath the bravado lies a vulnerability that makes her struggles feel achingly real. Dylan, meanwhile, is the quintessential "golden boy" with a hidden depth—charming, infuriating, and surprisingly perceptive. Their dynamic crackles with tension, both antagonistic and electric, as they navigate the blurred lines between performance and truth.

The fake-dating trope is executed with precision, avoiding the pitfalls of overused clichés. Mae doesn’t rely solely on the trope’s inherent drama; instead, she weaves in Athalia’s emotional journey, making the reader root for her not just as a love interest but as a person grappling with her own worth. The sibling relationship at the heart of the story is particularly poignant, adding layers of complexity to Athalia’s motivations. Her longing for Henry isn’t just about rekindling a bond—it’s about reclaiming a part of herself that feels fractured.

Mae’s prose is fluid and engaging, with a knack for pacing that keeps the story moving at a brisk clip. The dialogue sparkles, especially in scenes between Athalia and Dylan, where their banter feels organic and charged with subtext. The university setting is vividly rendered, with enough detail to immerse the reader without overwhelming them. The promise of art on the inside cover and stenciled edges suggests a book that’s as much a collector’s item as it is a compelling read—a nice touch for fans of aesthetically pleasing romances.

What truly sets *Lessons in Faking* apart, however, is its emotional honesty. The story doesn’t shy away from the messiness of real relationships, whether it’s the push-and-pull of sibling rivalry, the fear of vulnerability, or the intoxicating thrill of falling for someone you swore you’d never trust. Athalia’s journey is one of self-reckoning, and by the end, the question isn’t just whether she’ll choose Dylan over Henry—it’s whether she’ll choose herself.

Selina Mae has crafted a romance that’s as smart as it is swoon-worthy, proving that sometimes the most powerful lessons come from faking it till you make it. *Lessons in Faking* is a standout in the genre, a book that lingers long after the last page.
