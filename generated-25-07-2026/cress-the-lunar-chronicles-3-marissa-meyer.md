# Cress (The Lunar Chronicles, #3)

**Author:** Marissa Meyer  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

**Cress: A Cybernetic Twist on Rapunzel**

In the sprawling, tech-infused universe of Marissa Meyer's "Cress," the third installment in her Lunar Chronicles, the author weaves a captivating tale that seamlessly blends futuristic science fiction with the classic fairy tale of Rapunzel. Meyer's signature style, a harmonious blend of intricate world-building, engaging characters, and a dash of romance, shines brightly in this installment.

At the heart of "Cress" is the eponymous heroine, a brilliant computer hacker with a mysterious past, trapped in a satellite prison by the nefarious Lunar Queen Levana. Cress, with her Rapunzel-like hair that stretches for miles, is a refreshing take on the classic fairy tale princess. She's not just a damsel in distress; she's a formidable force, armed with her intellect and an uncanny ability to manipulate technology.

Meyer's strength lies in her ability to create compelling, diverse characters that readers can't help but root for. Cress, with her unique perspective and vulnerability, is no exception. Her journey from isolated captive to a key player in the rebellion against Levana is a testament to Meyer's skill in character development.

The author's world-building is another standout feature of "Cress." Meyer's future is a fascinating blend of advanced technology and political intrigue, with Lunar cyborgs and space travel coexisting with familiar fairy tale tropes. The Lunar throne's power dynamics, the political machinations, and the rebellion brewing in the shadows all add layers of complexity to the story.

Meyer's writing style is engaging and accessible, making "Cress" a page-turner that's hard to put down. She masterfully balances action, romance, and character development, ensuring that the story never feels stagnant. The pacing is excellent, with a good mix of intense, suspenseful moments and quieter, character-driven scenes.

However, "Cress" isn't without its flaws. Some plot points feel a bit predictable, and certain characters could use more depth. But these minor issues are easily overshadowed by the sheer enjoyment of the story.

In conclusion, "Cress" is a thrilling addition to the Lunar Chronicles, offering a fresh take on a classic tale. Meyer's unique blend of science fiction and fairy tale, coupled with her engaging storytelling, makes "Cress" a must-read for fans of the series and newcomers alike.
