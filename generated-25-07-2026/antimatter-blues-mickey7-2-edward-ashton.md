# Antimatter Blues (Mickey7, #2)

**Author:** Edward Ashton  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

In "Antimatter Blues," the highly anticipated sequel to Edward Ashton's "Mickey7," the stakes are higher, the tension is palpable, and the fate of two species hangs precariously in the balance. Ashton masterfully weaves a complex narrative that delves into the very essence of survival, sacrifice, and the true cost of progress.

At the heart of the story lies Mickey Barnes, the lovable and resilient expendable who has managed to cheat death once again. As the colony of Niflheim teeters on the brink of collapse, Mickey finds himself at the center of a maelstrom, forced to navigate the treacherous landscape of human politics and creeper diplomacy. With the antimatter that fuels the colony running perilously low, Commander Marshall's demands for the retrieval of the coveted bomb threaten to upend the fragile peace that Mickey has worked so hard to maintain.

Ashton's prose is as crisp as the frozen tundra of Niflheim, with a keen eye for detail that brings the alien world to vivid life. His characters are multidimensional and relatable, with Mickey's wry humor and vulnerability making him an endearing and rootable protagonist. The supporting cast is equally well-crafted, from the enigmatic creepers to the ruthless Commander Marshall, each with their own motivations and agendas that add depth and complexity to the narrative.

One of the most striking aspects of "Antimatter Blues" is its thought-provoking exploration of the human condition. Ashton raises important questions about the value of life, the true cost of progress, and the morality of sacrificing one individual for the greater good. Through Mickey's struggles, we are forced to confront the darker aspects of human nature and the consequences of our actions.

Ashton's style is a perfect blend of science fiction and noir, with a dash of dark humor that adds a layer of complexity to the narrative. His world-building is meticulous, with a keen attention to detail that immerses the reader in the harsh, unforgiving environment of Niflheim. The pacing is relentless, with a series of twists and turns that keep the reader on the edge of their seat.

In conclusion, "Antimatter Blues" is a gripping and thought-provoking sequel that surpasses its predecessor in every way. With its breakneck pacing, complex characters, and moral complexity, this novel is a must-read for fans of science fiction and noir. Ashton's masterful storytelling and vivid world-building make this a compelling and unforgettable read.
