# Claimed (Brides of the Kindred, #1)

**Author:** Evangeline Anderson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

Evangeline Anderson’s *Claimed* (Brides of the Kindred, #1) is a scorching sci-fi romance that blends high-stakes undercover work with a slow-burn, powerhouse dynamic between two partners who refuse to admit their attraction. At its core, this is a story about control—who holds it, who craves it, and the explosive consequences when lines blur between duty and desire.

Mia Deen is a detective who has spent her career proving she doesn’t need a man’s protection, let alone his dominance. She’s built a reputation in the Human Kindred Relations Department, where she navigates a world of towering, alien warriors with unshakable professionalism. Enter Severith, or Sev, a Dae’mon Kindred warrior whose very presence commands fear—his seven-foot-tall, muscular frame, crimson skin, and intimidating horns make him a walking nightmare for criminals. Yet, despite his fearsome exterior, Sev is undone by his unrequited attraction to Mia, a woman who sees him only as a partner, not a potential lover.

The premise alone—two professionals forced into a fake marriage undercover in a cult that glorifies male dominance and female submission—should be enough to set readers’ pulses racing. But Anderson doesn’t just rely on the high-concept hook; she crafts a story where the tension simmers beneath every interaction. Mia’s resistance to Sev’s dominance isn’t just about principle; it’s rooted in a troubled past that makes trust a luxury she can’t afford. Sev, meanwhile, is a warrior who has spent his life mastering combat but is utterly helpless when it comes to Mia’s stubborn independence. Their dynamic is a masterclass in push-and-pull, where every argument, every lingering glance, and every moment of forced proximity ratchets up the heat.

What makes *Claimed* stand out is Anderson’s ability to balance the external stakes of the undercover mission with the internal conflict of two people who are equally determined not to want what they can’t have. The cult’s disturbing beliefs—where wives are expected to submit unconditionally and husbands have the right to punish disobedience—serve as a dark mirror to Sev and Mia’s real-life friction. The cult’s ideology isn’t just backdrop; it’s a catalyst that forces them to confront their own desires. Is Mia’s resistance to Sev’s dominance a matter of principle, or is it fear? And is Sev’s attraction to her more than just physical—does he crave her submission because it’s what he truly wants, or because it’s the only way he can imagine claiming her?

Anderson’s prose is tight and engaging, with a knack for dialogue that crackles with tension. Her world-building is immersive without being overly explanatory, trusting readers to piece together the Kindred universe as they go. The pacing is relentless, with the undercover mission providing a ticking clock that keeps the story moving, while the romantic tension builds in a way that feels organic rather than forced. The sex scenes are steamy but not gratuitous, serving the narrative rather than existing solely for titillation. There’s a raw, almost primal quality to Sev’s desire for Mia, which makes their eventual surrender to it all the more satisfying.

The book’s title, *Claimed*, is a deliberate double entendre. On one level, it refers to the cult’s belief in a husband’s right to claim his wife. On another, it’s a promise of what’s to come for Sev and Mia. The question isn’t *if* they’ll give in to their attraction, but *when*—and how explosive that moment will be. Anderson doesn’t shy away from the darker implications of power dynamics, but she also doesn’t let the story devolve into a one-sided power play. Mia is a fully realized character with agency, and her journey isn’t about losing herself to Sev’s dominance but about finding a way to trust him with her vulnerabilities.

If there’s a critique to be made, it’s that the cult’s backstory and the broader Kindred universe feel slightly underdeveloped. While the focus on Sev and Mia’s relationship is the strength of the book, a bit more depth about the PPP’s origins or the Kindred’s society could have added another layer of intrigue. However, this is a minor quibble in a story that otherwise delivers exactly what it promises: a high-stakes, emotionally charged romance with a heroine who refuses to be tamed and a hero who’s determined to try.

*Claimed* is a book that lingers long after the last page. It’s not just about the heat between two people—it’s about the fight to claim not just a partner, but a piece of yourself you didn’t know you were missing. Anderson has crafted a romance that’s as much about surrender as it is about strength, and readers will be left breathless waiting for the next chapter in this series.
