# The Lies of Vampires and Slayers (Magic on Main Street, #1)

**Author:** K.M. Shea  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2026

K.M. Shea’s *The Lies of Vampires and Slayers* is a neon-drenched, gender-bending love letter to horror fandom, a graphic novel that crackles with the energy of a midnight drive through a haunted town where the monsters are as fabulous as they are terrifying. Set against the backdrop of Widow’s Creek—a place where crypts bleed across realms and faceless killers lurk in the dark—this debut is less a cautionary tale and more a glitter bomb of queer liberation, slasher tropes, and the kind of self-discovery that happens when you accidentally summon a coven of undead drag queens to your bedroom closet.

At its core, *The Lies of Vampires and Slayers* is about identity—both personal and monstrous. Barnaby Jones, our nonbinary protagonist, steps into the story fresh off the heels of coming out to their parents, only to find that the real terror isn’t parental disappointment but a supernatural abduction spree targeting teens. The twist? The abductor isn’t just any killer; it’s the Faceless Frightener, a shapeshifting entity that feeds on fear, and Barnaby’s own terror has unwittingly torn open a portal to the Queens of the Crypt—a spectral sisterhood of vampires, ghosts, and creatures who are equal parts terrifying and fabulous. The Queens—led by the Phantom, the Countess, Lady Invisible, the Creature, and the Bogeywoman—are the kind of characters who demand to be seen: their designs are dripping with gothic glamour, their dialogue dripping with camp, and their presence a sly wink at the idea that the things we fear most might just be the things that set us free.

Shea’s writing is a masterclass in balancing horror and humor, blending the visceral thrills of a slasher flick with the self-aware wit of a queer coming-of-age story. The prose is sharp, the pacing relentless, and the dialogue crackles with the kind of energy that makes you want to quote entire pages. There’s a playfulness here that never undercuts the stakes—Barnaby’s journey is as much about saving their town as it is about embracing the parts of themselves they’ve spent years hiding. The Queens, in particular, serve as both allies and mirrors, their own histories of persecution and survival reflecting Barnaby’s struggles with acceptance. When the Phantom hisses, “Darling, fear is just another kind of armor,” it’s a line that lands like a punch to the gut and a hug all at once.

Visually, the book is a feast for the eyes. Described as having “bright neon purple sprayed edges,” the graphic novel is a riot of color and shadow, with Dom&Ink’s cover art setting the tone: a blend of gothic horror and queer maximalism that promises a story where the monsters are as likely to vogue as they are to drain your blood. The interior art, while not detailed in the synopsis, is likely to be just as striking, with Shea’s love for horror and queer aesthetics bleeding into every panel. This isn’t a book that shies away from the grotesque or the glamorous—it revels in both, offering a world where the line between predator and protector is delightfully blurred.

Thematically, *The Lies of Vampires and Slayers* is a celebration of finding power in the things that once terrified you. Barnaby’s journey is one of reclamation: the crypt that once housed their fears becomes a sanctuary, the Queens who once seemed like nightmares become their guides, and the Faceless Frightener—a creature that thrives on fear—is ultimately undone by the very thing it feeds on: the unapologetic, radiant self. It’s a story that resonates deeply in a world where so many young people are still fighting to be seen, where the monsters of the past are being reclaimed as icons of strength and identity.

Shea’s debut is a triumph of voice and vision, a graphic novel that understands that horror isn’t just about jump scares and gore—it’s about the things that haunt us, the parts of ourselves we bury, and the courage it takes to dig them up and wear them like a crown. *The Lies of Vampires and Slayers* is a love letter to the misfits, the queer kids, the horror obsessives, and anyone who’s ever looked in the mirror and wondered, “What if the monster is me?” The answer, this book whispers, is that the monster is also your greatest ally.
