# Always Remember: Ben's Story (Ravenswood, #3)

**Author:** Mary Balogh  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2024

Mary Balogh’s *Always Remember: Ben’s Story* is a tender, emotionally rich addition to her beloved *Ravenswood* series, weaving together themes of resilience, love beyond societal constraints, and the quiet triumph of the human spirit. Set against the glittering backdrop of Regency England, this third installment delivers Balogh’s signature blend of warmth, wit, and heartfelt storytelling, proving once again why she remains the reigning queen of historical romance.

At its core, *Always Remember* is a story about longing—about the things we desire but are told we cannot have, and the courage it takes to reach for them anyway. Jennifer Arden, the spirited sister of the Duke of Wilby, has spent her life navigating a world that often overlooks her due to her physical limitations. Left unable to walk by a childhood illness, Jennifer has carved out a place for herself in high society, not as a victim, but as a woman who refuses to be defined by her circumstances. She dances in her mind, hosts gatherings with effortless charm, and finds joy in the small pleasures of life. Yet beneath her cheerful demeanor lies a quiet ache—a yearning for independence, for movement, for the kind of freedom others take for granted.

Enter Ben Ellis, the illegitimate son of the late Earl of Stratton, a man whose very existence is a contradiction in the rigid social hierarchy of the *ton*. Raised alongside the earl’s legitimate children, Ben has always been an outsider, a fact that has shaped his pragmatic, problem-solving nature. Where others see obstacles, he sees solutions. When he encounters Jennifer struggling with her crutches, he doesn’t offer pity—he offers innovation. His determination to help her walk, swim, and even drive a carriage is less about changing her and more about unlocking her potential. Their partnership is a dance of mutual respect, where Ben’s practical genius meets Jennifer’s indomitable spirit.

Balogh excels at crafting characters who feel achingly real, and Jennifer and Ben are no exception. Jennifer is a heroine who defies the trope of the delicate, suffering invalid. She is bold, witty, and unapologetically herself, even when society tries to box her in. Her vulnerability lies not in her body, but in her fear of never being seen as more than her limitations. Ben, meanwhile, is a hero who carries the weight of his illegitimacy with quiet dignity. His confidence in his abilities is matched only by his humility, and his love for Jennifer is as much about admiring her strength as it is about wanting to protect it.

The romance between them is slow-burning, built on shared moments of vulnerability and understanding. Balogh doesn’t rush their connection; instead, she lets it simmer, allowing the reader to feel the depth of their bond as it grows. The tension isn’t just about their differing social stations—it’s about the fear of disappointment, the risk of heartbreak, and the hope that love might just be worth the fight. Their story is a testament to the idea that sometimes, the greatest barriers aren’t physical, but the ones we impose on ourselves.

Balogh’s prose is elegant yet accessible, her dialogue sparkling with humor and authenticity. She has a knack for capturing the nuances of Regency society without letting it overshadow the emotional core of her stories. The world-building is rich but never overwhelming, and the pacing is such that the reader is drawn in from the first page, eager to see Jennifer and Ben’s journey unfold. The secondary characters, from Jennifer’s protective family to Ben’s complex relationship with his half-siblings, add depth to the narrative, grounding the romance in a sense of community and belonging.

What makes *Always Remember* particularly poignant is its exploration of memory and healing. The title itself is a nod to the past—both Jennifer’s and Ben’s—and the idea that sometimes, the only way forward is to confront what has held us back. Jennifer’s struggle to walk again isn’t just a physical challenge; it’s a metaphor for reclaiming her life on her own terms. Ben’s journey is equally compelling, as he grapples with his own sense of belonging and the fear that he will never truly be accepted, no matter how much he achieves.

Balogh’s ability to blend humor, heartache, and hope is what makes her work so enduring, and *Always Remember* is a perfect example of her talent. It’s a story that lingers, not just because of its satisfying romance, but because of the way it makes the reader reflect on their own perceptions of limitation and possibility.

For fans of historical romance, this book is a must-read—a reminder that love, in all its forms, is the ultimate rebellion against the odds.
