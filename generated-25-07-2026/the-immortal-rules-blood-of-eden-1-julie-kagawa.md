# The Immortal Rules (Blood of Eden, #1)

**Author:** Julie Kagawa  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

In the world of young adult fiction, it's not often that a book comes along and completely flips the script on a tired genre. But that's exactly what Julie Kagawa does in "The Immortal Rules," the first installment of her Blood of Eden trilogy. This isn't your typical vampire romance novel – there are no sparkles, no brooding love interests, and no simplistic moral lessons. Instead, Kagawa delivers a dark, gritty, and unapologetic tale of survival in a world where vampires rule and humans are nothing more than prey.

The story follows Allison Sekemoto, a feisty and determined young woman who has grown up on the fringes of vampire society. She's a scavenger, a thief, and a survivor, driven by a deep-seated hatred of the vampires who killed her mother and destroyed her world. But when Allie is attacked and left for dead, she's forced to make a choice: die, or become one of the monsters she's always despised.

As Allie navigates her new existence as a vampire, she's forced to confront the harsh realities of her world. She's no longer human, but she's not quite a monster either – she's something in between, caught in a limbo of moral ambiguity. And it's this gray area that makes "The Immortal Rules" so compelling. Kagawa doesn't shy away from the darker aspects of vampire lore, but she also doesn't resort to simplistic villainy. Instead, she creates a complex web of characters and motivations that blur the lines between good and evil.

One of the most striking aspects of Kagawa's writing style is her ability to create a sense of tension and unease. From the opening pages, it's clear that this is a world on the brink of collapse – a world where the rules are constantly shifting, and the stakes are always high. Allie's journey is a perilous one, fraught with danger and uncertainty, and Kagawa's prose perfectly captures the sense of urgency and desperation that drives her protagonist.

But what really sets "The Immortal Rules" apart is its thought-provoking exploration of what it means to be human. As Allie struggles to come to terms with her new existence, she's forced to confront the nature of humanity – and the ways in which it's both fragile and resilient. Kagawa raises important questions about the value of human life, the morality of survival, and the true cost of immortality. And she does it all without resorting to easy answers or simplistic moral lessons.

Overall, "The Immortal Rules" is a gripping and unapologetic thrill ride that will appeal to fans of dark fantasy and science fiction. Kagawa's writing is taut and suspenseful, her characters are complex and multi-dimensional, and her world-building is meticulous and immersive. If you're looking for a book that will keep you up all night, wondering what happens next, then this is the perfect choice.
