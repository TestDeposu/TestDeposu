# Crying in H Mart

**Author:** Michelle Zauner  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

Crying in H Mart, the debut memoir by Michelle Zauner, is a poignant and deeply personal exploration of identity, culture, and the power of food to evoke emotions and memories. As the lead singer of the indie rock band Japanese Breakfast, Zauner's writing is as evocative and lyrical as her music, transporting readers on a journey of self-discovery and healing.

With unflinching honesty, Zauner recounts her experiences growing up as a Korean American in Eugene, Oregon, where she often felt like an outsider among her peers. Her mother's high expectations and strict adherence to traditional Korean values often led to painful conflicts and a sense of disconnection from her heritage. However, it was through food that Zauner and her mother were able to bond and find common ground, sharing late-night meals of Korean delicacies in her grandmother's tiny apartment in Seoul.

As Zauner navigates her way through adolescence and young adulthood, her connection to her Korean roots begins to fray. Moving to the East Coast for college and pursuing a career in music, she finds herself increasingly distanced from the culture and traditions of her childhood. It isn't until her mother's diagnosis with terminal cancer that Zauner is forced to confront her own identity and the role that her heritage plays in her life.

Through her vivid and often humorous storytelling, Zauner takes readers on a journey of rediscovery, as she learns to reclaim and celebrate her Korean American identity. With the help of her mother's recipes and the memories they evoke, Zauner finds a way to honor her mother's legacy and connect with her own sense of self. The result is a powerful and moving exploration of the ways in which food, family, and culture shape us, and the ways in which we can reclaim and redefine our heritage in the face of loss and adversity.

One of the most striking aspects of Zauner's writing is her ability to balance humor and pathos, often in the same sentence. Her voice is plainspoken and accessible, yet also lyrical and poetic, making her story feel both deeply personal and universally relatable. The inclusion of family photos and recipes adds a touching and intimate element to the narrative, drawing readers into Zauner's world and making them feel like they are experiencing her journey alongside her.

Ultimately, Crying in H Mart is a book about the power of food to evoke emotions, memories, and a sense of connection to our past and our heritage. It is a testament to the enduring bond between mothers and daughters, and the ways in which our relationships with others shape us into the people we become. With its beautiful prose, vivid storytelling, and unflinching honesty, Zauner's memoir is a must-read for anyone who has ever struggled to find their place in the world, or to reconcile their cultural heritage with their own sense of identity.
