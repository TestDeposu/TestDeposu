# The Final Gambit (The Inheritance Games, #3)

**Author:** Jennifer Lynn Barnes  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

In "The Final Gambit," Jennifer Lynn Barnes brings to a close the Inheritance Games trilogy, a series that has captivated readers with its intricate plot twists, complex characters, and high-stakes games of wealth and power. This third installment is a masterclass in suspense and deception, as Avery Grambs navigates the treacherous landscape of the Hawthorne family's world.

At its core, "The Final Gambit" is a story about the lengths to which people will go to protect their interests and the true meaning of family. Avery, a brilliant and fiercely independent protagonist, finds herself caught in a web of secrets and lies as she tries to unravel the mystery of Tobias Hawthorne's will and the reason behind her unexpected inheritance. The Hawthorne grandsons, each with their own distinct personality and motivations, add to the complexity of the narrative, making it impossible to discern friend from foe.

Barnes' writing style is as engaging as ever, with a keen eye for detail and a talent for crafting scenes that are both atmospheric and tense. Her use of language is economical, yet evocative, painting a vivid picture of the opulent world of the Hawthornes and the dark underbelly that lies beneath. The pacing is relentless, with each chapter ending on a cliffhanger that propels the reader forward, desperate to uncover the truth.

One of the strengths of the series is its ability to balance character development with plot progression. Avery, in particular, undergoes significant growth as she navigates the challenges of her new reality. Her relationships with the Hawthorne brothers are multifaceted and nuanced, adding depth to the narrative. The themes of class, privilege, and the corrupting influence of power are timely and thought-provoking, making "The Final Gambit" more than just a simple thriller.

If there's a criticism to be made, it's that the supporting cast can feel somewhat one-dimensional at times. The Hawthorne brothers, while distinct, occasionally feel like archetypes rather than fully fleshed-out characters. Additionally, the plot twists, while surprising, can feel slightly contrived, relying on convenient coincidences and unexplained events.

However, these are minor quibbles in an otherwise engaging and addictive read. "The Final Gambit" is a satisfying conclusion to the Inheritance Games trilogy, providing answers to the questions that have been plaguing readers since the first book. Barnes' fans will be delighted by the return to the world of the Hawthornes, and new readers will find plenty to enjoy in this standalone thriller.

In short, "The Final Gambit" is a gripping, unputdownable read that will appeal to fans of "One of Us is Lying" and "Knives Out." With its breakneck pacing, complex characters, and timely themes, it's a must-read for anyone who enjoys a good thriller.
