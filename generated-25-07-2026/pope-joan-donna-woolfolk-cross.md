# Pope Joan

**Author:** Donna Woolfolk Cross  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1996

Pope Joan, written by Donna Woolfolk Cross, is a masterful historical novel that delves into the fascinating story of a woman who dared to defy the conventions of her time. The book tells the tale of a young girl named Joan, who, after her brother's brutal murder, assumes his identity and enters a monastery, eventually rising to become the first and only female Pope in the history of the Catholic Church. This gripping narrative is set against the backdrop of the Dark Ages, an era marked by violence, oppression, and strict social hierarchies.

Cross's writing is evocative and immersive, transporting the reader to a world of medieval splendor and brutality. Her vivid descriptions of the monasteries, the Vatican, and the landscapes of ninth-century Europe are meticulously researched and richly detailed, creating a sense of authenticity that draws the reader in. The author's use of language is engaging and accessible, making the book an enjoyable read for both history enthusiasts and fiction lovers alike.

At the heart of the novel lies the theme of female empowerment and the struggle for equality in a patriarchal society. Joan's journey is a testament to the strength and resilience of women, who, despite the odds stacked against them, can achieve greatness through determination and hard work. Cross's portrayal of Joan is nuanced and complex, revealing a multifaceted character driven by a desire to learn, to grow, and to make a difference in the world.

The author's style is notable for its blend of historical accuracy and creative license. While the story is rooted in fact, Cross takes liberties with the narrative to create a compelling and engaging tale. The result is a book that is both informative and entertaining, offering a unique perspective on a fascinating period in history. The characters are well-developed and relatable, with Joan being a particularly strong protagonist. Her relationships with the people around her, including her mentors, friends, and lovers, are authentic and emotionally resonant, adding depth and complexity to the story.

One of the most striking aspects of the book is its exploration of the tensions between faith and power. As Joan rises through the ranks of the Church, she must navigate the treacherous waters of medieval politics, confronting corruption, deceit, and betrayal. Cross's depiction of the inner workings of the Church is both fascinating and disturbing, revealing a world of intrigue and corruption that is both familiar and foreign.

In conclusion, Pope Joan is a captivating and thought-provoking novel that will appeal to readers of historical fiction, feminist literature, and anyone interested in the history of the Catholic Church. Cross's engaging writing style, combined with her meticulous research and creative vision, make for a compelling narrative that is both informative and entertaining. The book is a testament to the power of the human spirit, particularly the spirit of women, who, despite the obstacles in their path, can achieve greatness through determination and perseverance.
