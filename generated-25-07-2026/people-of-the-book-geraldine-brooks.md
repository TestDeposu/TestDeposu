# People of the Book

**Author:** Geraldine Brooks  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2008

In the masterful hands of Geraldine Brooks, the Pulitzer Prize-winning author of March, comes a sweeping narrative that delves into the mysteries of a centuries-old illuminated manuscript, the Sarajevo Haggadah. People of the Book is a mesmerizing tale that weaves together historical grandeur and intimate emotional intensity, transporting readers on a journey through time and across continents.

At the heart of the novel is Hanna Heath, a brilliant and fiercely independent Australian rare-book expert, who is tasked with the analysis and conservation of the Haggadah. As Hanna delves deeper into the book's secrets, she discovers a series of tiny artifacts hidden within its ancient binding, each one unlocking a new chapter in the manuscript's storied past. From the war-torn streets of Sarajevo to the salons of fin-de-siècle Vienna, the hedonistic courts of inquisition-era Venice, and the agonized exiles of 15th-century Spain, the Haggadah's journey is a testament to the power of art and faith to transcend time and circumstance.

Through Hanna's investigation, Brooks masterfully interweaves a rich tapestry of historical events, figures, and cultures, bringing to life the complex and often fraught relationships between Muslims, Jews, and Christians across the centuries. The novel is a powerful exploration of the themes of identity, community, and the human condition, raising profound questions about the nature of faith, art, and survival.

Brooks's prose is as elegant as it is evocative, conjuring the sights, sounds, and scents of a bygone era with breathtaking precision. Her characters are multidimensional and richly drawn, from the enigmatic Hanna to the scribes, artists, and scholars who created and protected the Haggadah over the centuries. The author's meticulous research and deep understanding of the historical period are evident on every page, imbuing the narrative with a sense of authenticity and authority.

One of the novel's greatest strengths is its ability to balance the epic sweep of history with the intimate, personal stories of its characters. Brooks's writing is both lyrical and accessible, making the novel an absolute pleasure to read. As Hanna's investigation unfolds, the reader is drawn into a world of intrigue and suspense, where the stakes are both personal and global.

People of the Book is a triumph of historical fiction, a novel that will appeal to fans of Umberto Eco, A.S. Byatt, and Tracy Chevalier. It is a testament to the power of storytelling to connect us across cultures, continents, and centuries, and to the enduring legacy of art and faith to inspire, uplift, and transform us.
