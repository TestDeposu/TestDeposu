# Sarah, Plain and Tall (Sarah, Plain and Tall, #1)

**Author:** Patricia MacLachlan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

Sarah, Plain and Tall, written by the acclaimed author Patricia MacLachlan, is a timeless and poignant tale that gently explores the themes of abandonment, loss, and love. This Newbery Medal-winning book, first published in 1985 but re-released in 2013, is a beautifully crafted chapter book that has captivated readers of all ages with its rich and evocative language.

The story, told from the perspective of young Anna, revolves around the arrival of Sarah Elisabeth Wheaton, a mail-order bride from Maine, who comes to the prairie to answer Papa's advertisement for a wife and mother. As Anna and her younger brother Caleb await Sarah's arrival, they are filled with questions and doubts about this stranger who may become their new mother. Will she be nice? Will she sing? And most importantly, will she stay?

Through Sarah's character, MacLachlan masterfully weaves a narrative that explores the complexities of love, loss, and identity. Sarah, a woman who loves the sea, must adapt to a new life on the prairie, where the vast open spaces and endless skies are a far cry from the familiar rhythms of the ocean. As she struggles to find her place in this new environment, she must also confront the pain of her own past and learn to love again.

One of the most striking aspects of MacLachlan's writing is her ability to convey complex emotions and themes through simple, yet powerful language. Her prose is like a gentle breeze on a summer day, soothing and calming, yet capable of stirring deep emotions. The characters in the book are multidimensional and relatable, with Anna's narrative voice being particularly noteworthy for its innocence, curiosity, and vulnerability.

The themes of abandonment and loss are handled with great sensitivity and care, making this book an excellent choice for children who have experienced similar situations. MacLachlan's portrayal of the healing process is both realistic and hopeful, offering a message of resilience and the transformative power of love.

The anniversary edition of Sarah, Plain and Tall includes a number of additional features, such as the author's Newbery speech, a discussion guide, and a reading list, which make it an excellent choice for both independent reading and classroom use.

In conclusion, Sarah, Plain and Tall is a beautifully written and deeply moving book that explores the complexities of love, loss, and identity. With its rich language, memorable characters, and timeless themes, it is a must-read for anyone who has ever experienced the pain of loss or the joy of finding love again.
