# Obsidian (Lux, #1)

**Author:** Jennifer L. Armentrout  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

Jennifer L. Armentrout’s *Obsidian* (Lux, #1) is a high-octane, genre-blending romp that fuses alien romance, supernatural intrigue, and the kind of snarky, slow-burn banter that makes even the most jaded reader’s heart flutter. Published in 2012, this debut novel in the *Lux* series doesn’t just dip its toes into the paranormal romance pool—it cannonballs in, splashing readers with a heady mix of danger, desire, and extraterrestrial drama. At its core, *Obsidian* is a story about identity, belonging, and the messy, electrifying chaos of falling for someone who’s not just *different*—but literally *not from here*.

The premise is deceptively simple: Katy Swartz, a bookish, blog-obsessed teen, moves to the backwoods of West Virginia just before her senior year, expecting a dull existence in a town where the biggest thrill is the occasional power outage. That is, until she meets her infuriatingly gorgeous neighbor, Daemon Black. Tall, green-eyed, and dripping with arrogance, Daemon is the kind of character who makes you want to slap him and kiss him in the same breath. But when a mysterious attacker targets Katy, Daemon’s true nature is revealed—he’s an alien from the Luxen species, a race of beings with godlike abilities, and he’s just saved her life. Cue the inevitable: Katy’s world implodes, her safety is compromised, and her heart is a traitorous mess of confusion because, despite everything, she’s *attracted* to this smug, otherworldly jerk.

Armentrout’s genius lies in her ability to balance the absurd with the emotionally resonant. The alien premise could easily veer into campy territory, but *Obsidian* grounds its sci-fi elements in raw, relatable human emotions. Katy’s internal monologue is a masterclass in wit and vulnerability—she’s smart, self-deprecating, and refreshingly honest about her insecurities. Her voice is the anchor that keeps the story from spiraling into pure fantasy. Meanwhile, Daemon is the quintessential "bad boy with a heart of gold" (or, in this case, a Luxen with a galaxy of enemies), but Armentrout avoids making him a one-dimensional trope. His arrogance is a defense mechanism, a shield against the loneliness of being an outsider in a world that would fear or exploit him. The push-and-pull between him and Katy isn’t just about attraction; it’s about two outsiders recognizing something in each other that no one else understands.

The pacing is relentless, with Armentrout doling out revelations like a seasoned thriller writer. The stakes escalate quickly—Katy’s life is in danger not just because she’s near Daemon, but because the Luxen’s enemies, the Arum, are hunting them both. The action sequences are vivid and cinematic, while the quieter moments between Katy and Daemon crackle with tension. Their chemistry is the kind that makes you groan at their bickering one minute and swoon at their vulnerability the next. Armentrout’s dialogue is sharp, snappy, and often hilarious, with Katy’s sarcastic asides providing a perfect counterbalance to Daemon’s brooding intensity.

What truly sets *Obsidian* apart, however, is its exploration of what it means to be an outsider. Katy, a city girl in a rural town, already feels like she doesn’t belong. Daemon, an alien hiding in plain sight, is literally not from Earth. Their relationship forces both characters to confront their fears about acceptance and trust. The novel doesn’t shy away from the darker implications of Daemon’s existence either—his powers come with a price, and his enemies are ruthless. The world-building is immersive, with Armentrout weaving in Luxen culture, intergalactic politics, and even a touch of mythology without ever overwhelming the reader.

Armentrout’s writing style is addictive. She has a knack for making the fantastical feel tangible, whether she’s describing the eerie glow of Daemon’s eyes or the visceral terror of an Arum attack. Her prose is punchy and accessible, with just enough lyrical flourish to elevate the storytelling without bogging it down. The romance isn’t just a subplot; it’s the driving force of the narrative, and Armentrout handles it with a deft touch. There’s no forced instalove here—Katy and Daemon’s relationship develops organically, built on shared secrets, mutual respect, and, yes, a healthy dose of lust.

That said, *Obsidian* isn’t without its flaws. Some readers might find Daemon’s possessiveness and occasional brooding grating, and the love triangle (or lack thereof) is resolved in a way that some might argue is too neat. The world-building, while rich, could benefit from even more depth in later books. But these are minor quibbles in a novel that delivers exactly what it promises: a thrilling, emotional, and utterly addictive ride.

At its heart, *Obsidian* is a story about finding your place in the universe—literally and figuratively. It’s about the people who make you feel like you’ve finally come home, even when the world is burning around you. And with its blend of humor, heart, and high-stakes drama, it’s a book that lingers long after the last page.
