# Small Gods (Discworld, #13)

**Author:** Terry Pratchett  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

In the boundless realm of the Discworld series, Terry Pratchett's "Small Gods" stands out as a scathing critique of organized religion, masterfully woven with the author's trademark wit and satire. This thirteenth installment in the series is a thought-provoking exploration of the corrupting influence of power and the exploitation of faith, set against the backdrop of a world where gods are as plentiful as they are petty.

At the heart of the narrative lies the unlikely duo of Om, a once-great god now manifested as a lowly tortoise, and Brutha, a simple-minded yet endearing young man who becomes Om's reluctant acolyte. As Om navigates the complexities of mortal life, Brutha finds himself drawn into a world of intrigue and deception, where the pursuit of power and wealth masquerades as devotion.

Pratchett's writing is a masterclass in comedic storytelling, with a keen eye for the absurdities of human nature and the institutions we create. His characters are multidimensional and relatable, with even the most minor figures imbued with a depth and richness that belies their humble roles. The dialogue is crisp and engaging, with a keen ear for the cadences of language and the way people interact.

One of the most striking aspects of "Small Gods" is its thought-provoking exploration of the nature of faith and power. Pratchett pulls no punches in his critique of organized religion, lampooning the excesses and hypocrisies of those who would seek to exploit the faithful for their own gain. And yet, despite the biting satire, the novel is ultimately a hopeful and humane work, one that suggests that even in the darkest of times, there is always the potential for redemption and growth.

Throughout the novel, Pratchett's pop culture allusions and clever wordplay add an extra layer of depth and complexity to the narrative, rewarding close attention and multiple readings. The world-building is, as ever, meticulous and detailed, with a keen sense of history and culture that underpins the entire Discworld series.

In short, "Small Gods" is a triumph of comedic fantasy, a novel that will appeal to fans of Pratchett's unique brand of humor and satire, as well as those looking for a thought-provoking exploration of the human condition. Whether you're a longtime fan of the Discworld series or simply looking for a compelling and engaging read, "Small Gods" is a must-read that is sure to leave you laughing, thinking, and maybe even questioning your own assumptions about the world.
