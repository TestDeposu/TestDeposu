# The Twisted Ones

**Author:** T. Kingfisher  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

**The Twisted Ones: A Chilling Tale of Rural Horror**

In the vein of classic folk horror, T. Kingfisher's "The Twisted Ones" is a gripping and atmospheric novel that weaves a tale of terror in the seemingly idyllic woods of rural North Carolina. Winner of the RUSA Award for Best Horror, this book is a must-read for fans of eerie, slow-burn chillers.

Our protagonist, Mouse, is a young woman with a dry wit and a penchant for sarcasm, making her an instantly relatable heroine. When she's tasked with clearing out her late grandmother's house, she expects to find clutter and dust, but not the dark secrets hidden within its walls. As she delves deeper, she uncovers her step-grandfather's journal, filled with disturbing rants about ancient beings lurking in the woods. Initially skeptical, Mouse's disbelief turns to dread as she begins to encounter the very creatures her step-grandfather described.

Kingfisher, a pseudonym for acclaimed author Ursula Vernon, masterfully builds tension, drawing readers in with vivid, almost lyrical prose. The woods become a character in their own right, shifting from a comforting, familiar landscape to a sinister, menacing force. The twisted, gnarled trees and the eerie silence create an unsettling atmosphere that permeates every page.

What sets "The Twisted Ones" apart is its unique blend of horror and humor. Mouse's snarky internal monologue provides moments of levity amidst the creeping dread, reminiscent of the dark comedy in films like "The Cabin in the Woods." Yet, Kingfisher never lets the humor detract from the horror. The creatures in the woods are genuinely terrifying, their otherworldly nature making them all the more chilling.

At its heart, "The Twisted Ones" is a story about the power of belief and the dangers of underestimating the unknown. Mouse's journey from skeptic to believer is compelling, and her growth as a character is one of the novel's strongest points. Kingfisher also explores themes of family, legacy, and the dark secrets that can be buried in small towns.

Fans of folk horror, slow-burn chillers, and atmospheric reads will find much to love in "The Twisted Ones." It's a novel that gets under your skin, leaving you questioning what might be lurking just beyond your own backyard. Just don't read it alone in the woods.
