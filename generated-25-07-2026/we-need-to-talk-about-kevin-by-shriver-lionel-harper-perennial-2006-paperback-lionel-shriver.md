# We Need to Talk About Kevin by Shriver, Lionel. (Harper Perennial,2006) [Paperback]

**Author:** Lionel Shriver  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

We Need to Talk About Kevin by Lionel Shriver is a heart-wrenching and thought-provoking novel that delves into the complexities of motherhood, marriage, and the darker aspects of human nature. The story revolves around Eva, a mother struggling to come to terms with her son Kevin's heinous crime, and her attempts to understand the events that led up to the tragedy.

Shriver's writing style is remarkable for its ability to balance psychological depth with wry humor, making the novel a compelling and unsettling read. The author's portrayal of Eva's inner turmoil and her relationships with those around her is both nuanced and unflinching, laying bare the intricacies of human emotions. The characters are multidimensional and relatable, with Eva being a particularly well-crafted protagonist.

One of the novel's greatest strengths lies in its ability to tackle a difficult and rarely explored subject in fiction. Shriver handles the topic of maternal ambivalence and the darker aspects of motherhood with sensitivity and candor, raising important questions about the nature of parenting and the complexities of family relationships.

The novel's structure, comprising a series of letters from Eva to her estranged husband, adds to the sense of intimacy and immediacy, drawing the reader into Eva's inner world. Shriver's prose is economical and evocative, conveying the complexity of Eva's emotions through subtle suggestions rather than overt statements.

Throughout the novel, Shriver raises important questions about the responsibilities of parenthood, the consequences of one's actions, and the blurred lines between nature and nurture. The novel is a powerful exploration of the human condition, laying bare the flaws and vulnerabilities that make us human.

In conclusion, We Need to Talk About Kevin is a masterpiece of contemporary literature that will leave readers unsettled, moved, and ultimately, changed. Shriver's writing is a testament to her skill as a storyteller, and her ability to craft a narrative that is both haunting and thought-provoking.
