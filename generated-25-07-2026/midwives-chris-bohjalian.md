# Midwives

**Author:** Chris Bohjalian  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1998

Midwives by Chris Bohjalian is a thought-provoking and emotionally charged novel that delves into the complexities of childbirth, human responsibility, and the ethics of medical decision-making. First published in 1998, this modern classic has captivated readers for decades, and its themes remain as relevant today as they were when the book first hit the shelves.

At the heart of the story is Sibyl Danforth, a seasoned midwife who finds herself at the center of a maelstrom when she performs an emergency cesarean section on a woman she believes has died of a stroke. However, when her assistant later questions whether the patient was indeed deceased, Sibyl's actions are called into question, and she is forced to confront the antagonism of the law, the hostility of traditional doctors, and the accusations of her own conscience.

Bohjalian's masterful storytelling weaves together a narrative that is both a gripping drama and a nuanced exploration of the human experience. Through Sibyl's story, he raises fundamental questions about the nature of responsibility, the limits of medical knowledge, and the complexities of human relationships. As the trial unfolds, the novel builds towards a dramatic climax, keeping readers on the edge of their seats as they grapple with the moral implications of Sibyl's actions.

One of the standout features of Bohjalian's writing is his ability to craft complex, multidimensional characters that leap off the page. Sibyl is a deeply human protagonist, full of flaws and contradictions, and her narrative voice is both compelling and relatable. The supporting cast is equally well-drawn, from the skeptical doctors who question Sibyl's methods to the loyal friends and family who rally around her in her time of need.

Bohjalian's prose is equally impressive, with a lyrical quality that brings the snowy landscapes of rural Vermont to vivid life. His writing is evocative and immersive, making it easy to become fully invested in Sibyl's world and the high-stakes drama that unfolds around her.

If there's a criticism to be made, it's that the novel's pacing can be a touch uneven at times. Some sections feel a little rushed, while others are more leisurely, but this is a minor quibble in an otherwise exceptional novel.

Overall, Midwives is a triumph of contemporary literature, a novel that will appeal to fans of character-driven fiction, medical drama, and stories about strong women. With its thought-provoking themes, vivid prose, and memorable characters, it's no wonder that this book has become a modern classic, selling over two million copies and earning a spot in Oprah's original Book Club.
