# Reflected in You (Crossfire, #2)

**Author:** Sylvia Day  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

Sylvia Day’s *Reflected in You* (Crossfire, #2) is a tempestuous, emotionally charged sequel that plunges readers deeper into the intoxicating—and often treacherous—world of Eva Tramell and Gideon Cross. If the first book in the *Crossfire* series set the stage for their explosive chemistry, this installment fans the flames into a full-blown inferno, both in passion and pain. Day’s prose is a masterclass in sensuality and psychological depth, crafting a narrative that is as intellectually stimulating as it is physically consuming.

At its core, *Reflected in You* is a story about obsession—not just the kind that burns bright in the bedroom, but the kind that gnaws at the soul. Eva and Gideon are two broken souls who recognize their brokenness in each other, a mirror image of each other’s wounds. Their relationship is a paradox: destructive yet necessary, painful yet healing. Day doesn’t shy away from the raw, unfiltered truth of their connection. She dissects it with surgical precision, exposing the vulnerabilities beneath their bravado. Gideon’s tormented past, his controlling nature, and his desperate need for control clash violently with Eva’s own demons—her past abuse, her fear of intimacy, and her struggle to reconcile love with self-preservation. Their love isn’t just a romance; it’s a survival mechanism, a way to outrun the ghosts that haunt them both.

Day’s writing style is a force of nature. She blends lyrical descriptions with sharp, biting dialogue, creating a rhythm that mirrors the push-and-pull of Eva and Gideon’s relationship. The prose is lush, almost decadent in its sensuality, but it never feels gratuitous. Instead, the physical intimacy between the characters serves as a metaphor for their emotional entanglement. When Gideon touches Eva, it’s not just about desire—it’s about reclaiming power, asserting dominance, and, in some twisted way, offering a form of salvation. Their sex scenes are electric, charged with a tension that borders on the unbearable, but they’re also deeply intimate, revealing layers of their psyches that no other character gets to see.

One of the most compelling aspects of the book is how Day handles the darker themes. Abuse, trauma, and psychological manipulation aren’t just backdrop elements; they’re central to the characters’ motivations. Gideon’s possessiveness isn’t romanticized—it’s a coping mechanism, a way to feel in control in a world that has repeatedly stripped him of agency. Eva’s journey is equally complex. She’s not just a damsel in distress; she’s a woman who has endured horrors and yet refuses to be defined by them. Her strength lies in her ability to see Gideon not as a monster, but as a man who is just as lost as she is. Their dynamic is messy, flawed, and utterly human. Day doesn’t offer easy resolutions or neat happy endings. Instead, she forces readers to sit with the discomfort, to question whether love can truly heal when the wounds run so deep.

The pacing of the novel is relentless. Day keeps the tension high, not just through the central romance but through external threats—jealous ex-lovers, corporate sabotage, and Gideon’s own self-destructive tendencies. The stakes feel real, the consequences tangible. There’s a sense of urgency that propels the story forward, making it difficult to put down. Yet, amidst the chaos, there are moments of breathtaking tenderness. Small gestures—a shared glance, a whispered confession—carry as much weight as the grand, sweeping declarations of love.

Day’s ability to balance the erotic with the emotional is what sets her apart. She doesn’t just write steamy scenes; she writes scenes that *matter*. The sex isn’t just about pleasure; it’s about power, vulnerability, and the blurred lines between love and obsession. It’s about two people who are so damaged that they can only express their deepest feelings through physical intimacy, even when it hurts. This isn’t a love story for the faint of heart. It’s raw, unapologetic, and sometimes brutal—but it’s also deeply moving.

For readers who loved the first book, *Reflected in You* will feel like coming home to a storm. It’s intense, addictive, and impossible to ignore. For those new to the series, be warned: this isn’t a light read. It’s a book that demands emotional investment, that pulls you into its orbit and doesn’t let go. Day’s writing is addictive, her characters unforgettable, and her exploration of love as both salvation and destruction utterly compelling.

In the end, *Reflected in You* is more than just a romance novel. It’s a psychological thriller disguised as a love story, a dark fairy tale where the prince is just as broken as the princess, and the only way out is through the fire. If you’re ready to dive into a world where love is as dangerous as it is intoxicating, then this book is your next obsession.
