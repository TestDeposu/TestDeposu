# Gideon the Ninth (The Locked Tomb, #1)

**Author:** Tamsyn Muir  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

Gideon the Ninth, the debut novel by Tamsyn Muir, is a mesmerizing blend of science fantasy, gothic horror, and lesbian romance that defies easy categorization. This gripping tale of swordplay, cut-throat politics, and necromancy is set in a solar system where the Emperor's power is absolute, and the loyal Houses are bound to serve him.

At the heart of the story is Gideon Nav, a skilled swordswoman and foundling of the Ninth House, who is desperate to escape the clutches of the Reverend Daughter, Harrowhark Nonagesimus. Gideon's complex and often fraught relationship with Harrow is a rich source of tension and emotional depth, as the two women are bound together by a shared history and a deep-seated resentment.

Muir's writing style is a major strength of the book, with a unique blend of humor, pathos, and visceral action sequences that will keep readers on the edge of their seats. Her characters are multidimensional and relatable, with Gideon's sharp wit and sarcasm providing a welcome respite from the darkness that pervades the novel.

One of the most striking aspects of Gideon the Ninth is its exploration of themes such as identity, power, and the complexities of female relationships. Muir's portrayal of a lesbian romance between Gideon and Harrow is both tender and nuanced, avoiding the cliches and stereotypes that often plague LGBTQ+ representation in science fiction.

The world-building in Gideon the Ninth is equally impressive, with a richly detailed solar system that is both fascinating and terrifying. The Ninth House, with its labyrinthine corridors and ancient secrets, is a character in its own right, exerting a malign influence over the lives of its inhabitants.

Throughout the novel, Muir's use of language is both evocative and precise, conjuring up vivid images of a decaying, gothic world that is both beautiful and repellent. Her prose is often poetic, with a keen eye for detail that immerses the reader in the world of the Ninth House.

If there are any criticisms to be made, it is that the pacing can be somewhat uneven, with long stretches of exposition that may test the patience of some readers. Additionally, the supporting cast of characters can feel somewhat one-dimensional at times, although this is largely mitigated by the strength of the central relationship between Gideon and Harrow.

In conclusion, Gideon the Ninth is a stunning debut novel that marks Tamsyn Muir as a major talent in the science fiction and fantasy genres. With its unique blend of action, romance, and horror, this book is sure to appeal to fans of authors such as V.E. Schwab and N.K. Jemisin. If you're looking for a compelling and immersive read that will keep you up all night, then Gideon the Ninth is an absolute must-read.
