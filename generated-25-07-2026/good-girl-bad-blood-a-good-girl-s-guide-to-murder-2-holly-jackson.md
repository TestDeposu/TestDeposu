# Good Girl, Bad Blood (A Good Girl's Guide to Murder, #2)

**Author:** Holly Jackson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

Holly Jackson’s *Good Girl, Bad Blood* is a masterclass in suspense, a sequel that deepens the mystery of *A Good Girl’s Guide to Murder* while sharpening its psychological edge. This isn’t just another YA thriller—it’s a meticulously plotted puzzle where every clue feels like a knife twist to the gut. Jackson doesn’t just write mysteries; she crafts them like a spider spinning a web, each thread pulling tighter until the reader is trapped in the story’s suffocating grip.

At its core, *Good Girl, Bad Blood* is about obsession—not just Pip’s relentless pursuit of the truth, but the way secrets fester in a town that refuses to let go of its past. Fairview is a character itself, a place where the shadow of Andie Bell’s murder still looms, where whispers cling to every street corner like cobwebs. Jackson excels at capturing the claustrophobic atmosphere of a small town where everyone knows everyone’s business, yet no one truly knows the truth. The book’s strength lies in its duality: it’s both a classic whodunit and a psychological thriller, where the real mystery isn’t just *who* killed Andie Bell, but *why* the town is so desperate to believe Sal Singh was the killer.

Jackson’s prose is razor-sharp, blending sharp dialogue with a narrative voice that’s equal parts sardonic and vulnerable. Pip, our protagonist, is a standout—a flawed, determined heroine whose intelligence is both her greatest asset and her Achilles’ heel. She’s not a perfect detective; she makes mistakes, she misreads people, and her obsession with the case borders on self-destruction. This imperfection makes her relatable, and Jackson ensures that the reader is just as invested in Pip’s well-being as they are in solving the mystery. The book’s pacing is relentless, with each chapter peeling back another layer of deception, each revelation more shocking than the last.

One of the most compelling aspects of *Good Girl, Bad Blood* is how it subverts expectations. Jackson doesn’t just give readers a mystery to solve; she gives them a psychological minefield. The line between past and present blurs dangerously, and the more Pip digs, the more she risks becoming a target herself. The tension is palpable, the stakes sky-high, and the twists? They’re the kind that leave you gasping, flipping pages frantically to see if you’ve missed something.

Jackson’s style is deceptively simple—clean, direct, and devoid of unnecessary flourish—but it’s this precision that makes the story so gripping. She trusts the reader to keep up, to piece together the clues alongside Pip, and the payoff is electrifying. The book’s structure, alternating between past and present, mirrors Pip’s own fractured state of mind, making the reading experience as disorienting as it is immersive.

What truly sets *Good Girl, Bad Blood* apart, however, is its emotional depth. Beneath the surface-level mystery lies a meditation on grief, guilt, and the way trauma lingers in a community. Pip isn’t just solving a cold case; she’s confronting her own demons, her own complicity in the town’s collective denial. The book’s climax is a gut-punch, a moment where the personal and the procedural collide in a way that feels both inevitable and heartbreaking.

Jackson’s ability to balance humor, heart, and horror is nothing short of remarkable. The dialogue crackles with wit, the characters are vividly drawn, and the suspense never lets up. By the final page, the reader is left breathless, questioning everything they thought they knew—and already desperate for the next installment.

*Good Girl, Bad Blood* is more than a sequel; it’s a triumph of the genre. It’s the kind of book that lingers long after the last page, the kind that makes you look at your own small town with a newfound sense of paranoia. Holly Jackson doesn’t just write mysteries—she writes experiences, and *Good Girl, Bad Blood* is one you won’t forget.
