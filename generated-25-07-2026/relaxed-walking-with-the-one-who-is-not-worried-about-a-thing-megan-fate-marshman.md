# Relaxed: Walking with the One Who Is Not Worried about a Thing

**Author:** Megan Fate Marshman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2024

Megan Fate Marshman’s *Relaxed: Walking with the One Who Is Not Worried about a Thing* is a timely and transformative invitation to shed the exhausting burden of spiritual performance and rediscover the peace of simply being with God. In a world that glorifies hustle, productivity, and self-reliance—even within Christian circles—Marshman’s message is a refreshing countercultural anthem. With over 100,000 copies already in circulation, it’s clear that readers are hungry for a faith that doesn’t demand constant striving but instead offers rest, trust, and presence.

At its core, *Relaxed* is a meditation on Proverbs 3:5-6: “Trust in the Lord with all your heart and lean not on your own understanding; in all your ways submit to him, and he will make your paths straight.” Marshman unpacks these verses with disarming honesty, blending personal storytelling, biblical exposition, and practical wisdom to challenge the reader’s default mode of anxiety-driven control. Her central thesis is simple yet radical: God doesn’t want us to live in a state of perpetual stress, micromanaging our lives as if we’re the ones steering the ship. Instead, he invites us into a relaxed, trusting relationship where we can stop trying to figure everything out and simply walk with him.

What makes Marshman’s approach so compelling is her refusal to sugarcoat the realities of life. She doesn’t offer a prosperity gospel or a quick fix for stress; rather, she acknowledges the very real struggles of grief, financial strain, fatigue, and shame—yet frames them within the context of God’s unshaken sovereignty. “God never intended for us to be in control,” she writes, and this theme permeates every chapter. Whether she’s discussing the weight of past mistakes or the pressure to perform spiritually, her tone is one of gentle conviction, never judgmental but always pointing toward grace.

Marshman’s writing style is warm, conversational, and deeply personal. She doesn’t write like a distant theologian but like a trusted friend who’s walked through the same anxieties and has found, albeit imperfectly, a better way. Her use of imagery—particularly the recurring motif of walking—is both poetic and practical. She invites the reader to imagine Jesus strolling through life at a leisurely pace, undistracted by urgency, fully present in each moment. This isn’t passivity; it’s a radical act of faith. By slowing down, we align ourselves with God’s rhythm, trusting that he is indeed at work even when we can’t see it.

One of the book’s strongest sections is where Marshman addresses shame and guilt. She doesn’t dismiss the pain of failure but reframes it through the lens of being “found in Christ.” Her words here feel like a balm: “The freedom and forgiveness of being in Christ means we don’t have to carry the weight of our past.” This isn’t just theological abstraction; it’s an invitation to experience the relief of resting in God’s acceptance rather than clawing our way toward it.

The book’s structure follows the flow of Proverbs 3:5-6, which gives it a natural, almost meditative rhythm. Each chapter digs deeper into a facet of trust—from releasing the need to control outcomes to embracing the mystery of God’s guidance. Marshman’s exegesis is accessible, avoiding jargon while still being theologically rich. She doesn’t just tell the reader to trust God; she shows them how, through stories from Scripture and her own life.

That said, *Relaxed* isn’t a book that demands immediate transformation. Marshman’s tone is invitational rather than prescriptive, which means some readers might find it slower going if they’re used to more direct, action-oriented Christian books. But this is precisely the point: the book itself models the relaxed posture it advocates. It doesn’t rush the reader; it walks alongside them.

For anyone feeling the weight of spiritual burnout, performance-based faith, or the relentless pressure to “do more for God,” *Relaxed* is a lifeline. It’s a call to stop performing and start trusting, to stop striving and start walking. And in a culture that rewards busyness, Marshman’s message is nothing short of revolutionary.
