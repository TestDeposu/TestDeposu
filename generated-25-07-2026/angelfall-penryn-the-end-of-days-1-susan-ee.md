# Angelfall (Penryn & the End of Days, #1)

**Author:** Susan Ee  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

In the ravaged streets of San Francisco, a desperate quest for survival unfolds in Susan Ee's captivating novel, Angelfall. This gripping tale of good vs. evil, set in a post-apocalyptic world, delves into the darkest corners of human nature and the unyielding power of hope.

The story follows Penryn, a fiercely determined young woman, as she navigates the treacherous landscape of a world overrun by angels. With her sister Paige kidnapped by the angelic forces, Penryn's resolve is tested like never before. Her journey is fraught with peril, and it is here that Ee's masterful storytelling shines. The author's vivid descriptions of a desolate San Francisco, now a mere shadow of its former self, are both haunting and mesmerizing.

As Penryn embarks on her perilous quest, she encounters Raffe, a fallen angel whose motives are shrouded in mystery. Their unlikely alliance is forged in the fires of desperation, and it is through their interactions that Ee explores the complexities of morality and the true nature of evil. Raffe's character, in particular, is a fascinating study in contrasts, embodying both the cruelty and the vulnerability that define the angelic host.

Ee's writing style is economical yet evocative, conveying the intensity of Penryn's emotions with unflinching honesty. Her prose is lean and mean, propelling the reader through a narrative that is both relentless and engaging. The pacing is expertly handled, with moments of high-octane action juxtaposed with quieter, more introspective scenes that add depth to the story.

One of the most striking aspects of Angelfall is its thought-provoking exploration of the human condition. In a world where the boundaries between good and evil are constantly blurred, Ee challenges her characters – and, by extension, the reader – to confront the darkness within. This is a novel that asks difficult questions and refuses to provide easy answers, instead inviting the reader to ponder the implications of a world in chaos.

If there is a criticism to be made, it is that the supporting cast is somewhat one-dimensional, with some characters feeling more like archetypes than fully fleshed-out human beings. However, this is a minor quibble in an otherwise compelling narrative.

In conclusion, Angelfall is a gripping and unsettling novel that will appeal to fans of dystopian fiction and urban fantasy. With its breakneck pacing, vivid world-building, and complex characters, it is a story that will linger in the reader's mind long after the final page has been turned.
