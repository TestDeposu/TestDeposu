# The Girl Who Kicked the Hornet’s Nest (Millennium, #3)

**Author:** Stieg Larsson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

The Girl Who Kicked the Hornet's Nest, the final installment in Stieg Larsson's Millennium trilogy, is a gripping and intense thriller that masterfully weaves together themes of corruption, power, and the resilience of the human spirit. Picking up where the previous book left off, Lisbeth Salander, the enigmatic and brilliant protagonist, lies in a hospital bed with a bullet in her head, fighting for her life. As she struggles to recover, she faces the daunting prospect of standing trial for three murders, a fate that seems all but certain.

Meanwhile, Mikael Blomkvist, the dogged and principled journalist, is determined to clear Lisbeth's name and uncover the truth behind the conspiracy that has driven her to the brink of destruction. As the story unfolds, Larsson masterfully exposes the dark underbelly of Swedish society, revealing a complex web of corruption and deceit that reaches the highest echelons of power.

One of the most striking aspects of Larsson's writing is his ability to craft complex, multidimensional characters that leap off the page. Lisbeth Salander, in particular, is a fascinating and deeply flawed protagonist, driven by a fierce determination to survive and a deep-seated need for revenge against those who have wronged her. Through her character, Larsson explores themes of trauma, abuse, and the long-term effects of violence on the human psyche.

Larsson's prose is lean and concise, with a keen attention to detail that makes the world of the novel feel eerily real. His writing is also notable for its strong sense of social justice, as he tackles issues such as government corruption, police brutality, and the exploitation of vulnerable individuals. The result is a novel that is both a gripping thriller and a scathing critique of modern society.

Throughout the book, Larsson's style is characterized by a sense of urgency and momentum, as the plot twists and turns through a complex series of events. The pacing is relentless, with a keen sense of tension that propels the reader forward to the very end. The supporting cast of characters is equally well-drawn, from the dogged police inspector, Jan Bublanski, to the sinister figures who lurk in the shadows, pulling the strings of power.

If there is a criticism to be made, it is that the novel can feel overwhelming at times, with a complex web of characters and plot threads that can be difficult to keep track of. However, this is a minor quibble in what is otherwise a masterful thriller.

In conclusion, The Girl Who Kicked the Hornet's Nest is a gripping and intense thriller that is sure to appeal to fans of the Millennium series. With its complex characters, intricate plotting, and strong sense of social justice, it is a must-read for anyone who enjoys a good mystery. Larsson's writing is lean and concise, with a keen attention to detail that makes the world of the novel feel eerily real.
