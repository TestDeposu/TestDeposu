# Rich Dad Poor Dad: What the Rich Teach Their Kids About Money—That the Poor and Middle Class Do Not!

**Author:** Robert T. Kiyosaki  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

Rich Dad Poor Dad: What the Rich Teach Their Kids About Money—That the Poor and Middle Class Do Not! by Robert T. Kiyosaki is a thought-provoking book that challenges conventional wisdom about money and wealth. The book, which has been a New York Times bestseller since its initial publication in 1997, has been widely acclaimed for its refreshing and insightful take on personal finance.

Through the lens of his own life experiences, Kiyosaki recounts the contrasting financial philosophies of his two fathers: his own "poor dad," who was a well-educated but financially struggling government worker, and his best friend's "rich dad," a successful businessman who became a mentor to him. The book is a distillation of the wisdom and knowledge that Kiyosaki gained from his "rich dad," who taught him the importance of financial literacy, investing, and building wealth.

One of the key takeaways from the book is the idea that the rich do not just work for money; they make their money work for them. Kiyosaki argues that the poor and middle class often get caught up in the "rat race" of working for a salary, paying taxes, and spending what's left, without ever really accumulating wealth. In contrast, the rich focus on building assets, such as real estate and businesses, that generate passive income and increase in value over time.

Kiyosaki's writing style is engaging and accessible, making the book an enjoyable read for anyone interested in personal finance. He uses anecdotes and real-life examples to illustrate complex financial concepts, making them easy to understand and relate to. The book is also peppered with motivational quotes and inspiring stories of people who have achieved financial freedom through smart investing and entrepreneurship.

One of the strengths of the book is its emphasis on financial education. Kiyosaki stresses the importance of learning about money and investing, rather than simply relying on others to manage one's finances. He also encourages readers to take control of their financial lives and to think creatively about how to build wealth.

If there is one criticism to be made of the book, it is that some of the advice may be overly simplistic or not applicable to everyone's circumstances. For example, Kiyosaki's emphasis on real estate investing may not be feasible for those who live in areas with high property prices or strict rental laws. Additionally, some readers may find his criticism of the education system and the conventional wisdom about money to be overly harsh or dismissive.

Overall, however, Rich Dad Poor Dad is a valuable resource for anyone looking to improve their financial literacy and build wealth. The book's core message – that financial freedom is within reach, but requires education, hard work, and smart investing – is inspiring and empowering. Whether you're a seasoned investor or just starting out on your financial journey, this book is sure to provide valuable insights and practical advice.
