# Real (Real, #1)

**Author:** Katy Evans  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

Real by Katy Evans is a captivating love story that delves into the intense world of underground fighting, where ambition and desire collide. The novel follows Brooke Dumas, a driven sports rehabilitation specialist, as she becomes entangled in a scorching romance with the infamous Remington Tate, a brooding and enigmatic fighter.

Evans' writing style is instantly immersive, drawing readers into the raw and visceral world of underground fighting. The author's vivid descriptions of Remy's physique and fighting prowess are undeniably captivating, and her portrayal of the high-octane atmosphere surrounding the fights is both thrilling and intense. However, it's the emotional depth and complexity of the characters that truly sets this novel apart.

At its core, Real is a story about the blurred lines between desire and obsession, and the devastating consequences that can arise when these emotions are left unchecked. Remy and Brooke's relationship is a powder keg of intensity, with a palpable chemistry that crackles between them from the very start. As their connection deepens, Evans skillfully explores the darker aspects of their personalities, revealing the deep-seated insecurities and fears that drive them.

One of the most striking aspects of Evans' writing is her ability to craft relatable, multidimensional characters. Brooke is a strong and determined protagonist, yet she's also vulnerable and unsure, struggling to reconcile her desire for Remy with her growing sense of unease. Remy, too, is a masterfully crafted character, with a tough exterior that slowly begins to crack, revealing a complex and troubled individual beneath.

The author's handling of the novel's themes is equally impressive. Evans tackles issues of addiction, family obligations, and the objectification of women with sensitivity and nuance, never shying away from the harsh realities of Remy's world. The result is a narrative that's both unflinchingly honest and deeply compassionate, with a strong sense of emotional authenticity that resonates long after the final page has been turned.

If there's one criticism to be made, it's that the supporting cast feels slightly underdeveloped at times, with some characters feeling more like caricatures than fully fleshed-out individuals. However, this is a minor quibble in an otherwise engaging and emotionally charged narrative.

In conclusion, Real is a gripping and intense love story that's sure to captivate fans of contemporary romance. With its vivid writing, complex characters, and unflinching exploration of themes, this novel is a must-read for anyone looking for a romance that's both passionate and thought-provoking.
