# Four: A Divergent Story Collection (Divergent, #0.1-0.4)

**Author:** Veronica Roth  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

Four: A Divergent Story Collection is more than just a companion to Veronica Roth’s blockbuster trilogy—it’s a masterclass in character-driven storytelling, offering readers a rare glimpse into the mind of Tobias Eaton before he became the brooding legend known as Four. This collection of four prequels and three bonus scenes from *Divergent* is a compelling exploration of identity, transformation, and the fractures within a society that demands conformity. Roth doesn’t just expand the world of *Divergent*; she deepens it by peeling back the layers of a character who has always been shrouded in mystery.

At its core, *Four* is a story about the cost of reinvention. Tobias’s journey from Abnegation to Dauntless is not just a physical transfer between factions—it’s a psychological unraveling of everything he was raised to believe. Roth’s prose captures the visceral discomfort of leaving behind a life of self-denial for one of raw, unfiltered action. The first three stories—"The Transfer," "The Initiate," and "The Son"—are brutal in their honesty. Tobias’s initiation into Dauntless is less about physical endurance and more about emotional endurance, as he grapples with the guilt of abandoning his family, the fear of failure, and the intoxicating allure of a faction that promises strength over sacrifice. Roth’s writing is sharp, almost clinical in its precision, yet it pulses with the raw emotion of a boy who is both running *toward* something and running *away* from everything else.

What makes *Four* so compelling is Roth’s ability to weave Tobias’s personal struggles into the larger dystopian framework of *Divergent*. The faction system isn’t just a backdrop here—it’s a character in itself, a force that shapes Tobias’s every decision. His transfer to Dauntless isn’t just a plot point; it’s a rebellion against a system that stifles individuality. Yet, even in his new faction, Tobias is never fully free. The initiation process is a metaphor for the suffocating expectations of society, and Roth doesn’t shy away from showing the psychological toll it takes. The scenes where Tobias is pushed to his limits—both physically and emotionally—are some of the most gripping in the collection. They’re not just about surviving; they’re about *becoming*.

The fourth story, "The Traitor," is where the collection truly shines. Set parallel to the events of *Divergent*, it offers a fresh perspective on the early days of Tobias and Tris’s relationship. Roth’s decision to tell this story from Tobias’s point of view is brilliant, as it humanizes a character who is often seen as aloof or even antagonistic in the original trilogy. We see his hesitation, his protectiveness, and the quiet moments of vulnerability that make him more than just a love interest. The bonus scenes from *Divergent* further enrich this narrative, giving readers a deeper understanding of Tobias’s motivations and the internal conflicts that define him.

Roth’s style is direct and immersive, with a focus on immediacy that pulls the reader into Tobias’s world. Her prose is clean and uncluttered, yet it carries a weight that lingers. There’s a sense of urgency in her writing, a feeling that every moment counts—because in the world of *Divergent*, time is always running out. The dystopian setting is vividly realized, but it never overshadows the emotional core of the story. Roth understands that the real conflict isn’t between factions; it’s between the self and the expectations imposed upon it.

What makes *Four* so resonant is its exploration of loyalty and betrayal. Tobias’s journey is one of constant questioning—of his faction, his leaders, and even himself. The revelation that a "foul plan" is brewing within the leadership of two factions adds a layer of political intrigue that feels eerily prescient. Roth doesn’t just tell a story about a boy becoming a man; she tells a story about a society on the brink of collapse, where the lines between right and wrong are blurred, and the only certainty is the need to choose.

For fans of the *Divergent* trilogy, *Four* is a gift—a chance to see the world they love through a new lens. For new readers, it’s an accessible entry point into Roth’s universe, offering a self-contained narrative that still feels essential to the larger story. And for anyone who has ever struggled with the weight of expectations, Tobias’s journey is a powerful reminder that reinvention is possible—but it comes at a cost.

Veronica Roth has crafted a collection that is both a love letter to her fans and a testament to the power of perspective. *Four* isn’t just a prequel; it’s a revelation.
