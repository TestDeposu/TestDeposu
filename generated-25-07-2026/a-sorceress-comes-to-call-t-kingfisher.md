# A Sorceress Comes to Call

**Author:** T. Kingfisher  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2024

A Sorceress Comes to Call by T. Kingfisher is a masterful reimagining of the Brothers Grimm's "The Goose Girl," a dark and captivating tale of secrets, murder, and forbidden magic. The story follows Cordelia, a young girl raised by her evil sorceress mother, who has been isolated from the world and forced to live a life of silence and obedience. But when they move into the manor home of a wealthy Squire and his sister Hester, Cordelia begins to feel a sense of belonging and freedom she has never known before.

As Cordelia's mother's sinister plans unfold, the young girl must confront the darkness of her own upbringing and decide whether to protect the people who have become like family to her. Kingfisher's writing is as enchanting as it is unsettling, weaving a spell that draws the reader into the world of the story and refuses to let go.

One of the most striking aspects of A Sorceress Comes to Call is its exploration of the complex and often fraught relationship between mothers and daughters. Cordelia's mother is a masterfully crafted villain, a woman whose love for her daughter is twisted and corrupted by her own dark desires. And yet, despite her evil nature, she is also a deeply human and relatable character, driven by a desire for power and control that is both terrifying and understandable.

Kingfisher's writing is also notable for its vivid world-building and atmospheric setting. The manor home of the Squire and his sister Hester is a character in its own right, a place of warmth and comfort that stands in stark contrast to the cold, dark world of Cordelia's mother. The author's use of language is equally impressive, conjuring up a sense of time and place that is both immersive and believable.

Throughout the book, Kingfisher's style is characterized by a sense of subtlety and restraint. The author never hits the reader over the head with overt moralizing or didacticism, instead trusting the story to speak for itself. The result is a narrative that is both nuanced and powerful, a tale that will linger in the reader's mind long after the final page has been turned.

In short, A Sorceress Comes to Call is a must-read for fans of dark fantasy and fairy tale retellings. With its masterful world-building, complex characters, and subtle yet powerful storytelling, it is a book that will captivate and haunt readers in equal measure. As Peter S. Beagle notes, "Kingfisher never fails to dazzle," and this latest offering is no exception.
