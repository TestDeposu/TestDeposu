# Diplomats & Admirals: From Failed Negotiations and Tragic Misjudgments to Powerful Leaders and Heroic Deeds, the Untold Story of the Pacific War from Pearl Harbor to Midway

**Author:** Dale A. Jenkins  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

Diplomats & Admirals: From Failed Negotiations and Tragic Misjudgments to Powerful Leaders and Heroic Deeds, the Untold Story of the Pacific War from Pearl Harbor to Midway by Dale A. Jenkins is a meticulously researched and engagingly written historical account that delves into the intricacies of the Pacific War, challenging the conventional narrative that the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor was inevitable. Jenkins' work is a testament to the complexity of historical events, where the interplay of human agency, diplomacy, and circumstance can have far-reaching consequences.

Through a detailed analysis of now-declassified original documents, Jenkins masterfully weaves a narrative that highlights the critical role of diplomats and admirals in shaping the course of the war. The book's central theme revolves around the idea that the events leading up to the attack on Pearl Harbor, and the subsequent naval battles, were not predetermined, but rather the result of a series of miscommunications, misjudgments, and blunders committed by powerful civilian and military leaders on both sides.

Jenkins' writing style is clear, concise, and engaging, making the book an enjoyable read for both historians and enthusiasts of World War II. The author's use of primary sources adds depth and authenticity to the narrative, allowing readers to gain a deeper understanding of the events that unfolded. The book's structure, which combines perspectives from both military and civilian leaders, provides a nuanced and multifaceted exploration of the Pacific War, shedding new light on the men behind the pivotal battles.

One of the book's greatest strengths is its ability to challenge prevailing assumptions about the Pacific War. Jenkins' meticulous research and analysis reveal a complex web of motivations, agendas, and circumstances that led to the outbreak of war. The author's contention that China played a significant role in the US ultimatum that triggered the attack on Pearl Harbor is a fascinating revelation that adds a new layer of complexity to our understanding of the events leading up to the war.

The book's focus on the human element of the war is also noteworthy. Jenkins' portrayal of courageous US navy pilots who snatched victory from defeat at the Battle of Midway is a compelling reminder of the bravery and sacrifice that defined the war. Similarly, the author's examination of the leadership styles and decision-making processes of key figures, such as Roosevelt and the Japanese military leaders, provides valuable insights into the psychological and strategic aspects of the war.

In conclusion, Diplomats & Admirals is a gripping and informative historical account that offers a fresh perspective on the Pacific War. Jenkins' engaging writing style, combined with his meticulous research and analysis, make the book an essential read for anyone interested in World War II history. By challenging conventional narratives and shedding new light on the events that shaped the war, Jenkins' work is a significant contribution to the field of historical scholarship.
