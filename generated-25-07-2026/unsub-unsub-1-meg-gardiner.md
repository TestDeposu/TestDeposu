# UNSUB (UNSUB, #1)

**Author:** Meg Gardiner  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Meg Gardiner's UNSUB is a gripping and intense thriller that delves into the darker corners of the human psyche, masterfully weaving a narrative that's as much about the devastating consequences of trauma as it is about the thrill of the chase. Inspired by the infamous Zodiac Killer, Gardiner's novel is a riveting exploration of the cat-and-mouse game between a determined detective and a ruthless serial killer who's haunted her family for decades.

At the heart of the story is Caitlin Hendrix, a young and ambitious Narcotics detective who's driven by a personal vendetta against the Prophet, the UNSUB who terrorized the Bay Area in the 1990s and nearly destroyed her father, the lead investigator on the case. Caitlin's character is complex and multi-layered, with a fragile barrier between her relentless pursuit of justice and a dangerous obsession that threatens to consume her. As she delves deeper into the case, ignoring her father's warnings and confronting her own demons, Caitlin's character becomes increasingly nuanced, revealing a depth of emotion and vulnerability that makes her impossible to root against.

Gardiner's writing style is crisp and concise, with a keen eye for detail that brings the dark and foreboding atmosphere of the Bay Area to life. Her prose is economical and direct, eschewing flowery descriptions for a more streamlined approach that propels the narrative forward with relentless momentum. The result is a thriller that's as much about the intellectual puzzle of deciphering the Prophet's twisted messages as it is about the visceral thrill of the chase.

One of the standout aspects of UNSUB is its thought-provoking exploration of trauma and its long-lasting effects on individuals and families. Gardiner's portrayal of the Hendrix family's struggles to come to terms with their past is both poignant and powerful, highlighting the ways in which trauma can be passed down through generations like a toxic legacy. The novel also raises important questions about the nature of obsession and the blurred lines between good and evil, leaving the reader to ponder the consequences of Caitlin's actions long after the final page has been turned.

If there's one criticism to be made, it's that the supporting cast feels somewhat one-dimensional at times, with some characters feeling more like ciphers than fully fleshed-out human beings. However, this is a minor quibble in an otherwise gripping narrative that's driven by Caitlin's complex and compelling character.

Overall, UNSUB is a gripping and intense thriller that's perfect for fans of crime fiction and psychological suspense. With its thought-provoking exploration of trauma and its long-lasting effects, Meg Gardiner's novel is a must-read for anyone who loves a good puzzle and isn't afraid to confront the darker corners of the human psyche.
