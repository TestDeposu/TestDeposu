# Kingdom of Ash (Throne of Glass, #7)

**Author:** Sarah J. Maas  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

A Fierce and Fitting Conclusion: A Review of Kingdom of Ash by Sarah J. Maas

The Throne of Glass series, a high fantasy epic by Sarah J. Maas, has captivated readers worldwide with its intricate world-building, complex characters, and pulse-pounding action sequences. The seventh and final installment, Kingdom of Ash, is a masterful conclusion to the series, tying together the various plot threads and character arcs in a satisfying and emotional finale.

At its core, Kingdom of Ash is a story about the power of love, loyalty, and sacrifice. Celaena Sardothien, the series' protagonist, has undergone significant development throughout the series, evolving from a self-centered assassin to a compassionate and courageous leader. Her relationships with the supporting characters, particularly Rowan, Dorian, and Chaol, have been a driving force behind her growth, and Maas skillfully explores the depths of these relationships in Kingdom of Ash.

Maas's writing style is one of her greatest strengths, and Kingdom of Ash is no exception. Her prose is engaging, descriptive, and immersive, making it easy to become fully invested in the world of Erilea and its inhabitants. The pacing is well-balanced, with a mix of action, romance, and emotional moments that will keep readers on the edge of their seats.

One of the most impressive aspects of Kingdom of Ash is its thematic resonance. Maas explores complex themes such as the nature of power, the cost of war, and the importance of forgiveness and redemption. These themes are woven throughout the narrative, adding depth and complexity to the story.

The world-building in Kingdom of Ash is also noteworthy. Maas has created a richly detailed and expansive world, with a complex history and politics. The various factions and characters are well-developed, with distinct motivations and backstories that add to the overall tension and conflict.

If there is one criticism to be made, it is that Kingdom of Ash can feel a bit overwhelming at times. The series has a large cast of characters, and the final installment tries to give each of them a satisfying conclusion. While Maas largely succeeds in this endeavor, some characters feel slightly shortchanged, and their storylines are resolved a bit too conveniently.

In conclusion, Kingdom of Ash is a fitting and satisfying conclusion to the Throne of Glass series. Maas's writing is engaging, the characters are well-developed, and the themes are complex and thought-provoking. While it may have some minor flaws, Kingdom of Ash is a must-read for fans of the series and the fantasy genre as a whole.
