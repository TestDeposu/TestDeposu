# Poison Study (Study, #1)

**Author:** Maria V. Snyder  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

In the captivating world of Ixia, Maria V. Snyder weaves a thrilling tale of survival, magic, and self-discovery in "Poison Study", the first installment of the Study series. This mesmerizing novel whisks readers away to a land of strict laws, ruthless politics, and ancient magic, where the protagonist, Yelena, finds herself trapped in a desperate fight for her life.

Yelena, a young and determined woman, is locked away in the palace dungeon, awaiting execution for a crime she committed in self-defense. However, her fate takes an unexpected turn when she is offered a chance to become the Commander's food taster, a position that comes with its own set of perils. With the constant threat of assassination and the deliberate poisoning by the chief of security, Yelena must navigate the treacherous landscape of the palace, all while trying to unravel the mysteries of her past and the magical powers that begin to emerge within her.

Snyder's writing style is masterful, with a keen attention to detail that brings the world of Ixia to vivid life. Her characters are multidimensional and complex, with Yelena being a particularly compelling protagonist. Her struggles, fears, and triumphs are deeply relatable, making it easy for readers to become invested in her journey. The pacing of the novel is expertly handled, with a perfect balance of action, suspense, and quiet moments of introspection.

One of the most striking aspects of "Poison Study" is its exploration of the theme of survival. Yelena's story is a powerful testament to the human spirit's capacity to endure even the most daunting challenges. Through her experiences, Snyder sheds light on the complexities of survival, revealing that it is not just a matter of physical endurance, but also of emotional and psychological resilience. Yelena's struggles to come to terms with her past, her relationships, and her own identity serve as a poignant reminder that survival is often a deeply personal and intimate journey.

The world-building in "Poison Study" is equally impressive, with Snyder creating a richly detailed and immersive environment that draws readers in. The magical system, in particular, is well-developed and intriguing, with a unique blend of mysticism and science that adds depth to the narrative.

Overall, "Poison Study" is a captivating and unputdownable read that will appeal to fans of fantasy, adventure, and romance. Snyder's writing is engaging, her characters are memorable, and the world she has created is nothing short of enchanting. With its themes of survival, self-discovery, and empowerment, this novel is sure to resonate with readers long after the final page has been turned.
