# Magician (The Riftwar Saga, #1-2)

**Author:** Raymond E. Feist  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1993

In the realm of fantasy literature, few authors have managed to craft a world as rich and immersive as Raymond E. Feist's "Magician". This epic novel, which combines the first two books of The Riftwar Saga, is a masterclass in storytelling, weaving together elements of magic, adventure, and politics to create a truly unforgettable experience.

At its core, "Magician" is a tale of two worlds: the Kingdom of the Isles, where our protagonist Pug hails from, and the mystical realm of Midkemia, where the fabric of spacetime is threatened by dark forces from another dimension. As Pug embarks on a journey to become a master magician under the guidance of Kulgan, he finds himself at the forefront of an ancient battle between Order and Chaos.

Feist's writing style is nothing short of enchanting. His vivid descriptions of Midkemia's lush landscapes, intricate magic systems, and complex characters transport readers to a world that feels both familiar and yet utterly unique. The author's use of language is evocative and engaging, with a keen sense of pacing that keeps the narrative hurtling forward.

One of the standout aspects of "Magician" is its well-developed characters. Pug, the orphaned protagonist, is a relatable and endearing hero whose struggles to find his place in the world resonate deeply. The supporting cast, including the enigmatic Kulgan and the lovely Princess Carline, is equally well-fleshed out, with each character bringing their own distinct voice and perspective to the narrative.

Feist's world-building is another highlight of the book. Midkemia is a richly detailed realm, with a complex history and politics that underpin the narrative. The author's use of mythology and folklore adds depth and texture to the world, making it feel fully realized and immersive.

The themes of "Magician" are equally impressive. Feist explores the nature of power, loyalty, and identity, raising important questions about the consequences of playing with forces beyond human control. The novel's central conflict, which pits the forces of Order against the forces of Chaos, serves as a powerful allegory for the struggle between good and evil.

Throughout the book, Feist's influences are evident. The author's love of mythology, folklore, and fairy tales is apparent in the novel's use of magical creatures, ancient prophecies, and legendary artifacts. The world of Midkemia owes a debt to Tolkien's Middle-earth, but Feist's unique voice and vision set his work apart from other fantasy authors.

In short, "Magician" is a fantasy epic that will captivate readers from start to finish. With its rich world-building, engaging characters, and epic scope, this novel is a must-read for fans of the genre. Feist's writing is evocative and immersive, making it easy to become fully invested in the world of Midkemia.
