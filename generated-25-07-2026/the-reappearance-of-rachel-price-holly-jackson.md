# The Reappearance of Rachel Price

**Author:** Holly Jackson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2024

Holly Jackson’s *The Reappearance of Rachel Price* is a masterclass in psychological suspense, a book that burrows under your skin and refuses to let go. If you thought true crime documentaries were just about solving mysteries, Jackson flips the script, forcing us to question whether the truth is ever as clear as the cameras make it seem. With razor-sharp prose and a plot that twists like a noose, this novel isn’t just a whodunit—it’s a *who-can-you-trust*.

At its core, the book explores the fragility of memory and the lies we tell to survive. Bel Price, our narrator, has spent her entire life defined by her mother’s disappearance—a trauma she can’t remember. Sixteen years later, Rachel Price walks back into her life, alive and with a story so outrageous it feels like fiction. Is she a victim? A fraud? A predator? Jackson doesn’t just ask these questions; she forces the reader to grapple with them, blurring the line between victim and villain until the very end.

What makes Jackson’s writing so compelling is her ability to balance high-stakes tension with intimate character depth. Bel isn’t just a girl with a missing mom; she’s a teenager drowning in the weight of her family’s grief, her own forgotten past, and the suffocating gaze of a documentary crew that treats her life like entertainment. The prose crackles with urgency, each chapter a ticking clock as Bel races to uncover the truth before the cameras distort it beyond recognition. Jackson’s dialogue is razor-sharp, laced with sarcasm and subtext, making every conversation feel like a minefield.

The true genius of *The Reappearance of Rachel Price* lies in its structure. Jackson doesn’t just drop us into the mystery—she immerses us in Bel’s paranoia. The documentary crew isn’t just a backdrop; they’re a character, their presence a constant reminder that nothing is private anymore. The tension escalates with each revelation, from Rachel’s implausible alibi to the unsettling gaps in Bel’s memory. By the time the truth starts to unravel, you’ll be gripping the book so tightly your knuckles turn white.

Jackson’s strength is her ability to make the unbelievable feel terrifyingly real. Rachel’s reappearance isn’t just a plot twist—it’s a psychological earthquake. Bel’s worldview shatters, and suddenly, nothing is as it seems. The mother she thought she knew might be a stranger. The past she’s tried to forget might be a lie. The documentary crew, who were supposed to bring closure, might be the ones manipulating the narrative.

This isn’t just a mystery; it’s a psychological thriller that lingers long after the last page. Jackson forces us to confront uncomfortable questions: How well do we ever really know our families? Can trauma erase entire memories, or is forgetting a choice? And when the truth finally surfaces, will it set you free—or destroy you?

*The Reappearance of Rachel Price* is Holly Jackson at her most audacious, a book that will leave you questioning every character’s motives, every word they say, every glance they cast. It’s a story about the stories we tell ourselves to survive—and the terrifying moment when those stories collapse.

If you loved *A Good Girl’s Guide to Murder*, this book will hook you from the first page. If you’ve ever binge-watched a true crime documentary and wondered how much of it is performance, this is your nightmare made real.
