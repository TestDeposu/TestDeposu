# As Good As Dead (A Good Girl's Guide to Murder, #3)

**Author:** Holly Jackson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2026

Holly Jackson’s *As Good As Dead* delivers a pulse-pounding finale to the *A Good Girl’s Guide to Murder* trilogy, cementing her reputation as a master of YA psychological suspense. With a plot that twists tighter than a noose and a protagonist whose brilliance is matched only by her vulnerability, Jackson crafts a story that’s as addictive as it is unsettling. This isn’t just a mystery—it’s a high-stakes game of cat and mouse where the reader, like Pip, is never quite sure who’s playing by the rules.

At its core, *As Good As Dead* explores the cost of obsession and the fine line between justice and vengeance. Pip Fitz-Amobi, the sharp-witted amateur detective introduced in the first book, has spent years dissecting the darkest corners of her small town, Fairview. But when her true-crime podcast *Dark Side of the Moon* exposes long-buried secrets, she becomes the target of a relentless stalker. Jackson brilliantly flips the script: Pip, who once chased truth for the sake of closure, now finds herself hunted by someone who knows her every move. The theme of justice is ever-present, but here it’s tangled in moral ambiguity—Pip’s relentless pursuit of answers has consequences, and the line between right and wrong blurs with every clue she uncovers.

Jackson’s writing is a masterclass in tension. She balances razor-sharp dialogue with prose that’s both economical and evocative, painting Fairview as a character in itself—a place where beauty and menace coexist. The pacing is relentless, with each chapter ending on a cliffhanger that forces the reader to keep turning pages. But what truly sets Jackson apart is her ability to make the reader *feel* Pip’s paranoia. The stalker’s presence is felt in every rustle of leaves, every shadow in the hallway. The psychological toll on Pip is palpable, and Jackson doesn’t shy away from showing her unraveling—her sleepless nights, her frayed nerves, her desperate need to outsmart a predator who seems to know her better than she knows herself.

The supporting cast is equally compelling. From Pip’s loyal best friend Connor to the morally gray figure of Ravi Singh (whose own family’s trauma adds another layer of complexity), each character feels fully realized. Even the antagonists are given depth, their motivations rooted in pain rather than pure malice. Jackson avoids caricatures, which makes the story’s darker turns even more chilling. The final act is a masterstroke of misdirection, where every revelation feels earned yet still manages to shock.

What makes *As Good As Dead* stand out is its refusal to offer easy answers. Pip’s journey is one of growth, but it’s not a neat arc—she makes mistakes, she misjudges people, and she pays for her curiosity in ways that linger long after the last page. The book’s climax is both satisfying and haunting, leaving the reader to question not just whodunit, but whether justice was truly served. Jackson doesn’t wrap things up with a neat bow; instead, she leaves room for doubt, for lingering unease, for the kind of ambiguity that lingers like a bruise.

In a genre often criticized for formulaic storytelling, Jackson’s trilogy feels fresh and vital. She takes the tropes of YA mystery—small-town secrets, amateur sleuths, high-stakes confrontations—and elevates them with psychological depth and emotional resonance. *As Good As Dead* isn’t just a conclusion; it’s a reckoning. It asks what happens when the truth-seeker becomes the prey, when the line between detective and victim blurs, and when the pursuit of justice exacts a personal toll.

For fans of the series, this finale will satisfy their craving for answers while leaving them breathless. For newcomers, it’s a masterclass in suspense that proves Jackson is one of the most exciting voices in YA thriller writing today. By the final page, you’ll be questioning everything—just as Pip does.
