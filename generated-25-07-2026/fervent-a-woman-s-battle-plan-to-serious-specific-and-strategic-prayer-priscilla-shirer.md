# Fervent: A Woman's Battle Plan to Serious, Specific and Strategic Prayer

**Author:** Priscilla Shirer  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

Priscilla Shirer’s *Fervent: A Woman’s Battle Plan to Serious, Specific and Strategic Prayer* is not just another book on prayer—it’s a battle cry. In a world where spiritual warfare often feels abstract or overwhelming, Shirer strips away the ambiguity and arms readers with a precise, tactical approach to prayer. Published in 2015, this book arrives at a time when many Christians, particularly women, are seeking deeper, more intentional ways to engage in their faith. And Shirer delivers, not with vague platitudes, but with a battle-ready manual that feels as urgent as it does practical.

At its core, *Fervent* is about reclaiming prayer as a weapon. Shirer doesn’t shy away from the reality of an enemy—one who is cunning, relentless, and deeply personal in his attacks. She frames life’s struggles—not as random hardships—but as targeted assaults from the devil, designed to derail faith, distort identity, and drain joy. This isn’t fear-mongering; it’s a wake-up call. The enemy doesn’t play fair, and neither should we in our response. Shirer’s genius lies in reframing prayer not as a passive religious duty, but as an active, strategic counterattack.

What sets *Fervent* apart is its structure. Each chapter zeroes in on a specific area of spiritual warfare—identity, family strife, regret, fear, bitterness, and more—exposing the enemy’s tactics before equipping the reader with a personalized prayer strategy. The inclusion of tear-out sheets is a brilliant touch, turning abstract concepts into tangible, actionable steps. Readers aren’t just encouraged to pray; they’re guided to *craft* prayers that hit where it hurts. It’s like being handed a sniper’s scope in the midst of a fog of war—suddenly, the fog lifts, and the targets become clear.

Shirer’s writing style is direct, conversational, and deeply personal. She writes as a fellow warrior, not a distant theologian. Her tone is urgent but never sensational, practical but never dry. She weaves in personal anecdotes, biblical examples, and sharp observations about modern life, making the book feel like a heart-to-heart with a trusted mentor. There’s no fluff here—every sentence serves a purpose, whether it’s convicting, encouraging, or equipping.

One of the book’s strongest themes is the idea of *specificity* in prayer. Shirer dismantles the notion that “God knows my heart anyway, so why bother with details?” Instead, she argues that precision in prayer aligns our hearts with God’s will and exposes the enemy’s schemes. It’s a call to move beyond generic requests (“Lord, bless my family”) to strategic declarations (“Lord, protect my daughter from the lure of comparison on social media”). This isn’t about manipulating God; it’s about engaging with Him in a way that reflects the warfare reality of our lives.

Shirer also excels in addressing the emotional and psychological battles women face. Chapters on regret, bitterness, and identity don’t just offer spiritual solutions—they acknowledge the deep, often hidden wounds that the enemy exploits. Her chapter on schedules, for example, is a masterclass in reframing busyness as a spiritual battleground, where time management becomes an act of worship and resistance against the enemy’s distractions.

That said, *Fervent* isn’t without its challenges. Some readers might find Shirer’s portrayal of the enemy overly vivid, bordering on dramatized. Others may bristle at the militaristic language, fearing it could foster an us-versus-them mentality that feels unbalanced. And while the tear-out sheets are a great idea, their practicality depends on how readers choose to use them—simply filling them out without applying the principles might leave the book feeling like an intellectual exercise rather than a transformative tool.

Yet, these critiques don’t diminish the book’s power. *Fervent* succeeds because it meets readers where they are—whether they’re new to prayer or seasoned veterans—and challenges them to go deeper. It’s not a book to be read once and shelved; it’s a manual to be revisited, prayed over, and put into action.

Ultimately, *Fervent* is a call to arms. It reminds women that their prayers are not just words—they’re weapons. And in a world that often feels chaotic and hopeless, that reminder is both timely and timeless. Priscilla Shirer doesn’t just tell you to pray harder; she shows you how to pray smarter. And in doing so, she equips an entire generation of women to fight back—not with anger, but with faith, focus, and fervency.
