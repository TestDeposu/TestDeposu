# Blue Moon (The Immortals, #2)

**Author:** Alyson Noel  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Blue Moon, the second installment in Alyson Noel's Immortals series, is a captivating tale of love, loss, and self-discovery. The story picks up where the first book left off, with Ever navigating her newfound immortality and her relationship with the enigmatic Damen. However, their love is put to the test when Damen falls ill, and Ever must embark on a perilous journey to save him.

Noel's writing style is as enchanting as ever, weaving a spell of mystique and wonder that draws the reader in from the very beginning. Her use of descriptive language paints a vivid picture of the world she has created, from the dark, seductive world of immortals to the mystical dimension of Summerland. The author's ability to craft relatable characters is also noteworthy, with Ever's inner turmoil and desperation to save Damen making her a sympathetic and endearing protagonist.

The main theme of Blue Moon is the power of love and the sacrifices that come with it. Ever is faced with an impossible decision: turn back the clock and save her family, or stay in the present and save Damen. This dilemma serves as a catalyst for Ever's growth, forcing her to confront her own desires and priorities. Through Ever's journey, Noel explores the complexities of love and relationships, highlighting the difficulties and challenges that come with loving someone unconditionally.

One of the most striking aspects of Blue Moon is its exploration of the human condition. Noel delves into the themes of mortality, identity, and the human experience, raising questions about what it means to be alive and to love. The author's use of the blue moon as a symbol of transformation and change adds a layer of depth to the story, underscoring the idea that our choices have consequences and that time is a precious commodity.

If there is one criticism to be made, it is that the supporting characters feel somewhat one-dimensional at times. While Damen's backstory is expertly woven into the narrative, some of the other characters feel like they are lacking in depth and development. However, this is a minor quibble in an otherwise engaging and immersive read.

In conclusion, Blue Moon is a mesmerizing tale of love, loss, and self-discovery. Alyson Noel's writing style is as captivating as ever, and her exploration of the human condition is both thought-provoking and poignant. While some of the supporting characters may feel somewhat one-dimensional, the story is driven by Ever's compelling narrative voice and her impossible decision. Fans of the Immortals series will not be disappointed, and new readers will find themselves drawn into the world of immortals and the mystical dimension of Summerland.
