# P.S. I Love You (P.S. I Love You, #1)

**Author:** Cecelia Ahern  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

A Timeless Tale of Love and Self-Discovery: A Review of P.S. I Love You by Cecelia Ahern

In the midst of a world where words can be both a blessing and a curse, Cecelia Ahern's P.S. I Love You is a poignant reminder of the transformative power of love and self-discovery. This beautifully crafted Regency novella, inspired by the classic tale of Cyrano de Bergerac, is a must-read for anyone who has ever been swept away by the thrill of romance and the agony of unrequited love.

At the heart of the story is Rosanne Lacy, a young woman who finds herself torn between her affection for the charming words of Frank Newnham and her inexplicable attraction to the brooding Earl of Bruton. As Rosanne navigates the complexities of her own heart, Ahern masterfully weaves a tale that is both a tender exploration of love and a nuanced examination of the human condition.

One of the most striking aspects of Ahern's writing style is her ability to craft characters that are at once relatable and flawed. Rosanne, with her sharp wit and vulnerable heart, is a heroine who is impossible not to root for. Her struggles to reconcile her feelings for Frank and the Earl are deeply human, and Ahern's portrayal of her inner turmoil is both authentic and affecting.

The supporting cast of characters is equally well-drawn, with the Earl of Bruton being a particular standout. His scarred face and air of melancholy make him a compelling and complex figure, and Ahern's slow reveal of his backstory is a masterclass in subtle characterization.

Ahern's prose is, as always, a joy to read. Her writing is effortlessly elegant, with a keen eye for detail and a deep understanding of the period in which the story is set. The Regency setting is vividly brought to life, with Ahern's descriptions of the ducal house party and its attendees being particularly evocative.

If there is a criticism to be made, it is that the story is perhaps a little too brief. At 32,000 words, P.S. I Love You is a novella that can be devoured in a single sitting, and some readers may find themselves wishing for a more expansive narrative. However, this is a minor quibble, and the story is ultimately well-suited to its concise format.

In conclusion, P.S. I Love You is a beautifully written and deeply moving tale of love and self-discovery. Ahern's masterful storytelling and nuanced characterization make this a must-read for fans of historical romance, and the story's timeless themes are sure to resonate with readers of all ages.
