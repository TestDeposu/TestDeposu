# Bully (Fall Away, #1)

**Author:** Penelope Douglas  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

Bully by Penelope Douglas is a captivating and intense romance novel that delves into the complexities of a tumultuous relationship between two former best friends, Tate and Jared. The story is set in a high school backdrop where Jared, once a close companion, turns into a ruthless bully who makes it his mission to destroy Tate's life.

Through Tate's narrative, Douglas masterfully portrays the emotional turmoil and vulnerability of a teenager who has been relentlessly tormented by someone she once trusted. The author's vivid descriptions of Tate's pain, fear, and humiliation are heart-wrenching, making it impossible for readers not to empathize with her plight. As the story progresses, it becomes apparent that Douglas is not just exploring the theme of bullying, but also the intricacies of human relationships, particularly the fine line between love and hate.

One of the most striking aspects of Douglas's writing style is her ability to craft complex characters with rich backstories. Jared, the bully, is not a one-dimensional character, but a multi-faceted individual with his own set of motivations and conflicts. His character adds depth to the narrative, making it difficult for readers to categorize him as simply a villain. This moral ambiguity raises important questions about the nature of bullying and the complexities of human behavior.

The author's use of language is evocative and engaging, making it easy to become fully immersed in the world of Fall Away. The pacing of the novel is well-balanced, with a mix of intense drama, witty dialogue, and poignant moments of introspection. Douglas's writing is unflinching and honest, tackling difficult themes with sensitivity and nuance.

What sets Bully apart from other romance novels is its unapologetic portrayal of the darker aspects of human relationships. Douglas does not shy away from exploring the harsh realities of bullying, its consequences, and the long-lasting impact it can have on individuals. At the same time, she weaves a narrative that is ultimately hopeful and redemptive, suggesting that it is possible for people to change and grow.

In conclusion, Bully is a thought-provoking and emotionally charged novel that will resonate with readers who enjoy complex characters, intense drama, and romance. Penelope Douglas's writing is captivating, and her ability to tackle difficult themes with sensitivity makes her a compelling author to watch. If you're looking for a novel that will keep you on the edge of your seat and leave you thinking long after you finish reading, then Bully is an excellent choice.
