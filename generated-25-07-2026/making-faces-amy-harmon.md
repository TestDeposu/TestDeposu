# Making Faces

**Author:** Amy Harmon  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

In "Making Faces", Amy Harmon weaves a poignant and thought-provoking tale that delves into the complexities of life, love, and the human condition. This novel, originally published as "Vor uns das Leben", is a masterful exploration of the struggles and triumphs that define us, and the power of love to transform and redeem.

At its core, "Making Faces" is a story about the beauty and brutality of life. Fern Taylor, the protagonist, has been in love with the charismatic Ambrose Young for as long as she can remember. But Ambrose's seemingly perfect facade hides a depth of pain and vulnerability that Fern can hardly begin to comprehend. As their paths converge, Fern finds herself drawn into Ambrose's world, where the lines between love and sacrifice are constantly blurred.

Harmon's writing is evocative and immersive, with a narrative voice that is both authentic and compelling. Her characters are multidimensional and relatable, with flaws and strengths that make them feel disarmingly real. The relationships between Fern, Ambrose, and their friends are nuanced and complex, with a depth of emotion that is both heart-wrenching and uplifting.

One of the most striking aspects of "Making Faces" is its exploration of the human condition. Harmon tackles themes of love, loss, and identity with sensitivity and courage, creating a narrative that is both deeply personal and universally relatable. Her characters grapple with the big questions of life - What does it mean to truly live? How do we find purpose and meaning in a world that can be cruel and unforgiving? - and the answers they find are both surprising and profound.

Through Fern's journey, Harmon shows us that life is not about avoiding pain or hardship, but about embracing it, and finding a way to transform it into something beautiful. This is a novel that will make you laugh and cry, often at the same time, and will leave you thinking long after the final page has been turned.

In short, "Making Faces" is a triumph of contemporary fiction, a novel that will resonate with fans of Colleen Hoover and anyone who has ever struggled to find their place in the world. Harmon's writing is a gift, and this novel is a testament to her skill and craftsmanship as a storyteller.
