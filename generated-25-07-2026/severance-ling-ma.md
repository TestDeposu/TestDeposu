# Severance

**Author:** Ling Ma  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

In the midst of a desolate, post-apocalyptic landscape, Ling Ma's Severance emerges as a scathing critique of contemporary society, wrapped in a darkly comedic package. This mesmerizing novel tells the story of Candace Chen, a millennial office drone, who finds herself at the epicenter of a global catastrophe. As a plague of biblical proportions, known as Shen Fever, ravages New York, Candace's mundane routine is disrupted, forcing her to confront the harsh realities of a world on the brink of collapse.

Through Candace's narrative, Ma masterfully explores the theme of disconnection in modern life. Candace's existence is marked by a sense of detachment, as she navigates the sterile corridors of her office building, and engages in lackluster relationships. Her obsession with routine is a coping mechanism, a desperate attempt to impose order on a chaotic world. As the apocalypse unfolds, Candace's detachment evolves into a form of resilience, allowing her to survive in a world that is rapidly disintegrating.

Ma's writing style is a perfect blend of wit, irony, and poignancy. Her prose is economical, yet evocative, conjuring vivid images of a deserted New York City. The author's use of satire is biting, as she skewers the excesses of contemporary culture, from the vacuity of office politics to the narcissism of social media. The character of Bob, the power-hungry IT tech, is a brilliant send-up of toxic masculinity, and the ways in which power is exercised in the most desperate of circumstances.

One of the most striking aspects of Severance is its exploration of identity, particularly in the context of immigrant experiences. Candace's Chinese heritage is woven throughout the narrative, as she grapples with the loss of her parents, and the disconnection from her cultural roots. Ma's portrayal of the immigrant experience is nuanced, highlighting the tensions between tradition and assimilation, and the ways in which identity is shaped by the complexities of cultural heritage.

Severance is also a powerful exploration of human connection in the face of adversity. As Candace joins a group of survivors, she is forced to confront the limits of her own resilience, and the importance of forming relationships in a world that is rapidly falling apart. The novel raises important questions about the nature of community, and the ways in which we come together in the face of catastrophic loss.

In conclusion, Severance is a masterpiece of contemporary literature, a novel that is both a scathing critique of modern society, and a powerful exploration of human connection. Ma's writing is a tour-de-force, a perfect blend of wit, irony, and poignancy. This is a novel that will resonate with readers, long after the final page has been turned.
