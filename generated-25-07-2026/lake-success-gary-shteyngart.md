# Lake Success

**Author:** Gary Shteyngart  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

Gary Shteyngart’s *Lake Success* is a masterclass in satirical storytelling, a novel that skewers the absurdities of modern American life while delivering a deeply human story about love, failure, and the relentless pursuit of meaning. At its core, the book is a meditation on the American Dream—how it seduces, distorts, and ultimately disappoints those who chase it. But Shteyngart, ever the sharp-eyed observer, doesn’t just mock the hollow promises of wealth and status; he dissects the very people who embody those illusions, revealing the fragility beneath their polished facades.

The novel follows Barry Cohen, a hedge-fund manager whose life is a carefully curated illusion. With a net worth of $2.4 billion, a Park Avenue penthouse, and a wife who embodies the aspirational ideal of the first-generation American dream, Barry appears to have it all. Yet, beneath the surface, he is a man unraveling—haunted by an SEC investigation, the diagnosis of his autistic son, and the gnawing sense that his success is a house of cards. When he impulsively flees New York on a Greyhound bus, leaving his wife and son behind, he’s not just running from his problems; he’s searching for something he can’t name, a return to a past that might offer redemption or at least distraction.

Shteyngart’s genius lies in his ability to balance humor and heartbreak. Barry is a deeply flawed protagonist, a narcissist whose self-delusion is both hilarious and tragic. His internal monologue—equal parts neurotic and profound—lays bare the absurdity of his existence. He clings to the idea of reconnecting with his college sweetheart, a woman from his past who represents a simpler, purer version of himself. But as Barry’s journey across America unfolds, it becomes clear that his quest is less about love and more about escape. The country he traverses is a grotesque carnival of excess and decay, a place where the American Dream has curdled into something grotesque and unsustainable.

The novel’s real power, however, lies in its portrayal of Seema, Barry’s wife. A woman who has spent her life chasing the trappings of success, she is just as trapped as he is, if not more so. Her story is one of quiet desperation, a woman who has built a life on performance, only to realize that the performance is all there is. Shteyngart gives her depth and nuance, avoiding the trap of making her a mere foil to Barry’s antics. Instead, she emerges as a fully realized character, her own struggles with identity, ambition, and motherhood making her just as compelling as her husband.

Shteyngart’s prose is a revelation—witty, incisive, and laced with a melancholic undercurrent. His sentences crackle with energy, whether he’s skewering the pretensions of the ultra-wealthy or capturing the quiet devastation of a parent watching their child struggle. The novel’s structure, which alternates between Barry’s chaotic journey and Seema’s more grounded perspective, creates a dynamic tension that keeps the reader engaged. It’s a testament to Shteyngart’s skill that even as he lampoons the absurdities of modern life, he never loses sight of the human heart at the center of the story.

Ultimately, *Lake Success* is a novel about the lies we tell ourselves to get through the day. Barry’s bus ride across America is a physical manifestation of his spiritual wandering—a man who has everything but feels nothing, who chases a past that no longer exists. Shteyngart doesn’t offer easy answers, but he does offer something rarer: a story that is both hilarious and heartbreaking, a satire that never loses sight of the people beneath the caricatures.

If *Super Sad True Love Story* established Shteyngart as a chronicler of a dystopian future, *Lake Success* cements him as a chronicler of the present—a writer who can hold up a mirror to society and make us laugh even as we wince.
