# The Eight (The Eight, #1)

**Author:** Katherine Neville  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

Katherine Neville’s *The Eight* is a dazzling, high-stakes thriller that weaves history, mystery, and adventure into a narrative as intricate as the chess set at its heart. Published in 2015 but rooted in a story that spans centuries, this novel is a masterclass in blending the past and present, where every move—whether on a chessboard or in a life-or-death chase—carries weight. At its core, *The Eight* is a quest for power, knowledge, and survival, but it’s also a celebration of intellect, resilience, and the unbreakable bonds between women across time.

The story unfolds in two timelines, separated by two centuries but united by a single, legendary chess set. In 1790s Paris, during the chaos of the French Revolution, two young novices—Valentine and Mireille—are tasked with protecting the set, which holds a secret capable of toppling empires. Their journey takes them from the bloodied streets of Paris to the scorching sands of the Algerian Sahara, where danger lurks in every shadow. Fast-forward to the 1970s, where computer expert Catherine Velis stumbles upon the same chess set’s legacy while on assignment in Algeria. Drawn into a deadly game by forces she doesn’t understand, Catherine must outwit her adversaries—both human and historical—if she hopes to survive.

Neville’s genius lies in her ability to make history feel alive and immediate. The novel isn’t just a thriller; it’s a historical odyssey, rich with details of 18th-century France, Napoleonic intrigue, and the mystique of the Sahara. The chess set itself is a brilliant metaphor—a symbol of strategy, foresight, and the delicate balance of power. Each piece, scattered across the globe, represents a clue, a challenge, and a potential weapon. The way Neville links the fates of Valentine, Mireille, and Catherine through this artifact is nothing short of genius, creating a tapestry that feels both timeless and urgently modern.

What sets *The Eight* apart is its unapologetic focus on women as the driving force behind the story. Valentine and Mireille are no damsels in distress; they are clever, resourceful, and fearless, navigating a world that seeks to control them. Their bond is the emotional anchor of the novel, a testament to female solidarity in the face of patriarchal oppression. Catherine Velis, in the modern timeline, is equally compelling—a woman in a male-dominated field who must rely on her wit and instincts to survive. Neville’s portrayal of these women is empowering, making *The Eight* a feminist adventure in the truest sense.

The prose is lush and immersive, with Neville’s descriptions transporting the reader from the opulent salons of Paris to the stark beauty of the desert. Her pacing is relentless, with each chapter ending on a cliffhanger that propels the reader forward. The blend of historical fact and fiction is seamless, making the chess set’s legend feel as real as the political upheavals of the time. Neville doesn’t just tell a story; she invites the reader into a world where every decision matters, where the past is never truly buried, and where the game is always afoot.

At its heart, *The Eight* is a meditation on the power of knowledge and the cost of ambition. The chess set’s secret is never fully revealed, leaving readers to ponder its significance—and whether the real treasure was the journey itself. Neville’s novel is a reminder that history is not just a record of the past but a living, breathing entity that shapes our present. It’s a story of survival, strategy, and the unbreakable spirit of those who dare to play the game.

For fans of historical thrillers, adventure, and stories of female resilience, *The Eight* is a must-read. It’s a book that lingers long after the final page, its themes and characters echoing in the mind like the echo of a chess piece sliding across a board. Katherine Neville has crafted not just a novel, but an experience—one that challenges, thrills, and inspires.
