# A Lady's Guide to Etiquette and Murder (Countess of Harleigh Mystery, #1)

**Author:** Dianne Freeman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

In the midst of the opulent Victorian era, Dianne Freeman's "A Lady's Guide to Etiquette and Murder" whisks readers away to the extravagant world of London's high society, where the façade of elegance and refinement conceals secrets, scandals, and even murder. This captivating debut novel is an expertly woven tapestry of mystery, romance, and wit, introducing the charming and intelligent Countess of Harleigh, Frances Wynn, as its endearing protagonist.

Frances, an American-born widow, is a breath of fresh air in the stuffy atmosphere of London's upper crust. With her newfound freedom, she navigates the complexities of the social season, all while protecting her young daughter and shielding her sister, Lily, from the prying eyes and wagging tongues of the ton. However, when an anonymous letter implicates Frances in her husband's death, she finds herself entangled in a web of intrigue and deception.

Freeman's writing style is reminiscent of the era itself – elegant, refined, and full of subtle humor. Her prose is effortlessly engaging, with a narrative voice that is both witty and endearing. As Frances navigates the treacherous waters of high society, Freeman skillfully balances the lighthearted and humorous moments with the darker, more sinister undertones of the mystery.

One of the most striking aspects of this novel is its well-crafted cast of characters. Frances, with her sharp intellect and quick wit, is a true delight. Her relationships with those around her – particularly her sister, Lily, and the dashing George Hazelton – are authentic and engaging, adding depth and warmth to the story. The supporting cast, from the enigmatic Inspector Delaney to the malicious gossipmongers, is equally well-developed, each character bringing their own unique flavor to the narrative.

At its core, "A Lady's Guide to Etiquette and Murder" is a tale of female empowerment and independence. Frances, as a widow, is finally free to forge her own path, and she does so with courage, determination, and a healthy dose of humor. Freeman's portrayal of women in this era is both accurate and refreshing, highlighting the limitations and restrictions placed upon them, while also showcasing their strength, resilience, and intelligence.

As the mystery unfolds, Freeman masterfully weaves together a complex and engaging plot, full of unexpected twists and turns. The pacing is expertly handled, with a narrative that flows effortlessly from one scene to the next. The author's use of historical details is impeccable, transporting readers to the gaslit streets and grand ballrooms of Victorian London.

In short, "A Lady's Guide to Etiquette and Murder" is a captivating and charming debut novel that will delight fans of historical mysteries, romance, and women's fiction. With its lighthearted tone, engaging characters, and intricate plot, this book is a true page-turner. Freeman's writing is a breath of fresh air, and her protagonist, Frances, is a true gem. If you're looking for a fun, engaging, and intelligent read, look no further than this delightful debut.
