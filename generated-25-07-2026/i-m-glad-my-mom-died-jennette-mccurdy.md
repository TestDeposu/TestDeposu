# I'm Glad My Mom Died

**Author:** Jennette McCurdy  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

I'm Glad My Mom Died by Jennette McCurdy is a heart-wrenching yet darkly comedic memoir that sheds light on the untold struggles of a former child star. With unflinching honesty, McCurdy recounts her tumultuous journey as a young actress, from her mother's relentless pursuit of stardom to her own battles with eating disorders, addiction, and toxic relationships.

The memoir is a candid and often disturbing portrayal of the exploitation and manipulation that can occur in the entertainment industry, particularly when it involves children. McCurdy's mother, driven by her own ambitions and insecurities, pushes her daughter to extreme lengths, enforcing a regime of "calorie restriction" and constant scrutiny. The author's account of being showered by her mother until age 16, sharing her diaries and income, and undergoing extensive at-home makeovers is both shocking and heartbreaking.

As McCurdy navigates the treacherous waters of fame, she finds herself lost and alone, struggling to cope with the pressures of her career and the weight of her mother's expectations. Her descent into eating disorders, addiction, and unhealthy relationships is a tragic consequence of the trauma and anxiety she endured as a child star. The author's unflinching account of her struggles is both deeply personal and universally relatable, offering a searing indictment of the entertainment industry's treatment of child actors.

Despite the darkness of her experiences, McCurdy's memoir is also a testament to her resilience and determination. With the help of therapy and a newfound independence, she begins to rebuild her life and rediscover her sense of self. The author's writing is marked by a dark humor and refreshing candor, making her story both deeply moving and strangely entertaining.

One of the most striking aspects of McCurdy's memoir is her willingness to confront the complexities of her relationship with her mother. Rather than offering a simplistic or sentimental portrayal, the author presents a nuanced and multifaceted exploration of their bond. Her mother is both a loving and supportive presence and a controlling and manipulative force, embodying the contradictions and paradoxes of their relationship.

Throughout the memoir, McCurdy's writing is characterized by a conversational tone and a keen sense of observation. Her descriptions of her experiences on set, her interactions with her co-stars, and her struggles with her own demons are vivid and engaging, drawing the reader into her world.

In the end, I'm Glad My Mom Died is a memoir about finding freedom and autonomy in the aftermath of trauma. McCurdy's story is a powerful reminder that it's never too late to take control of our lives and forge our own paths. With its unflinching honesty, dark humor, and inspiring message, this memoir is a must-read for anyone who has ever struggled to find their voice or assert their independence.
