# These Violent Delights (These Violent Delights, #1)

**Author:** Chloe Gong  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

In the midst of 1926 Shanghai's decadence, Chloe Gong's These Violent Delights unfolds as a mesmerizing tale of love, loyalty, and the unrelenting struggle for power. This stunning debut is an ingenious retelling of the classic Romeo and Juliet, set against the backdrop of the city's treacherous underworld, where two rival gangs, the Scarlet Gang and the White Flowers, have been embroiled in a generations-long blood feud.

Gong's vivid prose masterfully captures the essence of Shanghai's vibrant landscape, from the opium dens and jazz clubs to the city's underbelly of corruption and crime. The narrative is expertly woven, with each thread meticulously intertwined to create a rich tapestry that is as captivating as it is haunting.

At the heart of the story lies the tumultuous relationship between Juliette Cai, the proud heir of the Scarlet Gang, and Roma Montagov, the enigmatic leader of the White Flowers. Theirs is a love story fraught with betrayal, heartache, and the weight of family expectations. Gong's portrayal of their complex, often toxic, dynamic is both poignant and thought-provoking, raising questions about the true cost of loyalty and the devastating consequences of blind ambition.

As the body count rises and the city teeters on the brink of chaos, Juliette and Roma are forced to put aside their differences and work together to uncover the source of the mysterious contagion that is slowly destroying their world. Gong's masterful plotting is evident in the way she skillfully ratchets up the tension, expertly balancing action, suspense, and romance to create a narrative that is as unpredictable as it is compelling.

One of the most striking aspects of These Violent Delights is Gong's exploration of the immigrant experience and the struggle for identity in a city that is both familiar and foreign. Through Juliette's character, in particular, Gong sheds light on the challenges faced by Chinese immigrants in 1920s Shanghai, where cultural heritage and tradition are constantly at odds with the city's rapid modernization.

Gong's writing style is a character in its own right, with a unique voice that is both lyrical and visceral. Her prose is akin to a rich, velvety darkness that envelops the reader, drawing them into the depths of Shanghai's underworld. The author's use of language is deliberate and evocative, conjuring up the sights, sounds, and smells of the city with breathtaking precision.

In conclusion, These Violent Delights is a stunning debut that will appeal to fans of The Last Magician and Serpent & Dove. Chloe Gong's masterful storytelling, vivid world-building, and complex characters make for a compelling narrative that will leave readers breathless and eager for more. With its unique blend of historical fiction, fantasy, and romance, this book is sure to captivate readers of all ages.
