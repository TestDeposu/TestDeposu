# Mother of Death & Dawn (The War of Lost Hearts, #3)

**Author:** Carissa Broadbent  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

**A Dark, Seductive Dance of Light and Shadow: *Mother of Death & Dawn* by Carissa Broadbent**

Carissa Broadbent’s *Mother of Death & Dawn* (The War of Lost Hearts, #3) is a masterclass in gothic fantasy, where gods bleed into mortals, morality is a shifting tide, and love is both salvation and ruin. This final installment of the trilogy delivers a hauntingly beautiful climax, weaving together themes of redemption, sacrifice, and the intoxicating pull of darkness. With prose as lush as it is sharp, Broadbent crafts a world where every choice is a blade—cutting deep, leaving scars, and demanding blood.

At its core, *Mother of Death & Dawn* is a story about duality. Mische, the protagonist, is a former sun priestess turned vampire—a creature of both light and shadow, torn between the purity she once worshipped and the seductive power of the night. Her journey is one of internal conflict, a battle between duty and desire, faith and corruption. Broadbent doesn’t shy away from the messiness of this struggle; Mische’s evolution is messy, painful, and utterly human. She is neither hero nor villain, but a woman caught in the throes of a war within herself, making her one of the most compelling characters in modern fantasy.

The romance between Mische and Asar, the prince of shadows, is the beating heart of the novel. Their relationship is electric, charged with danger and mutual obsession. Asar is not your typical love interest—he is cold, calculating, and morally ambiguous, yet his devotion to Mische is undeniable. Their dynamic is a slow burn, built on stolen glances, whispered secrets, and the unspoken understanding that love in this world is as fleeting as it is destructive. Broadbent’s portrayal of their bond is visceral, making their connection feel both inevitable and doomed. It’s a love story for those who believe in redemption through ruin, where the only way to save someone is to drag them into the abyss with you.

The world-building in *Mother of Death & Dawn* is rich and immersive, blending mythology with a fresh, dark fantasy aesthetic. The underworld is a character in itself—claustrophobic, oppressive, and teeming with secrets. Broadbent’s descriptions are vivid, painting a world where gods are as flawed as mortals, and darkness is not just absence but a living, breathing force. The stakes are sky-high, with resurrection, betrayal, and the fate of realms hanging in the balance. Yet, amidst the chaos, there’s a sense of inevitability, as if the characters are all hurtling toward a destiny they can’t escape.

Broadbent’s writing style is a double-edged sword—lyrical yet brutal, poetic yet raw. She doesn’t waste words on unnecessary exposition; instead, she trusts the reader to sink into the atmosphere, to feel the weight of every decision. The pacing is relentless, with twists that land like punches and revelations that gut you. The dialogue crackles with tension, and the internal monologues are so intimate they feel like eavesdropping on a confession.

One of the novel’s greatest strengths is its refusal to offer easy answers. There are no clear villains or heroes here—just flawed beings making impossible choices. Mische’s struggle with her identity is mirrored in the world around her, where gods are dying, empires are crumbling, and the line between salvation and damnation is razor-thin. Broadbent doesn’t give her characters (or her readers) the luxury of certainty, which makes the story all the more gripping.

If there’s a flaw to be found, it’s in the occasional overwhelm of lore. The mythology is dense, and new readers might find themselves lost in the web of gods and prophecies. However, this is a minor quibble in a story that thrives on complexity. *Mother of Death & Dawn* is not a book for those who crave simplicity—it’s for those who crave depth, for those who want to be unmade and remade by the pages.

In the end, *Mother of Death & Dawn* is a story about the cost of power, the weight of legacy, and the terrifying beauty of choosing darkness when the light has failed you. It’s a tale of love as both salvation and destruction, of gods who are as broken as the mortals who worship them. Broadbent has crafted a finale that is as haunting as it is hopeful, leaving readers breathless and aching for more.

For fans of dark fantasy with a poetic edge, this is a must-read. For those who love stories about redemption through ruin, about love that burns as bright as it destroys—this is your book. And for everyone else? Well, the darkness is calling.
