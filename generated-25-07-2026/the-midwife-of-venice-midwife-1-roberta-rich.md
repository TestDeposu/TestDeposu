# The Midwife of Venice (Midwife #1)

**Author:** Roberta Rich  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

The Midwife of Venice, penned by Roberta Rich, is a captivating historical novel that whisks readers away to 16th-century Venice, a city of grandeur and mystique. At the heart of this tale lies Hannah Levi, a Jewish midwife with a remarkable gift – her "birthing spoons" – a secret technique that has earned her widespread acclaim for her ability to coax reluctant babies from their mothers. Rich masterfully weaves a narrative that not only delves into the intricacies of childbirth but also explores the complexities of a woman's role in a patriarchal society.

The story unfolds with a sense of urgency as Hannah is approached by a desperate count, who implores her to attend to his ailing wife and save their unborn son. The catch, however, lies in a papal edict that forbids Jews from providing medical treatment to Christians. Hannah is torn, but the promise of a substantial payment – enough to ransom her husband Isaac, who has been captured at sea – proves too enticing to resist. As she becomes embroiled in the treacherous world of the count's family, Hannah's choices put her own life, as well as the life of the unborn child, in peril.

Rich's writing is evocative and immersive, transporting readers to the narrow streets and canals of Venice. Her vivid descriptions of the city's architecture, culture, and customs are meticulous, demonstrating a deep understanding of the historical period. The author's use of language is equally impressive, with a narrative voice that is both lyrical and engaging.

One of the novel's greatest strengths lies in its ability to balance action, suspense, and drama with thoughtful explorations of themes such as identity, community, and the human condition. Rich's portrayal of Hannah is particularly noteworthy, as she skillfully conveys the midwife's inner turmoil, her deep love for her husband, and her unwavering dedication to her craft.

The Midwife of Venice is a testament to Rich's exceptional storytelling ability, and fans of historical fiction will undoubtedly be captivated by this tale. As Library Journal notes, the novel "will capture historical fiction fans and readers who enjoyed Anita Diamant's The Red Tent." With its richly detailed setting, complex characters, and gripping plot, this book is sure to leave readers eager for more.
