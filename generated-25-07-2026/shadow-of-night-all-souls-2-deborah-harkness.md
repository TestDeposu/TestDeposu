# Shadow of Night (All Souls, #2)

**Author:** Deborah Harkness  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

Deborah Harkness’s *Shadow of Night* is a spellbinding sequel that deepens the mysteries of the All Souls trilogy while immersing readers in a richly textured world of witchcraft, time travel, and forbidden love. Picking up where *A Discovery of Witches* left off, this novel thrusts Diana Bishop and Matthew Clairmont into the perilous streets of Elizabethan London, where magic is both a weapon and a curse, and the past is as treacherous as the present. Harkness masterfully blends historical intrigue with supernatural suspense, crafting a narrative that is as intellectually stimulating as it is emotionally gripping.

At its core, *Shadow of Night* explores the theme of identity—both personal and collective. Diana, a reluctant witch struggling to embrace her powers, must navigate a world where magic is both revered and feared. Her journey is one of self-discovery, as she grapples with the expectations placed upon her by both the witch community and her own insecurities. Meanwhile, Matthew’s past resurfaces in the form of the School of Night, a coterie of Elizabethan scholars and spies who blur the lines between ally and adversary. His struggle to reconcile his modern sensibilities with his historical roots adds a layer of complexity to his character, making him more than just a brooding vampire—he becomes a man torn between duty and desire.

Harkness’s prose is lush and evocative, painting a vivid portrait of 16th-century England with meticulous detail. Her descriptions of London’s streets, the opulence of the royal court, and the eerie beauty of the countryside are so immersive that readers will feel as though they’ve stepped into a time machine. Yet, despite the historical setting, the novel never loses sight of its supernatural elements. The magic in *Shadow of Night* is not just a backdrop—it’s a living, breathing force that shapes the characters’ fates. From alchemical experiments to witch trials, Harkness weaves magic into the fabric of the story, making it feel as natural as the air the characters breathe.

One of the novel’s greatest strengths is its pacing. Harkness balances action, romance, and mystery with deft precision, ensuring that the story never drags. The tension between Diana and Matthew is palpable, their love both a sanctuary and a source of conflict. Their relationship is tested by the dangers of the past, the secrets of Matthew’s history, and the looming threat of the missing *Ashmole 782*. Yet, even in the face of adversity, their bond remains the emotional anchor of the story, grounding the supernatural elements in something deeply human.

Harkness’s ability to blend genres is another standout feature of *Shadow of Night*. The novel is part historical fiction, part fantasy, and part romance, yet it never feels disjointed. Instead, these elements coalesce into a cohesive whole, each enhancing the others. The historical setting provides a rich tapestry against which the supernatural drama unfolds, while the romance adds a layer of emotional depth that keeps readers invested in the characters’ fates.

For fans of *A Discovery of Witches*, *Shadow of Night* will feel like a natural progression, deepening the lore of the All Souls world while expanding its scope. For newcomers, it’s a thrilling introduction to a universe where magic and history collide. Harkness’s writing is both accessible and sophisticated, making the novel appealing to a wide range of readers. Whether you’re drawn to the romance, the mystery, or the historical setting, *Shadow of Night* delivers on all fronts.

In the end, *Shadow of Night* is more than just a sequel—it’s a testament to the power of storytelling. Harkness crafts a world that is as enchanting as it is dangerous, where the past and present collide in a dance of magic and mayhem. It’s a book that lingers in the mind long after the final page, leaving readers eager for the next installment in the trilogy.
