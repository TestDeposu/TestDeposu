# Glass Sword (Red Queen, #2)

**Author:** Victoria Aveyard  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

Glass Sword, the second installment in Victoria Aveyard's Red Queen series, is a mesmerizing and intense tale of rebellion, self-discovery, and the blurred lines between right and wrong. The story picks up where the first book left off, with Mare Barrow, a Red with the ability to control lightning, on the run from the ruthless King Maven. As Mare navigates the complexities of war and resistance, she must confront her own demons and the weight of her newfound purpose.

Aveyard's writing is as captivating as it is thought-provoking, delving into the very heart of oppression, power, and the true cost of revolution. The world-building is meticulous, with a richly detailed landscape of Silver and Red, the ruling class and the oppressed, respectively. The author's use of color as a metaphor for social hierarchy is both striking and unsettling, serving as a constant reminder of the prejudices and injustices that Mare and her fellow Reds face.

One of the most compelling aspects of Glass Sword is its exploration of Mare's character, as she grapples with the consequences of her actions and the moral ambiguities of war. Aveyard masterfully crafts Mare's narrative, revealing a complex and multifaceted protagonist who is both fiercely determined and deeply vulnerable. As Mare struggles to come to terms with her own identity and the expectations placed upon her, she must also confront the darkness within herself, and the fear of becoming the very thing she is fighting against.

The supporting cast is equally well-developed, with a range of characters that add depth and nuance to the story. From the enigmatic and charismatic Cal, to the stoic and loyal Kilorn, each character brings their own unique perspective and motivations to the table, adding to the richness and complexity of the narrative.

Aveyard's prose is evocative and immersive, with a keen sense of pacing that propels the reader through the story. The action scenes are tense and suspenseful, while the quieter moments are infused with a sense of introspection and emotional depth. The author's use of language is both vivid and concise, painting a picture of a world on the brink of collapse, where the lines between loyalty and betrayal are constantly blurred.

Ultimately, Glass Sword is a gripping and thought-provoking exploration of the human condition, delving into the darkest corners of our nature and the true cost of rebellion. As Mare and her fellow rebels fight for freedom and justice, they must also confront the shadows within themselves, and the terrible choices that must be made in the name of revolution. With its stunning world-building, complex characters, and gripping narrative, Glass Sword is a must-read for fans of fantasy and science fiction.
