# Not Quite Dead Yet

**Author:** Holly Jackson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

With her explosive adult thriller debut, Not Quite Dead Yet, Holly Jackson pivots away from the high school halls of her mega-bestselling A Good Girl’s Guide to Murder series and steps into a much sharper, higher-stakes arena. Yet, true to form, Jackson retains the relentless pacing, labyrinthine plotting, and fierce, razor-sharp heroines that made her a global phenomenon. Released in 2025, this novel takes a brilliantly morbid and irresistible premise—a young woman racing against a literal ticking clock to solve her own impending homicide—and elevates it into a deeply psychological exploration of wasted potential, familial rot, and the true cost of time.

The narrative hook is deliciously grim. Jet Mason, twenty-seven and paralyzed by the chronic procrastination of the ultra-wealthy, is trapped in the gilded cage of her family’s Woodstock, Vermont estate. She is a woman waiting for her life to begin, perpetually whispering her favorite excuse: I’ll do it later. But fate calls her bluff on Halloween night, when an unseen intruder shatters her skull in a violent attack. The medical verdict is absolute and terrifying: a catastrophic head injury guarantees a fatal aneurysm in exactly seven days. Rejecting a high-risk, low-odds surgery, Jet makes a defiant choice. With a ticking bomb inside her own brain, she refuses to fade quietly. She has one week left, and she is going to spend it hunting the monster who killed her.

At its core, Not Quite Dead Yet is a thematic meditation on time—how we squander it, how we perceive it, and how violently precious it becomes when it is suddenly rationed. Jackson brilliantly uses Jet’s impending mortality as a magnifying glass. Confined to the final week of her life, Jet is forced to strip away the polite illusions of her privileged existence. Her wealthy parents, her estranged former best friend who shockingly married into the family as a sister-in-law, and her lingering ex-boyfriend all transform from benign fixtures of her routine into potential murderers. The idyllic autumn backdrop of Vermont serves as a chilling juxtaposition to the rot festering beneath the surface of Woodstock’s elite. 

Jackson’s stylistic fingerprint is unmistakable throughout the descent. She masters the art of the breathless countdown, structuring the novel with a visceral urgency that mirrors Jet’s deteriorating neurological state. As the physical symptoms of the trauma mount—dizziness, failing vision, cognitive slips—the psychological tension tightens in tandem. This is not just a whodunit; it is a race against biology. Jet’s journey is anchored by her reunion with Billy, a steadfast childhood friend who acts as her willing accomplice and grounding force. Their dynamic infuses the dark subject matter with a bittersweet, desperate loyalty that cuts straight to the emotional core of the book.

What makes Jackson’s adult debut such a triumphant evolution is her refusal to rely on cheap gimmicks. While the premise borders on high-concept thriller territory, the emotional stakes feel painfully grounded. Jet’s obsession with solving her own murder is paradoxically an obsession with finally living. For the first time in her twenty-seven years, she is forced to finish something, transforming her impending death into the catalyst for her ultimate awakening. 

Full of the writer’s signature, floor-dropping twists and turns, Not Quite Dead Yet snowballs into a roller-coaster ride of page-turning suspense that will leave readers utterly breathless. Jackson proves effortlessly that her transition to adult fiction is not just a successful pivot, but a masterclass in suspense. If you thought you knew what Holly Jackson was capable of, think again. Your time is up—and you won't want to put this down until the very last page.
