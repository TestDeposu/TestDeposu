# This Savage Song (Monsters of Verity, #1)

**Author:** Victoria Schwab  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

In a literary landscape frequently saturated by predictable tropes, Victoria Schwab’s 2016 urban fantasy, This Savage Song, slices through the noise with the precision of a razor’s edge. Set in the gritty, fractured metropolis of Verity—a city physically and ideologically torn down the middle—Schwab crafts a chilling premise where human depravity quite literally gives birth to physical nightmares. Violence breeds monsters here, and Schwab wastes no time immersing her readers in a world where safety is nothing more than a nostalgic fairy tale. 

At the pulsating heart of the narrative are two brilliantly realized protagonists whose very existence challenges the simplistic dichotomy of good versus evil. Kate Harker is the daughter of a ruthless crime lord who profits off human fear, desperate to prove her own merciless mettle in a world that demands stone-cold survival. August Flynn, conversely, is a Sunai—a rare, devastating creature born of mass slaughter who possesses the ability to steal a soul with a haunting melody, yet desperately yearns for nothing more than to be human and good. When their paths violently collide, Schwab sets in motion a high-stakes cat-and-mouse game that forces both teenagers to question the nature of their own identities.

The central theme humming beneath the surface of This Savage Song is an incisive exploration of humanity and monstrosity. Schwab asks a timeless, agonizing question: what truly makes a monster? Is it the creature born from violence, or the human who willingly inflicts it for power and profit? Through August and Kate, the author blurs the lines until they are practically indistinguishable. August fights every single day against the violent instincts coded into his very DNA, striving for grace and empathy, while humans in power commit atrocities with a smile and a ledger. In this grim mirror held up to our own society, Schwab suggests that true monstrosity is not a physical mutation, but a choice.

Schwab’s signature writing style is electric, lean, and atmospheric. She treats prose like sheet music, balancing sparse, impactful sentences with moments of haunting lyrical beauty. Verity feels remarkably alive—breathing, bleeding, and seething with a dark energy that invites inevitable comparisons to Gotham City. There is a cinematic quality to her pacing; action sequences slam into emotional beats with breathtaking velocity, keeping the reader thoroughly off-balance and endlessly captivated. She avoids purple prose, opting instead for a visceral, muscular storytelling approach that punches you in the gut and leaves you gasping for air. 

What makes This Savage Song stand out as a triumph of contemporary dark fantasy is its refusal to offer easy answers. There are no clean-cut heroes riding in to save the day, only flawed, traumatized kids trying to survive a broken system. Schwab examines how violence permeates a culture, poisoning relationships and institutions alike. It is a story about the weight of expectations, the burden of lineage, and the terrifying, beautiful agency found in choosing who you want to become when the world around you is burning.

Ultimately, Victoria Schwab has delivered a masterclass in modern speculative fiction. This Savage Song is dark, brooding, and fiercely compelling, anchored by a unique magic system and a deeply emotional core. It grabs you by the throat in the opening chapter and refuses to let go long after the final page. For anyone craving a story with teeth, heart, and a relentless pulse, this opening salvo of the Monsters of Verity duology is an absolute must-read.
