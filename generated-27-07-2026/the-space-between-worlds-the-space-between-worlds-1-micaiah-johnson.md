# The Space Between Worlds (The Space Between Worlds, #1)

**Author:** Micaiah Johnson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

**A Challenging Exploration of Identity, Privilege, and Belonging**

Micaiah Johnson's debut novel, The Space Between Worlds, is a thought-provoking and immersive narrative that explores the intricate connections between identity, privilege, and belonging in a multiverse where travel between worlds is possible. This stunning debut is a powerful commentary on the complexities of human existence, social hierarchies, and the human need to belong.

The story follows Cara, an outsider who has survived on a dystopian Earth while her counterparts have died on 372 other worlds. This peculiar ability makes her an ideal candidate for the Eldridge Institute, where she works alongside her enigmatic handler, Dell, collecting data from various worlds. Cara's life seems to be on an upward trajectory, but her world is turned upside down when one of her remaining counterparts dies under mysterious circumstances.

What follows is a gripping narrative that delves into the very fabric of the multiverse, revealing secrets that threaten the existence of entire worlds. Johnson skillfully navigates the complexities of Cara's identity, skillfully blurring the lines between reality and fantasy. As Cara navigates her way through different worlds and timelines, she is forced to confront her own privilege, social status, and belonging in a society that is inherently hierarchical.

Throughout the novel, Johnson's writing is nothing short of breathtaking. Her use of language is evocative, her world-building is meticulous, and her characters are well-developed and complex. Her prose is peppered with clever observations, surprise twists, and razor-sharp commentary on social and racial inequities. Her world-building is equally impressive, conjuring up vivid images of a multiverse that is both familiar and yet completely alien.

One of the most striking aspects of The Space Between Worlds is Johnson's unflinching exploration of privilege and social status. Johnson masterfully highlights the ways in which societal privilege can both empower and constrain individuals, creating a sense of agency and purpose while simultaneously limiting opportunities and choices. Through Cara's experiences, Johnson shines a light on the complexities of privilege, exposing its intricacies and nuance.

However, what really sets Johnson apart is her ability to balance action, suspense, and introspection. The plot is expertly crafted, propelling the reader through a thrilling journey that is both intellectually stimulating and emotionally resonant. The pacing is well-balanced, with moments of high-octane action and suspense punctuated by quieter introspective moments that allow the reader to catch their breath.

Johnson's greatest achievement, however, lies in her ability to create a narrative that is both universally relatable and uniquely personal. The Space Between Worlds is a story about identity, privilege, and belonging that is both deeply personal and yet strangely universal. It's a narrative that speaks to our collective humanity, our capacity for both good and evil, and our fundamental desire to belong.

In short, The Space Between Worlds is a stunning debut that will resonate with readers long after the final page has been turned. Micaiah Johnson is a writer to watch, and this novel is a testament to her skill and artistry.
