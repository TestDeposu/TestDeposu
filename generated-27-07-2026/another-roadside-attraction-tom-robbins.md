# Another Roadside Attraction

**Author:** Tom Robbins  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1990

In the midst of a literary landscape often dominated by the mundane and the ordinary, Tom Robbins' "Another Roadside Attraction" is a breath of fresh air, a wildly imaginative and unapologetically unconventional novel that defies easy categorization. First published in 1971, this semicomic thriller has lost none of its originality and charm, and its themes of spirituality, counterculture, and the search for meaning continue to resonate with readers today.

At its core, "Another Roadside Attraction" is a novel about the power of the human spirit to create its own meaning in a seemingly meaningless world. The story centers around a young couple, John Paul Ziller and Plucky Purcell, who embark on a series of misadventures that take them from a rundown roadside zoo to a mystical encounter with the "Corpse" – none other than the body of Jesus Christ himself. Along the way, they encounter a cast of eccentric characters, including a clairvoyant named Amanda who becomes the focal point of a spiritual movement that seeks to revive the ancient traditions of fertility worship.

Throughout the novel, Robbins' writing is characterized by its wit, humor, and a deep sense of irreverence. He is a master of language, able to craft sentences that are at once beautiful, profound, and hilarious. His style is reminiscent of Mark Twain, as the Los Angeles Times noted, but it is also uniquely his own, a blend of the poetic and the profane that is both captivating and thought-provoking.

One of the most striking aspects of "Another Roadside Attraction" is its ability to capture the spirit of the 1960s, a decade that was marked by a sense of idealism, experimentation, and rebellion. Robbins' novel is not a nostalgic look back at the era, but rather a recreation of its ethos and values, filtered through his own unique perspective. The result is a novel that is both a product of its time and a timeless work of art, one that continues to speak to readers today.

In short, "Another Roadside Attraction" is a novel that is hard to put down, not just because of its sheer brilliance and fun, but also because of its ability to challenge our assumptions and make us think. It is a book that will appeal to fans of Thomas Pynchon, Don DeLillo, and other writers who are known for their innovative and provocative style. But it is also a novel that will resonate with anyone who is looking for a story that is both entertaining and thought-provoking, a true original that will leave readers changed forever.
