# Those Who Leave and Those Who Stay (Neapolitan Novels, #3)

**Author:** Elena Ferrante  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

"Those Who Leave and Those Who Stay," the third installment in Elena Ferrante's Neapolitan Novels, is a compelling exploration of the intricate dynamics of female friendship, ambition, and the societal expectations that shape women's lives. Ferrante, a master of character study, delves deep into the psyches of her protagonists, Elena and Lila, as they navigate the tumultuous waters of womanhood in post-war Naples.

The novel opens with Elena, now a successful writer, reflecting on her friendship with Lila, who has chosen a different path. Lila, married young and now divorced, works as a laborer, a stark contrast to Elena's intellectual and literary pursuits. Yet, despite their divergent lives, the bond between these two women remains unbroken, a testament to the complex, often messy, nature of long-term friendships.

Ferrante's prose is as raw and unadorned as ever, allowing the characters' voices to resonate with an authenticity that is both powerful and unsettling. She has a unique ability to capture the essence of her characters' thoughts and emotions, laying them bare for the reader to examine. This is not a comfortable read; Ferrante does not shy away from the ugly truths of her characters' lives, nor does she offer easy resolutions. Instead, she presents a nuanced portrayal of women grappling with the consequences of their choices, and the societal structures that both constrain and enable them.

At its heart, "Those Who Leave and Those Who Stay" is a meditation on the choices women make, and the price they pay for them. It is a story about the tension between personal ambition and familial obligation, about the struggle to reconcile one's dreams with the reality of one's circumstances. It is also a story about the power of female friendship, about the ways in which women support and challenge each other, and about the complex, often fraught, nature of sisterhood.

Ferrante's exploration of these themes is as timely as it is timeless. In a world where women are still grappling with issues of equality and representation, her portrayal of Elena and Lila's struggles feels both urgent and resonant. This is not a book that offers easy answers or neat conclusions. Instead, it is a book that asks questions, that challenges the reader to think deeply about the nature of female friendship, ambition, and the choices we make.

In essence, "Those Who Leave and Those Who Stay" is a powerful addition to the Neapolitan Novels series, a book that showcases Ferrante's exceptional storytelling abilities and leaves the reader eager for the final volume. It is a book that demands to be read, discussed, and thought about. It is a book that will stay with you long after you've turned the last page.
