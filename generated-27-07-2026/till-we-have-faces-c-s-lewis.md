# Till We Have Faces

**Author:** C.S. Lewis  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

Till We Have Faces by C.S. Lewis is a profound and deeply moving exploration of the complexities of love, identity, and the human condition. On the surface, this novel appears to be a retelling of the ancient myth of Cupid and Psyche, but as one delves deeper into the narrative, it becomes clear that Lewis is using this timeless tale as a vehicle to examine the very fabric of our existence.

The story is told from the perspective of Orual, the embittered and unattractive older sister of the beautiful Psyche. Orual's narrative voice is a masterclass in characterization, as Lewis skillfully conveys the depth of her emotional pain and inner turmoil. Through Orual's eyes, we witness the events that unfold in the kingdom of Glome, a brutal and unforgiving world where the sacred and profane coexist in an uneasy tension.

As the novel progresses, it becomes clear that Orual's possessive love for Psyche is a double-edged sword, driving her to both protect and harm her sister. This complex dynamic serves as a catalyst for Orual's journey of self-discovery, as she grapples with the nature of love, sacrifice, and the divine. Lewis's prose is both elegant and incisive, as he dissects the intricacies of the human heart with precision and nuance.

One of the most striking aspects of Till We Have Faces is its exploration of the tension between sacred and profane love. Lewis masterfully illustrates the ways in which our understanding of love is often distorted by our own biases and limitations. Through Orual's narrative, we see how her perception of love is warped by her own experiences of rejection and abandonment. Conversely, Psyche's love for Cupid serves as a counterpoint, highlighting the redemptive power of selfless and unconditional love.

Lewis's writing style is, as always, a marvel to behold. His use of language is both poetic and precise, conjuring the world of Glome with vivid detail and depth. The novel's pacing is deliberate and measured, allowing the reader to absorb the full weight of Orual's emotional journey. The themes of love, identity, and the search for meaning are woven throughout the narrative with a level of sophistication and nuance that is characteristic of Lewis's work.

If there is a criticism to be made, it is that the novel's pacing can feel somewhat slow, particularly in the early stages. However, this is a minor quibble, as the rewards of persisting with the narrative far outweigh any initial frustrations.

In conclusion, Till We Have Faces is a masterpiece of contemporary fiction that deserves to be read and reread. Lewis's exploration of the human condition is both profound and deeply moving, and his writing style is, as always, a joy to behold. If you are looking for a novel that will challenge your perceptions and linger in your mind long after you finish reading, then Till We Have Faces is an absolute must-read.
