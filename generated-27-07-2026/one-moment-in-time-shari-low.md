# One Moment in Time

**Author:** Shari Low  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

Title: Rewinding the Clock to Ruin: A Review of Shari Low’s One Moment in Time

There is a distinct kind of narrative tightrope that multi-million-copy bestseller Shari Low walks so effortlessly, balancing laugh-out-loud domestic humor with the gut-punch of emotional devastation. In her 2023 release, One Moment in Time, Low masterfully demonstrates why she remains a powerhouse of contemporary fiction. This is not merely a story about a milestone anniversary; it is a meticulously constructed, high-stakes exploration of how a single, fleeting second in the past can ripple across three decades with catastrophic consequences.

At the heart of the novel lies a brilliantly cruel dramatic irony. Brenda Jones is entirely resolved to divorce her husband of thirty years, planning to pull the plug on her marriage the moment they return from a mysterious family getaway. Little does she know, her well-meaning daughter, Zara, has orchestrated an elaborate, high-rolling surprise: a trip to Las Vegas to secretly renew her parents vows, meticulously mirroring every detail of their 1993 wedding. To complete the nostalgia trip, Zara tracks down Aiden Gregg, the son of the couple who stood witness at the original ceremony, inviting them to join the festivities. 

What follows is a narrative pressure cooker. Low uses the countdown to Vegas as a brilliant structural device, ratcheting up the tension as readers are left wondering whether the destination will be a heartwarming celebration of enduring love or an absolute rendezvous with disaster. 

Low’s greatest strength in One Moment in Time is her trademark narrative style. She writes with an intimacy that makes you feel as though you are gossiping with a close friend over a glass of wine. Her character work is razor-sharp, presenting flawed, deeply human individuals whose internal dilemmas feel startlingly authentic. Brenda’s quiet desperation, Zara’s blind optimism, and Aiden’s navigation of his own contemporary relationship woes create a vibrant juxtaposition of perspectives. Low expertly shifts between past and present, peeling back the layers of 1993 to reveal the secrets, betrayals, and unspoken truths that have festered beneath the surface of these families for thirty years.

Fans of Jojo Moyes, Marian Keyes, and David Nicholls will find much to love here. Low shares their uncanny ability to make you laugh on one page and tear up on the next. She understands that long-term relationships are not fairy tales, but complex tapestries woven from compromise, endurance, and, sometimes, deeply buried regrets. 

One Moment in Time is an unputdownable, emotional rollercoaster that asks a haunting question: If you could rewind the clock, would you right the wrongs of the past, or would you leave sleeping dogs lie? Shari Low has crafted a stunning, suspenseful, and ultimately unforgettable exploration of love, marriage, and the choices that define us. Clear your schedule before you open this book—once you board Zara’s flight to Vegas, you won’t want to get off.
