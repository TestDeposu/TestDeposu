# The Sunne in Splendour

**Author:** Sharon Kay Penman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2008

**A Radiant Reappraisal: "The Sunne in Splendour" by Sharon Kay Penman**

In the sprawling landscape of historical fiction, few authors have left as indelible a mark as Sharon Kay Penman. Her magnum opus, "The Sunne in Splendour," is a resplendent retelling of the life of Richard III, a monarch long maligned by history. Published in 2008, this novel stands as a testament to Penman's mastery of scholarship and storytelling, offering readers a richly textured, revisionist portrait of a king.

Penman's narrative is a triumph of research and imagination, weaving together meticulous historical detail with a compelling, character-driven plot. Born into the tumultuous courts of fifteenth-century England, Richard, Duke of Gloucester, is a man shaped by the political storms of his time. Yet, Penman's Richard is far removed from the hunchbacked villain of Shakespeare's imagination. Instead, we encounter a complex, loyal, and passionate individual, a man far more sinned against than sinning.

Penman's prose is as sumptuous as the period it depicts, rich in sensory detail and evocative language. She paints her characters with a deft hand, bringing to life not just Richard, but also the vibrant tapestry of his world: from the scheming nobles of the court to the common folk caught in the crossfire of political intrigue. The novel's pacing is masterful, balancing political machinations with intimate, emotional beats, creating a narrative that is both epic and intimate.

At the heart of "The Sunne in Splendour" lies Penman's audacious reimagining of Richard III. She challenges the traditional narrative, presenting a king who is not a villain, but a victim of circumstance and slander. This revisionist approach is not without controversy, but Penman's argument is compelling, backed by rigorous research and presented with a novelist's flair.

In celebration of its thirtieth anniversary, "The Sunne in Splendour" has been reissued with a new preface by the author. Here, Penman discusses the astonishing discovery of Richard's remains, a find that has only served to fuel the debate about this most controversial of kings. This edition is a must-read for fans of historical fiction, offering not just a gripping narrative, but also a fascinating insight into the ongoing dialogue between history and fiction.

In conclusion, "The Sunne in Splendour" is a tour de force of historical fiction, a novel that challenges, engages, and enthralls in equal measure. It is a radiant reappraisal of a king, a testament to the power of storytelling, and a shining example of Sharon Kay Penman's extraordinary talent.
