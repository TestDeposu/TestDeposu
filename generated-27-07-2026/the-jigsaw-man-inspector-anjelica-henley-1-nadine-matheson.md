# The Jigsaw Man (Inspector Anjelica Henley, #1)

**Author:** Nadine Matheson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

The Jigsaw Man, Nadine Matheson's gripping debut, is a masterclass in crafting a heart-pounding, suspenseful narrative that will leave even the most seasoned thriller enthusiasts sleeping with the lights on. This is a crime novel that expertly weaves together the dark threads of a serial killer's twisted game, the meticulous investigation of a determined detective, and the blurred lines between good and evil.

At the center of this maelstrom is Detective Inspector Anjelica Henley, a complex and intriguing protagonist with a rich inner life. Her return to active duty with the Serial Crimes Unit coincides with the emergence of a copycat killer who is emulating the gruesome modus operandi of Peter Olivier, the notorious Jigsaw Killer. As the body count rises, Henley finds herself locked in a desperate game of cat and mouse with not one, but two ruthless adversaries.

Matheson's prose is lean and mean, with a keen attention to detail that immerses the reader in the dark world of serial killers and their twisted motivations. Her writing is both vivid and visceral, with a keen sense of pacing that expertly ratchets up the tension as the story unfolds. The author's experience as a criminal attorney is evident in the meticulous plotting and the nuanced characterization of the detectives and the killers.

One of the standout aspects of The Jigsaw Man is its thought-provoking exploration of the theme of identity and how it is constructed and deconstructed. Peter Olivier, the Jigsaw Killer, is a master manipulator who uses his crimes to assert his control over the world and to create a twisted sense of self. His copycat, on the other hand, is driven by a desire to supplant Olivier and claim the spotlight for himself. Meanwhile, DI Henley is grappling with her own identity and sense of purpose as she navigates the treacherous landscape of her job.

Through the lens of these characters, Matheson raises important questions about the nature of identity and how it is shaped by our experiences, our relationships, and our darkest impulses. The Jigsaw Man is not just a gripping thriller; it is also a nuanced exploration of the human condition, with all its complexities and contradictions.

Overall, The Jigsaw Man is a stunning debut that will appeal to fans of crime fiction, thrillers, and suspense. Matheson's writing is assured, her plotting is intricate, and her characters are richly drawn and complex. This is a must-read for anyone who loves a good thriller, and it will leave you breathless until the very last page.
