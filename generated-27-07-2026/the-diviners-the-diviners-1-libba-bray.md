# The Diviners (The Diviners, #1)

**Author:** Libba Bray  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

**The Diviners: A Mesmerizing Dance with the Supernatural**

In the pulsating heart of 1920s New York City, Libba Bray weaves a spellbinding tale that seamlessly blends the vibrant Jazz Age with the chilling shadows of the occult. "The Diviners," the first installment in Bray's captivating series, is a supernatural historical mystery that grips you from the very first page and doesn't let go until the thrilling conclusion.

At the story's core lies Evangeline "Evie" O'Neill, a spirited young woman with a secret power that has thus far brought her nothing but trouble. Exiled from her small-town life, Evie finds herself in the Big Apple, living with her uncle Will, a man obsessed with the occult. When a murdered girl, branded with a cryptic symbol, is discovered, Evie's gift could be the key to catching a serial killer. But as she dives headfirst into the investigation, she sets off a chain reaction that will forever change the lives of those around her.

Bray's prose is as intoxicating as a speakeasy's secret concoction, drawing you into the gritty, glamorous world of 1920s New York. She masterfully paints a vivid picture of the era, from the dazzling Ziegfeld girls to the seedy underbelly of the city, all while effortlessly weaving in elements of the supernatural. Bray's characters are as diverse and captivating as the city itself, each with their own unique voice and story arc that adds depth and richness to the narrative.

The main theme of "The Diviners" is the power of belief—belief in oneself, in one's abilities, and in the unseen forces that shape our world. Evie's journey is one of self-discovery and acceptance, as she grapples with her gift and the darkness it brings. Bray explores this theme through Evie's relationships with the other characters, each of whom has their own unique connection to the supernatural, and their own beliefs to challenge.

Bray's style is reminiscent of a master storyteller, spinning a yarn that keeps you on the edge of your seat, eager to know what happens next. She expertly balances suspense, humor, and heartache, creating a story that is as engaging as it is thought-provoking. The pacing is near-perfect, building to a climax that is as satisfying as it is shocking.

"The Diviners" is more than just a supernatural mystery; it's a love letter to the city of New York, a exploration of the power of belief, and a testament to the human spirit's resilience in the face of darkness. It's a book that will captivate you, haunt you, and leave you eager for more.
