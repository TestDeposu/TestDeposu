# Dark Age (Red Rising Saga, #5)

**Author:** Pierce Brown  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

Pierce Brown's Dark Age is a masterclass in storytelling, a sprawling epic that delves into the complexities of power, rebellion, and the true cost of revolution. The fifth installment in the Red Rising Saga, Dark Age is a testament to Brown's skill as a writer, weaving together multiple plot threads and character arcs into a cohesive and thrilling narrative.

At its core, Dark Age is a exploration of the consequences of Darrow's actions in the previous books. The hero of the Rising, who once brought hope to a desperate people, now finds himself an outlaw, cast out of the very Republic he helped create. This fall from grace serves as a catalyst for the events of the book, as Darrow wages a rogue war on Mercury, outnumbered and outgunned. It's a bold move, one that raises questions about the nature of heroism and the morality of those who would seek to challenge the status quo.

Brown's writing is, as ever, visceral and immersive. His prose is akin to a sledgehammer, pounding away at the reader's senses with its intensity and emotional depth. The action scenes are breathtaking, the characters richly drawn and complex. The world-building, too, is exceptional, with Brown expanding on the universe he created in the previous books.

One of the standout aspects of Dark Age is its cast of characters. Lysander au Lune, the displaced heir to the old empire, is a fascinating addition to the series, his motivations and actions driving much of the plot. Virginia au Augustus, the embattled Sovereign of the Republic, is another highlight, her struggles to preserve her democracy and her exiled husband serving as a powerful counterpoint to Darrow's narrative.

The themes of power and rebellion are woven throughout the book, as characters grapple with the consequences of their actions. Brown raises important questions about the nature of leadership, the dangers of unchecked power, and the true cost of revolution. It's a nuanced and thought-provoking exploration, one that adds depth and complexity to the series.

If there's a criticism to be made, it's that the book can feel overwhelming at times. The multiple plot threads and character arcs can be difficult to keep track of, and some readers may find themselves struggling to keep up. However, this is a minor quibble, and one that's easily forgiven in light of the book's many strengths.

In short, Dark Age is a triumph, a thrilling and thought-provoking addition to the Red Rising Saga. Pierce Brown's writing is, as ever, exceptional, and his characters and world-building are among the best in the genre. If you're a fan of science fiction, or just great storytelling in general, then Dark Age is a must-read.
