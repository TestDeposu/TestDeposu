# Blood Bound (Mercy Thompson, #2)

**Author:** Patricia Briggs  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

Stepping back into the asphalt-stained, grease-scented shoes of Mercedes Thompson, Patricia Briggs proves with Blood Bound that her breakout urban fantasy series is no fluke. Published in 2007, this second installment in the Mercy Thompson saga expands masterfully upon the richly textured supernatural underworld introduced in Moon Called. Briggs delivers a breathless, sharp-witted thrill ride that cements her protagonist not just as a kick-ass heroine, but as one of the most compelling and grounded voices in contemporary speculative fiction.

At the heart of Blood Bound lies a powerful and resonant central theme: the heavy toll of obligation and the true definition of family, forged not by blood, but by choice and loyalty. Mercy is fundamentally an outsider—a coyote shapeshifter living among the apex predators of the Pacific Northwest, too vulnerable to command the raw terror of werewolves yet too formidable to be ignored. When a routine favor called in by her vampire friend Stefan spirals into a catastrophic bloodbath, Mercy’s world is thrown into chaos. 

What makes Blood Bound so striking is how Briggs weaponizes this theme of indebtedness. Mercy’s life is defined by a code of honor; when she owes a favor, she pays it back, regardless of the personal cost. This moral compass drags her deep into a dark, visceral mystery involving a rogue vampire possessed by an ancient, predatory demon. Yet, unlike many urban fantasy protagonists who wield god-like powers to blast their way out of trouble, Mercy relies on her wits, her mechanical aptitude, her sheer stubbornness, and her status as a coyote—a trickster spirit. She is remarkably fragile compared to the immortal vampires and lethal werewolves surrounding her, making every confrontation crackle with genuine, high-stakes peril.

Briggs’s authorial style is the true engine driving the narrative. Her prose is deceptively lean, fast-paced, and effortlessly readable, yet it never sacrifices emotional depth or atmospheric world-building. Where other authors in the genre lean heavily into gratuitous grit or glossy melodrama, Briggs balances the supernatural horror with genuine warmth, biting humor, and a distinctly blue-collar aesthetic. Mercy is a Volkswagen mechanic who worries about rent and broken tools just as much as she worries about rogue demons and local politics. This clever juxtaposition of the mundane and the mythical grounds the story, making the fantastical elements feel terrifyingly real.

Furthermore, Briggs handles the romantic subplot with commendable restraint and nuance. Rather than bogging down the narrative in tiresome, protracted love triangles, the tension between Mercy and the two alpha werewolves circling her heart—Adam Hauptman and Samuel Cornick—is handled with psychological acuity. It reflects Mercy’s deep-seated trauma and her fierce independence; she refuses to be claimed, conquered, or defined by any man, werewolf or otherwise.

Blood Bound is a masterclass in urban fantasy pacing and character development. It takes the solid foundation of its predecessor and raises the stakes, offering a darker, punchier, and more emotionally resonant narrative. Patricia Briggs has crafted a fast-paced page-turner that satisfies cravings for supernatural action while delivering a protagonist you cannot help but root for. For fans of smart, mythologically rich, and character-driven fantasy, this book is an absolute must-read.
