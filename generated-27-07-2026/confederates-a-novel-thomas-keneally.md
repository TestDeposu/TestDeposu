# Confederates: A Novel

**Author:** Thomas Keneally  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

In "Confederates: A Novel", Thomas Keneally weaves a powerful and poignant tale of America's Civil War, delving into the complexities of human nature, loyalty, and the true face of war. Through the voices of Confederate soldiers, turncoats, and the enigmatic Stonewall Jackson, Keneally masterfully crafts a narrative that is both a historical epic and an intimate exploration of the human condition.

At the heart of the novel lies the story of Usaph Bumpass, a young man who leaves behind his wife, Ephie, to join the Shenandoah Volunteers, only to discover that her lover, Decatur Cate, is among his comrades. This personal conflict serves as a microcosm for the larger themes of the novel, as Usaph grapples with his devotion to a cause he doesn't fully understand, all while struggling with the memories of home that haunt him on the march north.

Keneally's prose is both evocative and unflinching, painting a vivid picture of the ragtag Confederate army as they gather in Virginia under Jackson's leadership. The author's masterful use of language brings the characters to life, imbuing them with a sense of depth and nuance that is all too rare in historical fiction. From the brilliant military strategist Jackson to the ordinary soldiers who make up the bulk of the army, each character is drawn with precision and care, their motivations and desires expertly woven into the larger narrative.

One of the novel's greatest strengths lies in its ability to capture the human face of war, laying bare the harsh realities of conflict and the toll it takes on those who fight it. Keneally pulls no punches, depicting the brutal and often senseless violence that characterized the Civil War, while also exploring the more subtle effects of war on the human psyche. The result is a novel that is both a powerful anti-war statement and a deeply personal exploration of the human condition.

Throughout the novel, Keneally's writing is reminiscent of Tolstoy's epic "War and Peace", with its sweeping narrative and deeply human characters. The comparison is apt, as "Confederates" is a novel that shares many of the same themes and concerns as Tolstoy's masterpiece. Like "War and Peace", "Confederates" is a novel that seeks to capture the essence of war, to convey the chaos and confusion that lies at its heart. And like Tolstoy, Keneally is a master of his craft, using his prose to conjure up a world that is both vivid and immersive.

In short, "Confederates" is a breathtaking work of historical fiction that is not to be missed. With its powerful themes, vivid characters, and masterful prose, it is a novel that will leave readers breathless and moved. Whether you're a fan of historical fiction, a student of the Civil War, or simply a lover of great literature, "Confederates" is a must-read.
