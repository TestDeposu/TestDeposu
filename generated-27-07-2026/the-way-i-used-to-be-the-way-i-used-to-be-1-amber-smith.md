# The Way I Used to Be (The Way I Used to Be, #1)

**Author:** Amber Smith  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

The Way I Used to Be, Amber Smith's poignant debut novel, delves into the complexities of trauma, identity, and the unrelenting passage of time. This heart-wrenching narrative is a powerful exploration of the long-lasting effects of pain and the resilience of the human spirit.

The story revolves around Eden, a high school student whose life is forever altered by a brutal act of violence. The night her brother's best friend rapes her, Eden's world is turned upside down, and she is left to navigate the treacherous landscape of trauma, guilt, and shame. As the novel progresses, Eden's voice becomes a haunting reminder of the devastating consequences of silence and the importance of finding one's voice in the face of adversity.

Smith's writing style is both evocative and unflinching, tackling the difficult subject matter with sensitivity and nuance. The narrative is divided into four parts, each representing a year in Eden's high school journey. This structure allows the reader to witness Eden's growth, her struggles, and her ultimate transformation. Smith's use of language is lyrical, yet raw, perfectly capturing the intensity of Eden's emotions as she grapples with the aftermath of the assault.

One of the most striking aspects of The Way I Used to Be is its thoughtful exploration of the complexities of trauma. Smith sheds light on the often-overlooked aspects of recovery, revealing the ways in which trauma can seep into every aspect of a person's life. Eden's journey is not one of easy redemption or simplistic solutions; instead, it is a messy, often painful, exploration of the human experience.

Through Eden's story, Smith highlights the importance of female empowerment, the need for open communication, and the value of seeking help. The novel is a powerful reminder that trauma is not something to be ashamed of, but rather something to be acknowledged and addressed. Eden's struggles serve as a testament to the strength and resilience of the human spirit, and her ultimate triumph is a beacon of hope for those who have suffered in silence.

The Way I Used to Be is a necessary read, one that will resonate with anyone who has experienced trauma, shame, or guilt. Smith's writing is a testament to the transformative power of storytelling, and her debut novel is a heart-twisting, yet ultimately hopeful, exploration of the human experience.
