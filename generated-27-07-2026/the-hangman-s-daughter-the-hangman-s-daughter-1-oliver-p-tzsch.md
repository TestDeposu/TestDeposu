# The Hangman's Daughter (The Hangman's Daughter, #1)

**Author:** Oliver Pötzsch  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

Step into the gaslit, shadow-drenched alleyways of 1893 Vienna with Oliver Pötzsch’s latest atmospheric masterpiece, a gripping historical thriller that brilliantly transports readers to the turbulent birth of modern criminology. While longtime fans of the author’s celebrated medieval tales will recognize his signature meticulous research and knack for macabre storytelling, this new series opener shifts the lens to the fin de siècle—a glittering era of high society masking a decadent, deeply superstitious underworld.

At the heart of the novel is a brilliant, unconventional pairing that instantly captivates. Augustin Rothmayer, a philosophical gravedigger at Vienna's sprawling Central Cemetery, finds comfort among the dead while penning the world's first almanac of his grim profession. His quiet existence is upended by Inspector Leopold von Herzfeldt, an ambitious young investigator fresh from Graz, who brings with him the radical, untrusted science of early forensics. When a series of young maids are discovered brutally murdered and staked to the earth—the unmistakable hallmarks of an ancient ritual meant to prevent the dead from rising—the detective turns to the gravedigger for expertise in mortality. Together with Julia Wolf, a sharp-witted telephone operator whose modern sensibilities and inner demons add layers of emotional depth, this unlikely trio forms the beating heart of the narrative.

Pötzsch’s greatest triumph here is his ability to seamlessly blend the gothic horrors of folklore with the nascent logic of fingerprinting, photography, and psychological profiling. The author evokes fin de siècle Vienna with intoxicating sensory detail: the smell of damp earth and cheap tobacco in the cemetery, the hum of the new telephone wires overhead, and the stark contrast between the city's opulent opera houses and its squalid slums. The mystery itself is tightly wound, playing skillfully on the tension between superstition and science in an era desperately trying to drag itself into the twentieth century. 

Inventive, atmospheric, and compulsively readable, this historical crime novel delivers the dark thrills and rich historical tapestry that international mystery lovers crave. Whether you are searching for a haunting read for spooky-season nights or simply appreciate a masterclass in historical pacing and character development, Pötzsch proves once again why he is a premier voice in modern historical fiction.
