# The Stone Sky (The Broken Earth, #3)

**Author:** N.K. Jemisin  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

In "The Stone Sky," the breathtaking conclusion to N.K. Jemisin's Broken Earth trilogy, the fate of humanity hangs precariously in the balance. This stunning novel is a masterful exploration of the cyclical nature of time, the destructive power of oppression, and the unyielding force of motherly love.

Jemisin's prose is as evocative as ever, weaving a rich tapestry of worlds both familiar and unknown. Her writing is a character in its own right, pulsing with a rhythmic intensity that propels the reader through the narrative. The world-building, too, is a marvel, with Jemisin's unique magic system and the intricacies of orogene culture adding depth and complexity to the story.

At the heart of "The Stone Sky" is the poignant and often fraught relationship between Essun and her daughter Nassun. Theirs is a bond forged in love and tempered by the harsh realities of a world that fears and reviles them. As they navigate their complicated past and the uncertain future, Jemisin raises important questions about the nature of power, identity, and the true meaning of motherhood.

Through Essun's narrative, Jemisin explores the tension between preservation and destruction, highlighting the impossible choices that must be made in the face of overwhelming oppression. Essun's desire to create a safer world for orogene children is admirable, but it is also fraught with the danger of perpetuating the very systems she seeks to dismantle. This nuanced portrayal of a complex issue is a hallmark of Jemisin's writing, and it adds depth and richness to the narrative.

Nassun's storyline, on the other hand, is a powerful exploration of the destructive power of trauma and the ways in which it can shape and define us. Her journey is a heart-wrenching portrayal of the long-term effects of oppression, and the ways in which it can both break and remake us. Through Nassun's eyes, Jemisin shows us a world that is both beautiful and terrible, a world that is capable of great cruelty and great kindness.

One of the most striking aspects of "The Stone Sky" is its use of non-linear storytelling. Jemisin's decision to weave together multiple timelines and narratives is a bold one, and it pays off in a big way. The result is a novel that is both epic in scope and intimate in its characterization, a true masterwork of modern fantasy.

In short, "The Stone Sky" is a triumphant conclusion to the Broken Earth trilogy. It is a novel that will appeal to fans of fantasy, science fiction, and literary fiction alike, and it is a testament to Jemisin's skill as a storyteller that it will resonate with readers long after they finish reading. If you haven't already, do yourself a favor and immerse yourself in this incredible world.
