# We'll Always Have Summer (Summer, #3)

**Author:** Jenny Han  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

We'll Always Have Summer, the final installment in Jenny Han's The Summer I Turned Pretty trilogy, is a poignant and evocative exploration of love, heartbreak, and the complexities of human emotions. Belly Conklin, the protagonist, has been entwined in a complicated web of relationships with the Fisher brothers, Jeremiah and Conrad, since the beginning of the series. As the narrative unfolds, Belly is faced with the daunting task of making a final choice between the two brothers, a decision that will irrevocably alter the course of her life and the lives of those around her.

Han's writing is masterful, weaving a tale that is both deeply personal and universally relatable. Her prose is infused with a sense of nostalgia, capturing the essence of summertime and the infinite possibilities that come with it. The characters are multidimensional and richly drawn, with Belly's inner turmoil and emotional struggles taking center stage. Her love for both Jeremiah and Conrad is palpable, making it impossible not to become invested in her journey.

One of the most striking aspects of Han's writing is her ability to convey the intricacies of human emotions. Belly's relationships with the Fisher brothers are fraught with complexity, and Han skillfully navigates the nuances of love, lust, and friendship. The tension between Belly and the brothers is palpable, and the reader is left questioning who Belly will ultimately choose.

The themes of love, heartbreak, and identity are woven throughout the narrative, making We'll Always Have Summer a relatable and engaging read. Han's exploration of first love, in particular, is noteworthy, capturing the intensity and all-consuming nature of this experience. The supporting characters, including Belly's family and friends, add depth and complexity to the narrative, making the world of the novel feel fully realized.

The bonus content, including Conrad's letters to Belly and an excerpt from Burn for Burn, adds a delightful layer of depth to the narrative. These additions provide insight into Conrad's thoughts and feelings, making his character even more nuanced and relatable.

In conclusion, We'll Always Have Summer is a captivating and emotional conclusion to The Summer I Turned Pretty trilogy. Han's writing is masterful, and her exploration of love, heartbreak, and identity is both poignant and relatable. This novel is a must-read for fans of young adult fiction, particularly those who enjoy stories about first love, summer romance, and the complexities of human emotions.
