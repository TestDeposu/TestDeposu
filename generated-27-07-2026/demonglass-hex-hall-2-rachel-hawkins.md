# Demonglass (Hex Hall, #2)

**Author:** Rachel Hawkins  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

Rachel Hawkins returns with Demonglass, the snappy, high-stakes second installment in the Hex Hall trilogy, proving that the transition from a reform-school comedy to a darker, world-building fantasy is as effortless as it is entertaining. If the first book was an introductory crash course in adolescent supernatural drama, Demonglass broadens the canvas, diving headfirst into identity, systemic betrayal, and the agonizing complexities of first love. 

The narrative's beating heart remains its protagonist, Sophie Mercer. Having recently discovered she is not merely a witch, but one of only two living demons in existence, Sophie is a ticking time bomb. Hawkins leans brilliantly into the theme of internal corruption and self-loathing. Sophie’s demonic heritage isn't just a cool supernatural upgrade; it is an existential threat to everyone she cares about. This drives the novel’s central dilemma: a desperate journey to London for the "Removal," a dangerous procedure meant to strip her of her powers permanently. Hawkins captures the quintessential teenage fear of outgrowing oneself—or worse, turning into a monster—and literalizes it with sharp, demonic irony.

The author’s distinct style is what truly elevates the material. Hawkins writes with a voice that is unapologetically modern, fast-paced, and laced with dry, self-deprecating wit. Even as the stakes escalate to apocalyptic heights and the shadow of The Eye—a shadowy organization dedicated to eradicating the Prodigium—looms ever larger, Sophie’s internal monologue keeps the tone grounded. She is sarcastic, occasionally reckless, and deeply vulnerable. Hawkins treats her supernatural cast not as stoic fantasy archetypes, but as recognizable teenagers dealing with hormones, authority issues, and heartbreak, injecting genuine humor into grim circumstances.

London introduces a fresh dynamic to the series, most notably through Sophie’s new housemates, who happen to be fellow demons being raised in secret. This revelation shifts Demonglass from a standard runaway-and-hide narrative into a gripping supernatural conspiracy. The underlying mystery—who is grooming these demons and to what end?—adds a layer of paranoid tension that keeps the pages turning rapidly. 

Yet, the emotional core of the novel arguably rests in its tangled romances and shifting loyalties. The return of Archer Cross, Sophie’s former crush and current operative for the very organization hunting her, provides delicious romantic agony. Hawkins excels at crafting chemistry fueled by betrayal and lingering affection. Sophie’s denial of her feelings for Archer feels earned and messy, reflecting the confusing reality of trusting someone who has weaponized your heart. 

Demonglass is a triumphant middle-chapter that avoids the classic sophomore slump. By expanding the lore of the Prodigium world while keeping the focus tightly fixed on Sophie’s emotional maturation, Rachel Hawkins delivers a witty, suspenseful, and emotionally resonant supernatural ride. It is a story about accepting the darkness within, proving that sometimes the only way to survive your demons is to face them head-on.
