# Resurrection

**Author:** Leo Tolstoy  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

**Resurrection: A Stirring Tale of Redemption and Social Awakening**

Leo Tolstoy's final masterpiece, "Resurrection," is a profound exploration of morality, redemption, and societal injustice, wrapped in a compelling narrative that resonates with contemporary relevance. Published in 1899 after a decade of meticulous crafting, this novel is not just a story; it's a call to conscience, a testament to Tolstoy's unyielding commitment to social justice, and a powerful indictment of the hypocrisies of his time.

At the heart of "Resurrection" lies the complex relationship between Prince Dmitri Ivanovich Nekhludoff and Katusha Maslova, a woman wronged by Nekhludoff's careless actions a decade prior. Their paths cross again when Maslova, now a prostitute, is accused of a crime she did not commit. Nekhludoff, serving as a jury member, finds himself confronted with the stark reality of Maslova's life, a reality he unwittingly helped shape. This encounter sparks a spiritual awakening in Nekhludoff, as he grapples with his role in Maslova's downfall and the broader injustices of Russian society.

Tolstoy's narrative style is characteristically immersive, drawing readers into the lives of his characters with unparalleled empathy and insight. He masterfully weaves together multiple storylines, each illuminating a different facet of societal ills: the hypocrisy of State Christianity, the brutality of the penal system, and the economic exploitation of the poor. Tolstoy's prose, though dense at times, is imbued with a moral urgency that compels the reader to confront uncomfortable truths.

"Resurrection" also serves as a political manifesto, advocating for radical reforms inspired by Tolstoy's Christian anarchist beliefs and his endorsement of Henry George's "single tax" economic theory. Through Nekhludoff's journey, Tolstoy argues for a society built on compassion, equality, and justice, challenging the reader to question their own complicity in perpetuating injustice.

In an era marked by widening social inequalities and political upheaval, "Resurrection" stands as a timeless reminder of the power of individual conscience and collective action. It is a novel that demands to be read, discussed, and acted upon. Tolstoy's final masterpiece is not just a literary gem; it's a clarion call for a more just and compassionate world.
