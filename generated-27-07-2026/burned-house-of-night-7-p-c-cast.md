# Burned (House of Night, #7)

**Author:** P.C. Cast  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

**Burned: A Fiery Journey in the House of Night**

In "Burned," the seventh installment of the House of Night series, P.C. Cast continues to weave a captivating tale of vampyres, love, and self-discovery, set against the backdrop of the mysterious and alluring House of Night. Zoey Redbird, our protagonist, is no longer the naive fledgling she once was. She has grown into her powers, embracing her destiny as a vampyre and a chosen daughter of Nyx, the vampyre Goddess. Yet, with growth comes new challenges, and Zoey finds herself navigating a labyrinth of political intrigue, dangerous alliances, and heart-wrenching choices.

Cast's writing style is as engaging as ever, drawing readers in with vivid descriptions of the vampyre world and its inhabitants. The story is told primarily from Zoey's perspective, allowing readers to experience her emotional journey firsthand. Cast masterfully balances suspense, romance, and action, keeping readers on the edge of their seats until the very end.

The main theme of "Burned" is the struggle between light and dark, a theme that has been present throughout the series but is particularly prominent in this installment. Zoey, as a chosen daughter of Nyx, is expected to embody the light, to be a beacon of hope and goodness. However, she is also drawn to the dark, to the power and allure it promises. This internal struggle is reflected in the external conflicts Zoey faces, as she grapples with the consequences of her actions and the choices she makes.

Cast explores this theme through a rich cast of characters, each with their own unique struggles and motivations. Zoey's friends and allies are as well-developed as she is, each with their own arcs and growth. This makes the world of the House of Night feel lived-in and real, despite its fantastical elements.

However, "Burned" is not without its flaws. The pacing can feel uneven at times, with some scenes dragging while others race by in a whirlwind of action. Additionally, some of the romantic entanglements can feel forced, with Zoey's love life becoming increasingly complicated in a way that feels more contrived than organic.

Despite these issues, "Burned" is a compelling addition to the House of Night series. Cast's writing is engaging and immersive, drawing readers into a world that is both familiar and fantastical. The exploration of the light and dark theme is thought-provoking and resonant, making "Burned" a satisfying read for fans of the series and new readers alike.
