# The Laughing Corpse (Anita Blake, Vampire Hunter, #2)

**Author:** Laurell K. Hamilton  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

The Laughing Corpse, the second installment in Laurell K. Hamilton's iconic Anita Blake, Vampire Hunter series, is a masterful blend of dark fantasy, mystery, and romance that delves deeper into the world of vampires, zombies, and shapeshifters. First published in 1994, not 2011, this book is a testament to Hamilton's skillful storytelling and her ability to craft a narrative that is both captivating and unsettling.

At its core, The Laughing Corpse is a story about power, corruption, and the blurred lines between good and evil. Anita Blake, the protagonist, is a necromancer and vampire hunter who finds herself entangled in a complex web of supernatural politics and ancient magic. As she navigates this treacherous landscape, Anita must confront her own dark past and the demons that haunt her.

Hamilton's writing style is a key aspect of the book's allure. Her prose is visceral, evocative, and unflinching, plunging the reader into a world of gore, violence, and sensuality. The author's use of vivid imagery and descriptive language creates a richly textured atmosphere that is both immersive and unsettling. Her characters, too, are multidimensional and complex, with Anita being a particularly compelling protagonist. Her tough, no-nonsense exterior hides a vulnerable and deeply human core, making her a relatable and empathetic character.

One of the most striking aspects of The Laughing Corpse is its exploration of the supernatural as a metaphor for human nature. Hamilton's vampires, zombies, and shapeshifters are not simply monsters; they are reflections of humanity's darker aspects, embodying our deepest fears and desires. This nuanced approach to the supernatural adds depth and complexity to the narrative, elevating it beyond a simple horror or fantasy tale.

The Laughing Corpse is also notable for its frank and unapologetic portrayal of sex and violence. Hamilton's writing is unflinching in its depiction of gore and sensuality, and the book's erotic content is explicit and unapologetic. While this may not be to every reader's taste, it is a key aspect of the book's appeal, adding a layer of realism and honesty to the narrative.

In conclusion, The Laughing Corpse is a gripping and unsettling read that will appeal to fans of dark fantasy, horror, and supernatural fiction. Hamilton's masterful storytelling, vivid prose, and complex characters make this book a compelling and unforgettable experience. If you're looking for a series that will push you out of your comfort zone and challenge your perceptions, then the Anita Blake, Vampire Hunter series is an excellent choice.
