# Whitney, My Love (Westmoreland, #2)

**Author:** Judith McNaught  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

Judith McNaught occupies a towering, almost mythic status in the realm of historical romance, and Whitney, My Love stands as a brilliant testament to her enduring legacy. Originally published to immense acclaim and re-released to capture new generations of readers, this sweeping saga remains a masterclass in emotional intensity, high-stakes drama, and undeniable chemistry. McNaught does not merely write romance; she constructs elaborate emotional labyrinths where pride, misunderstanding, and consuming passion collide with devastating grace.

At the heart of the novel is the timeless theme of transformation and the turbulent pursuit of unconditional love. The story follows Whitney Stone, a spirited debutante who returns from the sophisticated salons of Paris expecting to marry the boy of her childhood dreams. Instead, she finds herself betrayed by the cold economics of the era: her bankrupt father has bartered her hand away to the formidable and unbearably arrogant Clayton Westmoreland, the Duke of Claymore. What follows is a magnificent battle of wills. Whitney refuses to yield her autonomy or her romantic ideals to a man she perceives as a cold-hearted tyrant, while Clayton, utterly captivated by his vibrant new wife, struggles to bridge the chasm between dominance and devotion. 

McNaught’s stylistic brilliance lies in her ability to make readers feel every fractured hope and sudden spark. Her prose is lush, evocative, and unapologetically dramatic. She excels at crafting characters who are deeply flawed yet intensely magnetic. Whitney is no passive Victorian flower; she is a saucy spitfire with a fierce core of resilience, making her clashes with Clayton crackle with electrifying tension. Clayton, on the other hand, embodies the quintessential alpha hero of historical romance—proud, powerful, and utterly undone by the woman who dares to defy him. Their relationship evolves through a volatile crucible of misunderstanding, forcing both characters to confront their deepest vulnerabilities.

The pacing is a relentless emotional rollercoaster, seamlessly blending biting wit, agonizing heartbreak, and breathtaking sensuality. McNaught understands that the journey toward a happily-ever-after is most satisfying when the obstacles—both internal and external—seem insurmountable. The social commentary of Regency-era England serves as a compelling backdrop, highlighting the precarious position of women and the rigid expectations of the aristocracy, which only amplifies Whitney’s fierce rebellion.

Ultimately, Whitney, My Love is more than just a historical romance; it is a grand theatricality of the human heart. It challenges the reader to consider whether love can survive the weight of pride and the sting of betrayal. McNaught’s unique magic shines on every page, delivering an unforgettable, quintessential love story that continues to set the gold standard for the genre. For anyone seeking a novel that will sweep them off their feet, evoke genuine laughter and tears, and linger in the memory long after the final page is turned, this classic remains an absolute must-read.
