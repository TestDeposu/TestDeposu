# Found (The Missing, #1)

**Author:** Margaret Peterson Haddix  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2008

In the gripping opening installment of The Missing series, "Found" by Margaret Peterson Haddix, 13-year-old Jonah's ordinary life is turned upside down when he starts receiving enigmatic letters. The messages are simple yet ominous: "You are one of the missing" and "Beware! They're coming back to get you." As Jonah delves deeper into the mystery with his new friend Chip, who's also adopted, and his sister Katherine, they become entangled in a sinister web of intrigue that involves the FBI, smuggling operations, and supernatural events.

Haddix masterfully crafts a narrative that expertly balances action, suspense, and emotional depth, drawing readers into a world where nothing is as it seems. The author's signature style, which has captivated millions of readers in her Shadow Children series, shines through in her ability to weave complex plot threads into a seamless and thrilling tale.

One of the most striking aspects of "Found" is its exploration of the theme of identity. Jonah's adoption has never been a source of concern for him, but the mysterious letters force him to confront the possibility that his life is not what he thought it was. As he navigates this new reality, he must also consider the consequences of his choices and the potential outcomes of his actions. This introspection adds a layer of depth to the story, making it more than just a fast-paced adventure.

Haddix's writing is characterized by her ability to create relatable characters and place them in extraordinary circumstances. Jonah, Chip, and Katherine are well-developed and likable protagonists who drive the story forward with their curiosity and determination. The supporting cast is equally well-crafted, with each character bringing their own motivations and secrets to the table.

The pacing of the book is relentless, with each chapter ending on a cliffhanger that propels the reader forward. Haddix's use of short chapters and snappy dialogue adds to the sense of urgency, making it impossible to put the book down.

As the story unfolds, Haddix raises questions about the nature of reality, the consequences of playing with forces beyond human control, and the importance of self-determination. The novel's climax is both heart-pumping and thought-provoking, leaving readers eager to continue the series and uncover more secrets.

In conclusion, "Found" is a captivating and suspenseful novel that will appeal to fans of science fiction, mystery, and adventure. Margaret Peterson Haddix's masterful storytelling and well-developed characters make this book a must-read for anyone looking for a thrilling page-turner.
