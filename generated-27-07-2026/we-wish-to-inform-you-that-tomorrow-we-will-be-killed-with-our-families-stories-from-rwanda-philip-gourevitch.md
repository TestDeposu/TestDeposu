# We Wish to Inform You That Tomorrow We Will Be Killed With Our Families: Stories from Rwanda

**Author:** Philip Gourevitch  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1999

We Wish to Inform You That Tomorrow We Will Be Killed with Our Families is a haunting and powerful account of the Rwandan genocide, a brutal and devastating event that left an indelible mark on human history. Philip Gourevitch's masterpiece is a triumph of narrative nonfiction, weaving together a complex tapestry of stories, interviews, and historical context to create a work that is both deeply personal and universally relatable.

At its core, the book is an exploration of the darkest aspects of human nature, and the ways in which ordinary people can become complicit in extraordinary evil. The Rwandan genocide, which resulted in the deaths of over 800,000 people in just 100 days, was a meticulously planned and executed campaign of violence, perpetrated by the Hutu majority against the Tutsi minority. Gourevitch's account of the events leading up to the genocide, and its aftermath, is both meticulous and unflinching, shedding light on the complex web of historical, cultural, and economic factors that contributed to the crisis.

One of the most striking aspects of Gourevitch's writing is his ability to balance the macro with the micro, to juxtapose the grand narrative of historical events with the intimate, personal stories of those who lived through them. Through his portraits of Rwandans from all walks of life – survivors, perpetrators, politicians, and ordinary citizens – he humanizes the statistics, and brings the reader face to face with the raw emotion, trauma, and resilience that defined the experience of those who lived through the genocide.

Gourevitch's prose is elegant, concise, and unsparing, with a keen dramatic intensity that propels the reader through the narrative. He is unflinching in his portrayal of the horrors of the genocide, but also nuanced in his exploration of the complexities and contradictions of human behavior. He raises difficult questions about the nature of humanity, the power of propaganda, and the limits of forgiveness, and offers no easy answers.

One of the most compelling aspects of the book is its exploration of the aftermath of the genocide, and the challenges of rebuilding a society in the face of such trauma. Gourevitch examines the ways in which the Rwandan government, and the international community, responded to the crisis, and the consequences of those actions. He also explores the personal and psychological toll of the genocide on survivors, and the ways in which they struggled to come to terms with their experiences.

Ultimately, We Wish to Inform You That Tomorrow We Will Be Killed with Our Families is a book about the human condition, and the ways in which we respond to extremity. It is a powerful exploration of the darkest aspects of human nature, but also a testament to the resilience and courage of those who survived the genocide, and who continue to rebuild their lives and their communities today.
