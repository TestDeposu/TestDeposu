# The Burning Girls

**Author:** C.J. Tudor  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

The Burning Girls, C.J. Tudor's latest offering, is a masterfully crafted thriller that delves into the darkest recesses of human nature, where the past and present converge in a cauldron of secrets, conspiracies, and unspeakable horrors. Set in the remote village of Chapel Croft, this unsettling tale follows Reverend Jack Brooks and his daughter, Flo, as they navigate a complex web of lies, betrayal, and murder, all of which seem to be inextricably linked to the village's dark history of martyrdom, disappearances, and unexplained events.

Tudor's writing style is akin to a slow-burning fire that gradually builds up in intensity, casting a hypnotic spell on the reader. Her prose is atmospheric, evocative, and laced with a sense of creeping dread that permeates every aspect of the narrative. As Jack and Flo delve deeper into the mysteries of Chapel Croft, they begin to unravel a tangled skein of secrets, each one more shocking and disturbing than the last. The author's masterful handling of the plot ensures that the tension remains palpable throughout, with unexpected twists and turns that keep the reader on the edge of their seat.

One of the most striking aspects of The Burning Girls is its exploration of the theme of trauma and how it can be passed down through generations, festering like an open wound that refuses to heal. The village of Chapel Croft is a character in its own right, a place where the past continues to exert a malign influence over the present, where the living are haunted by the ghosts of the dead. Tudor's portrayal of the village and its inhabitants is both haunting and thought-provoking, raising important questions about the nature of collective guilt, the power of secrets, and the devastating consequences of unchecked trauma.

The characters in The Burning Girls are multidimensional and complex, with rich inner lives that add depth and nuance to the narrative. Jack, the unconventional vicar, is a particularly compelling figure, driven by a desire to uncover the truth about the village's dark past, even as he struggles to come to terms with his own demons. His relationship with Flo, his daughter, is beautifully rendered, and their interactions are fraught with a deep emotional resonance that adds to the overall tension of the story.

As the narrative unfolds, Tudor's writing becomes increasingly atmospheric and suspenseful, conjuring up a sense of unease and disquiet that is impossible to shake off. The author's use of imagery is vivid and unsettling, with descriptions of the village and its surroundings that are both beautiful and terrifying. The burning girls of the title are a potent symbol of the horrors that have taken place in Chapel Croft, their ghostly apparitions a reminder that some wounds may never fully heal.

In conclusion, The Burning Girls is a gripping and unsettling thriller that is sure to appeal to fans of the genre. Tudor's masterful storytelling, combined with her vivid prose and well-developed characters, makes for a compelling read that is both haunting and thought-provoking. With its exploration of themes such as trauma, secrets, and the power of the past to shape the present, this book is a must-read for anyone who enjoys a good mystery.
