# Cinderella Is Dead

**Author:** Kalynn Bayron  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

In the realm of fairy tale retellings, it's rare to come across a story as bold and captivating as Kalynn Bayron's Cinderella Is Dead. This stunning debut novel shatters the glass slipper, presenting a world where the beloved classic is nothing more than a distant memory, a cautionary tale used to control and oppress. Bayron's unique voice weaves a narrative that is both a powerful feminist statement and a beautifully crafted queer romance.

At its core, Cinderella Is Dead is a story about resistance and the fight for freedom. In the kingdom of Lille, women are forced to conform to societal norms, with their only options being to attend the ball and compete for the attention of potential husbands or face dire consequences. Sophia, the protagonist, is a fierce and determined young woman who refuses to accept this fate. Her decision to flee sets off a chain of events that will change the course of her life and the lives of those around her.

Bayron's writing is evocative and immersive, transporting readers to a world that is both familiar and yet, utterly foreign. Her use of language is deliberate and precise, creating a sense of tension and urgency that propels the story forward. The characters are multidimensional and complex, with Sophia and Constance, the last known descendant of Cinderella, forming a powerful and romantic bond.

One of the most striking aspects of Cinderella Is Dead is its exploration of themes that are both timely and timeless. Bayron tackles issues of oppression, resistance, and identity with a deft hand, creating a narrative that is both a commentary on our current society and a testament to the enduring power of fairy tales. The novel is a love letter to those who have been marginalized and silenced, offering a message of hope and empowerment.

The queer romance at the heart of the story is beautifully rendered, with Sophia and Constance's relationship developing naturally and organically. Bayron's portrayal of their love is tender and authentic, avoiding the tropes and stereotypes that often plague queer representation in fiction.

In short, Cinderella Is Dead is a masterpiece of storytelling, a novel that will appeal to fans of fairy tale retellings, queer stories, and powerful female protagonists. Kalynn Bayron's unique voice and style make her a stunning new talent in the world of young adult fiction, and this debut novel is sure to leave readers eagerly anticipating her next work.
