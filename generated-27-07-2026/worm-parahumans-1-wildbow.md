# Worm (Parahumans, #1)

**Author:** Wildbow  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Unknown

**Worm: A Masterclass in Superhero Subversion**

In the sprawling metropolis of Brockton Bay, a new hero emerges, not with a flash of light or a thunderous roar, but with a whisper. "Worm," the debut novel in Wildbow's Parahumans series, is a breath of fresh air in the superhero genre, offering a gritty, realistic, and often unsettling exploration of power, morality, and identity.

Our protagonist, Taylor Hebert, is no caped crusader. She's a bullied teenage girl, overweight and socially awkward, who stumbles upon a strange entity that grants her the power to manipulate bio-energy. Instead of donning a costume, she becomes 'Skitter,' a stealthy, insect-like vigilante, using her powers to fight crime from the shadows.

Wildbow's prose is lean and efficient, painting vivid, often haunting images of a city teetering on the brink of chaos. His world-building is meticulous, with a complex web of capes (both heroes and villains), each with their own unique powers and agendas. Yet, it's his character development that truly shines. Taylor's journey is a slow burn, her growth as a hero paralleling her growth as a person, warts and all.

The main theme of "Worm" is the corrupting influence of power. As Taylor navigates the treacherous waters of Brockton Bay's underworld, she grapples with the moral ambiguity of her actions. She's not a perfect hero, and her mistakes have consequences. This isn't your typical superhero tale; it's a dark, thought-provoking exploration of what it truly means to be a hero.

Wildbow's style is reminiscent of a blend between Warren Ellis' grimy, street-level storytelling and Mark Millar's sharp, subversive take on the superhero genre. Yet, "Worm" is distinctly its own beast, a sprawling, intricate tale that will keep you on the edge of your seat until the very end.

If you're looking for a superhero story that doesn't pull its punches, that explores the darker side of power and the human condition, then "Worm" is a must-read. It's a testament to Wildbow's skill as a storyteller that he can take a genre as well-worn as the superhero tale and make it feel fresh, exciting, and utterly compelling.
