# Light (Gone, #6)

**Author:** Michael Grant  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

"Light", the sixth and final installment of Michael Grant's bestselling "Gone" series, is a heart-stopping, action-packed ride that culminates in a thrilling conclusion to the FAYZ saga. The story picks up where the previous book left off, with Sam Temple and his friends facing their greatest challenges yet as they struggle to survive in a world without adults.

One of the most striking aspects of Grant's writing style is his ability to craft a narrative that is both fast-paced and thought-provoking. The FAYZ, a small town in southern California where all the adults have mysteriously disappeared, is a microcosm of society, and Grant uses this setting to explore complex themes such as power, morality, and the human condition. As the series progresses, the stakes grow higher, and the characters are forced to confront their own darker impulses in order to survive.

In "Light", Grant masterfully weaves together the various plot threads from the previous books, bringing the series full circle in a satisfying and often unexpected way. The characters, who have grown and evolved over the course of the series, are faced with their greatest challenges yet, and the consequences of their choices are more dire than ever before.

One of the strengths of the "Gone" series is its ability to balance action and suspense with deeper themes and moral dilemmas. Grant is not afraid to tackle tough subjects, such as violence, bullying, and the exploitation of power, and he does so in a way that is both realistic and thought-provoking. The characters are complex and multi-dimensional, with flaws and strengths that make them relatable and human.

The supernatural elements that have been a hallmark of the series are also present in "Light", and Grant uses them to explore the consequences of playing with forces beyond human control. The powers that some of the characters possess are both a blessing and a curse, and Grant raises important questions about the responsibility that comes with great power.

Overall, "Light" is a fitting conclusion to the "Gone" series, and it will keep readers on the edge of their seats until the very end. Grant's writing is engaging and suspenseful, and the characters are well-developed and complex. The themes of power, morality, and survival are timely and thought-provoking, and the supernatural elements add an extra layer of tension and excitement to the story.

If you're a fan of dystopian fiction, supernatural thrillers, or just great storytelling in general, then the "Gone" series is a must-read. And if you're already invested in the series, then "Light" is a satisfying conclusion that will keep you guessing until the very end.
