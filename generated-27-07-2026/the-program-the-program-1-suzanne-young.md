# The Program (The Program, #1)

**Author:** Suzanne Young  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

"The Program" by Suzanne Young is a chilling dystopian tale that delves into the heavy themes of mental health, love, and the lengths society will go to in the name of 'cure'. Set in a world where teen suicide has reached epidemic proportions, the government's solution is The Program, a controversial treatment that erases patients' memories, including their depression, but also their identities and relationships.

Young's prose is stark and unadorned, reflecting the bleak landscape of her narrative. She masterfully builds tension, keeping readers on edge as Sloane, our protagonist, navigates the treacherous waters of her world. Sloane's struggle is palpable, her fear of being sent to The Program a constant, looming threat. Yet, it's her love for James that gives her strength, and their relationship is the heart of the story. Young explores their bond with a tender honesty, making their plight all the more poignant.

The novel raises thought-provoking questions about the nature of identity, the value of memories, and the right to choose one's own path, even if it's a painful one. It's a stark reminder of the real-world stigma surrounding mental health and the desperate need for understanding and support.

However, "The Program" isn't without its flaws. Some plot points feel contrived, and certain characters could use more depth. Yet, these are minor quibbles in an otherwise gripping read. Young's exploration of her themes is brave and unflinching, making "The Program" a standout in the dystopian genre.
