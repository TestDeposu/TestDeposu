# Every Day (Every Day, #1)

**Author:** David Levithan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

imagine waking up tomorrow not as yourself, but as someone entirely different. a different face in the mirror, a different gender, a different race, a different set of struggles, privileges, and pains. now imagine living that way every single day of your existence, never anchoring to a permanent physical form. this is the breathtaking and profoundly original premise of david levithan’s 2012 young adult masterwork, every day.

levithan introduces us to a nomadic consciousness known simply as a. having lived sixteen years hopping from one teenage body to another across the same geographic region, a has mastered the art of detachment. the rules are simple and absolute: blend in, do not disrupt the host’s life, and never, ever get attached. a is a silent observer of the human condition, passing through lives like a ghost leaving no footprints. but this delicate, carefully maintained equilibrium shatters the moment a wakes up in the body of a sullen, neglectful teenager named justin and meets justin’s girlfriend, rhiannon. 

suddenly, the mechanics of a’s solitary existence are upended. struck by an instantaneous and magnetic connection with rhiannon, a breaks every rule in the book just to spend one more day with her. what follows is not merely a romance, but a profound philosophical exploration of what it truly means to love someone beyond the superficial confines of flesh and bone. 

at its core, the main theme of every day is the transcendence of identity. levithan uses a’s fluid, ever-changing nature as a brilliant literary vehicle to strip away the societal markers we use to define ourselves—race, gender, social status, and physical appearance. by forcing the protagonist to inhabit everything from a depressed teenager to a transgender boy, a drug addict, and a deeply religious girl, levithan examines the vast spectrum of the human experience. the novel asks a piercing, existential question: if you strip away the body, what is the soul? and can love survive when the vessel changes daily? 

levithan’s authorial style is both lyrical and sharply pragmatic. writing in the first-person present tense, he captures the immediacy and disorientation of a’s daily transposition. his prose is economical yet emotionally resonant, favoring poignant observations over flowery descriptions. levithan refuses to shy away from the darker, heavier truths of adolescence—depression, addiction, body dysmorphia, and isolation are handled with grace and unfiltered honesty. yet, the narrative never sinks into despair. instead, it is imbued with a quiet, resilient hope, balancing the melancholy of a’s transience with the electric, giddy rush of first love. 

what makes every day so striking is its ability to challenge the reader's own worldview. by viewing the world through dozens of borrowed eyes, we are reminded of the hidden battles fought by the people around us. levithan crafts a narrative that is as much about empathy as it is about romance. it is a mirror reflecting our own capacity for prejudice and our deep, innate desire to be truly seen—not for our packaging, but for who we are inside.

wise, wildly unique, and fiercely compassionate, every day is a triumph of speculative contemporary fiction. levithan has engineered a modern fairy tale that is deeply relevant to our times, proving that true connection defies boundaries. it is a mesmerizing read that lingers long after the final page, urging us all to look closer, love deeper, and embrace the magnificent complexity of the human heart.
