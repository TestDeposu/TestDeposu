# I Hunt Killers (I Hunt Killers, #1)

**Author:** Barry Lyga  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

"I Hunt Killers" by Barry Lyga is a gripping, unsettling exploration of nature versus nurture, wrapped in a chilling serial killer thriller. The novel follows Jasper "Jazz" Dent, son of the infamous Billy Dent, a notorious serial killer known as the "Lobo's Nod Butcher." Jazz, now a teenager, has grown up in the shadow of his father's crimes, a shadow that looms large and dark over his every move.

Lyga's prose is taut and tense, drawing readers in with a sense of urgency that never lets up. He masterfully balances the thrilling cat-and-mouse game of the serial killer hunt with the psychological drama of Jazz's internal struggle. Jazz is a compelling protagonist, his voice authentic and relatable despite his horrifying upbringing. Lyga doesn't shy away from the darkness of Jazz's past, but neither does he wallow in it. Instead, he uses it to fuel Jazz's determination to prove that he is not his father's son.

The main theme of "I Hunt Killers" is the age-old question of whether we are products of our environment or our genes. Jazz, having grown up in a house of horrors, is constantly grappling with the fear that he might inherit his father's violent tendencies. Lyga explores this theme with nuance and depth, never resorting to easy answers or clichés. He challenges readers to consider the extent to which our pasts shape our futures, and whether it's ever possible to truly escape our origins.

The novel is also a gripping mystery in its own right, with a serial killer on the loose in Jazz's small town. Lyga expertly builds suspense, doling out clues and red herrings with a master's touch. The pacing is flawless, keeping readers on the edge of their seats until the very end.

"I Hunt Killers" is not for the faint of heart. It's a dark, intense read that doesn't pull punches. But for those who can stomach the subject matter, it's a rewarding, thought-provoking exploration of one of humanity's most enduring questions. Barry Lyga is a master of his craft, and "I Hunt Killers" is a testament to his skill.
