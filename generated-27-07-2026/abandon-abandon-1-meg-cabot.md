# Abandon (Abandon, #1)

**Author:** Meg Cabot  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

When #1 New York Times bestselling author Meg Cabot turns her pen toward the shadows, readers should expect nothing less than a spellbinding reinvention of the familiar. With Abandon, the first installment in the eponymous series, Cabot steps away from the sunlit halls of princess diaries and high school romance, descending instead into the murky, mist-shrouded corridors of Greek mythology. The result is a dark, atmospheric, and utterly compelling young adult fantasy that breathes chilling new life into the ancient legend of Persephone and Hades.

At the heart of the novel is Pierce Olivieri, a resilient teenager who learns all too well that death is not always the end. After experiencing a fatal three-minute flatline during a freak accident, Pierce returns to the land of the living, only to find herself fundamentally altered. She is no longer entirely of this world, existing in a strange, liminal space where the veil between the mortal realm and the Underworld grows paper-thin. Compounding her isolation is a relentless, brooding presence who tracks her every move. This is John Hayden—not a guardian angel, but the terrifyingly seductive ruler of the dead himself. As Pierce relocates to a gloomy, perpetually windswept island town in an attempt to outrun her past, John follows, pulling her into a magnetic and perilous dance with mortality.

Cabot’s greatest triumph in Abandon lies in her fearless exploration of its central theme: the irresistible, often destructive pull of fate versus free will, framed through the lens of grief and survival. Unlike the typical paranormal romance where a supernatural suitor swoops in as a protective savior, Cabot crafts a dynamic defined by tension, inevitability, and psychological resilience. Pierce is not a helpless damsel waiting for rescue; she is a survivor grappling with the trauma of her own death. Her near-death experience serves as a profound metaphor for alienation and teenage detachment—the feeling of walking among the living while secretly belonging to another world entirely. When John enters her life, he represents both the terror of her impending doom and the intoxicating allure of total surrender. Their relationship subverts the traditional light-versus-dark tropes by asking a more subversive question: what happens when the darkness actually understands you better than the light?

Stylistically, Abandon showcases a striking evolution in Cabot’s writing. Known for her signature wit, sparkling dialogue, and breezy contemporary pacing, the author retains her trademark conversational accessibility while successfully pivoting to a moodier, gothic tone. The prose is laced with a cold, coastal dread that mirrors Pierce’s internal state. The setting—Isla Huesos, an island steeped in superstition and surrounded by restless waters—acts almost as a character in its own right, heightening the claustrophobic atmosphere. Cabot masterfully weaves modern teenage angst with ancient mythological lore, ensuring that the transition from contemporary realism to underworld fantasy feels earned and immersive. She trades the pink-hued covers of her past hits for a shadowy aesthetic, proving her versatility across genres without losing the narrative momentum that makes her work so intensely readable.

Ultimately, Abandon is a striking and provocative opener that lays the groundwork for a high-stakes supernatural saga. It captures the bittersweet ache of first love combined with the ultimate stakes of life, death, and eternity. Meg Cabot has successfully resurrected classic mythology for a new generation of readers, crafting a chilling romance where the dead won't stay buried, and the living can't seem to stay away. For fans of dark fantasy and romance with bite, this journey to the Underworld is impossible to resist.
