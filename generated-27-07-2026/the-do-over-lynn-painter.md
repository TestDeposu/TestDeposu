# The Do-Over

**Author:** Lynn Painter  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

Groundhog Day meets teen heartbreak in Lynn Painter’s 2022 New York Times bestseller, The Do-Over, a sparkling, laugh-out-loud romantic comedy that asks a deliciously agonizing question: what would you do if you were forced to relive the absolute worst day of your life, over and over again? 

Painter, widely celebrated for her sharp wit and deeply relatable contemporary YA voice, wastes no time throwing her protagonist into the narrative deep end. Meet Emilie Hornby, a hopeless romantic whose Valentine’s Day goes from bad to catastrophically worse in a matter of hours. Not only does she discover her boyfriend cheating on her, but the entire day unfolds like a domino effect of social humiliation and personal disaster. Seeking solace, Emilie flees to her grandmother’s couch for a pint of ice cream and exhaustion-induced sleep. 

When she wakes up, the universe hits the cruelest kind of rewind. It is February 14th all over again. And again. And yet again. 

What elevates The Do-Over far above a standard time-loop gimmick is Painter’s signature authorial style: a potent blend of razor-sharp snark, emotional vulnerability, and undeniable chemistry. Emilie is not a stoic sci-fi heroine trying to hack the space-time continuum; she is a painfully authentic teenager dealing with the messy indignities of growing up. Painter captures the internal monologue of modern youth with pitch-perfect accuracy, balancing laugh-out-loud physical comedy with moments of genuine introspection. As Emilie watches her relationship implode repeatedly, the narrative explores the central theme of agency. How much of our lives do we spend passively accepting mistreatment, and at what point do we finally decide to rewrite our own script? 

Enter Nick, the enigmatic, sarcastic thorn in Emilie’s side whom she keeps running into across multiple iterations of the fateful holiday. Their dynamic provides the beating heart of the novel. Painter excels at enemies-to-lovers tension, crafting banter that crackles with electricity and emotional depth. As the days bleed together, Nick becomes the only unpredictable variable in Emilie’s inescapable loop, transforming a nightmare of stagnation into an unexpected journey of self-discovery and romance. 

Painter’s pacing is relentless, keeping pages turning with the same frantic energy as a character trying to break a cosmic curse. Yet, beneath the comedic chaos and the fun of the premise lies a touching message about resilience, letting go of toxic attachments, and learning to embrace the unpredictable beauty of a life without do-overs. 

The Do-Over is a thoroughly delightful, wildly entertaining rom-com that cements Lynn Painter as a master of contemporary YA. It is sweet, biting, wildly funny, and impossible to put down.
