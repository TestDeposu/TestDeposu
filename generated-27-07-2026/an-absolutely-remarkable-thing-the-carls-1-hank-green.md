# An Absolutely Remarkable Thing (The Carls, #1)

**Author:** Hank Green  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

**An Absolutely Remarkable Thing: A Fresh, Thought-Provoking Debut**

In his first foray into fiction, Hank Green delivers a captivating and timely novel that masterfully blends the mundane with the extraordinary. "An Absolutely Remarkable Thing" is a gripping exploration of fame, identity, and our collective response to the unknown, wrapped in a compelling narrative that keeps you guessing until the very end.

At its heart, this novel is a love letter to the internet age, with all its chaos, connectivity, and confusion. When April May stumbles upon a mysterious, otherworldly sculpture in the heart of New York City, she becomes an unwitting participant in a global phenomenon. The Carls, as they come to be known, are simultaneously fascinating and frightening, and April finds herself at the center of a media storm that challenges her understanding of fame, selfhood, and the power of viral content.

Green's prose is crisp and engaging, with a wit and humor that belies the depth of his exploration. He deftly navigates the complexities of internet culture, from the instant gratification of viral fame to the echo chambers of online rhetoric. April's journey is a fascinating study in the dehumanizing effects of celebrity, as she struggles to reconcile her newfound notoriety with her own sense of self.

Yet, "An Absolutely Remarkable Thing" is more than just a commentary on our digital age. It's a thought-provoking meditation on fear, uncertainty, and the ways we respond to the unknown. The Carls, with their enigmatic presence and inscrutable purpose, serve as a catalyst for a global conversation about fear, faith, and the human impulse to make sense of the world around us.

Green's style is reminiscent of contemporary masters like Dave Eggers and Gary Shteyngart, but with a unique voice that feels fresh and exciting. He has a knack for creating characters that feel real and relatable, even as they navigate extraordinary circumstances. April is a compelling protagonist, her struggles with identity and purpose resonating long after the final page.

In essence, "An Absolutely Remarkable Thing" is a remarkable debut. It's a novel that speaks to our times, exploring the complexities of internet culture and the human condition with equal parts humor, insight, and heart. It's a must-read for anyone who's ever wondered what happens when the extraordinary invades the everyday, and how we navigate the chaos that follows.
