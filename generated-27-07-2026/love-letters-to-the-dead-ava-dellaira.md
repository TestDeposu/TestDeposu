# Love Letters to the Dead

**Author:** Ava Dellaira  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

Love Letters to the Dead by Ava Dellaira is a poignant and emotionally charged debut novel that delves into the complexities of grief, love, and family. The story begins with a simple yet profound assignment - writing a letter to a dead person. Laurel, the protagonist, chooses Kurt Cobain, a musician her sister May adored, and soon finds herself pouring her heart out in letters to various deceased icons, from Janis Joplin to Heath Ledger.

As Laurel navigates the challenges of high school, new friendships, and first love, her letters become a therapeutic outlet for her emotions. However, it's not until she confronts the dark secrets of her past, including the abuse she suffered while her sister was supposed to be her guardian, that she can begin to heal and find her own path. Through this process, Laurel comes to accept the truth about her sister's passing and, more importantly, sees May as a multifaceted person - flawed yet lovely and amazing.

Dellaira's writing style is a character in its own right, weaving a narrative that is both haunting and beautiful. Her use of epistolary format, where the story unfolds through Laurel's letters, creates an intimate and immersive reading experience. The author's prose is evocative and lyrical, making it easy to become fully invested in Laurel's journey.

One of the most striking aspects of Love Letters to the Dead is its exploration of the complexities of sisterly love and the long-lasting impact of trauma. Dellaira handles these sensitive topics with care and nuance, never shying away from the harsh realities. The result is a narrative that is both heartbreaking and hopeful, a testament to the human spirit's capacity for resilience and healing.

The supporting characters in the novel are equally well-crafted, with each one bringing their own unique perspective to the story. Laurel's relationships with her family members, particularly her sister May, are multifaceted and authentic, adding depth to the narrative.

In conclusion, Love Letters to the Dead is a powerful and moving novel that will resonate with readers long after they finish the book. Ava Dellaira's writing is a masterclass in storytelling, and her ability to tackle tough subjects with sensitivity and empathy is a testament to her skill as a writer. If you're looking for a book that will make you laugh, cry, and reflect on the complexities of human relationships, then this is the perfect read for you.
