# Dodging Satan: My Irish/Italian, Sometimes Awesome, But Mostly Creepy, Childhood

**Author:** Kathleen Zamboni McCormick  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

In "Dodging Satan: My Irish/Italian, Sometimes Awesome, But Mostly Creepy, Childhood", Kathleen Zamboni McCormick weaves a captivating narrative that delves into the complexities of growing up in an Irish/Italian Catholic family. This poignant and humorous memoir is a striking portrayal of a young girl's struggle to navigate the intricacies of her heritage, faith, and family dynamics.

Through the voice of Bridget, the protagonist, McCormick masterfully crafts a narrative that is both deeply personal and universally relatable. Bridget's experiences in a Catholic school, where the ideals of the holy family are rigidly enforced, serve as a backdrop for her exploration of identity, morality, and the search for her own form of grace. As she navigates the patriarchal structures of her Irish, Italian, and Catholic upbringing, Bridget's observations are marked by a keen intelligence, wit, and a deep sense of empathy.

One of the most striking aspects of McCormick's writing style is her ability to balance humor and grit. Her prose is infused with a wicked sense of humor, which serves as a perfect counterpoint to the more somber moments of the narrative. This delicate balance allows the reader to engage with the story on a deeper level, as the humor and pathos work in tandem to create a rich and nuanced portrait of Bridget's childhood.

McCormick's writing is also notable for its vivid characterization and dialogue. The cast of characters that populate Bridget's world are meticulously drawn, from her lively and arguing relatives to the stern and unyielding nuns who oversee her education. The dialogue is sharp and authentic, capturing the rhythms and cadences of family conversations and the stilted formalities of Catholic school life.

At its core, "Dodging Satan" is a memoir about the search for identity and the struggle to find one's place within the complexities of family, faith, and culture. Bridget's story is a powerful exploration of the tensions that arise when tradition and modernity collide, and the ways in which we must navigate these tensions in order to forge our own paths.

Throughout the narrative, McCormick raises important questions about the nature of faith, morality, and identity. How do we reconcile our desire for autonomy and self-expression with the demands of family and tradition? How do we navigate the complexities of our own heritage, particularly when it is marked by conflict and contradiction? These questions are at the heart of "Dodging Satan", and McCormick's thoughtful and engaging exploration of them makes for a truly compelling read.

In short, "Dodging Satan" is a joy to read – a hilarious and heartbreaking memoir that is both a love letter to the author's heritage and a powerful exploration of the complexities of identity and faith. With its vivid characterization, sharp dialogue, and nuanced exploration of the human experience, this memoir is a must-read for anyone interested in stories of identity, family, and the search for one's own form of grace.
