# American Marxism

**Author:** Mark R. Levin  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

In "American Marxism," Mark R. Levin delivers a scathing critique of the pervasive Marxist ideology that has infiltrated American society and culture. Levin, a seven-time #1 New York Times bestselling author, Fox News star, and radio host, builds upon his previous work, "Liberty and Tyranny," to expose the dangers of progressivism and its threat to Constitution-based values.

Levin's writing style is characteristic of his trenchant analysis, as he digs deep into the psychology and tactics of the movements that are driving this revolution. He expertly dissects the deceptive labels used to cloak Marxist ideology, such as "progressivism," "democratic socialism," and "social activism." Levin's critique is not limited to the intellectual and academic circles but also extends to the widespread brainwashing of students, the anti-American purposes of Critical Race Theory, and the Green New Deal.

One of the most striking aspects of Levin's work is his ability to expose the institutions, intellectuals, scholars, and activists who are leading this revolution. He names names and provides a detailed analysis of their tactics and strategies. Levin's critique is not limited to the Democratic Party or the Biden presidency but also extends to the corporations, Hollywood, and the press, which he argues are complicit in promoting Marxist ideology.

Levin's central argument is that the counter-revolution to the American Revolution is in full force, and it can no longer be dismissed or ignored. He argues that this revolution is devouring our society and culture, swirling around our everyday lives, and ubiquitous in our politics, schools, media, and entertainment. Levin's call to action is clear: he seeks to rally the American people to defend their liberty.

Throughout the book, Levin's passion and conviction are evident. His writing is engaging, and his arguments are well-reasoned. Levin's use of historical and philosophical context adds depth to his analysis, making the book a compelling read for those interested in politics, history, and philosophy.

However, some readers may find Levin's critique too broad, and his arguments too simplistic. Levin's tendency to label anyone who disagrees with him as a Marxist or a progressive may alienate some readers. Additionally, Levin's solutions to the problems he identifies may seem too vague or unrealistic to some.

Despite these criticisms, "American Marxism" is a thought-provoking book that challenges readers to think critically about the state of American society and culture. Levin's critique is a wake-up call to those who are concerned about the erosion of Constitution-based values and the rise of Marxist ideology. This book is a must-read for anyone interested in politics, history, and philosophy.

In conclusion, "American Marxism" is a powerful critique of the pervasive Marxist ideology in American society and culture. Levin's trenchant analysis and passionate writing make the book a compelling read. While some readers may find Levin's critique too broad or his solutions too vague, the book is a thought-provoking challenge to think critically about the state of American society and culture.
