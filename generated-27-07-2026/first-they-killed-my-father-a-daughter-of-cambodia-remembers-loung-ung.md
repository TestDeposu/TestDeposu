# First They Killed My Father: A Daughter of Cambodia Remembers

**Author:** Loung Ung  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

**First They Killed My Father: A Daughter of Cambodia Remembers**

Loung Ung's memoir, "First They Killed My Father," is a gut-wrenching, yet profoundly moving account of her childhood during the Cambodian genocide under the Khmer Rouge regime. The author's unique perspective as a five-year-old when the nightmare began lends an innocence and raw honesty to her narrative that is both heartbreaking and compelling.

Ung's prose is stark and unadorned, much like the stark reality she describes. She doesn't sensationalize or dwell on the horrors she endured, but simply states them, allowing the brutal facts to speak for themselves. This understated approach makes the atrocities all the more chilling and unforgettable. Ung's story is not just about survival; it's about the resilience of the human spirit and the enduring power of love, even in the face of unspeakable evil.

The author's ability to convey the complexity of her emotions and experiences is remarkable. She was just a child when she was forced to become a soldier, yet she understood the horror of her situation. Her descriptions of her feelings of fear, confusion, and anger are poignant and relatable, making her story all the more powerful.

"First They Killed My Father" is not an easy read. It's a brutal, unflinching look at the darkest side of humanity. But it's also a testament to the indomitable human spirit. Ung's story is a must-read, not just for its historical significance, but for its universal message of hope and resilience.
