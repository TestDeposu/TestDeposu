# The Daughters' War (Blacktongue, #0)

**Author:** Christopher Buehlman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2024

**A Feast of Darkness and Resilience: "The Daughters' War" by Christopher Buehlman**

In the realm of fantasy literature, Christopher Buehlman has carved a niche for himself with his unique blend of dark humor, intricate world-building, and compelling narratives. His latest offering, "The Daughters' War," is an enticing prequel to "The Blacktongue Thief," set in a war-torn world teeming with goblin hordes and giant war corvids. This is not a tale for the faint-hearted, but rather a gripping, unapologetic exploration of war, resilience, and the bonds that bind us.

Buehlman's prose is as sharp as a goblin's blade, slicing through the mundane to reveal the brutal reality of war. He spares no detail in painting the grim landscape of a world ravaged by conflict, where cities lie in ruins, fields are scorched, and hope seems a distant memory. Yet, amidst this desolation, Buehlman finds beauty, not in the traditional sense, but in the resilience of the human spirit and the unbreakable bonds of family.

At the heart of "The Daughters' War" is Galva, a young woman who defies her family's wishes to join the Raven Knights, an untested unit marching towards a goblin-infested city. Galva is no demure damsel; she is a warrior, fierce and determined, yet also vulnerable and relatable. Through her eyes, we witness the horrors of war, the camaraderie forged in the heat of battle, and the complex dynamics of family and loyalty.

Buehlman's world is richly textured, filled with intriguing creatures, dark magics, and a history that feels as real and complex as our own. The goblins are not mere monsters but sentient beings with their own motivations and strategies, making them formidable foes. The war corvids, too, are more than just beasts of burden; they are creatures of dark magic, their intelligence and unpredictability adding a layer of tension to the narrative.

Buehlman's style is reminiscent of Joe Abercrombie's grimdark fantasy, with its unflinching portrayal of war and its consequences. Yet, there's a unique spark in Buehlman's writing, a dark humor that leavens the grimness, making the reading experience all the more engaging. He has a knack for creating memorable characters, each with their own voice and arc, making the story feel more like a tapestry woven with many threads than a single, linear narrative.

"The Daughters' War" is not just a prequel, but a standalone story that enriches the world of "The Blacktongue Thief." It's a tale of daughters taking up arms, of families torn apart and bound together by war, of hope persevering in the face of overwhelming odds. It's a grim, gritty, and gripping read that will appeal to fans of dark fantasy, war stories, and compelling character-driven narratives.
