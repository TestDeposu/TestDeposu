# A Brief History of Intelligence: Evolution, AI, and the Five Breakthroughs That Made Our Brains

**Author:** Max Solomon Bennett  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

Why can an artificial intelligence system effortlessly defeat a chess grandmaster, yet stand utterly paralyzed by the simple, mundane task of loading a dishwasher? This paradox lies at the beating heart of Max Solomon Bennett’s riveting debut, A Brief History of Intelligence: Evolution, AI, and the Five Breakthroughs That Made Our Brains. 

In a literary and scientific landscape crowded with speculative doom-mongering about machine supremacy and dry, academic neuroscience, Bennett’s 2023 book carves out a refreshing, highly original niche. Part evolutionary safari, part computer science roadmap, Bennett tackles the grand enigma of cognition by looking backward. He posits a compelling and disruptive thesis: to build the artificial intelligence of tomorrow, we must first decode the biological trials and errors of yesterday.

Bennett, an AI entrepreneur turned evolutionary chronicler, structures his narrative around a sweeping biological timeline. Instead of treating the human brain as a monolith, he breaks down its development into five monumental evolutionary breakthroughs. These are not merely anatomical upgrades, but revolutionary leaps in problem-solving architecture. From the first nervous systems navigating a dangerous primordial world to the sophisticated internal simulations that allow humans to plan for the future, Bennett traces a billion-year arc of adaptation, calamity, and brilliant innovation. 

What elevates this book from a standard pop-science summary into a must-read critique is Bennett’s stylistic dexterity. He possesses a rare gift for translation, taking dense, esoteric concepts from neurology and machine learning and rendering them with cinematic clarity. The prose moves with the page-turning momentum of a thriller, a quality enthusiastically vouched for by heavyweights like Nobel laureate Daniel Kahneman and bestselling author Angela Duckworth. Bennett avoids the trap of academic obscurantism, opting instead for vivid analogies and narrative pacing that make complex neural algorithms feel as intuitive as a well-told story.

Crucially, the book avoids getting bogged down in pure history. Every evolutionary milestone Bennett uncovers serves as a direct mirror to contemporary debates in artificial intelligence. He astutely diagnoses the current limitations of modern AI—its narrow specialization, its lack of genuine common sense, and its staggering energy inefficiency—by demonstrating how today’s algorithms skip the very foundational steps that biological evolution required millions of years to hardcode. Until AI architecture mirrors the complete evolutionary journey of the animal and human brain, Bennett argues, true human-like general intelligence will remain perpetually out of reach.

A Brief History of Intelligence is ultimately a bridge builder. It connects the disparate worlds of biology and computer science, offering a pragmatic lens through which we can view both our own minds and the machines we are desperately trying to create. It is a vital, paradigm-shifting read for anyone captivated by the trajectory of technology and the ancient, magnificent machinery sitting right between our ears.
