# The Demon

**Author:** Hubert Selby Jr.  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

**Title: The Demon: A Raw Plunge into Male Vanity**

Hubert Selby Jr., the literary provocateur who brought us "Last Exit to Brooklyn" and "Requiem for a Dream," returns with "The Demon," a searing exploration of male vanity, sexual obsession, and the dangerous consequences of suppressed desire. Published posthumously in 2011, this novel is a stark, unflinching journey into the psyche of Harry White, a blue-collar Brooklyn boy who's clawed his way up the social ladder, only to find himself ensnared in a web of his own making.

At its core, "The Demon" is a cautionary tale about the perils of unchecked libido and the destructive power of male vanity. Harry White, with his charm, intelligence, and good looks, is a serial seducer, taking particular delight in bedding married women. But when he decides to rein in his promiscuity, he discovers that his libido is a hydra, and suppressing one urge only unleashes another, darker one.

Selby's prose is as raw and uncompromising as ever, a visceral, often brutal exploration of the human psyche. He spares no detail in his portrayal of Harry's descent into madness, painting a stark, unflinching portrait of a man unraveling. The narrative is punctuated by sudden shifts in perspective, jarring the reader like a punch to the gut, reflecting Harry's increasingly fragmented mental state.

Yet, despite its grim subject matter, "The Demon" is not without its moments of dark humor. Selby's dialogue is sharp and snappy, his characters' banter often serving as a grim counterpoint to the horror unfolding around them. This is a novel that will make you squirm, that will challenge you, that will leave you questioning your own desires and the lengths you might go to satisfy them.

In many ways, "The Demon" feels like a culmination of Selby's career, a final exploration of the themes that have defined his work: the destructive power of addiction, the corrupting influence of desire, and the human capacity for self-destruction. It's a challenging, often uncomfortable read, but it's also a compelling one, a literary gut-punch that will leave you reeling.
