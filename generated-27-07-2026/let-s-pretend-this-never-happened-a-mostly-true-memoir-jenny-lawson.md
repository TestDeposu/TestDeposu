# Let's Pretend This Never Happened: A Mostly True Memoir

**Author:** Jenny Lawson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

In "Let's Pretend This Never Happened: A Mostly True Memoir," Jenny Lawson weaves a captivating narrative that is at once poignant, disturbing, and wonderfully humorous. With unflinching candor, Lawson recounts her unconventional childhood, marked by a fantastically unbalanced father and a morbidly eccentric upbringing that defied all conventions of normalcy. This memoir is a testament to the author's remarkable ability to find humor in the darkest corners of her life, transforming the strange shame-spiral that threatened to consume her into a rich tapestry of self-discovery and growth.

Lawson's writing style is irreverent, audacious, and refreshingly honest. She tackles topics that would make most authors blanch, from her father's outlandish antics to her own struggles with mental health, with a wit and vulnerability that is nothing short of remarkable. Her use of language is akin to a masterful tightrope walker, balancing the tragic and the hilarious with ease, never once faltering into sentimentality or mawkishness.

Throughout the book, Lawson's long-suffering husband and sweet daughter emerge as beacons of love and support, helping her navigate the treacherous waters of her past and uncover the surprising truth that it is our most human moments – the ones we'd rather pretend never happened – that ultimately define us. This is a memoir that will resonate deeply with anyone who has ever felt like an intellectual misfit, who has ever thought the things that Lawson dares to say out loud.

One of the most striking aspects of "Let's Pretend This Never Happened" is its ability to balance the dark and the light. Lawson confronts the demons of her childhood with unflinching courage, yet she also celebrates the beauty of her relationships and the redemptive power of love. This is a book that will make you laugh out loud, nod your head in recognition, and perhaps even shed a tear or two.

In short, "Let's Pretend This Never Happened" is a tour-de-force of memoir writing, a hilarious and poignant exploration of the human condition that will leave you breathless and yearning for more. If you're a fan of authors like David Sedaris or Augusten Burroughs, you'll devour this book in one sitting. And if you're simply looking for a story that will make you feel less alone in your own eccentricities, then this is the memoir for you.
