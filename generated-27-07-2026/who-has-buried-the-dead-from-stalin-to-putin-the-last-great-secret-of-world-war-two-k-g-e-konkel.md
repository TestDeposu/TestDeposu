# Who Has Buried the Dead?: From Stalin to Putin … The last great secret of World War Two

**Author:** K.G.E. Konkel  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Unknown

**Who Has Buried the Dead?: Unveiling the Last Great Secret of World War Two**

In the labyrinthine corridors of history, some secrets remain stubbornly hidden, even decades after the events they shroud. K.G.E. Konkel, in his meticulously researched and gripping work, "Who Has Buried the Dead?", drags one such secret into the light, casting a chilling glow on the aftermath of World War Two.

Konkel's narrative is a masterclass in historical detective work, unraveling the enigma of the Katyn Massacre, a crime that has long haunted the shadows of the Soviet Union's past. The author deftly weaves together a tapestry of testimonies, declassified documents, and eyewitness accounts, painting a vivid picture of a tragedy that has been obscured by the fog of war and the iron curtain of Soviet propaganda.

The book's strength lies in Konkel's ability to balance the grim details of the massacre with a broader perspective on the geopolitical landscape of the time. He masterfully navigates the complex web of alliances and betrayals that characterized the war's endgame, illuminating how the Katyn Massacre became a pawn in the great power struggle between the United States and the Soviet Union.

Konkel's style is engaging and accessible, making complex historical events and political machinations understandable to the lay reader. He wears his extensive research lightly, using it to propel the narrative forward rather than bogging it down in academic minutiae. The author's prose is clear and concise, yet rich in detail, drawing the reader in and keeping them on the edge of their seat until the very end.

"Who Has Buried the Dead?" is not just a book about a long-buried secret; it's a testament to the power of truth and the importance of historical reckoning. Konkel's work serves as a stark reminder that the past, no matter how dark or inconvenient, must be confronted if we are to understand the present and shape the future.
