# The Perfect Child (Hannah Bauer, #1)

**Author:** Lucinda Berry  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Unknown

**Title: The Perfect Child - A Chilling Exploration of Motherhood and Obsession**

In the vein of Gillian Flynn and Paula Hawkins, Lucinda Berry's debut novel, "The Perfect Child," is a gripping psychological thriller that delves into the dark heart of motherhood, obsession, and the thin line between love and madness.

The story unfolds through the eyes of Hannah Bauer, a woman grappling with postpartum depression and a desperate longing for a child. When she finally gives birth to little Elodie, Hannah's world should be complete, but instead, it spirals into a terrifying descent into paranoia and delusion. As Elodie grows, so does Hannah's obsession with creating the 'perfect' child, leading her to make increasingly disturbing decisions in the name of love.

Berry's prose is taut and unyielding, drawing the reader into Hannah's fractured psyche with unsettling intimacy. She masterfully builds tension, each chapter a step further into the abyss, leaving readers questioning what they would do in Hannah's shoes. The narrative is punctuated by Hannah's journal entries, offering a chilling first-person perspective on her descent into obsession.

At its core, "The Perfect Child" is a cautionary tale about the dangers of perfectionism and the corrosive power of unchecked love. It's a book that will make you question your own beliefs about motherhood and the lengths a parent might go to protect their child.

Berry's style is confident and assured, her characters complex and flawed. She expertly weaves a tale that keeps you guessing until the very end, leaving you breathless and questioning your own perceptions of reality.
