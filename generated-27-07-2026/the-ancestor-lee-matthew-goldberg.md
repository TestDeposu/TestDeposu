# The Ancestor

**Author:** Lee Matthew Goldberg  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

**The Ancestor: A Tale of Boundaries, Stories, and Self-Discovery**

In "The Ancestor," Lee Matthew Goldberg weaves a captivating tale that explores the intricate dance of human connection, the power of storytelling, and the courage it takes to confront our past. The novel is a profound exploration of boundaries—those we erect to protect ourselves, and those we cross to connect with others.

At the heart of the story are two seemingly disparate characters: Raymona Washington Goldberg, an African American woman living in self-imposed isolation in her Upper East Side apartment, and Matthew Pierson, a gay man lost in a world of romantic fantasies, residing in Minneapolis but spiritually adrift. Their relationship, which unfolds primarily through phone conversations, is the lifeblood of the novel. Goldberg masterfully uses these dialogues to reveal the complexities of his characters, their vulnerabilities, and their shared hunger for connection.

Raymona, adopted as an infant during the 1960s Freedom Rides, has built a fortress around herself, terrified of the stories that lurk in the world outside. Matthew, meanwhile, escapes into romantic narratives, creating elaborate fantasies that allow him to avoid confronting his own reality. Their dynamic is a delicate balance of push and pull, each drawing the other out of their respective shells while also retreating into their own safe spaces.

The narrative takes a dramatic turn when Matthew becomes entangled in a real-life adventure in the Boundary Waters of Minnesota. Simultaneously, Raymona, deprived of Matthew's constant storytelling, is forced to venture out into the world, embarking on her own epic journey through the subway system. As their physical boundaries shift, so too do their emotional ones, leading both characters to confront the carefully guarded stories of their pasts.

Goldberg's prose is elegant and evocative, painting vivid portraits of his characters and the worlds they inhabit. He has a keen eye for detail, using the mundane to reveal the profound. The novel is also a love letter to storytelling, exploring its power to both heal and harm, to connect us and drive us apart.

"The Ancestor" is a novel about the stories we tell ourselves and the ones we inherit. It's about the courage it takes to confront our pasts and the power of connection to transform our lives. Goldberg's exploration of these themes is profound and moving, making "The Ancestor" a standout novel that will resonate with readers long after they've turned the final page.
