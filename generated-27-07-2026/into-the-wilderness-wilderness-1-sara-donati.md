# Into the Wilderness (Wilderness, #1)

**Author:** Sara Donati  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1998

Into the Wilderness, Sara Donati's sweeping epic, is a masterful blend of historical fiction, romance, and adventure that whisks readers away to the untamed landscapes of 18th-century America. Set in December 1792, the novel follows Elizabeth Middleton, a strong-willed and determined Englishwoman, as she leaves her comfortable estate to join her family in a remote New York mountain village.

Donati's vivid descriptions of the rugged wilderness and its inhabitants are nothing short of breathtaking. Her prose is lush and evocative, conjuring the sights, sounds, and scents of a bygone era with remarkable precision. As Elizabeth navigates this unforgiving yet beautiful landscape, she must confront the harsh realities of frontier life, including the tensions between Native American tribes, slave owners, and European settlers.

At the heart of the novel is the complex and passionate relationship between Elizabeth and Nathaniel Bonner, a white man who has adopted the ways of the Mohawk people. Their love story is expertly woven into the fabric of the narrative, adding depth and emotional resonance to the plot. Through their interactions, Donati explores themes of identity, community, and the struggle for survival in a rapidly changing world.

One of the novel's greatest strengths is its ability to balance historical accuracy with engaging storytelling. Donati's meticulous research is evident throughout, from the intricate details of Native American culture to the broader social and economic currents of the time. Yet, she never allows historical fact to overwhelm the narrative, instead using it to inform and enrich the characters' experiences.

The author's style is characterized by a keen sense of observation, a deep empathy for her characters, and a willingness to tackle complex moral issues. Her writing is both elegant and unflinching, capable of conveying the beauty and brutality of the wilderness in equal measure.

As Diana Gabaldon notes, Into the Wilderness is "the sort of book you live in, rather than read." It's a testament to Donati's skill that she can transport readers to another time and place, immersing them in the sights, sounds, and emotions of a bygone era. This is a novel that will appeal to fans of historical fiction, romance, and adventure alike, offering a rich and rewarding reading experience that lingers long after the final page is turned.
