# Procession of the Dead (The City Trilogy, #1)

**Author:** Darren Shan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

Step off a train and into a waking nightmare where your past is completely erased, yet your ambition burns brighter than ever. This is the disorienting, neon-soaked welcome Darren Shan extends to readers in Procession of the Dead, the first installment of The City Trilogy. Published in 2010, this novel marks a sharp, brilliant pivot for an author widely celebrated for his YA horror staples. Here, Shan sheds his younger-skewing skin to deliver an edgy, unapologetically adult noir that pulses with danger, sin, and existential dread.

At the heart of the novel is Capac Raimi, a young man who arrives in the titular City with a sharp wit, supreme confidence, and a baffling affliction: total amnesia. He remembers stepping onto the platform, but the years before are a terrifying void. Instead of seeking safety, Capac plunges headfirst into the underworld of the City, a place where exotic threats lurk in every shadow. Shan populates this labyrinth with a menagerie of unforgettable, larger-than-life figures. There is a legendary assassin whose face is coiled with tattooed snakes and who moves with the fluid menace of smoke; blind Incan priests who walk the streets like ghosts, seemingly unseen by the frantic populace; and a ruthless kingpin who orchestrates his empire while playing with puppets. 

Yet, all roads in this sprawling metropolis ultimately lead to one man: The Cardinal. The narrative’s masterstroke lies in the symbiotic relationship between this shadowy ruler and the urban sprawl itself. The Cardinal is the City, and the City is The Cardinal—they are fused at the soul. When Capac inevitably crosses paths with this absolute authority, his trajectory shifts permanently. As Capac uncovers the extent of the Cardinal's invisible strings pulling at his own life, he is forced to confront the dangerous limits of his soaring ambition. 

Thematic exploration is where Procession of the Dead truly elevates itself above standard genre fare. The central theme hinges on identity versus destiny. How does a man forge a path to the top when he does not even know who he was at the bottom? Capac’s amnesia is not merely a convenient plot device; it is a profound philosophical hurdle. In a world defined by deceit, murder, and shifting loyalties, self-trust becomes the ultimate luxury. Capac must piece together his capabilities in real-time, risking total destruction with every wrong turn. It is a gripping exploration of post-apocalyptic fatalism mixed with classic gangster ambition, creating an atmosphere that feels both timeless and relentlessly modern.

Stylistically, Shan proves his versatility as a master of suspense. Known for his breathless pacing, he translates that kinetic energy seamlessly into the realm of adult noir. The prose is stylish, sharp, and dripping with atmosphere. The City feels oppressive and intoxicating all at once, rendered in vivid sensory details that make the neon glare and the dark alleys feel dangerously real. Shan weaves unexpected plot twists with surgical precision, ensuring that the reader remains just as disoriented and hungry for answers as protagonist Capac Raimi. 

Procession of the Dead is a riveting, stylish triumph that challenges the reader to question how much of our past actually dictates our future. With its unforgettable characters, labyrinthine plot, and relentless pacing, Shan’s foray into adult fiction is nothing short of hypnotic. It is a haunting, stellar beginning to a trilogy that leaves you desperate to descend back into the perilous streets of the City long after the final page has turned.
