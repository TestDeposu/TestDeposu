# Powers That Be (Petaybee, #1)

**Author:** Anne McCaffrey  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1993

When science fiction royalty join forces, the result is rarely anything short of mesmerizing. Co-authored by Anne McCaffrey and Elizabeth Scarborough, Powers That Be (1993) marks the beginning of the Petaybee series, a captivating journey into a world where corporate greed clashes with the untamed, sentient majesty of nature. Blending ecological themes with a classic David-versus-Goliath narrative, this novel showcases McCaffrey’s legendary gift for world-building and character empathy, offering a story that is as intellectually stimulating as it is emotionally resonant.

At the heart of Powers That Be lies a profound exploration of human ecology and corporate exploitation. The icy, unforgiving planet of Petaybee is viewed by the monolithic Intergal Company merely as a ledger of untapped resources, a wasteland ripe for mineral extraction. Yet, as the narrative unfolds, Petaybee reveals itself to be much more than a collection of ice and ore; it is a living, breathing entity. The central theme examines the symbiotic relationship between humanity and the environment. McCaffrey and Scarborough posit that nature is not something to be conquered, subdued, or commodified, but a conscious force demanding respect. The planet’s subtle, protective resistance against the Company serves as a powerful metaphor for environmental stewardship, reminding readers of the delicate balance of ecosystems and the disastrous consequences of ignoring the voice of the earth.

This thematic depth is anchored by the novel's protagonist, Yanaba Maddock, a disabled combat veteran whose personal arc mirrors the healing of the planet itself. Sent to Petaybee as a corporate spy, Yana’s initial frailty is weaponized by her employers, who assume her vulnerability will disarm the locals. Instead, the harsh yet nurturing environment of the ice world acts as a catalyst for her physical and emotional restoration. Yana’s transition from a broken instrument of a cold corporation to a fierce protector of her new home forms the emotional core of the book. Through Yana, the authors explore the themes of identity, disability, and rebirth, proving that true strength comes not from military might or corporate dominance, but from belonging and a willingness to protect what is sacred.

Stylistically, Powers That Be bears the unmistakable hallmarks of Anne McCaffrey’s signature storytelling. Much like her beloved Pern series, this novel excels at creating a deeply immersive community. The authors craft a rich tapestry of hardy colonists, enigmatic locals, and fascinating, genetically altered fauna that populate the frozen landscape. The pacing is deliberate, allowing readers to acclimate to the biting cold and the slow, rhythmic cadence of Arctic life before ramping up the tension of the corporate conspiracy. 

Furthermore, the prose is characterized by warmth and accessibility. Even in the face of corporate corruption and the stark danger of a hostile climate, McCaffrey and Scarborough infuse the narrative with a sense of wonder and human connection. The dialogue is grounded, and the character interactions feel genuine, making the transition of the inhabitants from guarded strangers to a tightly knit family entirely believable. The authors eschew cynical grit in favor of a hopeful, humanist sci-fi tradition, where empathy is the ultimate survival tool.

Powers That Be is a triumph of speculative fiction that transcends its genre boundaries. It marries the high-stakes intrigue of corporate espionage with the quiet, transformative magic of ecological awakening. For fans of intelligent world-building, resilient protagonists, and stories that champion the earth, this novel is an absolute must-read. McCaffrey and Scarborough have crafted a frozen frontier that warms the heart, leaving readers eager to plunge deeper into the mysteries of Petaybee.
