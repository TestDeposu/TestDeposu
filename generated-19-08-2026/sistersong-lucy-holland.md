# Sistersong

**Author:** Lucy Holland  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

To step into Lucy Holland’s 2021 debut, Sistersong, is to tumble headfirst into a pre-Arthurian Britain where history and myth bleed together, breathing new life into the ancient ballad "The Twa Sisters." Set against the rugged backdrop of Dumnonia as the shadow of Saxon invasion looms, Holland crafts a mesmerizing tapestry of old magic, family fracture, and the brutal cost of survival. This is not merely a retelling; it is an atmospheric reclamation, a haunting exploration of identity and destiny that resonates with the primal pull of the earth itself.

At the beating heart of the novel are King Cador’s three children, each struggling against the rigid confines of expectation and inheritance. Riva, the eldest, walks in the shadow of physical and emotional scars, bearing a gift for healing that cannot mend her own spirit. Keyne, fiercely battling to be recognized as the king’s son, offers a poignant and tender narrative of transgender identity woven seamlessly into ancient folklore, fighting for a truth that the rigid laws of his people refuse to see. Meanwhile, the youngest, Sinne, burns with a restless desire for a world beyond the stone walls of the hold. Through these distinct, beating hearts, Holland explores her primary theme: the friction between personal authenticity and the crushing weight of duty to family and crown. When the enigmatic magician Myrdhin and the mysterious warrior Tristan arrive on the heels of falling ash, these three siblings are hurled onto a collision course of betrayal, ambition, and inescapable tragedy.

Holland’s greatest triumph lies in her prose and style, which are as lyrical and evocative as the wild magic inhabiting Dumnonia’s whispering winds and deep-rooted trees. Her writing possesses a tactile, visceral quality; readers can practically smell the woodsmoke, taste the salt of the sea, and feel the damp earth beneath their fingernails. She treats mythology not as a distant museum exhibit, but as a living, breathing force that demands sacrifice. The pacing moves with the deliberate, hypnotic cadence of a folk song, slowly building tension until the emotional crescendos shatter the quiet. 

Fans of Madeline Miller, Jennifer Saint, and historical fantasy rich in folklore will find themselves utterly spellbound. Sistersong is a triumph of dark, poetic storytelling—magical, beautiful, and ultimately heartbreaking. Holland has proven that the oldest stories are the ones that still hold the power to change us, provided they are told by the right voice.
