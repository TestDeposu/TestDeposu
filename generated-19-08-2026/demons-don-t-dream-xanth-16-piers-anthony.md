# Demons Don't Dream (Xanth #16)

**Author:** Piers Anthony  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

Step into the magical, pun-drenched landscape of Xanth once again with Piers Anthony’s sixteenth installment, Demons Don't Dream. Published in 2019, this vibrant addition to the legendary fantasy series proves that Anthony’s imagination remains as playfully relentless as ever. Known for crafting worlds where geography is defined by wordplay and flora and fauna are living punchlines, Anthony takes a delightfully meta approach this time around, blending the ancient magic of Xanth with the dawn of digital gaming.

At its core, the novel explores the blurring lines between reality and virtual illusion. The main narrative engine is a magical computer game called Companions of Xanth, which pulls two unsuspecting teenagers, Dug and Kim, straight from the mundane real world into the heart of a high-stakes fantasy realm. Dug, initially unimpressed by technology, finds himself utterly captivated—particularly by his guide, Nada Naga, a beguiling princess-serpent who must juggle the monumental task of keeping Dug focused on the quest while concealing her human form. Meanwhile, Kim pairs up with the spirited Jenny Elf. While Kim treats the journey as a mere game, Jenny faces the uphill battle of convincing her skeptical player that the perils and wonders of Xanth are chillingly, brilliantly real. 

True to the author's signature style, Demons Don't Dream is an absolute masterclass in linguistic whimsy and comedic world-building. Anthony’s prose relies heavily on his trademark penchant for groan-worthy, brilliant puns. Readers will find themselves navigating a terrain populated by bizarrely named creatures, treacherous voids, menacing loan sharks, and the ultimate technological baddie, the "Com Pewter." This unique brand of humor has become the beating heart of the Xanth series, creating an infectious, lighthearted atmosphere that refuses to take itself too seriously, even when the fate of an entire magical realm hangs in the balance.

Yet beneath the avalanche of puns and fantastical set-pieces lies a clever commentary on escapism and the power of interactive media. Anthony cleverly uses the framing device of a video game to explore player motivation, cooperation, and the unexpected emotional bonds that form between humans and their magical avatars. The dynamic between the players and their companions introduces an extra layer of tension, as saboteurs lurk in the shadows and trust becomes the ultimate currency.

For longtime fans of the series, Demons Don't Dream offers a comforting, nostalgic return to a well-worn sandbox packed with familiar comedic beats and imaginative flair. For newcomers, it serves as a delightfully eccentric entry point into one of fantasy literature's longest-running and most uniquely stylized universes. Anthony successfully proves that even after sixteen books, the magic of Xanth is far from depleted—it just needed an upgrade.
