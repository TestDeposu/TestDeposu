# Robot Adept (Apprentice Adept, #5)

**Author:** Piers Anthony  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1982

Stepping back into the dual-natured universe of Piers Anthony is akin to plugging directly into a high-octane mainframe while simultaneously waving a wizard’s wand. Robot Adept, the fifth installment in the celebrated Apprentice Adept series, takes everything that made Split Infinity a genre-blending triumph and doubles down on the chaos, ingenuity, and sheer narrative audacity. Published in 1982, this novel finds Anthony operating at the absolute peak of his imaginative powers, effortlessly bridging the seemingly incompatible realms of hard science fiction and high fantasy.

At the core of Robot Adept lies a brilliant thematic exploration of duality and balance. The protagonist, Stile, remains the ultimate bridging figure, perpetually caught in a high-stakes cosmic tug-of-war. On one side is Proton, a sterile, hyper-technological planet of ruthless robotic efficiency and deadly Great Games, where survival depends on wit, metal, and mastery of mechanics. On the other side is Phaze, a vibrant, whimsical, yet perilous dimension where science is null and void, replaced entirely by magic, mythical beasts, and sorcerous ancestry. Anthony uses this bifurcated setting not merely as a colorful backdrop, but as a philosophical playground. Through Stile, the novel asks a compelling question: Can technology and magic truly coexist, or must one inevitably consume the other? Stile is forced to become a master of both, proving that human adaptability is the ultimate bridge between cold logic and mystical wonder.

Anthony’s authorial style is immediately recognizable—playful, brisk, relentlessly inventive, and deeply entrenched in the speculative fiction sensibilities of the early 1980s. He possesses an uncanny knack for world-building that is dense with rules, yet light on its feet. The narrative pace is breathless. Stile is never afforded a moment to rest, plunging readers headfirst into a relentless gauntlet of trials. Whether he is navigating the treacherous political machinations of Proton’s corporate overlords, deciphering the cryptic laws of Phaze’s magical ecosystem, or attempting to romance the Lady Blue, the stakes are continually ratcheted upward. 

What elevates Robot Adept above standard genre fare is Anthony's penchant for subverting expectations. Just when the plot seems cornered by a familiar trope, the author introduces a fresh complication—such as the unforgettable mortal duel with the unicorn Herd Leader, where standard magical resolutions are rendered entirely useless. Furthermore, Anthony weaves a lingering mystery throughout the saga: the identity of the paranoid Adept and the shadowy Citizen orchestrating assassinations from the shadows. This whodunit element injects a palpable noir tension into the otherwise fantastical and futuristic proceedings.

While critics of the era sometimes pointed to Anthony’s cheeky humor and unabashedly pulpy sensibilities, these very traits form the heartbeat of the Apprentice Adept series. Robot Adept does not take itself too seriously, yet it treats its protagonist's existential dilemmas with genuine emotional weight. Stile is an everyman thrust into extraordinary circumstances, driven by a simple, desperate need to exist and protect those he loves.

Ultimately, Robot Adept is a quintessential artifact of 1980s speculative fiction that still packs a considerable punch. It rewards returning readers with rich continuity while offering a dazzling playground for newcomers willing to suspend their disbelief and embrace the madness. Piers Anthony successfully fuses microchips and sorcery into a cohesive, highly entertaining narrative tapestry. For anyone who has ever wondered what happens when a futuristic serf picks up a magic wand, this novel remains an essential, unputdownable journey.
