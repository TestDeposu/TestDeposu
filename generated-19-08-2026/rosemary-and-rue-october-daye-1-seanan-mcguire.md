# Rosemary and Rue (October Daye, #1)

**Author:** Seanan McGuire  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Image a world where the shimmering, dangerous veil between our mundane reality and the ancient courts of Faerie has never truly fallen, but merely stepped into the shadows. In her 2009 debut urban fantasy novel, Rosemary and Rue, Seanan McGuire introduces readers to this hidden San Francisco, establishing a rich, gritty, and deeply atmospheric universe that forever changed the landscape of modern urban fantasy. This milestone first installment in the celebrated October Daye series does not merely visit Faerie; it drags the reader kicking and screaming into its politics, its bloodlines, and its unforgiving magic.

At the heart of McGuire's narrative style is a relentless, hard-boiled detective energy seamlessly blended with classic folkloric mythology. October "Toby" Daye is far from the pristine, glittering heroines of traditional fantasy. She is a changeling—half-human, half-fae—caught in the agonizing space between two worlds that largely despise her. When we first meet Toby, she is licking her wounds from past betrayals, having retreated entirely into the mortal world to escape the cruelty of her immortal bloodline. But McGuire's signature pacing refuses to let her protagonists rest. The murder of Countess Evening Winterrose acts as an inevitable catalyst, plunging Toby back into a labyrinth of court intrigue, forgotten duties, and deadly curses.

The central theme of Rosemary and Rue is alienation and the heavy price of belonging. McGuire explores what it means to be an outsider forced to navigate a rigid, archaic society where mercy is viewed as a fatal weakness. Toby’s journey is not just about solving a murder; it is a desperate race against a literal ticking clock imposed by a dying curse. This narrative device elevates the stakes from a standard whodunit into a visceral exploration of trauma, resilience, and identity. Toby must confront the very institutions that cast her out, weaponizing her sharp intellect and her stubborn refusal to break, even as the magic slowly consumes her from the inside out.

McGuire’s prose is both lyrical and punchy, capturing the dual nature of her protagonist. She paints San Francisco with a vibrant, noir-infused brush, where neon lights bleed into the impossible twilight of hidden fae courts. The world-building is layered and immersive, revealing centuries of political maneuvering, broken oaths, and hidden bloodlines without ever bogging down the forward momentum of the plot. Every character, from arrogant pureblood fae to street-smart changelings, breathes with dangerous life. McGuire doesn’t shy away from the brutality of her universe, ensuring that every victory Toby claws her way toward feels desperately earned.

Rosemary and Rue is a masterclass in urban fantasy debut writing. It introduces a protagonist who is deeply flawed, remarkably resilient, and utterly unforgettable. Seanan McGuire has crafted a compelling mystery wrapped in ancient magic, proving that the shadows of Faerie are closer—and more lethal—than we ever dared to imagine. For readers hungry for sharp wit, high-stakes mystery, and a heroine who refuses to stay down, this modern classic is an absolute must-read.
