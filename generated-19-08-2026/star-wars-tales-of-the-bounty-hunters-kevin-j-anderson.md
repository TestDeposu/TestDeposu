# Star Wars: Tales of the Bounty Hunters

**Author:** Kevin J. Anderson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1996

**A Dark and Desperate Galaxy: *Star Wars: Tales of the Bounty Hunters* Revisits the Scum and Villainy of the Expanded Universe**

Kevin J. Anderson’s *Star Wars: Tales of the Bounty Hunters* (1996) is a gritty, atmospheric anthology that strips away the heroism of the original trilogy to plunge readers into the morally bankrupt underbelly of the *Star Wars* galaxy. This isn’t a story about Luke Skywalker’s redemption or Han Solo’s charm—it’s a tale of survival, betrayal, and cold calculation, where the only law is profit, and the only mercy is a bullet to the back of the head. With six original stories penned by Anderson and a roster of talented contributors—including M. Shayne Bell and Dave Wolverton—this collection reimagines the galaxy as a lawless frontier where bounty hunters are the true rulers, and Darth Vader’s shadow looms over every deal.

At its core, *Tales of the Bounty Hunters* explores the brutal pragmatism of the galaxy’s most infamous mercenaries. The premise is simple: when Vader orders a six-man hunt for Han Solo and the *Millennium Falcon*, six of the galaxy’s most ruthless trackers—including the legendary Boba Fett—are assembled for the job. But this isn’t just a chase; it’s a descent into the psychology of men (and women) who see life as a transaction. Anderson’s writing excels in portraying these characters as more than just muscle—they’re survivors, each with their own code, their own demons, and their own reasons for doing what they do. Whether it’s the cynical efficiency of a hunter like Fett or the desperate gambles of a rogue like Jango Fett (before he became a clone), the anthology captures the tension between loyalty and self-interest, between the thrill of the hunt and the terror of being hunted.

The anthology’s strength lies in its diversity of voices. Anderson’s own contributions—particularly the story featuring Boba Fett—are masterclasses in tension and atmosphere, dripping with the kind of dialogue and action that make *Star Wars* feel alive. But it’s the other writers who truly shine in their portrayals of lesser-known bounty hunters. M. Shayne Bell’s story, for instance, introduces a bounty hunter with a tragic past, forcing the reader to question whether these men are truly monsters or just products of a galaxy that rewards only the strongest. Dave Wolverton’s contribution, meanwhile, leans into the noir tone, with a hunter who operates in the shadows of the underworld, where deals are made in back alleys and trust is a liability.

What makes *Tales of the Bounty Hunters* so compelling is its willingness to embrace the darker side of the *Star Wars* universe. Unlike the more sentimental stories of the original films, this collection doesn’t shy away from the cost of war or the moral ambiguity of its characters. There are no clear heroes here—only survivors, and the galaxy is a place where survival often means becoming what you hunt. The anthology’s bonus section, which includes excerpts from other *Star Wars* novels and a primer on the expanded universe, serves as a reminder that this isn’t just a standalone story—it’s a glimpse into a larger, more complex galaxy where the Rebellion is just one faction among many, and the real power lies with those who operate outside the law.

Anderson’s style is deft, balancing the epic scale of *Star Wars* with the intimate, often brutal details of bounty hunting. His prose is lean, efficient, and packed with the kind of sharp dialogue that makes the galaxy feel real. The anthology doesn’t waste time on exposition; instead, it throws the reader into the action, letting the characters’ actions and words speak for themselves. This is a galaxy where trust is a luxury, and every deal has a price—sometimes in credits, sometimes in blood.

For fans of *Star Wars*, *Tales of the Bounty Hunters* is a must-read. It’s not just a collection of stories—it’s an immersion into the kind of world where the heroes of the original films are just another target on a bounty board. The anthology captures the essence of the expanded universe at its best: a place where the law is what you can enforce, and the only certainty is that someone is always watching. Whether you’re a longtime fan or a newcomer to the lore, this book offers a fresh, dark, and deeply satisfying look at the galaxy far, far away—one where the real villains are the ones who walk away from the fight.
