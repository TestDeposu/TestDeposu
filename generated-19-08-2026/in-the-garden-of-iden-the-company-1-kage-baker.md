# In the Garden of Iden (The Company, #1)

**Author:** Kage Baker  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2026

Kage Baker’s literary presence remains a towering, irreplaceable monument in modern speculative fiction, and her enduring legacy is laid bare in all its breathtaking glory within this essential collection. Though she began her professional writing career later in life, Baker’s voice arrived fully formed—richly textured, sharply witty, and possessing a rare, timeless elegance. This volume serves as both a treasure trove for longtime devotees and a stunning gateway for newcomers eager to discover why her absence is still so keenly felt by lovers of imaginative literature.

At the heart of Baker’s fictional universe lies her most celebrated creation: The Company, a vast, shadowy organization of time-traveling immortals who harvest Earth’s lost treasures across the centuries. Yet, Baker’s brilliance was never merely about the high-concept mechanics of time travel; rather, it was about the deeply human, vulnerable souls caught within the gears of grand historical machinery. Whether exploring the sun-drenched landscapes of nineteenth-century California through the eyes of Mendoza the botanist in “Noble Mold,” or chronicling the poignant, unexpected connection between an autistic Company operative and a lost soul in “The Carpet Beds of Sutro Park,” Baker masterfully blends historical scope with intimate emotional resonance. 

Her style is defined by a meticulous attention to detail, a sharp, understated irony, and an effortless command of historical atmosphere. Baker treats the past not as a flat backdrop, but as a living, breathing tapestry full of contradictions, beauty, and tragedy. This is prominently displayed in Hugo-nominated triumphs like “Son, Observe the Time” and the dazzling “Welcome to Olympos, Mr. Hearst,” a tour de force set against the glamorous yet cutthroat backdrop of 1930s Hollywood. 

Beyond the chronology of The Company, this collection brilliantly showcases the breathtaking versatility of Baker’s imagination through a curated selection of standalone tales. From the deceptively charming resort town that slides effortlessly into Lovecraftian terror in “Calamari Curls” to the World Fantasy Award-nominated “Caverns of Mystery,” where ancient myths persistently reshape reality, Baker proves her mastery across diverse subgenres. She effortlessly bridges the gap between science fiction and fantasy, infusing both with a lyrical prose style that feels lovingly crafted down to the very last syllable.

This volume is a triumphant reminder of a master at the peak of her powers. Anyone with a passion for intelligent, beautifully written, and deeply resonant speculative fiction needs to read this book. It is a fitting testament to a brilliant and irreplaceable writer whose stories will continue to captivate readers for generations to come.
