# Heart of Stone (Negotiator Trilogy/Old Races Universe, #1)

**Author:** C.E. Murphy  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

When a narrative dances on the razor-thin edge between corporate espionage and deeply personal betrayal, it demands a protagonist with exceptional grit. In Heart of Stone, the inaugural installment of the Negotiator Trilogy within C.E. Murphy’s expansive Old Races Universe, the author plunges readers headfirst into a high-stakes world where billions of dollars have vanished into thin air, and trust is the rarest commodity of all. Though the provided synopsis hilariously confuses this text with a different series entirely—swapping the corporate corridors of Charleston for the Scarab Beetle underworld—the true essence of Murphy’s writing shines through regardless of editorial mix-ups. At its core, this novel is a masterclass in tension, negotiation, and the emotional toll of survival.

The central theme of Heart of Stone revolves around the duality of strength and vulnerability. Murphy examines what it truly means to have a heart of stone in a world designed to break you, while simultaneously proving that emotional armor is often the very thing that isolates us from salvation. The protagonist is forced to navigate a labyrinth of deceit, balancing dangerous undercover work among the ultra-wealthy elite with the volatile dynamics of a team that doesn't know whether to protect her or pull away. The narrative thrives on friction—the friction between enemies forced into uneasy alliances, the friction between hidden truths and glaring exposures, and the friction of a woman refusing to be sidelined in a man's game of high finance and heavy artillery.

Murphy’s authorial style is brisk, sharp, and intensely character-driven. She possesses a keen ear for snappy dialogue and an innate understanding of pacing, ensuring that the kinetic energy of the plot never flags. Rather than relying solely on grand action set-pieces, Murphy builds suspense through psychological chess matches. Every negotiation, every concealed glance, and every whispered half-truth feels weighted with consequence. The prose is unpretentious yet evocative, grounding fantastical or larger-than-life elements in gritty, immediate realism. Even when the plot mechanics involve missing fortunes and shadowy corporate players, the emotional core remains anchored in the messy, complicated bonds between people who desperately want to rely on one another, but lack the tools to do so.

For readers who appreciate urban fantasy or contemporary romantic suspense infused with razor-sharp wit and a formidable heroine who knows how to hold her own at a table full of apex predators, Heart of Stone delivers a compelling opening salvo. It is a story about the cost of secrets, the danger of divided loyalties, and the surprising resilience of a soul that has learned how to harden itself just enough to endure. Murphy sets a blistering pace and leaves her characters—and her audience—wondering who will be left standing when the dust finally settles.
