# Blood Trail (Vicki Nelson #2)

**Author:** Tanya Huff  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

Stepping back into the shadowy, neon-lit underbelly of Tanya Huff’s urban fantasy universe is always a visceral experience, but *Blood Trail*—the second installment in the celebrated Vicki Nelson series—delivers a masterclass in expanding a beloved supernatural mythos through the sharp lens of short fiction. Published in 2020, this collection serves as both a nostalgic reunion for longtime devotees and a punchy, atmospheric entry point for newcomers. Huff does not merely revisit her world; she gleefully tears up the map, plunging her iconic ensemble of monster-hunters, ancient vampires, and reluctant wizards into perils that are as emotionally resonant as they are blood-curdling.

At the heart of *Blood Trail* lies a compelling thematic exploration of vulnerability disguised as power. Whether Vicki Nelson is staring down a desperate man demanding the dark gift of vampirism in *Quid Pro Quo*, or untangling the deceptive, wish-granting malice of a jinn in *If Wishes Were*, the overarching narrative heartbeat remains consistent: the supernatural is rarely as terrifying as the human desires that summon it. Huff expertly navigates the friction between the mundane and the macabre. Her characters do not inhabit a world of pristine, romanticized gothic tropes; instead, they grapple with the gritty, exhausted reality of fighting monsters while trying to pay rent, manage relationships, and survive their own birthdays.

Huff’s authorial style remains one of her greatest assets, characterized by a seamless blend of hard-boiled detective noir and contemporary urban fantasy. Her prose is punchy, laced with dry wit and an unyielding momentum that refuses to let the reader pause for breath. Even within the compact constraints of short fiction, Huff establishes mood with devastating efficiency. A siren’s seductive call in *Songs Sung Red* feels genuinely intoxicating, just as the dimensional rift in *No Matter Where You Go* evokes a claustrophobic, cosmic dread. Furthermore, the shifting perspectives—ranging from Vicki’s world-weary grit to the earnest, youthful bravado of wizard Tony Foster and the immortal ennui of vampire author Henry Fitzroy—ensure that the collection maintains a vibrant, kaleidoscopic rhythm.

What elevates *Blood Trail* above standard genre anthologies is the palpable affection Huff holds for her creations. She allows them to be messy, flawed, and deeply human, even when they are dealing with the inhuman. The banter is sharp, the stakes feel perpetually high, and the emotional core never gets lost beneath the flash of fangs and spells. For readers who crave urban fantasy with teeth, heart, and an unapologetic sense of fun, Tanya Huff’s latest dispatch from the dark side is an absolute triumph.
