# King's Test (Star of the Guardians, #2)

**Author:** Margaret Weis  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1991

Margaret Weis returns to the expansive, high-stakes cosmos of her Star of the Guardians series with King's Test, a 1991 space opera that firmly establishes her skill in blending classical heroic tropes with gritty, political intrigue. Picking up the threads of a galaxy fractured by war, Weis wastes no time plunging the reader back into a universe where loyalty is a luxury and survival is a daily battle. 

At the heart of King's Test lies a compelling thematic exploration of power, legitimacy, and the corruption inherent in absolute control. The narrative pivots on the uneasy alliance between freedom and tyranny, brilliantly embodied by Derek Sagan. Sagan is no traditional, white-hatted hero; he is a complex puppet master driven by pragmatic ruthlessness, aiming to elevate Dion to the Starfire dynasty throne while acting as the true power in the shadows. This dynamic dissects the old philosophical question: does the end justify the means when the means require manipulation and deceit? Weis uses this central conflict to examine how leadership often demands a forfeiture of innocence, forcing her characters to weigh the salvation of the galaxy against the corruption of their own souls.

Interwoven with this political chess match is a classic science fiction MacGuffin—a ten-centimeter crystal cube capable of tearing the fabric of creation apart. While a world-ending weapon is a staple of the genre, Weis elevates the trope by using it as a crucible for her characters. The race for the artifact on a wretched planetary sinkhole of sin serves as a pressure cooker, forcing figures like Sagan, the enigmatic Lady Maigrey, and the terrifying, slave-commanding genius Abdiel to confront their deepest flaws and ambitions.

Stylistically, Weis writes with a cinematic flair and a sharp ear for dialogue, traits that made her a titan of speculative fiction. Her prose is brisk and atmospheric, capturing the stark contrasts between the glittering corruption of galactic courts and the grimy desperation of planetary underworlds. She excels at world-building through action rather than exposition, dropping the reader directly into a richly layered universe populated by drugged armies, rebel factions, and royal bloodlines. The pacing is relentless, balancing large-scale tactical maneuvers with intimate, character-driven moments of betrayal and realization.

Dion’s arc in this installment is particularly noteworthy, acting as the emotional anchor of the story. As he faces the titular "test," he transitions from a pawn in Sagan’s grand design to a man forged in the fires of adversity, grappling with what it truly means to wear a crown. 

King's Test is a triumphant middle-chapter adventure that deepens the mythology of the Starfire dynasty while delivering breathless tension. Weis proves that space opera can be both grand in scale and deeply psychological, making this a must-read for fans of epic science fiction and complex character studies.
