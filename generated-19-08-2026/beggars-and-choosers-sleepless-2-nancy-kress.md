# Beggars and Choosers (Sleepless, #2)

**Author:** Nancy Kress  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2004

An Epic Conclusion on the Brink of Extinction

In the grand tradition of hard science fiction, few authors explore the sociological fracture lines of tomorrow quite like Nancy Kress. With *Beggars and Choosers*, Kress returns to the sprawling, high-stakes universe of her acclaimed series to deliver a masterclass in tension, existential dread, and human resilience. Though the publisher's provided synopsis mistakenly weaves in the cosmic scope of her *Probability Trilogy*, the true beating heart of the broader "Beggars" saga has always been Kress's laser-focused interrogation of bioengineering, societal stratification, and what it truly means to be human. 

The central theme of this gripping installment hinges on the dangerous chasm widening between the "Superolivers"—the genetically altered, hyper-intelligent elite who require virtually no sleep and possess cognitive gifts that leave baseline humanity in the dust—and the "Sleepless" and normal humans struggling to maintain relevance in a rapidly shifting world. Kress masterfully uses this biological divide as a mirror for our own societal anxieties. She asks the uncomfortable questions: When technological and genetic advancements outpace our collective empathy, who gets to decide the future? Are we entering a utopian era of boundless potential, or are we engineering our own obsolescence? 

What sets Kress apart in the crowded landscape of science fiction is her refusal to rely on easy answers or black-and-white morality. Her characters are rarely galaxy-saving heroes in the traditional sense; instead, they are deeply flawed, pragmatic, and frequently overwhelmed survivors navigating a sociopolitical earthquake. Whether she is exploring the ruthless economics of corporate-dominated futures or the quiet tragedies of marginalized underclasses, Kress grounds her grand speculative concepts in hyper-realistic human emotion. The prose is lean, sharp, and propulsive, driving the narrative forward without ever sacrificing the philosophical weight that has become her trademark.

As civil unrest boils over and the fragile social contract shatters, Kress orchestrates a narrative balancing act that spans corporate boardrooms, underground resistance networks, and the intimate, everyday struggles of ordinary people caught in the machinery of progress. The result is a striking, thought-provoking speculative journey that lingers long after the final page is turned. For readers who crave intelligent, character-driven science fiction that challenges the intellect while stirring the soul, this installment stands as essential reading.
