# The Number of the Beast (The World As Myth #2)

**Author:** Robert A. Heinlein  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2026

Robert A. Heinlein’s late-career masterpiece, The Number of the Beast, stands as a dizzying, unapologetic victory lap through the grand architecture of speculative fiction. Long before the modern obsession with multiverses dominated pop culture, the undisputed Dean of Science Fiction was already dismantling the fragile walls separating reality from imagination. This newly authorized edition reintroduces readers to a work where Heinlein is at his most playful, unguarded, and structurally audacious. 

The narrative engine begins with deceptive swiftness. Dr. Richard Ames finds himself framed for murder before he can even digest the stranger's opening demand. What starts as a breathless interstellar thriller—hurtling from the elegant habitat of the Golden Rule out across the lawless, dust-choked surface of Luna—quickly metastasizes into something far wilder. When relentless assassins force Ames and his brilliantly lethal, enigmatic bride Gwen Novak to flee, they don't just run across space; they run clean out of the universe entirely. 

At its core, The Number of the Beast explores the ultimate fluidity of existence, anchoring itself firmly in Heinlein’s sprawling "World As Myth" framework. Here, the central theme is the democratization of creation itself. Heinlein posits a universe where fiction and fact are not opposed forces, but interchangeable currencies. Every written word, every invented realm, and every alternate timeline exists somewhere within the infinite geometry of the cosmos. This meta-fictional playground allows the Grand Master to fold his own bibliography back onto itself, serving as a vital bridge connecting beloved milestones like The Moon Is a Harsh Mistress and To Sail Beyond the Sunset. 

Heinlein’s signature style is on full, unfiltered display here. It is a heady pastiche of hard science fiction, philosophical musings, libertarian bravado, and eccentric humor. The dialogue is snappy, relentless, and occasionally exasperating in its loquaciousness—hallmarks of Heinlein’s later works where the author seems less interested in pleasing traditional editors and more inclined to entertain himself and his devoted readership. The characters are larger-than-life archetypes, yet they carry an undeniable infectious energy. And anchoring the madness is the indomitable presence of Pixel, a feline of impossible geometry who simply refuses to accept that walls exist, serving as both a literal and metaphorical symbol for the novel's boundary-shattering ambition.

For modern readers, approaching The Number of the Beast requires abandoning rigid expectations of conventional plotting. It is a sprawling, chaotic, and delightfully unhinged meta-narrative that gleefully shatters the fourth wall long before it was fashionable. It is not merely a novel, but an invitation into the mind of a master craftsman enjoying the absolute zenith of his creative freedom. Whether you are a lifelong pilgrim of Heinlein’s universes or a newcomer curious about the roots of multiverse storytelling, this return to the fold is an essential, wildly entertaining journey across the infinite.
