# Throne of Jade (Temeraire, #2)

**Author:** Naomi Novik  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2006

Navigating the turbulent waters where Patrick O’Brian meets Anne McCaffrey, Naomi Novik returns with Throne of Jade, the dazzling second installment in her critically acclaimed Temeraire series. Following the triumphant events of His Majesty’s Dragon, Novik expands her brilliantly realized alternative historical canvas, trading the mist-shrouded skies of Britain for the perilous, vast expanse of the high seas and the opulent, complex courts of imperial China. 

At the heart of Novik’s narrative style is a seamless, elegant marriage of historical fiction and high fantasy. She constructs an 1800s Napoleonic era where the theater of war is fought not just with wooden ships and iron cannons, but with magnificent, sentient dragons. Novik’s prose is meticulously researched, capturing the formal cadence, naval protocol, and sociopolitical anxieties of the Regency period with startling authenticity. Yet, beneath the polished veneer of naval decorum lies a deeply moving, contemporary exploration of empathy, autonomy, and the complexities of companionship.

The central theme of Throne of Jade hinges on ownership, cultural identity, and the heavy burden of duty. Captain Will Laurence, once an uncompromising officer of the Royal Navy, finds his worldview continuously challenged by his bond with Temeraire, a celestial dragon of rare intellect and formidable presence. When an outraged Chinese delegation demands the return of their imperial gift—intercepted en route to Napoleon—Laurence is thrust into a diplomatic quagmire. The ensuing voyage to the Far East serves not merely as an adventure across uncharted waters, but as a profound philosophical journey. Novik uses this long voyage to dissect the arrogance of Western imperialism, contrasting British military utilitarianism with the sophisticated, highly civilized, and entirely different perspective of the Chinese court, where dragons are treated not merely as beasts of burden or weapons of war, but as scholars, aristocrats, and cherished equals.

Novik’s world-building is breathtaking in its scope. The grueling sea voyage is fraught with natural perils, maritime intrigue, and the quiet psychological tension between a captain torn from his cultural roots and a dragon stepping into a heritage he never knew. Once the duo reaches China, the novel truly ascends. The Celestial Empire is rendered with rich sensory details—the intricate court politics, the breathtaking architecture, and the astonishing revelation of a society where dragons hold immense cultural and political sway. This cultural clash forces Laurence to confront his own prejudices and redefine his place in a rapidly expanding world.

For readers who cherish rich character development, witty banter, and grand-scale historical adventure infused with mythological wonder, Throne of Jade is an absolute triumph. Novik does not merely repeat the formula of her debut; she elevates it, offering a richer, deeper, and more globe-spanning narrative that cements the Temeraire series as a modern classic of speculative fiction.
