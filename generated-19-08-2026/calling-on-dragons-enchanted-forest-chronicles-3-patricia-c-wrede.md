# Calling on Dragons (Enchanted Forest Chronicles, #3)

**Author:** Patricia C. Wrede  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

**Calling on Dragons: A Whimsical, Witty, and Unforgettable Fantasy Adventure**

Patricia C. Wrede’s *Calling on Dragons*—the third installment in the beloved *Enchanted Forest Chronicles*—is a masterclass in blending sharp humor, clever subversion of fantasy tropes, and heartfelt storytelling. If you’ve ever wanted a queen who’s more likely to trip over her own feet than deliver a grand speech, or a dragon king who’s as lovable as he is chaotic, this is your book. Wrede’s signature style—equal parts irreverent and tender—shines here as Cimorene, the reluctant queen of the Enchanted Forest, faces her greatest challenge yet: outsmarting a cunning (if dim-witted) cabal of wizards determined to seize control of her kingdom.

At its core, *Calling on Dragons* is a story about leadership, resilience, and the messy, beautiful reality of growing up. Cimorene isn’t a warrior princess or a noble savior; she’s a woman who stumbles into power, often by accident, and must learn to navigate the complexities of rule—all while dealing with the absurdities of her own life. The wizards, far from being terrifying villains, are more like bumbling, overconfident fools, their schemes repeatedly undone by Cimorene’s quick thinking and the unlikely allies she gathers. There’s the dragon king, a creature of immense power but equally immense silliness; a witch who’s more interested in gossip than grand magic; a magician whose spells go hilariously wrong; and, of course, Killer the blue donkey-rabbit, who may be the most competent (and adorable) member of the group.

Wrede’s humor is razor-sharp, dripping with wit and self-awareness. She doesn’t shy away from poking fun at fantasy conventions—dragons aren’t noble beasts here, and magic isn’t always reliable. Instead, she crafts a world where the absurdity of the situation is matched only by the characters’ determination to make the best of it. The dialogue crackles with personality, whether it’s Cimorene’s dry sarcasm, the wizards’ delusional confidence, or the dragon king’s deadpan observations. It’s a joy to read, a book that laughs *with* its characters rather than at them, and that’s what makes it so special.

But beneath the humor lies a story rich with heart. Cimorene’s journey is one of self-discovery, as she learns that being a queen isn’t about perfection—it’s about making tough choices, trusting her instincts, and embracing the chaos of life. The book also explores themes of friendship and found family, as Cimorene’s ragtag group of allies becomes her true support system. There’s a warmth to these relationships, a sense that even in the most ridiculous circumstances, loyalty and love matter most.

Wrede’s writing is effortless, her prose clean and engaging without ever sacrificing depth. She balances action and levity seamlessly, ensuring that the stakes feel real even when the situations are absurd. The world-building is immersive, with the Enchanted Forest feeling alive and vibrant, full of quirky creatures and eccentric characters. And while the book is part of a series, *Calling on Dragons* stands perfectly on its own—it’s a complete, satisfying adventure that leaves readers grinning from ear to ear.

For fans of fantasy that’s clever, funny, and full of heart, this is a must-read. It’s the kind of book that makes you laugh out loud one minute and feel deeply for its characters the next. Patricia C. Wrede has a rare talent for crafting stories that feel both timeless and utterly unique, and *Calling on Dragons* is a shining example of that talent.
