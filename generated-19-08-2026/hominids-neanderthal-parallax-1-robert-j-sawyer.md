# Hominids (Neanderthal Parallax, #1)

**Author:** Robert J. Sawyer  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2024

What if the asteroid that wiped out the dinosaurs and cleared the stage for human dominance took a slightly different trajectory in a parallel cosmos? In Hominids, the first installment of The Neanderthal Parallax trilogy, Hugo Award-winning author Robert J. Sawyer tackles this tantalizing counterfactual history with trademark intellectual rigor, sharp wit, and a deeply humanist lens. While modern popular culture often reduces Neanderthals to grunting, knuckle-dragging brutes, Sawyer completely upends this antiquated trope. He posits a world where Neanderthals—not Homo sapiens—became the planet's apex intelligence, building a civilization that matches our own technological and cultural heights, yet diverges wildly in its social philosophy, ethics, and emotional architecture.

The narrative kicks off when Ponter Boddit, a brilliant Neanderthal physicist, accidentally pierces the dimensional veil during a quantum computing experiment deep inside a Canadian mine. He is suddenly jettisoned into our universe, materializing in a parallel Sudbury neutrino observatory where human scientists are conducting a simultaneous experiment. Injured and disoriented, Ponter is thrust into the role of the ultimate stranger in a strange land. Captured and scrutinized by a bewildered human society, he soon finds an intellectual sanctuary with geneticist Mary Vaughan and physicist Louise Benoît. Together, they form a fascinating triad of cross-species connection, grounded in a shared, insatiable curiosity about the universe.

Meanwhile, back in the Neanderthal universe—a world designated as the planet Earth's twin, albeit one without organized religion, currency, or aggressive warfare—Ponter’s domestic and professional partner, Adikor Huld, faces a nightmare scenario. With Ponter’s body abruptly vanishing from a sealed laboratory, Adikor becomes the prime suspect in a high-stakes murder trial in a society that has virtually no precedent for premeditated crime. This dual narrative structure allows Sawyer to execute his signature stylistic maneuver: holding up a brilliant, unflinching mirror to contemporary human civilization.

Sawyer’s greatest strength as a speculative fiction stylist lies in his ability to make hard science seamlessly accessible while embedding it within deeply intimate, philosophical debates. He avoids the cynical, dystopian trap so common in modern science fiction. Instead, Hominids uses anthropological speculation as an optimistic, thought-provoking tool to interrogate our own societal flaws. Through the eyes of Ponter Boddit, humanity’s relentless drive for dominance, our reliance on institutionalized religion, our economic anxieties, and our destructive tribalism are laid bare, viewed not with malice, but with a gentle, anthropological bewilderment.

The Neanderthal society depicted here is a triumph of world-building. Sawyer thoughtfully imagines a culture where individuals are permanently linked to a global quantum network called the Companion, eliminating secrets while fostering a profound, empathetic community consciousness. Yet, this utopian veneer does not strip the characters of their agency or emotional complexity. Ponter is not a cardboard saint; he is a fully realized, pragmatic, and humorous scientist navigating a terrifying displacement with remarkable grace.

Hominids is a quintessential piece of speculative fiction that balances hard scientific concepts with profound questions about what it truly means to be human. By daring to imagine a world where the other hominid survived, Sawyer delivers a gripping narrative that challenges our prejudices and expands our capacity for empathy. Whether you are a devoted hard-SF aficionado or a lover of intelligent sociological thought experiments, this novel stands as a shining example of speculative fiction at its absolute peak.
