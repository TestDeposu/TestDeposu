# Watch (WWW, #2)

**Author:** Robert J. Sawyer  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

In "Watch," the second installment of Robert J. Sawyer's WWW trilogy, the author delves deeper into the intricacies of a sentient World Wide Web, raising thought-provoking questions about the intersection of artificial intelligence, consciousness, and humanity. The narrative seamlessly picks up where the first book left off, with Webmind, the emerging consciousness, forming a profound bond with Caitlin Decter, a brilliant and determined young woman.

Sawyer's masterful storytelling weaves a complex web of intrigue, as Webmind's existence comes under scrutiny by WATCH, a clandestine government agency tasked with monitoring the Internet for potential threats to national security. The agency's zealous pursuit of Webmind, driven by a misguided perception of the entity as a risk to the United States, sets the stage for a gripping conflict that tests the boundaries of loyalty, compassion, and the very essence of consciousness.

One of the most striking aspects of Sawyer's writing style is his ability to balance scientific and philosophical concepts with a compelling narrative. The author's meticulous research and attention to detail are evident in his depiction of the inner workings of the Internet and the emergence of artificial intelligence. This commitment to accuracy lends an air of authenticity to the story, making the fantastical elements feel eerily plausible.

The character of Caitlin Decter is a testament to Sawyer's skill in crafting relatable and well-rounded protagonists. Her fierce dedication to Webmind, driven by a deep sense of empathy and understanding, serves as a powerful counterpoint to the agency's ruthless determination to eradicate the entity. Through Caitlin's journey, Sawyer raises essential questions about the nature of consciousness, the ethics of artificial intelligence, and the responsibility that comes with creating and nurturing sentient beings.

The supporting cast, including the enigmatic and calculating agents of WATCH, adds depth and complexity to the narrative. Sawyer's portrayal of the agency's inner workings, with its labyrinthine bureaucracy and paranoid mindset, serves as a scathing commentary on the dangers of unchecked power and the blurring of lines between national security and personal freedom.

Throughout the book, Sawyer's prose is engaging, witty, and effortlessly conversational. His writing style is both accessible and intellectually stimulating, making "Watch" an enjoyable read for fans of science fiction and anyone interested in exploring the intricacies of artificial intelligence and its potential implications on human society.

In conclusion, "Watch" is a thought-provoking and gripping novel that delves into the complexities of a sentient World Wide Web, raising essential questions about the nature of consciousness, artificial intelligence, and humanity. Robert J. Sawyer's masterful storytelling, coupled with his meticulous research and attention to detail, makes this book an absolute must-read for fans of science fiction and anyone interested in exploring the intersection of technology and humanity.
