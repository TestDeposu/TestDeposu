# My Life as a White Trash Zombie (White Trash Zombie, #1)

**Author:** Diana Rowland  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

Title: Resurrection in the Swamps: Finding Redemption One Brain at a Crap Job

Meet Angel Crawford. By her own admission, and the general consensus of her swamp-dwelling Louisiana community, she is a certified loser. A high school dropout with a debilitating pill habit, a fresh felony on her record, and a living situation defined by an alcoholic deadbeat dad, Angel is a master of the downward spiral. When we first encounter her in Diana Rowland’s 2011 urban fantasy breakthrough, My Life as a White Trash Zombie, her life has hit rock bottom—and then she overdoses on painkillers and drives her car straight into a watery grave. 

Except death doesn't quite take. Angel wakes up in the ER, entirely unmarked by a horrific crash, only to receive a cryptic, non-negotiable job offer at the county morgue. Welcome to undeath. 

What follows is a delightfully gritty, humorous, and surprisingly poignant exploration of the ultimate second chance. Rowland takes the heavily saturated urban fantasy genre and infuses it with a blast of raw, regional flavor and working-class grit. The primary theme of the novel centers on redemption and self-worth through the most unlikely of lenses. Angel’s transformation from a hopeless addict to a functional (if undead) member of society is brilliantly mirrored by her new existence. Her struggle to kick her pharmaceutical addiction is replaced by an even more urgent, biological craving for fresh brains. It is a metaphor with teeth: Angel must literally consume the dead to keep herself moving forward, learning to process not just gray matter, but the memories and humanity attached to it. 

Rowland’s authorial style is sharp, fiercely unapologetic, and laced with pitch-black humor. She embraces Angel’s self-deprecating voice with complete authenticity, avoiding cheap sentimentality while still engineering deep reader empathy. The southern Louisiana setting acts almost as a character itself, dripping with humidity, swamp water, and systemic poverty. Rowland doesn't romanticize Angel's background; instead, she uses it to anchor the supernatural elements in a gritty reality where a job at the morgue actually counts as a step up the socioeconomic ladder. 

As if navigating the nuances of being a newly minted zombie weren't enough, Angel quickly finds herself knee-deep in a murder mystery. The local morgue is suddenly flooded with the headless victims of a brutal serial killer—a terrifying professional hazard just as her cravings peak. Toss in a swoon-worthy crush on a local deputy and the constant, breathless terror of getting caught, and Rowland delivers a breathless, high-stakes plot that never loses its comedic edge. 

My Life as a White Trash Zombie is a triumph of voice and world-building. Diana Rowland successfully takes a ridiculous premise and elevates it into a compelling, fast-paced narrative about reclaiming one's life when there is literally nothing left to lose. Angel Crawford is flawed, foul-mouthed, resilient, and utterly unforgettable. For readers weary of pristine, brooding heroines who effortlessly save the world, Angel is the chaotic, brain-craving antidote you didn't know you needed.
