# Betrayer (The Horus Heresy, #24)

**Author:** Aaron Dembski-Bowden  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

**Betrayer: A Masterclass in Betrayal and the Shadows of the Horus Heresy**

Aaron Dembski-Bowden’s *Betrayer*—the latest anthology in *The Horus Heresy* series—is more than just a collection of stories; it’s a dark, immersive deep dive into the psychology of treason, the allure of power, and the monstrous beauty of the War in Heaven’s most infamous traitors. With contributions from some of the most talented voices in Warhammer 40,000 lore, this book doesn’t just retell the fall of the Primarchs—it dissects it, exposing the cracks in their armor before they shattered entirely.

At its core, *Betrayer* is a meditation on **betrayal as a personal and cosmic act**. Each story peels back the layers of the traitor Primarchs—Lugurus, Fulgrim, Mortarion, Angron, and the rest—not as mythic villains, but as men (and one godlike figure) who were once heroes. The anthology forces readers to ask: *What would make a warrior of such unmatched skill and devotion turn against the Emperor?* The answers are as varied as they are chilling. Some stories, like **Chris Wraight’s *Child of Chaos*** and **Guy Haley’s *Grandfather’s Gift***, explore the primal, almost biological corruption that gnaws at the edges of their minds, whispering promises of power beyond mortal comprehension. Others, such as **Dan Abnett’s *Misbegotten*** and **Graham McNeill’s *The Ancient Awaits***, delve into the political machinations and psychological warfare that turned brothers against brothers, painting the Heresy as less a sudden rebellion and more a slow, inevitable unraveling.

What makes *Betrayer* so compelling is its **diverse yet cohesive storytelling style**. The anthology blends **gothic horror, military sci-fi, and psychological thriller** elements, each story tailored to its subject. **LJ Goulding’s *Prince of Blood*** is a visceral, almost mythic tale of blood and vengeance, while **James Swallow’s *Lantern’s Light*** reads like a Lovecraftian nightmare, where the traitors’ sins are measured in the weight of their own shadows. **Mike Brooks’ *Council of Truth*** and **David Guymer’s *The Sixth Cult of the Denied*** excel in political intrigue, revealing how the traitors manipulated the Imperium’s own institutions to their advantage. Meanwhile, **Aaron Dembski-Bowden’s *The Abyssal Edge*** and **Gav Thorpe’s *The Conqueror’s Truth*** deliver hard-hitting, character-driven narratives that make the traitors feel tragically human—flawed, desperate, and ultimately doomed by their own hubris.

The anthology’s strength lies in its **unflinching portrayal of the traitors’ humanity**. Even the most monstrous figures—like the blood-drunk Angron or the delusional Fulgrim—are given moments of vulnerability. **Chris Wraight’s *Terminus*** and **David Annandale’s *The Verdict of the Scythe***, in particular, humanize the chaos cultists and the traitors’ most fanatical followers, showing how ideology warps loyalty into fanaticism. This isn’t just a story about war; it’s about **the cost of absolute power and the seduction of the abyss**.

Dembski-Bowden’s curation ensures that no story feels out of place. The anthology flows like a single, sprawling epic, with each tale reinforcing the others’ themes. Whether it’s the **hereditary corruption** of *Child of Chaos*, the **betrayal of trust** in *Council of Truth*, or the **inevitable doom** of *The Ancient Awaits*, the collection reinforces the idea that the Horus Heresy wasn’t just a war—it was a **civil war of the soul**.

For fans of *The Horus Heresy*, this is essential reading. For newcomers, it’s a gateway into the darkest, most fascinating corners of the 41st millennium. *Betrayer* doesn’t just entertain—it **haunts**, leaving readers to wonder: *How far would you go for power? And what would it cost you to betray everything you once believed in?*
