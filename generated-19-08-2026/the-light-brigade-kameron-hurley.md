# The Light Brigade

**Author:** Kameron Hurley  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

Kameron Hurley has never been a writer interested in sanitized, bloodless futures. With her 2019 science fiction thriller, The Light Brigade, the Hugo Award-winning author tackles the horrors of corporate warfare with a blistering intensity that echoes the timeless anxieties of Joe Haldeman’s The Forever War and Robert Heinlein’s Starship Troopers. But make no mistake: Hurley carves out her own brutal, brilliant path. The result is a disorienting, furious, and deeply human examination of how institutional violence rewrites not just nations, but the very fabric of reality for the individuals unfortunate enough to fight.

At the core of the novel is a terrifying technological premise: soldiers are literally atomized, broken down into light, and beamed across the solar system to interplanetary battlefronts on Mars. The corporate conglomerates pulling the strings promise glory and a better world, but the reality is mechanical and soul-crushing. Those who survive the trip and the meat-grinder of combat are dubbed the Light Brigade—returning soldiers who come back fractured, fundamentally changed, and often discarded. 

We experience this nightmare through the eyes of Dietz, a fresh and eager recruit desperate to be counted among the heroes. Yet, almost immediately, Dietz’s experience shatters. Through a series of chaotic combat drops that refuse to sync with the rest of the platoon, Dietz begins slipping out of chronological order. These fractured jumps reveal a terrifying truth about the war—one that the corporate brass have carefully buried beneath sanitized mission briefs and manufactured propaganda. Is Dietz succumbing to combat madness, or is witnessing the true timeline of a manipulated war the ultimate curse? 

Hurley’s signature style shines brightly here: visceral, unflinching, and relentlessly paced. She uses the sci-fi concept of time-slippage not merely as a neat narrative gimmick, but as a visceral metaphor for the psychological fracturing of soldiers and the systemic gaslighting inherent in modern warfare. The prose is gritty, smelling of ozone, plasma, and copper, perfectly capturing the dehumanizing nature of fighting a war for profit. As Dietz struggles to untangle memory from conditioning, the line between hero and villain blurs until it vanishes entirely. 

The Light Brigade is a triumph of speculative fiction that forces readers to question who truly profits when human lives are reduced to commodities, data, and mere light. It is an urgent, paranoid, and fiercely intelligent novel that demands to be read, absorbed, and debated long after the final page.
