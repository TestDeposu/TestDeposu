# Fables, Vol. 14: Witches

**Author:** Bill Willingham  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

When Bill Willingham’s acclaimed Fables series reaches its fourteenth volume, Witches, the grand mosaic of fairy-tale lore has long ceased to be a simple whimsical reimagining. It has transformed into a sweeping, high-stakes epic where the nursery rhymes of our childhood collide with the grim realities of survival, politics, and ancient magic. Published in 2010, this collection of issues #86-93 arrives at a crucial narrative juncture, following the devastating destruction of Fabletown and the ascent of one of the series' most terrifying antagonists yet: the brooding, enigmatic Mister Dark.

At the core of Witches lies a profound thematic exploration of power, institutional reliance, and generational divides. Throughout the series, Fabletown’s displaced magical beings relied heavily on bureaucratic infrastructure, mundane weapons, and the safe-guarded artifacts of the Woodland Building’s legendary thirteenth floor. With that sanctuary shattered and the business office sealed away, the exiled Fables are stripped of their modern crutches. They are forced to confront a sobering reality: civilization cannot save them; only raw, untamed magic can. This shifts the thematic focus inward, examining how communities organize themselves when forced to look past political diplomacy to the primordial forces that birthed their legends in the first place.

Wingham’s narrative style continues to shine through its masterful juxtaposition of the mundane and the mythical. He possesses a rare talent for making immortal, larger-than-life archetypes feel deeply human, flawed, and exasperatingly petty. As the witches and warlocks step out of the shadows to lead the resistance against Mister Dark, they do not present a united front of enlightened benevolence. Instead, Willingham infuses their coalition with bitter rivalries, ancient grudges, and ideological fractures that threaten to destroy them from within long before Mister Dark strikes a blow. This interpersonal friction grounds the supernatural elements, ensuring the stakes feel immediate and emotionally resonant.

The pacing in Witches is relentless. Willingham cleverly splits the narrative tension, running parallel storylines that heighten the claustrophobia and desperation of the displaced Fables. While the sorcerers grapple with their fragile alliance, those trapped within the business office face a waking nightmare against a legion of uncaged foes. This structural duality allows Willingham to showcase the breadth of his fictional universe, balancing intimate character moments with large-scale, apocalyptic dread. The dialogue remains sharp, witty, and distinctly character-driven, cutting through the dark atmosphere with flashes of the trademark dark humor that has defined the Eisner Award-winning series from its inception.

Ultimately, Fables, Vol. 14: Witches is a masterclass in dark urban fantasy comic writing. It challenges its characters—and its readers—to reconsider the price of power and the fragility of sanctuary. Willingham proves that even in the ashes of defeat, the oldest stories harbor the fiercest sparks of resistance. For both longtime devotees and those following the epic march toward the series' endgame, this volume delivers a mesmerizing, tension-filled chapter that cements Fables as a towering achievement in modern graphic literature.
