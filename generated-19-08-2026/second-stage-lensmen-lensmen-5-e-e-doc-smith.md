# Second Stage Lensmen (Lensmen, #5)

**Author:** E.E. "Doc" Smith  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

Second Stage Lensmen, the fifth installment in E.E. “Doc” Smith’s legendary Lensman series, plunges readers deeper into the galaxy‑spanning conflict between the benevolent Galactic Patrol and the sinister Boskone empire. Kim Kinnison, now the Lensman‑Number‑One, receives his most perilous assignment yet: to become a loyal Boskonian in every thought, gesture, and deed, climbing the alien hierarchy until he stands at the very pinnacle of power—ready to issue the orders that will annihilate his own civilization. The premise is a high‑stakes study of dual identity, loyalty, and the moral cost of espionage, and Smith handles it with the relentless momentum that has defined his space‑opera oeuvre.

At its core, the novel explores the tension between self and role. Kinnison’s infiltration forces him to suppress his Lensman instincts, to think and act as a Boskonian while retaining a hidden core of humanity that guides his ultimate mission. This internal schism drives the narrative forward, turning each encounter with Boskonian officials into a tense chess match where a slip of language or a flicker of empathy could betray him. Smith treats this psychological tug‑of‑war not as a sidebar but as the engine of the plot; every promotion Kinnison earns within Boskone’s ranks is measured not just by strategic gain but by how much of his true self he must bury. The theme resonates beyond the pulp façade: it asks what price a hero pays when he must become the monster he seeks to destroy, and whether the ends can ever justify the means when the means erode the very identity they aim to protect.

Smith’s prose remains unmistakably his own—bursting with grandiose scale, rapid-fire action, and a delightfully earnest belief in the power of intellect and will. He peppers the text with technobabble that feels both archaic and oddly prescient: energy shields, wave‑form weapons, and the ever‑present “lens” that grants its wearer heightened perception and mental fortitude. Yet, rather than bogging the story down in jargon, Smith uses these inventions as colorful props that amplify the sense of a universe where science is both a tool and a manifestation of moral order. His sentences sprint forward, rarely pausing for introspection; instead, the internal conflict surfaces through Kinnison’s terse, decisive thoughts and the stark contrast between his public Boskonian demeanor and his private Lensman resolve.

The pacing is relentless. Chapters often end on a cliffhanger—a whispered order, a sudden ambush, a revelation that shifts the balance of power—propelling the reader forward with the same urgency that drives Kinnison’s ascent. This kinetic rhythm is a hallmark of Smith’s style: he trusts the reader to keep up, rewarding attention with payoff that feels both inevitable and surprising. While some modern readers might find the occasional expositional stretch or the archetypal heroism a touch dated, these elements serve to reinforce the novel’s core ethos: the Lensman is a paragon of virtue operating in a morally ambiguous universe, and his steadfastness is what makes the story compelling.

One of the most striking aspects of Second Stage Lensmen is how Smith manages to make an alien culture feel both exotic and eerily familiar. Boskone’s hierarchy, its rituals, its cold efficiency, and its ruthless ambition mirror the totalitarian regimes of Smith’s era, yet they are filtered through a lens of interstellar weirdness—strange physiologies, alien etiquette, and technologies that defy human intuition. By forcing Kinnison to navigate this world, Smith invites readers to consider how ideology can warp perception, how loyalty can be manufactured, and how the thin veneer of civilization can crack under the pressure of absolute power.

The novel also benefits from its position within the larger Lensman arc. Readers who have followed Kinnison from his early days as a rookie Lensman will recognize the growth in his tactical acumen and the deepening of his philosophical outlook. Second Stage Lensmen does not merely repeat earlier triumphs; it raises the stakes, introduces fresh moral dilemmas, and showcases a protagonist who must now wield influence not just through personal combat but through the subtle art of command and deception. The result is a narrative that feels both a continuation and an evolution—a story that honors the series’ roots while pushing its themes into darker, more intricate territory.

In sum, Second Stage Lensmen delivers a potent blend of high‑octane space opera and psychological intrigue. E.E. “Doc” Smith’s signature style—expansive imagination, brisk pacing, and an unwavering faith in heroic endeavor—serves a narrative that asks what it means to wear a mask for the greater good. Fans of classic sci‑fi will revel in the grandiose battles and inventive gadgetry, while readers interested in the ethics of espionage will find plenty to ponder beneath the glittering surface of interstellar intrigue.
