# Catseye (Dipple, #1)

**Author:** Andre Norton  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

Andre Norton's Catseye is a captivating tale of friendship and rebellion set in a distant future where the lines between humans and animals are blurred. The novel follows Troy Horan, a young man exiled to the planet of Korwar after his home planet was transformed into a military outpost. Living in the Dipple, a restricted area for subcitizens, Troy's life takes a dramatic turn when he discovers his unique ability to communicate silently with animals, particularly a kinkajou.

As Troy navigates the complexities of his newfound power, a murder sets off a chain of events that forces him to flee into the Wild, a mysterious and uncharted region of Korwar. Here, he must rely on his animal friends, including the kinkajou, to survive and unravel the secrets hidden within the sealed ruins that dot the landscape. With the government in pursuit, Troy finds himself at the forefront of a rebellion that will determine the fate of Korwar's subcitizens.

One of the standout aspects of Catseye is Norton's masterful exploration of the bond between humans and animals. Through Troy's telepathic connection with the kinkajou, Norton delves into the depths of their relationship, revealing a profound understanding and trust that transcends species. This theme is woven throughout the narrative, adding a rich layer of emotional depth to the story.

Norton's writing style is equally impressive, with a keen attention to detail that brings the world of Korwar to life. Her prose is descriptive and immersive, making it easy to become fully invested in Troy's journey. The pacing is well-balanced, with a mix of action, suspense, and quiet moments of introspection that keep the reader engaged.

What sets Catseye apart from other science fiction novels is its thought-provoking commentary on the nature of power and control. The government's oppressive regime and the exploitation of subcitizens serve as a stark reminder of the dangers of unchecked authority. Through Troy's rebellion, Norton offers a powerful exploration of resistance and the human (and animal) spirit's capacity for resilience and courage.

In conclusion, Catseye is a gripping and thought-provoking novel that will appeal to fans of science fiction and fantasy alike. Norton's unique blend of action, adventure, and social commentary makes for a compelling read that will leave you eager to explore more of the Dipple series.
