# The City Who Fought (Brainship, #4)

**Author:** Anne McCaffrey  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

When science fiction royalty Anne McCaffrey joins forces with master world-builder S.M. Stirling, the result is nothing short of breathtaking. *The City Who Fought*, the fourth installment in the beloved Brainship series, takes the classic trope of space opera and injects it with a fresh, visceral pulse. Returning to the unique universe where crippled human beings are integrated into the cybernetic cores of spaceships and installations, this novel asks a profound question: What happens when a stationary sanctuary is forced to become a fortress?

At the heart of the narrative is Simeon, a "shell-person" who operates Space Station SSS-900 on the perilous fringes of human space. McCaffrey and Stirling paint Simeon not as a cold, calculating machine, but as a deeply human, somewhat restless soul. Bored with routine maintenance and adjusting to a newly assigned brawn—the fiercely independent and strong-willed Channa Hap—Simeon indulges in his favorite pastime: complex wargaming. It is a brilliant character choice that establishes early on that Simeon’s mind is already attuned to strategy, survival, and defense. When the brutal Kolnari pirates devastate the nearby colony planet of Bethel and refugees flood the station, Simeon's hobby ceases to be a mere game. It becomes the ultimate blueprint for survival.

The central theme of *The City Who Fought* is the transformation of community and commerce into resistance. McCaffrey has always excelled at exploring symbiosis—the emotional and operational bond between a ship and its brawn—and here, that partnership is expanded to an entire space station and its diverse population. The station is no longer just a port of call; it is a living, breathing entity with a conscience, fighting alongside its inhabitants to protect the vulnerable. The transition from peaceful neutrality to active, defiant warfare is handled with gripping pacing and strategic grit, a hallmark of Stirling’s influence on the collaboration. 

Stylistically, the novel is a masterclass in accessible, character-driven military science fiction. While the stakes are galactic and the violence of the Kolnari pirates is unflinchingly portrayed, the authors never lose sight of the human element. The dialogue between Simeon and Channa crackles with sharp wit and growing mutual respect, providing a steady emotional anchor amidst the flashing lasers and tactical maneuvers. The world-building is seamless, dropping readers directly into a lived-in future where technology is intimate, flawed, and profoundly vital.

*The City Who Fought* is a triumphant reminder of why Anne McCaffrey remains one of the most enduring and beloved figures in speculative fiction, and how S.M. Stirling elevates collaborative military sci-fi. It is a rousing, deeply satisfying tale of resilience, partnership, and the unbreakable spirit of those who refuse to be preyed upon. For fans of classic space opera and tactical ingenuity, this classic back-in-print edition is an absolute must-read.
