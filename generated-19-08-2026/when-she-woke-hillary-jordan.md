# When She Woke

**Author:** Hillary Jordan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

Hillary Jordan’s provocative 2012 novel, When She Woke, is a chillingly plausible descent into a near-future America where the fragile barrier between church and state has been entirely obliterated. Winner of the Bellwether Prize, Jordan crafts a feverishly conceived dystopia that invites inevitable, yet entirely earned, comparisons to speculative masterworks like Margaret Atwood’s The Handmaid’s Tale and Nathaniel Hawthorne’s classic The Scarlet Letter. 

At the pulsating heart of the narrative is Hannah Payne, a woman navigating a hostile society that has fundamentally redefined criminal justice. In this grimly reimagined world, prisons have been abolished in favor of "chroming"—a terrifying genetic alteration where a convicted felon's skin color is permanently changed to visually broadcast the severity of their crime. Hannah is a Red, marked indelibly for murder. Thrust back into a judgmental, unforgiving population with no safety net, she must fight simply to survive. 

Jordan’s narrative style is both deeply intimate and unflinchingly tense. She avoids the trap of treating her world merely as an abstract ideological playground; instead, she anchors the high-concept dystopian premise in the visceral, heartbeat-to-heartbeat reality of her protagonist. Through Hannah’s harrowing journey, the author examines the destructive consequences of a nation that weaponizes faith and politicizes morality. The prose is propulsive, urgent, and relentlessly compelling, ensuring that readers devour the pages not just out of shock at the world-building, but out of profound empathy for a complex, magnetic heroine caught in its gears.

As Hannah seeks refuge in a society that views her as an abomination, her struggle transcends mere physical survival. She embarks on a profound journey of self-discovery that forces her to strip away the dogmas she once held sacred and question the righteousness of her oppressors. It is a story about the heavy price of love, the crushing weight of systemic shame, and the arduous, necessary work of personal reclamation. 

Utterly engrossing, politically charged, and terrifyingly prescient, When She Woke is essential reading for anyone who cares about the intersection of autonomy, belief, and power. It is a book designed to provoke fierce debates, making it an absolute must-read for book clubs and a towering achievement in modern speculative fiction.
