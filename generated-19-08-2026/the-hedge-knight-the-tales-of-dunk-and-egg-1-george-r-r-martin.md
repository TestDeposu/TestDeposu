# The Hedge Knight (The Tales of Dunk and Egg, #1)

**Author:** George R.R. Martin  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Long before the War of the Five Kings ravaged the Seven Kingdoms and before the Iron Throne was a perpetual seat of ruthless betrayal, George R.R. Martin invited readers into a Westeros that was quieter, yet no less dangerous. Published in this striking graphic novel edition, The Hedge Knight—the inaugural tale of the beloved Dunk and Egg series—strips away the sprawling geopolitical chess matches of A Game of Thrones to focus on something much rarer in Martin's grim universe: genuine chivalry.

Set during a rare era of fragile peace under King Aerys I, the narrative follows Ser Duncan the Tall, a towering, low-born hedge knight newly minted by his late mentor, and his sharp-tongued, bald young squire known simply as Egg. Their journey to a lucrative local tournament at Ashford Meadow seems like a harmless diversion, offering Dunk a chance at glory, coin, and a hot meal. However, beneath the festive surface of tourney tents and banners, the ghosts of a failed rebellion linger, and Egg carries a royal secret that could alter the fate of the realm if discovered.

The central theme of The Hedge Knight is the very definition and cost of true knighthood. In a world where nobility is often synonymous with cruelty, corruption, and self-preservation, Dunk embodies the romantic ideals of chivalry that most Westeros inhabitants have long since abandoned. He is not a polished lord or a master tactician; he is clumsy, self-doubting, and poor, yet fiercely protective of the innocent. Through Dunk's eyes, Martin interrogates the hollow nature of feudalism and asks a poignant question: What does it mean to be a knight when the system itself is unjust? The dynamic between the towering, well-meaning giant and the precocious, secretly royal squire forms the emotional beating heart of the story, blending witty banter with subtle character growth.

Martin’s signature style shines brilliantly in this format. Even stripped of the dense internal monologues of his traditional prose, his world-building remains unmatched. He populates Westeros with vivid, flawed secondary characters—from arrogant princes to weary veteran knights—who feel intensely human. The dialogue crackles with authenticity, capturing both the high-born arrogance of the Targaryen royalty and the earthy, unpolished vernacular of the common folk. 

This graphic novel adaptation brilliantly complements Martin’s storytelling. The visual medium breathes vibrant new life into the Ashford tourney, capturing the thunderous impact of the jousts, the colorful pageantry of the heraldry, and the stark contrast between the glittering aristocracy and the muddy reality of a hedge knight's life. The pacing is brisk, maintaining the high stakes of a political thriller while never losing sight of the intimate, character-driven charm that makes this duo so enduring.

Ultimately, The Hedge Knight is a triumphant distillation of everything that makes George R.R. Martin’s fantasy writing legendary. It trades apocalyptic stakes for a grounded, deeply human adventure about honor, friendship, and the heavy burden of doing the right thing. Whether you are a die-hard fan of the realm or a newcomer looking for an accessible entry point into Martin's vast universe, this tale of a squire and his knight is an absolute must-read.
