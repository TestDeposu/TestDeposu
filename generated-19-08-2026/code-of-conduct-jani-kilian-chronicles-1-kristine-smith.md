# Code of Conduct (Jani Kilian Chronicles, #1)

**Author:** Kristine Smith  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2024

**Code of Conduct: A Gritty, Emotionally Charged Sci-Fi Thriller That Blends War, Identity, and Redemption**

Kristine Smith’s *Code of Conduct*—the first installment in the *Jani Kilian Chronicles*—is a relentless, morally complex sci-fi thriller that doesn’t just ask *what it means to be human* but forces its protagonist to confront the cost of survival. Set against the backdrop of a fractured interstellar Commonwealth, the novel follows Jani Kilian, a woman who should have died twice—once by execution, once in a crash—but was instead resurrected by a shadowy medical program, leaving her with a body that betrays her and a past she can’t outrun. What emerges is a story as much about the scars of war as it is about the scars of being *fixed*—both physically and psychologically.

At its core, *Code of Conduct* is a tale of **identity fractured and rebuilt**. Jani isn’t just a soldier; she’s a woman who has spent 18 years in hiding, her body stitched together with experimental tech that’s slowly failing her. The novel’s opening pages establish her as a woman who has long since accepted that she’s a liability—both to herself and to those around her. But when her former lover, Interior Minister Evan van Reuter, begs for her help in tracking down his wife’s killer, she’s pulled back into a world she thought she’d left behind. The tension between duty and self-preservation drives the narrative, making Jani a protagonist who is as compelling as she is flawed.

Smith’s writing is **sharp, visceral, and unflinching**, particularly in her depiction of war’s psychological toll. The novel’s opening scene—a brutal, no-holds-barred firefight—sets the tone: this is a story where violence isn’t just physical; it’s existential. Jani’s memories, which begin to resurface as her body deteriorates, are rendered with a haunting clarity, making the reader feel the weight of her guilt and the desperation of her choices. The author doesn’t shy away from the grim realities of military life, the ethics of medical experimentation, or the moral ambiguities of survival. Instead, she weaves them into a narrative that feels both urgent and deeply personal.

The setting—Chicago, the Commonwealth’s political capital—serves as more than just a backdrop; it’s a character in its own right. Smith paints the city as a den of intrigue, where power plays and betrayals unfold with the same ruthlessness as any battlefield. The political maneuvering, the underworld dealings, and the high-stakes conspiracy all contribute to a sense of inevitability—Jani is caught in a web she didn’t ask for, and the question isn’t *if* she’ll get out alive, but *how much of herself she’ll have to lose* to do it.

What makes *Code of Conduct* truly stand out, however, is its **exploration of trauma and redemption**. Jani isn’t a hero; she’s a survivor, and her journey is one of reckoning rather than triumph. The novel doesn’t offer easy answers, nor does it romanticize the choices she’s made. Instead, it forces the reader to sit with the discomfort of a woman who has spent years running from her past, only to realize that the only way forward is to face it. The emotional core of the story lies in Jani’s struggle to reconcile the woman she was with the woman she’s become—one who is both broken and unbreakable.

Smith’s prose is **efficient yet evocative**, with a knack for delivering punchlines and emotional gut-punches in equal measure. Dialogue crackles with tension, and the pacing never falters, even as the plot spirals into deeper layers of conspiracy. The world-building is immersive without being overwhelming, grounding the sci-fi elements in a sense of realism that makes the Commonwealth feel like a lived-in, flawed society rather than a distant fantasy.

The biggest strength of *Code of Conduct* is its **unapologetic focus on female agency and resilience**. Jani is no damsel in distress; she’s a woman who has spent years outsmarting her enemies, only to find that the real battle is the one waged within her own mind. The novel doesn’t shy away from depicting her as capable, ruthless, and deeply human—flaws and all. This makes her one of the most compelling protagonists in recent sci-fi, a woman who is as much a product of her circumstances as she is their architect.

That said, the novel isn’t without its challenges. Some readers may find the political intrigue slightly dense, particularly those who prefer faster-paced action over slow-burning conspiracy. Additionally, the sci-fi elements—while intriguing—are introduced in a way that feels more like a backdrop than a fully realized world. However, these are minor quibbles in an otherwise gripping narrative that rewards patience with its payoffs.

*Code of Conduct* is a book for readers who crave **sci-fi with teeth**, a story that doesn’t just entertain but *haunts*. It’s for those who love morally complex protagonists, high-stakes political thrillers, and narratives that explore the cost of survival. Jani Kilian is a woman who has spent her life running from the past, but *Code of Conduct* proves that sometimes, the only way forward is to finally look back—and face the truth, no matter how painful it is.

In the end, this is a novel that lingers, not just for its action or its world-building, but for the way it forces the reader to confront the question: *How much of yourself are you willing to lose to stay alive?*
