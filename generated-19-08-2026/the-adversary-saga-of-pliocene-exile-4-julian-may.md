# The Adversary (Saga of Pliocene Exile, #4)

**Author:** Julian May  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1984

Step through the dimensional portal into a six-million-year-old Earth, and you will find yourself immersed in one of speculative fiction’s most audacious triumphs. Julian May’s Saga of Pliocene Exile concludes with The Adversary, a grand finale that cements the series as a monumental bridge between hard science fiction and high myth. When May’s eclectic band of human time-travelers—outcasts, dreamers, and rogues escaping a utopian yet stifling 22nd-century Earth—arrived in the Pliocene epoch, they expected refuge. Instead, they walked into a brutal feudal struggle between two warring alien races: the majestic, psychically gifted Tanu and the subterranean, monstrous Firvulag. 

In The Adversary, this clash of civilizations reaches its apocalyptic crescendo. May’s narrative prowess shines brightest in her ability to weave intimate, deeply flawed human drama against a canvas of cosmic scale. The central theme of the novel, and the Pliocene Quartet as a whole, explores the corrupting nature of absolute power and the redemptive power of human will. The alien races, despite their god-like metapsychic abilities, are bound by ancient hatreds and tragic flaws. It is humanity—adaptable, resilient, and morally complex—that becomes the ultimate catalyst for change, forcing both conqueror and conquered to redefine their existence. 

May’s literary style is a masterclass in world-building synthesis. She effortlessly marries the rigorous, clinical wonders of futuristic science—such as the Galactic Milieu and advanced metapsychology—with the poetic resonance of Celtic folklore and Arthurian legend. The prose is dense, richly textured, and unapologetically ambitious, demanding the reader’s full attention yet rewarding them with breathtaking vistas and profound philosophical inquiries. Every character, from the towering rebel Marc Remillard to the tormented human-alien hybrids, is rendered with psychological depth and moral ambiguity. There are no simple heroes or villains here, only survivors navigating the crucible of history.

For those who have journeyed alongside May’s unforgettable ensemble, The Adversary offers a thrilling, emotionally resonant closure to a peerless epic. It stands as a testament to a writer who dared to dream across epochs, creating a universe that continues to rival the greatest achievements of fantasy and science fiction alike.
