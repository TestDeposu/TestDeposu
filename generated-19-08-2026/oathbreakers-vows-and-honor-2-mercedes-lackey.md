# Oathbreakers (Vows and Honor, #2)

**Author:** Mercedes Lackey  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1989

Oathbreakers, the second installment in Mercedes Lackey's Vows and Honor series, is a gripping fantasy novel that delves into the complexities of loyalty, duty, and the unbreakable bonds of friendship. Published in 1989, this book is a testament to Lackey's mastery of the fantasy genre, weaving a tale that is both epic in scope and intimate in character development.

The story begins with the disappearance of Idra, the enigmatic leader of the Sunhawks mercenaries, prompting her loyal companions, Tarma and Kethry, to embark on a perilous journey to uncover the truth behind her vanishing. As they navigate the treacherous landscape of Rethwellan, they must confront their own demons and the dark forces that threaten to destroy the kingdom.

One of the most striking aspects of Oathbreakers is its exploration of the themes of loyalty and duty. Tarma and Kethry's unwavering commitment to Idra and their fellow Sunhawks is a powerful testament to the bonds of friendship and the unshakeable oaths that bind them together. Lackey skillfully examines the complexities of these relationships, revealing the depths of their emotional resonance and the sacrifices that come with upholding one's vows.

Lackey's writing style is, as always, engaging and immersive, with a keen sense of pacing that propels the reader through the narrative. Her world-building is meticulous, with a richly detailed landscape that is both familiar and yet uniquely fantastical. The characters of Tarma and Kethry are, in particular, noteworthy, with their distinct personalities and motivations expertly woven into the fabric of the story.

One of the most impressive aspects of Oathbreakers is its ability to balance action, adventure, and emotional depth. Lackey seamlessly shifts between intense battle scenes, intricate magical rituals, and poignant moments of character introspection, creating a narrative that is both thrilling and emotionally resonant.

If there is a criticism to be made, it is that the book's supporting cast can sometimes feel a little one-dimensional, with some characters serving primarily as plot devices rather than fully fleshed-out individuals. However, this is a minor quibble in an otherwise engaging and immersive narrative.

In conclusion, Oathbreakers is a compelling and engaging fantasy novel that explores the complexities of loyalty, duty, and friendship. Mercedes Lackey's masterful storytelling and immersive world-building make this book a must-read for fans of the genre. With its richly detailed landscape, engaging characters, and epic scope, Oathbreakers is a testament to Lackey's enduring legacy as a fantasy author.
