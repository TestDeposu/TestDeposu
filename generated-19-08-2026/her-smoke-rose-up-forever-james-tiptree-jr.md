# Her Smoke Rose Up Forever

**Author:** James Tiptree Jr.  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

James Tiptree Jr.—the brilliant, fiercely guarded literary persona of pioneering science fiction writer Alice Sheldon—has long cast a towering, enigmatic shadow over speculative fiction. While Tiptree’s classic career retrospective *Her Smoke Rose Up Forever* cemented her legacy as a master of psychological depth and cosmic dread, a treasure trove of her finest work remained scattered or out of print. Enter the essential new volume, *The Voice That Murmurs in the Darkness* (published in 2023 and co-edited by Jeffrey D. Smith and Karen Joy Fowler), which rectifies this omission by drawing directly from a curated list Sheldon herself designated as “the cream of Tiptree.” 

Spanning the breadth of her career from 1968 to 1987, this landmark collection of thirteen exceptional stories and one darkly humorous essay (“How to Have an Absolutely Hilarious Heart Attack”) serves as a vital reminder of why Tiptree remains one of the genre’s most singular voices. The book’s title is pulled from Sheldon’s own haunting description of her pseudonym, and it is an apt metaphor for the haunting, visceral experience of reading her prose. Tiptree writes from the shadowed edges of human consciousness, plumbing the murky depths of desire, trauma, and existence with an unflinching gaze.

At the heart of Tiptree’s enduring style is an intense preoccupation with the fragility of the human condition, set against the vast, indifferent expanse of the cosmos. Her stories are relentlessly daring, blending hard-edged sociological speculation with poetic, hallucinatory surrealism. In “Excursion Fare,” disaster at sea transforms into a surreal voyage aboard the *Charon*, a hospice ship catering exclusively to the dying. Tiptree effortlessly maneuvers between grand adventure and intimate grief, questioning the nature of mortality and the strange journeys we take when all hope of survival is stripped away.

The collection further showcases Tiptree’s astonishing range through its wildly inventive premises. In “The Man Doors Said Hello To,” a surreal urban odyssey unfolds when a mysterious man carrying miniature female tenants in his pockets leads the narrator through the hidden, forgotten corners of a sprawling city. Yet, for all her penchant for the bizarre, Tiptree’s fiction is always deeply grounded in psychological realism and urgent contemporary anxieties. “Time-Sharing Angel” tackles the creeping dread of planetary overpopulation and ecological collapse, introducing an interstellar entity that offers a startling, deceptively simple intervention in the lives of Earth's children. Meanwhile, “Yanqui Doodle” delves into the harrowing psychological fallout of modern warfare, examining a brutal detox treatment designed to purge the conscience of soldiers who have committed atrocities, only to find that confronting one’s own memory can be more agonizing than the trauma itself.

What makes Tiptree’s style so striking—and what editors Smith and Fowler capture so brilliantly in this collection—is her ability to synthesize the political and the deeply personal. Her prose is relentlessly kinetic, shot through with fierce intelligence, biting irony, and a profound, aching empathy for the miraculous oddity of life. She refuses to offer easy moral binaries, choosing instead to interrogate what it truly means to be human in a universe defined by both cruelty and wonder. 

*The Voice That Murmurs in the Darkness* is not merely a gathering of previously overlooked tales; it is a vital expansion of the Tiptree canon. It captures the author at her most inventive, uncompromising, and psychologically astute. For both longtime devotees and newcomers stepping into her orbit for the first time, this collection is an absolute triumph—a mesmerizing dispatch from a brilliant mind whispering secrets from the dark.
