# Yarrow

**Author:** Charles de Lint  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

**Yarrow by Charles de Lint: Where Dreams Bleed into Reality**

Charles de Lint’s *Yarrow* is a spellbinding blend of urban fantasy and lyrical realism, a story that weaves the mundane with the mythic so seamlessly that the line between them dissolves entirely. At its heart, this novel is a meditation on the tension between imagination and reality—a theme as timeless as it is urgent in a world that often dismisses dreams as frivolous or escapist. Through the eyes of Cat Midhir, a weaver of stories who inhabits a liminal world between sleep and waking, de Lint crafts a narrative that is equal parts enchanting and deeply human.

Cat’s existence is one of quiet magic. Each night, she crosses into the Otherworld, a realm where gnomes whisper among standing stones, selkies dance beneath the waves, and the antlered Mynfel roams by moonlight. These are not mere fantasies; they are as real to her as the Ottawa streets she traverses by day. Her books—labeled fantasy by the world outside—are born from these nocturnal journeys, yet they carry the weight of something far more profound. De Lint doesn’t treat the supernatural as a gimmick; instead, he embeds it in the fabric of everyday life, making the extraordinary feel intimate. The result is a world where belief in the unseen isn’t a choice but a necessity, a testament to the author’s belief that magic isn’t separate from reality—it’s woven into it.

What makes *Yarrow* so compelling is its refusal to simplify its themes. De Lint doesn’t shy away from the darker currents beneath the surface. There’s a dark eroticism to the story, a sense of longing and danger that lingers like the scent of autumn leaves. The theft of Cat’s dreams isn’t just a plot device; it’s a metaphor for the loss of imagination, the quiet erosion of wonder in a world that demands pragmatism. The novel explores trust, honor, and the cost of faith—both in others and in oneself. It’s a story about the stories we tell, not just the ones we write, but the ones we live, the ones that shape our identities and our relationships.

De Lint’s prose is a masterclass in atmospheric storytelling. His writing is rich with sensory detail, from the "humdrum streets of the city" to the "standing stones where gnomes hide." He balances the mundane with the mythic effortlessly, creating a prose that feels both grounded and ethereal. The dialogue crackles with authenticity, and the characters—especially Cat—are deeply relatable. She’s flawed, curious, and deeply connected to the world around her, whether it’s the tangible or the intangible. Her journey isn’t just about reclaiming her dreams; it’s about reclaiming her sense of self in a world that often tries to box her in.

The novel’s structure is another of its strengths. De Lint doesn’t rely on clichés or easy resolutions. Instead, he crafts a plot that unfolds with a sense of inevitability, pulling the reader along while leaving room for interpretation. The pacing is perfect—never rushed, never stagnant—holding attention until the very last page. It’s a testament to de Lint’s skill as a fantasist, one who understands that the best stories aren’t just about what happens, but *how* it’s told.

*Yarrow* is more than just a fantasy novel; it’s a celebration of the imagination. It reminds us that dreams aren’t just for children or escapists—they’re the lifeblood of creativity, of connection, of meaning. De Lint’s work here is profound, yet never preachy. It’s a story that invites readers to believe in the possible, to see the magic in the ordinary, and to question what happens when we lose touch with the extraordinary.

In a genre often dominated by spectacle, *Yarrow* stands out for its depth and its humanity. It’s a novel that lingers in the mind long after the final page, a reminder that the best stories are the ones that feel true—whether they’re set in the Otherworld or the world next door.
