# Empire of Ivory (Temeraire, #4)

**Author:** Naomi Novik  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

Empire of Ivory thrusts readers into the desperate heart of a Napoleonic‑era England where the very wings that have kept the kingdom aloft are being ground down by a mysterious plague. Naomi Novik opens the fourth Temeraire novel with a grim tableau: the Aerial Corps, once a glittering armada of dragons and their captains, lies hushed in quarantine, its ranks thinning as an unseen sickness spreads like a silent storm. Only Temeraire, the brilliant Chinese‑born dragon, and his steadfast captain Will Laurence remain uninfected, becoming the last airborne bulwark against Bonaparte’s ever‑more daring sorties.

From this precarious perch, Novik sends Laurence and Temeraire southward to the African continent, a setting that expands the series’ geographical and cultural horizons. The quest for a cure becomes more than a medical scavenger hunt; it is an exploration of empire, trust, and the fragile bonds forged between disparate peoples. As the duo navigates scorching savannas, bustling ports, and wary tribal kingdoms, Novik deftly interweaves historical detail with her signature dragon‑laden fantasy. The prose shines with a lyrical clarity—battle scenes pulse with the thunder of wings and cannon fire, while quieter moments linger on Laurence’s internal conflict between duty to Crown and growing empathy for the African societies he encounters. Temeraire’s voice, both regal and inquisitive, provides a counterpoint that highlights the novel’s central theme: the responsibility that comes with power, whether wielded by a monarch, a dragon, or a nation.

Novik’s style remains grounded in meticulous research, yet she never lets historical accuracy eclipse the wonder of her world. She balances the regimented cadence of military correspondence with the lyrical, almost poetic introspection of her protagonists, allowing readers to feel the weight of each decision as if it were a scale on a dragon’s hide. The disease that ravages the dragons serves as a potent metaphor for the corrosive effects of war and colonial ambition, urging both characters and audience to consider what true healing requires—cooperation, humility, and a willingness to look beyond one’s own borders.

Empire of Ivory is a compelling continuation of the Temeraire saga, marrying high‑stakes adventure with thoughtful commentary on empire and empathy. Fans of alternate history, dragon lore, and richly drawn character dynamics will find themselves soaring alongside Laurence and Temeraire, eager to see whether the cure they seek lies in a vial of rare ivory or in the willingness to forge new alliances across continents.
