# Witch Born (Witch Song, #2)

**Author:** Amber Argyle  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Amber Argyle returns with Witch Born, the compelling second installment in the Witch Song series, plunging readers back into a world where magic demands an agonizing toll and survival is a daily battle. Picking up the threads of a fractured universe, Argyle expands the scope of her dark fantasy landscape, shifting the narrative focus from mere survival to the heavy burdens of leadership, sacrifice, and the haunting ghosts of history.

At the heart of Witch Born lies a profound exploration of empathy as a double-edged sword. Two months after escaping the iron grip of the Black Witch, protagonist Senna finds herself safe within the Sanctuary, yet entirely unshielded from collective agony. Forced to curse the land of Tartennie to achieve victory, Senna now physically and emotionally internalizes the suffering of the earth and its people. Argyle brilliantly uses this premise to examine the psychological weight of power. Magic in this universe is not a glamorous tool for triumph; it is a living, breathing entity that bleeds, aches, and exacts a toll on the soul. Senna is no longer just a gifted apprentice learning to harness her voice; she is a young woman grappling with the moral complexities of wielding a power that intimately connects her to the destruction around her. 

Argyle’s signature style shines through her lyrical yet taut prose. She weaves musical metaphors throughout the narrative, reinforcing the series' core concept that magic is rooted in song. This musicality gives the pacing an organic rhythm, alternating between moments of quiet, haunting introspection and breathless, high-stakes danger. When the Sanctuary—long heralded as an impenetrable safe haven—is breached, the illusion of safety shatters, propelling the plot forward with relentless momentum. 

As Senna defies her Protector, Joshen, to venture into the unknown in search of answers, Argyle deepens the world-building by unearthing the dark, buried history of the witches. This historical excavation serves as the novel's thematic anchor. Argyle questions the narratives handed down by authority figures, forcing her characters—and the reader—to confront uncomfortable truths about the origins of their power. The tension between protection and freedom, obedience and self-determination, drives the emotional core of the story. 

While the romantic undertones with Joshen add a layer of personal vulnerability, it is Senna's fierce independence and her willingness to question the very fabric of her society that truly elevate the narrative. She is a flawed, resilient, and deeply human heroine, making her internal struggles just as riveting as the external threats she faces. 

Witch Born is a masterclass in continuation, deepening the mythology established in the first book while raising the emotional stakes significantly. Amber Argyle has crafted a mesmerizing, atmospheric dark fantasy that will leave readers spellbound, eagerly listening for the next haunting notes of Senna’s journey.
