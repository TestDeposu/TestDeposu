# Entangled

**Author:** Graham Hancock  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

When a master of fringe history and alternative archaeology turns his pen to fiction, the result is nothing short of a metaphysical fever dream. In Entangled (2010), Graham Hancock—renowned for his sprawling, paradigm-challenging nonfiction investigations into lost civilizations—channels his decades of research into a high-stakes, speculative timeslip novel. Though originally envisioned as the explosive opening salvo of a trilogy that sadly never materialized, this standalone journey stands as a fascinating artifact of speculative fiction, fusing academic inquiry with pulse-pounding supernatural horror.

At its thematic core, Entangled explores the fragile boundary between ancient myth and modern reality, driven by the profound and often terrifying nature of human consciousness. Hancock weaves a narrative that spans vast chasms of geography and time, alternating between the neon-lit grit of present-day Los Angeles, the vibrant landscapes of Brazil, and the primordial, unforgiving terrain of Stone Age Spain. The plot hinges on two teenage heroines: Leoni, a troubled modern youth, and Ria, a young woman struggling for survival in prehistoric Europe. These two distinct souls find themselves tethered across millennia in a parallel dimension existing entirely outside the normal flow of time. 

Their mission is as classic as it is daunting: to prevent the total annihilation of the human race at the hands of Sulpa, an unrelentingly evil master magician. Sulpa represents a primordial, bloodthirsty force determined to hijack the materialization of the modern world, turning human evolution into a nightmare of spiritual and physical subjugation. Through this epic confrontation between Leoni, Ria, and their tormentor, Hancock explores the central theme that human history is not a gentle, linear progression, but a volatile battleground of consciousness where ancient evils continually seek to manifest in new forms.

What elevates Entangled above standard young adult or speculative fantasy is Hancock’s signature stylistic integration of hard science and fringe anthropology. Rather than relying solely on fantastical world-building, the author grounds his metaphysical concepts in cutting-edge theories. Readers familiar with his nonfiction will recognize his fascination with the Neanderthal Enigma, altered states of consciousness, out-of-body and near-death experiences, and the many-worlds interpretation of quantum physics. This rigorous intellectual backing gives the fantastical elements a chilling weight of plausibility. Hancock treats magic not as a whimsical parlor trick, but as an ancient, forgotten technology of the mind—a manipulation of quantum realities and parallel realms that feels startlingly tangible.

Stylistically, the novel moves at a breakneck, cinematic pace. Hancock transitions between the primitive desperation of Paleolithic Spain and the sprawling sprawl of modern California with deft assurance, building a mounting sense of dread. Fans of speculative fiction who appreciate the dark, liminal wonder of Neil Gaiman, the terrifying ancient evils found in Stephen King, or the deeply researched historical tapestry of Kate Mosse will find much to admire here. 

While the abrupt termination of the planned trilogy leaves certain cosmic questions lingering in the dark, Entangled remains a solid, fast-moving adventure that rewards the curious reader. It is a thrilling collision of deep-time anthropology and metaphysical dread, proving that Graham Hancock’s imagination is just as potent when untethered from the constraints of the historical record.
