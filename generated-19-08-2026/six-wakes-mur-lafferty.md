# Six Wakes

**Author:** Mur Lafferty  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Murder is rarely a straightforward affair, but in Mur Lafferty’s 2017 Hugo-nominated science fiction triumph, *Six Wakes*, it is rendered utterly dizzying. Imagine waking up naked in a cloning vat, covered in dried blood, with a severe case of amnesia regarding your own violent demise. Now, imagine stepping out of that vat only to discover that your five crewmates have suffered the exact same fate—and that one of the six of you is a brutal, cold-blooded killer. 

Lafferty wastes no time launching the reader into this locked-room mystery set against the cold, indifferent backdrop of deep space aboard the generation ship *Dormire*. The central premise is as brilliant as it is chilling: the crew are cloners, criminals who have traded centuries of penal servitude for centuries of technological immortality. Every twenty-five years, their memories are backed up, their bodies are wiped, and they are reborn into fresh clones to complete their grueling journey to a new home world. But when the entire active crew is simultaneously murdered and rebirthed with wiped memories, the ultimate whodunit begins. Who killed them, and why? More importantly, when the suspects are literally a roomful of your past selves, how do you trust anyone—let alone your own mind?

The heartbeat of *Six Wakes* lies in its exploration of identity, humanity, and the ethics of technological rebirth. Lafferty uses her claustrophobic setting to dissect what truly makes a person human. If your memories can be downloaded, backed up, and printed into a brand-new body, are you the same person who went to sleep? Or are you merely a copy playing a role, burdened by the sins of an earlier iteration? The novel grapples with these heavy existential questions without ever slowing its breakneck pacing. The theme of ownership over one's own body and soul permeates every chapter, as the clones must confront not only the current threat to their lives, but also the dark, secret pasts of their previous incarnations—pasts they didn't ask for, but are forced to answer for.

Stylistically, Lafferty proves herself to be a master of narrative architecture. Crafting a locked-room mystery is difficult enough, but layering it with complex sci-fi world-building and a non-linear timeline makes *Six Wakes* an astonishing technical achievement. The author employs a clever structure of flashbacks that slowly peel back the layers of the crew members' past lives. Through these revelations, we learn why these notorious criminals were on the ship in the first place, turning what starts as a straightforward thriller into a deeply layered psychological drama. 

The prose is sharp, economical, and relentlessly engaging. Lafferty avoids the trap of getting bogged down in overly dense hard-sci-fi jargon, choosing instead to focus on the raw, visceral human reactions to impossible situations. The fear, paranoia, and confusion of the characters are palpable. Every glance is suspicious, every action is weighed for ulterior motives, and every piece of recovered memory shifts the fragile power dynamics among the crew. 

Ultimately, *Six Wakes* is a thrilling collision of Agatha Christie and classic speculative fiction. It is a smart, fast-paced puzzle box that challenges our notions of soul and science while delivering a deeply satisfying mystery. Mur Lafferty has crafted a breakout novel that demands to be devoured in a single sitting, leaving readers breathless, paranoid, and profoundly fascinated by the dark side of immortality.
