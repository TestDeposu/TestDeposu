# CryoBurn (Vorkosigan Saga, #14)

**Author:** Lois McMaster Bujold  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

CryoBurn, the 14th installment in Lois McMaster Bujold's acclaimed Vorkosigan Saga, is a thought-provoking and engaging novel that delves into the complex and timely theme of life extension and the consequences of playing with the fundamental nature of human existence. Set on the planet Kibou-daini, where the cryogenics industry has created a culture of postponing death, the story follows Miles Vorkosigan as he navigates a web of intrigue and deception to unravel the ruthless schemes of the powerful cryogenics corporation.

Bujold's masterful storytelling weaves together a complex plot that is both intellectually stimulating and emotionally resonant. Her writing is characterized by its wit, intelligence, and action, making the novel an enormously satisfying read. The author's ability to balance humor, social commentary, and scientific experimentation creates a unique and captivating narrative that will keep readers engaged until the very end.

One of the standout features of CryoBurn is its exploration of the human condition. Bujold raises important questions about the implications of life extension and the consequences of tampering with the natural order of life and death. Through Miles' character, the author delves into the emotional and psychological complexities of facing mortality and the impact it has on personal relationships and societal structures.

The novel also showcases Bujold's exceptional character development. Miles, a familiar character in the series, is joined by a new cast of characters, including a young boy whose mother was forcibly frozen by the cryogenics industry. The author's ability to craft relatable and nuanced characters is a hallmark of her writing style, and CryoBurn is no exception.

Bujold's writing is also notable for its feminist social commentary. The novel touches on issues of power, control, and the exploitation of vulnerable individuals, particularly women. The author's critique of patriarchal societies and the ways in which they perpetuate inequality is both subtle and powerful, adding depth and complexity to the narrative.

Overall, CryoBurn is a testament to Bujold's skill as a writer and her ability to craft compelling and thought-provoking stories. The novel is a must-read for fans of science fiction, particularly those interested in exploring the implications of emerging technologies on human society. With its engaging plot, well-developed characters, and timely themes, CryoBurn is an outstanding addition to the Vorkosigan Saga.
