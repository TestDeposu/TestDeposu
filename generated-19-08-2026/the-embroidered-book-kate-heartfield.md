# The Embroidered Book

**Author:** Kate Heartfield  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

Title: Stitches in Time and Blood: A Review of Kate Heartfield’s The Embroidered Book

History is written by the victors, but what happens when it is rewritten by magic? In her Aurora Award-winning novel, *The Embroidered Book* (2022), Kate Heartfield takes a familiar and heavily romanticized era—the late eighteenth century of the Enlightenment, opulent courts, and impending revolution—and breathes a dark, dangerous new life into it. Blending meticulous historical research with a visceral, high-stakes magic system, Heartfield crafts a sweeping epic that asks a sharp, feminist question: what does it cost for women in power to actually keep it?

At the beating heart of the novel are two Habsburg princesses: Charlotte, sent to Naples into a loveless political marriage, and Antoine, dispatched to the glittering, suffocating mirrors of Versailles, where history will eventually know her as Marie Antoinette. Long before they were pawns in the grand chess match of European dynastic politics, the sisters stumbled upon a mysterious, forbidden book of spells. These are not the whimsical charms of fairy tales; this is magic that demands a heavy toll. Every invocation requires a sacrifice, a dark exchange that slowly warps the boundaries between the natural and the supernatural, ambition and desperation. 

Heartfield’s greatest triumph in *The Embroidered Book* is her style. Historical fantasy is a notoriously difficult tightrope walk. Lean too hard into the history, and the fantasy feels like an afterthought; lean too hard into the fantasy, and the historical stakes lose their gravity. Heartfield sustains a breathtaking balance. Her prose is richly imagined, brimming with arresting sensory details that make the silk-and-powder decadence of Versailles and the sun-baked politics of Naples palpable. Yet, threaded through the historical names, treaties, and assassinations is an undercurrent of eerie, visceral enchantment that evokes comparisons to Susanna Clarke’s *Jonathan Strange and Mr Norrell*. 

The thematic core of the novel rests on female agency within a rigidly patriarchal cage. In 1768 Europe, a royal woman’s body and life belonged to the state, used merely to secure alliances through marriage. Heartfield uses the magic book as a potent metaphor for power itself. As the synopsis astutely notes, power is not given; it must be taken, and for women, the climb is steeper and the fall much farther. As Charlotte and Antoine navigate vicious court intrigue, their secret use of magic becomes their only equalizer. 

However, Heartfield avoids turning the sisters into one-dimensional heroines. The true emotional anchor of the narrative is the complex, evolving relationship between Charlotte and Antoine. What begins as a fierce, childhood bond of sisterly solidarity gradually curdles into a bitter, dangerous rivalry. As their magical interventions ripple outward—unknowingly pushing a volatile Europe closer and closer to the flames of revolution—their love is tested by jealousy, differing ideologies, and the relentless demands of the spells they cast. Heartfield handles this descent with heartbreaking nuance, making their tragedy feel both grandly epic and devastatingly intimate.

Richly plotted, deeply characterized, and unapologetically dark, *The Embroidered Book* is a triumph of historical feminist fantasy. Heartfield has engineered a seamless collision between the harsh realities of the past and the uncanny possibilities of the occult. It is an absorbing, spellbinding read that will leave readers questioning every historical text they thought they knew, proving that sometimes, the threads of history need a little dark magic to hold together—or to unravel completely.
