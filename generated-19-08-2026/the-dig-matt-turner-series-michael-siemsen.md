# The Dig (Matt Turner Series)

**Author:** Michael Siemsen  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

Michael Siemsen’s breakout success, The Dig, established the Matt Turner series as a formidable force in contemporary psychological thriller fiction, capturing the imaginations of over 130,000 readers. While the main novels thrust us into the complicated, often fractured mind of an adult protagonist, the foundational lore truly takes shape when exploring the psychological roots of his extraordinary gift. Siemsen has carved out a distinct niche in the genre by blending traditional police procedurals with supernatural realism, creating an atmospheric and deeply unsettling narrative style that immediately hooks the reader.

At the core of The Dig and its associated prequel materials is the haunting theme of burden and inheritance—specifically, the heavy toll of psychic ability when weaponized in the pursuit of justice. Matt Turner is not a typical superhero gifted with wondrous powers; he is a psychometrist, cursed with the ability to glean memories and emotional echoes from physical objects. Siemsen explores this ability not as a neat plot device, but as a visceral, psychological trauma. The author peels back the layers of Matt’s psyche to examine how a young mind processes violence, grief, and the suffering of strangers. This central theme investigates the blurred lines between empathy and possession, asking how much of oneself remains when constantly absorbing the final, desperate moments of others.

Siemsen’s literary style is marked by a lean, kinetic urgency. He avoids purple prose in favor of sharp, observational pacing that mirrors the frantic nature of a criminal investigation. When Matt interacts with an object, Siemsen’s writing shifts gears, immersing the reader in sensory-rich flashbacks that feel claustrophobic and intensely real. The dialogue is grounded and naturalistic, anchoring the uncanny elements of the story in a gritty, believable reality. This stylistic contrast—between the cold, procedural methods of police work and the hot, chaotic flashes of psychic intuition—creates a propulsive rhythm that makes the book notoriously difficult to put down. 

Furthermore, Siemsen excels at character dynamics, particularly the complex relationship between a father and son thrust into the darkest corners of human depravity. The emotional stakes are always high because the author makes us care deeply about the collateral damage of crime. By focusing on the vulnerability of youth colliding with profound evil, Siemsen elevates the material from a standard genre thriller into a poignant exploration of loss and protection.

Ultimately, The Dig and the broader Matt Turner saga succeed because Michael Siemsen understands that the most terrifying monsters are human, and the most powerful magic is often just a profound, agonizing connection to the pain of others. It is a striking, fast-paced read that satisfies fans of supernatural mysteries and hard-boiled detectives alike, leaving a lingering chill long after the final page is turned.
