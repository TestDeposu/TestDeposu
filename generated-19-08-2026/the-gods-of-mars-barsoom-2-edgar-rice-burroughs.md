# The Gods of Mars (Barsoom #2)

**Author:** Edgar Rice Burroughs  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

Edgar Rice Burroughs returns to the crimson dust and blistering sands of the Red Planet in The Gods of Mars, the thrilling second installment of his legendary Barsoom series. Picking up where A Princess of Mars left off, this novel plunges the immortal adventurer John Carter back into a world of towering green warriors, perilous beasts, and a relentless quest for true love. Far from resting on the laurels of his initial triumph, Burroughs expands the horizons of his imagination, transforming Mars from a simple dying planet of feuding factions into a sprawling, spiritually complex cosmos ripe for epic exploration.

At the heart of the narrative lies a profound thematic exploration of institutional illusion and the courage required to shatter it. In this volume, Burroughs shifts his focus from terrestrial warfare to the psychological and societal grip of religion. Carter finds himself trapped in the Valley of Dor, the legendary Martian afterlife believed by all native races to be a peaceful paradise from which no traveler returns. However, Carter soon uncovers a terrifying truth: the holy paradise is a monstrous deception orchestrated by the false goddesses known as the Issus and their priesthood, the Therns, who harvest the unsuspecting faithful for sustenance. 

Through this harrowing revelation, Burroughs critiques blind faith and the danger of unquestioned dogma. John Carter acts not merely as a swashbuckling hero, but as an existential liberator. His mission transforms from a personal crusade to rescue his beloved Dejah Thoris into a revolutionary campaign to free an entire planet from the shackles of spiritual tyranny. Burroughs uses the exotic backdrop of Barsoom to examine timeless human flaws, showing how fear and superstition can be weaponized by the powerful against the vulnerable.

Stylistically, Burroughs remains the undisputed master of early twentieth-century planetary romance. His prose is breathless, muscular, and unapologetically romantic, evoking a sense of childlike wonder combined with relentless pulp momentum. The author possesses a rare genius for world-building; with a few strokes of his pen, he conjures sprawling subterranean oceans, terrifying carnivorous flora and fauna, and intricate civilizations with their own complex codes of honor. Every chapter is a masterclass in pacing, ending on cliffhangers that mirror the breathless urgency of serialized fiction, ensuring that readers are constantly propelled forward through the Martian night.

This specific edition enhances the classic text by including a comprehensive glossary and rich contextual material about the lore of Barsoom, offering both nostalgic veterans and new initiates a deeper appreciation of Burroughs’ visionary universe. Complete with an enticing preview of the third installment, Warlord of Mars, this book is an irresistible invitation to adventure.

The Gods of Mars is a triumphant sequel that successfully raises the stakes of its predecessor. By marrying high-octane sword-and-planet action with deeper thematic undercurrents of liberation and truth, Burroughs cements his legacy as the grandfather of modern science fiction and fantasy. It is a dazzling, timeless masterpiece of escapism that proves true heroism lies not just in swinging a sword, but in dismantling the lies that bind the world.
