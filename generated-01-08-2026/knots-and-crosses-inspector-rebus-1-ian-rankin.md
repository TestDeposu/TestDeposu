# Knots and Crosses (Inspector Rebus, #1)

**Author:** Ian Rankin  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

Knots and Crosses, the first installment in Ian Rankin's iconic Inspector Rebus series, is a gripping and atmospheric thriller that sets the tone for the rest of the series. Published in 1987, not 2014, this novel introduces readers to the complex and deeply human detective, John Rebus, as he navigates the dark and gritty streets of Edinburgh.

Rankin's writing style is a masterclass in atmosphere and tension. His vivid descriptions of Edinburgh's streets, alleys, and landmarks transport readers to the heart of Scotland's capital city, immersing them in the sights, sounds, and smells of the city. The author's use of language is economical yet evocative, painting a picture of a city in the grip of fear and uncertainty.

At the center of the novel is Inspector Rebus, a man haunted by his past and struggling to come to terms with his present. A former member of the elite SAS, Rebus is a complex and deeply flawed character, driven by a strong sense of justice and a desire to protect his city from the forces of evil that seek to destroy it. As the body count rises and the investigation unfolds, Rebus finds himself drawn into a dark and twisted game of cat and mouse with a killer who seems to be always one step ahead.

The main theme of Knots and Crosses is the exploration of the human psyche, particularly the darker aspects of human nature. Rankin expertly weaves together a complex narrative that delves into the minds of both the killer and the detective, creating a sense of tension and suspense that is almost palpable. As the novel hurtles towards its shocking conclusion, readers are left questioning the true nature of evil and the motivations of those who commit heinous crimes.

Throughout the novel, Rankin's writing is infused with a sense of gritty realism, reflecting the harsh realities of life in a city plagued by crime and violence. The author's use of dialect and local references adds to the sense of authenticity, creating a sense of place that is both vivid and immersive.

In conclusion, Knots and Crosses is a gripping and atmospheric thriller that sets the tone for the rest of the Inspector Rebus series. With its complex characters, intricate plot, and vivid descriptions of Edinburgh, this novel is a must-read for fans of crime fiction and detective novels. Rankin's writing style is masterful, weaving together a complex narrative that explores the darker aspects of human nature.
