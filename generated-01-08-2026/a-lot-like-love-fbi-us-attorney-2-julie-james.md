# A Lot like Love (FBI/US Attorney, #2)

**Author:** Julie James  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

When it comes to contemporary romantic suspense, few authors execute the formula with the razor-sharp wit and seamless pacing of Julie James. In her 2011 hit novel, A Lot Like Love, the second installment in her acclaimed FBI/US Attorney series, James proves once again why she is a master of the romantic comedy-thriller hybrid. Striking a delicate balance between high-stakes federal investigations and delicious, slow-burn emotional tension, the book delivers a compelling reading experience that captivates from the first page to the last.

At the heart of the novel is a brilliant exploration of the enemies-to-lovers trope, elevated by a dynamic central theme: the collision of two vastly different worlds. On one side sits Jordan Rhodes, a sharp-tongued, self-made billionaire's daughter and boutique wine store owner. On the other is Nick McCall, a relentlessly disciplined, top-tier FBI undercover agent who lives by strict rules and operational precision. When circumstances force Jordan to cooperate with the Bureau to bail out her brother, she is paired with Nick for a high-stakes undercover assignment at a mob-linked charity gala. 

James uses this setup to explore the theme of preconceptions and vulnerability. Both protagonists are fiercely independent, guarded individuals who have built emotional armor—Jordan through her biting sarcasm and Nick through his absolute dedication to protocol. As the mission goes sideways and forces them into close quarters hiding out together, that armor begins to chip away. The true brilliance of the narrative lies in how their romance develops not merely through physical attraction, but through mutual respect. They are intellectual equals who challenge, test, and ultimately balance one another. 

The author’s signature style is defined by snappy, crackling dialogue and brisk, cinematic pacing. James writes with the confidence of someone who has spent time in the legal field, giving her procedural elements a layer of authenticity that grounds the more romantic escapades. There are no bloated descriptions or meandering subplots; every scene serves to advance either the dangerous mob investigation or the simmering chemistry between the leads. The banter between Nick and Jordan is pure literary electricity, sharp enough to cut glass yet dripping with undeniable underlying affection. 

Furthermore, James excels at crafting strong, capable heroines who never need rescuing in the traditional sense. Jordan holds her own against a hardened federal agent, matching him wit for wit and nerve for nerve. Nick, meanwhile, avoids the cliché of the overbearing alpha hero, offering instead a grounded, protective, and deeply attentive partner who appreciates Jordan's strength rather than resenting it.

A Lot Like Love is a triumph of romantic fiction, offering a masterclass in how to blend suspense, humor, and heart. Julie James delivers a striking, thoroughly entertaining page-turner that proves love often strikes when you least expect it, especially when you are knee-deep in an FBI sting operation.
