# Sing Me to Sleep

**Author:** Angela Morrison  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

Can music mend a shattered world? Can the resonance of a drum or the cadence of a poem stitch together the torn fabric of a community ravaged by war? In When Blood and Bones Cry Out, pioneering peace-builder John Paul Lederach and his co-author Angela Jill Lederach present a profound and haunting answer: yes. 

Often, contemporary conversations surrounding global conflict focus strictly on political treaties, ceasefires, and linear models of justice. Yet, the Lederachs pivot away from the conference room and step directly into the lived, breathing reality of trauma. They introduce us to the concept of social healing—an essential, intermediary bridge between active warfare and true reconciliation. Through deeply moving global narratives, the book explores how communities across Liberia, Somalia, Colombia, and Sierra Leone find a way forward not through dry political discourse, but through the primal, transcendent forces of art, metaphor, and sound.

What makes this book extraordinary is the authors' literary and philosophical style. Rather than writing a clinical sociological study, the Lederachs blend rigorous peace-building theory with lyrical storytelling and personal reflection. They argue that trauma lives not just in the mind, but in the body and the collective voice. Therefore, healing must be equally visceral. The prose pulses with the vibrations of Tibetan singing bowls, the enduring lyrics of Van Morrison, and the echoing laments of mothers in West Africa. The authors demonstrate that healing is never a straight line; it is circular, dynamic, and continuous, persisting even against the backdrop of ongoing violence. 

The book is rich with striking, unforgettable images of resilience. We witness a mass women’s protest movement in Liberia singing warlords into submission, elders in Somalia walking perilous miles between warring clans simply to keep dialogue alive, former child soldiers transforming the tools of war into drum workshops, and rape survivors in Sierra Leone channeling their unspeakable pain into poetry. These are not merely stories of survival; they are masterclasses in human dignity.

When Blood and Bones Cry Out is an urgent, deeply compassionate masterpiece that challenges how we view conflict and recovery. It proves that even when blood and bones cry out from the depths of violence, the human voice possesses the miraculous power to sing healing back into the world. This is essential reading for anyone who believes in the defiant, unbreakable beauty of the human spirit.
