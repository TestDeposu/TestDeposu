# The Sunbearer Trials (The Sunbearer Duology, #1)

**Author:** Aiden Thomas  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

**The Sunbearer Trials: A Dazzling, High-Stakes Fantasy Where Light and Luck Collide**

Aiden Thomas, the visionary behind *Cemetery Boys*, has done it again—crafting a Mexican-inspired fantasy that is as electrifying as it is deeply human. *The Sunbearer Trials* is a sun-drenched, mythic competition where power, sacrifice, and identity collide, wrapped in a narrative that feels both timeless and urgently modern. At its core, this book is a story about belonging, the weight of legacy, and the courage to step into the spotlight—even when the stakes are life itself.

The premise is nothing short of breathtaking. Every decade, the Sun God Sol demands a sacrifice to sustain his journey across the sky, keeping the chaotic Obsidian gods at bay. Ten semidioses—half-gods, half-mortals—are chosen to compete in the Sunbearer Trials, where only one will be crowned the bearer of light, and one will be melted into the Sun Stones to fuel the next ten years. But when Teo, a quiet, self-deprecating Jade semidiós, is unexpectedly selected alongside Xio—a young, luckless child of the god of bad luck—he finds himself thrust into a world where his worth has suddenly become a matter of survival. The Gold semidioses, trained from birth for greatness, are the clear favorites, but Teo’s underdog status, paired with his deep-seated fear of failure, makes him the most compelling protagonist in a story where every move could be your last.

Thomas’s writing is a masterclass in atmospheric immersion. The world of *Reino del Sol* is rich with cultural depth, blending Aztec and Mesoamerican mythology with a fresh, vibrant energy. The trials themselves are a feast for the imagination—each one a test of not just physical prowess, but also wit, strategy, and emotional resilience. The first trial, a race through the sacred mountains, sets the tone: it’s not just about speed, but about navigating the land’s hidden dangers, where the earth itself seems to whisper warnings. The later trials escalate in brutality and creativity, from battles against shadowy creatures to trials of endurance that push the competitors to their limits. Thomas doesn’t shy away from the horror of the sacrifice, making the tension palpable. The question isn’t just *who will win*, but *who will be left standing*—and at what cost.

What truly elevates *The Sunbearer Trials* is its exploration of identity and self-worth. Teo, a trans boy whose father is the goddess of birds, carries the weight of being "just a Jade"—a lesser semidiós, unremarkable in a world that values gold and power. His internal monologue is raw and relatable: *"I’m not a real hero."* Yet, as the trials progress, Teo is forced to confront his own potential, his loyalty to his friends, and the fear that has kept him small. His dynamic with Xio, the young and impulsive child of the god of bad luck, is both heartwarming and heartbreaking. Xio’s luck may be cursed, but her spirit is unbreakable, and their friendship becomes the emotional anchor of the story. Meanwhile, Aurelio, the golden-boy semidiós and Teo’s ex-best friend, embodies the toxic ideal of heroism—charismatic, powerful, and utterly ruthless. His presence looms large, not just as a rival, but as a mirror to Teo’s own insecurities.

Thomas’s signature blend of humor and heart shines through, particularly in the banter between Teo and his friends. There’s a warmth to their camaraderie that makes the high-stakes world feel intimate. Even in the darkest moments, there’s a spark of levity, a reminder that these characters are more than just pawns in a divine game—they are people, flawed and fighting for their lives.

Themes of sacrifice and legacy are woven throughout, but what makes this story resonate is its refusal to glorify the trials. The winner is celebrated, but the loser is erased—literally. This moral ambiguity forces readers to question what it truly means to be a hero. Is it fame? Is it survival? Or is it the courage to stand up for those you love, even when the odds are against you?

Visually, *The Sunbearer Trials* is a feast for the senses. The descriptions of the sun-drenched temples, the glowing Sun Stones, and the eerie Obsidian gods create a world that feels both ancient and alive. The action sequences are thrilling, with a rhythm that keeps readers on the edge of their seats, while the emotional beats land with precision. Thomas’s ability to balance mythic grandeur with intimate character moments is what makes this book so compelling.

For fans of *Cemetery Boys*, there’s a sense of familiarity in Thomas’s storytelling—his knack for creating complex, queer characters and his willingness to tackle heavy themes with tenderness. But *The Sunbearer Trials* is its own beast, a standalone fantasy that stands proudly in the duology’s shadow. It’s a story about rising to the occasion, about finding strength in unexpected places, and about the light that can be found even in the darkest sacrifices.

In the end, *The Sunbearer Trials* is more than just a competition—it’s a celebration of resilience, a meditation on what it means to be chosen, and a reminder that even the smallest sparks can ignite the brightest flames. It’s a book that will leave you breathless, cheering for the underdogs and questioning the cost of glory.
