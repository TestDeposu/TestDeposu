# The Black Unicorn (Magic Kingdom of Landover, #2)

**Author:** Terry Brooks  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Step through the mists of the Sterling Silver and into a realm where corporate logic collides head-on with archaic myth. In The Black Unicorn, Terry Brooks returns to the vibrant, idiosyncratic world of Landover in a sophomore outing that tests the mettle of its reluctant monarch, Ben Holiday. Once a cynical Chicago trial lawyer, Ben has traded the sterile stress of the courtroom for a crumbling fairy-tale kingdom plagued by scheming barons, eccentric sorcery, and bureaucratic nightmares. Brooks uses this second installment to deepen the exploration of his central theme: the heavy, often unglamorous burden of true leadership and the constant struggle to preserve magic in a pragmatic universe.

What sets Brooks apart in the crowded landscape of fantasy fiction is his signature style—a seamless blend of high-stakes heroic adventure and disarming, down-to-earth humor. Unlike traditional fantasy protagonists who are born to the crown or gifted with innate mystical destiny, Ben Holiday is an everyday mortal armed with little more than common sense, stubborn determination, and a deep-seated refusal to quit. Brooks writes with a brisk, accessible pacing that turns potential political tedium into high fantasy entertainment. He balances grand, sweeping descriptions of enchanted forests and otherworldly domains with witty, snappy dialogue among a ragtag ensemble of unforgettable characters. 

The narrative of The Black Unicorn hinges on a perilous quest involving the titular mythical beast, testing not only Ben's physical endurance but his psychological resilience. Brooks expertly highlights the psychological toll of kingship. Ben is constantly reminded that ruling Landover requires more than just wielding a magic sword; it demands diplomacy, emotional vulnerability, and the courage to trust an unconventional support system. Speaking of allies, Brooks shines brightest when breathing life into his eccentric supporting cast. From the bumbling, self-doubting wizard Questor Thews to the sharp-tongued, canine court scribe Abernathy, and the enigmatic, arboreal beauty Willow, these characters inject genuine heart and comedic relief into the unfolding peril. 

The author’s thematic brilliance lies in how he grounds fantastic elements in deeply human dilemmas. The black unicorn of the title serves as a potent symbol of elusive desires and the unintended consequences of chasing dreams without understanding their true cost. Brooks reminds the reader that escape is rarely a permanent solution; rather, true fulfillment comes from rolling up one's sleeves and fixing the mess you inherit. The obstacles Ben faces are physical manifestations of his own doubts and the kingdom's lingering distrust of a human interloper. 

For fans of portal fantasy and whimsical world-building, The Black Unicorn delivers a thoroughly engaging theatrical experience. Terry Brooks proves that the magic of Landover is not merely in its spells and fantastical creatures, but in the enduring resilience of the human spirit when faced with the impossible. It is a striking, fast-paced, and ultimately rewarding journey that captures the very essence of why we read fantasy in the first place—to escape into our dreams, only to wake up better equipped to handle our own realities.
