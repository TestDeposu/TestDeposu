# Dictionary of the Khazars

**Author:** Milorad Pavić  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

To open Milorad Pavić’s Dictionary of the Khazars is to step into a labyrinth where time dissolves, geography becomes fluid, and the act of reading is transformed into an archaeological excavation of the soul. Though the specific edition at hand carries the resonance of a recent 2021 reissue, the book remains a timeless monument to postmodern literary craftsmanship—a shimmering, multifaceted artifact that defies traditional narrative structure and invites the reader to become an active co-creator of its myths.

At the heart of this mesmerizing work lies a brilliant framing device, one that immediately captures the imagination: a maritime box, once used by sailors to safeguard compasses, pistols, and journals, is repurposed here as a vessel for storytelling. Passing from hand to hand across the cultural fault lines of Europe and the Balkans—stretching from Paris and Budapest to Salonique, Trieste, and the ancient Adriatic shores of Kotor—the box becomes a projection of the author’s own speculative genius. Inside its compartments lie secrets worthy of The Thousand and One Nights, blending love, mythology, and the heavy shadows of history into an intoxicating elixir.

Pavić’s narrative architecture is famously unorthodox. Eschewing a linear plot, the novel unfolds as a lexicon, a dictionary composed of cross-referenced entries that can be read in any direction. This structural audacity mirrors the book's central theme: the elusive search for the truth behind the Khazars, a vanished medieval people who famously debated whether to adopt Christianity, Islam, or Judaism, ultimately disappearing into the folds of history. Within this framework, Pavić weaves striking, esoteric inventions—such as the love clepsydra and the erotic alarm clock—alongside deeply human vignettes of longing and survival. We encounter a forced-conscription Serbian soldier desperately seeking a technique to forget his native tongue during the turbulent wars of ex-Yougoslavie, and two sisters trading dangerous secrets to deceive their husbands, embarking on metaphysical journeys across Europe fueled by equal parts eroticism and gastronomy.

The author’s style is nothing short of vertiginous. Pavić writes with a gourmand’s appetite for sensory detail, saturating his prose with exotic perfumes, ancient legends, and philosophical inquiry. He makes disparate languages converse, history bleed into myth, and physical space collapse entirely. Reading Dictionary of the Khazars is not a passive experience; it is an intoxicating sensory immersion. Pavić challenges us to dismantle our preconceptions of how a novel should function, offering in return a dazzling literary mosaic where every entry unlocks a new chamber of the human heart and the infinite mysteries of the past.
