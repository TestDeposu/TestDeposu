# The Devils (The Devils, #1)

**Author:** Joe Abercrombie  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

Joe Abercrombie does not merely write fantasy; he takes the genre’s most cherished tropes, drags them through the mud, beats them senseless, and somehow leaves them looking brilliantly alive. With The Devils, the master of grimdark fantasy launches a brand-new series that promises to be every bit as visceral, cynical, and impossibly entertaining as his celebrated First Law universe. Landing with the weight of an instant New York Times bestseller, this opening salvo proves that Abercrombie’s signature blend of razor-sharp wit and uncompromising brutality remains entirely unmatched.

At the heart of The Devils lies a theme as old as storytelling itself, yet twisted in that distinct, cynical Abercrombie way: the slippery slope of holy necessity. The narrative centers on Brother Diaz, a man who believes his pious devotion has earned him a comfortable commendation in the Holy City. Instead, he is handed a suicide mission and a flock of absolute degenerates. His companions are not noble knights or shining paragons of virtue, but a notorious band of unrepentant murderers, practitioners of ghastly magic, and outright monsters. 

This dynamic drives the book’s central exploration of morality, hypocrisy, and the dirty mechanics of power. Abercrombie loves to interrogate the space between holy work and unholy deeds. Through Diaz and his wretched entourage, the novel asks a compelling, uncomfortable question: how much evil are we willing to unleash in the name of the greater good? When elves lurk at the borders hungering for human flesh and greedy princes care for nothing but their own ambitions, righteousness becomes a luxury no one can afford. The line separating the saviors from the monsters dissolves entirely, leaving only blood, mud, and survival. 

Stylistically, Abercrombie is at the absolute peak of his powers here. His prose is lean, punchy, and effortlessly cinematic, moving at a breakneck pace that refuses to let the reader catch their breath. What elevates Abercrombie above his peers is his peerless ear for dialogue and character voice. Even the most horrific creatures and remorseless killers are rendered with a vibrant, cynical humanity that makes them compulsively readable. You will find yourself rooting for scoundrels, wincing at the squalor, and laughing out loud at the gallows humor that permeates every chapter. 

The physical presentation of the book matches the savage grandeur of its contents—featuring gorgeous illustrated endpapers, a striking foil case stamp, and full-page interior illustrations that make this edition a collector’s dream. But it is the sheer kinetic energy of the storytelling that keeps the pages turning. 

The Devils is a delightfully bloody, raucous, and unapologetically dark journey into the abyss. Abercrombie takes a band of outcasts and crafts a masterclass in anti-hero fantasy, proving once again that when the world is going to hell, you want the worst possible bastards on your side. If this explosive first volume is any indication, the road ahead is going to be gloriously treacherous.
