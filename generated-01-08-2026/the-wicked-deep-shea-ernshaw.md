# The Wicked Deep

**Author:** Shea Ernshaw  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

The Wicked Deep pulls readers into the fog‑cloaked harbor town of Sparrow, where the past refuses to stay buried. Two centuries ago three sisters were drowned for alleged witchcraft, and each summer they return, slipping into the bodies of vulnerable girls to exact a vengeful tide on any boy foolish enough to wander near the water. Seventeen‑year‑old Penny Talbot has learned to live with the curse, accepting the ritual as an inevitable part of her world—until Bo Carter, an outsider unaware of the danger, arrives on the eve of the sisters’ resurgence. What follows is a tense dance of suspicion, secrecy, and seductive peril as Penny and Bo circle each other, each wondering whether the other hides the key to breaking the cycle—or feeding it.

At its heart, the novel explores the corrosive power of revenge and the thin line between hatred and love. Ernshaw treats the sisters’ wrath not as a simple monster tale but as a manifestation of inherited trauma, a curse that binds the living to the sins of the dead. Love, in this story, is both a potential antidote and a dangerous lure; it can soften the edges of vengeance yet also become the very bait that pulls the unwary deeper into the abyss. The author’s prose is lush and atmospheric, dripping with salty brine and moon‑lit mist. She weaves lyrical descriptions that make the sea feel like a character in its own right—hungry, ancient, and forever whispering promises of both death and redemption. The pacing mirrors the tides: slow, inexorable pulls of dread interrupted by sudden, shocking surges of violence that leave the reader gasping.

Ernshaw’s skill lies in balancing gothic horror with a delicate, almost fairy‑tale sensibility. Her dialogue is crisp, revealing character motivations with subtle nuance, while her internal monologues—Penny’s in particular—are rich with introspection and a quiet, aching yearning for agency in a town that seeks to dictate every soul’s fate. The result is a haunting, immersive read that lingers like the echo of a siren’s song long after the final page.
