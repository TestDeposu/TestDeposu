# The Dragon and the George (Dragon Knight, #1)

**Author:** Gordon R. Dickson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

Step into a world where twentieth-century academic pragmatism collides head-on with medieval absurdity, and you will begin to understand the delightful chaos of Gordon R. Dickson’s 2013 classic, The Dragon and the George. Originally published in 1976 and enduring as a beloved cornerstone of fantasy literature, this novel kicks off the celebrated Dragon Knight series with a premise so delightfully eccentric that it immediately demands the reader's attention. When twentieth-century graduate student Jim Eckert and his fiancée Angie are unexpectedly transported to a parallel, magic-infused realm, Jim suffers a rather drastic ontological upgrade—or downgrade, depending on who you ask—and finds himself trapped in the scaly, emerald-green body of a dragon named Gorbash. Meanwhile, Angie remains frustratingly human and promptly gets kidnapped by an evil dragon and locked away in the impenetrable Loathly Tower. What follows is a whimsical, high-stakes rescue mission that subverts every traditional trope of chivalric romance.

At the heart of the novel lies a brilliant and engaging thematic exploration: the friction between rigid logic and the fluid, unpredictable nature of magic. Dickson uses Jim Eckert’s analytical, academic mindset as a brilliant narrative anchor. In a land where spells operate on belief and metaphor rather than scientific principles, Jim’s attempts to apply human rationality to magical phenomena provide endless amusement and intellectual depth. The core theme examines how adaptability triumphs over dogmatism. Jim cannot survive or rescue Angie by relying solely on brute dragon-strength or modern skepticism; he must learn to think like a creature of fantasy, understanding that in this strange ecosystem, magic is its own form of physics. This juxtaposition of the mundane modern world with medieval myth creates a satirical yet deeply affectionate homage to Arthurian legends. 

Dickson’s authorial style is a masterclass in witty, fast-paced world-building and character-driven humor. He writes with a light, conversational touch that makes complex magical rules feel intuitive and deeply entertaining. Rather than bogging the narrative down with dense exposition, Dickson lets the mechanics of this alternate universe unfold through snappy dialogue and the endearing friction between Jim and his reluctant allies—most notably the cowardly knight Sir Brian of Galloway and a delightfully arrogant dragon named Aragh. The prose is sparkling with ironic wit, constantly poking fun at the self-serious nature of traditional fantasy while simultaneously delivering a genuinely thrilling adventure. Dickson balances the internal, psychological transformation of a man learning to inhabit a non-human body with external peril, ensuring that the stakes always feel real despite the comedic tone.

The Dragon and the George is a triumph of inventive fantasy that rewards both newcomers and veteran readers of the genre. It captures the pure joy of speculative fiction: taking an absurd, high-concept idea and executing it with absolute conviction, charm, and heart. Gordon R. Dickson has crafted a timeless portal fantasy that reminds us that sometimes, to save the day, you have to embrace your inner monster and look at the world from a completely different scale. Whether you are looking for clever satire, lovable characters, or a heartwarming tale of devotion across species lines, this novel is an absolute must-read that sets a high-water mark for comedic fantasy.
