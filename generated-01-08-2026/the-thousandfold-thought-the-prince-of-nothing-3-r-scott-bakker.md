# The Thousandfold Thought (The Prince of Nothing, #3)

**Author:** R. Scott Bakker  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2008

In "The Thousandfold Thought", the third installment of R. Scott Bakker's "The Prince of Nothing" trilogy, the stage is set for a monumental conclusion that will leave readers questioning the very fabric of reality. This epic fantasy is as much a philosophical treatise as it is a thrilling tale of war and politics, weaving together complex moral dilemmas that challenge the reader to confront the darkest aspects of human nature.

Bakker's meticulous world-building is a hallmark of this series, and "The Thousandfold Thought" does not disappoint. The intricately crafted world of Eärwa is a rich tapestry of cultures, ideologies, and histories, each one expertly interwoven to create a narrative that is at once sweeping and intimate. From the imperial politics of the Consult to the mysticism of the Schools, Bakker's world is a vast and wondrous place, full of hidden patterns and unseen machinations.

But what truly sets Bakker's work apart is his unflinching examination of the human condition. Like a razor, he slices through the conventions of traditional fantasy, laying bare the brutal realities of power, politics, and identity. The characters in "The Thousandfold Thought" are not heroes or villains in the classical sense; they are complex, multidimensional beings that embody the contradictions and paradoxes of human experience.

At the center of this maelstrom is Anasurimbor Kellhus, the enigmatic Warrior-Prophet who has captivated readers with his mysterious allure. Kellhus is a master strategist and a formidable leader, but he is also a deeply conflicted individual torn between his own ambition and a growing sense of unease about the nature of his existence. As the Holy War reaches its climax, Kellhus must confront the darkness within himself and the secrets of his past that threaten to destroy everything he holds dear.

One of the most striking aspects of Bakker's writing is his willingness to blur the lines between good and evil. The Fanim, once the clear villains of the series, are revealed to be complex, nuanced characters with their own motivations and justifications. Similarly, the Consult, the supposed embodiment of evil, is shown to be a far more complex entity, driven by a twisted form of altruism that challenges the reader to question their assumptions about the nature of morality.

This is, of course, the hallmark of philosophical fantasy: the ability to pose profound questions about the human condition and the nature of reality. Bakker's work is infused with a deep sense of skepticism, a willingness to challenge the reader's assumptions and to offer no easy answers. This is a series that asks more questions than it provides solutions, and "The Thousandfold Thought" is no exception.

Throughout the book, Bakker's prose is characterized by its vivid, cinematic style, which brings the world of Eärwa to life with a richness and detail that is simply breathtaking. From the brutal battles of war-torn Shimeh to the mystical landscapes of the Consult's hidden world, every setting is meticulously crafted to evoke a sense of wonder and awe.

In conclusion, "The Thousandfold Thought" is a monumental conclusion to the "Prince of Nothing" trilogy, a fantasy epic that challenges readers to confront the darkest aspects of human nature. Bakker's unflinching examination of power, politics, and identity is a work of masterful prose that will leave readers questioning the very fabric of reality. If you're ready for a journey that will leave you breathless, questioning, and transformed, then "The Thousandfold Thought" is the book for you.
