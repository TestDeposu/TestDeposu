# Wise Children

**Author:** Angela Carter  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

Wise Children, Angela Carter's final novel, is a mesmerizing tale of love, family, and the magic of show business. Written in Carter's signature style, this book is a masterful blend of humor, drama, and wit, with a dash of Shakespearean flair. The story revolves around the lives of twin chorus girls, Nora and Dora Chance, who are celebrating their 75th birthday in Brixton, while their father, the illustrious Melchior Hazard, marks his 100th birthday in Chelsea.

Through the eyes of Dora, the older and more world-weary twin, we are transported to a bygone era of music halls, theater, and spectacle. Carter's writing is a joy to behold, as she weaves a rich tapestry of characters, each with their own secrets, scandals, and stories to tell. The twins' lives are a testament to the power of sisterly love, as they navigate the ups and downs of show business, men, and family dynamics.

At its core, Wise Children is a celebration of the human spirit, in all its complexity and messiness. Carter's characters are multidimensional and relatable, with all their flaws and imperfections on full display. The novel is also a love letter to the world of theater and performance, with its own unique language, customs, and traditions. The author's use of language is playful, inventive, and evocative, conjuring up the sights, sounds, and smells of a bygone era.

One of the novel's greatest strengths is its exploration of the tensions between high art and low culture. Melchior Hazard, the twins' father, is a renowned Shakespearean actor, while Nora and Dora make their living in the more populist world of music halls and variety shows. Carter delights in blurring these boundaries, showing how the two worlds intersect and influence each other.

The novel's themes of identity, family, and belonging are timeless and universal. The twins' quest to uncover the truth about their parentage and their place in the world is a powerful metaphor for the human search for meaning and connection. Carter's writing is both poignant and hilarious, often at the same time, as she tackles the complexities of family dynamics, love, and relationships.

In Wise Children, Angela Carter has created a true masterpiece, a novel that will appeal to fans of theater, music, and literature. It is a testament to the power of storytelling and the human imagination, a celebration of the beauty and diversity of human experience. This is a book that will make you laugh, cry, and cheer, often on the same page.
