# I Who Have Never Known Men

**Author:** Jacqueline Harpman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

I Who Have Never Known Men, a haunting post-apocalyptic novel by Jacqueline Harpman, is a masterful exploration of female friendship, intimacy, and the unyielding human spirit. This modern classic, first published in 1995 and now re-released in 2022, is a testament to the power of speculative fiction in illuminating the darkest corners of human experience.

The story takes place in a desolate underground world where thirty-nine women are held captive in a cage, their memories of the past shrouded in mystery. The arrival of a young girl, the fortieth prisoner, sets in motion a chain of events that will ultimately lead to their escape and a perilous journey into the unknown. As they navigate the treacherous landscape above ground, the women must confront the harsh realities of a world ravaged by devastation and discover the true meaning of resilience, hope, and humanity.

Harpman's writing style is characterized by a lyrical, poetic prose that belies the darkness of the subject matter. Her use of language is deliberate and evocative, conjuring a sense of claustrophobia and disorientation that perfectly captures the women's underground existence. As the story unfolds, the prose expands, reflecting the women's growing awareness of their surroundings and their own identities. The result is a narrative that is both deeply personal and profoundly universal, speaking to the experiences of women everywhere.

One of the most striking aspects of the novel is its exploration of female relationships and the ways in which women support and sustain each other in the face of adversity. The bonds that form between the women are forged in the fire of their shared struggles, and it is these relationships that ultimately provide the key to their survival. Harpman's portrayal of female intimacy is nuanced and multifaceted, revealing the complex web of emotions, desires, and needs that underpin human connection.

The novel is also a powerful exploration of the human condition, raising fundamental questions about the nature of identity, community, and existence. In a world without men, the women are free to redefine themselves and their place in the world, untethered from the societal expectations and norms that have traditionally governed their lives. This freedom is both exhilarating and terrifying, as the women must navigate the uncharted territory of their own desires, fears, and aspirations.

Harpman's background as a psychoanalyst is evident in her sensitive and insightful portrayal of the human psyche. Her characters are richly drawn and multidimensional, with a depth and complexity that is rare in speculative fiction. The novel is also informed by her experiences as a refugee during World War II, lending a sense of urgency and authenticity to the narrative.

In conclusion, I Who Have Never Known Men is a masterpiece of speculative fiction that will appeal to fans of Ursula K. LeGuin, Margaret Atwood, and Cormac McCarthy. Harpman's writing is a testament to the power of language to evoke, to inspire, and to transform. This novel is a must-read for anyone interested in feminist literature, post-apocalyptic fiction, and the human condition. With its haunting prose, nuanced characterization, and profound themes, I Who Have Never Known Men is a novel that will linger in the reader's mind long after the final page is turned.
