# Green River, Running Red

**Author:** Ann Rule  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2004

In Green River, Running Red, Ann Rule masterfully weaves a haunting and suspenseful narrative that delves into the darkest recesses of human nature, exploring the nearly twenty-year-long search for America's most prolific and horrifying serial killer. The book is a gripping and thought-provoking account of the Green River Killer's reign of terror, which began with the discovery of eighteen-year-old Wendy Coffield's body floating near the sandy shore of Washington's Green River on July 15, 1982.

With her signature riveting prose and in-depth research, Rule takes us behind the scenes of the investigation, revealing the meticulous and often frustrating efforts of the authorities to catch the killer. The author's writing style is characterized by a keen eye for detail and a deep understanding of the complexities of the human psyche, making the book a fascinating and unsettling read. Rule's ability to balance the grim reality of the crimes with a thoughtful and compassionate approach to the victims and their families is a testament to her skill as a writer and her commitment to telling the story with sensitivity and respect.

One of the most striking aspects of the book is Rule's personal connection to the case. As a true crime writer, she had been following the investigation and even attended book signings where the killer was in attendance, unaware of his true identity. This chilling realization adds a layer of depth to the narrative, highlighting the blurred lines between the perpetrator and the community, and the ease with which evil can hide in plain sight. The fact that Rule sought the help of incarcerated serial killer Ted Bundy to gain insight into the mind of the Green River Killer is a further testament to her dedication to understanding the motivations and actions of these heinous individuals.

Throughout the book, Rule raises important questions about the nature of evil, the flaws in the justice system, and the impact of trauma on individuals and communities. Her exploration of the social and economic factors that contributed to the vulnerability of the victims is particularly poignant, highlighting the need for greater awareness and support for those at risk. The book is a powerful reminder of the importance of empathy, compassion, and justice, and the need for continued efforts to prevent such atrocities from happening in the future.

In conclusion, Green River, Running Red is a masterful and thought-provoking book that is both a gripping true crime narrative and a nuanced exploration of the human condition. Ann Rule's writing is engaging, informative, and deeply respectful of the victims and their families, making the book a must-read for anyone interested in true crime, psychology, or social justice. With its unique blend of meticulous research, personal insight, and compelling storytelling, Green River, Running Red is a book that will stay with readers long after they finish reading it.
