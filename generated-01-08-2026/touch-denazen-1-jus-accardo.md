# Touch (Denazen, #1)

**Author:** Jus Accardo  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

When seventeen-year-old adrenaline junkie Deznee Cross fishes a mysterious, ice-eyed boy out of a river embankment, she thinks she has simply found the ultimate weapon to irritate her overbearing father. But as Kale struggles with the foreign concepts of DVDs, wears shoes in the shower, and acts as though a single brush of skin will turn Dez to dust, it becomes chillingly clear that he belongs to a world far beyond suburban rebellion. Kale is a prisoner of the shadowy Denazen Corporation, an organization dedicated to weaponizing "special" youths known as Sixes. Oh, and his touch? It is utterly lethal.

With this high-concept premise, Jus Accardo launches the Denazen series, delivering a fast-paced young adult sci-fi thriller that pulses with kinetic energy. At its core, Touch explores the harrowing theme of agency versus ownership. Kale has spent his entire existence as a caged asset, viewed not as a human being, but as a biological instrument of war. His deadly touch is both a literal and metaphorical barrier to connection, symbolizing the supreme isolation of those who are exploited for their differences. In stark contrast stands Dez, a girl whose reckless craving for danger masks a deeply buried secret of her own. When their paths collide, the narrative transforms into an exploration of trust, defiance, and the terrifying vulnerability of letting someone close when closeness could mean destruction. 

Accardo’s style is unapologetically commercial, lean, and relentlessly paced. She favors snappy dialogue and cinematic action sequences over dense world-building, plunging the reader immediately into the breathless panic of flight and survival. The chemistry between Dez and Kale sizzles with the classic YA friction of opposites attracting under impossible pressure. Dez's brash, protective exterior nicely balances Kale's wide-eyed bewilderment and lethal burden, creating an emotional anchor amidst the corporate espionage and supernatural stakes. 

While the novel leans into familiar tropes of the dystopian genre—the shadowy corporation, the superpowered teens on the run, and the paternal betrayal—Accardo infuses the narrative with enough heart and snappy momentum to keep the pages turning rapidly. Touch does not reinvent the wheel of speculative YA fiction, but it executes its formula with an undeniable flair that will leave readers hungry for the next installment. It is an entertaining, high-voltage thrill ride about finding your own power in a world designed to strip it away.
