# Tully

**Author:** Paullina Simons  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

Paullina Simons is a master of emotional excavation, and her stunning debut novel, Tully, stands as a testament to the raw, unfiltered complexities of female friendship and resilience. Long before she captured international acclaim with *The Bronze Horseman*, Simons laid the groundwork for her signature style here: a potent blend of sweeping narrative scope, relentless character development, and a fearless willingness to put her protagonists through the absolute wringer of human experience. 

At the heart of the novel is Tully Makker, a tough, hardened young woman hailing from the wrong side of the tracks. Tully is not your conventional heroine; she is prickly, guarded, and occasionally difficult to like, carrying the heavy baggage of a tumultuous childhood. Yet, it is precisely her gritty authenticity that makes her unforgettable. Simons crafts Tully with such psychological depth that her fierce loyalty and capacity for profound love feel entirely earned. When Tully forms an enduring bond with Jennifer and Julie—two schoolfriends from vastly different socioeconomic backgrounds—the dynamic immediately anchors the narrative in the universal truths of youth, belonging, and the fierce tribes we choose for ourselves.

Simons’s authorial style is defined by emotional maximalism. She does not merely tell a story; she envelops the reader in the texture of an era. Set against the vibrant backdrop of the 1970s and 1980s, the novel pulses with nostalgia, tracking the trio as they navigate the perilous transition from girlhood into the bewildering terrain of young adulthood. The prose is vivid, empathetic, and relentlessly paced. Simons expertly balances the mundane joys of youth with the looming shadows of tragedy, particularly when two young men, Robin and Jack, enter the fold and irrevocably alter the course of all three women's lives. 

The central theme of Tully is the duality of fate and human endurance. Simons explores how the past clings to us, shaping our reactions to trauma, betrayal, and love. Just as Tully manages to outrun her origins, emerging into a stable marriage and a promising career with life seemingly under control, Simons pulls the rug out from under both her protagonist and the reader. It is a masterclass in narrative tension, reminding us that life can strike back in the most unexpected and devastating ways. 

Prepare to have your heart wrung out. Simons evokes the sheer turbulence of youth—moments of dazzling joy juxtaposed with crushing despair—in a way that forces you to reflect on your own deepest bonds. Tully is not an easy read, nor is it meant to be. It is an emotional marathon that lingers long after the final page is turned, celebrating the unbreakable, messy, and glorious resilience of the human spirit.
