# The Paper Girl of Paris

**Author:** Jordyn Taylor  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

**The Paper Girl of Paris: A Love Letter to Paris, History, and the Unspoken Truths We Carry**

Jordyn Taylor’s *The Paper Girl of Paris* is a novel that unfurls like a carefully folded letter—elegant, layered with meaning, and impossible to resist opening again. It’s a story that weaves two timelines together with the precision of a Parisian cobblestone street, where the past and present collide in a dance of secrets, love, and the relentless pull of history. Taylor’s debut is more than a historical romance or a mystery; it’s a meditation on legacy, the weight of silence, and the courage it takes to rewrite one’s own story.

At its heart, *The Paper Girl of Paris* is about the stories we inherit—and the ones we refuse to tell. The novel opens in the present with sixteen-year-old Alice, an American girl who inherits an abandoned Parisian apartment from her late grandmother. The place has been sealed for seventy years, a relic of a life her grandmother never spoke of. Alice, armed with curiosity and a stubborn determination, begins to peel back the layers of her family’s past, only to discover that the truth is far more tangled than she imagined. Her journey mirrors the novel’s central tension: the stories we choose to keep and the ones we bury.

But Taylor’s genius lies in how she bridges Alice’s modern quest with the gripping, heart-wrenching narrative of Adalyn, a young woman in 1942 Paris navigating the treacherous waters of the Nazi Occupation. Adalyn is a paper girl—a courier for the Resistance—her life a delicate balance between the glamorous facade of a socialite and the dangerous reality of fighting back. Her story is a masterclass in tension, blending the romance of a forbidden love with the stark brutality of war. Luc, the enigmatic leader of her resistance group, is a character who lingers in the mind, his charm as intoxicating as it is dangerous. Taylor doesn’t shy away from the moral complexities of Adalyn’s choices; her compromises are not just personal but political, forcing readers to confront the cost of resistance and the price of survival.

Taylor’s prose is a delight—a blend of lyrical beauty and sharp precision. Her descriptions of Paris are immersive, transporting readers to the city’s iconic streets and hidden corners. The Seine glistens under the moonlight, the scent of fresh bread mingles with the acrid tang of war, and the weight of history presses in on every page. Yet, her writing is never sentimental; it’s grounded in the realities of both timelines. The contrast between Alice’s modern, introspective journey and Adalyn’s high-stakes, high-risk world creates a rich tapestry that keeps readers engaged from start to finish.

What makes *The Paper Girl of Paris* truly compelling is its exploration of silence and the stories we choose to share. Alice’s grandmother, a woman who left her past behind, becomes a symbol of the ways we all carry unspoken burdens. Adalyn, meanwhile, embodies the courage it takes to speak up, even when the consequences are dire. Taylor doesn’t offer easy answers; instead, she invites readers to reflect on the stories we tell ourselves and the ones we leave untold. It’s a theme that resonates deeply in an era where identity, heritage, and the search for truth are constantly under scrutiny.

The novel’s pacing is another strength. Taylor balances the two timelines with skill, ensuring that neither feels like an afterthought. Alice’s journey is introspective, her discoveries leading her to question not just her grandmother’s past but her own place in the world. Adalyn’s story, on the other hand, is a pulse-pounding adventure, filled with intrigue, danger, and the kind of romantic tension that makes the heart race. The two narratives intertwine seamlessly, each illuminating the other in ways that feel both inevitable and surprising.

Ultimately, *The Paper Girl of Paris* is a love letter to Paris—a city that has inspired countless stories but rarely with such depth and emotional resonance. It’s a novel that celebrates the power of history to shape our present, and the courage it takes to confront the past. Taylor’s writing is accessible yet profound, her characters unforgettable, and her themes timeless. Whether you’re a history lover, a romance enthusiast, or simply someone who enjoys a gripping story, this book will leave you with a sense of wonder and a newfound appreciation for the stories we choose to tell—and the ones we dare not.
