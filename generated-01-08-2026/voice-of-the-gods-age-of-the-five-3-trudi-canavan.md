# Voice of the Gods (Age of the Five, #3)

**Author:** Trudi Canavan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

In "Voice of the Gods," the third installment of the Age of the Five trilogy, Trudi Canavan masterfully weaves a complex narrative that delves deeper into the intricate world she has created. The story picks up where the previous book left off, with Auraya, now a protector of the Siyee, struggling to navigate the treacherous landscape of her new role.

Canavan's writing style is one of the standout features of this series. Her prose is engaging and accessible, making it easy to become fully immersed in the world she has created. The characters are multidimensional and relatable, with Auraya being a particularly compelling protagonist. Her journey from a devout priestess to a protector of a marginalized group is fraught with challenges, and Canavan expertly conveys the emotional turmoil that Auraya faces as she grapples with her newfound responsibilities.

One of the main themes of "Voice of the Gods" is the exploration of power and its corrupting influence. The Wilds, a group of immortals, are relentless in their pursuit of powerful secrets, and their actions have far-reaching consequences. Canavan raises important questions about the nature of power and whether the ends justify the means. Through Auraya's character, she also examines the consequences of blind obedience and the importance of questioning authority.

The world-building in this series is another notable aspect. Canavan has created a richly detailed and immersive environment, with a complex pantheon of gods and a deep history that underpins the narrative. The magical system is well-developed and logical, with a clear set of rules that govern its use.

The pacing of the book is well-balanced, with a mix of action, suspense, and quieter moments of character development. Canavan's plotting is intricate, with multiple storylines that intersect and overlap in unexpected ways. The tension builds steadily throughout the book, culminating in a dramatic and unexpected conclusion.

Overall, "Voice of the Gods" is a compelling and engaging read that will appeal to fans of epic fantasy. Canavan's writing is engaging, and her world-building is top-notch. The themes of power and corruption are timely and thought-provoking, and the characters are well-developed and relatable.

If you're a fan of fantasy series like Robert Jordan's Wheel of Time or George R.R. Martin's A Song of Ice and Fire, you'll likely enjoy the Age of the Five trilogy. With its complex characters, intricate plotting, and immersive world-building, "Voice of the Gods" is a must-read for anyone who loves epic fantasy.
