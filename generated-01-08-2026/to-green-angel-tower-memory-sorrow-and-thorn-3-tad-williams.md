# To Green Angel Tower (Memory, Sorrow, and Thorn, #3)

**Author:** Tad Williams  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1994

To Green Angel Tower, the final installment in Tad Williams' seminal Memory, Sorrow, and Thorn trilogy, is a masterclass in epic fantasy storytelling. Williams weaves a rich tapestry of magic, politics, and adventure, as the fate of the kingdom of Osten Ard hangs precariously in the balance.

At its core, To Green Angel Tower is a story about the struggle between good and evil, as embodied by the opposing forces of Prince Josua's loyal allies and the dark sorceries of the undead Sithi Storm King. Williams' world-building is meticulous and immersive, with a deep history that underpins the narrative and lends depth to the characters and their motivations.

One of the standout features of Williams' writing is his ability to craft complex, nuanced characters that defy easy categorization. Simon, the young kitchen boy turned hero, is a prime example of this. His journey from humble beginnings to becoming a key player in the fate of the kingdom is both compelling and authentic, and his relationships with the other characters are multifaceted and richly drawn.

The supporting cast is equally well-realized, with characters like the enigmatic Binabik and the stoic Josua adding depth and texture to the narrative. Williams' characterization is notable for its subtlety, with even minor characters possessing a sense of agency and motivation that adds to the overall sense of realism.

The pacing of the book is well-balanced, with moments of high tension and action juxtaposed with quieter, more introspective scenes. Williams' prose is descriptive and evocative, conjuring vivid images of the world of Osten Ard and its inhabitants. His use of language is economical, with a focus on conveying the emotional and psychological states of the characters rather than indulging in flowery descriptions.

The themes of the book are equally well-wrought, with Williams exploring the nature of power, loyalty, and sacrifice in a way that is both thought-provoking and emotionally resonant. The Storm King's dark sorceries serve as a potent metaphor for the corrupting influence of power, while the relationships between the characters highlight the importance of loyalty and friendship in the face of adversity.

If there is a criticism to be made, it is that the book's scope is occasionally overwhelming. With a large cast of characters and multiple plot threads, it can be difficult to keep track of who is doing what and why. However, this is a minor quibble in what is otherwise a masterful work of epic fantasy.

In conclusion, To Green Angel Tower is a triumphant conclusion to the Memory, Sorrow, and Thorn trilogy. Williams' writing is masterful, with a focus on character, world-building, and thematic resonance that sets the book apart from its peers. If you're a fan of epic fantasy, this is a must-read.
