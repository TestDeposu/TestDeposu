# All Good People Here

**Author:** Ashley Flowers  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

"All Good People Here" by Ashley Flowers is a gripping and unsettling debut novel that delves into the darker aspects of human nature and the secrets that lie beneath the surface of seemingly idyllic small-town America. The story follows Margot Davies, a journalist who returns to her hometown of Wakarusa, Indiana, to care for her ailing uncle and finds herself drawn into a decades-old mystery surrounding the unsolved murder of her childhood neighbor, January Jacobs.

Flowers' writing style is engaging and propulsive, with a keen eye for detail and a deep understanding of the complexities of human relationships. She skillfully weaves together multiple narrative threads, slowly unraveling the tangled web of secrets and lies that have haunted Wakarusa for years. The pacing is expertly handled, with a steady build-up of tension that culminates in a shocking and unexpected climax.

One of the most striking aspects of "All Good People Here" is its exploration of the theme of appearances versus reality. The town of Wakarusa is portrayed as a tight-knit community where everyone knows each other's business, but beneath this façade of geniality and neighborliness lies a complex network of secrets, lies, and deceit. Flowers expertly exposes the darker underbelly of small-town America, where the lines between good and evil are often blurred and the truth is frequently hidden behind a mask of respectability.

The character of Margot Davies is well-developed and relatable, with a strong narrative voice that drives the story forward. Her obsession with uncovering the truth about January's murder and Natalie's disappearance is both understandable and admirable, and her determination to expose the secrets of Wakarusa is admirable, even if it puts her own life at risk.

The supporting cast of characters is equally well-drawn, with a range of complex and nuanced individuals who add depth and richness to the story. From the enigmatic and reclusive neighbor who may hold the key to the mystery to the tight-lipped and uncooperative local police, each character is skillfully crafted to add to the overall sense of tension and unease.

Overall, "All Good People Here" is a gripping and thought-provoking thriller that will appeal to fans of true crime podcasts and small-town mysteries. Flowers' writing is engaging and suspenseful, with a keen eye for detail and a deep understanding of human psychology. This is a must-read for anyone who enjoys a good mystery, and it will keep you on the edge of your seat until the very end.
