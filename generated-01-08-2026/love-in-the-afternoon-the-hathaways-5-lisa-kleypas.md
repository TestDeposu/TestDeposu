# Love in the Afternoon (The Hathaways, #5)

**Author:** Lisa Kleypas  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

**Love in the Afternoon: Where Deception Blooms into Passion**

Lisa Kleypas, the queen of lush, emotionally charged historical romance, delivers another masterpiece with *Love in the Afternoon*—the fifth installment in her beloved *Hathaway* series. This story is a testament to her signature ability to weave heartache, wit, and undeniable chemistry into a tale that lingers long after the last page. At its core, *Love in the Afternoon* explores the fragile, intoxicating dance between deception and destiny, proving that sometimes the greatest love stories begin with a lie.

Beatrix Hathaway is no ordinary debutante. A free-spirited naturalist with a heart as wild as the English countryside, she has spent her life chasing butterflies and avoiding the ballroom—until now. When her best friend, the flirtatious Prudence Mercer, finds herself jilted by a soldier, Beatrix steps in, forging letters in Prudence’s name to keep her friend’s spirits up. What starts as a small kindness spirals into something far more dangerous: a correspondence with Captain Christopher Phelan, a man whose letters reveal a soul as tormented as he is handsome. Christopher, once a dashing soldier, returns from war changed, his faith in love shattered. But Beatrix, who has never believed in grand romantic gestures, finds herself drawn into a correspondence that feels dangerously real. When he arrives in London, he is determined to claim the woman who has stolen his heart—only to discover that the love he thought was forged in letters is now a fire neither can ignore.

Kleypas’ genius lies in her ability to make the reader *feel* every twist of this story. Beatrix’s initial reluctance to engage with the world of romance is palpable; she is a woman who values truth over illusion, and yet, she is the first to admit that Christopher’s letters have unraveled her carefully constructed indifference. His own struggle—between the man he was and the man he must become—adds depth to their connection, making their eventual passion feel earned rather than forced. The tension between them is electric, a slow burn that simmers with the kind of quiet intensity only Kleypas can craft. There are no grand declarations here; instead, love is a series of stolen glances, whispered confessions, and the quiet understanding that some bonds cannot be broken, no matter how carefully they were constructed.

What makes *Love in the Afternoon* particularly compelling is its exploration of the themes of trust and vulnerability. Beatrix’s deception is not just about letters—it’s about her fear of being seen as anything less than the woman she presents herself to be. Christopher, meanwhile, is a man who has lost faith in the world, and yet, he is the first to offer Beatrix the chance to be more than she thinks she can be. Their relationship is a study in contrasts: her practicality against his idealism, her independence against his need for redemption. Yet, in the end, it is their shared humanity that bridges the gap. Kleypas never shies away from the messy, emotional reality of love—there are misunderstandings, heartbreak, and moments of raw vulnerability that make their reunion all the more satisfying.

The prose is rich, the dialogue sharp, and the historical detail immersive without ever feeling heavy-handed. Kleypas’ knack for creating characters who feel like real people—flawed, complex, and deeply human—is on full display here. Beatrix is a heroine who refuses to be defined by societal expectations, while Christopher is a love interest who evolves from a brooding soldier into a man capable of true devotion. Their dynamic is one of the most satisfying in the series, a perfect blend of tension and tenderness that keeps readers hooked from the first page.

For fans of Kleypas, *Love in the Afternoon* is a love letter to the power of second chances and the unexpected ways love can find us. It’s a story about the courage to admit when you’ve been wrong, the strength to forgive, and the recklessness of falling in love when you’ve spent a lifetime believing it wasn’t for you. It’s romantic, it’s steamy, and it’s utterly unforgettable—just as you’d expect from one of the most beloved voices in historical romance.

If you’ve ever loved a story where the greatest love story begins with a lie, where a woman’s independence is tested by the man who sees her as she truly is, and where passion is the only thing that can heal the wounds of war, then *Love in the Afternoon* is for you. Kleypas once again proves that love, in all its messy, beautiful complexity, is the most powerful force of all.
