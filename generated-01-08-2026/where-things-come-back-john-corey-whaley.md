# Where Things Come Back

**Author:** John Corey Whaley  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

In his remarkable 2011 debut, *Where Things Come Back*, John Corey Whaley achieves something deceptively difficult: he makes the quiet ache of a small-town summer feel both monumental and deeply universal. Winner of both the Michael L. Printz and William C. Morris awards, this novel is far more than a standard coming-of-age story. It is a poignant, darkly funny, and ultimately heart-wrenching exploration of absence, faith, and the desperate human need for things—and people—to return.

The narrative centers on Cullen Witter, a teenager living in a painfully dull Arkansas town whose life is upended during a single, bizarre summer. While his cousin battles drug addiction and the local populace loses its collective mind over the rumored reappearance of the extinct ivory-billed woodpecker, Cullen faces a far more devastating reality: the sudden, inexplicable disappearance of his fifteen-year-old brother, Gabriel. Whaley captures the suffocating weight of grief not through melodrama, but through a lens of sharp, often cynical teenage wit. Cullen’s internal monologue grounds the narrative, balancing the absurdity of the woodpecker-obsessed media circus with the crushing silence left in Gabriel's wake.

Parallel to Cullen’s ordeal is the story of Benton Ryder, a young missionary in Africa experiencing a profound and paralyzing crisis of faith. At first glance, Benton’s narrative feels entirely disconnected from the dusty roads of Arkansas. Yet, Whaley’s true genius lies in his structural craftsmanship. He weaves these seemingly disparate storylines together with a taut, deliberate pacing that keeps the reader perpetually off-balance, hurtling toward a climax that is as surprising as it is harrowing. 

Whaley’s authorial style is defined by its lyrical economy and emotional honesty. He refuses to offer easy answers or sugarcoat the messy reality of trauma. Instead, he explores the process of grief as a series of echoes and empty spaces. The motif of things "coming back" runs through every page—whether it is an extinct bird, a lost brother, or a stray shred of faith—serving as a constant reminder of our fragility and our stubborn capacity for hope. 

*Where Things Come Back* is a stunning literary achievement that resonates long after the final page is turned. It examines the moments when our world shatters and asks what remains when the dust settles. Whaley finds quiet wonder in the most ordinary and devastating corners of life, delivering a striking reminder that sometimes, the only way forward is to wait for the return of what we thought was lost forever.
