# Intertwined

**Author:** Jerilee Kaye  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

Title: Navigating the Delicate Fault Lines of Love: A Review of Intertwined by Jerilee Kaye

In the landscape of modern romance, few tropes carry the bittersweet weight of the "enemies-to-lovers" narrative quite like the one built on a foundation of grief, duty, and lingering proximity. In her 2015 novel Intertwined, author Jerilee Kaye takes this familiar dynamic and stretches it across a vast developmental canvas, tracking her protagonists from the vulnerable, tempestuous years of adolescence well into the sobering realities of adulthood. The result is a deeply emotional, slow-burn exploration of what happens when the person you swore to despise becomes the only oxygen left in the room.

At the narrative’s heart is a compelling, high-stakes premise: a generational family curse dictating that the women of Brianne Montgomery’s family must marry before turning thirty-one. While initially framed as a whimsical family lore, the stakes of this curse catastrophically shift following a sudden, life-shattering tragedy that strips Brianne of her seemingly idyllic world. Enter Travis Cross—her brother’s relentlessly annoying best friend, a boy she spent years trading barbs with. Bound by a solemn deathbed-adjacent promise to protect her, serve as her "safety guy," and crucially, never cross the line into romance, Travis steps into the role of her unwavering anchor. 

Kaye’s primary thematic triumph in Intertwined lies in her deconstruction of emotional codependency versus genuine intimacy. The novel brilliantly toys with the tension of enforced proximity. Travis and Brianne’s relationship does not ignite in a sudden flash of passion; rather, it is forged in the grueling fires of shared trauma, quiet domesticity, and the heavy burden of keeping a promise. Kaye explores the psychological toll of grief with a raw brushstroke, showing how loss can either fracture a foundation or fuse two fragile souls together. For Brianne, Travis becomes synonymous with survival; for Travis, Brianne is an intoxicating addiction he has sworn to resist. This creates a mesmerizing push-and-pull where every lingering glance and accidental touch carries the gravity of a forbidden transgression.

Kaye’s writing style is characterized by its immersive pacing and emotional endurance. Spanning years of growth, the narrative refuses to rush the maturation of its characters. Instead, the author lingers in the messy interstices of youth—dealing with teen peer pressure, shifting family dynamics, college, breakups, and the agonizingly slow realization that childhood animosity has morphed into profound devotion. Kaye captures the interiority of her characters with sharp authenticity. Brianne’s terror of risking her last remaining emotional lifeline is palpable, grounding the fantastical weight of the family curse in very real, very human fears of abandonment and heartbreak. 

While the book traverses well-worn romantic territory, Kaye elevates the material through her commitment to the slow-burn dynamic. The prose thrives on angst, restraint, and the agonizing beauty of longing. It is a testament to the author’s stylistic control that the tension remains electric even during the quietest scenes. The dialogue snaps with residual adolescent friction before deepening into the shorthand of lifelong companions. 

Intertwined is ultimately a story about the terrifying courage it takes to gamble everything you have left for the sake of love. Jerilee Kaye has crafted a poignant, sweeping tribute to bonds that defy definition—relationships built on shared history, profound sacrifice, and an unbreakable emotional gravity. For readers who revel in angst-ridden journeys, high emotional stakes, and the agonizing sweetness of a hero who will fight the entire world—and his own deepest desires—to keep a promise, this novel is an unforgettable voyage.
