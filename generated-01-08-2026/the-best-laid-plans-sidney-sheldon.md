# The Best Laid Plans

**Author:** Sidney Sheldon  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

Power, vengeance, and the fragile architecture of human ambition take center stage in Sidney Sheldon’s riveting 2010 novel, The Best Laid Plans. True to form, the legendary master of suspense delivers a fast-paced narrative that explores the dangerous intersection where politics and personal vendettas collide. While Sheldon passed away in 2007, this posthumously published work—crafted from his outlines and notes—retains the signature high-stakes drama and relentless plotting that made him a household name. 

At the heart of the novel is a classic Sheldon dynamic: two formidable, equally matched adversaries locked in a lethal game of chess. On one side is Oliver Russell, the charismatic governor of a small southern state whose sights are set firmly on the Oval Office. He is a man of calculated moves, political charm, and ruthless ambition, willing to sacrifice anything and anyone to achieve the ultimate prize. Standing directly in his path is Leslie Stewart, a breathtakingly beautiful and intensely ambitious woman who possesses a brilliant business mind and an unforgiving memory. When Oliver betrays her trust in the most personal and devastating way, Leslie transforms her heartbreak into a singular, consuming mission: to destroy his political career and watch him lose everything he holds dear.

The central theme of the novel is encapsulated by its title, subverting the old adage about the best-laid schemes of mice and men. Sheldon explores how human hubris inevitably invites chaos. Both Oliver and Leslie craft meticulous, flawless strategies to achieve their opposing goals, yet they repeatedly underestimate the unpredictable nature of human emotion, scandal, and fate. As their collision course accelerates, the narrative transforms into a high-stakes psychological thriller where every victory feels precarious, and every tactical advance carries the seed of potential ruin. 

Sheldon’s authorial style remains instantly recognizable in this late-career offering. He utilizes his trademark short, punchy chapters that act as narrative hooks, pulling the reader relentlessly forward. There is no wasted prose; every scene is engineered to advance the plot or reveal a new layer of psychological warfare. While literary critics often pigeonholed Sheldon as a purveyor of guilty pleasures, his true genius lay in his pacing and his creation of larger-than-life protagonists. Leslie Stewart fits seamlessly into the pantheon of strong, defiant Sheldon heroines who refuse to play the victim, choosing instead to manipulate the system that wronged them. Similarly, Oliver Russell is no cardboard villain, but a complex study of the moral compromises required in the pursuit of supreme power.

The Best Laid Plans may not reinvent the thriller genre, but it offers a masterclass in narrative momentum. It is a sleek, satisfying cautionary tale about the cost of vengeance and the terrifying fragility of political dreams. For longtime fans of Sidney Sheldon, it is a welcome return to a world where nobody can be trusted, stakes are always life-or-death, and the unexpected is the only guarantee.
