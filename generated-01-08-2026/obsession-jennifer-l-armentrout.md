# Obsession

**Author:** Jennifer L. Armentrout  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

Title: Lethal Attraction: Jennifer L. Armentrout Ignites Sci-Fi Romance in Obsession

When it comes to blending high-octane danger with blistering romantic tension, few authors operate at the peak of their powers quite like Jennifer L. Armentrout. Published in 2013, Obsession stands as a compelling testament to the author’s signature style, seamlessly bridging the gap between adult paranormal romance and fast-paced science fiction. Stripping away the typical sweetness of young adult tropes, Armentrout delivers a darker, sexier, and more lethal narrative that proves her versatility and cements her mastery over the genre.

At the heart of the novel lies a classic, yet relentlessly executed theme: the dangerous, world-altering power of unexpected intimacy in a hostile environment. Hunter is not your average romantic hero. As a ruthless operative bound to the Department of Defense, he embodies the cold-belligerence of a man bred for violence. His existence is transactional, ruled by strict boundaries and the visceral thrill of the hunt. Yet, the introduction of Serena Cross—an ordinary human hurled into an extraordinary, extraterrestrial nightmare—fractures his rigid world. 

Armentrout uses the dynamic between Hunter and Serena to explore the theme of humanity versus monstrosity. Serena has witnessed the horrific murder of her best friend at the hands of the Luxen, an alien species hiding in plain sight. Thrown into a covert war where the government and extraterrestrials are equally ruthless, Serena is the ultimate outsider. Yet, her resilience in the face of sheer terror is what truly captivates Hunter. Through their friction and undeniable physical attraction, Armentrout poses a fundamental question: When the world is governed by monsters, what separates the savior from the executioner? 

The author’s style in Obsession is distinctly propulsive. Armentrout weaves snappy, witty dialogue with palpable, slow-burn romantic tension that leaves readers breathless. Even amidst intense action sequences—where alien tech and government conspiracies collide—the emotional core of the story never falters. She excels at internal pacing, ensuring that the quiet, charged moments between the protagonists carry just as much weight as the explosive confrontations with their enemies. Hunter and Serena's banter provides a necessary anchor of levity, contrasting sharply with the grim reality of their survival stakes.

While the plot treads familiar territory for fans of sci-fi romance—involving secret organizations, hidden alien races, and the ultimate protective-alpha trope—Armentrout elevates the material through sheer execution. The world-building is tight, the stakes feel immediately personal, and the chemistry sizzles off the page from their very first encounter. 

Obsession is a striking, adrenaline-fueled ride that delivers precisely what romance and sci-fi fans crave: danger, emotional depth, and a romance so fierce it threatens to burn the rulebook entirely. Armentrout proves that when the universe conspires against you, the most dangerous thing you can do is fall in love.
