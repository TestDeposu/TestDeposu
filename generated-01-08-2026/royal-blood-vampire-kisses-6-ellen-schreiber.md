# Royal Blood (Vampire Kisses, #6)

**Author:** Ellen Schreiber  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Step back into the delightfully gloomy atmosphere of Dullsville with Royal Blood, the sixth installment in Ellen Schreiber’s enduringly popular Vampire Kisses series. Released in 2009, this novel proves that even after half a dozen books, the gothic romance between goth-girl extraordinaire Raven Madison and her brooding, immortal boyfriend Alexander Sterling still holds plenty of atmospheric charm. Schreiber has carved out a unique space in young adult literature by blending classic monster lore with small-town teenage angst, and Royal Blood leans heavily into the signature stylistic choices that made the series a cult favorite.

At its core, the main theme of Royal Blood revolves around acceptance, anticipation, and the daunting reality of meeting the parents—with a distinctly supernatural twist. Raven is the quintessential outsider, a teenager who proudly wears her individuality like a badge of honor. Yet, even the most confident goths get jittery when introduced to high society vampiric royalty. When Alexander receives an unexpected letter announcing his parents' arrival in Dullsville, the dynamic of the relationship shifts. Schreiber uses this long-awaited family reunion to explore the friction between human and immortal worlds, testing the boundaries of Raven and Alexander’s devotion. The secrecy surrounding the parents' delayed introduction to Raven introduces a fresh layer of tension, forcing readers to question what skeletons—or coffins—might be hidden in the Sterling family closet.

Schreiber’s authorial style remains light, breezy, and unapologetically melodramatic in the best possible way. While many vampire tales from the late 2000s veered toward hyper-gritty or overly dark territory, Schreiber maintains a playful, tongue-in-cheek innocence. Dullsville High serves as a humorous backdrop of mundane teenage life that sharply contrasts with Raven’s nocturnal, romantic ideals. Her style relies on vivid, highly visual descriptions—satin capes, pale skin, dramatic moonlight, and lace—creating an aesthetic that feels like a Tim Burton film tailored specifically for teen readers. The pacing is brisk, making Royal Blood a fast, highly addictive read that effortlessly transports its audience back to the height of mid-2000s gothic-pop culture.

For longtime fans of the series, Royal Blood delivers precisely what is expected: the comforting thrill of teenage romance mixed with paranormal intrigue, anchored by a protagonist who refuses to compromise who she is. It is a striking, fun-sized piece of comfort reading for anyone nostalgic for an era when vampires ruled the YA landscape and being different was the ultimate cool.
