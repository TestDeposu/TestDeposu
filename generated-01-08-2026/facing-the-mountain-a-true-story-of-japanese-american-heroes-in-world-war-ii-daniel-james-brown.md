# Facing the Mountain: A True Story of Japanese American Heroes in World War II

**Author:** Daniel James Brown  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

"Facing the Mountain: A True Story of Japanese American Heroes in World War II" by Daniel James Brown is a gripping and emotive book that delves into the complexities of war, racism, and resilience. Published in 2023, this young adult novel is an adapted version of the bestselling author's masterpiece, which recounts the remarkable true story of three courageous Japanese American soldiers who fought for the United States during World War II, despite facing intense discrimination at home.

The story begins with the infamous bombing of Pearl Harbor in 1941, which sets off a chain of events that lead to the internment of Japanese Americans in concentration camps. However, amidst this turmoil, a group of patriotic Japanese Americans saw an opportunity to prove their loyalty to the country that they had been born and raised in. The 442nd Regimental Combat Team, consisting of Japanese American soldiers, was formed to fight in Europe, and Brown masterfully brings to life the stories of three brave soldiers: Rudy Tokiwa, Fred Shiosaki, and Kats Miho. These young men, despite being victims of racism, volunteered to serve in the military, and their journey will take you on a rollercoaster of emotions as they fight against the enemy and against their own prejudice.

As the book progresses, Brown also sheds light on the untold story of Gordon Hirabayashi, a Japanese American who refused to comply with the forced evacuation order and stood up against the government in defense of his rights. This narrative thread adds a layer of depth and complexity to the book, highlighting the courageous acts of those who dared to challenge the status quo.

The author's writing style is impeccable, weaving historical facts with powerful storytelling that will captivate young readers. The battle scenes are vividly described, putting readers in the midst of the war, while the personal struggles and triumphs of the soldiers make them relatable and memorable. Brown has a unique ability to balance history and suspense, keeping readers engaged from start to finish.

One of the standout aspects of this book is the way it tackles the difficult topic of racism and xenophobia in a way that is accessible to young readers. The story is both a testament to the resilience of the human spirit and a call to action, reminding readers of the importance of standing up against injustice. Brown's research is exhaustive and meticulous, ensuring that the narrative is both accurate and compelling.

The inclusion of black and white photos and backmatter adds a layer of authenticity to the book, making readers feel like they are reading a historical account rather than a work of fiction. The notes and references section at the end of the book is also a testament to the author's dedication to historical accuracy, providing readers with a wealth of information to further explore the topics covered in the book.

In conclusion, "Facing the Mountain" is a phenomenal book that deserves to be read by anyone interested in history, war stories, or the experiences of Japanese American soldiers during World War II. Daniel James Brown's masterful storytelling and meticulous research make this book a must-read for young adult readers and history enthusiasts alike.
