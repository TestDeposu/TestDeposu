# Skinwalker (Jane Yellowrock, #1)

**Author:** Faith Hunter  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Faith Hunter’s 2009 urban fantasy powerhouse, Skinwalker, introduces readers to a gritty, atmospheric world where the supernatural hides in plain sight and predators wear human skin. As the inaugural entry in the New York Times bestselling Jane Yellowrock series, this novel wastes no time establishing its territory, plunging the audience headfirst into a sultry, dangerous New Orleans where ancient bloodsuckers and forgotten mythologies collide. 

At the heart of the novel is its defining theme: identity, survival, and the heavy burden of being the last of one's kind. Jane Yellowrock is a Cherokee skinwalker—a rare being capable of shifting into any animal form. Living on the fringes of society, she is a solitary hunter who makes her living eliminating rogue vampires. Hunter uses Jane’s unique heritage not merely as a cool superpower, but as a deep psychological anchor. Jane’s internal struggle revolves around the duality of her human mind and the feral instincts of the beasts she inhabits, particularly Beast, a lion-like soul-companion who shares her body. This dynamic brilliantly explores the theme of control versus instinct. Jane must constantly fight to retain her humanity in a world that treats her as a weapon, emphasizing that survival often requires sacrificing pieces of oneself.

Hunter’s authorial style is sharp, sensory-driven, and relentlessly fast-paced. The prose captures the humid, suffocating, and intoxicating atmosphere of New Orleans, transforming the city into a living, breathing character. Unlike the polished, high-society urban fantasies of its era, Skinwalker embraces the muck, sweat, and shadows. The dialogue is snappy, and the action sequences are choreographed with brutal precision. Hunter avoids the trap of making her protagonist an invincible superhero; Jane gets bruised, bloodied, and pushed to her absolute limits, making her victories earned and deeply satisfying. Furthermore, the world-building is layered and organic. Hunter weaves Cherokee folklore seamlessly with classic vampire lore, creating a fresh hierarchy of undead politics centered around Katherine Fontaneau, a centuries-old vampire madam. 

Romance and tension are sprinkled into the narrative just enough to spice the pot without overshadowing the mystery, courtesy of a brooding Cajun biker hiding his own wild secrets. Yet, the true romance of the book lies in Jane's journey toward self-reliance and finding her place in a hostile world. 

Skinwalker is a masterclass in urban fantasy pacing and atmospheric world-building. Faith Hunter delivers a compelling, claw-and-fang thriller that grabs readers by the throat on page one and doesn't let go until the very end. For fans of gritty supernatural mysteries and fierce, complex heroines, this book is an absolute must-read.
