# Girl, Serpent, Thorn

**Author:** Melissa Bashardoust  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

In "Girl, Serpent, Thorn," Melissa Bashardoust masterfully weaves a captivating narrative that defies traditional fairy tale tropes, instead embracing a rich, feminist retelling of a timeless classic. This mesmerizing tale delves into the complexities of identity, power, and the blurred lines between human and monster.

At the heart of the story lies Soraya, a princess cursed to be poisonous to the touch, forced to live a life of isolation and solitude. Bashardoust skillfully crafts Soraya's character, imbuing her with a depth and nuance that makes her both relatable and endearing. As Soraya navigates the treacherous landscape of her family's palace, she must confront the harsh realities of her existence and the choices that will ultimately define her.

One of the most striking aspects of Bashardoust's writing is her ability to balance the darker elements of the narrative with a sense of hope and resilience. The world she creates is steeped in Persian mythology, adding a unique layer of depth and richness to the story. The author's use of language is equally impressive, with a writing style that is both lyrical and evocative.

Throughout the novel, Bashardoust explores the theme of female empowerment, challenging traditional notions of power and identity. Soraya's journey is a powerful exploration of self-discovery, as she grapples with the constraints placed upon her and the expectations of those around her. The supporting cast of characters is equally well-developed, with complex motivations and backstories that add depth to the narrative.

One of the most compelling aspects of "Girl, Serpent, Thorn" is its exploration of the monster within. Bashardoust raises important questions about the nature of monstrosity and what it means to be human. Soraya's struggles to reconcile her own identity with the expectations of those around her serve as a powerful metaphor for the complexities of human experience.

If there is one criticism to be made, it is that the pacing of the novel can be somewhat uneven at times. Certain plot threads feel slightly rushed, while others are given more attention than perhaps necessary. However, this is a minor quibble in an otherwise captivating narrative.

In conclusion, "Girl, Serpent, Thorn" is a stunning work of fantasy that will appeal to fans of feminist retellings and mythology-inspired fiction. Bashardoust's writing is masterful, with a unique voice and perspective that sets her apart from other authors in the genre. With its rich world-building, complex characters, and thought-provoking themes, this novel is sure to leave readers spellbound.
