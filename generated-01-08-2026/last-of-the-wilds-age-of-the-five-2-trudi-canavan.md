# Last of the Wilds (Age of the Five, #2)

**Author:** Trudi Canavan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

**Last of the Wilds: A Desperate Dance with Fate in Trudi Canavan’s Epic Fantasy**

Trudi Canavan’s *Last of the Wilds*—the second installment in *The Age of the Five*—is a masterclass in high-stakes fantasy, where political intrigue, divine prophecy, and personal sacrifice collide in a world teetering on the brink of ruin. Following the chaotic climax of *The White*, Auraya, the priestess of the Five Gods, finds herself thrust into a mission fraught with danger and moral ambiguity. Her quest to broker peace with the enigmatic Dreamweavers, a reclusive order of healers, becomes not just a diplomatic endeavor but a race against time—against a creeping plague that threatens to wipe out her allies, against resurgent enemies, and against the very weight of her own past choices.

At its core, *Last of the Wilds* is a story about the cost of power and the fragility of alliances. Canavan, a master of world-building, crafts a setting where magic is both a blessing and a curse, where divine favor is as precarious as it is sought after. The Dreamweavers, with their arcane healing arts, represent a potential salvation—but their isolation and distrust of the outside world force Auraya to navigate a minefield of suspicion. Her journey is less about grand battles and more about the quiet, devastating truths she uncovers: that some wounds cannot be healed, that some secrets are better left buried, and that the gods themselves may be as fickle as mortals.

Canavan’s prose is rich with atmosphere, her descriptions vivid enough to make the lush, war-torn landscapes of the Five Nations feel tangible. Yet, it’s her characters who truly steal the show. Auraya, though still grappling with the aftermath of her lover’s disappearance, grows in complexity, her faith in the gods tested to its limits. The Dreamweavers, with their cryptic wisdom and haunting beauty, add layers of mystery, while the returning adversaries—now more cunning than ever—force Auraya into a desperate choice that will echo through the ages.

Themes of sacrifice and redemption weave through the narrative, but it’s the sense of inevitability that lingers. Canavan doesn’t shy away from the bleakness of her world; the plague isn’t just a plot device but a metaphor for the decay of trust, the erosion of hope. Yet, there’s a quiet defiance in Auraya’s resolve, a refusal to let despair win. The book’s pacing is relentless, balancing political maneuvering with heart-pounding action, all while deepening the lore of the Five Gods and their complex interplay.

For fans of epic fantasy, *Last of the Wilds* delivers the kind of immersive storytelling that keeps you turning pages long into the night. Canavan’s ability to blend mythic grandeur with intimate character drama makes this a standout in the genre. It’s a tale of a world on the edge, where every decision could be the last—and where the line between hero and villain is as blurred as the fate of the gods themselves.
