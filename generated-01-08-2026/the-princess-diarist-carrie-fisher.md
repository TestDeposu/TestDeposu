# The Princess Diarist

**Author:** Carrie Fisher  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

Before she was the indomitable galactic general or the sharp-tongued literary force of nature, Carrie Fisher was a nineteen-year-old girl sitting in a London hotel room, agonizing over a secret that would take four decades to fully unpack. Published in 2016, The Princess Diarist stands as a poignant and fiercely candid literary monument, serving as the final book released during Fisher’s lifetime. Built upon the foundation of the actual journals she kept while filming the original Star Wars in 1977, the memoir strips away the glossy mythos of Hollywood royalty to reveal a young woman grappling with intense vulnerability, crippling self-doubt, and an all-consuming, highly publicized affair with her married costar, Harrison Ford.

The central theme of The Princess Diarist is the profound disconnect between the public icon and the private human being. Fisher explores the surreal machinery of celebrity, tracing the trajectory of a girl born into the glaring spotlight of Hollywood pedigree only to be thrust into an entirely new stratosphere of fame as Princess Leia Organa—a metal-bikini-clad fantasy for millions of teenage boys who had no concept of the insecure writer trapped inside the costume. Fisher does not merely recount history; she interrogates it. Through the lens of maturity, she looks back at her teenage poetry and earnest diary entries with a blend of tender amusement and sharp-edged wisdom. The book captures the universal agony of youthful infatuation, but it magnifies that pain against the surreal backdrop of a billion-dollar pop-culture phenomenon being built in real-time by a cast and crew who had no idea what they were unleashing upon the world.

What makes the book truly unforgettable is Fisher’s inimitable voice. Her writing style is a masterclass in autodidactic wit—conversational, fiercely intelligent, unapologetically cynical, yet startlingly tender. Fisher possessed a rare linguistic alchemy that allowed her to skewer her own trauma with laugh-out-loud humor while simultaneously breaking the reader’s heart. She narrates her past with the cadence of a seasoned stand-up comedian standing at the edge of a precipice, daring you to laugh so you won't have to cry. There is no self-pity in her prose, nor is there any attempt to sanitize her mistakes or soften her edges. Instead, Fisher offers a masterclass in radical self-awareness, dissecting the absurdity of fame with the precision of a surgeon and the joy of a gossip columnist.

Ultimately, The Princess Diarist is much more than a behind-the-scenes tell-all for Star Wars enthusiasts. It is a retrospective reckoning, a witty feminist post-mortem on the commodification of young women in cinema, and a final, brilliant dispatch from a woman who spent her entire life translating her survival into art. Fisher’s prose crackles with vitality on every page, reminding us precisely why her voice remains irreplaceable. Essential, hilarious, and devastatingly honest, this book is a fitting final bow for a true original.
