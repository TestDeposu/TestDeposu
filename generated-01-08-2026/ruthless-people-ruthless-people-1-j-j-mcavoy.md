Liam, meanwhile, is introduced as a man who equates control with safety, his worldview shaped by a upbringing where emotion is a liability. McAvoy deftly layers his internal conflict: the clash between his ingrained need to dominate and the unsettling fascination he feels for a wife who refuses to yield.

The author’s narrative technique leans heavily on dual perspectives, shifting seamlessly between Melody’s and Liam’s viewpoints.
