# Unmasking AI: My Mission to Protect What Is Human in a World of Machines

**Author:** Joy Buolamwini  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

Title: The Coded Gaze: Why Dr. Joy Buolamwini’s Unmasking AI Is the Most Urgent Read of Our Time

To the average observer, the artificial intelligence revolution feels like an abrupt tidal wave, crashing onto our shores with sudden, dizzying force. We marvel at generative tools, panic over shifting labor markets, and whisper anxiously about existential threats dreamed up by Silicon Valley executives. But behind the sleek interfaces and corporate utopian promises lies a darker, more entrenched reality—one that has been quietly shaping marginalized lives for years. In her landmark 2023 book, Unmasking AI: My Mission to Protect What Is Human in a World of Machines, Dr. Joy Buolamwini dismantles the illusion of algorithmic neutrality, offering a piercing, deeply personal, and urgently necessary manifesto for the future of humanity.

Buolamwini, famously dubbed the conscience of the AI revolution, does not write as an outside critic looking in. As a computer scientist, artist, and founder of the Algorithmic Justice League, she writes as a pioneer who stumbled upon injustice by sheer accident. While a graduate researcher at the MIT Media Lab, Buolamwini encountered a glaring flaw: commercial facial analysis software repeatedly failed to recognize her dark-skinned face until she donned a white theatrical mask. This simple, startling experiment birthed a movement. It laid bare what she defines as the coded gaze—the systemic, often unconscious embedding of racism, sexism, colorism, and ableism into the very code that powers our modern world.

The central theme of Unmasking AI is both simple and devastating: technology does not exist in a vacuum, and computers are not infallible arbiters of truth. Instead, they are hyper-efficient mirrors reflecting the aspirations, blind spots, and prejudices of the humans who build them. Buolamwini guides the reader through her personal journey from a robotics-tinkering teenager in Memphis and a Fulbright fellow in Zambia to a frontline whistleblower holding tech giants accountable. Her prose is remarkably accessible, translating dense computer science concepts into gripping, human narratives. She avoids dry academic jargon, opting instead for a storytelling style that blends scientific rigor with the emotional resonance of a memoir. 

What elevates Unmasking AI above typical tech-critique is its intersectional lens. Buolamwini illustrates how automated systems do not just discriminate randomly; they disproportionately harm those already vulnerable to societal oppression. From flawed facial recognition tools used in law enforcement to algorithmic bias in healthcare and hiring, she exposes a world where entire populations are rendered excoded—erased, misidentified, or unfairly targeted by digital gatekeepers. 

Yet, for all its exposure of corporate negligence and algorithmic harm, Unmasking AI is not a book driven by despair. It is a clarion call to action. Buolamwini powerfully argues that algorithmic justice is the civil rights frontier of our time. She insists that AI should be built by the people and for the people, not merely the privileged few. By demystifying the technology and empowering everyday citizens to demand accountability, she transforms the reader from a passive consumer into an active participant in the fight for a humane digital future. 

Striking, profoundly illuminating, and fiercely hopeful, Unmasking AI is essential reading. It forces us to strip away the glossy veneer of technological progress and ask the most fundamental question of the digital age: what does it mean to protect our humanity in a world increasingly run by machines?
