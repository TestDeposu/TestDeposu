# Collide (Off the Ice, #1)

**Author:** Bal Khabra  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

Title: Sparks on the Ice: A Critique of Bal Khabra’s Collide

When the rigid architecture of a twenty-five-year plan meets the chaotic, penalty-box energy of a college hockey captain, romantic fiction does what it does best: it creates delicious, high-friction chaos. Bal Khabra’s 2023 bestselling novel, Collide, the first installment in the Off the Ice series, takes a well-trodden romance trope—the disciplined academic and the arrogant athlete—and infuses it with enough sharp wit and emotional depth to make it stand out in the crowded contemporary sports romance genre.

At its core, Collide is an exploration of control versus surrender. Summer Preston is the quintessential honors student, a young woman whose life is meticulously color-coded and meticulously scheduled. Her ambition to become a sport psychologist is both her passion and her shield, yet it comes with a glaring irony: she despises the very sport she aims to study. Enter Aiden Crawford, the charismatic, unapologetically arrogant captain of the campus hockey team. Aiden is everything Summer tries to avoid—reckless, charming, and entirely focused on the ice. When academic necessity forces them together through a mandatory research project, their initial interactions are less a meeting of the minds and more a collision of opposing forces. 

Khabra’s central theme examines how rigidly we curate our lives to avoid pain, and how vulnerability often arrives disguised as the exact person we swore we didn't need. Summer’s obsession with her future is a trauma response to unpredictability, while Aiden’s carefree exterior masks the immense weight of leadership and the fear of failure. Through their forced proximity, Khabra constructs a narrative where growth happens not when characters conquer their flaws, but when they admit they cannot do it alone. The ice rink serves as a brilliant metaphor throughout the novel: it is slippery, dangerous, and requires both immense balance and a willingness to fall.

From a stylistic standpoint, Khabra leans heavily into the enemies-to-lovers dynamic, executing it with snappy pacing and crackling dialogue. The banter between Summer and Aiden is the engine that drives the first half of the book forward. Khabra has a sharp ear for contemporary collegiate voice, ensuring that the arguments feel authentic to twenty-somethings navigating the high-stress environment of university life. Instead of relying solely on physical attraction, the author builds the tension through intellectual sparring. Summer’s initial resistance to Aiden’s charms gives the narrative a necessary bite, preventing the romance from descending into instant, unearned adoration. 

However, Khabra’s greatest stylistic triumph lies in her ability to balance levity with emotional stakes. While the premise sounds like pure romantic comedy fluff, the author isn't afraid to let her characters sit in genuine discomfort. The transition from bitter bickering to playful banter, and finally to profound emotional intimacy, feels earned because Khabra allows both protagonists to lower their armor incrementally. Aiden is not just a one-dimensional jock; his layers peel back to reveal a man deeply protective of those he loves, while Summer learns that spontaneity isn't the enemy of success.

Collide succeeds because it respects the rules of the genre while injecting fresh energy into its tropes. Bal Khabra delivers a thoroughly entertaining, emotionally resonant setup for her Off the Ice series, proving that the best love stories aren't the ones where everything goes according to plan, but the ones that completely derail it for the better. For readers looking for sharp wit, undeniable chemistry, and a heartwarming journey of letting go, this book hits the net.
