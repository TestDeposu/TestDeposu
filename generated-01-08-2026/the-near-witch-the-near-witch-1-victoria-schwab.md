# The Near Witch (The Near Witch, #1)

**Author:** Victoria Schwab  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

Before she became the powerhouse fantasy author known simply as V.E. Schwab, enchanting millions with tales of dark Londoners and shadowy magicians, she introduced readers to a much colder, lonelier world. Long out of print and fiercely coveted by fans, the deluxe reissue of *The Near Witch* is not merely a collector’s item; it is a fascinating glimpse into the formative genius of a master storyteller. Blending the timeless cadence of a campfire tale with the atmospheric dread of a gothic nightmare, Schwab’s debut novel is a striking exploration of folklore, isolation, and the terrifying cost of keeping secrets.

At the heart of *The Near Witch* lies a powerful thematic exploration of the stories we tell ourselves to survive. The isolated town of Near is built on absolute truths—or rather, absolute dogma. There are no strangers, the wind must not be answered, and the Near Witch is nothing more than a cautionary fable used to tuck children into bed. Schwab uses this insular community to examine how fear dictates human behavior. When the fragile illusion of safety is shattered by the arrival of a mysterious, smoke-like boy and the subsequent disappearance of the town's children, the villagers immediately resort to paranoia and scapegoating. The novel brilliantly deconstructs the mob mentality, showing how quickly a tight-knit society can turn on the unfamiliar when confronted with the unexplained. 

Lexi, the novel’s fiercely independent protagonist, serves as the perfect lens through which to explore these themes. She is not a passive damsel waiting to be rescued, but a young woman accustomed to the wild moors and the unspoken rules of her home. Her journey is one of unlearning—stripping away the comforting lies she has been fed since childhood to face the stark, howling reality of the wind and the witch. 

Stylistically, *The Near Witch* reads like a dark lullaby. Schwab’s prose is exceptionally lyrical, drenched in sensory details that make the cold moor air practically bite at the reader’s skin. The pacing is deliberate, echoing the relentless, whispering nature of the wind that haunts the margins of the narrative. Schwab leans heavily into the traditions of classic fairy tales, where the boundaries between the natural and supernatural are razor-thin, and where magic always demands a sacrifice. Every sentence feels weighed and measured, contributing to a cohesive, melancholic atmosphere that lingers long after the final page. While seasoned readers of Schwab will recognize the narrative DNA that later blossomed in her *Shades of Magic* series and *The Invisible Life of Addie LaRue*, the debut possesses a raw, haunting innocence that makes it wholly unique.

Complete with the bonus in-universe short story "The Ash-Born Boy" and a deeply personal introduction from the author, this edition honors a foundational piece of modern fantasy. *The Near Witch* is a spellbinding reminder that the scariest monsters are often the ones we refuse to name, and that sometimes, the only way to find the truth is to open the window and listen to the wind.
