# Our Wives Under the Sea

**Author:** Julia Armfield  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

Julia Armfield’s *Our Wives Under the Sea* is a novel that lingers like the last breath of a diver ascending too quickly—unnerving, beautiful, and impossible to shake. At its core, this debut is a meditation on love, loss, and the uncharted territories of the human psyche, rendered through the fractured lens of Leah, a marine biologist who returns from a submerged expedition irrevocably altered. The book’s power lies not just in its haunting premise—what happened to Leah beneath the waves?—but in how Armfield transforms the ordinary into the surreal, weaving a narrative that feels both intimate and vast, like the ocean itself.

The novel’s strength is its refusal to offer easy answers. Leah’s transformation is never fully explained, leaving Miri—and the reader—grappled with the same unsettling questions: Is Leah haunted by something tangible, or is her change a metaphor for the unknowable depths of trauma, grief, or even love itself? Armfield’s prose is a masterclass in restraint and precision, each sentence a coral reef of layered meaning. The writing is achingly poetic, yet never saccharine; it’s the kind of language that makes you pause, as if you’ve just surfaced from a long dive and the air tastes different. Descriptions of the sea—its weight, its silence, its indifference—become metaphors for Leah’s internal world, while the mundane rituals of domestic life (running taps, pacing, the hum of a refrigerator) take on a surreal, almost ritualistic weight.

What makes *Our Wives Under the Sea* so compelling is its exploration of the spaces between people. Miri’s love for Leah is both her anchor and her undoing; she clings to her wife, desperate for clarity, while Leah drifts further into her own silence. The tension between them is electric, charged with the kind of quiet fury that comes from loving someone who has become, in some way, unrecognizable. Armfield doesn’t shy away from the discomfort of this dynamic—there’s no neat resolution, no grand revelation. Instead, she invites the reader to sit with the ambiguity, to question what it means to love someone who is slipping away, whether through illness, grief, or something far stranger.

The novel’s title itself is a double entendre, a nod to the wives left behind and the wives Leah might have become—monsters, ghosts, or something in between. Armfield’s world-building is immersive, blending the scientific rigor of marine biology with the mythic weight of the deep sea. The submarine’s descent isn’t just a physical journey; it’s a descent into the unknown, both external and internal. By the time Leah surfaces, she carries something with her—not just the physical remnants of her ordeal, but the weight of what she’s seen or become. The question isn’t just *what happened*, but *what does it mean to return changed?*

Armfield’s style is both lyrical and sharp, her prose capable of tenderness and brutality in the same breath. There’s a sadistic elegance to her suspense, a deliberate withholding of answers that keeps the reader hooked. The novel’s structure mirrors Leah’s fractured state—her thoughts are disjointed, her memories fragmented, and the narrative itself feels like a piece of a puzzle missing its center. Yet, in this fragmentation, there’s a profound beauty. Armfield doesn’t offer comfort; she offers truth, raw and unflinching.

Ultimately, *Our Wives Under the Sea* is a novel about the love that persists even when understanding is impossible. It’s a story about the ways we cling to each other, about the moments when the bond between two people becomes both their salvation and their prison. Armfield’s debut is a haunting, poetic, and deeply human exploration of what it means to love someone who has been lost to the depths—whether by choice, circumstance, or something far more mysterious.
