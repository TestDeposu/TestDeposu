# Kingdom of the Wicked (Skulduggery Pleasant, #7)

**Author:** Derek Landy  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

In the seventh instalment of the Skulduggery Pleasant series, Kingdom of the Wicked, Derek Landy masterfully weaves a tale of magic, mayhem, and the blurred lines between good and evil. The story takes place in a world where magic has become a disease, infecting ordinary people and transforming them into powerful, yet unstable, beings. It's a world where the boundaries between reality and chaos are constantly shifting, and the only ones who can restore order are the enigmatic Skulduggery Pleasant and his trusted ally, Valkyrie Cain.

One of the standout features of Landy's writing style is his ability to balance humor and horror. He effortlessly shifts between witty banter and dark, suspenseful moments, keeping readers on the edge of their seats. The characters in the series are multidimensional and complex, with each one bringing their unique personality to the table. Skulduggery, with his wisecracking humor and sharp wit, is the perfect counterbalance to Valkyrie's more serious and brooding nature.

The main theme of Kingdom of the Wicked is the corrupting influence of power. Kitana, a normal teenage girl, becomes infected with magic and quickly descends into madness and destruction. Her story serves as a cautionary tale about the dangers of unchecked power and the importance of accountability. Through Kitana's character, Landy explores the idea that even the most well-intentioned individuals can become consumed by their own ambition and desire for control.

Landy's world-building is also noteworthy. He creates a rich, immersive environment that's full of magical creatures, ancient prophecies, and hidden worlds. The Sanctuary, a secret organization dedicated to protecting the world from magical threats, is a particularly interesting aspect of the series. It's a place where magic is studied, practiced, and sometimes, exploited.

The pacing of the book is relentless, with action-packed sequences and plot twists that keep readers guessing. Landy's writing is engaging, and his characters are relatable and well-developed. The story is full of clever references and nods to other works of fantasy, which adds to the overall sense of fun and adventure.

In conclusion, Kingdom of the Wicked is a thrilling addition to the Skulduggery Pleasant series. It's a book that will appeal to fans of fantasy, adventure, and humor. With its unique blend of magic, mayhem, and wit, it's a must-read for anyone looking for a compelling and entertaining story.
