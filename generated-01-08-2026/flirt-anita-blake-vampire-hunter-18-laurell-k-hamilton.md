# Flirt (Anita Blake, Vampire Hunter, #18)

**Author:** Laurell K. Hamilton  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

Laurell K. Hamilton's Anita Blake series has captivated readers with its unique blend of supernatural mystery, romance, and dark fantasy. The 18th installment, "Flirt," is part of a collection of books 16-19, which also includes "Blood Noir," "Skin Trade," and "Bullet." While this review focuses on "Flirt," it's essential to acknowledge the series' progression and Hamilton's distinctive style.

In "Flirt," Anita Blake is asked to raise the dead, but the results aren't going to please everyone. This central plot thread allows Hamilton to delve into the complexities of Anita's powers as a necromancer and her moral dilemmas when dealing with the consequences of her actions. As Anita navigates the world of vampire politics and supernatural creatures, she must confront her own darkness and the blurred lines between good and evil.

One of the standout features of Hamilton's writing is her ability to create a rich, immersive world that draws readers in. The Anita Blake series is known for its intricate mythology, and "Flirt" is no exception. Hamilton's vampires, werewolves, and other supernatural creatures are multidimensional and complex, with their own motivations and conflicts. This depth of world-building is a testament to Hamilton's skill as a storyteller and her dedication to crafting a believable and engaging universe.

Hamilton's writing style is also noteworthy for its blend of dark humor, witty dialogue, and intense action sequences. Anita Blake is a strong, capable protagonist who is not afraid to speak her mind and stand up for herself. Her relationships with her lovers, including Jean-Claude and Richard, are a central aspect of the series, and "Flirt" is no exception. The romance and eroticism in the novel are integral to the plot, adding depth and complexity to the characters and their interactions.

One of the criticisms of the Anita Blake series is that it has become increasingly focused on the romantic and erotic aspects of Anita's life, at the expense of the mystery and suspense that drove the earlier novels. While "Flirt" does contain its fair share of romantic entanglements and explicit content, it also delivers on the mystery and suspense fronts. The plot is engaging and well-paced, with a number of unexpected twists and turns that keep the reader guessing.

In conclusion, "Flirt" is a solid addition to the Anita Blake series, offering a compelling blend of supernatural mystery, romance, and dark fantasy. Hamilton's writing style is engaging and immersive, and her world-building is as strong as ever. While the series may have evolved over time, "Flirt" is a must-read for fans of the series and anyone looking for a captivating urban fantasy novel.
