# City of Lies (Poison Wars, #1)

**Author:** Sam Hawke  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

In the world of fantasy, it's not often that a debut novel captures the attention of readers and critics alike with such force. Sam Hawke's "City of Lies" is a masterful tale of intrigue, deception, and ancient magic that has earned its place among the most acclaimed works in the genre. This gripping epic fantasy, winner of multiple awards, including the Ditmar Awards, the Norma K Hemming Award, and the Aurealis Award for Best Fantasy Novel, is a must-read for anyone who delights in complex characters, moral ambiguity, and rich world-building.

At the heart of "City of Lies" lies the intricate web of relationships and alliances that govern the city-state of Silasta. Jovan, the protagonist, is a nobleman with a secret: he's a skilled poisoner, trained to protect the Chancellor's family from threats both internal and external. With his sister Kalina, a warrior with a troubled past, Jovan must navigate the treacherous landscape of court politics and ancient magic to save the Heir and their city from destruction.

Hawke's writing style is characterized by its subtlety and nuance. The world of Silasta is richly detailed, with a deep history that underpins the narrative. The author's use of language is evocative, conjuring vivid images of the city's architecture, its people, and the mysterious forces that shape their lives. The pacing is deliberate, with a steady build-up of tension that propels the reader toward the novel's climax.

One of the most striking aspects of "City of Lies" is its exploration of the complexities of human relationships. Jovan's bond with the Heir, his sister Kalina, and the other characters in the novel is multifaceted and often fraught with tension. Hawke skillfully portrays the moral ambiguities that govern human behavior, where right and wrong are not always clear-cut, and where the line between loyalty and betrayal is constantly blurred.

The novel's themes of power, corruption, and the responsibility that comes with knowledge are timeless and thought-provoking. Hawke's portrayal of a world where ancient magic and modern politics collide is both captivating and unsettling, echoing the darker aspects of our own world. The author's use of poison and chemistry as a metaphor for the destructive power of knowledge is particularly effective, highlighting the dangers of unchecked ambition and the corrupting influence of power.

In "City of Lies," Sam Hawke has created a world that is both familiar and strange, where the boundaries between good and evil are constantly shifting. This is a novel that will appeal to fans of Scott Lynch, Patrick Rothfuss, and N.K. Jemisin, with its complex characters, rich world-building, and gripping narrative. If you're looking for a fantasy novel that will challenge your assumptions and keep you on the edge of your seat, look no further than "City of Lies."
