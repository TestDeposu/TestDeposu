# Murder Is Easy (Superintendent Battle, #4)

**Author:** Agatha Christie  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2026

Agatha Christie remains the undisputed queen of deception, and in Murder Is Easy, she crafts a masterclass in psychological misdirection that lingers long after the final page. Although often associated with her more famous sleuths, this installment places the formidable Superintendent Battle at the helm of an investigation that is as chilling as it is brilliant. Published with the timeless allure that defines the Golden Age of detective fiction, this novel explores a dark and unsettling theme: the weaponization of ordinary gossip and the terrifying ease with which malice can hide behind a polite smile.

The narrative kicks off when an unassuming errand pulls protagonist Anthony Cade into a web of international intrigue, eventually drawing the combined forces of Scotland Yard and the French Sûreté to the grand and secretive country estate of Chimneys. But beneath the veneer of country-house elegance lies a toxic undercurrent of murder. Christie brilliantly juxtaposes the idyllic English village setting with a high body count, subverting the cozy trope by proving that evil does not always lurk in dark alleys; sometimes, it pours tea in the parlor. 

Christie’s signature style shines brightest in her manipulation of human psychology. She understands that the most effective killers are those who blend seamlessly into the background. The central mystery hinges on the concept that people are rarely suspicious of the unremarkable. While the original synopsis hints at international conspiracies and grand estates, the true beating heart of the novel is the insidious nature of rural gossip and the quiet accumulation of suspicion. 

As Superintendent Battle navigates the labyrinth of lies at Chimneys, readers are treated to a masterclass in clue-dropping. Christie plays fair with her audience, scattering vital pieces of the puzzle in plain sight, yet she manages to pull the rug out from under even the most astute mystery aficionados. The pacing is crisp, balancing the tension of an unfolding conspiracy with the razor-sharp wit and astute social observation that made Christie a literary legend. 

Murder Is Easy is a triumphant reminder of why Agatha Christie’s works are timeless. It is a sleek, clever, and endlessly entertaining puzzle that proves human nature is the most dangerous mystery of all. Whether you are a lifelong devotee of the genre or a newcomer stepping into the world of Chimneys for the first time, this gripping tale will keep you guessing until the very end.
