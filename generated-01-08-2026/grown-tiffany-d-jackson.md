# Grown

**Author:** Tiffany D. Jackson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

**Grown by Tiffany D. Jackson: A Searing, Unflinching Mirror of Black Girlhood and the Cost of the American Dream**

Tiffany D. Jackson’s *Grown* is a masterclass in tension, a razor-sharp thriller that doesn’t just tell a story—it *unravels* one, layer by agonizing layer. At its core, this book is a brutal examination of misogynoir, the systemic erasure of Black women’s voices, and the way power—especially in the music industry—exploits and destroys those who dare to dream. But what makes *Grown* so devastatingly compelling isn’t just its gripping plot; it’s Jackson’s unrelenting honesty, her refusal to soften the edges of trauma, and her ability to make the reader *feel* the weight of a system that would rather believe a Black girl is guilty than confront the monsters who protect abusers.

The story follows Enchanted Jones, a young Black woman with a voice so raw and powerful that Korey Fields, a legendary R&B artist, takes notice. Enchanted’s audition becomes her ticket to stardom—or so she thinks. But Korey Fields isn’t just a star; he’s a predator, and his charm masks a darkness that will leave Enchanted’s life in ruins. When Korey is found dead, blood on her hands, Enchanted is the prime suspect. And here’s the horror: she doesn’t remember what happened. Her memory is fractured, her trust in herself shattered. The dual timeline—one where Enchanted is a suspect, the other where she’s a victim—creates a psychological pressure cooker, forcing readers to question not just *who* did it, but *why* no one believes her.

Jackson’s writing is electric, her prose sharp enough to cut through the gloss of fame and expose the rot beneath. She doesn’t shy away from the brutality of Black women’s experiences in a world that would rather silence them than listen. The music industry, with its toxic masculinity and complicit systems, serves as a perfect microcosm for the broader themes of *Grown*: how Black girls are policed, how their pain is dismissed, and how the pursuit of dreams can become a nightmare when the people in power refuse to hold abusers accountable. Enchanted’s journey is one of survival, but also of reckoning—with her own complicity, with the lies she’s been told, and with the truth that the system will always favor the powerful.

What sets *Grown* apart isn’t just its gripping mystery (though the twists are relentless) but its emotional gut-punch. Jackson doesn’t offer easy answers or neat resolutions. Instead, she forces readers to sit with the discomfort, to confront the ways in which Black women are made to carry the weight of others’ sins. The book is a #MeToo narrative for young adults, a searing indictment of how easily young Black girls are villainized while the real monsters walk free. Enchanted’s struggle to reclaim her voice is both heartbreaking and empowering, a testament to the resilience of survivors in a world that would rather see them broken.

Jackson’s style is immersive, her dialogue crackling with authenticity, and her ability to blend suspense with social commentary is nothing short of brilliant. *Grown* isn’t just a thriller—it’s a necessary conversation starter, a book that lingers long after the last page because it doesn’t just tell a story; it *challenges* the reader to look harder, to listen closer, and to never forget the faces of the girls who are made to disappear.

If you’ve ever wanted a story that’s as gripping as it is necessary, *Grown* is it. Tiffany D. Jackson doesn’t just write about the underbelly of fame—she exposes it, with all its ugliness and all its truth. And in doing so, she gives voice to the girls who have been silenced for far too long.
