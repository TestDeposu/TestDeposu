# The Dead-Tossed Waves (The Forest of Hands and Teeth, #2)

**Author:** Carrie Ryan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

In the haunting world of Carrie Ryan's The Dead-Tossed Waves, the lines between safety and danger, love and loss, are constantly blurred. The second installment of The Forest of Hands and Teeth series, this novel plunges readers into a desolate landscape where the undead roam, and the living are forced to fight for survival.

Gabry, the protagonist, is a complex and multi-dimensional character, whose quiet life is shattered by the secrets of her mother's past. Ryan masterfully weaves together the threads of Gabry's narrative, slowly unraveling the mysteries that have haunted her family for years. As Gabry navigates the treacherous world beyond her town's Barrier, she must confront the darkness that lurks within herself, and the true horrors that await her in the Forest of Hands and Teeth.

Ryan's writing is atmospheric and evocative, conjuring up images of a world gone mad, where the dead are a constant presence, and the living are forced to live in fear. Her prose is lyrical and engaging, drawing the reader into Gabry's world, and refusing to let go. The pacing is relentless, with each chapter ending on a cliffhanger, propelling the reader forward into the unknown.

One of the most striking aspects of The Dead-Tossed Waves is its exploration of the human condition. In a world where death is a constant presence, Ryan's characters are forced to confront their own mortality, and the true meaning of life. Gabry's journey is a powerful exploration of love, loss, and the human spirit, and raises questions about what it means to be alive in a world that is determined to destroy us.

The supporting cast of characters is equally well-developed, with each one bringing their own unique perspective to the narrative. The character of Elias, in particular, is a standout, with his brooding intensity, and complex motivations. The romance between Gabry and Elias is sweet, and tender, but never overshadows the main plot, which is a testament to Ryan's skill as a storyteller.

The world-building in The Dead-Tossed Waves is equally impressive, with Ryan expanding on the mythology of the Forest of Hands and Teeth. The cult of religious zealots who worship the dead is a chilling addition to the narrative, and adds a new layer of complexity to the world. The Barrier, which separates Gabry's town from the Forest, is a powerful symbol of the fragility of human existence, and the dangers that lurk beyond our perceived safety nets.

In conclusion, The Dead-Tossed Waves is a gripping, and thought-provoking novel, that will appeal to fans of dystopian fiction, and horror. Ryan's writing is atmospheric, and engaging, and her characters are complex, and multi-dimensional. The themes of love, loss, and the human condition are timeless, and will resonate with readers long after they finish the book.
