# Murder of Crows (The Others, #2)

**Author:** Anne Bishop  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

Murder of Crows, the second installment in Anne Bishop's The Others series, is a gripping and immersive tale that delves into the complexities of a world where supernatural entities and humans coexist in an uneasy harmony. The story picks up where the first book left off, with Meg Corbyn, a cassandra sangue, or blood prophet, navigating her unique position within the Lakeside Courtyard, a community of shape-shifters, vampires, and other supernatural beings.

Bishop's world-building is once again a highlight of the series, as she expertly weaves together the intricate social dynamics of the Others and the humans who live among them. The arrival of two addictive drugs sparks a wave of violence between the species, threatening the fragile peace that has been established. As tensions rise, Meg's prophetic abilities become increasingly important, and she finds herself at the center of a maelstrom of danger and intrigue.

One of the most compelling aspects of Bishop's writing is her ability to create complex, multidimensional characters. Meg, in particular, is a fascinating protagonist, whose struggles to understand her place in the world are deeply relatable. Her relationships with the Others, particularly Simon Wolfgard, the shape-shifting leader of the Lakeside Courtyard, are nuanced and multifaceted, adding depth and richness to the story.

The author's style is characterized by a unique blend of dark fantasy and social commentary, as she explores themes of prejudice, tolerance, and the dangers of unchecked power. Bishop's writing is evocative and atmospheric, with a keen sense of pacing that propels the reader through the story. Her use of language is deliberate and concise, conjuring vivid images of the world and its inhabitants.

As the story unfolds, Bishop skillfully ratchets up the tension, introducing a range of threats to the stability of the Courtyard and the relationships between the characters. The plot is expertly constructed, with a series of twists and turns that keep the reader guessing until the very end.

Throughout the book, Bishop's exploration of the main theme – the struggle for coexistence between humans and supernatural entities – is thoughtful and provocative. She raises important questions about the nature of prejudice and the dangers of fear and mistrust, and her characters' struggles to overcome these obstacles are both compelling and deeply human.

In conclusion, Murder of Crows is a gripping and immersive read, with a richly detailed world, complex characters, and a thought-provoking exploration of the human condition. Bishop's unique blend of dark fantasy and social commentary makes for a compelling narrative that will appeal to fans of the genre. With its intricate plotting, memorable characters, and atmospheric setting, this book is a must-read for anyone looking for a compelling and immersive fantasy series.
