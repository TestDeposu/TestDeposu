# Tenth of December

**Author:** George Saunders  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

George Saunders does not merely write short stories; he captures the frantic, fractured heartbeat of modern existence and lays it bare on the page. In his critically acclaimed and widely celebrated 2013 collection, Tenth of December, Saunders cements his status as one of the most vital and blazingly original voices of his generation. This is a book that arrives with immense literary fanfare—a National Book Award finalist, a New York Times bestseller, and a fixture on nearly every major "Best of the Decade" list—yet it effortlessly transcends the hype, delivering an emotional resonance that lingers long after the final page.

At the thematic core of Tenth of December is a relentless, deeply empathetic interrogation of modern morality. Saunders is endlessly fascinated by the fault lines of human nature: the quiet compromises we make, the invisible walls of class and socialization that separate us, and the desperate, often clumsy ways we try to do the right thing. Across these pages, characters are consistently thrust to the absolute precipice of choice. In the collection’s taut, brilliant opener, “Victory Lap,” a young boy watches an abduction unfold next door, forced to weigh his parents’ suffocating rules against the raw impulse to act. In “Home,” a war-weary veteran returns to a domestic sphere that feels entirely alien, struggling to bridge the chasm between his trauma and civilian normalcy. And in the breathtaking title story, a cancer-stricken man wandering into a snowy forest to end his life is unexpectedly intercepted by an awkward, imaginative boy. It is a stunning meditation on memory, dignity, and the profound grace of human connection.

What makes Saunders singular is his stylistic alchemy. He possesses a rare linguistic virtuosity that effortlessly marries manic, high-energy satire with profound, unyielding tenderness. His characters often speak and think in heavily stylized, consumer-addled vernaculars—invented slang, corporate jargon, and fragmented internal monologues that initially disorient the reader. Yet, beneath these linguistic quirks lies a wellspring of desperate, aching humanity. Saunders never looks down upon his hapless protagonists; whether they are pharmaceutical guinea pigs manipulated into chemical emotional states, or struggling mothers trying to scrape by, he illuminates their flaws with dazzling wit and fierce compassion. 

This duality is what elevates the collection. Saunders is acutely aware of the absurdities, commercial pressures, and cruelties of contemporary life, but he refuses to succumb to cynicism. Instead, his stories pulse with an underlying generosity of spirit. They remind us of the miraculous capacity for redemption that exists even in the most ordinary, downtrodden souls. 

Tenth of December is unsettling, darkly hilarious, and devastatingly moving. It fulfills Anton Chekhov’s famous dictum that the role of an artist is not to solve problems, but to ask the right questions and prepare us for tenderness. For anyone seeking fiction that vibrates with intelligence, stylistic audacity, and a profound understanding of what it means to be human, Saunders’s collection is nothing short of essential reading.
