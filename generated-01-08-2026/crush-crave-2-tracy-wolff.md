# Crush (Crave, #2)

**Author:** Tracy Wolff  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

Shadows, Secrets, and the Seductive Trap of YA Paranormal Romance: A Review of Tracy Wolff’s Crush

When Tracy Wolff’s *Crave* hit bookshelves, it seized the young adult fantasy landscape with the ferocious grip of a starved vampire. It tapped into a nostalgic hunger for brooding immortals, high-stakes boarding school drama, and ordinary heroines thrust into extraordinary, terrifying worlds. Now, with the second installment in the blockbuster series, *Crush* (2020), Wolff doubles down on the chaos, confusion, and combustible chemistry that made its predecessor a #1 *New York Times* bestseller. 

At its core, *Crush* explores the dizzying, often terrifying theme of identity and reclamation. Grace Vega returns to the snow-draped, deadly halls of Katmere Academy fundamentally altered. She is haunted by amnesiac fragments of days she cannot remember living, wrestling with a shifting, monstrous truth of who—and what—she really is. Wolff uses this literal memory loss as a clever metaphor for the universal adolescent experience: that disorienting phase where the self feels entirely alien, and the boundaries between who we were and who we are becoming blur dangerously. Grace is no longer just the grieving mortal cousin trying to survive a supernatural school; she is a walking enigma, a vessel of power that even she does not understand.

This theme of fragmented selfhood extends outward into the book’s central conflict: a high-stakes power struggle where trust is the ultimate currency and everyone, from the Vampire Court to the secretive Circle, wants a piece of Grace. Enter Hudson, the enigmatic, dangerous brother of Jaxon. Banished to the periphery in the first book, Hudson returns with a vengeance in *Crush*, injecting a volatile dynamic into the narrative. He claims to hold the missing pieces of Grace’s memory, threatening to wedge an unbridgeable chasm between Grace and Jaxon. The love triangle here is not merely romantic; it is existential. Choosing a side means choosing a version of reality, a version of history, and ultimately, a version of Grace’s own soul.

Wolff’s authorial style in *Crush* is unapologetically fast-paced, binge-worthy, and aggressively modern. She writes with a cinematic urgency that treats every chapter like a cliffhanger. Gone are the days of languid, gothic world-building found in classic vampire lore; Wolff swaps Victorian slow-burn for punchy dialogue, pop-culture-laced internal monologues, and breathless pacing that mirrors Grace’s frantic heartbeat. The prose is designed to overwhelm the senses, plunging the reader directly into the emotional whirlygig of teenage angst amplified by immortal stakes. 

Critics of the genre might point out that the dialogue leans heavily into snark and the plotting relies on relentless twists, but that is precisely where the charm of *Crush* lies. Wolff understands her audience. She crafts a sensory overload of snow, blood, magic, and hormones that is impossible to put down. Every time Grace seems to find her footing, the rug is violently pulled out from under her, escalating a local school drama into an apocalyptic battle for survival. 

Ultimately, *Crush* is a triumphant sophomore slump-breaker. It takes the addictive formula of the first book and expands the mythology, deepens the emotional stakes, and complicates the loyalties that bind its characters. It is a messy, glittering, emotional rollercoaster about the terrifying price of power and the sacrifices required to protect the people we love. For readers willing to surrender to its frantic, supernatural momentum, *Crush* is an absolute page-turner that proves the paranormal romance phenomenon is far from dead—it is only getting hungrier.
