# The Cow in the Parking Lot: A Zen Approach to Overcoming Anger

**Author:** Leonard Scheff  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

In "The Cow in the Parking Lot: A Zen Approach to Overcoming Anger," Leonard Scheff and Susan Edmiston present a refreshingly straightforward guide to managing anger by applying simple yet profound Buddhist principles. This thought-provoking book has garnered praise from esteemed authors and publications, including Dr. Howard C. Cutler, coauthor of "The Art of Happiness," who hails it as "one of the best books I've ever read on anger."

The authors' approach is both lucid and comprehensive, making it accessible to readers from diverse backgrounds, regardless of their familiarity with Buddhist teachings. The book's foundation is rooted in the Transforming Anger workshop created by Scheff, which offers a step-by-step process for transforming anger into compassion.

At its core, "The Cow in the Parking Lot" reveals that anger stems from unmet demands, ranging from the reasonable to the irrational and impossible. By recognizing and understanding these demands, readers can begin to dissolve their anger and replace it with a deeper sense of happiness. The authors skillfully illustrate how laughter and self-awareness can be powerful tools in this transformation.

One of the book's greatest strengths lies in its ability to address the complexities of anger in a way that is both engaging and easy to understand. Scheff and Edmiston provide practical advice on dealing with the anger of others, as well as strategies for transforming anger into compassion. A critical aspect of their approach is the recognition that individuals have the power to choose how they respond to anger-provoking situations.

Throughout the book, the authors' writing style is clear, concise, and free of jargon, making it an enjoyable read for those seeking a fresh perspective on anger management. The use of relatable anecdotes and real-life examples adds depth and context to the authors' arguments, rendering the book an invaluable resource for anyone struggling with anger.

Ultimately, "The Cow in the Parking Lot" offers a liberating message: only we can make ourselves angry. By embracing this truth and applying the principles outlined in the book, readers can break free from the cycle of anger and cultivate a more compassionate, happy life.

In conclusion, "The Cow in the Parking Lot" is a remarkable book that provides a unique and effective approach to managing anger. With its engaging writing style, practical advice, and profound insights, it is an essential read for anyone seeking to transform their relationship with anger and cultivate a deeper sense of happiness.
