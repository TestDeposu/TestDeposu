# That Wedding (That Boy, #2)

**Author:** Jillian Dodd  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

When Jillian Dodd released That Wedding, the highly anticipated sequel to her bestselling debut That Boy, readers eagerly rushed back into the sun-soaked, emotionally charged world of Jadyn and Philip. If the first book was a nostalgic, butterflies-in-your-stomach celebration of first love and teenage yearning, this sequel dives headfirst into the messy, high-stakes reality of growing up and walking down the aisle. Dodd crafts a striking exploration of what happens after the fairy tale ending is chosen, proving that happily ever after is not a destination, but a relentless choice.

At its core, That Wedding explores the universal and terrifying theme of transition—the fragile space between youthful romance and permanent maturity. Jadyn’s gleaming engagement ring is supposed to be the ultimate symbol of triumph, the tangible proof that she secured her prince. Yet, Dodd brilliantly subverts the traditional romance trope by stripping away the glitter and forcing her protagonist to confront existential dread. The narrative thrives on the friction between fantasy and reality. Jadyn’s anxiety is not merely about finding the right flowers or a flawless gown; it is a profound psychological wrestling match with her own past, her deep-seated insecurities, and the daunting finality of forever. 

Dodd’s signature writing style is undeniably addictive, characterized by a breezy, conversational tone that reads like a confessional diary entry from a best friend. She excels at capturing the sensory details of youth—the pop-culture references, the glittering anticipation, and the intoxicating physical chemistry that often threatens to cloud better judgment. However, in this installment, Dodd matures alongside her characters. She injects a welcome layer of psychological tension into the narrative. The mounting pressure from judgmental parents, intrusive pre-marital counseling, ominous stress dreams, and the sudden reappearance of an ex-boyfriend serve as narrative catalysts that push Jadyn to her absolute breaking point. 

What makes the critique of the book so compelling is how unflinchingly honest Dodd is about the flaws in her characters. Jadyn is not a passive, perfect bride; she is anxious, occasionally manipulative, and fiercely overwhelmed. Her internal monologue poses the questions every reader secretly fears: Can childhood sweethearts truly survive adulthood? Is physical attraction enough to sustain a marriage when the ghosts of the past refuse to stay buried? By allowing her characters to stumble, sweat, and question their life choices, Dodd elevates the story from a fluffy beach read into a relatable examination of commitment anxiety.

Ultimately, That Wedding is a striking and emotionally resonant sequel that delivers both comfort and chaos. Jillian Dodd maintains her trademark effortless pacing while offering a deeper, more mature look at the sacrifices required for true love. It is a cautionary yet deeply romantic tale about silencing the noise of expectations, managing cold feet, and discovering whether the boy of your dreams is truly the man you want beside you for a lifetime.
