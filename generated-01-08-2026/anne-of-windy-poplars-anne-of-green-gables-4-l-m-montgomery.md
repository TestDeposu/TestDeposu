# Anne of Windy Poplars (Anne of Green Gables, #4)

**Author:** L.M. Montgomery  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

L.M. Montgomery returns to the beloved world of Prince Edward Island with Anne of Windy Poplars, a charming episodic journey that captures the bittersweet transition from youth to maturity. While often celebrated for its cozy atmosphere, this fourth installment in the iconic Anne Shirley series offers a deeper, more intimate exploration of resilience, community, and the quiet art of winning over the human heart. 

At its core, the main theme of the novel is the transformative power of empathy and imaginative grace when confronting entrenched hostility. Moving away from the idyllic pastures of Green Gables and the academic halls of Redmond College, Anne finds herself in Summerside as the principal of the local high school. However, she immediately encounters a formidable social blockade: the Pringles, a sprawling clan who rule the town like literary royalty and make no secret of their disdain for the new educator. 

Rather than engaging in petty conflict, Montgomery uses Anne’s struggles with the Pringles to showcase a masterclass in emotional intelligence. Anne’s journey is not merely about surviving her opposition, but about actively dismantling it through warmth, wit, and unwavering integrity. Through this central conflict, Montgomery champions the idea that true community is not given, but built—one difficult personality, one shared secret, and one act of unexpected kindness at a time. 

Stylistically, Montgomery employs a unique epistolary-narrative hybrid that sets this book apart from its predecessors. Told largely through the vibrant, affectionate letters Anne writes to her eternal sweetheart, Gilbert Blythe, the novel possesses an intensely personal rhythm. This format allows Montgomery to lean heavily into her signature epistolary charm, blending laugh-out-loud humor with moments of profound, lyrical introspection. 

The author’s descriptive prose remains as vivid as ever, breathing undeniable life into the physical and social landscape of Summerside. Windy Poplars itself—with its quirky tower room, the delightful eccentricities of the widowed hostesses Aunt Kate and Aunt Chatty, and the unforgettable, delightfully dramatic housekeeper Rebecca Dew—becomes a character in its own right. Montgomery has an uncanny knack for populating her fictional universes with unforgettable eccentrics, yet she grounds them in genuine human emotion. The tragicomic subplots of Summerside's residents, ranging from eccentric romances to generational feuds, reflect Montgomery’s sharp eye for the absurdities and heartbreaks of small-town life. 

Anne of Windy Poplars is a striking reminder of why L.M. Montgomery remains a literary icon. It is a comforting yet intellectually engaging read that celebrates the triumph of spirit over stubbornness. For longtime fans and new readers alike, stepping back into Anne’s world is like coming home—only to realize that even familiar places hold new lessons in grace, love, and laughter.
