# A Darkness at Sethanon (The Riftwar Saga, #4)

**Author:** Raymond E. Feist  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1987

**A Darkness at Sethanon: The Epic Clash of Light and Shadow in Feist’s Masterpiece**

Raymond E. Feist’s *A Darkness at Sethanon*—the fourth installment in *The Riftwar Saga*—is a towering achievement, a novel that distills the grandeur of mythic fantasy into a relentless, emotionally charged battle between good and evil. Here, the stakes are not just the fate of Midkemia but the very fabric of existence, as the ancient Enemy, a force of pure malice, stirs from the depths of time to unmake all creation. Feist, ever the architect of sweeping sagas, crafts a climax that is both intellectually profound and visceral in its intensity, proving once again why his work endures as a cornerstone of fantasy literature.

At its heart, *A Darkness at Sethanon* is a meditation on the cost of power and the fragility of order. Pug, the once-humble slave turned master magician, stands at the precipice of destiny, his journey from obscurity to near-divinity a testament to the transformative power of will. Yet Feist doesn’t glorify his protagonist’s rise—he exposes its toll. Pug’s growth is marked by sacrifice, moral ambiguity, and the crushing weight of responsibility. His alliance with the dark sorcerer Chogath, his betrayals, and his eventual confrontation with the Enemy are not just plot devices but a study in the corrupting influence of absolute knowledge. Feist forces his characters to grapple with the question: *How much of yourself must you surrender to stop the unthinkable?* The answer is never simple, and that’s what makes Pug’s arc so compelling.

The novel’s strength lies in its balance—between the personal and the cosmic, the political and the metaphysical. Feist weaves together the fates of kingdoms, the intrigues of courts, and the personal vendettas of warriors like the noble but flawed Duke Borric and the relentless assassin Arutha. Yet beneath these earthly struggles looms the looming shadow of the Enemy, a being whose existence defies logic, whose malice is not just personal but existential. Feist doesn’t shy away from the horror of the unknown; instead, he uses it to heighten the tension, making the reader feel the weight of an impending doom that cannot be reasoned away. The battle at Sethanon isn’t just a fight for a city—it’s a last stand against the erosion of all meaning itself.

Feist’s prose is deceptively simple, a hallmark of his style that belies its depth. He favors clarity over florid description, allowing the power of his storytelling to emerge from the characters’ actions and the inevitability of their fates. His dialogue crackles with authenticity, whether it’s the sharp wit of the mage Pug or the weary pragmatism of the warrior Arutha. The world-building, too, is masterful—Midkemia feels lived-in, its cultures and conflicts rooted in history yet fresh enough to feel timeless. Feist’s ability to make magic feel tangible, to ground the supernatural in the mundane, is what elevates *A Darkness at Sethanon* from a mere adventure into something transcendent.

The climax itself is a masterclass in fantasy storytelling. The siege of Sethanon is not just a battle but a symphony of chaos and order, where every character’s arc converges in a final, desperate struggle. Feist doesn’t offer easy victories; the cost of triumph is measured in blood, betrayal, and the loss of those we love. The Enemy’s true nature is revealed not through grand speeches but through the quiet, creeping dread of its presence, a force that twists reality itself. When the dust settles, the reader is left with a sense of catharsis—not because the world is saved without sacrifice, but because the fight was worth it.

For fans of epic fantasy, *A Darkness at Sethanon* is a must-read. It’s a novel that demands your attention, not just for its action but for its depth, its moral complexity, and its unflinching portrayal of the struggle between light and dark. Feist doesn’t just tell a story; he creates a myth, one that lingers long after the last page. If you’ve followed Pug’s journey from the beginning, this is the payoff—a novel that delivers on the promise of its predecessors while standing on its own as a work of unparalleled ambition.

In the end, *A Darkness at Sethanon* is more than a conclusion; it’s a beginning. It sets the stage for what comes next, but its legacy is already secure. Feist’s vision is timeless, his characters unforgettable, and his storytelling unmatched. This is fantasy at its finest—bold, beautiful, and brutally honest.
