# 13 Treasures (Thirteen Treasures, #1)

**Author:** Michelle Harrison  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

Stepping away from the sanitized, sugar-coated magic popularized by modern children’s media, debut author Michelle Harrison delivers a delightfully dark and atmospheric reawakening of authentic folklore in her 2010 fantasy novel, 13 Treasures. Rather than presenting winged sprites granting wishes in sunlit meadows, Harrison introduces readers to a world where fairies are malicious, cunning, and terrifyingly cruel. This central theme—the subversion of innocence and the dangerous allure of the hidden realm—drives a narrative that seamlessly bridges the gap between whimsical fantasy and bone-chilling mystery.

The story follows Tanya, a young girl whose unique and burdensome gift of seeing fairies has driven her exhausted mother to wits' end. Banished to the brooding, isolated expanse of Elvesden Manor to live with her grandmother, Tanya finds herself on the edge of Hangman's Wood, a forbidding forest steeped in local tragedy. Fifty years prior, another child vanished into those very woods, leaving behind a chilling silence that Tanya’s grandmother stubbornly refuses to break. What begins as a tale of youthful displacement quickly evolves into a high-stakes race against time, as Tanya uncovers the dark history of the manor and finds herself perilously close to slipping permanently behind the veil of the fairy realm.

Harrison’s writing style is one of the book’s greatest triumphs. Writing with an assuredness that belies her status as a debut novelist, she crafts a setting that is simultaneously seductive and deeply unsettling. Elvesden Manor and the claustrophobic shadows of Hangman's Wood are rendered with a rich, tactile prose that makes the chill of the English countryside practically seep off the page. The author excels at pacing, constructing a compelling page-turner that keeps readers on the edge of their seats. The mystery of the missing girl is doled out in expertly measured breadcrumbs, ensuring that the puzzle pieces click into place just as the danger reaches a fever pitch. 

Beyond the atmospheric world-building, Harrison populates her dark fairytale with memorable, fully developed characters. Tanya is far from a standard, passive protagonist; she is vulnerable yet fiercely resilient, wrestling not only with supernatural adversaries but with the very real isolation of being misunderstood. Her journey resonates deeply, grounding the fantastical elements in genuine emotional truth. 

Ultimately, 13 Treasures is a triumphant opening act to a trilogy that respects its audience's appetite for suspense and shadow. Harrison proves herself to be an exceptional storyteller who understands that the best fairy tales always carry a sharp edge. For readers seeking a fast-paced, spine-tingling adventure steeped in rich mythology and gothic flair, this captivating debut is an absolute must-read.
