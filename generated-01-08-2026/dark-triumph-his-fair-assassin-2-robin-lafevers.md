# Dark Triumph (His Fair Assassin, #2)

**Author:** Robin LaFevers  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

In "Dark Triumph," the second installment of Robin LaFevers' captivating "His Fair Assassin" trilogy, the author masterfully weaves a tale of revenge, redemption, and the unbreakable bonds of sisterhood. Set in 15th-century France, this historical fantasy novel delves into the darker aspects of human nature, expertly balancing the brutality of the time period with the lyrical, almost poetic prose that has become a hallmark of LaFevers' writing style.

At the heart of the story is Sybella, a young woman trained by the convent of Saint Mortain to be an elite killer, driven by a desire to escape the trauma of her past. Her journey is a complex exploration of the human psyche, as she grapples with the moral implications of her actions and the true meaning of justice. LaFevers' portrayal of Sybella's inner turmoil is both poignant and thought-provoking, raising important questions about the nature of right and wrong in a world where the lines between good and evil are often blurred.

One of the most striking aspects of "Dark Triumph" is its ability to balance the darker elements of the narrative with moments of tenderness and vulnerability. LaFevers' writing is evocative and immersive, conjuring the opulent world of the high court of Brittany with vivid descriptions and a keen sense of historical detail. The characters that inhabit this world are equally well-crafted, with Sybella's dry wit and determination making her a compelling and relatable protagonist.

Throughout the novel, LaFevers explores themes of identity, loyalty, and the power of female relationships. The bond between Sybella and her fellow assassins is a highlight of the series, offering a nuanced portrayal of female friendship and solidarity in a world where women are often marginalized and oppressed. The author's commitment to creating strong, complex female characters is evident throughout the narrative, and Sybella's journey is a testament to the enduring power of female resilience and determination.

LaFevers' writing style is a key element of the novel's success, with her use of language that is both beautiful and unflinching. Her prose is rich and evocative, conjuring the sights, sounds, and emotions of 15th-century France with ease. The pacing of the narrative is well-balanced, with moments of high tension and drama expertly interwoven with quieter, more introspective scenes.

In "Dark Triumph," Robin LaFevers has created a novel that is both a gripping historical fantasy and a thought-provoking exploration of the human condition. Sybella's journey is a powerful reminder of the enduring power of female relationships and the importance of staying true to oneself, even in the face of adversity. This is a must-read for fans of historical fantasy and anyone looking for a compelling, immersive narrative with complex characters and a richly detailed world.
