# Rakkety Tam (Redwall, #17)

**Author:** Brian Jacques  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2004

Title: Fury from the North: Brian Jacques Delivers a Masterclass in Epic Fantasy with Rakkety Tam

In the vast and enduring landscape of modern fantasy, few worlds are as instantly recognizable or as sensory-rich as Brian Jacques’s Mossflower universe. With *Rakkety Tam*, the seventeenth installment in the beloved *Redwall* series originally published in 2004, Jacques proves that his creative well is far from dry. Blending the high-stakes heroics of Homeric epic with the cozy, rustic charm of Kenneth Grahame, this novel delivers a thunderous tale of loyalty, destiny, and unyielding courage that stands shoulder-to-shoulder with the best entries in the franchise.

At the heart of *Rakkety Tam* lies a timeless thematic exploration: the collision between unprovoked malevolence and righteous valor. The narrative wastes no time plunging the reader into the action, introducing us to Rakkety Tam MacBurl, a fiercely independent border warrior wandering south in search of honest adventure. But adventure, as it so often does in the annals of Redwall, arrives cloaked in terror. Enter Gulo the Savage—a colossal, flesh-eating wolverine whose very name strikes dread into the hearts of beasts. Driven by a brutal fratricidal feud over a mystical stone meant to crown a king of the ice and snow, Gulo carves a path of absolute destruction toward the peaceful sanctuary of Redwall Abbey. 

Jacques masterfully juxtaposes the serene, domestic warmth of the Abbey—brimming with feasts, riddles, and loyal friends—against the cold, relentless nightmare of Gulo’s advance. This contrast is the cornerstone of the author's enduring style. Jacques does not merely write for children; he orchestrates grand operas of good versus evil where morality is stark, yet the emotional stakes feel profoundly real. The pacing is relentless, keeping readers perpetually on their toes as the drumbeat of war draws closer to the abbey walls. 

What elevates *Rakkety Tam* above a standard formulaic adventure, however, is its distinct and vibrant personality. The novel introduces a delightful Scottish flavor, most notably through Tam himself and his loyal companions, infusing the dialogue and atmosphere with a roguish charm and poetic cadence. The characters are painted in bold, unforgettable strokes: heroes you will cheer for with a lump in your throat, and villains so delightfully, shudder-inducingly wicked that you will relish every page they dominate. From ferocious woodland fighters to endearing, eccentric woodlanders, Jacques populates his world with a rich tapestry of life that makes the ultimate defense of Redwall feel like a battle for the soul of nature itself.

For both lifelong fans of the series and newcomers looking for a gateway into high fantasy, *Rakkety Tam* is an absolute triumph. It is a romping, heartfelt, and pulse-pounding saga that reminds us why Brian Jacques remains a titan of the genre. When the dust settles and the final battle echoes fade into memory, you will be left with a profound appreciation for friendship, valor, and the enduring strength of those who stand tall against the darkness.
