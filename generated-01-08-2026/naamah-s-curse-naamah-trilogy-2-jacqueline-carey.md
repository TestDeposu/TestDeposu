# Naamah's Curse (Naamah Trilogy, #2)

**Author:** Jacqueline Carey  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

Jacqueline Carey has long held a revered place in the pantheon of modern epic fantasy, celebrated for her intoxicating prose, intricate world-building, and unapologetic exploration of sacred sexuality. In Naamah’s Curse, the second installment of the Naamah Trilogy, Carey returns to the expansive, richly textured universe first established in her acclaimed Kushiel’s Legacy series. Transporting readers far from the familiar political intrigue of Terre d’Ange, this novel dives headfirst into the untamed landscapes of Tatar territory and the enigmatic realms of the East. At its core, the book is a mesmerizing continuation of Moirin’s journey—a tale where geography, destiny, and the human heart collide in a breathtaking dance of devotion and danger.

The central theme of Naamah’s Curse is the relentless, transformative power of connection—both spiritual and carnal. Moirin, the daughter of the sorceress Queen Joscelin and Imriel, carries within her the diadh-anam, the divine soul-spark of her mother’s nomadic people. This spark is not merely a metaphor; it is a literal tether to another soul, a missing fragment that leaves Moirin incomplete until she finds Bao, the proud and formidable Ch’in warrior. Carey uses this metaphysical bond to explore the ancient philosophical idea of the divided soul seeking reunion. The quest across the treacherous Tatar steppes becomes a grand metaphor for the lengths to which one will go to achieve spiritual and emotional wholeness. 

Yet, Carey’s brilliance lies in how she intertwines this high-stakes spiritual yearning with the visceral realities of human passion. In Moirin and Bao’s union, sex is never gratuitous; it is elevated to an act of profound communion and healing. The novel asks a compelling question: What happens when the person who completes your soul is also the catalyst for your greatest peril? The bittersweet nature of their hard-won happiness underscores the thematic reality that true love is inextricably linked to vulnerability. When betrayal strikes, it tests the very foundation of their bond, forcing Moirin to rely on her inner strength, her burgeoning magic, and the tenets of her faith—Love as thou wilt—rather than just the man who holds the other half of her soul.

Stylistically, Jacqueline Carey remains peerless in her ability to craft prose that feels like a decadent tapestry. Her writing is unapologetically lush, sensory, and immersive. Every page is saturated with rich descriptions of spices, textiles, landscapes, and emotional nuances, inviting the reader to experience the world through Moirin’s deeply empathetic eyes. Carey’s world-building is seamless; she does not merely invent cultures, she breathes life into them, complete with their own mythologies, social structures, and poetic cadences. The pacing in Naamah’s Curse mirrors the journey of its protagonists—deliberate, sweeping, and punctuated by moments of intense, heart-stopping urgency. 

For fans of epic fantasy who crave depth, emotional resonance, and a celebration of resilience wrapped in gorgeous prose, Naamah’s Curse is a triumph. Carey proves once again that she is a master storyteller, capable of blending high fantasy with deeply intimate human drama. It is a novel that lingers in the mind long after the final page, a testament to the enduring quest for love, identity, and belonging in a vast and unpredictable world.
