# The Pool of Two Moons (The Witches of Eileanan, #2)

**Author:** Kate Forsyth  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1999

**The Pool of Two Moons: A Witch’s Revenge and the Weight of Legacy**

Kate Forsyth’s *The Pool of Two Moons*—the second installment in *The Witches of Eileanan*—is a spellbinding sequel that deepens the mythic stakes of its predecessor while weaving a tapestry of vengeance, fractured kinship, and the relentless pull of destiny. Set sixteen years after the brutal suppression of magic in Eileanan, this novel is less a straightforward adventure and more a haunting meditation on the cost of power, the ghosts of the past, and the impossible bonds that tie even the most estranged souls together.

At its core, *The Pool of Two Moons* is a story about the legacy of Meghan of the Beasts, the Arch-Sorceress whose very existence has been a specter haunting the kingdom. Forsyth, a master of lush, atmospheric prose, crafts a world where magic is not just a force but a living, breathing entity—one that demands sacrifice, memory, and the blood of those who would reclaim it. The novel’s opening lines set the tone: Eileanan is on the brink of collapse, its people ruled by the tyrannical Queen Maya the Ensorcellor, whose grip on power is as fragile as it is cruel. To dismantle her reign, Meghan must assemble a fractured army—one that includes the long-lost twin sister she never knew she had, the enigmatic warrior Iseult, and the winged man whose lost powers may be the key to restoring the Lodestar, the ancient light that once guided the land.

Forsyth’s strength lies in her ability to blend mythic grandeur with intimate, visceral storytelling. The prose is rich with sensory detail—the scent of salt on the wind, the weight of a sword in a warrior’s hand, the quiet terror of a child’s voice whispering in the dark. Yet beneath the surface lies a darker current: the novel is steeped in themes of betrayal, loss, and the inescapable weight of history. Meghan’s journey is not one of triumph alone but of reckoning. She is a woman who has spent years in exile, her magic a curse as much as a gift, her heart a battleground of grief and fury. When she learns of her twin sister’s existence—another witch, another heir to the power she was denied—she is forced to confront the question: Can forgiveness coexist with vengeance? Or is the past too heavy a burden to bear?

The novel’s most compelling character is undoubtedly Meghan herself, a figure both terrifying and tragically human. Forsyth avoids the pitfall of making her a one-dimensional villain or a noble savior; instead, she presents Meghan as a woman shaped by loss. Her relationship with her twin sister, though never fully explored in this volume, looms large, a promise of deeper emotional conflict. Meanwhile, Iseult, the red-haired warrior, is a fascinating counterpoint—her loyalty is tested, her past shrouded in mystery, and her bond with Meghan is as much a partnership as it is a dance of mutual distrust. Together, they embody the novel’s central tension: the idea that true power requires not just strength, but the willingness to let go of the past.

The world-building is another standout feature. Eileanan is a land of stark contrasts—lush forests and crumbling cities, ancient magic and brutal oppression. Forsyth’s descriptions of Lucescere, the capital city under Maya’s rule, are particularly chilling, painting a picture of a society where fear has become the only law. The magic system, though not heavily explained, is woven seamlessly into the narrative, its rules as mysterious as its origins. The three parts of the enchanted key, the winged man’s lost powers, and the twin sisters’ connection all hint at a deeper lore that fans of the series will eagerly unravel.

That said, *The Pool of Two Moons* is not without its flaws. Some readers may find the pacing uneven, particularly in the early sections where the plot feels slightly stretched as Meghan and her allies prepare for their assault on Lucescere. The emotional payoff, while satisfying, sometimes comes after a slow burn, and the novel’s focus on political intrigue occasionally overshadows the personal stakes. However, these are minor quibbles in an otherwise immersive and thought-provoking tale.

Ultimately, *The Pool of Two Moons* is a novel that lingers in the mind long after the final page. It is a story about the impossible choices we make in the name of justice, the bonds that defy even the darkest of fates, and the cost of reclaiming what was once lost. Forsyth’s writing is evocative, her characters complex, and her world brimming with mythic weight. For fans of dark fantasy, political intrigue, and deeply layered storytelling, this is a book that will not only captivate but also haunt.
