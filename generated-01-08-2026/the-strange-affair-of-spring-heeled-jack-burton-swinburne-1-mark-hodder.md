# The Strange Affair of Spring Heeled Jack (Burton & Swinburne, #1)

**Author:** Mark Hodder  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

"Mark Hodder's 'The Strange Affair of Spring Heeled Jack' is a masterful blend of science fiction, historical fiction, and horror, set in the dark and atmospheric London of 1861. This is the first book in the Burton & Swinburne series, and what a thrilling starting point it is.

Our protagonist, Sir Richard Francis Burton, is a man with a tarnished reputation and a storied past. A skilled explorer, linguist, and swordsman, Burton's career has been reduced to ruin, and his former partner is presumed dead. Enter Algernon Charles Swinburne, an unsuccessful poet with a penchant for pain and a taste for the excesses of life. The two men could not be more different, but they are bound together by a shared sense of curiosity and a desire to uncover the truth.

As Burton and Swinburne delve into the mystery of the Spring Heeled Jack, they begin to unravel a web of deceit and terror that threatens to upend everything they thought they knew about the world. This is no ordinary case of murder and mayhem, but a conspiracy that threatens the very fabric of reality itself.

Hodder's writing is a true marvel, weaving together historical fact and fiction with ease. His prose is evocative and immersive, transporting the reader to the fog-shrouded streets of Victorian London. The author's use of language is both elegant and brutal, perfectly capturing the atmosphere of corruption and decay that pervades the city.

One of the most striking aspects of 'The Strange Affair of Spring Heeled Jack' is the way in which Hodder subverts our expectations of the historical figure of Burton. Rather than portraying him as a stuffy, traditionalist explorer, Hodder presents a more nuanced and complex character, haunted by his past and struggling to come to terms with the changing world around him. Burton's relationship with Swinburne is also fascinating, highlighting the tensions and contradictions that exist between two men who are both outsiders and misfits.

Throughout the book, Hodder also explores themes of identity, morality, and the nature of reality itself. The title of the book, 'The Strange Affair of Spring Heeled Jack', is a reference to a mysterious and terrifying figure from English folklore, who is said to be able to leap great heights and inflict wounds on his victims with his sharp metal claws. As Burton and Swinburne dig deeper into the mystery, they begin to realize that the truth may be far stranger - and more terrifying - than they ever could have imagined.

Overall, 'The Strange Affair of Spring Heeled Jack' is a triumph of historical fiction, a gripping and thought-provoking mystery that will keep you on the edge of your seat from start to finish. With its richly detailed world-building, complex characters, and breakneck pace, this is a book that will appeal to fans of authors like China Miéville and Neil Gaiman. If you're looking for a truly unforgettable reading experience, then look no further than 'The Strange Affair of Spring Heeled Jack'.
