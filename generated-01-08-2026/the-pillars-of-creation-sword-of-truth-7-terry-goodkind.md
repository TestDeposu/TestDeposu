# The Pillars of Creation (Sword of Truth, #7)

**Author:** Terry Goodkind  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

The Pillars of Creation, the seventh installment in Terry Goodkind's Sword of Truth series, is a sprawling epic that delves deep into the darkest corners of the human soul. This book is a testament to Goodkind's mastery of the fantasy genre, weaving a complex narrative that is both breathtaking in its scope and unflinching in its exploration of the human condition.

At its core, The Pillars of Creation is a story about the nature of evil and the resilience of the human spirit. The protagonist, Lauren, is a young woman haunted by inhuman voices that threaten to consume her very existence. Her journey is a powerful exploration of the fragility of the human psyche and the ways in which we are all vulnerable to the whispers of darkness that lurk within.

Meanwhile, Richard Rahl and his wife Kahlan must navigate a treacherous landscape of conquest and revenge, as they face off against an enemy that seems almost invulnerable to defeat. Their quest is a classic tale of good vs. evil, but Goodkind's nuanced characterization and moral complexity elevate the story to a level that is both thought-provoking and deeply unsettling.

One of the hallmarks of Goodkind's writing style is his ability to craft compelling characters that are both flawed and relatable. Richard and Kahlan are no exception, and their relationship is a highlight of the book. Their love for each other is a beacon of hope in a world that seems determined to destroy them, and their interactions are fraught with tension and emotional depth.

Goodkind's world-building is also noteworthy, as he creates a richly detailed landscape that is both familiar and yet utterly unique. The Pillars of Creation are a mysterious and ancient presence that hold the key to unlocking the secrets of the universe, and Goodkind's description of them is both vivid and awe-inspiring.

If there is one criticism to be made of The Pillars of Creation, it is that the book can be overwhelming in its scope. The narrative is complex and multifaceted, and the sheer number of characters and plot threads can be daunting at times. However, this is a minor quibble in what is otherwise a masterful work of fantasy.

In conclusion, The Pillars of Creation is a breathtaking adventure that is both a thrilling page-turner and a deeply thought-provoking exploration of the human condition. Goodkind's writing is masterful, and his characters are both compelling and relatable. If you are a fan of epic fantasy, then this book is a must-read.
