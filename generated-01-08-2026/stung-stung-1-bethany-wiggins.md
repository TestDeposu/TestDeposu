# Stung (Stung, #1)

**Author:** Bethany Wiggins  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

In the gripping dystopian novel "Stung" by Bethany Wiggins, the world as we know it has come to a screeching halt. A pandemic, born from the mysterious disappearance of the honeybee population, has ravaged the planet, and the government's attempted cure has proven to be a recipe for disaster. The story follows Fiona, a young woman who awakens to find her world turned upside down, with no recollection of the events that led to the downfall of society.

The author's vivid depiction of a desolate, barren landscape sets the tone for a thrilling narrative that explores the darker aspects of human nature. Wiggins' writing style is concise and engaging, making it easy to become fully immersed in Fiona's desperate struggle to survive. The protagonist's character development is well-paced, and her determination to uncover the truth about her past and the world around her is both admirable and relatable.

One of the most striking aspects of "Stung" is its exploration of the consequences of playing with nature. The government's attempt to bio-engineer a cure for the pandemic has resulted in the creation of ferocious, deadly beasts that roam the earth, preying on the unvaccinated. This cautionary tale serves as a reminder of the importance of respecting the natural world and the potential dangers of scientific hubris.

Wiggins' world-building is impressive, with a clear vision of a society torn apart by fear, mistrust, and desperation. The concept of a fortress-like wall, separating the protected from the unprotected, serves as a powerful metaphor for the division and inequality that can arise in times of crisis. The author's use of the black oval tattoo, branded on Fiona's wrist, is a clever plot device that adds an air of mystery and tension to the narrative.

Throughout the book, Wiggins raises important questions about identity, community, and the human condition. Fiona's journey is not just about survival; it's about self-discovery and the search for meaning in a world that seems to have lost its way. The author's portrayal of the protagonist's emotional struggles is authentic and poignant, making it easy to become invested in her story.

In conclusion, "Stung" is a captivating and thought-provoking novel that will appeal to fans of dystopian fiction. Bethany Wiggins' engaging writing style, combined with her well-developed characters and plot, makes for a compelling read. The book's exploration of the consequences of scientific hubris and the importance of respecting nature serves as a timely reminder of the importance of responsible innovation.
