# Enemy of God (The Warlord Chronicles, #2)

**Author:** Bernard Cornwell  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1998

In "Enemy of God," the second installment of Bernard Cornwell's Warlord Chronicles series, the masterful storyteller weaves a captivating narrative that delves into the complexities of power, loyalty, and the blurred lines between friends and foes. Set after the tumultuous times of "The Winter King," this latest chapter in the legendary tale of King Arthur plunges readers into the midst of a Britain torn between the fading remnants of its ancient past and the emerging forces of a new era.

As Arthur, now firmly entrenched as the ruler of Britain, finds himself at the helm of a newly forged Mordred's throne, he must confront the far-reaching consequences of his conquests. The echoes of civil war still linger, casting a dark shadow over the landscape, and it is here that Cornwell's narrative truly comes alive. With his hallmark precision and mastery of language, Cornwell brings to life the familiar faces of legend, including Arthur, Guinevere, and Merlin, as they navigate the labyrinthine corridors of power, politics, and personal relationships.

One of the most striking aspects of "Enemy of God" is Cornwell's nuanced portrayal of Arthur's character. No longer the invincible warrior of legend, Arthur is a more mature and world-weary king, grappling with the weight of his responsibilities and the consequences of his actions. His relationships with those closest to him, particularly Guinevere and Merlin, are fraught with tension, as the boundaries between loyalty, duty, and personal desire begin to blur.

At the heart of "Enemy of God" lies a profound exploration of the human condition, where the distinction between good and evil becomes increasingly ambiguous. As the stakes grow higher and the conflicts more intense, Cornwell's characters are forced to confront the darker aspects of their own natures, revealing the depth of their motivations and the moral complexity of their decisions.

Through Cornwell's masterful storytelling, the world of "Enemy of God" is brought vividly to life, with the rich tapestry of British history and mythology woven into a narrative that is both epic in scope and deeply personal. The author's skillful use of language, coupled with his meticulous attention to historical detail, creates a sense of immersion that is nothing short of breathtaking.

One of the hallmarks of Cornwell's writing is his ability to balance action and drama with introspection and character development, resulting in a narrative that is both suspenseful and emotionally resonant. As the threads of the story begin to weave together, Cornwell masterfully ratchets up the tension, hurtling his characters towards a showdown that will determine the fate of Britain and the destiny of King Arthur.

In short, "Enemy of God" is a triumph, a captivating and immersive epic that cements Cornwell's reputation as one of the most skilled storytellers of our time. With its nuanced portrayal of King Arthur, its exploration of the human condition, and its richly detailed world-building, this latest installment in The Warlord Chronicles series is an absolute must-read for fans of historical fiction, fantasy, and adventure.
