# Manhunt: The 12-Day Chase for Lincoln's Killer

**Author:** James L. Swanson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

"Manhunt: The 12-Day Chase for Lincoln's Killer" is a breathtakingly thrilling account of one of the most pivotal moments in American history, as James L. Swanson masterfully weaves a gripping narrative of the relentless pursuit and capture of John Wilkes Booth, the notorious assassin of President Abraham Lincoln. Published in 2006, (Not 2012), this masterpiece has earned its place among the top-notch non-fiction works of our time.

Swanson's engaging writing style immerses readers in the midst of the chaos that unfolded in Washington, D.C. on April 14, 1865, as John Wilkes Booth and his co-conspirators unleashed their heinous plan. The subsequent manhunt is portrayed with electrifying intensity, as the author skillfully interweaves the threads of the hunt, expertly maneuvering among the labyrinthine streets of the capital city, the swamps of Maryland, and the dense forests of Virginia. With meticulous attention to historical detail and insight, Swanson vividly recreates the high-stakes drama of the pursuit, expertly conjuring an atmosphere of raw urgency and desperation that will keep readers on the edge of their seats until the very end.

At the core of this gripping narrative lies the intricate web of relationships and motives that drove the assassin and his pursuers. Swanson deftly humanizes the key figures in this tragic drama, revealing the depth and complexity of their individual characters. John Wilkes Booth, the troubled actor with a genius for manipulation, emerges as a multifaceted and deeply troubled figure, driven by a maelstrom of conflicting passions and ideologies. Conversely, his pursuers - including the fearless Sergeant Boston Corbett, Lieutenant Edward Doherty, and the courageous members of the 16th New York Cavalry Regiment - take center stage as heroes of unyielding determination, driven by their unwavering commitment to justice.

What truly sets "Manhunt" apart, however, is its groundbreaking research and meticulously researched historical insights. Drawing upon previously untapped archival materials, obscure trial records, and eyewitness accounts from relatives of the conspirators and manhunters, Swanson has constructed a meticulously detailed and authentic portrait of the era, painstakingly recreating the sights, sounds, and emotions that defined the era.

Through this extraordinary work of historical fiction, Swanson sheds light on an often-overlooked chapter in American history, offering a profoundly evocative exploration of the turbulent forces that defined mid-19th-century America. His masterful storytelling not merely recounts but reanimates the past, imbuing readers with a visceral connection to the pivotal events that forever reshaped the fate of the nation.

Ultimately, "Manhunt: The 12-Day Chase for Lincoln's Killer" stands as an indispensable testament to the enduring power of historical narrative to captivate and enlighten. Swanson's exceptional skill as a storyteller, combined with his meticulous commitment to historical accuracy, has resulted in a work that will continue to captivate readers for generations to come.
