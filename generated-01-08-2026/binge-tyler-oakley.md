# Binge

**Author:** Tyler Oakley  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

Title: Confessions of a Professional Over-Sharer: A Review of Tyler Oakley’s Binge

In the digital age, the currency of the internet is vulnerability, and few have minted that currency quite as successfully as Tyler Oakley. As one of YouTube’s earliest breakout stars and a pioneering LGBTQ+ voice, Oakley built an empire on the premise of unfiltered authenticity. Transitioning from the luminous glow of a webcam to the printed page in his 2015 bestselling collection Binge, Oakley proves that his brand of chaotic charm translates surprisingly well to literature. The book is not merely a collection of essays; it is an extended, hilarious, and occasionally poignant confessional booth for a generation raised on the internet.

At the heart of Binge lies a central, unifying theme: the radical embrace of imperfection. For a public figure who spent years curating a colorful, hyper-energetic online persona, this book serves as a welcome antidote to the polished highlight reels of modern social media. Oakley pulls back the curtain to reveal the delightfully messy reality of growing up queer, ambitious, and deeply flawed. Whether he is dissecting the sting of adolescent alienation or navigating the surreal landscapes of sudden digital fame, Oakley explores what it means to live life out loud. The theme resonates deeply because it strips away the untouchable aura of celebrity, replacing it with the universal comfort of shared embarrassment. 

Stylistically, Binge reads exactly how Tyler Oakley sounds: fast-paced, conversational, campy, and bursting with manic energy. If you have ever spent an afternoon binge-watching his YouTube channel, you will hear his distinct cadence echoing off every page. Oakley’s writing style is rooted in the tradition of self-deprecating humor. He has a rare comedic gift for hyperbole, turning mundane disasters into cinematic catastrophes. 

Consider the sheer breadth of his misadventures chronicled here: a legendary rage blackout inside a Cheesecake Factory, a high-stakes geopolitical standoff with a White House official, a humiliating car crash executed in full view of his high school while wearing an Arby’s uniform, and a projectile vomiting incident during a high-stakes negotiation with a bewildered grandmother. These are not tales of a polished icon; they are the epic fails of a mortal human being who refuses to take himself too seriously.

What elevates Binge above a mere compilation of internet anecdotes, however, is the emotional undercurrent that anchors the comedy. Beneath the glitter, the pop-culture references, and the side-splitting physical comedy, Oakley weaves genuine introspection. He balances the uproarious humor with moments of quiet sincerity, addressing mental health, the pressures of representation, and the courage it takes to stand unapologetically in one's truth. He doesn't just ask readers to laugh at his misfortunes; he invites them to reflect on their own awkward journeys toward self-acceptance.

For longtime fans, Binge is the ultimate collector's item—a deeper, more permanent dive into the mind of an internet pioneer. For the uninitiated, it serves as a wildly entertaining introduction to a comedic voice that helped shape modern digital culture. It is messy, loud, deeply personal, and unapologetically fun. In a literary landscape often dominated by rigid memoirs and po-faced self-help guides, Tyler Oakley’s Binge is a refreshing reminder that the best stories usually come from our biggest mistakes.
