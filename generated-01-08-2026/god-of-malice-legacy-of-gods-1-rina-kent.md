# God of Malice (Legacy of Gods, #1)

**Author:** Rina Kent  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

Rina Kent’s God of Malice, the explosive first installment in the Legacy of Gods series, is not a romance for the faint of heart. It is a masterclass in psychological tension, toxic obsession, and the intoxicating allure of the forbidden. Set within a gilded cage of wealth, privilege, and dark academia aesthetics, the novel dives headfirst into the abyss of human morality, exploring what happens when two deeply fractured souls collide in the dead of night.

At the center of this storm are Glyndon King and Killian Carson. Glyndon, burdened by the immense expectations of her elite British family and consumed by a silent, agonizing grief, channels her pain into her art. She is a canvas of hidden sorrows until a fateful, wind-swept night on a cliff edge brings her face-to-face with Killian. To the world, Killian is a brilliant medical student with an angelic face. But beneath the pristine exterior lies the soul of a predator. Where others see Glyndon’s perfection, Killian recognizes something far more intimate: a profound, matching darkness. 

What follows is not a gentle courtship, but a meticulously orchestrated descent into obsession. Kent’s central theme revolves around the idea that damage recognizes damage. Killian doesn't just enter Glyndon’s life; he dissects it with the cold precision of a surgeon, establishing a dangerous dynamic of power, control, and psychological surrender. The boundary between tormentor and savior blurs into non-existence, forcing the characters—and the reader—to question whether true intimacy can only be found in the total erasure of boundaries. Glyndon’s inability to resist Killian’s magnetic pull speaks to a universal, albeit terrifying, human desire: the yearning to be fully seen, flaws and all, by someone who is equally unafraid of the dark.

Rina Kent’s writing style is unapologetically intense, fast-paced, and darkly hypnotic. She crafts prose that mirrors the suffocating yet thrilling atmosphere of her plot. Every interaction between Killian and Glyndon is charged with palpable electricity, laden with subtext, psychological warfare, and an unrelenting push-and-pull. Kent does not shy away from the gritty, uncomfortable realities of her characters' psyches. Instead, she leans into the taboos, utilizing a stark, unflinching narrative voice that strips away romantic idealization in favor of raw, visceral emotional truth. 

It is crucial to note that God of Malice comes with heavy trigger warnings. Dealing frankly with themes of suicide ideation and morally grey dynamics involving dubious or non-consensual elements, the novel demands a mature readership prepared to navigate the darkest corridors of fiction. Kent makes no apologies for her characters' lack of moral fiber; she presents them as they are—broken monsters finding twisted solace in each other's company.

Ultimately, God of Malice is a thrilling, provocative page-turner that sets a formidable standard for dark romance. It is a story that proves love, in its most distorted and feral form, can sometimes look remarkably like a nightmare. For readers who revel in high-stakes power dynamics, morally grey antiheroes, and stories where the villains get the girl, Rina Kent delivers an unforgettable, adrenaline-fueled triumph.
