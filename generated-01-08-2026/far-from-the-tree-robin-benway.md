# Far from the Tree

**Author:** Robin Benway  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

"Far from the Tree" by Robin Benway is a powerful, poignant, and deeply moving novel that explores the meaning of family in all its forms. With a narrative as fluid as it is unflinching, Benway masterfully weaves together the lives of three estranged siblings - Grace, Maya, and Joaquin - who are bound together by a shared biological mother.

At the heart of this book lies a universal question: what constitutes family? For each of the siblings, the answer is vastly different. For Grace, an only child who was adopted at birth, family has always been a mystery waiting to be unlocked. Her journey to find her biological parents is a search for identity and belonging that becomes an all-consuming obsession. 

But for Maya, her younger bio sister, family is a complicated web of emotions and expectations. Growing up in a household full of adopted redheads, she's always struggled to find a place for herself. Her snarky wit and quick tongue serve as a shield, protecting her from the insecurities that threaten to consume her at every turn.

Then there's Joaquin, the stoic older bio brother, who has given up on the idea of family altogether. Having spent 17 years in the foster care system, he's learned to rely only on himself and to build walls around his heart to prevent any more pain. His story is a heart-wrenching portrayal of the harsh realities of foster care, and the resilience that it takes to survive.

Throughout "Far from the Tree", Benway's masterful characterization shines. Each of the siblings has a unique voice, which is both relatable and authentic. This is no small feat, given the complexities of family dynamics and the various struggles they face. Yet, Benway makes it look effortless, crafting a narrative that feels both true to life and deeply personal.

At its core, this novel is a stunning exploration of the many forms that family can take. It speaks to those who have been adopted, fostered, or estranged from their biological families. But it also speaks to anyone who has ever felt a sense of disconnection from those around them.

The story is a beautifully interwoven tapestry of love, forgiveness, and understanding. It's a testament to the resilience of the human spirit, and a shining example of how even the most broken and fractured families can learn to heal and love one another.

In short, "Far from the Tree" is a must-read. It's a book that will break your heart, yet fill it with hope. It's a reminder that family is not just a biological connection, but a choice that we make every day.

With "Far from the Tree", Benway solidifies her position as one of the most talented and compassionate authors working today. This novel will stay with you long after you finish reading it, leaving you to ponder the meaning of family in a world that often seems determined to blur its boundaries.

As such, I highly recommend it to anyone who is ready to be moved, to be challenged, and to be inspired by a story that will stay with you for a lifetime.
