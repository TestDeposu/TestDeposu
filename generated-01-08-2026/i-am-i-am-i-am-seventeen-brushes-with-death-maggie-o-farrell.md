# I Am, I Am, I Am: Seventeen Brushes with Death

**Author:** Maggie O'Farrell  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

**I Am, I Am, I Am: Seventeen Brushes with Death by Maggie O’Farrell – A Memoir That Rewrites the Rules of Living**

Maggie O’Farrell’s *I Am, I Am, I Am* is not merely a memoir—it is a masterclass in the art of survival, a meditation on fragility, and a testament to the way life, in all its unpredictability, demands to be lived with both terror and wonder. Structured around seventeen near-death encounters—some fleeting, some lingering—O’Farrell weaves these moments into the fabric of her existence, revealing how each brush with mortality reshapes her understanding of herself, her family, and the world. What emerges is not just a story of survival, but a profound exploration of what it means to *be*, to endure, and to find meaning in the thin, trembling line between life and loss.

At its core, the book is a reflection on vulnerability. O’Farrell’s prose is razor-sharp, each sentence precise and charged with an undercurrent of tension. She does not flinch from the raw, visceral details of her near-death experiences—a childhood illness that left her bedridden for a year, a teenage near-fatal accident, the daily anxiety of raising a daughter with a rare, life-threatening condition—but she also refuses to wallow in despair. Instead, she transforms these moments into something transcendent. There is a quiet defiance in her writing, a refusal to let fear dictate her narrative. She writes, *"I am. I am still here."* And in that repetition, she claims her existence, again and again.

The structure of the book is as bold as its themes. Rather than a linear autobiography, O’Farrell presents these encounters as discrete, almost lyrical interludes, each a snapshot of a life suspended between breath and nothingness. The effect is haunting. We are left with the sense that death is never far away—not just as an abstract concept, but as a living, breathing presence, lurking in the shadows of every choice, every relationship, every moment of joy. Yet, rather than a book of doom, it is one of fierce, almost religious devotion to life. O’Farrell’s writing is imbued with a sense of awe, as if she is constantly marveling at the miracle of being alive. She writes of her daughter’s fragility with a tenderness that is both heartbreaking and uplifting, framing her struggles not as a burden, but as a testament to the resilience of love.

Her style is unmistakably hers—elegant, unpretentious, and deeply human. O’Farrell has a gift for distilling complex emotions into simple, devastating sentences. Consider this passage from the book: *"Death is not a single event. It is a series of small deaths, each one a surrender."* It is a line that lingers, a truth that resonates long after the words are read. She does not offer easy answers or neat resolutions; instead, she invites the reader to sit with the ambiguity, to acknowledge the fragility of existence without succumbing to despair.

What makes *I Am, I Am, I Am* so compelling is its refusal to romanticize suffering. O’Farrell does not glorify her near-death experiences; she treats them with the gravity they deserve, but she also refuses to let them define her. Life, she suggests, is not about avoiding danger, but about embracing it—about living fully, even when the ground beneath you feels precarious. The book is, in many ways, a love letter to life itself, a celebration of its unpredictability, its beauty, and its terrifying, intoxicating allure.

The most powerful sections of the book are those that focus on her daughter, a child with a rare condition that leaves her vulnerable to life’s myriad dangers. O’Farrell’s love for her daughter is palpable, but so too is her unflinching honesty about the fear and responsibility that come with it. She writes of the daily rituals of protection, the constant vigilance, the moments of terror when she is forced to confront the fragility of her child’s existence. Yet, even in these darkest passages, there is a sense of hope. She does not shy away from the pain, but she also refuses to let it consume her. Instead, she finds beauty in the small, ordinary moments—the way sunlight filters through a window, the sound of her daughter’s laughter—that remind her, and us, that life is worth fighting for.

O’Farrell’s writing is a reminder that memoir does not have to be a linear journey. It can be a series of fragments, a collage of moments that together paint a portrait of a life lived at the edge of existence. She does not seek to impress with grand prose or dramatic revelations; instead, she offers something far more valuable—a raw, unfiltered glimpse into the human condition. *I Am, I Am, I Am* is a book that lingers in the mind long after the last page is turned, a testament to the power of storytelling to illuminate the darkest corners of the human experience.

In the end, this is a book about the art of living. It is about the way we hold onto life, even when it is slipping through our fingers. It is about the moments that define us, the fears that shape us, and the love that sustains us. Maggie O’Farrell’s prose is a balm for the soul, a reminder that even in the face of death, we are still here—and that is something to be celebrated.
