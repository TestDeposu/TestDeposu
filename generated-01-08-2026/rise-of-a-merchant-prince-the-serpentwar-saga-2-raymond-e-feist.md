# Rise of a Merchant Prince (The Serpentwar Saga, #2)

**Author:** Raymond E. Feist  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Stepping away from the traditional, blood-soaked battlefields of epic fantasy, Raymond E. Feist achieves something remarkably daring in Rise of a Merchant Prince, the second installment of The Serpentwar Saga. While the first book, Shadow of a Dark Queen, set the stage with military heroism and looming apocalyptic threats from the invading armies of the Emerald Queen, this sequel pivots brilliantly. Feist shifts the lens from swinging swords and grand sorcery to the gritty, high-stakes, and surprisingly cutthroat world of commerce, economics, and personal ambition. 

The primary theme of the novel is transformation and the pursuit of power through unorthodox means. Rather than focusing solely on frontline warriors, Feist anchors the narrative on the post-war struggles of his young protagonists, most notably the charismatic and ambitious James. Having survived the initial confrontation with the fearsome reptilian Sauur, these characters must now navigate a world that is rebuilding. Yet, the threats they face are no less perilous for being boardroom-bound rather than battlefield-oriented. 

Feist explores how wealth is a weapon as potent as any enchanted blade. The author meticulously details the mechanics of trade monopolies, market manipulation, smuggling, and corporate espionage. In doing so, he elevates the fantasy genre, proving that the rise of a merchant prince can be just as thrilling—and infinitely more treacherous—than the rise of a king. It is a story about ambition, the corrupting nature of gold, and the heavy price of success in a recovering economy. The narrative acts as a fascinating study of human ingenuity and greed, showing that rebuilding a shattered world requires as much cunning as defending it.

True to form, Feist’s authorial style remains a masterclass in pacing and accessibility. He possesses a rare, effortless talent for storytelling that keeps the reader turning pages long into the night. His prose is fluid, unpretentious, and driven by relentless momentum. Even when dealing with complex economic concepts or intricate trade routes, Feist never bogs the reader down in dry exposition. Instead, he infuses every negotiation, betrayal, and business venture with palpable tension. 

Furthermore, Feist’s greatest strength has always been his character work, and here it shines with grounded realism. The characters are not static heroes; they are flawed, evolving individuals who must adapt to a society changing faster than they can. Their triumphs feel earned because Feist subjects them to deeply personal, financial, and social trials. For fans addicted to the immersive, sprawling worlds crafted by George R.R. Martin, Terry Brooks, or Terry Goodkind, Feist offers a similar depth of world-building while maintaining his own distinct, highly readable voice.

Rise of a Merchant Prince proves that Raymond E. Feist is a writer who refuses to rest on his laurels or rely solely on formulaic tropes. By daring to write an epic fantasy where the ledger is mightier than the sword, he delivers a refreshing, compelling, and utterly addictive adventure. It is an essential read for anyone who loves Midkemia and appreciates a story where wits, grit, and gold pave the road to destiny.
