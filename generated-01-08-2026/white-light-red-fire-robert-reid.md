# White Light Red Fire

**Author:** Robert Reid  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

In "White Light, Red Fire" by Robert Reid, the author weaves a gripping tale of conquest, ambition, and the struggle for power in a world where ancient magic and technology coexist. Set on the island of Andore, the story revolves around Oien, an old alchemist driven by a singular desire for world domination. His quest for power is fueled by the red stone othium, a source of immense energy that has shaped the course of history in the world of Andore.

Reid's narrative is a thought-provoking exploration of the dangers of unchecked power and the devastating consequences of imperialism. Through Oien's character, the author masterfully portrays the ruthless and cunning nature of a strong aggressor, who will stop at nothing to achieve his goals. The alliance between Oien and the tyrant King Dewar serves as a catalyst for the conflict, highlighting the dangers of unholy alliances and the exploitation of resources for destructive purposes.

One of the most striking aspects of Reid's writing style is his ability to create a richly detailed world, with a complex history that underpins the narrative. The author's use of descriptive language effectively transports the reader to the world of Andore, immersing them in the sights, sounds, and emotions of the characters. The pacing of the story is well-balanced, with moments of high tension and suspense that keep the reader engaged and invested in the outcome.

The theme of resistance is a powerful thread that runs throughout the narrative, as the brave men and women of Bala dare to challenge the might of King Dewar's forces. Reid's portrayal of these characters is nuanced and multi-dimensional, highlighting the complexities of human nature and the difficult choices that must be made in the face of overwhelming odds.

The author's use of symbolism is also noteworthy, particularly in the contrast between the "white light" and the "red fire". The white light represents hope, goodness, and the power of resistance, while the red fire symbolizes destruction, chaos, and the destructive power of the othium. This dichotomy serves as a powerful metaphor for the struggle between good and evil, and the need for balance and harmony in a world torn apart by conflict.

Overall, "White Light, Red Fire" is a compelling and thought-provoking read that will appeal to fans of epic fantasy and science fiction. Reid's writing style is engaging and immersive, making it easy to become fully invested in the world and characters he has created. The themes of power, resistance, and the struggle between good and evil are timeless and universal, making this book a must-read for anyone looking for a gripping and meaningful story.
