# Blue is for Nightmares (Blue is for Nightmares, #1)

**Author:** Laurie Faria Stolarz  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

**Blue is for Nightmares: A Haunting Blend of Horror and Self-Discovery**

Laurie Faria Stolarz’s *Blue is for Nightmares* isn’t just a book—it’s a descent into the kind of psychological dread that lingers long after the last page. Set in the eerie, candlelit confines of a boarding school, this debut novel from the *Blue is for Nightmares* series isn’t your typical teen thriller. Instead, it’s a chilling exploration of paranoia, friendship, and the terrifying power of secrets, wrapped in a gothic aesthetic that feels like a cross between *The Secret Circle* and *Goosebumps*—but with a far darker edge.

At its core, *Blue is for Nightmares* follows Stacey Brown, a sharp-witted junior with a knack for divination and a habit of seeing the supernatural in everyday life. But when her best friend, Drea, becomes the target of a sinister stalker, Stacey’s nightmares—vivid, prophetic, and all too real—become her only weapon. Armed with candles, tarot cards, and a growing suspicion that the universe is whispering warnings she can’t ignore, Stacey must navigate a web of lies, hidden motives, and something far more sinister lurking in the shadows. The question isn’t just *who* is hunting Drea—it’s *why*, and whether Stacey’s own powers are making her the next victim.

Stolarz’s writing is a masterclass in atmospheric tension. The prose is lush yet precise, dipping into a gothic lexicon that feels both nostalgic and fresh. Phrases like *"the air hummed with the weight of secrets"* or *"the candlelight flickered like a dying heartbeat"* aren’t just descriptive—they’re immersive, pulling the reader into a world where every creak in the hallway and every whispered name could be a harbinger of doom. The boarding school setting is a character in itself, its labyrinthine halls and dimly lit common rooms serving as the perfect backdrop for Stacey’s growing unease. Stolarz doesn’t rely on jump scares or over-the-top monsters; instead, the horror comes from the creeping realization that the real danger might be closer than anyone realizes—perhaps even within Stacey herself.

What makes *Blue is for Nightmares* truly compelling is its exploration of female friendship and the lengths to which people will go to protect those they love. Stacey’s bond with Drea is tender and complex, but it’s also tested by the very real fear that their trust could be their undoing. The novel doesn’t shy away from the darker aspects of loyalty, showing how secrets can both bind and betray. Meanwhile, the inclusion of the *Deluxe Spellbook Edition*—filled with fan-contributed spells, poems, and meditations—adds a layer of interactive horror, inviting readers to engage with the book’s themes on a deeper level. It’s a clever touch that enhances the novel’s immersive quality, making the world of *Blue is for Nightmares* feel alive and breathing.

The pacing is another strength. Stolarz balances slow-burn mystery with moments of heart-pounding urgency, ensuring that the reader is never left guessing for too long—but never so rushed that the dread fades. The characters, particularly Stacey, are deeply flawed and compelling. She’s not a heroine in the traditional sense; she’s impulsive, sometimes reckless, and haunted by her own visions. This makes her a relatable protagonist, one who stumbles through the horror rather than masterfully navigating it. Her journey is as much about self-discovery as it is about survival, and that duality gives the story its emotional weight.

That said, *Blue is for Nightmares* isn’t without its flaws. Some readers might find the supernatural elements a bit heavy-handed at times, particularly the way Stacey’s visions sometimes feel like plot conveniences rather than organic extensions of her character. Additionally, the pacing in the middle sections can drag slightly, as the novel takes its time building to the inevitable confrontation. But these are minor quibbles in an otherwise gripping narrative. Stolarz’s strength lies in her ability to make the ordinary feel sinister, and in a genre where horror often relies on shock value, that’s a rare and valuable skill.

Ultimately, *Blue is for Nightmares* is a book for anyone who loves a good scare—but not just any scare. It’s for those who enjoy stories where the real terror comes from the ambiguity, the psychological depth, and the quiet, creeping dread of knowing that something is wrong, and you might not be able to stop it. It’s a novel that lingers in the mind long after the last page, leaving readers to wonder: *What did I really see in the dark?*
