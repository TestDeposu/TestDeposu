# The Hollow Kingdom (The Hollow Kingdom Trilogy, #1)

**Author:** Clare B. Dunkle  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2006

The Hollow Kingdom, the first installment of Clare B. Dunkle's captivating trilogy, is a mesmerizing tale that whisks readers away to a mystical realm of ancient magic and forgotten lore. This 2004 Bank Street - Best Children's Book of the Year winner has been hailed by Newbery Award winner Lloyd Alexander as "as persuasive as it is remarkable," and it's easy to see why.

At its core, The Hollow Kingdom is a story about the transformative power of female relationships and the resilience of the human spirit. The narrative follows Kate and Emily, two sisters who unwittingly stumble into a centuries-old mystery when they move to Hallow Hill, a place where young women have been vanishing for thousands of years. As they become entangled in the secrets of the land, they must confront the dark forces that have haunted the hill for so long.

Dunkle's masterful storytelling weaves a complex web of mythology, folklore, and fairy tale elements, creating a richly detailed world that is both eerie and enchanting. Her writing style is characterized by a dreamlike quality, with vivid descriptions of the natural world that transport readers to the mist-shrouded hills and forests of Hallow Hill.

One of the standout features of The Hollow Kingdom is its cast of complex, multidimensional characters. Kate and Emily are more than just passive protagonists; they are active agents of change, each with their own distinct voice and agency. Marak, the enigmatic goblin king, is a fascinating figure, full of contradictions and surprises. His motivations are expertly layered, making him a compelling and nuanced villain.

Dunkle's exploration of the relationships between women is a highlight of the book. The bond between Kate and Emily is particularly well-drawn, capturing the intensity and complexity of sisterly love. The ways in which the female characters support and empower each other in the face of adversity are a testament to the power of female solidarity.

The Hollow Kingdom is also a thought-provoking exploration of the tensions between tradition and modernity. Dunkle's depiction of the clash between old and new ways of thinking is both timely and timeless, highlighting the dangers of blindly adhering to ancient customs and the importance of embracing change.

In conclusion, The Hollow Kingdom is a captivating and thought-provoking read that will appeal to fans of fantasy, mythology, and fairy tales. Dunkle's masterful storytelling, combined with her vivid world-building and complex characters, make this book a must-read for anyone looking to escape into a magical realm.
