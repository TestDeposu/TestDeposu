# Because of Low (Sea Breeze, #2)

**Author:** Abbi Glines  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

Because of Low drops readers straight into the sultry, salt‑kissed atmosphere of Sea Breeze, a Gulf‑side town where the heat outside mirrors the simmering tension inside Cage’s ever‑changing apartment. Abbi Glines wastes no time establishing her signature blend of quick‑witted dialogue, vivid physical description, and a pulse‑pounding romance that leans heavily on the “opposites attract” trope. Cage, the charismatic playboy who treats his home like a revolving door for fleeting conquests, is instantly recognizable as the archetype Glines has honed in her previous self‑published hits. Yet it is Marcus—soft‑spoken, nursing a fresh heartbreak, and earnestly seeking something deeper—who provides the emotional counterbalance that drives the story forward.

The novel’s central theme revolves around the clash between lust and genuine connection, framed through the unlikely bond that forms between Marcus and Willow, nicknamed “Low.” Low is portrayed as both fiercely independent and unexpectedly vulnerable, a woman who tolerates Cage’s womanizing not out of naïveté but because she has learned to protect her own heart in a world that offers little stability. Glines uses this dynamic to explore how past wounds shape our expectations of love, and how a steady, sensible partner can offer a safe harbor without sacrificing passion. The tension between Cage’s charismatic, surface‑level allure and Marcus’s grounded sincerity creates a push‑pull that keeps the pages turning, even as the plot occasionally leans on familiar romance beats.

Glines’s writing style is accessible and energetic; she favors short, punchy sentences that mirror the rapid tempo of the characters’ lives. Descriptive passages are lush enough to evoke the sticky humidity of the Gulf coast, yet never linger so long that they stall the narrative momentum. Dialogue crackles with flirtatious banter and raw confession, allowing readers to feel the immediacy of each encounter. While some critics might point out a reliance on formulaic tropes— the bad‑boy hero, the wounded yet hopeful newcomer, the “girl next door” with a secret strength—Glines executes them with enough charm and sincerity that they feel fresh within the confines of the New Adult romance genre.

Ultimately, Because of Low succeeds as a breezy, steamy escape that delivers on its promise of heat and heartache. It offers a satisfying blend of sensuality and emotional growth, making it a solid entry for fans of Glines’s Sea Breeze universe and newcomers looking for a quick, engaging read that lingers just long enough to feel the tide’s pull.
