# Career of Evil (Cormoran Strike, #3)

**Author:** Robert Galbraith  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

Career of Evil, the third installment in Robert Galbraith's Cormoran Strike series, is a gripping and twisted tale of mystery, suspense, and intrigue. The story begins with a gruesome discovery - a severed leg delivered to Robin Ellacott, Strike's trusted assistant. As the investigation unfolds, Strike's troubled past comes back to haunt him, and he is forced to confront four men capable of unspeakable brutality.

One of the most striking aspects of Galbraith's writing is her ability to weave together multiple storylines and themes. On the surface, Career of Evil is a classic whodunit, with Strike and Robin racing against time to uncover the identity of the killer. However, as the narrative progresses, it becomes clear that the novel is also a nuanced exploration of the human psyche. The four suspects, each with their own dark and troubled past, serve as a catalyst for Strike's introspection, forcing him to confront his own demons and re-evaluate his relationships.

Galbraith's writing is, as always, masterful. Her prose is concise and evocative, conjuring vivid images of the characters and settings. The pacing is relentless, with each chapter ending on a cliffhanger that propels the reader forward. The characters, too, are expertly crafted, with Strike and Robin's complex and often fraught relationship taking center stage.

Throughout the novel, Galbraith explores themes of trauma, power dynamics, and the blurred lines between good and evil. The four suspects, each with their own brand of depravity, serve as a reminder that evil can take many forms, and that even the most seemingly ordinary people can harbor dark secrets. Meanwhile, Strike's own backstory, marked by a troubled childhood and a complicated relationship with his famous father, serves as a poignant reminder of the lasting impact of trauma.

One of the most impressive aspects of Career of Evil is its ability to balance the dark and disturbing subject matter with a wry sense of humor. Galbraith's wit is on full display, particularly in the banter between Strike and Robin, which adds a welcome levity to the narrative.

If there is one criticism to be made, it is that the novel's supporting cast can feel somewhat one-dimensional at times. The four suspects, while expertly crafted as villains, feel somewhat cartoonish in their depravity, and the police officers tasked with investigating the crime often feel like little more than cardboard cutouts.

Overall, however, Career of Evil is a triumph. It is a gripping, suspenseful, and thought-provoking novel that will appeal to fans of mystery, thriller, and crime fiction. With its complex characters, intricate plotting, and nuanced exploration of the human psyche, it is a must-read for anyone looking for a compelling and unsettling page-turner.
