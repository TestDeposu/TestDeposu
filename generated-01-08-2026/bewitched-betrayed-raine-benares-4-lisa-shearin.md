# Bewitched & Betrayed (Raine Benares, #4)

**Author:** Lisa Shearin  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

**Bewitched & Betrayed: A Spellbinding Confrontation of Power and Purpose**

Lisa Shearin’s *Bewitched & Betrayed*—the fourth installment in the *Raine Benares* series—is a masterclass in blending high-stakes fantasy with the raw, unfiltered intensity of a heroine who refuses to be defined by her past. At its core, this novel is a meditation on the cost of power, the weight of legacy, and the fragile line between salvation and damnation. Raine Benares, the series’ relentless seeker, is no longer just a woman who finds what’s lost; she is now a vessel for the Saghred, a soul-stealing stone that grants her godlike abilities—but at what price?

Shearin’s writing is as sharp as it is immersive, weaving a narrative that feels both timeless and urgently modern. The prose is lean, the dialogue crackles with wit and menace, and the world-building is so rich it becomes a character in its own right. The Saghred isn’t just a plot device; it’s a metaphor for the seductive allure of absolute control. Raine’s bond with it forces her to confront the very nature of her quest: is she still seeking the lost, or has she become the thing she once hunted? The tension between her humanity and the monstrous potential of the stone creates a psychological depth that elevates the story beyond mere action. Shearin doesn’t shy away from the moral ambiguities—Raine’s choices are never black and white, and neither are the consequences.

What makes *Bewitched & Betrayed* particularly compelling is its exploration of betrayal—not just the external threats lurking in the shadows, but the betrayal of trust in oneself. The stone whispers promises of power, but it also strips away the very essence of what makes Raine who she is. Shearin excels at making the supernatural feel visceral, turning abstract concepts like soul-stealing into something tangible and terrifying. The pacing is relentless, with each chapter revealing new layers of the Saghred’s influence and the lengths to which its escapees will go to reclaim it—or destroy Raine in the process.

Yet, for all its darkness, the novel never loses sight of Raine’s resilience. She’s a heroine who has faced demons, literal and metaphorical, and she’s not about to let the Saghred break her. The battle isn’t just physical; it’s a clash of wills, a test of whether she can reclaim her agency in a world that keeps trying to steal it from her. Shearin’s ability to balance action with introspection ensures that the stakes feel personal, even when the threats are cosmic.

The real strength of *Bewitched & Betrayed* lies in its refusal to offer easy answers. The Saghred isn’t just a tool; it’s a curse, and Raine’s journey is one of learning to wield it without becoming it. Shearin’s world is vast, her characters are flawed and compelling, and her storytelling is as gripping as it is thought-provoking. This isn’t just another fantasy adventure—it’s a story about the price of power, the weight of choice, and the courage to fight for what’s right, even when the odds are stacked against you.

If you’ve followed Raine’s arc from the beginning, you’ll be rewarded with a story that deepens her character in ways that feel earned and inevitable. And if you’re new to the series, *Bewitched & Betrayed* is a perfect entry point—it’s self-contained enough to stand alone, yet rich enough to draw you into a world where the line between hero and villain is as blurred as the soul-stealing stone itself.
