# Conclave

**Author:** Robert Harris  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

In the midst of a secretive and centuries-old institution, Robert Harris masterfully weaves a tale of intrigue, corruption, and power struggles in his 2016 novel, Conclave. This gripping thriller delves into the mysterious world of the Vatican, where one hundred and eighteen cardinals gather to elect the next pope, following the sudden death of the incumbent. Behind the locked doors of the Sistine Chapel, these holy men are not immune to the temptations of power, glory, and tribalism that plagues humanity.

Harris's writing style is impeccable, as he skillfully crafts a narrative that pulsates with intrigue, keeping readers on the edge of their seats. With his meticulous research and attention to detail, he sheds light on the inner workings of the Vatican, exposing the dark underbelly of corruption, abuse, and scandals that threaten to destroy the very fabric of the Church. The author's use of language is concise, yet evocative, painting vivid pictures of the Sistine Chapel's grandeur and the cardinals' intense debates.

As the cardinals engage in a cloistered debate, Harris masterfully explores the complexities of human nature, revealing the flaws and weaknesses of these supposedly holy men. The novel is a scathing critique of the Church's hierarchy, exposing the hypocrisy and self-interest that often masquerade as piety. Through the characters' interactions and backroom dealings, Harris lays bare the cutthroat politics that govern the Vatican, where alliances are forged and broken with ease.

One of the most striking aspects of Conclave is its ability to balance the grandeur of the Vatican with the petty squabbles and power struggles of its inhabitants. Harris's portrayal of the cardinals is nuanced, revealing their humanity beneath the robes and mitres. The characters are multidimensional, with their own motivations, desires, and fears, making them relatable and, at times, even sympathetic.

The novel's pacing is expertly managed, with Harris skillfully ratcheting up the tension as the cardinals' debates reach a fever pitch. The plot twists and turns, keeping readers guessing until the very end, when a shocking revelation throws everything into chaos. Harris's masterful storytelling is a testament to his skill as a writer, as he weaves a complex narrative that is both engaging and thought-provoking.

In conclusion, Conclave is a gripping thriller that offers a fascinating glimpse into the secretive world of the Vatican. Robert Harris's writing is impeccable, and his attention to detail is impressive. The novel is a must-read for anyone interested in politics, power struggles, and the inner workings of the Catholic Church. With its intricate plot, nuanced characters, and thought-provoking themes, Conclave is a page-turner that will keep readers on the edge of their seats until the very end.
