# Burner (Gray Man, #12)

**Author:** Mark Greaney  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

Mark Greaney's latest addition to the Gray Man series, Burner, is an electrifying thriller that plunges readers into a world of high-stakes espionage and corruption. At its core, the novel is a gripping exploration of the blurred lines between loyalty, power, and survival.

When Alex Velesky, a Swiss banker, stumbles upon a web of corruption, he unwittingly sets off a chain reaction that puts him squarely in the crosshairs of the Russian mafia and the CIA. As the story unfolds, Court Gentry, the enigmatic Gray Man, and his former lover, Zoya Zakharova, find themselves on opposite sides of a deadly cat-and-mouse game, each with their own reasons for wanting Velesky.

Greaney masterfully weaves together a complex tapestry of intrigue, deception, and betrayal, keeping readers on the edge of their seats as the body count rises and alliances are forged and broken. The author's writing style is as sleek and efficient as his protagonist, with a focus on propulsive action and razor-sharp dialogue.

One of the standout features of Burner is its exploration of the gray areas that exist between right and wrong. Gentry, in particular, is a fascinating study in moral ambiguity, driven by a personal code that is both noble and ruthless. As the story hurtles towards its conclusion, Greaney raises important questions about the nature of loyalty and the true cost of survival in a world where the stakes are always high.

Greaney's prose is as lean and mean as his protagonist, with a focus on delivering maximum impact with minimum fuss. The pacing is relentless, with barely a moment to catch one's breath as the action unfolds. The supporting cast is equally well-drawn, with Velesky's desperation and Zoya's calculating intensity adding depth to the narrative.

If there's one criticism to be made, it's that the supporting cast can sometimes feel a little one-dimensional. However, this is a minor quibble in what is otherwise a gripping and expertly crafted thriller.

In conclusion, Burner is a must-read for fans of the Gray Man series and anyone who loves a good espionage thriller. With its breakneck pacing, complex characters, and thought-provoking themes, it's a compelling addition to the genre.
