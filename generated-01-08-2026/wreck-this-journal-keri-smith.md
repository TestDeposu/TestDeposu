# Wreck This Journal

**Author:** Keri Smith  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

Title: The Art of Getting Lost: Keri Smith’s Invitation to the Unknown

In an age dominated by GPS navigation, hyper-scheduled itineraries, and the relentless pursuit of optimization, Keri Smith offers a radical antidote: the art of getting lost. Best known for her interactive masterpiece *Wreck This Journal*, Smith returns with a publication from 2016 that shifts from physical destruction to mental liberation. This work serves as an investigative guide, manifesto, and field manual into the clandestine world of The Wander Society—a secretive group dedicated to the philosophy of aimless exploration.

To review this book through a traditional literary lens is to miss the point entirely. It is not merely a book to be read; it is an artifact to be experienced. Smith’s style is brilliantly idiosyncratic, blending elements of historical research, scrapbooking, manifesto writing, and creative provocation. The pages mimic the tactile feel of a discovered journal, filled with handwritten notes, striking typography, vintage ephemera, and cryptic diagrams. This aesthetic reinforces the central theme of the book: that true knowledge and creativity cannot be found on a pre-plotted screen, but rather in the physical dirt, unexpected alleyways, and margins of our daily lives.

The core theme of the book is the reclamation of wonder. Smith explores how purposeful drifting—walking without a destination, following a random color or sensory cue, and embracing a complete openness to the unknown—can act as a profound catalyst for the creative mind. Drawing inspiration from historical figures and a mysterious society she stumbled upon within the pages of Walt Whitman’s *Leaves of Grass*, Smith argues that society has traded curiosity for convenience. By treating wandering as a disciplined artistic practice, the author challenges readers to break free from habitual routines and rediscover their immediate environment with childlike awe.

What makes Smith's approach so compelling is her ability to make the mundane feel magical. She does not instruct you to travel to exotic locales; instead, she teaches you how to become a stranger in your own neighborhood. Her writing style is encouraging, subversive, and gently demanding. She acts less like an author imparting wisdom and more like a mysterious mentor slipping a secret map into your pocket, whispering an invitation to step off the designated path.

Ultimately, this 2016 release is a timely and timeless reminder of the value of serendipity. It challenges the modern obsession with productivity by championing the productive power of doing nothing with purpose. For anyone feeling creatively blocked, spiritually fatigued, or simply trapped by the monotony of modern life, Smith provides the ultimate key to escape. You will never look at a city sidewalk, a park bench, or an ordinary Tuesday afternoon the same way again.
