# Evil for Evil (Engineer Trilogy, #2)

**Author:** K.J. Parker  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

"Evil for Evil", the second installment of K.J. Parker's Engineer Trilogy, plunges readers into a gritty, unforgiving world of war, technology, and moral complexity. Building upon the momentum established in the first book, Devices and Desires, Parker's latest offering cements his reputation as a masterful storyteller, deftly weaving a narrative that challenges conventions and probes the very fabric of power, responsibility, and the human condition.

At its core, "Evil for Evil" is a tale of siege warfare, pitting the beleaguered city of Civitas Vadanis against the ruthless invaders from Mezentia. However, this is no traditional fantasy epic; Parker's world is one where the boundaries between 'good' and 'evil' are constantly blurred, and the consequences of actions are starkly evident. As our protagonist, Duke Valens, grapples with the impossible decision to surrender or flee, he is forced to confront the darkest aspects of his own nature and the nature of the world he inhabits.

Meanwhile, Ziani Vaatzes, the brilliant but morally ambiguous engineer, navigates the treacherous landscape of his own past, wrestling with the ghosts of his former creations. Parker's expertly crafted characters are neither caricatures nor one-dimensional heroes; they are flawed, multidimensional beings, rife with contradictions and contradictions of their own making. This is a world where the line between genius and monstrosity is a fine one, and Parker's writing skillfully navigates these gray areas.

What sets "Evil for Evil" apart from other fantasy epics is its unique blend of wit, intelligence, and compassion. Parker's prose is economical yet evocative, conjuring vivid images of ancient cities, ingenious machines, and the devastating consequences of war. The narrative is as much a character study as it is a gripping page-turner, probing the intricacies of human psychology and the nature of creativity itself.

This is fantasy writing at its finest, replete with intellectual depth, emotional resonance, and a keen-eyed insight into the human condition. Parker's world is a complex tapestry of conflicting values and motivations, reflecting the darker aspects of our own world and our own capacity for good or evil. As readers delve deeper into the Engineer Trilogy, they will find themselves grappling with questions that linger long after the final page has turned.

K.J. Parker's "Evil for Evil" is not simply a great fantasy novel; it's a work of literary artistry, a testament to the power of storytelling to illuminate the human experience. This is a must-read for anyone fascinated by the intersection of technology, society, and individual morality, and a fitting sequel to the critically acclaimed Devices and Desires.
