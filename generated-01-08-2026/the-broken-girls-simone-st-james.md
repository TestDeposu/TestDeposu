# The Broken Girls

**Author:** Simone St. James  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

In "The Broken Girls," Simone St. James masterfully weaves a haunting and suspenseful tale that delves into the dark recesses of human nature, exposing the sinister secrets that lie hidden beneath the surface of a seemingly idyllic world. This gripping novel is a testament to the author's skill in crafting a narrative that seamlessly blends the past and the present, creating a sense of unease and tension that propels the reader forward.

At its core, "The Broken Girls" is a story about the power of female relationships and the unbreakable bonds that form between women who have been marginalized and oppressed. The novel is set in two distinct time periods: 1950 and 2014. In the earlier era, we are introduced to four roommates at Idlewild Hall, a boarding school for girls who have been deemed "troublemakers" or "unwanted." These young women form a tight-knit circle, supporting and protecting each other from the cruel whims of the world. However, their friendship is short-lived, as one of them mysteriously disappears, leaving behind only whispers and rumors.

The narrative then shifts to 2014, where we meet Fiona Sheridan, a journalist who is still grappling with the traumatic loss of her sister, Deb, whose body was found near the ruins of Idlewild Hall twenty years earlier. Fiona's elder sister's murder was attributed to her boyfriend, but Fiona has always harbored doubts about the case. When she discovers that Idlewild Hall is being restored, she sees an opportunity to write a story about the school's dark past and, perhaps, uncover the truth about her sister's death.

As Fiona delves deeper into the history of Idlewild Hall, she begins to unravel a web of secrets and lies that have been hidden for decades. The novel's tension builds slowly, as Fiona's investigation awakens a malevolent force that seems determined to keep the truth buried. Through Fiona's eyes, we experience the creeping sense of dread that permeates the abandoned school, and the author's vivid descriptions of the setting effectively evoke a sense of unease and foreboding.

One of the most striking aspects of "The Broken Girls" is St. James's masterful use of language. Her writing is atmospheric and evocative, conjuring the mist-shrouded hills and crumbling buildings of rural Vermont. The author's prose is economical, yet effective, conveying the complexities of human emotions and relationships with remarkable precision.

The characters in the novel are multidimensional and relatable, with Fiona being a particularly well-crafted protagonist. Her determination and resilience in the face of adversity make her a compelling and admirable character, and her emotional vulnerability adds depth to the narrative.

Ultimately, "The Broken Girls" is a story about the power of female voices and the importance of listening to the whispers of the past. It is a testament to the enduring bonds of female friendship and the unbreakable spirits of women who have been marginalized and oppressed. St. James's writing is a powerful tribute to the women who have been silenced, and her novel is a must-read for anyone who enjoys a gripping and suspenseful tale.
