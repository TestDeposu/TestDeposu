# What My Bones Know

**Author:** Stephanie Foo  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

In the thought-provoking and deeply personal memoir, "What My Bones Know," Stephanie Foo embarks on a transformative journey to unravel the intricacies of complex post-traumatic stress disorder (C-PTSD). With unflinching candor, Foo confronts the lingering effects of childhood trauma, immigration, and intergenerational pain, illuminating the often- overlooked science behind C-PTSD and its profound impact on her life.

Foo's narrative is a masterclass in emotional vulnerability, as she recounts the seemingly mundane aspects of her daily struggles – from panic attacks at her desk to the persistent weight of abandonment by her parents. As she grapples with her diagnosis, Foo sets out to redefine what it means to heal from trauma, rather than merely "moving on" from it. This is a powerful assertion, one that will resonate with countless individuals who have been silenced or shamed into hiding their trauma.

Throughout the book, Foo's research-driven approach provides a rich tapestry of understanding, weaving together the latest scientific discoveries and theoretical frameworks with her own lived experience. She consults with experts in the field, attempts novel therapies, and delves into the experiences of others who have navigated similar journeys. This approach not only underscores the complexity of C-PTSD but also underscores Foo's determination to reclaim agency over her own life.

One of the most striking aspects of "What My Bones Know" is Foo's refusal to shy away from the darker corners of her own psyche. Her writing is unflinching and, at times, breathtakingly honest, laying bare the intricacies of her own trauma and its effects on her relationships and career. Her bravery in sharing this narrative serves as a beacon of hope for those who have been marginalized or dismissed by the very systems meant to support them.

At the heart of Foo's memoir lies a profound exploration of intergenerational trauma, which she uncovers through her investigations into her family's past in Malaysia. This thread is expertly woven throughout the narrative, illustrating the ways in which systemic trauma can be transmitted across borders and generations. Foo's inquiry into the cultural narratives surrounding trauma and healing serves as a potent reminder of the urgent need for more nuanced, culturally sensitive approaches to trauma recovery.

Foo's writing style is a testament to her skill as a journalist and storyteller. Her prose is lucid, evocative, and effortlessly accessible, making complex scientific concepts and theoretical frameworks feel deceptively simple. Her narrative voice is a true triumph – warm, compassionate, and unafraid to confront the depths of human suffering. Whether she is recounting a poignant encounter with an expert or a personal moment of epiphany, Foo's writing has a magnetic quality that draws the reader in and refuses to let go.

Ultimately, "What My Bones Know" is a triumphant, searing memoir that challenges the dominant narratives surrounding trauma and healing. Foo's work is a testament to the power of storytelling as a tool for both recovery and redemption. In its willingness to confront the darkest aspects of human experience, this book serves as a beacon of hope for those who have been silenced or shamed into hiding their trauma. Through Foo's courage and tenacity, we are reminded that healing is a process that can be learned, one that allows us to move with, rather than merely move on from, our past.
