# Wicked (A Wicked Trilogy, #1)

**Author:** Jennifer L. Armentrout  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

Wicked, the first installment in Jennifer L. Armentrout's captivating trilogy, delves into a world of magic and mystery set against the enchanting backdrop of New Orleans. This 2014 release introduces readers to Ivy Morgan, a 22-year-old college student with a secret: she's a hunter of supernatural creatures that lurk in the shadows of the French Quarter.

Armentrout masterfully weaves a narrative that balances action, suspense, and romance, drawing readers into Ivy's world of duty and danger. The protagonist's past is marred by tragedy, having lost everything four years prior at the hands of the very creatures she now hunts. This traumatic experience has left Ivy with emotional scars, making her wary of forming close relationships. However, the arrival of Ren Owens, a charming and mysterious figure, disrupts her carefully constructed life.

Ren's presence ignites a spark within Ivy, and their undeniable chemistry becomes a recurring theme throughout the novel. As they grow closer, Ivy finds herself torn between her duty to the Order and her desire for Ren. This internal conflict raises questions about the nature of duty, loyalty, and the human need for connection.

Armentrout's writing style is engaging and immersive, making it easy for readers to become invested in Ivy's story. Her use of descriptive language effectively transports readers to the vibrant city of New Orleans, where the lines between reality and the supernatural are blurred. The author's ability to craft relatable characters adds depth to the narrative, making it simple for readers to empathize with Ivy's struggles.

One of the most striking aspects of Wicked is its exploration of the human condition. Ivy's character, in particular, serves as a reflection of the human experience. Her struggles with grief, trust, and identity are universally relatable, making her a compelling and authentic protagonist. As Ivy navigates her complicated world, she must confront the darkness within herself and the secrets that surround her.

The romance in Wicked is a slow-burning flame that simmers beneath the surface, adding a layer of tension to the narrative. Ren's character is expertly crafted, with a mysterious past that adds to the intrigue. The chemistry between Ivy and Ren is undeniable, but their relationship is not without its challenges. As they navigate their feelings for each other, they must also confront the secrets that threaten to tear them apart.

In conclusion, Wicked is a captivating novel that seamlessly blends elements of fantasy, romance, and suspense. Armentrout's writing style is engaging, and her characters are well-developed and relatable. The novel's exploration of the human condition adds depth to the narrative, making it a compelling read for fans of the genre.
