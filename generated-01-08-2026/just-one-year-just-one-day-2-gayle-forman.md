# Just One Year (Just One Day, #2)

**Author:** Gayle Forman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

In Gayle Forman's poignant and captivating companion novel, Just One Year, we are privy to Willem's side of the story, a compelling counterpoint to Allyson's tale in Just One Day. Through Willem's lens, we embark on a transformative journey of self-discovery, love, and questioning the true meaning of fate. 

The novel's narrative is deeply rooted in the existentialism that permeates Willem's year of quiet longing, a period during which he grapples with the hand of fate and the elusive Lulu. Forman masterfully crafts a story that oscillates between the poignant and the humorous, never shying away from Willem's raw emotions as he navigates the complexities of love, loss, and growth. Willem's yearning for Lulu becomes an odyssey in itself, as he traverses the globe in search of an unfulfilled connection that seems to be perpetually out of reach.

Through Willem's story, Forman presents us with a nuanced examination of the human experience, probing the notion of fate, free will, and the search for meaning in an increasingly chaotic world. Willem's existential crisis serves as a powerful catalyst for growth, as he begins to re-evaluate his life choices and the roles others have played in shaping his identity. The journey, though marked by moments of disillusionment and heartache, ultimately yields profound self-awareness and a redefinition of his place in the world.

One of the most striking aspects of Forman's writing style is her ability to imbue her characters with authentic emotions and vulnerabilities. Willem, in particular, is a refreshingly candid protagonist, whose introspections and observations paint a vivid picture of his inner world. We are privy to his innermost thoughts, insecurities, and aspirations, making him a relatable and endearing character.

Furthermore, Forman's writing is characterized by its effortless fluidity, transporting readers to the streets of Paris, Tokyo, and beyond. Her descriptions of the places Willem visits are rendered with a level of detail and poignancy that makes readers feel as though they, too, are walking alongside him on his journey. Whether depicting the fleeting moments of connection with strangers or the quiet moments of introspection in foreign landscapes, Forman's prose is both evocative and accessible.

What emerges from Just One Year is a narrative that is both a travelogue and a romance, with Forman deftly weaving together the two strands to create a compelling narrative that will resonate with readers. Fans of the movie Before Sunrise and Maureen Johnson's 13 Little Blue Envelopes will find much to appreciate in this novel, which shares a similar sense of wanderlust and longing.

Ultimately, Just One Year is a poignant and thought-provoking exploration of love, self-discovery, and the human experience. Through Willem's journey, Forman reminds us that the search for meaning and connection is a lifelong pursuit, one that demands courage, vulnerability, and an openness to the unknown. As readers, we are invited to join Willem on his transformative odyssey, one that will leave us pondering the mysteries of fate, free will, and the complexities of the human heart.
