# The Search for Mother Missing: A Peek Inside International Adoption (Adoption Books for Adults)

**Author:** Janine Vance  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

"The Search for Mother Missing: A Peek Inside International Adoption" by Janine Vance offers a thought-provoking and deeply personal exploration of the complexities of international adoption, particularly that of Korean adoptees (KADs). Published in 2020, Vance's book delves into the intriguing connection between KADs and the Black Lives Matter movement, advocating for a bridge-building approach that transcends racial and cultural divides.

Throughout the book, Vance presents compelling profiles of Korean adoptees who have found their place within the Black Lives Matter movement. These narratives humanize the experiences of KADs, exposing the reader to the unique challenges and opportunities that arise from their bicultural and biracial identities. By highlighting the commonalities between KADs and Black Americans, Vance masterfully demonstrates that their experiences are intertwined, and that they can serve as a conduit for greater understanding and solidarity.

Vance's writing style is engaging, empathetic, and free of jargon, making the book accessible to readers from diverse backgrounds. Her dedication to amplifying the voices of Korean adoptees is evident throughout the narrative, as she shares their stories with sensitivity and respect. The author's conviction that KADs can play a vital role in supporting the Black Lives Matter movement is inspiring, and her enthusiasm is contagious.

One of the most striking aspects of "The Search for Mother Missing" is its ambitious three-part structure. The first section, comprising profiles of Korean adoptees, sets the tone for the book's central theme: the connections between Black Americans and KADs. The second part, which delves into background history, provides a richer understanding of the historical and social contexts that have shaped these relationships. Finally, the how-to section, written by Korean adoptee Yi Woo Ae, offers a practical guide for others who aspire to follow in their footsteps.

Vance's advocacy for the inclusion of adoption-based and race-based trauma in the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM) is particularly noteworthy. By acknowledging the need for greater inclusivity in the manual's definitions of trauma, the author underscores the importance of recognizing the unique experiences of marginalized communities. This aspect of the book is not only informative but also deeply humane, highlighting the urgent need for compassion and understanding in our increasingly complex world.

Ultimately, "The Search for Mother Missing" is a call to action, urging readers to confront the complexities of international adoption and the intersections of racial identity. Through Vance's masterful storytelling, KADs emerge as powerful bridge-builders, capable of fostering empathy, understanding, and solidarity between communities that may have seemed insurmountably divided. By embracing this vision, readers may discover a new world of possibility, where the boundaries between cultures and identities are blurred, and the power of connection is palpable.
