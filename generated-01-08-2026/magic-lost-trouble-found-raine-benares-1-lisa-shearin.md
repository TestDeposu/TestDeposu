# Magic Lost, Trouble Found (Raine Benares #1)

**Author:** Lisa Shearin  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

In the realm of urban fantasy, it's not often that a debut novel makes as bold a statement as Lisa Shearin's "Magic Lost, Trouble Found." This inaugural installment of the Raine Benares series, published in 2007, introduces us to a world where magic and mayhem collide in a swirl of action, adventure, and humor.

At the heart of the story is Raine Benares, a Sorceress Seeker of unremarkable talent, until she stumbles upon an ancient amulet that catapults her into a maelstrom of power and peril. This artifact, known as the Saghred, amplifies Raine's magical abilities, making her a coveted prize for various factions vying for control. As Raine navigates this treacherous landscape, she must confront her own limitations, as well as the darkness within herself.

Shearin's writing style is a perfect blend of wit, sarcasm, and humor, making Raine a relatable and endearing protagonist. The author's use of first-person narration allows the reader to experience the story through Raine's eyes, creating an intimate and immersive connection. The world-building is equally impressive, with a richly detailed magical system and a complex web of characters that add depth to the narrative.

One of the standout aspects of "Magic Lost, Trouble Found" is its exploration of the theme of power and its corrupting influence. As Raine becomes increasingly entwined with the Saghred, she must confront the danger of losing herself to its power. This internal struggle serves as a compelling counterpoint to the external conflicts that drive the plot, adding a layer of complexity to the story.

Shearin's pacing is well-balanced, moving seamlessly between action sequences, humorous moments, and poignant character interactions. The supporting cast is equally well-developed, with a diverse array of characters that add to the story's richness. From the enigmatic Tam Nathrach to the roguish Mychael Eiliesor, each character brings their own unique dynamic to the narrative.

If there's one criticism to be made, it's that the story occasionally relies on convenient coincidences and plot contrivances. However, these minor quibbles are easily overlooked in light of the book's many strengths.

In conclusion, "Magic Lost, Trouble Found" is a captivating debut that sets the stage for a thrilling series. With its unique blend of magic, adventure, and humor, this book is sure to appeal to fans of urban fantasy and action-packed storytelling. Lisa Shearin's writing style is engaging, witty, and immersive, making this a must-read for anyone looking to escape into a richly detailed world of magic and mayhem.
