# Underworld (Abandon, #2)

**Author:** Meg Cabot  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

In "Underworld", the second installment of Meg Cabot's "Abandon" series, the lines between life and death are blurred, and the boundaries of the afterlife are tested. Pierce Oliviera, the protagonist, is a character who has experienced death firsthand and has been forever changed by the encounter. Her journey is a poignant exploration of what it means to be alive, and the consequences of being drawn to the darkness that lies beyond.

Cabot's writing is, as always, engaging and accessible, making it easy to become fully immersed in Pierce's world. Her use of vivid imagery and descriptive language brings the Underworld to life, making it a tangible and believable place. The author's ability to balance action, suspense, and romance is impressive, and she expertly weaves these elements together to create a narrative that is both captivating and emotional.

One of the most striking aspects of "Underworld" is its exploration of the human condition. Pierce's struggles to reconcile her life with her experiences in the Underworld serve as a powerful metaphor for the challenges we all face in our own lives. Her feelings of disconnection and isolation are deeply relatable, and her desire to find her place in the world is a universal one.

Cabot also explores the theme of mortality in "Underworld", raising important questions about the nature of life and death. Pierce's encounters with the dead and the Underworld's ruler, John Hayden, serve as a reminder that death is a natural part of life, and that the afterlife is not always what we expect it to be. This theme is handled with sensitivity and nuance, and adds depth to the narrative.

The characters in "Underworld" are well-developed and complex, with Pierce being a particularly strong protagonist. Her growth and development throughout the novel are significant, and her relationships with the other characters are authentic and engaging. John Hayden, the love interest, is a compelling and enigmatic figure, and his interactions with Pierce are charged with tension and emotion.

Overall, "Underworld" is a compelling and thought-provoking read that will appeal to fans of fantasy, romance, and adventure. Cabot's writing is engaging and immersive, and her exploration of the human condition is both poignant and relatable. This novel is a must-read for anyone looking for a story that will capture their imagination and leave them eager for more.
