# Frankly in Love

**Author:** David Yoon  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

Frankly in Love, the debut novel by David Yoon, is an extraordinary exploration of identity, culture, and first love. The story revolves around Frank Li, a high school student caught between his Korean heritage and American upbringing. Frank's parents, though well-intentioned, are traditional in their expectations, wanting him to date a "nice Korean girl." However, Frank's heart belongs to Brit Means, a white girl who makes him laugh and feel alive.

As Frank navigates this cultural divide, he turns to his friend Joy Song, who is also dealing with similar parental pressures. Together, they concoct a fake dating scheme to appease their parents while secretly pursuing their true loves. This plan sets off a chain of events that challenges Frank's perceptions of love, identity, and family.

Yoon's writing is humorous, heartfelt, and authentic, making it easy to become invested in Frank's journey. The author tackles complex themes like racism, forgiveness, and acceptance without being preachy or heavy-handed. Instead, Yoon presents a nuanced exploration of the Asian American experience, highlighting the struggles of balancing cultural heritage with personal identity.

One of the novel's greatest strengths is its ability to balance lighthearted moments with deeper, more meaningful themes. Yoon's use of humor and wit makes the story relatable and entertaining, while his thoughtful exploration of identity and culture adds depth and resonance.

Through Frank's story, Yoon raises important questions about who we are and how we define ourselves. Frank's struggles to reconcile his Korean and American identities serve as a powerful reminder that identity is complex and multifaceted. The novel shows that it's possible to embrace multiple cultures and identities without sacrificing one's sense of self.

Ultimately, Frankly in Love is a beautifully layered novel that will resonate with readers of all ages. Yoon's writing is engaging, and his characters are well-developed and relatable. The story is a powerful exploration of identity, culture, and first love, making it a must-read for anyone looking for a thoughtful and entertaining novel.
