# Four to Score (Stephanie Plum, #4)

**Author:** Janet Evanovich  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

Janet Evanovich continues her wildly successful and delightfully chaotic mystery series with Four to Score, the fourth installment featuring everyone's favorite inept bounty hunter, Stephanie Plum. Published in 2010, this chapter in the beloved Trenton, New Jersey saga proves that Evanovich’s formula of high-stakes danger blended with lowbrow domestic absurdity remains endlessly entertaining. At its core, the main theme of this novel explores the resilience of ordinary people thrust into extraordinary chaos, navigating life's financial and professional failures with sheer stubbornness and a healthy dose of dark humor. 

The plot kicks into gear when Stephanie takes on the case of Maxine Nowicki, a revenge-seeking waitress who has jumped bail after facing charges of theft and extortion. As Stephanie tracks Maxine down, she finds herself in a lethal game of cat-and-mouse, not only against her target and a rising body count of terrified witnesses, but also against Joyce Barnhardt, her vicious archenemy and professional rival. Yet, the external threats of extortionists and firebombs are only half the battle. True to Evanovich’s style, the real emotional core—and the source of the greatest comedic tension—lies in Stephanie's domestic upheaval. Forced into homelessness and near bankruptcy, Stephanie and her trusty hamster, Rex, are compelled to move in with her complicated romantic interest, the brooding vice cop Joe Morelli. This forced proximity elevates the romantic subplot, injecting a palpable, libidinous tension that matches the PG-35 rating playfully assigned to the book.

Evanovich’s writing style is the true engine driving the Stephanie Plum series. She masters the art of the fast-paced, first-person narrative, infusing every page with a snappy, street-smart voice that instantly endears the protagonist to the reader. The author excels at balancing genuine suspense with laugh-out-loud slapstick comedy. Where other mystery authors might bog down a murder investigation with grim procedural details, Evanovich leans into the absurd. The danger feels real—escalating swiftly from threatening messages to devastating firebombs—yet it is continually undercut by the eccentric supporting cast. 

Speaking of the supporting cast, Evanovich populates her Trenton underworld with some of the most memorable, larger-than-life characters in contemporary mystery fiction. The appearances of the gun-toting, funeral-obsessed Grandma Mazur, the larger-than-life ex-hooker turned wannabe bounty hunter Lula, and the unforgettable transvestite rock musician Sally Sweet inject vibrant color and unpredictable energy into every scene. These characters are not just comedic relief; they represent Stephanie's chaotic safety net, proving that while she may be broke, homeless, and constantly dodging explosives, she is never truly alone.

Four to Score is a stellar testament to Janet Evanovich's mastery of the comedic mystery genre. It delivers all the hallmarks that fans have come to adore: snappy dialogue, breakneck pacing, a volatile love triangle, and a mystery that keeps the pages turning late into the night. It is a striking, fast-moving adventure that proves even when Stephanie Plum hits rock bottom, she does it with unbeatable style and a laugh.
