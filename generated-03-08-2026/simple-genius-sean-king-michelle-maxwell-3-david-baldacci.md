# Simple Genius (Sean King & Michelle Maxwell, #3)

**Author:** David Baldacci  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

Simple Genius, the third installment in David Baldacci's Sean King and Michelle Maxwell series, is a gripping and suspenseful thriller that delves into the dark world of espionage, codes, and spies. Published in 2007, this #1 New York Times bestseller has been praised for its compulsively readable narrative and Baldacci's masterful storytelling.

At the heart of the novel lies a complex web of intrigue and deception, centered around a secret CIA training camp and a mysterious laboratory near Washington, D.C. When a murder occurs at one of these clandestine institutions, ex-Secret Service agent Sean King is drawn into a world of mathematicians, codes, and spies, where nothing is as it seems. As King searches for answers, he encounters an autistic girl with an extraordinary genius, who becomes a crucial player in unraveling the mystery.

Baldacci's writing style is one of the standout features of this novel. His prose is lean and mean, with a keen focus on propelling the plot forward. He expertly weaves together multiple storylines, keeping the reader on the edge of their seat as they try to piece together the puzzle alongside King. The author's use of short, snappy chapters adds to the sense of urgency, making it impossible to put the book down.

One of the strengths of Simple Genius is its well-developed characters. Sean King and Michelle Maxwell, his partner and fellow ex-Secret Service agent, are complex and multi-dimensional. Both characters are battling their own personal demons, which adds an extra layer of depth to the narrative. Maxwell, in particular, is a fascinating character, whose struggles and vulnerabilities make her a more relatable and human protagonist.

The themes of Simple Genius are classic Baldacci – a mix of politics, espionage, and high-stakes action. However, the author also explores more nuanced ideas, such as the nature of genius and the exploitation of vulnerable individuals. The novel raises important questions about the morality of using exceptional individuals for national gain, and the consequences of playing with fire when it comes to sensitive information.

Overall, Simple Genius is a gripping and thought-provoking thriller that showcases Baldacci's mastery of the genre. With its breakneck pace, complex characters, and intricate plot, this novel is sure to appeal to fans of espionage and suspense fiction. If you're looking for a compelling and addictive read, Simple Genius is an excellent choice.
