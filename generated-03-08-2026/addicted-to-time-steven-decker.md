# Addicted to Time

**Author:** Steven Decker  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

In the dystopian landscape of Steven Decker’s 2014 novel, *Addicted to Time*, time is not merely a measure of existence; it is the ultimate currency, a weapon of subjugation, and the central obsession of a broken world. Decker constructs a chilling corporate dystopia where humanity has been commodified, and the private sector is ruled by a drug-addicted, tyrannical CEO named Jacobson. At its core, the novel is a gripping exploration of personal autonomy versus systemic control, asking a haunting question: how much of your humanity are you willing to sacrifice just to survive another day?

The narrative follows Stevens, an Enforcer for the ruthless Attila Corporation. Stevens is a man trapped between his present brutality and his desperate hope for a future with his estranged love, Aubrey. When a rebel attack shatters his monotonous existence, Stevens is thrust into a terrifying game of cat and mouse. Branded a traitor by the corporation he once served, he must navigate a hostile wasteland alongside Decker, a rebel who ironically destroyed his former life. 

Decker’s thematic brilliance lies in the claustrophobia of his world-building. The Attila Corporation represents the logical, horrifying extreme of late-stage capitalism, where workers are stripped of their families, subjected to constant fear, and bound by inescapable contracts. The drug addiction plaguing the leadership mirrors the moral rot at the top of the corporate ladder—a desperate, frantic consumption of power that trickles down as relentless oppression. Against this bleak backdrop, Stevens’ journey is not just about physical survival, but about reclaiming a sense of agency in a society designed to grind individuality into dust. 

Stylistically, *Addicted to Time* moves at a relentless, adrenaline-fueled pace. Decker’s prose is punchy, visceral, and unyielding, mirroring the high-stakes desperation of his protagonist. He wastes no time on flowery exposition, instead dropping the reader directly into the grit, smoke, and blood of the refinery attacks and corporate manhunts. The action sequences are tightly choreographed and cinematic, yet they never overshadow the psychological weight of the story. The tension between Stevens and his reluctant rebel ally provides a sharp, character-driven anchor amidst the chaos, exploring themes of trust, betrayal, and the high cost of resistance. 

While the premise draws on classic dystopian tropes, Decker infuses the narrative with a gritty realism and emotional urgency that sets it apart. The world is morally gray, where the choice between running to fit in and killing to stand out forces characters into impossible moral corners. There are no easy heroes here, only survivors desperately clawing for a glimpse of freedom. 

*Addicted to Time* is a striking, fast-paced thriller that grabs the reader by the throat and refuses to let go. Steven Decker delivers a poignant warning about the dangers of corporate totalitarianism wrapped in a high-octane adventure. For fans of dystopian science fiction and gritty speculative thrillers, this 2014 gem is a compelling, thought-provoking ride that lingers long after the final page is turned.
