# Until Friday Night (The Field Party, #1)

**Author:** Abbi Glines  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

Anatomy of a Southern Tragedy: A Review of Abbi Glines's Until Friday Night

In the sun-baked, football-obsessed landscape of Lawton, Alabama, Friday nights are sacred. They are built on the roar of the stadium crowd, the rumble of pickup trucks, and the intoxicating illusion of small-town perfection. But beneath the glossy veneer of youthful invincibility lies a darker, more fragile reality. In the opening installment of her bestselling Field Party series, Until Friday Night (2015), author Abbi Glines strips away the pom-poms and stadium lights to examine what happens when teenage royalty is forced to confront devastating, adult-sized grief.

Glines, a veteran of New Adult and Young Adult contemporary romance, is well-known for her ability to tap directly into the heightened emotional states of adolescence. Her signature style is fast-paced, dialogue-driven, and unapologetically melodramatic. In Until Friday Night, she leans heavily into this formula, contrasting the hyper-masculine, boisterous culture of high school sports with intense, isolated moments of emotional vulnerability. The result is a narrative that moves at a relentless, addictive clip, pulling readers through the turbulence of teenage trauma with the gravitational pull of a soap opera.

At the heart of the novel are its two wounded protagonists, West Ashby and Maggie Carleton. West is the quintessential golden boy—the cocky, devastatingly handsome quarterback whose broad shoulders carry the weight of a town's championship hopes. Yet, Glines subverts the classic arrogant-jock trope by anchoring West in a profound, quiet agony. Behind closed doors, he is slowly unraveling as he watches his father succumb to terminal cancer. West is a boy running out of time, masking his panic behind charm and athletic prowess because he does not know how to exist without his hero.

Enter Maggie, the quiet new girl whose trauma is so absolute it has literally rendered her mute. Two years prior, Maggie’s life was obliterated when her father murdered her mother—a horrific, defining tragedy that she witnessed and subsequently reported. Swallowed by grief and isolation, Maggie has retreated into absolute silence, hiding her fractured heart behind an impenetrable wall. 

The central theme of Until Friday Night is not merely romance, but the dangerous, magnetic solace found in shared suffering. When West, desperate for an escape from his own suffocating reality, opens up to Maggie in the shadows of a post-game party, he expects a one-sided confession. Instead, the silent girl speaks. This pivotal moment shatters the boundaries of their respective isolations and births a profound connection. Glines excels at charting this emotional collision. The romance develops not through sweeping, lighthearted dates, but through raw, late-night confessions and the mutual recognition of profound pain. They are two drowning people clinging to each other in the dark, desperate for air.

Critics of Glines's work often point to her penchant for rapid-fire emotional shifts and heightened, almost tragicomic circumstances. Indeed, the plot mechanics of Until Friday Night rely heavily on extreme trauma—cancer, murder, muteness—stacked high within a standard high school setting. Yet, this excess is precisely where Glines finds her power. She does not write subtle literary fiction; she writes emotional catharsis. Lawton, Alabama, functions less like a real town and more like an emotional crucible where every cheerleading squad initiation and keg party serves as a stark backdrop to the characters' internal wars.

Ultimately, Until Friday Night is a striking testament to the resilience of the human heart, no matter how fractured. Glines captures the terrifying vulnerability of adolescence, proving that sometimes the strongest people in the room are the ones quietly fighting the hardest battles. For readers seeking a fast, emotionally charged romance that trades superficial sweet-talk for heavy-hitting vulnerability, Glines delivers a compelling opening salvo that leaves an indelible mark long after the stadium lights go out.
