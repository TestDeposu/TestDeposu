# A Little Hatred (The Age of Madness, #1)

**Author:** Joe Abercrombie  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

Progress, it turns out, is a blood sport. With A Little Hatred, the explosive first installment of The Age of Madness trilogy, Joe Abercrombie returns to the grimdark universe of his acclaimed First Law series to witness an empire on the precipice of a violent awakening. Published in 2019, this brilliant narrative captures a world standing violently between two eras: the smoke-choked dawn of the industrial machine and the stubborn, bloody survival of ancient magic. 

Abercrombie’s signature style has always been defined by its unflinching cynicism, razor-sharp wit, and psychological realism. In A Little Hatred, these elements are refined to a razor’s edge. The author trades the traditional high-fantasy tropes of noble heroes and destined quests for a cast of deeply flawed, morally gray, and thoroughly human characters. There are no saviors here, only survivors, opportunists, and fools caught in the grinding gears of history. 

The thematic core of the novel explores the cyclical nature of societal violence and the illusion of upward mobility. As the chimneys of industry rise over Adua, casting long, soot-stained shadows, the class divide widens into a chasm. Abercrombie brilliantly contrasts the cold, calculated cruelty of high-society capitalism with the raw, volatile desperation of the slums. 

The narrative is driven by a stellar ensemble of new faces carrying the heavy burdens of their bloodlines. We follow Leo dan Brock, a glory-obsessed young lion fighting on the frozen, blood-soaked borders of Angland against the marauding Stour Nightfall, abandoned by the apathy of the crown’s feckless Prince Orso. We watch Savine dan Glokta, brilliant investor and daughter of the Union’s most feared torturer, as she ruthlessly climbs the social ladder, blind to the simmering fury of the underclass boiling beneath her feet. Meanwhile, in the rugged north, the young Rikke struggles to master the terrifying burden of the Long Eye, guided by the unpredictable hillwoman Isern-i-Phail, all while the shadowy, immortal strings continue to be pulled by the First of the Magi.

What makes A Little Hatred a masterclass in modern fantasy is Abercrombie’s ability to weave profound social commentary into a relentlessly fast-paced, visceral plot. The dialogue is snappy and painfully authentic, the action is brutal and consequential, and the humor is as dark as a coal mine. Abercrombie dissects how old hatreds are repackaged into modern grievances, proving that while technology changes, human malice remains wonderfully, tragically consistent.

For both longtime fans of the First Law universe and newcomers stepping into the fray for the first time, A Little Hatred is an absolute triumph. It is a striking, gripping, and bitterly funny preview of a world tearing itself apart in the name of progress. Abercrombie reminds us that when the smoke clears and the machines finally start turning, the cost of a new age is always paid in blood.
