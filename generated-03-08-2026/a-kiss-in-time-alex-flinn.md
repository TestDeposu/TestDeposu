# A Kiss in Time

**Author:** Alex Flinn  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

**A Kiss in Time: Where Time Stands Still, but Love Doesn’t**

Alex Flinn’s *A Kiss in Time* is a whirlwind of romance, rebellion, and the kind of magical realism that makes you question whether you’re reading a fairy tale or a fever dream. At its heart, this novel is a love story—one that defies the very fabric of time itself. But it’s more than just a tale of two people falling for each other across centuries; it’s a story about the chaos that follows when fate, or a cursed spindle, or sheer stubbornness (depending on who you ask) throws two strangers together in a way neither could have predicted.

The premise is as irresistible as it is unconventional. Talia, a modern-day college student, stumbles upon a town frozen in time—literally. The townsfolk are comatose, and the only one awake is a stunningly beautiful woman named Jack, who turns out to be a princess from a medieval kingdom. When Talia kisses Jack—unaware of the curse or the consequences—she wakes up in the past, in Jack’s body, and with a problem: she’s now a princess in a kingdom that thinks she’s a traitor. Jack, meanwhile, wakes up in the present, in Talia’s body, and is just as confused. Their only way out? Run away together, steal a trunk full of jewels, and hope their parents don’t murder them first.

Flinn’s writing is sharp, witty, and effortlessly engaging. She has a knack for blending humor with heartache, making the absurdity of the situation feel both ridiculous and deeply relatable. The banter between Talia and Jack is electric, their chemistry sizzling off the page. Talia is the modern, sarcastic, "I just ditched my tour group for a day off" kind of heroine, while Jack is the regal, stubborn princess who’s used to getting her way—until she doesn’t. Their dynamic is a delightful mix of friction and attraction, with Flinn deftly balancing their bickering with moments of genuine vulnerability. You can’t help but root for them, even when they’re being infuriating.

But *A Kiss in Time* isn’t just a rom-com with a magical twist. It’s a story about identity, consequence, and the lengths people will go to for love. Talia and Jack are both forced out of their comfort zones—one by time travel, the other by betrayal—and neither is particularly happy about it. Yet, as they navigate their new realities, they begin to see each other in ways they never expected. There’s a tenderness in their relationship that grows alongside their frustration, a quiet understanding that they’re both lost, but together, they might just find their way.

Flinn’s world-building is clever, weaving in elements of fairy-tale lore with a modern twist. The curse, the frozen town, the royal intrigue—it all feels like something out of a classic tale, but with a contemporary edge. The humor never overshadows the stakes, and the romance never feels forced. Instead, it feels earned, a natural progression from two people who are as different as they are drawn to each other.

What makes *A Kiss in Time* truly special is how it plays with the idea of time itself. Love isn’t just about the present; it’s about the past and the future, about the choices that bind us together across eras. Talia and Jack’s journey isn’t just about escaping their problems—it’s about learning who they are when they’re not who they were supposed to be. And in that, Flinn delivers a story that’s as much about self-discovery as it is about romance.

For fans of *Beauty and the Beast* meets *The Time Traveler’s Wife*, with a dash of *Bridget Jones’s Diary* sass, *A Kiss in Time* is a love letter to the idea that sometimes, the greatest adventures come from the places—and the people—you least expect. It’s funny, it’s heartfelt, and it’s impossible to put down. If you’re looking for a story that’s equal parts enchanting and rebellious, where a single kiss can change everything, then this is the book for you.
