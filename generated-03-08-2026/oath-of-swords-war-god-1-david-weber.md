# Oath of Swords (War God, #1)

**Author:** David Weber  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

In the realm of fantasy, it's not often that we come across a protagonist as unlikely as Bahzell Bahnakson, the Horse Stealer Hradani from David Weber's 'Oath of Swords'. This first installment of the War God series is a captivating tale of an anti-hero who embodies the complexities of a world where the lines between good and evil are blurred.

Weber masterfully weaves a rich tapestry of a world where five distinct races coexist, each with their own prejudices and biases. The hradani, in particular, are shunned and feared by the others due to their propensity for uncontrolled rages and bloodthirsty tendencies. Bahzell, as a representative of this maligned race, is a refreshing change from the traditional paladin archetype. His gruff demeanor and tendency to disregard authority make him a compelling and relatable character.

Throughout the book, Weber explores the theme of prejudice and acceptance, expertly subverting the reader's expectations. Bahzell's journey is not one of redemption, but rather a nuanced exploration of his own identity and the consequences of his actions. As he navigates the complex web of politics, magic, and divine intervention, he must confront his own demons and the biases of those around him.

One of the standout features of 'Oath of Swords' is Weber's world-building. The author has crafted a meticulously detailed universe, replete with a complex history, politics, and mythology. The world of the Five Races is immersive and richly textured, with a depth that's all too rare in modern fantasy.

Weber's writing style is engaging and accessible, making 'Oath of Swords' an easy read despite its dense world-building. His use of language is evocative and descriptive, painting vivid pictures of the characters and their surroundings. The pacing is well-balanced, with a mix of action, humor, and introspection that keeps the reader invested in Bahzell's journey.

One of the most striking aspects of 'Oath of Swords' is its exploration of the nature of divinity. The War God, in particular, is a fascinating character, whose motivations and actions are shrouded in mystery. Weber raises important questions about the role of gods in shaping the world and the lives of mortals, adding a layer of depth to the narrative.

In conclusion, 'Oath of Swords' is a gripping and immersive fantasy novel that will appeal to fans of the genre. David Weber's masterful world-building, coupled with his engaging writing style, makes for a compelling read. Bahzell Bahnakson is a complex and relatable protagonist, whose journey is both captivating and thought-provoking. If you're looking for a fantasy novel that challenges your expectations and explores the complexities of the human condition, then 'Oath of Swords' is an excellent choice.
