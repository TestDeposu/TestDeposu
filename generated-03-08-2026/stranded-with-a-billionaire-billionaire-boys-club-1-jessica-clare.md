# Stranded with a Billionaire (Billionaire Boys Club, #1)

**Author:** Jessica Clare  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

Escape to Paradise with Jessica Clare’s Stranded with a Billionaire

When it comes to contemporary romance, the billionaire trope is a well-worn path, often saturated with clichés, icy corporate moguls, and predictable plotlines. Yet, every so often, an author manages to inject a fresh dose of witty banter, genuine emotional stakes, and sizzling chemistry into the formula. Published in 2013, Jessica Clare’s Stranded with a Billionaire, the inaugural installment in her wildly popular Billionaire Boys Club series, does precisely that. It takes a classic setup—enforced proximity on a tropical island—and turns it into a thoroughly engaging, laugh-out-loud, and undeniably steamy page-turner.

At its core, the novel explores the timeless theme of vulnerability versus control. Logan Hawkings is a man accustomed to pulling the strings. As a member of a secret society of ultra-wealthy men who pride themselves on absolute success, Logan’s life has recently been upended by personal tragedy and betrayal. Seeking solace on a newly acquired private island in the Bahamas, he expects peace, quiet, and isolation. Instead, he gets a hurricane, a broken elevator, and Bronte Dawson. 

Bronte is everything Logan’s high-society world is not: she is a down-to-earth Midwestern waitress with a penchant for quoting Plato and an absolute refusal to be impressed by corporate posturing. Mistaking Logan for the resort’s hotel manager rather than its billionaire owner, Bronte treats him not as a titan of industry, but as an equal. This misunderstanding forms the narrative engine of the book, shifting the theme toward the stripping away of artificial facades. Stripped of his wealth and status, Logan must win Bronte over on charm, grit, and raw chemistry alone. 

Clare’s writing style is crisp, breezy, and effortlessly readable. She has a sharp ear for dialogue, injecting humor into moments of high tension and grounding the romance in authentic emotional growth. While the plot relies on the familiar "secret identity" trope, Clare avoids the most frustrating pitfalls of the genre by ensuring that Bronte’s reaction to the eventual reveal is grounded in self-respect rather than prolonged, melodramatic angst. The pacing is swift, moving gracefully from the comedic claustrophobia of the stalled elevator to the sun-soaked, passionate nights of their island fling, and finally to the inevitable reality check when they return to civilization. 

The strength of the novel lies in the dynamic between the protagonists. Logan is wonderfully protective without crossing entirely into overbearing territory, and Bronte’s quick wit and resilience prevent her from falling into the trap of the passive romance heroine. They balance each other out, teaching one another that love requires letting go of armor and allowing oneself to be truly seen. 

Stranded with a Billionaire delivers precisely what romance readers crave: escapism, humor, and intense emotional payoff wrapped in a high-gloss package. Jessica Clare successfully lays the groundwork for her secretive society of wealthy men while telling a complete, highly satisfying standalone love story. It is a breezy, delightful indulgence that proves sometimes the best way to find yourself is to get stranded with the right person.
