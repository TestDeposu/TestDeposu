# Revolting Rhymes

**Author:** Roald Dahl  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2026

Unapologetically subversive, brilliantly wicked, and endlessly entertaining, Roald Dahl’s classic collection of verse gets a fresh lease on life with Revolting Rhymes. While the provided synopsis leans into a contemporary framing of the Cinderella tale—highlighting female independence and modern narrative twists—the true heartbeat of this timeless book lies in Dahl’s signature dark humor and his absolute refusal to pander to wholesome nursery-tale tropes.

For over half a century, the World's No. 1 Storyteller has delighted generations by dragging beloved fairy tales out of the magical forest and dragging them through the mud of reality, slapstick, and sharp-tongued satire. Revolting Rhymes is the ultimate antidote to saccharine bedtime stories, offering young readers a chance to see classic princesses, wolves, and pigs get a delightfully chaotic makeover. 

Dahl’s literary style is instantly recognizable and endlessly infectious. Written in galloping, flawless rhyming couplets, the text begs to be read aloud, rolling off the tongue with a musicality that masks its delicious maliciousness. Yet, beneath the bouncy rhythm lies a razor-sharp wit and a distinct lack of sentimentality. Dahl understood that children possess a healthy appetite for the macabre and the absurd. He never sugarcoats his narratives; instead, he sprinkles them with unexpected violence, greedy villains, and utterly unconventional happily-ever-afters. 

The central theme running through Revolting Rhymes is the deconstruction of perfection. Dahl gleefully dismantles the traditional tropes of virtue, beauty, and blind obedience. Cinderella is no longer a passive damsel waiting for a royal rescue; the wicked step-sisters are thwarted not just by magic, but by common sense and consequence; and handsome princes are rarely the noble heroes of lore. Through these comedic subversions, Dahl champions independence, skepticism, and street smarts. He teaches young readers to question authority, look past superficial charm, and rewrite their own destinies with a healthy dose of humor.

Paired with wickedly funny illustrations that capture the anarchic spirit of the verse, this book remains as fresh, biting, and splendiferous as ever. Revolting Rhymes is an absolute must-read for children who prefer their fairy tales with a generous pinch of salt, a lot of laughs, and a total lack of conventional boundaries.
