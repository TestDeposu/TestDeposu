# Elatsoe (Elatsoe, #1)

**Author:** Darcie Little Badger  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

**Elatsoe: A Haunting Masterpiece of Mystery, Magic, and Indigenous Resilience**

Darcie Little Badger’s *Elatsoe* isn’t just a book—it’s a revelation. In this electrifying debut, the author weaves together a gripping murder mystery, a love letter to Lipan Apache heritage, and a hauntingly vivid world where the dead refuse to stay buried. At its core, *Elatsoe* is a story about justice, ancestral memory, and the power of reclaiming one’s narrative, all wrapped in a mystery so rich it feels like a breath of fresh air in a genre often dominated by the same tired tropes.

The novel follows Ellie, a sharp-witted, asexual teen who can summon the spirits of dead animals—most notably her loyal ghost dog, Kirby. When her beloved cousin’s death is ruled a tragic accident, Ellie’s instincts scream otherwise. Her cousin’s ghost, trapped in the liminal space between life and death, reveals the truth: he was murdered. With the help of her eccentric family, her best friend Jay, and the spectral wisdom of her great-great-great-great-great-great grandmother, Elatsoe, Ellie sets out to uncover the killer. But in a town steeped in dark secrets and colonial violence, the line between justice and vengeance blurs dangerously.

What makes *Elatsoe* so compelling is its seamless fusion of genres. Little Badger blends noir detective work with supernatural horror, infusing the narrative with the eerie atmosphere of a small Texas town where the past isn’t just buried—it’s *haunting*. The mystery unfolds with a relentless pace, but it’s the world-building that truly shines. This isn’t just an alternate America; it’s a world where Indigenous knowledge, magic, and resilience are central to survival. The novel’s prose is lyrical yet grounded, with a voice that’s both playful and profound. Little Badger’s writing is so vivid that the reader can almost taste the spice of the ghostly meals Ellie prepares or feel the chill of the spirits that linger in the abandoned church where the mystery unfolds.

Themes of colonialism and Indigenous erasure run deep beneath the surface. The novel doesn’t shy away from the brutal history of Native displacement, but it also refuses to let that history define the present. Instead, it celebrates the strength of Ellie’s community—her family, her friends, and the spirits who guide her—as they fight back against the forces that seek to silence them. The inclusion of ancestral magic isn’t just a plot device; it’s a testament to the enduring power of Indigenous traditions, passed down through generations.

Visually, *Elatsoe* is a feast for the senses. The illustrations scattered throughout the book—created by the author herself—add another layer of depth, blending eerie linework with the story’s themes of memory and the supernatural. They’re not just decorative; they’re integral to the narrative, reinforcing the idea that the past is never truly gone.

Little Badger’s debut is also a rare gem in YA literature for its representation. Ellie is asexual, queer-coded, and unapologetically herself, and her journey is one of self-discovery as much as it is of solving a crime. The novel doesn’t just include Indigenous perspectives—it centers them, offering a story where Native voices aren’t just heard but celebrated.

In a year that already felt oversaturated with YA releases, *Elatsoe* stands out as a must-read. It’s a book that will stay with you long after the last page, its themes of justice, memory, and resilience echoing in your mind. Whether you’re a fan of mystery, horror, or simply stories that challenge and inspire, this novel is a triumph—one that deserves a place on every shelf.
