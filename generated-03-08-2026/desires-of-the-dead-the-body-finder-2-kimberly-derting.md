# Desires of the Dead (The Body Finder, #2)

**Author:** Kimberly Derting  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

In Desires of the Dead, the compelling second installment of Kimberly Derting’s acclaimed Body Finder series, the delicate boundary between normal teenage angst and terrifying supernatural reality shatters completely. Derting returns to the eerie, atmospheric world she crafted in her debut, elevating the stakes and deepening the psychological tension. Where the first novel introduced us to Violet Ambrose and her chilling, involuntary gift for sensing the dead and the echoes they leave behind, this sequel explores the heavy toll that maintaining such a secret takes on the human heart, especially when love, danger, and the FBI collide.

The central theme of Derting’s narrative is the vulnerability of trust. Violet and Jay have finally transitioned from best friends to a couple, a dynamic that Derting handles with authentic sweetness juxtaposed against suffocating dread. Romance in Violet's world is never simple; every touch, every shared glance, is haunted by the corpses she is drawn to find. Derting brilliantly uses Violet's supernatural abilities as a metaphor for teenage alienation. Just as adolescents often feel isolated by their changing bodies and hidden thoughts, Violet literally carries the weight of the dead, invisible to the peers who demand her normal attention. When a mysterious stalker begins leaving threatening notes, proving that someone is watching her every move and knows her deepest secret, that isolation turns into a claustrophobic nightmare. 

Derting’s writing style is brisk, atmospheric, and deeply immersive. She excels at pacing, seamlessly weaving high-stakes thriller elements with the grounded, emotional beats of high school life. The introduction of new characters—specifically Mike and his overly obsessed younger sister—adds a layer of small-town unease that keeps readers constantly guessing. Derting does not rely on cheap jump scares; instead, she builds suspense through sensory details. The lingering, unsettling echoes attached to the living are described with a haunting resonance that lingers long after the chapter ends. 

Furthermore, the integration of the FBI into the plot forces Violet out of her comfort zone, brilliantly shifting the narrative from a localized mystery to a high-pressure cat-and-mouse game. Violet cannot simply rely on Jay anymore; she must confront the terrifying reality that the people who can help her are the very ones she fears most. This forces character growth, pushing Violet to become a stronger, more resilient protagonist who must navigate betrayal, jealousy, and mortal peril.

Desires of the Dead is a striking evolution of the Body Finder series. It delivers a masterclass in YA romantic suspense, offering a deliciously dark plot driven by a heroine you cannot help but root for. Derting proves that the monsters hiding in plain sight are far more terrifying than the ghosts in the woods.
