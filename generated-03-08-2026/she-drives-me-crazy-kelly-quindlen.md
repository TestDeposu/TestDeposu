# She Drives Me Crazy

**Author:** Kelly Quindlen  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

High school is a battlefield of bruised egos, lingering heartbreaks, and social hierarchies that feel entirely life-or-death when you are seventeen. In She Drives Me Crazy, Kelly Quindlen captures this tumultuous landscape with a sharp wit and emotional sincerity that cements her status as a master of the contemporary teen experience. Blending the classic enemies-to-lovers trope with a modern queer romance, Quindlen delivers a laugh-out-loud rom-com that is as sweet as it is biting.

At the center of the story is Scottie Zajac, a high schooler still reeling from a devastating breakup with her ex-girlfriend. Fresh off an embarrassing opening-game loss on the basketball court, Scottie's life takes a delightfully disastrous turn when she ends up in a fender bender with her ultimate nemesis, Irene Abraham. Irene is the school’s head cheerleader—stunning, intimidating, and seemingly carved from pure ice. Thanks to the accident, Irene’s car is out of commission, leaving the two adversaries with no choice but to share a daily commute. 

What follows is the kind of forced proximity that literary gold is made of. Quindlen uses this carpool setup to strip away the armor both girls wear. Scottie is dealing with the vulnerability of being publicly dumped and a desperate desire to reclaim her dignity, while Irene is managing her own hidden pressures. When a petty opportunity arises for Scottie to make her toxic ex jealous and simultaneously climb back up the social ladder, she proposes a fake-dating scheme to Irene. Naturally, the lines between pretense and reality begin to blur almost immediately, giving readers that delicious, slow-burn tension where every accidental touch and lingering glance carries monumental weight.

Quindlen’s greatest strength lies in her effortless dialogue and deeply relatable character arcs. She avoids making her protagonists one-dimensional archetypes. Scottie’s snark is a defense mechanism masking profound insecurity, and Irene is far more complex than her cheerleader exterior suggests. The author explores the messiness of teenage love with a refreshing lack of judgment, acknowledging that figuring out who you are while trying to love someone else is an inherently bumpy ride. 

The pacing moves with the energetic bounce of a fourth-quarter basketball game, keeping readers hooked through every contrived date, emotional revelation, and witty retort. Fans of Becky Albertalli and Casey McQuiston will find a familiar warmth here, yet Quindlen carves out her own distinct voice—one that leans slightly sharper, trading purely syrupy sweetness for a realistic, grounded look at queer youth navigating high school politics.

She Drives Me Crazy is a sparkling triumph of contemporary YA. It reminds us that sometimes the person who drives you up the wall is the exact same person you want by your side when you finally reach the top. Quindlen has crafted a rom-com that is effortlessly charming, profoundly validating, and compulsively readable from the first page to the last.
