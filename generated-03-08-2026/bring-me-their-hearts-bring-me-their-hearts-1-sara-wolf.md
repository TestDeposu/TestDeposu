# Bring Me Their Hearts (Bring Me Their Hearts, #1)

**Author:** Sara Wolf  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

In the dark, twisted world of Bring Me Their Hearts, Sara Wolf masterfully weaves a tale of freedom, deception, and the true cost of one's heart. This gripping young adult fantasy follows Zera, a Heartless – an immortal, ageless soldier bound to the witch Nightsinger – as she navigates the treacherous landscape of the royal court in pursuit of her ultimate goal: freedom.

Wolf's writing style is akin to a razor-sharp blade – cutting, precise, and unapologetic. Her prose is a delicate balance of dark humor, witty banter, and poignant introspection, drawing the reader into the world of the Heartless and the witches who control them. The narrative is expertly paced, hurtling towards a crescendo of tension and suspense as Zera infiltrates the court and plays a deadly game of cat and mouse with Crown Prince Lucien d'Malvane.

At its core, Bring Me Their Hearts is a story about the human condition – or rather, the inhuman condition. Zera, as a Heartless, is a being without a heart, forced to serve her witch mistress without question or emotion. Yet, as she navigates the complexities of the royal court, Zera begins to experience the very emotions she was once devoid of. This journey of self-discovery is both captivating and heartbreaking, as Zera grapples with the true cost of her freedom.

The characters in Bring Me Their Hearts are multidimensional and expertly crafted, with even the most minor characters possessing a depth and richness that adds to the overall narrative. Prince Lucien, in particular, is a fascinating study in contrasts – a young man with the weight of the world on his shoulders, yet simultaneously carefree and reckless. His dynamic with Zera is electric, their banter and verbal sparring a highlight of the novel.

One of the most striking aspects of Bring Me Their Hearts is its exploration of the themes of power, control, and the objectification of women. Zera, as a Heartless, is a being without agency, forced to serve her witch mistress without question. Her journey towards freedom is a powerful exploration of the human desire for autonomy and self-determination. Similarly, the character of Prince Lucien serves as a commentary on the dangers of toxic masculinity and the objectification of women.

In conclusion, Bring Me Their Hearts is a gripping, thought-provoking novel that will appeal to fans of dark fantasy and young adult fiction. Sara Wolf's writing style is engaging, witty, and unapologetic, making this a must-read for anyone looking for a compelling narrative with complex characters and moral depth.
