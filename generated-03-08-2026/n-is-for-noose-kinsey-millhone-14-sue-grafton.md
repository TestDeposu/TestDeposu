# N is for Noose (Kinsey Millhone, #14)

**Author:** Sue Grafton  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

In "N is for Noose," the 14th installment of Sue Grafton's beloved Kinsey Millhone series, the author once again masterfully weaves a complex and intriguing mystery that delves into the darker corners of human nature. Published in 2010, this novel marks a turning point in the series, as Grafton skillfully explores the intricacies of relationships, the power of secrets, and the devastating consequences of uncovering the truth.

At its core, "N is for Noose" is a character-driven novel that revolves around the enigmatic and troubled Detective Tom Newquist, whose sudden and unexpected demise sets the narrative in motion. Selma Newquist, Tom's wife, is driven by a sense of determination and curiosity to uncover the truth behind her husband's death. As she navigates the complexities of small-town politics and societal expectations, Selma seeks the expertise of none other than Kinsey Millhone, the feisty and resourceful private investigator with a penchant for solving the unorthodox.

As Kinsey digs deeper into Tom's final days, she uncovers a web of secrets, half-truths, and unspoken fears that threaten to destroy the very fabric of Nota Lake's seemingly idyllic community. Grafton's keen insight into the human psyche is evident in the manner in which she crafts the townspeople's responses to Tom's death, ranging from suspicion to complicity. This masterful portrayal of human psychology is a hallmark of Grafton's writing style, which effortlessly balances wit, charm, and intellectual curiosity.

One of the defining features of the Kinsey Millhone series is its ability to blend elements of humor and drama, often in the same sentence. "N is for Noose" is no exception, as Grafton seamlessly juxtaposes Kinsey's wisecracking humor with the more somber and introspective moments. This dichotomy is a testament to the author's skill, allowing the reader to engage with Kinsey's world on multiple levels. Whether she's navigating a complex case or reflecting on the complexities of her own life, Kinsey's observations are consistently laced with a sharp wit and a deep understanding of human nature.

Furthermore, Sue Grafton's portrayal of Nota Lake as a microcosm of small-town America serves as a potent commentary on the darker aspects of human society. Through Grafton's eyes, the reader is treated to a nuanced exploration of the relationships that exist between the townspeople, often revealing a tangled web of secrets, lies, and unspoken fears. As Kinsey delves deeper into the mystery, she encounters a cast of characters whose motivations and intentions are not always as they seem.

Ultimately, "N is for Noose" is a standout entry in the Kinsey Millhone series, demonstrating once again Grafton's mastery of the detective novel genre. With its richly drawn characters, razor-sharp wit, and thought-provoking themes, this novel will keep readers enthralled from start to finish. As Kinsey navigates the treacherous world of Nota Lake, Grafton skillfully weaves a narrative that is both entertaining and intellectually stimulating.
