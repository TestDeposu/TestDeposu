# Kerri's War (The King Trilogy, #3)

**Author:** Stephen Douglass  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

**Kerri’s War: A Battle of the Heart and the Law**

Stephen Douglass’ *Kerri’s War*—the third and final installment of *The King Trilogy*—is not merely a legal drama; it is a searing exploration of defiance, love, and the crushing weight of institutional power. At its core, this novel follows Kerri, a thirty-three-year-old woman who, in a move as bold as it is reckless, takes on the IRS, a billion-dollar lawsuit, and the corporate titans who seek to destroy her. But Douglass doesn’t stop there. He weaves into this high-stakes battle a love story that is as complicated as it is compelling—one that forces Kerri to choose between vengeance and vulnerability, between the man she loves and the woman who holds his heart.

What makes *Kerri’s War* so gripping is its unflinching portrayal of the modern battlefields where women like Kerri are forced to fight. Douglass, a master of psychological tension, crafts a narrative that feels both intimate and epic. The IRS isn’t just a faceless bureaucracy here; it’s a predator, a system that preys on the desperate, the ambitious, and the vulnerable. The billion-dollar lawsuit isn’t just a legal battle—it’s a metaphor for the relentless, dehumanizing forces of capitalism that demand sacrifice, even at the cost of a person’s soul. And yet, Kerri refuses to be broken. Her war isn’t just against these forces; it’s against the fear that has kept her small for far too long.

Douglass’ prose is sharp, unapologetic, and deeply human. He doesn’t shy away from the messiness of his characters—Kerri is flawed, impulsive, and sometimes cruel, but she is also fiercely resilient. The author’s strength lies in his ability to balance high-stakes drama with raw emotional honesty. The love triangle at the heart of the story isn’t just a plot device; it’s a mirror held up to Kerri’s own internal conflict. She loves a man who is engaged to another, and in her desperation to claim him, she risks everything—her career, her reputation, even her sanity. Douglass doesn’t offer easy answers. Instead, he forces the reader to sit with the discomfort of Kerri’s choices, to question whether love can ever be worth the cost of war.

What elevates *Kerri’s War* beyond a typical legal thriller is its unrelenting focus on the human cost of ambition. Kerri isn’t fighting just for herself; she’s fighting for the right to exist in a world that has spent her entire life telling her she doesn’t deserve to take up space. The IRS, the lawsuit, the man she loves—all of them represent different forms of control, different ways the world has tried to keep her down. And yet, Douglass doesn’t romanticize her defiance. Kerri’s war is messy, exhausting, and sometimes self-destructive. She makes mistakes. She lashes out. She second-guesses herself. But in the end, her refusal to surrender is what makes her story so compelling.

The author’s style is a blend of gritty realism and emotional intensity. Douglass doesn’t pull punches, and neither should the reader. The legal battles are described with the precision of a courtroom drama, but the personal stakes are what truly drive the narrative forward. The tension between Kerri and her lover’s fiancée is handled with a delicate but unflinching hand, never descending into melodrama but always staying true to the emotional weight of the situation. By the time the final pages unfold, the reader isn’t just invested in Kerri’s victory—they’re invested in her survival, in her right to choose her own path, even if that path is paved with ruin.

*Kerri’s War* is more than a story about fighting back; it’s a story about the cost of defiance. Douglass challenges his readers to ask themselves: How far would you go to reclaim your life? How much pain are you willing to endure to prove that you deserve to exist? The answer, for Kerri, is everything. And in a world that often tells women to be quiet, to be compliant, to be small, her war is a radical act of resistance.

For fans of legal thrillers with emotional depth, for readers who love stories about women who refuse to be silenced, and for anyone who has ever felt the crushing weight of institutional power, *Kerri’s War* is a must-read. It’s a novel that doesn’t just entertain—it demands to be felt.
