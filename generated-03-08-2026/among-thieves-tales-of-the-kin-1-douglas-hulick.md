# Among Thieves (Tales of the Kin, #1)

**Author:** Douglas Hulick  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

Among Thieves, the debut novel by Douglas Hulick, is a gripping tale of intrigue, deception, and power struggles in a richly detailed fantasy world. Set in a sprawling metropolis where the underworld reigns supreme, this first installment of the Tales of the Kin series introduces readers to the complex and enigmatic Drothe, a member of the Kin, a powerful crime syndicate.

At its core, Among Thieves is a story about the blurred lines between loyalty, duty, and self-preservation. Drothe, a skilled thief and smuggler, has managed to carve out a niche for himself within the Kin, but his life is turned upside down when he acquires an ancient book with the potential to topple empires. This powerful relic, known as the "Dead Man's Book," sets off a chain reaction of events that puts Drothe at the center of a deadly game of cat and mouse.

Hulick's writing style is one of the standout features of this novel. His use of vivid descriptions and meticulous world-building creates a immersive experience, drawing readers into the dark alleys, cramped taverns, and opulent mansions of the city. The author's prose is concise, yet evocative, making it easy to visualize the characters, settings, and events that unfold.

One of the strengths of Among Thieves is its well-developed cast of characters. Drothe, in particular, is a fascinating protagonist. His narrative voice is engaging, and his motivations are multifaceted, making it easy to become invested in his fate. The supporting cast is equally well-crafted, with a range of complex and intriguing characters that add depth to the story.

The pacing of the novel is well-balanced, with a mix of action, suspense, and introspection that keeps the reader engaged. Hulick's use of plot twists and surprises adds to the tension, making it difficult to predict what will happen next.

One of the most compelling aspects of Among Thieves is its exploration of the gray areas between right and wrong. The Kin, as an organization, exists in a moral gray zone, and Drothe's own actions often walk the line between loyalty and self-interest. This moral ambiguity makes for a thought-provoking read, as the characters are forced to confront their own motivations and the consequences of their actions.

In conclusion, Among Thieves is a captivating and immersive fantasy novel that will appeal to fans of complex characters, rich world-building, and gripping storylines. With its well-developed cast, intricate plot, and thought-provoking themes, this debut novel from Douglas Hulick is a must-read for anyone looking for a compelling and engaging fantasy series.
