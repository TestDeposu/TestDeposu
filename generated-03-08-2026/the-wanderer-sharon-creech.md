# The Wanderer

**Author:** Sharon Creech  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

The Wanderer by Sharon Creech is a captivating and introspective novel that masterfully weaves together themes of self-discovery, grief, and the power of human resilience. This Newbery Honor Book tells the story of 13-year-old Sophie, who embarks on a transatlantic journey with her three uncles and two cousins aboard the sailboat, The Wanderer. As they set sail from America to England, Sophie and her cousin Cody keep travel logs, which serve as the narrative backbone of the story, offering a unique and intimate glimpse into their thoughts, feelings, and experiences.

Through Sophie's and Cody's journals, Creech skillfully explores the complexities of their inner worlds, revealing the intricacies of their relationships, fears, and aspirations. Sophie, in particular, is a quietly luminous heroine, whose voice is both authentic and relatable. Her stories of her grandfather, Bompie, are a poignant reminder of the significance of family history and the importance of connecting with one's roots.

One of the most striking aspects of The Wanderer is its thoughtful exploration of grief and survival. As the crew faces treacherous storms, equipment failure, and the unpredictable power of the Atlantic Ocean, they are forced to confront their own vulnerabilities and limitations. Creech handles these themes with sensitivity and nuance, creating a narrative that is both suspenseful and emotionally resonant.

Creech's writing style is characterized by its poetic simplicity, which adds to the novel's sense of intimacy and immediacy. Her use of language is evocative and expressive, conjuring the vast expanse of the ocean and the cramped, claustrophobic spaces of the sailboat. The alternating journal entries of Sophie and Cody create a sense of tension and suspense, as the reader is drawn into their distinct perspectives and experiences.

The Wanderer is also a powerful exploration of the human need for connection and belonging. As Sophie and Cody navigate their relationships with each other and the rest of the crew, they must confront their own sense of identity and purpose. Creech raises important questions about the nature of family, community, and the human condition, inviting readers to reflect on their own experiences and relationships.

In short, The Wanderer is a gripping and emotionally charged novel that will resonate with readers of all ages. Creech's masterful storytelling, combined with her thoughtful exploration of universal themes, makes this book a must-read for anyone who has ever felt the call of adventure or the need for self-discovery.
