# Where the Dark Stands Still

**Author:** A.B. Poranek  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2024

**Where the Dark Stands Still by A.B. Poranek: A Gothic Bargain with the Unseen**

A.B. Poranek’s *Where the Dark Stands Still* is a haunting, atmospheric descent into the heart of fear—where magic is a curse, bargains are traps, and the line between monster and victim blurs into something far more terrifying. This *New York Times* bestseller isn’t just another fantasy about defying destiny; it’s a slow-burning gothic fable about the monsters we make ourselves, the lies we tell to survive, and the cost of wanting to be ordinary in a world that demands the extraordinary.

At its core, the novel is a story about repression and its consequences. Liska, our protagonist, is a girl who has spent her life suppressing her magic—not because she wants it, but because she’s been taught that power is inherently monstrous. Her desperation to erase herself from the world’s gaze drives her into the spirit-wood, a place where the air itself hums with forgotten horrors. There, she meets The Leszy, a demon warden whose manor is a labyrinth of decaying secrets, where every shadow seems to whisper. The bargain he offers—one year of servitude for a single wish—is the kind of deal that sounds like salvation until you realize the price is always higher than you bargained for.

Poranek’s strength lies in her ability to weave dread into the very fabric of the story. The spirit-wood isn’t just a setting; it’s a character, alive with the echoes of those who came before Liska. The author’s prose is lush yet restrained, dripping with the kind of eerie beauty that makes the reader’s skin prickle. Descriptions of the forest are visceral—“the trees groaned like old men under too much weight”—while the dialogue crackles with tension, sharp enough to cut. There’s a poetic brutality to Poranek’s writing, where every line feels deliberate, where the gothic and the folkloric merge seamlessly. It’s the kind of storytelling that lingers long after the last page, like the scent of damp earth and something older, something hungry.

But *Where the Dark Stands Still* isn’t just about the beauty of its prose or the chilling atmosphere. It’s a meditation on the monsters we create when we refuse to accept ourselves. Liska’s journey isn’t about escaping her magic—it’s about learning to live with it, to see it not as a curse but as a part of her, something that defines her rather than destroys her. The Leszy, too, is a figure of tragic complexity. He’s not a villain in the traditional sense; he’s a man bound by his own past, his own failures, and the weight of the bargains he’s made. His silence isn’t just a narrative device; it’s a reflection of the isolation that comes from carrying too much guilt.

The novel’s pacing is deliberate, almost methodical, which serves the story’s themes perfectly. There’s no rushed action here; instead, Poranek lets the tension build, layer by layer, until the reader is left breathless. The slow reveal of the spirit-wood’s secrets, the creeping realization that Liska isn’t the first to make this bargain, and the growing sense that something ancient and malevolent stirs beneath the surface—all of these elements create a sense of inevitability. You know, deep down, that Liska’s choices will lead her to a place she might not want to go, but you can’t look away.

For fans of dark, lyrical fantasy, *Where the Dark Stands Still* will feel like a breath of fresh air—one that’s thick with the scent of decay and old magic. It shares DNA with *An Enchantment of Ravens* in its gothic sensibilities and *House of Salt and Sorrows* in its exploration of power and sacrifice, but Poranek’s voice is distinctly her own. She doesn’t shy away from the bleakness of her story, but she also refuses to wallow in it. There’s a quiet hopefulness beneath the surface, a belief that even in the darkest places, there’s room for redemption.

Ultimately, *Where the Dark Stands Still* is a story about the monsters we fear becoming—and the monsters we become when we refuse to face them. It’s a haunting, beautiful, and sometimes devastating read, one that will stay with you long after you’ve turned the last page. If you love fantasy that’s as much about the soul as it is about the supernatural, this is a book you won’t want to miss.
