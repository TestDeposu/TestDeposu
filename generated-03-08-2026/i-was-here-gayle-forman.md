# I Was Here

**Author:** Gayle Forman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

When the sun goes out in your universe, how do you find your way back to the light? This is the haunting question at the heart of Gayle Forman’s 2015 young adult novel, I Was Here, a poignant and unflinching exploration of grief, guilt, and the devastating aftermath of suicide. Known for her ability to pierce the emotional armor of her readers—most notably in her bestselling breakthrough, If I Stay—Forman returns with a story that trades supernatural tragedy for the stark, agonizing reality of modern heartbreak.

The narrative centers on Cody, an ordinary small-town girl whose world shatters into a million jagged pieces when her charismatic, larger-than-life best friend, Meg, deliberately drinks a bottle of industrial-strength cleaner alone in a secluded motel room. Meg was the vibrant anchor of Cody's existence, the person who made her feel seen and alive. In the wake of Meg's sudden and violent exit, Cody is left drowning in a suffocating ocean of "what ifs." How could she have missed the warning signs? How could someone so bright harbor such profound darkness?

Driven by a desperate need for closure, Cody travels to Tacoma, Washington, where Meg spent her final days. What begins as a simple quest for answers soon transforms into a harrowing journey into the shadowy fringes of her best friend's secret life. As Cody sifts through Meg’s digital footprint, unearths hidden belongings, and interacts with people Meg met online—including a charismatic yet dangerous online community centered around self-destruction—she uncovers unsettling truths. She learns that the people we think we know best are often the ones keeping the deepest secrets, and that love alone is sometimes not enough to save someone from themselves.

Forman’s authorial style is the driving force behind this emotional labyrinth. She writes with a raw, kinetic urgency that mirrors the turbulent psychology of a grieving teenager. Her prose is stripped of pretense, favoring emotional authenticity over poetic embellishment. She captures the visceral weight of survivor’s guilt—the way it manifests physically in tight chests, shallow breaths, and a pervasive numbness. Yet, despite the bleakness of the premise, Forman never lets despair swallow the narrative entirely. There is a relentless forward momentum to the storytelling, propelled by Cody’s fierce determination to honor Meg not by wallowing in her death, but by finally understanding her life.

The central theme of I Was Here is not merely the tragedy of suicide, but the agonizing bravery required to keep living after a devastating loss. Forman confronts a difficult, taboo subject with immense grace, empathy, and responsible storytelling. She explores the ripple effect of suicide, showing how it shatters families and shatters the fragile foundations of friendship. At the same time, the novel champions the redemptive power of forgiveness—both forgiving others for their hidden battles and forgiving oneself for the limits of human intervention. 

Cody’s evolution from a passive, insecure shadow of her best friend into a resilient young woman capable of standing in her own light is masterfully paced. Along the way, the introduction of nuanced supporting characters, including a deeply sympathetic love interest who aids in her investigation, adds warmth and grounding to an otherwise harrowing plot. 

I Was Here is not an easy read; it is an emotional gauntlet that will test the tear ducts of even the most stoic readers. But it is an essential one. Gayle Forman has crafted a compelling, striking cautionary tale that lingers long after the final page is turned, serving as a vital reminder to anyone struggling in the dark that their presence matters, and that they are truly seen.
