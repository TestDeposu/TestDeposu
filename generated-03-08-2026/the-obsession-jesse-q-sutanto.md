# The Obsession

**Author:** Jesse Q. Sutanto  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

The Obsession grips you from the first line and never loosens its hold. Jesse Q. Sutanto, already celebrated for the riotous Dial A for Aunties, shifts gears to deliver a razor‑thin teen thriller that feels both a cautionary tale and a relentless ride. The novel’s premise—boy meets girl, boy stalks girl, girl gets revenge—is deceptively simple, yet Sutanto layers it with urgent social commentary that transforms a familiar trope into something startlingly fresh.

At its core, the book dissects the thin line between romantic obsession and criminal stalking, especially in an era where smartphones and hidden cameras make surveillance embarrassingly easy. Logan’s self‑justifying narration (“I’m just being romantic”) is chillingly plausible, exposing how entitlement and toxic masculinity can masquerade as affection. Sutanto doesn’t let Logan off the hook; instead, she lets Delilah—sharp wit‑se a painful, insecurities, but with a steelier resolve. The dual‑perspective structure forces readers to inhabit both the predator’s delusion and the survivor’s fury, making the eventual reversal not just satisfying but morally resonant.

Stylistically, Sutanto writes with the propulsive energy of a thriller screenplay: short, punchy chapters that end on cliff‑hangers, dialogue that snaps with teenage sarcasm, and vivid, almost cinematic set‑pieces (the private‑school corridors, the dimly lit basement where Logan’s camera hums). Her prose is lean, yet she finds moments to linger on the psychological toll of abuse, weaving in threads of racism and misogyny that feel organic rather than tacked‑on. The result is a narrative that races forward while still inviting reflection—exactly the balance the Booklist and Kirkus praise.

If there’s a quibble, it’s that the novel’s breakneck pace occasionally sacrifices deeper exploration of secondary characters; some of Delilah’s allies appear more as plot devices than fully realized figures. Yet this minor shortcoming does little to dull the overall impact. The Obsession succeeds as both a gut‑punch page‑turner and a thoughtful interrogation of how love can curdle into control when power goes unchecked.

In short, Sutanto has crafted a fast‑moving, socially aware YA thriller that will keep readers hooked, unsettled, and ultimately questioning the stories we tell ourselves about desire and domination. A must‑read for fans of Karen McManus, Gretchen McNeil, and anyone who enjoys a dark‑twisted romance that bites back.
