# Les jeux sont faits

**Author:** Jean-Paul Sartre  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Les jeux sont faits, a philosophical novel by Jean-Paul Sartre, is a profound exploration of the human condition, delving into the intricacies of existence, morality, and the complexities of interpersonal relationships. Published posthumously in 2017, this novel is a testament to Sartre's enduring legacy as a master of existentialist thought.

The story revolves around Ève, a woman who has been declared dead by the authorities, and her subsequent journey to navigate a world that no longer recognizes her existence. As she grapples with the absurdity of her situation, Ève must confront the harsh realities of her past and the motivations of those around her. The novel's central theme is the concept of freedom, and how it is both empowering and crippling.

Through Sartre's characteristic philosophical lens, the novel poses fundamental questions about the nature of existence and the human condition. The author's distinctive writing style, marked by its directness and simplicity, belies the complexity of the ideas being explored. Sartre's prose is economical, yet evocative, allowing the reader to fill in the gaps and engage with the narrative on a deeper level.

One of the most striking aspects of Les jeux sont faits is its exploration of the tension between freedom and responsibility. Ève's declaration of death serves as a catalyst for her newfound freedom, yet it also raises questions about the consequences of her actions. As she navigates her new existence, Ève must confront the reality that her choices have repercussions, not just for herself, but for those around her.

Sartre's philosophical ideas are woven seamlessly into the narrative, creating a rich tapestry of existentialist thought. The novel is a scathing critique of societal norms and the ways in which individuals are constrained by the expectations of others. Through Ève's journey, Sartre highlights the absurdity of a world that values conformity over individuality.

The character of Ève is a fascinating study in contrasts. On the surface, she appears to be a victim of circumstance, yet as the novel progresses, her complexity and agency are revealed. Her relationships with others are multifaceted, and Sartre masterfully exposes the power dynamics at play in each interaction.

Les jeux sont faits is a thought-provoking and deeply unsettling novel that challenges readers to confront the complexities of human existence. Sartre's unique blend of philosophy and fiction creates a narrative that is both intellectually stimulating and emotionally resonant. This novel is a must-read for anyone interested in existentialist thought, philosophical fiction, or simply great storytelling.
