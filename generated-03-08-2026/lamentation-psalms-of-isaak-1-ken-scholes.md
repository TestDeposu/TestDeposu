# Lamentation (Psalms of Isaak, #1)

**Author:** Ken Scholes  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

In the realm of epic fantasy, few novels have managed to capture the essence of a post-apocalyptic world with the same level of depth and nuance as Ken Scholes' Lamentation, the first installment in the Psalms of Isaak series. This remarkable debut novel, published in 2009, whisks readers away to a distant future where the remnants of human civilization are struggling to survive in a world ravaged by war, faith, and the unrelenting pursuit of power.

At its core, Lamentation is a tale of devastation, loss, and the unyielding human spirit. The story unfolds in the aftermath of the destruction of the city of Windwir, a cataclysmic event that sets off a chain reaction of chaos and upheaval throughout the Named Lands. As the various kingdoms and factions vie for power and survival, Scholes masterfully weaves a complex tapestry of characters, each with their own distinct voice and motivations.

One of the most striking aspects of Lamentation is Scholes' unique writing style, which blends elements of science fiction, fantasy, and mythology to create a richly detailed world that is both familiar and yet, utterly alien. His prose is evocative and immersive, conjuring vivid images of a world that is both beautiful and brutal. The author's use of language is deliberate and measured, with a focus on conveying the emotional depth and complexity of his characters.

Through the eyes of Rudolfo, the Lord of the Nine Forest Houses, and Petronus, a young apprentice who survives the destruction of Windwir, Scholes explores the human condition in all its messy, imperfect glory. These characters, along with a cast of supporting players, must navigate the treacherous landscape of the Named Lands, confronting their own demons and making impossible choices in the face of overwhelming adversity.

Throughout the novel, Scholes raises important questions about the nature of power, faith, and the human condition. What does it mean to be a leader, to wield power and influence over others? How do we respond to catastrophic loss and trauma? What role does faith play in shaping our understanding of the world and our place within it? These are just a few of the themes that Scholes explores with nuance and sensitivity, adding depth and complexity to the narrative.

If there is a criticism to be made, it is that the pacing of the novel can be somewhat uneven at times, with certain plot threads and characters feeling slightly underdeveloped. However, this is a minor quibble in what is otherwise a masterful debut novel.

In conclusion, Lamentation is a gripping and thought-provoking epic fantasy that sets the stage for a compelling series. With its unique blend of science fiction, fantasy, and mythology, Scholes' novel is sure to appeal to fans of authors like Gene Wolfe, China Miéville, and N.K. Jemisin. If you're looking for a richly detailed and immersive reading experience, look no further than Lamentation.
