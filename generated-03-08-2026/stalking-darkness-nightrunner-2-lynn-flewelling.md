# Stalking Darkness (Nightrunner, #2)

**Author:** Lynn Flewelling  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1997

Stalking Darkness, the second volume of Lynn Flewelling’s Nightrunner saga, deepens the mythic stakes introduced in Luck in the Shadows while sharpening the emotional core of its protagonists. At its heart the novel grapples with the tension between destiny and agency: a prophecy binds Seregil, Alec, Nysander, and Micum to a quest that could either save or doom their world, yet each character constantly wrestles with how much of their fate they are willing to surrender. The ancient evil they confront—the resurrected Dead God Seriamaius—serves less as a generic monstrosity than as a mirror reflecting the cost of power, the seduction of forbidden knowledge, and the sacrifices demanded of those who guard secrets.

Flewelling’s prose remains lush yet restrained, a lyrical cadence that makes even the grimmest battlefields feel intimate. She layers world‑building with subtle gestures—a whisper of incense in a hidden chamber, the weight of a silver‑lined cup, the chill of a stone corridor—allowing readers to infer the breadth of Skala’s history without exhaustive exposition. This restraint amplifies the novel’s emotional punch; moments of quiet camaraderie between Alec and Seregil resonate as loudly as the clang of steel in the climactic rites. The author’s handling of their relationship is particularly noteworthy: their bond is portrayed with tenderness and realism, navigating jealousy, trust, and the unspoken language of lovers who have fought side by side for years. Flewelling treats their queerness as an intrinsic facet of character rather than a plot device, enriching the narrative’s texture without letting it overshadow the epic struggle.

The pacing mirrors the story’s titular darkness—steady, inexorable, with occasional flashes of sudden danger that keep the reader’s pulse aligned with the protagonists’ own. Flewelling balances intrigue and action deftly; political machinations in the citadel of Oreska are as tense as the covert races to retrieve the missing pieces of the Helm of Seriamaius. Each revelation feels earned, each setback a genuine threat to the fragile alliance forming around the prophecy.

Stalking Darkness ultimately asks what we are willing to lose for the greater good, and whether the light we seek can survive the shadows we must traverse. Flewelling answers with a story that is both sweeping in scope and intimately human, marking a confident step forward in a series that blends classic high‑fantasy tropes with fresh, emotionally resonant storytelling.
