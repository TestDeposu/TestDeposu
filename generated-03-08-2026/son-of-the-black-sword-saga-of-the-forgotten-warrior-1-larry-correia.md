# Son of the Black Sword (Saga of the Forgotten Warrior, #1)

**Author:** Larry Correia  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

In the world of fantasy, where gods and monsters roam, it's not often you come across a tale that masterfully blends epic scope with personal struggle. Larry Correia's Son of the Black Sword is just that, a gripping narrative that plunges readers into a realm of demons, magic, and politics. This is a book about the blurred lines between good and evil, a story that challenges its protagonist to redefine his place in a world built on strict moral codes.

At the heart of Son of the Black Sword lies Ashok Vadal, a skilled Protector and the embodiment of this rigid moral code. He's a ruthless enforcer tasked with eliminating those who dare to challenge the Order of Law. But when Ashok discovers his true identity and the lies that underpin his existence, the very foundations of his world begin to crumble. This is not just a story about a character's awakening, but about the disintegration of an entire society built on the back of fear and dogma.

Correia's world-building skills are on full display, as he weaves a rich tapestry of mythology and history. The author's vision of a post-apocalyptic realm, where gods and demons have been cast out, is detailed and immersive. The Age of Law, a period where humans cling to the remnants of their faith, is a fascinating commentary on our own world's struggles with extremism and intolerance.

But what truly sets Son of the Black Sword apart is Correia's ability to craft compelling, complex characters. Ashok's transformation from a zealous enforcer to a questioning hero is both believable and heart-wrenching. The supporting cast is equally well-developed, with Correia taking pains to humanize even the most villainous of characters.

Correia's writing style, often described as 'hard science fiction meets fantasy', provides a refreshing take on the genre. His attention to detail and commitment to realism infuse his world with a sense of authenticity that's all too rare in modern fantasy. The action scenes are fast-paced and thrilling, while the quieter moments are both poignant and emotionally resonant.

If there's a criticism to be made, it's that Correia's penchant for detail sometimes leads to an overly dense narrative. Fans of more streamlined storytelling may find themselves getting lost in the wealth of information provided. However, this is a minor quibble in an otherwise engaging and thought-provoking tale.

Ultimately, Son of the Black Sword is a triumph of modern fantasy. Correia's masterful world-building and complex characters elevate this already thrilling narrative into something truly special. If you're a fan of action-packed adventures, thought-provoking storytelling, or just great world-building, then this is a must-read.
