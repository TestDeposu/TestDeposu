# King of Thorns (Broken Empire, #2)

**Author:** Mark Lawrence  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

Mark Lawrence returns with King of Thorns, the ferocious second installment of his acclaimed Broken Empire trilogy, and proves that his brand of grimdark fantasy is here to stay. Picking up where Prince of Thorns left off, Lawrence tightens the noose around his protagonist, Jorg Ancrath, transforming the narrative from a brutal cross-country vendetta into a claustrophobic, high-stakes chess match of survival, kingship, and psychological torment. At the heart of the novel lies a striking and uncomfortable theme: the agonizing price of power and the inevitability of one's past. Jorg has secured a crown through torture, blood, and treachery, yet Lawrence strips away any romantic notions of victory. At eighteen, Jorg is not a triumphant conqueror; he is a haunted survivor trapped in the very castle he stole, tormented by the ghosts of his atrocities and the crushing weight of a throne that threatens to swallow him whole. 

Lawrence’s narrative style remains his greatest weapon. Written in a piercing first-person perspective, the prose is sharp, poetic, and unapologetically visceral. Lawrence employs a dual-timeline structure, alternating between the harrowing present—where an overwhelming enemy force marches relentlessly toward Jorg’s stronghold—and the formative road journey of the past that forged the king he has become. This structural choice elevates the tension, allowing the author to dissect Jorg's psyche layer by layer. Jorg is an antihero for the ages, a sociopathic monster capable of unspeakable cruelty who simultaneously possesses a razor-sharp intellect, a twisted moral code, and a devastating self-awareness. Lawrence refuses to redeem Jorg too easily, forcing the reader to walk a tightrope between revulsion and mesmerizing fascination. 

Furthermore, Lawrence continues to enrich his decaying world—our own Earth, long fallen into a post-apocalyptic dark age—by weaving remnants of "lost" science into the medieval tapestry. As an encroaching army threatens annihilation, Jorg discovers a cache of ancient artifacts, blurring the line between forgotten technology and dark magic. This blending of genres adds a layer of dread and unpredictability, reminding us that the horrors of the past are never truly buried. King of Thorns is a masterclass in modern dark fantasy: relentless, beautifully written, and emotionally bruising. Lawrence does not just tell a story of war; he holds a mirror to the darkest corners of human ambition and asks what remains of a soul once the crown is finally won.
