# Streams of Silver (Forgotten Realms: The Icewind Dale, #2; Legend of Drizzt, #5)

**Author:** R.A. Salvatore  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

R.A. Salvatore’s Streams of Silver, the fifth installment in the sprawling Legend of Drizzt series and the second chapter of the foundational Icewind Dale Trilogy, captures the beating heart of what makes epic fantasy enduring. While fantasy literature often drowns in endless wars and political maneuvering, Salvatore anchors his narrative in something rarer and infinitely more resonant: the profound, unbreakable strength of found family. 

At the center of the novel is Bruenor Battlehammer’s obsessive, lifelong dream to reclaim Mithral Hall, the legendary lost dwarven stronghold wrested from his people long ago by a shadow dragon. But this is not merely a tale of treasure and territory; it is an emotional crucible. Alongside Bruenor stand the iconic companions of the hall: Wulfgar the barbarian, Regis the halfling, and Drizzt Do'Urden, the dark elf whose internal battle against the dark heritage of his people remains one of the genre’s most compelling psychological portraits. 

Salvatore’s writing style is a masterclass in pacing and visceral action. He balances grand, sweeping world-building with intimate, character-driven moments. His prose moves with the kinetic energy of a well-rolled Dungeons and Dragons encounter, yet it never sacrifices emotional depth for spectacle. The perils the companions face—ranging from the nightmarish expanse of the Trollmoors to the stinging, systemic prejudice directed at Drizzt wherever he goes—are designed to test not just their physical endurance, but their faith in one another. Drizzt’s internal struggle, haunted by the whispers of the Underdark calling him back to the abyss, provides a poignant thematic counterweight to Bruenor’s roaring ambition. 

The primary theme of Streams of Silver is belonging. Each member of this unlikely fellowship is an outcast in their own right, displaced by history, culture, or circumstance. Yet, through shared peril and fierce loyalty, they forge an identity together that transcends the loneliness of their origins. Salvatore illustrates that home is not merely a place like Mithral Hall waiting to be excavated from the stone, but the people who stand shoulder-to-shoulder with you against the dark.

For both longtime veterans of Forgotten Realms lore and newcomers looking for high-stakes fantasy rooted in genuine camaraderie, Streams of Silver is a triumphant, gripping read. It proves that even in a world filled with monsters and magic, the most powerful force is the unwavering bond of friendship.
