# When Christ and His Saints Slept (Plantagenets #1; Henry II and Eleanor of Aquitaine, #1)

**Author:** Sharon Kay Penman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

History is rarely a neat arrangement of heroes and villains; more often, it is a chaotic tapestry woven from ambition, loyalty, betrayal, and the heavy burdens of inheritance. In her magnificent historical novel When Christ and His Saints Slept, master storyteller Sharon Kay Penman plunges readers directly into the brutal, tumultuous heart of twelfth-century England. Serving as the explosive first installment of her acclaimed Plantagenet trilogy centering on the legendary Henry II and Eleanor of Aquitaine, this novel brilliantly resurrects an era that the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle famously described with haunting despair: a time when Christ and his saints slept.

The narrative ignites in the wake of a single, devastating event: the tragic death of King Henry I’s legitimate sons in the infamous White Ship disaster. This maritime catastrophe leaves the English crown without a clear, undisputed male heir, triggering a bitter, decades-long civil war known to history as the Anarchy. On one side stands Maude—Henry’s proud, intelligent, and fiercely determined daughter, who was promised the throne yet finds herself fighting a patriarchal society reluctant to bow to a queen. On the other side stands Stephen of Blois, a charming and affable man who seizes the crown in a moment of opportunistic vulnerability, plunging the realm into fire, famine, and lawless ruin. 

Penman’s central theme explores the corrupting nature of power and the tragic paradox of human morality. As the Publishers Weekly starred review astutely observes, perhaps the most impressive feature of the narrative is the author’s rare skill in showing how essentially good, well-meaning people can end up committing great evil. The Anarchy was not a simple struggle between absolute right and absolute wrong; it was a devastating meat grinder where loyalties were tested, compromises curdled into cruelty, and survival demanded unthinkable moral concessions. Penman refuses to sanitize her characters. Instead, she presents them in all their complex, contradictory glory—flawed human beings desperately trying to navigate a world plunging into darkness.

Stylistically, Penman sets a gold standard for the historical fiction genre. Her prose is rich, immersive, and remarkably disciplined, balancing sweeping geopolitical machinations with deeply intimate, visceral human moments. Unlike authors who rely on cheap narrative gimmicks or anachronistic sensibilities to make the Middle Ages digestible, Penman respects her readers enough to steep them in the authentic textures of the period. Every castle wall, muddy encampment, blood-soaked battlefield, and whispered court intrigue feels palpably real. Her meticulous historical research is worn lightly, serving as the sturdy scaffolding for a narrative driven primarily by emotional truth and psychological depth.

Moreover, Penman excels at pacing an epic tale. Even as the political landscape shifts and the fortunes of war swing violently between Maude and Stephen, the human pulse of the story never falter. We feel the biting cold of winter sieges, the agonizing grief of parents sacrificing their children to the altar of dynastic ambition, and the fragile glimmer of hope for a lasting peace. 

When Christ and His Saints Slept is far more than a dry recounting of medieval battles and royal lineages. It is a vibrant, breathing epic that honors the complexity of the past while speaking directly to the enduring frailties of the human heart. For fans of immersive historical fiction, intricate political drama, and characters who linger in the mind long after the final page is turned, Penman’s masterwork is an absolute triumph that demands to be read.
