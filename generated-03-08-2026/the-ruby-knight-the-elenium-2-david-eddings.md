# The Ruby Knight (The Elenium #2)

**Author:** David Eddings  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1990

In "The Ruby Knight," the second installment of David Eddings' epic fantasy series, "The Elenium," the masterful author weaves a rich tapestry of magic, politics, and adventure, transporting readers to a world of wonder and awe. Published in 1990, this novel continues the journey of Sir Sparhawk, a Pandion Knight, and his companions as they navigate the treacherous landscape of a medieval-inspired world.

At its core, "The Ruby Knight" is a story about the struggle between good and evil, with the fate of the world hanging precariously in the balance. Eddings masterfully explores the complexities of human nature, revealing the gray areas that exist between absolute good and absolute evil. Through the characters' experiences, the author shows that even the most well-intentioned individuals can be driven to questionable actions by circumstances and their own flaws.

One of the most striking aspects of Eddings' writing style is his ability to create a sense of depth and history in his world-building. The world of Eosia, where the story takes place, is meticulously crafted, with a detailed geography, politics, and mythology that immerse the reader in the narrative. The author's use of descriptive language is vivid and evocative, painting a picture of a world that is both familiar and yet, utterly unique.

Eddings' characters are equally well-crafted, with complex motivations and backstories that add depth to the narrative. Sir Sparhawk, the protagonist, is a particularly compelling character, whose dry wit and sarcasm provide a welcome respite from the gravity of the story. The supporting cast is equally well-developed, with characters like Sephrenia, the Styric sorceress, and Kurik, Sparhawk's squire, adding richness to the story.

The pacing of the novel is well-balanced, with a mix of action, suspense, and introspection that keeps the reader engaged. Eddings' writing is engaging and accessible, making the story easy to follow, even for readers who are new to the series.

In conclusion, "The Ruby Knight" is a captivating and engaging read that will appeal to fans of epic fantasy and adventure. David Eddings' masterful storytelling, coupled with his vivid world-building and complex characters, makes this novel a must-read for anyone who enjoys a good story. With its themes of good vs. evil, friendship, and the struggle for power, this book is a compelling addition to any fantasy reader's library.
