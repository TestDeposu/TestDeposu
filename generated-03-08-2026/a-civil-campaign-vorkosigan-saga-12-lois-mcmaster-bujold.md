# A Civil Campaign (Vorkosigan Saga, #12)

**Author:** Lois McMaster Bujold  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

Lois McMaster Bujold has long been celebrated as one of speculative fiction’s most brilliant architects, but with A Civil Campaign, the twelfth installment of her beloved Vorkosigan Saga, she achieves something truly rare. Eschewing the traditional space opera blueprint of galactic wars and laser-fire, Bujold crafts a masterclass in the comedy of manners set against a richly realized far-future backdrop. As *The Chicago Sun Times* aptly notes, Bujold possesses a nearly unique gift in science fiction for this specific brand of social satire, and here, that gift is on absolute, glittering display. 

The narrative centers on the recently widowed Ekaterin Vorsoisson, who returns to her conservative home planet of Barrayar only to find herself besieged by a squadron of prospective suitors. Chief among them is Miles Vorkosigan, whose intelligence, wit, and military renown are matched only by his utterly catastrophic romantic ineptitude. What follows is a multi-front war of the heart, where Miles’s earnest yet disastrous courtship efforts collide with high-society politics, sibling turmoil—as Miles’s brother Mark faces his own parental roadblocks in love—and the venomous maneuvers of ambitious Vor aristocrats looking to exploit the chaos. 

Yet, to categorize Aischylos-level mishaps simply as a rom-com in space would be to overlook the profound emotional intelligence that anchors the entire endeavor. As *SF Site* observes, Bujold is not just a master of plot; she is a master of emotion. Beneath the sparkling banter, biting wit, and high hilarity that earned the novel well-deserved Hugo and Nebula finalist honors, the book delves into deeply resonant themes of grief, personal reinvention, and the daunting prospect of second chances. Bujold handles her characters with a tender psychological realism, ensuring that the stakes feel intensely personal even as the political backdrop threatens to implode.

Stylistically, this is Bujold at the absolute peak of her powers. Her prose sings with effortless grace, marrying the grand scope of world-building with the intimate intricacies of human interaction. She takes the basic space operatics of a futuristic feudal society and breathes such vibrant, idiosyncratic life into it that Barrayar feels as tactile and lived-in as any historical drama. 

Ultimately, A Civil Campaign stands as a towering achievement in contemporary speculative fiction. It proves, as *Library Journal* suggests, what marvels genius can create when liberated from formulaic boundaries. Whether you are a devoted veteran of the Vorkosigan Saga or a newcomer stepping into Bujold’s universe for the first time, this novel is an utterly rewarding, laugh-out-loud, and deeply moving triumph.
