# This One Summer

**Author:** Mariko Tamaki  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

In the heart-wrenching and profoundly relatable graphic novel 'This One Summer', Mariko Tamaki and Jillian Tamaki, the renowned cousins known for their work in 'Skim', masterfully weave together the tangled threads of adolescence, self-discovery, and the blurred lines between childhood and adulthood. Published in 2014, 'This One Summer' has captivated readers and critics alike, earning the coveted titles of New York Times bestseller, Caldecott Honor Book, and Eisner Award Winner.

The narrative unfurls during a pivotal summer in the lives of two young protagonists, Rose and her inseparable friend Windy. Their idyllic summer escapade to Awago Beach, a secluded haven from the stresses of everyday life, takes an unexpected turn as Rose's parents' increasingly turbulent relationship threatens to upend their tranquility. The fragile web of friendships, secrets, and long-held emotions is expertly woven by Tamaki, laying bare the turmoil that erupts when seemingly impermeable walls are breached.

One of the standout aspects of 'This One Summer' is its nuanced portrayal of Rose, a character grappling with the turmoil of her parents' marital strife and her own nascent sense of identity. As Tamaki skillfully guides the reader through Rose's world, we witness the character's gradual awakening to the harsh realities of life and her role within it. This beautifully encapsulates the essence of growing up – a poignant and often painful process of self-discovery, as one navigates through the intricacies of relationships, friendships, and personal growth.

Throughout the narrative, Tamaki employs her distinctive visual style to convey the emotional depth and complexity often lost in traditional prose literature. Jillian Tamaki's illustrations are a masterclass in emotive storytelling, rendering a world that is at once idyllic and brutal, serene and terrifying. Her bold lines, expressive characters, and masterful use of color imbue the narrative with a profound emotional resonance.

The themes of 'This One Summer' are equally impressive in their thoughtfulness and subtlety. Tamaki tackles topics such as identity, family, first love, and the fragility of life with a sensitivity and candor that resonates deeply. The story is not about grand, sweeping resolutions or tidy conclusions, but rather about the jagged edges and messy, human realities that make living so precious.

By refusing to shy away from the darker aspects of life, Tamaki imbues 'This One Summer' with a profound sense of authenticity, one that is all the more impressive for being presented in the seemingly innocent guise of a graphic novel. The result is a narrative that is both heartbreaking and hopeful, a story that will linger in your mind long after the final page has turned.

In short, 'This One Summer' is a triumph – a rich, poignant, and deeply moving portrayal of girlhood and the trials of growing up. Tamaki's writing is both evocative and unsparing, capturing the intensity of adolescent emotion while refusing to shy away from the complexity and beauty of life itself.
