# Are We Smart Enough to Know How Smart Animals Are?

**Author:** Frans de Waal  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

In "Are We Smart Enough to Know How Smart Animals Are?", renowned primatologist Frans de Waal takes readers on a fascinating journey through the complex and often overlooked world of animal cognition. With meticulous research and engaging storytelling, de Waal reveals the extraordinary abilities of animals like crows, dolphins, and octopuses, challenging our long-held assumptions about their intelligence and behavior.

Throughout the book, de Waal masterfully weaves together anecdotes from his own research, as well as contributions from other leading scientists, to paint a rich tapestry of animal intelligence. He shows us how animals navigate their environments, use tools, and even exhibit cultural behaviors, highlighting the striking similarities between their cognitive abilities and our own.

One of the book's greatest strengths is de Waal's ability to balance scientific rigor with accessibility. His writing is clear, concise, and devoid of jargon, making this book an ideal read for anyone interested in animal behavior and cognition, regardless of their scientific background. At the same time, de Waal never shies away from delving into the complex scientific concepts underpinning his arguments, providing a depth of understanding that will satisfy even the most experienced researchers.

De Waal's central argument – that we have consistently underestimated the intelligence of animals – is well-supported by a wealth of evidence from various fields, including primatology, ethology, and comparative psychology. He cites examples like the use of coconut shells by octopuses as tools, the classification of humans by elephants, and the exceptional memory of a young male chimpanzee at Kyoto University. These fascinating anecdotes serve as a powerful reminder of the complexities and surprises that await us in the animal kingdom.

One of the most compelling aspects of de Waal's book is its ability to challenge our assumptions about the human-nature dichotomy. By highlighting the remarkable cognitive abilities of animals, de Waal forces us to confront the possibility that our own intelligence may not be as unique as we have traditionally believed. This idea is both exhilarating and humbling, encouraging us to reevaluate our place within the natural world and to cultivate a greater appreciation for the intricate social and cognitive lives of animals.

Throughout the book, de Waal's passion and conviction shine through, infusing his writing with a sense of wonder and a commitment to uncovering the secrets of animal intelligence. His love of animals and his dedication to scientific inquiry are palpable, making "Are We Smart Enough to Know How Smart Animals Are?" a compelling and thought-provoking read.

In conclusion, "Are We Smart Enough to Know How Smart Animals Are?" is a triumph of scientific storytelling, one that will appeal to animal lovers, scientists, and anyone interested in rethinking the boundaries between humans and animals. De Waal's landmark work is a must-read for anyone seeking to expand their understanding of the complexities and wonders of the animal kingdom.
