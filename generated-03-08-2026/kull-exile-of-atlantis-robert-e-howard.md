# Kull: Exile of Atlantis

**Author:** Robert E. Howard  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Kull: Exile of Atlantis by Robert E. Howard is a behemoth of a book, a sprawling collection of stories that showcase the author's mastery of the sword and sorcery genre. But what sets this book apart is not just the sheer volume of content, but the way it contextualizes Howard's work within the broader landscape of his life and career.

The introduction to this collection is a masterclass in literary analysis, providing a detailed and insightful look at the evolution of Howard's writing style and the development of the Hyborian Age, the fictional world in which many of his stories are set. It's clear that the editor has done their research, drawing on a deep understanding of Howard's life and work to provide a nuanced and engaging introduction.

But what of the stories themselves? Howard's writing is, as ever, a joy to read. His prose is muscular and evocative, conjuring up vivid images of ancient civilizations and mythological creatures. His characters are equally well-drawn, from the titular Kull to the more famous Conan, each one a fully realized and complex individual with their own motivations and desires.

One of the most striking things about Howard's writing is his ability to blend historical fiction with fantasy elements. His stories are set in a pre-historical period, but they're not simply fantasy tales - they're grounded in a deep understanding of history and mythology. This blend of fact and fiction creates a rich and immersive world that's a joy to explore.

Of course, no discussion of Howard's work would be complete without mentioning his most famous creation: Conan. The Cimmerian warrior is a cultural icon, and it's easy to see why. He's a complex and multifaceted character, driven by a combination of noble and base motivations. He's a hero, but he's also a product of his time and place - a time of war and violence, and a place of myth and legend.

Throughout the book, Howard's writing is marked by a sense of energy and vitality. His stories are fast-paced and action-packed, with a focus on adventure and excitement. But they're also thoughtful and introspective, exploring themes of morality and mortality in a way that's both nuanced and profound.

If there's a flaw to the book, it's that the sheer volume of content can be overwhelming. With over 800 pages to contend with, it's a significant commitment to read the entire thing from cover to cover. But the individual stories are relatively short, and they can be easily read and enjoyed in isolation.

In short, Kull: Exile of Atlantis is a must-read for fans of sword and sorcery fiction. It's a collection of stories that showcase the best of Robert E. Howard's writing, and provide a fascinating glimpse into the development of the Hyborian Age. Whether you're a longtime fan of Conan or just discovering Howard's work for the first time, this book is sure to delight and entertain.
