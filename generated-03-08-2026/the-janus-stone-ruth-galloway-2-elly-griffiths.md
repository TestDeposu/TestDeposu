# The Janus Stone (Ruth Galloway, #2)

**Author:** Elly Griffiths  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

The Janus Stone, the second installment in Elly Griffiths' Ruth Galloway series, is a masterfully woven archaeological mystery that delves into the darker aspects of human nature. The story begins with the discovery of a child's remains at a demolition site in Norwich, a former children's home, and forensic archaeologist Ruth Galloway is called in to examine the bones. As Ruth and DCI Harry Nelson delve deeper into the case, they uncover a haunting cold case from forty years earlier, when two children went missing from the home.

Griffiths' writing style is characterized by her ability to craft a sense of atmosphere and tension, drawing the reader into the world of her characters. The setting of the old country manor, with its secrets and mysteries, is expertly woven into the narrative, creating a sense of foreboding and unease. The author's use of descriptive language adds to the overall ambiance, making it easy to visualize the scenes and become fully immersed in the story.

One of the standout aspects of The Janus Stone is its exploration of the theme of identity and how it is shaped by our past experiences. Ruth Galloway, the protagonist, is a complex and multi-faceted character, whose own identity is closely tied to her work as a forensic archaeologist. As she navigates the case, she is forced to confront her own demons and the fears that have been lurking beneath the surface. The title of the book, The Janus Stone, refers to the Roman god Janus, who had two faces, one looking forward and one backward. This symbolism is reflected in the story, as Ruth and Nelson are forced to look back into the past to uncover the truth, while also facing the dangers that are lurking in the present.

The author's handling of the mystery elements is also noteworthy. The plot is cleverly constructed, with a number of twists and turns that keep the reader guessing until the very end. The pacing is well-balanced, with a steady flow of revelations and discoveries that maintain the tension and suspense. The supporting characters, including DCI Nelson and Father Hennessey, are well-developed and add depth to the story.

As the story unfolds, Ruth becomes the killer's next target, and the stakes are raised. The sense of danger and urgency is palpable, and the reader is left wondering if Ruth and Nelson will be able to uncover the truth before it's too late. The author's ability to create a sense of tension and suspense is impressive, making The Janus Stone a compelling and unputdownable read.

In conclusion, The Janus Stone is a gripping and atmospheric mystery that explores the darker aspects of human nature. Elly Griffiths' writing style is characterized by her ability to craft a sense of atmosphere and tension, and her handling of the mystery elements is expertly done. The story is a must-read for fans of archaeological mysteries and those who enjoy a good whodunit. With its complex characters, clever plot, and well-balanced pacing, The Janus Stone is a compelling and unputdownable read.
