# Call It Sleep

**Author:** Henry Roth  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

**A Timeless Portrait of Immigration and Coming of Age: "Call It Sleep" by Henry Roth**

In this poignant and beautifully crafted novel, Henry Roth weaves an unforgettable tale of David Schearl, a Jewish immigrant child growing up in the slums of New York's Lower East Side. Published in 1934, **Call It Sleep** has long been regarded as a masterpiece of American literature, offering a searing portrayal of the immigrant experience and the struggles of childhood.

Roth's masterful writing transports readers to the early 20th-century world of poverty, overcrowding, and cultural dislocation, yet the novel remains as relevant today as it was upon its initial release. With an uncanny eye for detail, Roth brings to life the sights, sounds, and emotions of David's tumultuous life, as the young protagonist navigates the treacherous landscape of his family's apartment building, streets, and churches.

At the heart of the novel is David's desperate struggle to assert his identity within a family that seems intent on stifling his imagination and individuality. His father, a stern and authoritarian figure, embodies the conflicting values of tradition and modernity, while his mother, a fragile and long-suffering woman, is torn between her loyalty to her husband and her daughter, who embodies the American Dream they so desperately seek to realize.

Through David's eyes, Roth masterfully conveys the dislocation and disorientation of the immigrant experience, as the young protagonist grapples with the conflicting demands of family, culture, and society. His inner world is a rich tapestry of myth, legend, and fantasy, drawn from the stories of his grandmother, the Bible, and the urban streets that serve as his playground.

Roth's prose is a marvel of lyrical simplicity and poetic power, conjuring the sensory experiences of David's world with a precision and beauty that is nothing short of breathtaking. His writing is a perfect blend of the concrete and the abstract, capturing the harsh realities of poverty and immigration while also conveying the dreamlike quality of David's inner life.

In many ways, **Call It Sleep** is a companion piece to Joyce's **Ulysses**, sharing a similar ambition to capture the inner lives of its characters through a fluid, impressionistic narrative. While Roth's novel lacks the modernist experimentation and stream-of-consciousness technique of Joyce's masterpiece, it shares a similar commitment to plumbing the depths of the human experience.

The result is a novel that is both timeless and utterly of its time, a work of literary art that transcends its historical context to speak to universal themes of identity, culture, and the immigrant experience. As **The New York Times** so aptly observed, **Call It Sleep** is a novel of profound distinction, one that confirms Henry Roth's place as one of the leading writers of his generation.

In short, **Call It Sleep** is a novel that should be read and reread, savored and treasured for its beauty, its intelligence, and its unflinching portrayal of the human experience. It is a masterpiece of American literature that will continue to captivate and move readers for generations to come.
