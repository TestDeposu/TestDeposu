# Rich People Problems (Crazy Rich Asians, #3)

**Author:** Kevin Kwan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Greed, Glamour, and the Grand Finale: A Review of Kevin Kwan’s Rich People Problems

With Rich People Problems, Kevin Kwan triumphantly brings his internationally bestselling trilogy to a dazzling, uproarious, and deeply satisfying conclusion. Following the runaway cultural phenomena *Crazy Rich Asians* and *China Rich Girlfriend*, this third installment delivers everything that made the series a global sensation while offering a surprisingly poignant resolution to the sprawling saga of Asia’s most privileged dynasties. If the previous novels were about the dizzying heights of modern wealth and the shock of old money meeting new, *Rich People Problems* is a masterclass in inheritance, family politics, and the inescapable gravity of blood.

The narrative kicks into high gear when the matriarch of the Shang-Young clan, the formidable Su Yi, takes a turn for the worse. As rumors of her impending demise ripple across the globe, the entire family converges on Tyersall Park—a legendary, sprawling estate sitting on 64 prime acres in the heart of Singapore. What should be a solemn vigil quickly devolves into a theater of absurdity. Greed, paranoia, and decades-old resentments take center stage as relatives circle the dying matriarch like glittering vultures, all desperate to stake their claim on her astronomical fortune and the iconic trophy estate. 

Kwan uses this central premise to explore the novel’s defining theme: the corrupting and consuming nature of immense wealth. While the title tongue-in-cheekily reduces the characters' struggles to trivialities, Kwan demonstrates how money complicates every human emotion, turning romance, grief, and familial love into high-stakes transactions. Yet, beneath the razor-sharp satire, there is a genuine exploration of legacy. As the family tears itself apart over heirlooms and real estate, the novel asks what truly survives a lifetime of accumulation. 

Beyond the main estate drama, Kwan masterfully juggles a dizzying array of subplots featuring the beloved ensemble cast. Fan favorite Astrid Leong finds herself navigating a treacherous storm of romance and scandal, torn between her enduring love for Charlie Wu and the calculated sabotage of her vindictive ex-husband. Meanwhile, the irrepressible social climber Kitty Pong—now married to one of China’s richest billionaires—continues her relentless crusade for elite social validation, only to find herself hilariously upstaged by her impossibly chic stepdaughter, Colette Bing. 

Kwan’s authorial style remains his greatest asset. His prose is effervescent, wickedly funny, and dripping with sensory overload. He treats the reader to a whirlwind tour of global excess, transporting us seamlessly from the opulent, art-filled mansions of Manila to secluded private islands in the Sulu Sea, from exclusive elite schools in Hong Kong to breathtaking palaces in India. True to form, Kwan peppers his narrative with footnotes that explain designer labels, historical lineages, and cultural minutiae, functioning as an affectionate, tongue-in-cheek guide to the 0.01 percent. This encyclopedic obsession with luxury is not merely superficial; it creates a fully realized, hyper-vivid world that feels entirely authentic to its absurd reality.

What elevates *Rich People Problems* above a standard comedic romp is Kwan’s underlying affection for his characters. Even the most vapid and conniving socialites are rendered with a wink and a degree of humanity that keeps the reader rooting for them. By the time the final wills are read and the dust settles at Tyersall Park, Kwan successfully balances the biting satire with genuine emotional resonance. 

Ultimately, *Rich People Problems* is a pure, unadulterated pleasure to read. It is a laugh-out-loud glitz fest that delivers a masterclass in pacing, wit, and escapism. Kwan bids farewell to the Crazy Rich Asians universe with a magnificent fireworks display of drama, romance, and laugh-out-loud comedy that will leave fans both thoroughly entertained and yearning for just one more glimpse into this fabulously decadent world.
