# The Edge of Always (The Edge of Never, #2)

**Author:** J.A. Redmerski  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

**A Love That Defies the Edge: The Raw, Relentless Heart of *The Edge of Always***

J.A. Redmerski’s *The Edge of Always* isn’t just the sequel to *The Edge of Never*—it’s a searing, unflinching exploration of love as both salvation and survival. Where the first book introduced us to Camryn Bennett, a woman adrift in the numbness of loss, this one forces us to witness the brutal, beautiful struggle to reclaim what was nearly lost. Redmerski doesn’t just write about love; she dissects it—its fragility, its stubborn resilience, and the ways it can either heal or destroy when tested by grief. The result is a novel that pulses with emotional intensity, where every page feels like a lifeline thrown to someone drowning in their own pain.

At its core, *The Edge of Always* is a story about the love that refuses to let go. Andrew Parrish, the charismatic, fiercely devoted soul from the first book, is no longer content with waiting for Camryn to heal on her own terms. When he discovers she’s drowning in self-destructive habits—numbing the ache of her past with dangerous distractions—he doesn’t just accept it. He *fights* for her. And in doing so, Redmerski crafts one of the most compelling portrayals of love as active, relentless, and sometimes even reckless. Andrew’s determination isn’t just romantic; it’s desperate, a testament to the kind of love that refuses to surrender, even when the other person is determined to walk away.

What makes this book so gripping is Redmerski’s refusal to romanticize suffering. Camryn isn’t a passive victim; she’s a woman who has built walls around herself, and Andrew’s challenge isn’t just to break them down but to convince her that healing isn’t weakness—it’s survival. The tension between their two paths—Camryn’s retreat into self-destruction and Andrew’s desperate push toward hope—creates a narrative that’s as tense as it is tender. Redmerski doesn’t shy away from the messiness of recovery; she lays bare the moments of relapse, the arguments, the silent treatments, and the quiet victories. It’s raw, unfiltered, and deeply human.

The author’s prose is a masterclass in sensory storytelling. Redmerski doesn’t just describe emotions; she *immerses* the reader in them. The way she renders Camryn’s numbness—her hollow laughter, her avoidance of touch, the way she numbs herself with alcohol or reckless decisions—is visceral, making the reader feel the weight of her despair as if it were their own. And when Andrew’s love finally begins to pierce through, the scenes of their rekindled connection are electric, charged with a passion that feels earned, not forced. Redmerski’s ability to balance emotional depth with palpable tension is what makes this book so hard to put down.

But *The Edge of Always* isn’t just about love’s triumph—it’s also a meditation on the cost of survival. Camryn’s journey isn’t linear; there are setbacks, moments of self-sabotage, and the haunting realization that some wounds never fully close. Redmerski doesn’t offer easy answers. Instead, she presents love as a verb—a daily choice, a fight, a willingness to show up even when the other person isn’t ready. Andrew’s love isn’t passive; it’s a force that refuses to let Camryn slip away, even when she’s determined to disappear into the past.

The book’s strength lies in its authenticity. Camryn isn’t a one-dimensional victim, and Andrew isn’t a saint. They’re flawed, stubborn, and deeply human, which makes their love story feel real. Redmerski’s writing is lush but never indulgent; her dialogue crackles with chemistry, and her pacing keeps the story moving at a breakneck speed, even when the emotional stakes are highest. The result is a novel that’s as addictive as it is heartbreaking—a love story that doesn’t just endure the edge but *defies* it.

For readers who loved the slow-burn passion and emotional depth of *The Edge of Never*, *The Edge of Always* delivers even more. It’s a story about the love that persists when everything else falls apart, about the courage it takes to face your demons, and about the quiet, stubborn hope that can emerge from the ashes of loss. Redmerski doesn’t just write about love; she *recreates* it on the page, making it tangible, painful, and ultimately, impossible to resist.

If you’re looking for a love story that’s as fierce as it is tender, as desperate as it is hopeful, then *The Edge of Always* is for you. It’s a book that will leave you breathless, that will make you ache for Camryn and Andrew, and that will remind you that sometimes, the greatest love stories aren’t about the beginning—they’re about the fight to keep going.
