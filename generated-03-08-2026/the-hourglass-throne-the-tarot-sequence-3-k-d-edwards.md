# The Hourglass Throne (The Tarot Sequence, #3)

**Author:** K.D. Edwards  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

Urban fantasy often struggles to balance the relentless adrenaline of high-stakes action with the nuanced, slower-burning machinery of political intrigue. Yet, with The Hourglass Throne, the explosive third installment of The Tarot Sequence, author K.D. Edwards achieves this difficult equilibrium with breathtaking swagger and emotional gravity. Serving as the climactic capstone to the first major arc of a planned nine-book epic, this novel cements Edwards’s place as a formidable voice in contemporary speculative fiction, delivering a story where time, trauma, and loyalty collide within the neon-drenched, mythic sprawl of New Atlantis.

The narrative immediately hooks the reader with a chilling, locked-room mystery: a formidable, impenetrable magical barrier seals off the city’s renowned rejuvenation center, trapping dozens inside. For Rune Saint John, the scarred and sharp-tongued last scion of the Sun Throne, investigating this anomaly alongside his towering, steadfast bodyguard Brand is supposed to be a standard duty. Instead, it spirals into a harrowing confrontation with a threat both devastatingly new and deeply ancient. Edwards uses this central investigation not merely as a vehicle for suspense, but as a crucible for his protagonist’s ongoing psychological evolution.

At the heart of The Hourglass Throne lies a resonant, unflinching exploration of trauma and found family. Edwards has never shied away from the brutal history of his protagonist; Rune is a survivor of unspeakable torture, a man whose body and mind bear the permanent scars of a fallen court. In this novel, the past is quite literally weaponized against him. As the investigation forces Rune to revisit the darkest hours of his youth, the book brilliantly tackles the theme of how memory and time shape identity. The barrier around the immortality clinic becomes a potent metaphor for the walls survivors build around themselves, and the agonizing, necessary work of tearing them down. 

Yet, for all its emotional weight, the book never loses its signature pulse. Edwards’s authorial style remains a masterclass in modern urban fantasy pacing. His prose is slick, fiercely clever, and laced with a distinctive, mordant wit that serves as both armor and relief in the face of overwhelming darkness. The banter between Rune, Brand, and Addam Saint Nicholas—Rune’s lover and political equal—crackles with authentic warmth and devotion. This found family is the emotional anchor of the entire Tarot Sequence, and watching them navigate the treacherous, backstabbing waters of New Atlantis’s high court provides a delicious contrast to the grim supernatural threats looming outside.

As Rune formally steps into his power as ruler, the political machinations intensify. Edwards crafts a courtly landscape where every alliance is a calculated risk and romance is inextricably tied to statecraft. The deepening relationship between Rune and Addam is handled with admirable nuance, showcasing two powerful men carving out a space for tenderness amid the ruthless demands of leadership. 

Ultimately, The Hourglass Throne succeeds because it refuses to offer easy resolutions. It honors the pain of its characters while driving them relentlessly forward toward a destiny that will alter New Atlantis forever. Edwards delivers a climax that is emotionally resonant, visually stunning, and fiercely satisfying, closing the first chapter of this sprawling saga with an unforgettable bang. For fans of intricate world-building, high-octane magic, and protagonists who bleed for those they love, this book is an absolute triumph.
