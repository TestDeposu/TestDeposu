# Sabotaged (The Missing, #3)

**Author:** Margaret Peterson Haddix  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

Time is a fragile construct, and in the hands of Margaret Peterson Haddix, it is also a ticking time bomb. With Sabotaged, the third installment in her gripping The Missing series, Haddix proves once again why she is a master of speculative historical fiction for young readers. Building upon the intricate mythology established in Found and Sent, this 2010 novel takes the time-traveling exploits of Jonah and Katherine and thrusts them into a mystery that is darker, more disorienting, and infinitely more dangerous than anything they have faced before. 

The premise hinges on one of history's most enduring and eerie enigmas: the Lost Colony of Roanoke. Andrea, the latest missing child whom Jonah and Katherine are summoned to save, is actually Virginia Dare, the first English child born in the Americas. In typical Haddix fashion, the history we thought we knew is just a cover story for a much larger, time-spanning conspiracy. However, what makes Sabotaged stand out as a narrative achievement is not just the destination, but the disastrous detour. When the time-travel mechanism malfunctions—or rather, when it is deliberately hijacked—Jonah and Katherine find themselves stranded in the wrong era, dealing with a stubborn girl who actively refuses to be rescued. 

Haddix’s greatest strength as an author lies in her ability to weave high-stakes science fiction seamlessly with tangible, grounded human emotion. Her style is characterized by relentless pacing and crisp, accessible prose that wastes no time on superfluous world-building, instead dropping the reader directly into the psychological panic of her characters. In Sabotaged, she elevates the tension by introducing a palpable sense of paranoia. The realization that their mission has been sabotaged transforms the narrative from a straightforward rescue operation into a tense whodunit. Who can they trust? Is someone manipulating history from the inside, or are they merely pawns in a game played by unseen, omnipotent architects of time?

The central theme of Sabotaged explores the heavy burden of identity versus destiny. Virginia Dare’s reluctance to return to the future forces Jonah and Katherine—and by extension, the reader—to question the ethics of their mission. Is it right to rip a child away from the only life and family she has ever known, simply because a history book dictates she belongs elsewhere? Haddix refuses to offer easy answers. She challenges her youthful protagonists to mature rapidly, realizing that tampering with time is not a grand adventure, but a moral labyrinth where every choice carries catastrophic consequences. 

The character dynamics continue to be the beating heart of the series. Jonah provides the cautious, moral compass, while Katherine supplies the necessary irreverence, wit, and impulsive bravery to keep the plot moving. Their banter provides much-needed levity against the bleak, foreboding backdrop of a hostile past that seems actively conspiring against them. 

Sabotaged is a masterclass in middle-grade and young adult pacing. Haddix keeps the pages turning with cliffhangers that feel earned rather than cheap, culminating in a revelation that recontextualizes everything we thought we knew about the missing children of history. It is a smart, thought-provoking, and relentlessly thrilling addition to a series that refuses to talk down to its audience. For fans of time-travel puzzles and moral dilemmas, this installment is an absolute triumph that leaves you desperately turning the page to see what era Haddix will fracture next.
