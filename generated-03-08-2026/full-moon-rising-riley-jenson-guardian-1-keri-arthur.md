# Full Moon Rising (Riley Jenson Guardian, #1)

**Author:** Keri Arthur  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2006

Keri Arthur’s 2006 debut, Full Moon Rising, arrives like a jolt of adrenaline to the urban fantasy genre, blending high-stakes criminal investigation with an uninhibited, visceral exploration of desire. As the opening installment of the Riley Jenson Guardian series, this novel wastes no time establishing a gritty, neon-lit underworld where myth and modernity collide, and where the line between predator and protector is dangerously blurred. 

At the heart of the narrative is Riley Jenson, a protagonist who immediately shatters the passive tropes often found in early 2000s supernatural fiction. As a rare hybrid—half-vampire and half-werewolf—Riley is biologically caught between two worlds, a condition that Arthur uses to explore the central theme of identity and duality. Riley does not merely exist within her dual nature; she is constantly negotiating the conflicting instincts of her heritage. Her vampiric side grants her predatory grace and resilience, while her werewolf blood ties her intimately to the cyclical demands of nature, most notably the consuming frenzy of the moon heat. 

Arthur’s signature style is defined by this relentless pacing and a fearless commitment to sensuality. The author does not shy away from the carnal aspects of her characters' natures; instead, sexuality is treated as an intrinsic force—a source of both vulnerability and immense power. When Riley’s twin brother, Rhoan, goes missing, the narrative gains a desperate ticking-clock momentum that forces Riley out from behind her desk at the Directorate of Other Races and onto the mean streets of Melbourne. This descent into the criminal underworld pairs the urgency of a classic noir detective thriller with the heightened sensory experiences characteristic of paranormal romance. 

What makes Full Moon Rising striking is how Arthur weaves the intimate, visceral struggles of her protagonist with a larger, chilling conspiracy. Beneath the pulsating nightlife and the intoxicating encounters with dangerously alluring figures like the enigmatic vampire Kellen, a dark science is at play. The plot expands into ethical territory as Riley uncovers a sinister plot involving genetic cloning and the harvesting of nonhuman genomes to manufacture the ultimate warrior. This elevates the novel from a standard monster-hunter tale into a sharp commentary on commodification, bodily autonomy, and the dangerous pursuit of perfection. 

Arthur’s prose is punchy, atmospheric, and unapologetically bold. She crafts a Melbourne that feels alive, sensory-drenched, and thoroughly perilous. Through Riley, Arthur champions a fierce, flawed, and resilient heroine who refuses to be pigeonholed by society’s expectations or her own biological imperatives. Full Moon Rising is a thrilling, genre-bending triumph that sets a high bar for urban fantasy, offering readers a darkly seductive world where survival demands embracing every ounce of one's inner power.
