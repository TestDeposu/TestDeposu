# Through the Zombie Glass (White Rabbit Chronicles, #2)

**Author:** Gena Showalter  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

In "Through the Zombie Glass," Gena Showalter weaves a captivating narrative that delves into the darker aspects of human nature, love, and the struggle for survival in a world overrun by the undead. This second installment in The White Rabbit Chronicles is a masterful blend of action, romance, and fantasy, further solidifying Showalter's reputation as a skilled storyteller.

At the heart of the novel lies Alice Bell, a strong-willed and determined protagonist who has lost so much in her battle against the zombies. Despite her tough exterior, Alice is vulnerable and relatable, her emotions and fears palpable as she navigates the treacherous landscape of her new reality. As she faces the darkness growing within her, Alice must confront the ultimate question: will she succumb to the evil that threatens to consume her, or will she find a way to overcome it?

Showalter's writing style is engaging and immersive, with a keen focus on character development and relationships. The bond between Alice and her best friend, Kat, is particularly noteworthy, their friendship a beacon of hope in a world gone mad. The romance between Alice and Cole Holland, the enigmatic leader of the zombie slayers, is equally compelling, their love strong enough to transcend even the darkest of circumstances.

One of the most striking aspects of "Through the Zombie Glass" is its exploration of the human condition. Showalter raises important questions about the nature of good and evil, encouraging readers to ponder the gray areas that exist between. As Alice struggles to maintain her grip on reality, she must confront the darkness within herself, a powerful metaphor for the internal battles we all face.

The world-building in the novel is equally impressive, Showalter's vision of a zombie-infested world both terrifying and believable. The addition of supernatural elements, including the mysterious mirrors and whispers of the dead, adds a layer of complexity to the narrative, further enriching the reading experience.

If there is one criticism to be made, it is that the pacing of the novel can be somewhat uneven at times. Certain plot threads feel slightly rushed, while others are more leisurely in their development. However, this is a minor quibble in an otherwise engaging and suspenseful narrative.

In conclusion, "Through the Zombie Glass" is a gripping and emotionally charged novel that will appeal to fans of urban fantasy, romance, and horror. With its strong protagonist, engaging characters, and thought-provoking themes, this book is sure to leave readers eagerly anticipating the next installment in The White Rabbit Chronicles.
