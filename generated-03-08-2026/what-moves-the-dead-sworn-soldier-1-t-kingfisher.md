# What Moves the Dead (Sworn Soldier, #1)

**Author:** T. Kingfisher  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

What Moves the Dead, the latest offering from award-winning author T. Kingfisher, is a masterful reimagining of Edgar Allan Poe's classic tale, "The Fall of the House of Usher". This gripping and atmospheric novel is a must-read for fans of horror, mystery, and suspense. Kingfisher's unique style weaves a complex and unsettling narrative that explores the darker aspects of human nature and the natural world.

The story follows Alex Easton, a retired soldier, as they return to the ancestral home of their childhood friend Madeline Usher, who is dying under mysterious circumstances. Upon arrival, Alex finds the house and its surroundings shrouded in an aura of decay and malevolence. The once-grand estate is now overrun with fungal growths, and the local wildlife seems to be possessed by some dark force. Madeline's condition is equally disturbing, as she sleepwalks and speaks in strange voices at night, while her brother Roderick is consumed by a mysterious nervous disorder.

As Alex delves deeper into the mystery, they are aided by a British mycologist and an American doctor, who bring their expertise to bear on the strange occurrences. Together, they unravel a dark and sinister plot that threatens to consume them all. Kingfisher's writing is evocative and immersive, conjuring a sense of foreboding and dread that permeates every aspect of the story. The author's use of language is deliberate and measured, creating a sense of tension that builds slowly but inexorably.

One of the standout features of What Moves the Dead is Kingfisher's ability to balance atmosphere and plot. The novel is both a slow-burning thriller and a thought-provoking exploration of the human condition. The author's use of the fungal growths and possessed wildlife creates a sense of unease and uncertainty, mirroring the decay and corruption that lies at the heart of the Usher family. At the same time, the novel raises important questions about the nature of trauma, grief, and the power of the past to shape our present.

Kingfisher's style is characterized by a deep sense of empathy and understanding for her characters. Alex, the protagonist, is a complex and nuanced figure, whose military background and personal history inform their actions and motivations. The supporting cast is equally well-drawn, with each character bringing their own unique perspective and expertise to the mystery. The author's use of dialogue is natural and effortless, creating a sense of authenticity that draws the reader into the world of the story.

What Moves the Dead is a testament to Kingfisher's skill and versatility as a writer. The novel is both a loving homage to Poe's original tale and a bold reimagining that adds new layers of depth and complexity. The result is a story that is both familiar and innovative, with a unique blend of horror, mystery, and suspense that will appeal to fans of the genre.

In short, What Moves the Dead is a must-read for anyone who loves a good horror story, a complex mystery, or a thought-provoking exploration of the human condition. Kingfisher's masterful writing and atmospheric setting create a sense of tension and foreboding that will keep you on the edge of your seat until the very end. With its unique blend of suspense, horror, and mystery, this novel is sure to leave a lasting impression on readers.
