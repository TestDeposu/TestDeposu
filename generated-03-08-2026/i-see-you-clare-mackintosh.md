# I See You

**Author:** Clare Mackintosh  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

I See You, the gripping thriller by Clare Mackintosh, is a masterfully crafted tale that delves into the darkest corners of everyday life, where the familiar and mundane can quickly turn sinister. The story follows Zoe Walker, a ordinary woman with a routine that is turned upside down when she discovers her own face in a classified ad, accompanied by a phone number and a website called FindTheOne.com. As Zoe delves deeper into the mystery, she realizes that other women who have appeared in the same ad have become victims of violent crimes, including murder.

Mackintosh's writing style is characterized by her ability to create a sense of claustrophobia and unease, drawing the reader into Zoe's world and making them feel like they are experiencing her fear and paranoia firsthand. The author's use of descriptive language and vivid imagery adds to the tension, making it impossible to look away from the unfolding events. The pace of the book is well-balanced, with a steady stream of revelations and plot twists that keep the reader guessing until the very end.

One of the most striking aspects of I See You is its exploration of the theme of surveillance and the blurring of boundaries between public and private spaces. In today's world, where technology has made it easier than ever to track and monitor people's movements, Mackintosh's book serves as a timely reminder of the dangers of complacency and the importance of being aware of one's surroundings. The author's portrayal of Zoe's growing paranoia and desperation is both haunting and thought-provoking, raising important questions about the consequences of living in a society where personal freedom is constantly being eroded.

Mackintosh's characters are well-developed and complex, with Zoe being a particularly relatable and sympathetic protagonist. The supporting cast, including the determined cop who helps Zoe uncover the truth, adds depth and nuance to the story, making it feel more realistic and immersive. The author's ability to create a sense of unease and uncertainty among the characters is impressive, making it difficult for the reader to distinguish between friend and foe.

The book's use of the classified ad as a plot device is a clever touch, highlighting the ways in which technology can be used to manipulate and exploit people. The website FindTheOne.com is a chilling creation, serving as a symbol of the darker aspects of human nature and the dangers of online anonymity. As the story unfolds, it becomes clear that nothing is as it seems, and the truth behind the ads is far more sinister than Zoe could have ever imagined.

Overall, I See You is a gripping and unsettling thriller that will keep readers on the edge of their seats. Mackintosh's writing is engaging and suspenseful, making it impossible to put the book down. The themes of surveillance, paranoia, and the blurring of boundaries are timely and thought-provoking, adding depth and complexity to the story. If you're a fan of psychological thrillers, I See You is a must-read.
