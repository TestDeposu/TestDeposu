# In Other Lands

**Author:** Sarah Rees Brennan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

In Other Lands throws open the gates to a realm where the ordinary collides with the fantastical, inviting readers to wander alongside Elliot—a thirteen‑year‑old whose sharp tongue and keen intellect make him both endearing and exasperating. When a field trip reveals a shimmering wall that no one else can see, Elliot steps through and discovers a boarding school unlike any he’s imagined: lessons in swordplay replace algebra, elven warriors stride the halls, and mermaids glide in nearby lagoons. Yet the true magic of Sarah Rees Brennan’s novel lies not in its spectacular setting but in the way it uses that setting to explore the universal ache of finding where you belong.

At its heart, the book is a coming‑of‑age tale wrapped in witty banter and earnest friendship. Elliot’s voice crackles with sarcasm and self‑awareness, a hallmark of Brennan’s style that keeps the narrative buoyant even when the stakes rise. His interactions with Serene‑Heart‑in‑the‑Chaos‑of‑Battle—a striking elven warrior whose full name is as formidable as her skill—and Luke, the sunny, blond human who somehow manages to be both irritating and irresistibly likable, form a trio that feels both fresh and familiar. Their camaraderie is tested by cultural misunderstandings, budding romance, and the looming pressure of diplomacy between humans and the Borderlands’ myriad inhabitants. Through these relationships, Brennan deftly tackles themes of identity, loyalty, and the courage required to redefine oneself when the world insists on a single label.

Brennan’s prose is crisp and conversational, peppered with humor that never undercuts the emotional core of the story. She excels at showing rather than telling: the Borderlands feel lived‑in because we experience them through Elliot’s eyes—the scent of enchanted forests, the clang of practice blades, the sudden, startling beauty of a mermaid’s song. The world‑building is economical yet vivid, allowing the plot to sprint forward without sacrificing depth. Chapters are punctuated by Casey Nowak’s illustrations, which add a visual whimsy that mirrors the text’s playful tone.

If there is a minor quibble, it lies in the pacing of the middle section, where the focus shifts occasionally to world‑exposition that can slow the momentum. Yet even these moments serve a purpose, grounding the fantastical elements in a coherent sociopolitical framework that makes the eventual payoff—Elliot’s realization that he can influence change without sacrificing his sense of self—feel earned and resonant.

In Other Lands is a vibrant reminder that adventure isn’t only about battling monsters or mastering spells; it’s also about the quiet battles we fight to understand who we are and whom we choose to walk beside. Brennan delivers a story that is as heartfelt as it is hilarious, as thought‑provoking as it is entertaining—a perfect pick for readers who crave fantasy with a sharp, contemporary edge.
