# Ask the Passengers

**Author:** A.S. King  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

In A.S. King's thought-provoking and poignant novel "Ask the Passengers," the author masterfully weaves a narrative that challenges readers to confront the complexities of societal expectations and the fragility of human connections. Astrid Jones, the protagonist, finds herself lost in a world where her identity is defined by the people around her, and she's desperate to break free from the suffocating constraints of her family, friends, and the society she lives in.

The novel begins with Astrid's confidants being the people she's never met, the passengers who soar above her in airplanes. It's a fascinating premise that sets the tone for the book – one that's both whimsical and poignant. As Astrid navigates her complicated relationships, her desire to be understood and accepted by the people around her becomes increasingly urgent. However, her attempts to open up to those closest to her are met with disappointment and rejection, forcing her to find solace in the anonymity of the airplane passengers.

King's writing is as lyrical as it is evocative, painting a world that's as poignant as it is relatable. Astrid's voice is raw and unflinching, offering readers a glimpse into the inner turmoil of a teenager struggling to come to terms with her identity. With each passing page, the stakes rise, and Astrid's world becomes increasingly complex, forcing her to confront the harsh realities of the world around her.

One of the most striking aspects of "Ask the Passengers" is its exploration of the theme of loneliness. Astrid's desperation to connect with others is palpable, and it's in these moments that the novel shines. King's portrayal of Astrid's interactions with the airplane passengers is a poignant reminder that even the smallest connections can have a profound impact on our lives. These exchanges, though brief and seemingly insignificant, serve as a lifeline for Astrid, offering her a sense of hope and belonging.

Throughout the novel, King raises important questions about identity, societal expectations, and the fragility of human connections. As Astrid's relationships with her family, friends, and the people she meets on the plane become increasingly complicated, it's clear that she's struggling to find her place in the world. King's portrayal of Astrid's journey is both heartbreaking and inspiring, urging readers to re-examine their own relationships and connections with others.

Ultimately, "Ask the Passengers" is a novel about the power of human connections, no matter how tenuous they may seem. Astrid's story is a testament to the idea that even in the darkest of times, there's always hope to be found in the smallest of gestures. King's writing is a masterclass in subtlety, never explicitly stating the complexities of Astrid's emotions but instead allowing readers to infer and interpret her experiences for themselves.

In the end, "Ask the Passengers" is a novel that will leave you breathless, questioning your own relationships and connections with others. It's a powerful exploration of the human experience, one that's as poignant as it is relatable. If you're looking for a novel that will challenge your perceptions and leave you thinking long after you've finished reading, then "Ask the Passengers" is the perfect choice.
