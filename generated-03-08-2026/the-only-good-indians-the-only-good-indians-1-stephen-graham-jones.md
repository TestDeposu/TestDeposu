# The Only Good Indians (The Only Good Indians, #1)

**Author:** Stephen Graham Jones  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

"The Only Good Indians", the latest offering from Stephen Graham Jones, is a masterclass in modern horror. A sweeping narrative that defies the conventions of the genre, Jones weaves a complex tale of revenge, identity, and the fraught relationship between tradition and progress.

At its core, "The Only Good Indians" is a story about the long shadow of colonialism. The novel follows four American Indian men, each with their own unique struggles and triumphs, as they navigate the treacherous landscape of their own culture and the expectations placed upon them by their communities. Theirs is a world where the ghosts of the past linger, where the land itself seems to remember the atrocities committed against their ancestors, and where the traditional ways of living are pitted against the modern world.

Jones's prose is a work of art. His writing is evocative, precise, and unflinching, capturing the subtleties of human emotion and the nuances of the natural world. His descriptions of the desert landscapes are breathtaking, conjuring images of endless expanses of sand and rock that stretch out to the horizon like an unyielding expanse of pain. His characters are multidimensional, each with their own distinct voice and perspective, and their struggles are both deeply personal and universally relatable.

But what truly sets "The Only Good Indians" apart is its fearless examination of the American Indian experience. Jones tackles topics such as cultural erasure, colonialism, and the ongoing trauma inflicted upon Indigenous communities with unflinching honesty and sensitivity. His is a narrative that refuses to shy away from the brutal truths of history, instead embracing them as a necessary catalyst for healing and reconciliation.

Through his characters, Jones illuminates the tensions that arise when tradition and progress collide. The novel's themes of identity and belonging are particularly striking, as the characters grapple with the weight of their ancestors' legacies and the expectations placed upon them by their communities. Jones's portrayal of these internal struggles is raw and unflinching, yet also deeply compassionate, acknowledging the humanity and vulnerability that lies at the heart of each character's experience.

One of the most striking aspects of "The Only Good Indians" is its masterful blend of psychological horror and sociopolitical commentary. Jones's use of the supernatural is both subtle and unnerving, hinting at the darker forces that lurk beneath the surface of the world. As the novel hurtles towards its devastating conclusion, the boundaries between the natural and supernatural worlds begin to blur, plunging the characters (and the reader) into a world of primal terror.

In "The Only Good Indians", Jones has crafted a work of art that is both deeply disturbing and profoundly beautiful. It is a novel that will leave you changed, haunted by the images and themes that linger long after the final page is turned. For anyone interested in exploring the complexities of the American Indian experience, this is a book that you will not want to miss.
