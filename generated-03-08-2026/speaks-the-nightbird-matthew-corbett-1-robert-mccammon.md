# Speaks the Nightbird (Matthew Corbett, #1)

**Author:** Robert McCammon  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

Speaks the Nightbird, the first installment in Robert McCammon's Matthew Corbett series, is a masterful blend of historical mystery, suspense, and social commentary that delves into the dark recesses of human nature. Set in the late 17th century, the novel whisks readers away to the fledgling settlement of Fount Royal in the Carolinas, where fear, superstition, and hysteria reign supreme.

At the heart of the story lies Rachel Howarth, a young widow accused of witchcraft, murder, and various other nefarious crimes. As Matthew Corbett, a young clerk to the traveling magistrate, becomes embroiled in her case, he begins to unravel a tangled web of deceit, corruption, and small-town politics. McCammon skillfully crafts a narrative that not only critiques the witch hunts of the time but also explores the destructive power of unchecked fear and the devastating consequences of blindly following the crowd.

One of the standout aspects of Speaks the Nightbird is its thoughtful and nuanced portrayal of the era. McCammon's meticulous research and attention to detail transport readers to a bygone age, immersing them in the sights, sounds, and sensibilities of a long-forgotten world. From the squalid conditions of the gaol to the fervent religiosity of the townsfolk, every aspect of the novel feels meticulously crafted to evoke a sense of authenticity.

The author's writing style is equally impressive, weaving together elements of mystery, thriller, and historical fiction to create a narrative that is both propulsive and thought-provoking. McCammon's prose is evocative and atmospheric, conjuring the sense of foreboding and unease that pervades the settlement. His characters, too, are multidimensional and richly drawn, with Matthew Corbett proving a particularly compelling protagonist. His skepticism and curiosity drive the investigation forward, even as he grapples with the moral complexities of the case.

As the story unfolds, McCammon expertly ratchets up the tension, hurtling readers toward a climax that is both shocking and haunting. The author's exploration of the human condition is both unflinching and compassionate, laying bare the darker aspects of human nature while also celebrating the resilience and courage of those who dare to challenge the status quo.

In short, Speaks the Nightbird is a gripping and thought-provoking historical mystery that will appeal to fans of authors like Stephen King and Laura Purcell. With its richly detailed setting, complex characters, and suspenseful plot, this novel is a must-read for anyone who enjoys a compelling and immersive story.
