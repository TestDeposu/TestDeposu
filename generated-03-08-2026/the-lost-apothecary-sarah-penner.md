# The Lost Apothecary

**Author:** Sarah Penner  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

**The Lost Apothecary: A Poisonous Love Letter to Female Revenge and Forgotten History**

Sarah Penner’s *The Lost Apothecary* is a novel that seethes with the quiet fury of women silenced by history—one that weaves together the suffocating oppression of 18th-century London with the haunting echoes of its past, all through the lens of poison, power, and the unbreakable bonds of sisterhood. This is not just a historical thriller; it’s a defiant reclamation of stories that were meant to be buried.

At its core, the book is a dual narrative that collides two timelines with the precision of a well-aimed dagger. In 1760s London, Nella, a clandestine apothecary, supplies women with poisons to exact vengeance against the men who control them—husbands, fathers, employers—those who wield authority with impunity. Her shop is a sanctuary, a secret network where whispers of justice replace the screams of the silenced. But when a reckless 12-year-old girl’s mistake sparks a chain of murders, Nella’s world unravels, forcing her to confront the cost of her defiance. The prose here is sharp, immersive, and steeped in the stifling atmosphere of Georgian England, where the air itself feels thick with the weight of unspoken rebellion. Penner’s descriptions of the apothecary’s hidden world—its alchemical recipes, the hushed negotiations, the tension between survival and morality—are so vivid you can almost smell the bitter herbs and feel the dread coiled in the shadows.

Then comes Caroline Parcewell, a modern-day historian drowning in the hollow anniversary of her failed marriage, who stumbles upon the unsolved murders tied to Nella’s apothecary. What follows is a chilling investigation that blurs the line between past and present, as Caroline’s own demons mirror those of the women who came before her. Penner’s strength lies in her ability to make history feel visceral, to show how the sins of the past are never truly buried. The novel’s suspense is relentless, but it’s never just about the chase—it’s about the women who refuse to be victims. Whether it’s Nella’s quiet ruthlessness or Caroline’s reluctant reckoning, *The Lost Apothecary* is a celebration of female agency, even in the face of systemic cruelty.

Penner’s writing style is a masterclass in atmospheric tension. She doesn’t rely on gore or melodrama; instead, she lets the tension simmer in the details—the way a woman’s hands tremble as she measures a dose, the way a modern-day detective’s fingers brush against an old ledger, the way the city itself seems to hold its breath. Her dialogue crackles with wit and urgency, especially in the scenes between Caroline and the enigmatic figures she encounters. And while the plot is intricate, Penner never loses sight of the emotional core: the loneliness of women fighting alone, the fragile alliances that form in the dark, and the way vengeance—whether justified or not—can be both a curse and a liberation.

What makes *The Lost Apothecary* truly unforgettable is its unapologetic feminism. This isn’t a story where women are passive; they are architects of their own fate, even when the world tries to erase them. Nella’s apothecary isn’t just a place to buy poison—it’s a symbol of resistance, a testament to the idea that survival often requires defiance. And Caroline’s journey, though more hesitant, is equally powerful. Penner doesn’t romanticize revenge; she shows its cost, its moral ambiguity, and the way it can bind generations together in cycles of pain and resilience.

The novel’s pacing is impeccable, balancing the slow burn of historical intrigue with the urgency of a modern-day mystery. The reveal of the apothecary’s fate is as shocking as it is inevitable, and the way the past and present intertwine feels like a masterstroke. By the final pages, you won’t just be turning to the next book in Penner’s catalog—you’ll be thinking about the women who never got their stories told, and the ones who still fight to keep theirs alive.

*The Lost Apothecary* is more than a thriller; it’s a love letter to the women who have ever needed to be fierce, even when the world told them to be quiet. Penner’s debut is a triumph—bold, haunting, and impossible to forget.
