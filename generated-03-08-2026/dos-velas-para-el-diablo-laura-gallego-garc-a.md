# Dos velas para el diablo

**Author:** Laura Gallego García  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

**A Spark of Fire in the Shadows: *Dos velas para el diablo* by Laura Gallego García**

Laura Gallego García’s *Dos velas para el diablo* (2010) is a novel that burns with the intensity of a candle in a storm—equal parts mythic grandeur and raw, emotional turmoil. Set against the eternal conflict between angels and demons, this is not merely a tale of vengeance, but a meditation on sacrifice, the weight of legacy, and the blurred line between justice and obsession. With her signature lyrical prose and deft storytelling, Gallego weaves a narrative that feels both timeless and urgently modern, where the stakes are as high as the heavens themselves.

At its core, *Dos velas para el diablo* follows Cat, a young woman whose life is shattered by the death of her father—a man who, unbeknownst to her, was caught in the crossfire of an ancient war. Now, thrust into a role she never sought, Cat must navigate a world where divine and infernal forces collide. Her mission is clear: vengeance. But vengeance, as Gallego explores, is a double-edged sword. It promises power but demands a price, and Cat’s journey becomes a descent into the moral ambiguities of her quest. Is she fighting for her father’s honor, or is she being consumed by the very darkness she seeks to destroy?

Gallego’s strength lies in her ability to balance grand cosmic themes with deeply human emotions. The novel’s setting—an otherworldly realm where angels and demons wage war—feels richly imagined, yet the emotional core remains grounded in Cat’s struggle. Her grief, her fear, and her growing realization that vengeance may not bring peace but only more chaos are portrayed with a raw honesty that makes the reader root for her, even as she stumbles toward destruction. The author’s writing is evocative, painting vivid scenes of celestial battles and earthly despair with equal mastery. Her descriptions of the supernatural world are both awe-inspiring and unsettling, while her portrayal of Cat’s internal conflict is achingly relatable.

What makes *Dos velas para el diablo* particularly compelling is its exploration of family and duty. Cat’s relationship with her father is central to the story, and Gallego uses it to examine how love and loss shape our choices. The novel asks: How far would you go for the ones you love? And at what cost? The answer is never simple, and Gallego refuses to offer easy resolutions. Instead, she leaves the reader with the unsettling truth that some battles cannot be won without becoming what you once hated.

Stylistically, Gallego’s prose is a delight—a blend of poetic beauty and gripping narrative tension. She has a knack for creating characters who feel real, even in a world of gods and demons. The pacing is tight, the world-building immersive, and the twists, while not always surprising, serve the story’s deeper themes rather than relying on shock value. The novel’s title itself—*Two Candles for the Devil*—hints at the duality of Cat’s journey: one candle for her father’s memory, another for the darkness she must confront.

Ultimately, *Dos velas para el diablo* is a novel about the cost of war, both literal and metaphorical. It’s a story that lingers long after the last page, not just because of its epic scope, but because of the quiet, devastating questions it poses about morality, power, and the lengths to which we’ll go in the name of justice. Laura Gallego García may be best known for her fantasy epics, but this book proves she is also a master of psychological depth and emotional resonance. It’s a tale of fire and fury, but also of the fragile, flickering light that guides us through the dark.
