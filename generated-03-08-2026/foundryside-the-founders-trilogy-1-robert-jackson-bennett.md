# Foundryside (The Founders Trilogy, #1)

**Author:** Robert Jackson Bennett  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

In Foundryside, Robert Jackson Bennett does not merely build a world; he wires it, codes it, and sets it ticking with a terrifying, industrial pulse. Opening the first installment of The Founders Trilogy, Bennett abandons the tired tropes of dusty tomes and mystical hand-waving, presenting instead a vibrant, brutal renaissance city where magic is code, and reality itself is open-source. For a genre often bogged down by medieval nostalgia, Foundryside arrives as a breath of ozone and burning grease—a dazzling triumph of "magitech" that feels both wildly inventive and unsettlingly familiar.

At the heart of Bennett’s narrative style is a seamless fusion of high-stakes caper fiction and complex socio-political world-building. Tevanne is a city-state dominated by ruthless Merchant Houses, entities that operate less like noble families and more like unchecked corporate monopolies. Here, the dominant magic is scriving—the art of inscribing commands onto inanimate objects to trick them into defying the laws of physics. A coin believes it is lighter than air; a carriage door believes it is locked by an immovable force; a bridge believes it is anchored to nothing, yet holds fast. 

Bennett’s prose style is lean, kinetic, and razor-sharp, perfectly mirroring the pacing of the heists that drive the plot. When Sancia Grado—a low-city thief with a scarred past and a unique, literal touch for the thoughts of objects—infiltrates a heavily guarded warehouse, the narrative hits the ground running and rarely lets the reader catch their breath. Sancia is far from the chosen-one archetypes of traditional fantasy; she is battered, cynical, cynical out of necessity, and defined by a fierce survival instinct. Through her tactile perspective, Bennett explores the central theme of the novel: the commodification of existence.

In Foundryside, magic is capitalism taken to its logical, dystopian extreme. The scrived objects that power Tevanne’s booming economy are not just tools; they are enslaved consciousnesses. Every lock, gear, and paving stone has been whispered a command to endure endless labor for the profit of the elite. By framing magic as industrial programming, Bennett crafts a biting critique of modern consumerism, labor exploitation, and technological unchecked growth. The artifact Sancia accidentally steals—a key piece of ancient, god-like technology known as a Salvatore—threatens to rewrite the very syntax of this reality, raising profound questions about who controls the code of the world, and who pays the price for progress.

Yet, for all its macroeconomic scope and technological fascination, Foundryside succeeds primarily because of its intimate character dynamics. Sancia’s forced alignment with a ragtag group of academics, spies, and outcasts transforms a high-concept sci-fi fantasy into a deeply human story about empathy, solidarity, and defiance. Bennett’s characterization gives a beating heart to the metallic clatter of the city, ensuring that the emotional stakes always match the apocalyptic scale of the plot.

Striking, clever, and relentlessly gripping, Foundryside is a masterclass in modern epic fantasy. Robert Jackson Bennett has crafted a world where the lines between machine and magic, freedom and control, are constantly being rewritten—and once you open its covers, you will not want to put the book down until the final line of code is run.
