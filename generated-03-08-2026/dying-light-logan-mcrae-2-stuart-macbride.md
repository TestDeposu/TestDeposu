# Dying Light (Logan McRae, #2)

**Author:** Stuart MacBride  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2008

**Dying Light: A Gritty, Unrelenting Descent into Darkness**

Stuart MacBride’s *Dying Light* is the second installment in his Logan McRae series—a relentless, visceral crime thriller that plunges readers into the bleak underbelly of a city where evil thrives in the shadows. If the first book established Logan McRae as a morally complex detective navigating the seedy depths of Edinburgh’s criminal underworld, *Dying Light* deepens the stakes, delivering a story that is as brutal as it is brilliantly constructed. MacBride, a master of the dark procedural, doesn’t just write crime fiction; he crafts a world where the line between justice and vengeance blurs, and where the human cost of solving crimes is as heavy as the bodies piling up.

At its core, *Dying Light* is a tale of obsession—both the killer’s and Logan’s. The novel opens with a brutal murder by the docks, a crime so cold and calculated that it immediately signals this isn’t just another case. As the body count rises, Logan and his team realize they’re dealing with something far worse than a serial killer: a predator who operates with a chilling method, leaving no witnesses, no loose ends. The tension coils tighter with each passing chapter, MacBride’s prose never wavering in its unflinching realism. He doesn’t shy away from the grotesque—violence here is visceral, often graphic, but never gratuitous. Instead, it serves a purpose, reinforcing the novel’s central theme: that evil doesn’t just lurk in the dark; it thrives there, and those who hunt it must be willing to get just as dirty.

What sets *Dying Light* apart is MacBride’s ability to balance brutal realism with dark humor and a keen sense of character. Logan McRae isn’t just a detective; he’s a man carrying the weight of his past, his relationships, and the moral compromises that come with the job. The novel’s supporting cast—his colleagues, his allies, and even his enemies—are all vividly drawn, their flaws and strengths making the story feel lived-in. There’s a warmth to these relationships, a sense of camaraderie that contrasts sharply with the horror unfolding around them. This duality is what makes MacBride’s work so compelling: he doesn’t romanticize the police force, but he doesn’t demonize them either. They’re human, flawed, and often broken, just like the rest of us.

The pacing is relentless, with MacBride weaving together red herrings, psychological twists, and a relentless sense of urgency. The killer’s motives remain shrouded in mystery, forcing Logan and the reader alike to piece together the puzzle. The novel’s structure mirrors the chaos of the investigation itself—no neat, linear progression, but a series of explosive revelations that keep the pages turning. By the time the truth comes to light, it’s not just a cathartic moment but a devastating one, underscoring the cost of the hunt.

Critics have long praised MacBride’s ability to blend gritty realism with sharp storytelling, and *Dying Light* is no exception. It’s a novel that doesn’t just entertain but lingers, its themes of obsession, morality, and the lengths to which people will go resonating long after the final page. For fans of crime fiction that doesn’t pull punches, this is a book that delivers in spades—dark, dense, and impossible to put down.

If you’ve ever wondered what it would be like to step into the mind of a killer while simultaneously battling your own demons, *Dying Light* is your answer. It’s crime fiction at its most uncompromising, a testament to Stuart MacBride’s skill as a writer who knows how to make the shadows feel alive.
