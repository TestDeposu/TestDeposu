# Never Let You Go

**Author:** Chevy Stevens  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Never Let You Go by Chevy Stevens is a razor‑sharp thriller that proves how easily love can curdle into something far more sinister. Eleven years after fleeing an abusive marriage, Lindsey Nash has rebuilt a life that feels solid: a thriving business, a steady boyfriend, and a teenage daughter who looks to her for guidance. The fragile equilibrium shatters the moment her ex‑husband, Andrew, walks out of prison claiming he’s a changed man. Stevens wastes no time in letting the reader feel the prickle of unease that settles over Lindsey’s world—shadows lengthen, phones buzz with unknown numbers, and the familiar comfort of home becomes a stage for silent stalking.

At its core, the novel explores the lingering trauma of domestic violence and the ways obsession can masquerade as concern. Stevens shows that escaping an abuser does not erase the psychological imprint they leave; instead, the past can lurk in the periphery, ready to pounce when the victim least expects it. Lindsey’s conviction that Andrew is behind the threats forces her—and the reader—to confront a terrifying question: Is the danger truly external, or has it taken root inside her own psyche, amplified by fear and guilt? The narrative steadily blurs the line between external menace and internal paranoia, making every creak of the floorboard and every flicker of a streetlamp feel like a potential harbinger of doom.

What drives the tension forward is Stevens’s signature style: taut, economical prose that never wastes a word. She writes with a journalist’s eye for detail, grounding the horror in the mundane—Lindsey’s morning coffee routine, the hum of her daughter’s school bus, the scent of rain on pavement—before letting those ordinary moments erupt into sudden, jarring violence. The pacing is relentless; chapters are short, each ending on a hook that compels the reader to turn the page. Stevens employs a close third‑person perspective that lingers inside Lindsey’s heightened senses, allowing us to feel her racing heart, her clenched jaw, and the exhausting hypervigilance that comes from living under constant threat. This intimacy amplifies the psychological stakes, turning the novel into a study of how fear can warp perception and decision‑making.

Characterization is another strength. Lindsey is neither a helpless victim nor a superhero; she’s a flawed, resilient woman whose instincts are both her greatest asset and her biggest liability. Andrew, though largely off‑page, looms as a chilling presence through letters, phone calls, and the testimonies of those who claim he’s reformed. Stevens avoids caricature, giving him a veneer of charm that makes his potential duplicity all the more unsettling. The supporting cast—Lindsey’s boyfriend, her daughter, and a few wary friends—serve as mirrors reflecting different facets of her struggle: loyalty, doubt, and the yearning for normalcy.

Thematically, Never Let You Go interrogates the idea that safety is a destination rather than a state of mind. Stevens suggests that true security comes not from locking doors but from confronting the ghosts that haunt us, even when those ghosts wear familiar faces. The book also comments on society’s tendency to underestimate the lingering impact of abuse, highlighting how legal sentences do not equate to emotional closure.

In sum, Chevy Stevens delivers a gripping, psychologically nuanced thriller that balances pulse‑pounding suspense with a deep, empathetic exploration of trauma and obsession. Her lean, visceral prose keeps the reader breathless, while the unsettling question of who—or what—is really watching lingers long after the final page. For fans of domestic suspense that feels both immediate and haunting, Never Let You Go is a must‑read.
