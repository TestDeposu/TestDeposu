# The Name of All Things (A Chorus of Dragons, #2)

**Author:** Jenn Lyons  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

When Jenn Lyons burst onto the epic fantasy scene with *The Ruin of Kings*, she delivered a masterclass in narrative gymnastics, utilizing framing devices, unreliable narrators, and a dizzying labyrinth of lore. With *The Name of All Things*, the second installment in her *A Chorus of Dragons* series, Lyons proves that her dazzling debut was no fluke. Instead, she doubles down on her signature complexities, crafting a sophomore novel that is as intellectually demanding as it is relentlessly thrilling. 

At the beating heart of *The Name of All Things* is the heavy, often brutal thematic exploration of sacrifice and bodily autonomy. The central philosophical question posed by the narrative is deceptively simple yet harrowing: How much of your soul, your morals, and your very identity are you willing to barter for the things you desperately desire? Kihrin D’Mon, our beleaguered and perpetually hunted protagonist, finds himself dragged deeper into a cosmic chessboard where he is treated less like a human being and more like a tactical piece. Lyons brilliantly subverts the traditional "chosen one" trope. Rather than feeling empowered by destiny, Kihrin is perpetually crushed by it. The book examines how systemic power structures—whether they are tyrannical empires, ancient magical orders, or well-meaning rebellions—demand the subjugation of the individual for the "greater good." Through Kihrin and the introduction of the fierce Joratese warrior Janel Theranon, Lyons interrogates the cost of survival in a world that views people as collateral damage. 

Stylistically, Lyons remains a fearless maximalist. Readers who prefer linear, straightforward storytelling will find themselves profoundly tested, as *The Name of All Things* revels in non-linear chronology, shifting perspectives, in-world historical footnotes, and layer upon layer of political and mythological deceit. Yet, this complexity is precisely where Lyons’s brilliance shines. Her prose is sharp, witty, and unapologetically dark, balancing high-stakes cataclysms with intimate, biting character interactions. The world-building is breathtakingly rich; every city, myth, and magical artifact feels ancient and weighty, contributing to a tapestry that is as gorgeous as it is cruel. 

While the narrative density can occasionally border on overwhelming, the payoff is undeniably satisfying. Lyons orchestrates a symphony of shifting allegiances, ancient dragons, and eldritch horrors that keeps the pages turning at a breakneck pace. *The Name of All Things* is not a comfortable read, nor does it offer easy answers. It is a brilliant, labyrinthine epic that demands your full attention—and rewards it tenfold. For fans of intricate, high-stakes fantasy who like their heroes battered, their villains terrifyingly nuanced, and their plots layered with secrets, Jenn Lyons continues to be an essential voice in the genre.
