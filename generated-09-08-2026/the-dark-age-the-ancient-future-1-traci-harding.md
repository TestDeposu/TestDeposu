# The Dark Age (The Ancient Future, #1)

**Author:** Traci Harding  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

"In the midst of the English countryside, a chance encounter with ancient history sets off a chain of events that will alter the course of British politics, mystique, and war. Traci Harding's magnum opus, The Dark Age, defies genre boundaries as it weaves together elements of historical fiction, fantasy, and romance to create a narrative that is both captivating and thought-provoking.

At its core, The Dark Age explores the intricate and often fraught relationship between the past and the present. By dropping a modern-day protagonist, Tory, into the midst of the Dark Ages, Harding invites readers to question the notion of a fixed timeline. Tory's arrival in the 6th century sets off a ripple effect that begins to alter the very fabric of British history, prompting a reevaluation of the myths and legends that have long been taken for granted.

One of the most striking aspects of Harding's writing is her ability to seamlessly blend historical and mythological events with contemporary sensibilities. Her descriptions of life in the Dark Ages are vivid and immersive, conjuring images of a harsh and unforgiving world where survival was a daily struggle. At the same time, Harding's portrayal of Tory's modern outlook and values adds a welcome dose of realism and humor to the narrative, as she navigates a world that is both familiar and yet utterly alien.

Tory's character, in particular, is a highlight of the book. Her transformation from a confident and determined young woman to a more vulnerable and introspective individual is both painful and inspiring to watch. As she navigates the complexities of life in the Dark Ages, Harding reveals the depth of Tory's emotions and the strength of her convictions, making her a truly compelling protagonist.

The supporting cast of characters is equally well-crafted, with Prince Maelgwn of Gwynedd emerging as a complex and nuanced figure. His encounters with Tory are fraught with tension and uncertainty, as he grapples with the implications of her arrival in his world. Harding's portrayal of the prince's inner turmoil is a testament to her skill as a writer, as she navigates the complexities of his character with sensitivity and nuance.

Throughout The Dark Age, Harding's writing is characterised by a sense of wonder and curiosity. Her prose is lyrical and engaging, conjuring images of a rich and vibrant world that is both familiar and yet utterly alien. Her use of language is evocative and precise, conjuring the sights, sounds, and smells of life in the Dark Ages with ease.

If there is one criticism to be made of the book, it is that Harding's pacing can sometimes be a little slow. The narrative is dense and immersive, with a complex web of plot threads and character arcs that can be overwhelming at times. However, this is a minor quibble in an otherwise outstanding novel.

In conclusion, The Dark Age is a truly exceptional novel that will appeal to fans of historical fiction, fantasy, and romance alike. Traci Harding's writing is engaging, immersive, and captivating, conjuring images of a rich and vibrant world that is both familiar and yet utterly alien. With its complex characters, nuanced plot, and thought-provoking themes, this book is a must-read for anyone interested in exploring the intricacies of history, myth, and culture.
