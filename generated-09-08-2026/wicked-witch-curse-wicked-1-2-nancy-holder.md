# Wicked: Witch & Curse (Wicked, #1-2)

**Author:** Nancy Holder  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

Wicked: Witch & Curse thrusts readers into a simmering magical feud that has burned through generations, and Nancy Holder handles the blaze with a confident, almost cinematic flair. Holly Cathers, once an ordinary teen, now stands at the crossroads of two warring witch lineages—the ancient House of Cahors and its bitter rivals, the Deveraux. Holder wastes no time establishing the stakes: Holly’s acceptance of her Cahors birthright is immediately tested by love, loyalty, and the looming threat of an all‑out supernatural war. The novel’s core theme is the destructive weight of inherited grudges versus the redemptive power of choice. Holly’s journey asks whether she can break a cycle of vengeance that has claimed countless lives, or whether she will become another casualty of a history she never asked to inherit.

Holder’s prose is brisk yet lush, favoring short, punchy sentences that drive the action forward while pausing for vivid, sensory details when the magic erupts. She paints the witching world with a tactile realism—crackling sigils, the scent of ozone after a spell, the whisper of ancient runes—making the fantastical feel immediate and dangerous. Dialogue crackles with teenage urgency and underlying tension, especially in the scenes between Holly and Jer, whose forbidden romance adds a personal stakes layer that fuels the larger conflict. The romance never overwhelms the plot; instead, it underscores the theme that love can be both a weapon and a shield in a feud that thrives on hatred.

Where the novel shines is in its world‑building. Holder hints at a deep mythology—coven rituals, forgotten prophecies, and the lingering echoes of past battles—without drowning the reader in exposition. Instead, she doles out lore through Holly’s discoveries and the consequences of her choices, keeping the narrative momentum high. The pacing is relentless; each chapter ends on a hook that propels the reader toward the next revelation, making the book difficult to put down. However, this speed occasionally sacrifices depth in secondary characters, who sometimes serve more as plot devices than fully realized individuals. A slower exploration of the Deveraux perspective could have added nuance to the feud, but Holder’s focus remains firmly on Holly’s internal struggle, which ultimately drives the story forward.

The emotional core of Wicked: Witch & Curse lies in Holly’s willingness to sacrifice herself for those she loves, a motif that recurs with haunting clarity. Holder balances the high‑octane magical battles with quiet, introspective moments where Holly grapples with identity, destiny, and the moral ambiguity of wielding power. These interludes prevent the novel from becoming a mere spectacle of spells and instead root it in a genuine, coming‑of‑age struggle that resonates beyond the fantasy veneer.

In sum, Nancy Holder delivers a fast‑paced, emotionally charged entry into the Wicked series that successfully blends romance, witchcraft, and the timeless question of whether we are bound by our bloodlines or free to rewrite them. Fans of urban fantasy who crave strong heroines, intricate magical systems, and a love that defies ancestral hatred will find much to admire—and perhaps a few lingering questions about what the next generation will inherit.
