# The Tournament at Gorlan (Ranger’s Apprentice: The Early Years, #1)

**Author:** John Flanagan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

The Tournament at Gorlan launches John Flanagan’s new prequel series with the same kinetic energy that made Ranger’s Apprentice a worldwide favorite. Set years before Will ever dons his cloak, the novel follows the seasoned Ranger Halt and his eager protégé Crowley as they uncover a conspiracy that threatens the very heart of the Kingdom. Morgarath, a charismatic yet ruthless noble, has wormed his way into the Ranger Corps, sowing doubt and weakening the order that has long guarded the throne. To stop him, Halt and Crowley must win the trust of Prince Duncan, navigate the treacherous politics of the Baron of Gorlan, and ultimately prove their mettle in the kingdom’s famed tournament—a crucible where skill, honor, and loyalty are tested in brutal, breath‑stopping duels.

At its core, the book explores the tension between duty and ambition. Halt embodies the Ranger creed: vigilance, self‑discipline, and an unwavering commitment to protect the realm, even when the cost is personal sacrifice. Crowley, meanwhile, represents the youthful spark that questions tradition while still yearning to belong to something greater. Their dynamic drives the narrative forward, allowing Flanagan to examine how ideals survive—or crumble—under political manipulation. The tournament itself becomes a metaphor for this struggle: each clash is not just a test of swordsmanship but a battle of wits, where the Rangers must outthink a foe who prefers deceit over open combat.

Flanagan’s style remains instantly recognizable. He leans on short, punchy chapters that propel the reader from one tense encounter to the next, interspersing moments of dry humor that relieve the ever‑present danger. Dialogue is crisp and character‑driven, revealing Halt’s weary wisdom and Crowley’s eager bravado without lengthy exposition. Action scenes are vivid yet economical; a single sentence can convey the clang of steel, the sting of a missed blow, and the rush of adrenaline that follows a hard‑won victory. Meanwhile, the world‑building feels both familiar and fresh—readers of the original series will recognize the forest‑silent ethos of the Rangers, while newcomers receive just enough detail to picture the mist‑shrouded hills of Gorlan without being overwhelmed by lore.

What makes this preview strike a chord is how Flanagan balances the familiar with the new. Fans will delight in seeing the origins of Halt’s legendary reputation, while the fresh political intrigue offers a stakes‑raising puzzle that feels distinct from the later books’ monster‑filled quests. The novel’s pacing never flags; each chapter ends with a hook that urges the reader onward, and the climax at the tournament delivers a satisfying blend of swordplay, strategy, and emotional payoff.

In short, *The Tournament at Gorlan* is a tight, exhilarating tale that honors the spirit of Ranger’s Apprentice while carving out its own niche. It reminds us that true strength lies not just in the blade we wield, but in the convictions that guide our swing—a lesson as timeless as the Rangers themselves.
