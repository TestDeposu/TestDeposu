# The Color of Vengeance (The Ties That Bind, #2)

**Author:** Rob J. Hayes  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

In the realm of dark epic fantasy, few authors have managed to carve out a niche as distinct as Rob J. Hayes. With "The Color of Vengeance," the second installment in The Ties That Bind series, Hayes delivers a gritty, unapologetic tale of revenge, retribution, and the blurred lines between good and evil.

At the heart of this narrative is the Black Thorn, a brooding anti-hero driven by a singular focus: vengeance against those who have wronged him. His list is long, and the wounds run deep, particularly the loss of his eye at the hands of the Arbiter. As Thorn navigates the treacherous landscape of Sarth, he must assemble a new crew of misfits, each with their own agendas and scores to settle.

Hayes's world-building is a character in its own right, a dark tapestry woven from the threads of zealous witch hunters, roving warlords, dark magic, and demons. This is a realm where the strong prey on the weak, and the rule of law is but a distant memory. The author's prose is economical, yet evocative, conjuring vivid images of a world in chaos.

One of the most striking aspects of Hayes's writing style is his ability to craft complex, morally ambiguous characters. Thorn is no hero; he's a flawed, often brutal individual driven by a desire for revenge. His crew is equally multifaceted, with motivations that are frequently at odds with one another. This moral ambiguity lends a sense of realism to the narrative, making it difficult to discern right from wrong.

The pacing of "The Color of Vengeance" is relentless, with a narrative that hurtles forward like a runaway wagon. Hayes's action scenes are visceral and intense, with a focus on the brutal, often disturbing consequences of violence. His world is not a pretty place, and he pulls no punches in his depiction of the darker aspects of human nature.

Fans of Joe Abercrombie and Brent Weeks will find much to appreciate in Hayes's work. Like these authors, he has a keen eye for the gritty, often brutal realities of a world in chaos. His writing is unflinching, yet strangely compelling, drawing the reader into a world that is both disturbing and fascinating.

In conclusion, "The Color of Vengeance" is a gripping, unapologetic tale of revenge and retribution. With its morally complex characters, dark world-building, and visceral action scenes, it's a must-read for fans of dark epic fantasy. If you're looking for a narrative that will challenge your assumptions and leave you breathless, look no further than Rob J. Hayes's latest offering.
