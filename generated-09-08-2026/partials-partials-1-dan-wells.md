# Partials (Partials, #1)

**Author:** Dan Wells  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

In the crowded landscape of young adult dystopian fiction, Dan Wells’s 2012 novel Partials carves out a distinct and terrifying niche, merging the claustrophobic tension of biological thrillers with the sweeping stakes of a post-apocalyptic war for species survival. Drawing clear thematic DNA from classics like Blade Runner and Battlestar Galactica, Wells constructs a bleak yet deeply compelling future where humanity is not merely conquered, but methodically backed into a corner of extinction.

The central premise hinges on a masterclass in existential dread: a devastating war against the Partials—engineered, organic human lookalikes—culminating in the release of the RM virus. This engineered plague ruthlessly targets every newborn, ensuring that the remaining tens of thousands of human survivors huddled on Long Island are living on borrowed time. Wells wastes no time setting the stakes. This is not a world of slow, lingering recovery; it is a ticking clock, and the alarm is running out of batteries.

At the beating heart of this ruined world is Kira Walker, a sixteen-year-old medic who serves as the narrative’s fierce anchor. Wells crafts Kira not as a chosen one imbued with mystical powers, but as a pragmatic, hard-nosed realist driven by scientific curiosity and desperate hope. Through Kira, the author explores the novel's most potent theme: the dangerous fragility of societal dogmas in the face of annihilation. As Kira embarks on perilous salvage runs into the skeletal ruins of New York and clashes with the rigid, authoritarian laws of her own government, Wells interrogates how far a dying society will go to preserve its own rules, even when those very rules guarantee its demise.

Wells’s writing style is brisk, cinematic, and relentlessly action-packed. He excels at pacing, seamlessly weaving high-stakes medical mystery with heart-pounding combat sequences and tactical espionage. Yet, what elevates Partials above standard genre fare is its philosophical nuance. The author refuses to paint the conflict in simple shades of good versus evil. By slowly peeling back the layers of the war's origins and introducing the complex, human-like motivations of the Partials themselves, Wells challenges both Kira and the reader to reconsider the definitions of humanity, morality, and justice. Perspective, the novel argues, is the ultimate weapon—and the ultimate blind spot.

Partials is a gripping, intellectually stimulating ride that combines the adrenaline of a survival thriller with the thought-provoking depth of classic science fiction. Dan Wells has delivered a striking opening salvo to a sequence that dares to ask whether humanity deserves to survive if it loses its soul along the way. For fans of intelligent, high-stakes dystopian adventures, this is an essential journey into the ruins of tomorrow.
