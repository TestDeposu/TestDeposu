# Unblemished (Unblemished, #1)

**Author:** Sara Ella  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

Unblemished opens with a striking image: a girl who cannot bear to see her own face, haunted by a birthmark that marks her as different in a world that prizes conformity. Sara Ella uses this visceral reluctance as the gateway into a dual‑reality fantasy where the protagonist’s perceived flaw becomes the key to saving an enslaved realm. The novel’s central theme is the transformation of shame into strength—a journey that mirrors the universal adolescent struggle to claim self‑worth amid external judgment. Ella weaves this theme through Eliyana’s hesitant romance with Joshua, the sudden thrust into guardianship, and the looming menace of a tyrant who thrives on the very insecurities she carries. By tying personal insecurity to a larger cosmic battle, the story elevates a familiar YA trope into something that feels both intimate and epic.

Ella’s prose is lyrical yet accessible, favoring vivid sensory details that make the shift from gritty New York streets to the strange, oppressive realm of the Reflection almost palpable. She alternates between Eliyana’s first‑person intimacy and occasional third‑person glimpses of the world beyond her, a technique that deepens the sense of two intertwined realities without sacrificing narrative momentum. The romance is deliberately low‑spice, focusing on emotional tenderness rather than physical intensity, which allows the plot’s fantastical stakes to take center stage. Dialogue feels natural, capturing the awkward earnestness of teenagers thrust into extraordinary responsibility.

What works best is the novel’s ability to make the metaphor tangible: Eliyana’s birthmark is not merely a decorative flaw but a literal marker of power in the Reflection, turning her insecurity into a tangible asset. This literalization invites readers to reconsider their own “blemishes” as potential sources of resilience. The world‑building, while imaginative, leans on familiar dystopian motifs—a tyrannical ruler, a hidden resistance, a chosen‑one prophecy—so seasoned fantasy fans may crave deeper lore or more original twists. Nevertheless, Ella compensates with brisk pacing and a cliff‑hanger ending that propels the sequel, Unraveling, forward with urgent curiosity.

Critically, the book occasionally leans on exposition to explain the mechanics of the Reflection, which can slow the immediate emotional punch of a scene. Some secondary characters, particularly the hooded stalker, feel more like plot devices than fully realized figures, leaving room for richer development in later installments. Yet these are minor quibbles in a debut that successfully balances heart‑wrenching personal growth with the sweep of high‑stakes fantasy.

Unblemished is a compelling entry point for readers who crave a romance‑tinged fantasy where love is less about passion and more about acceptance, and where the hero’s greatest weapon is the very part of herself she has learned to hide. Ella’s debut proves that a blemished exterior can conceal an extraordinary interior—a message that resonates long after the final page is turned.
