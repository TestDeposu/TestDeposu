# Kingdoms at War (Dragon Gate, #1)

**Author:** Lindsay Buroker  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

Title: Mapping the Rebellion: Lindsay Buroker’s Kingdoms at War Ignites a Thrilling New Fantasy Journey

In the crowded landscape of modern fantasy, finding a story that balances grand magical world-building with an intimate, character-driven adventure is becoming increasingly rare. Enter Lindsay Buroker and her 2023 release, Kingdoms at War, the explosive first installment in the complete Dragon Gate series. Buroker, a veteran author renowned for her snappy dialogue, relentless pacing, and lovable underdogs, has crafted a novel that takes classic fantasy tropes—ancient magic, sky-ruling tyrants, and lost artifacts—and breathes fresh, exhilarating life into them. 

At the narrative heart of Kingdoms at War is a compelling thematic exploration of destiny versus autonomy. The protagonist, Jak, is a refreshing departure from the usual prophesied heroes of epic fantasy. He is not a warrior yearning for glory, nor is he a ambitious scholar seeking dominion; he is simply a cartographer. He wants to map the physical world, chart the unknown, and remain entirely out of the crosshairs of the oppressive wizards who rule humanity from their opulent sky cities. Buroker uses Jak’s love for cartography as a brilliant thematic anchor. Just as a map represents order, understanding, and the desire to connect distant lands, the wizards represent chaotic oppression and division. 

When Jak and his archaeologist mother unearth the legendary dragon gate, their quiet pursuit of history abruptly collides with a brutal present. What makes the premise truly striking is Buroker’s subversion of the magic-as-a-blessing trope. For Jak, developing magical powers is not a triumphant awakening; it is an existential threat. Magic is the instrument of the enemy, the very power used to enslave humanity. By forcing her protagonist to wield the tools of his oppressors, Buroker dives deep into a nuanced moral dilemma: can a corrupting power be used for liberation, or does the wielder inevitably become the monster they fight?

True to Buroker’s signature style, the novel wastes no time sinking its hooks into the reader. Eschewing the sluggish, exposition-heavy openings that frequently plague first-in-series fantasy novels, Kingdoms at War hits the ground running. The exposition is seamlessly woven into the breathless action, dialogue, and archaeological discoveries. Buroker’s prose is clean, accessible, and cinematic, painting vivid pictures of forgotten ruins, towering sky cities, and the terrifying efficiency of elite wizard assassins. 

Yet, the true magic of Buroker’s writing lies in her characters. Jak is delightfully grounded, blending academic curiosity with a desperate, frantic survival instinct. His dynamic with his mother brings a grounded, emotional realism to the fantastical chaos unfolding around them. And because this is a Buroker book, readers can anticipate plenty of witty banter, high-stakes tension, and a growing ensemble of memorable secondary characters who elevate the journey from a simple escape mission into a burgeoning rebellion.

Kingdoms at War is a masterclass in modern indie fantasy pacing and world-building. It delivers everything a reader could want: high-stakes peril, a fascinating magic system rooted in ancient lore, and a reluctant hero you cannot help but root for. Because the Dragon Gate series is already complete, readers can dive into this opening volume without fear of the dreaded cliffhanger abyss, knowing the full arc of Jak’s rebellion is waiting to be explored. 

If you crave a fast-paced, imaginative adventure where a mapmaker holds the key to toppling a sky-born empire, Lindsay Buroker’s latest offering is an absolute must-read. Pick up Kingdoms at War and take your first step through the gate.
