# Time Streams (Magic: The Gathering: Artifacts Cycle, #3)

**Author:** J. Robert King  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

Time Streams plunges readers deep into the heart of Dominaria’s most desperate hour, where the legendary planeswalker Urza gathers the brightest scholars of Tolaria to forge a weapon capable of turning the tide against the relentless Phyrexian menace. J. Robert King frames this high‑stakes endeavor not merely as a battle of steel and sorcery, but as a meditation on the perilous allure of manipulating time itself—a theme that reverberates through every decision, sacrifice, and betrayal on the tiny island academy.

At its core, the novel explores how the desire to control destiny can warp both intention and morality. Urza’s vision of a temporal safeguard is noble, yet the very act of bending chronology introduces fissures that threaten to unravel the fabric of reality. King deftly mirrors this external conflict with internal struggles: characters grapple with the temptation to rewrite personal histories, to erase losses, or to seize fleeting advantages, only to confront the unintended consequences that ripple outward. The result is a tense, almost claustrophobic atmosphere where every tick of the clock feels like a countdown not just to invasion, but to potential self‑annihilation.

King’s prose is lean and propulsive, echoing the relentless march of the Phyrexian horde. He favors crisp, vivid imagery—describing the hum of arcane machinery, the flicker of temporal anomalies, and the stark, rain‑slicked corridors of Tolaria with equal precision. Dialogue snaps with the urgency of wartime councils, while descriptive passages linger just long enough to let the weight of a moment settle before the narrative surges forward again. This stylistic balance keeps the pacing brisk without sacrificing depth, allowing readers to feel both the intellectual grind of the academy’s research and the visceral dread of an looming apocalypse.

What sets Time Streams apart within the Artifacts Cycle is King’s willingness to treat time not as a mere plot device but as a character in its own right. The temporal experiments conducted by Urza’s team are rendered with a quasi‑scientific rigor that grounds the fantastical elements in a palpable sense of cause and effect. As the scholars push the boundaries of what can be seen, heard, and felt across eras, the novel poses unsettling questions about responsibility: when you can glimpse the future, does knowing it obligate you to act, or does it strip away the agency that makes choice meaningful?

The supporting cast—ranging from eager apprentices to skeptical veterans—provides a rich tapestry of perspectives that highlight the thematic tensions. Their loyalties shift, secrets surface, and alliances fracture, all under the looming shadow of a possible temporal paradox. King handles these interpersonal dynamics with a keen eye for motive, ensuring that each betrayal feels earned rather than contrived, and each moment of camaraderie carries a hard‑won resonance.

Critically, the novel does lean heavily on familiarity with the broader Magic: The Gathering lore; newcomers may find themselves needing to pause and absorb references to earlier cycles, planeswalker histories, and the ever‑present Phyrexian threat. However, for fans of the franchise, these layers enrich the experience, rewarding attentive readers with Easter eggs and callbacks that deepen the sense of an expansive, interconnected universe.

In sum, Time Streams delivers a tightly wound narrative that marries the grandeur of epic fantasy with the intellectual intrigue of temporal speculation. King’s style—sharp, evocative, and relentlessly forward‑moving—mirrors the very theme he investigates: the inexorable pull of time and the perilous temptation to master it. The result is a compelling addition to the Artifacts Cycle that will leave readers pondering the cost of altering destiny long after they’ve turned the final page.
