# Trek to Kraggen-Cor (Mithgar, #4; The Silver Call, #1)

**Author:** Dennis L. McKiernan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

The echoes of Middle-earth resonate deeply within the pages of Dennis L. McKiernan’s fantasy catalog, but never quite as thunderously as in Trek to Kraggen-Cor. As the fourth installment in the expansive Mithgar series and the opening volume of The Silver Call durology, this novel strips away the sweeping fellowship dynamics of traditional high fantasy to focus on a singular, gritty, and fiercely desperate objective: reclaiming a lost homeland. Two centuries after the defeat of a dark lord, a new shadow creeps across the lands of Mithgar. Yet, rather than waiting for doom to consume them, the resilient race of dwarves takes up the hammer and the axe. Led by the steadfast King Durek, an army marches forth on a perilous quest toward Kraggen-Cor, the legendary subterranean metropolis that once beat as the proud heart of the dwarven empire, but now serves as a festering stronghold for unspeakable evils.

McKiernan’s central theme is undeniably the reclamation of heritage and the crushing weight of legacy. The trek to Kraggen-Cor is not merely a military campaign or a straightforward dungeon crawl; it is a profound psychological and cultural resurrection. The dwarves are not fighting for territory they have never seen; they are fighting for the ghostly memory of their ancestors' halls, attempting to stitch together a fractured history. This quest explores how ancient griefs and lost glories can drive a civilization to either total redemption or absolute ruin. The mountain itself acts as a living antagonist—claustrophobic, ancient, and deeply resistant to the return of its former masters. 

Stylistically, McKiernan wears his primary literary influence—J.R.R. Tolkien—as both a badge of honor and a guiding framework. Fans of classic, mythic-scale fantasy will find themselves immediately at home in his prose, which carries a solemn, epic cadence reminiscent of old Norse sagas and high-fantasy mythmaking. McKiernan’s world-building is meticulous, favoring rich terminology, genealogical weight, and a palpable sense of ancient history layered into the very stone of Mithgar. The pacing shifts brilliantly from the cautious, dread-filled preparations on the surface to the suffocating, labyrinthine darkness of the subterranean march. The author excels at generating atmospheric tension; every shadowed corridor and distant dripping echo in Kraggen-Cor feels pregnant with menace. 

While detractors occasionally point out the strong derivative nature of McKiernan's work in relation to Tolkien's legendarium, Trek to Kraggen-Cor carves out its own distinct identity by shifting the narrative spotlight squarely onto the dwarven perspective—a cultural focus that Tolkien himself only partially explored. McKiernan gives these stout warriors a distinct voice, blending stubborn pride, craftsmanship, and a tragic, poetic melancholy that makes their perilous crusade deeply moving. 

Ultimately, Trek to Kraggen-Cor is a muscular, atmospheric, and emotionally resonant epic that delivers everything a lover of traditional high fantasy could crave. It is a tale of iron, stone, and unbreakable will—a gripping reminder that reclaiming the past often requires descending straight into the heart of darkness. For anyone who has ever gazed at a mountain range in a fantasy novel and wondered what horrors and treasures lie buried in its roots, this book is an essential, gripping journey.
