# Tales of Nevèrÿon (Return to Nevèrÿon, #1)

**Author:** Samuel R. Delany  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

To read Samuel R. Delany’s *Tales of Nevèrÿon* is to witness a master architect systematically dismantling the very foundation of the fantasy genre, only to rebuild it into something infinitely more complex, intellectually stimulating, and profoundly human. Originally published in 1979 and presented here in its enduring conceptual weight, this opening volume of Delany’s monumental *Return to Nevèrÿon* series defies the easy comforts of traditional sword-and-sorcery. If conventional fantasy asks us to escape into simplified worlds of good versus evil, Delany invites us into the messy, exhilarating dawn of civilization itself—a primitive Nevèrÿon grappling with the birth of currency, literacy, organized labor, and systemic power.

At the heart of the collection’s interconnected narrative arc is Gorgik, a boy from the bustling docks of port Kolhari who is thrust into brutal enslavement in the obsidian mines of the Faltha mountains following a political coup and the murder of his parents. His eventual emancipation sparks a lifelong evolution from traumatized captive to revolutionary, a through-story that anchors the volume. Yet, Gorgik’s journey is not merely a tale of personal vengeance or physical triumph. Instead, Delany uses Gorgik’s enduring connection to the mines and the iron collar—and later, his relationship with the scholar Smallwood and the enigmatic, ambitious woman known as Raven—to interrogate the intricate psychologies of domination and submission. 

What elevates *Tales of Nevèrÿon* far beyond standard genre fare is Delany’s fearless fusion of high literary theory, anthropological inquiry, and uninhibited eroticism. As the *USA Today* aptly noted, reading Delany is akin to imagining Umberto Eco writing the adventures of Conan the Barbarian. The prose is an epic feat of language: dense, lush, self-aware, and playfully ironic. Delany does not merely tell stories; he constructs a semiotic playground where myths are forged in real-time, language is analyzed as a tool of oppression and liberation, and the invention of writing is treated as a technological disruption as seismic as any modern digital revolution. 

Furthermore, the collection boldly confronts sexuality, gender, and marginalization without the puritanical gauze typical of vintage fantasy. Eroticism in Nevèrÿon is tangled with politics, economics, and trauma, serving as another vital lens through which power dynamics are negotiated. Delany understands that the bedrock of civilization is not merely stone and mortar, but capital, desire, and the stories we tell ourselves to justify who holds the whip and who bears the chain. 

Brilliant, challenging, and gorgeously entertaining, *Tales of Nevèrÿon* remains a towering achievement in speculative fiction. It demands an active reader—one willing to unpack irony, question historical inevitabilities, and marvel at the ways language constructs our realities. For anyone weary of tired tropes and formulaic world-building, Delany’s prehistoric yet startlingly modern landscape offers a thrilling, revolutionary breath of fresh air.
