# Stonecutter's Story (Lost Swords, #3)

**Author:** Fred Saberhagen  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

In Stonecutter's Story, the third installment of his iconic Lost Swords series, Fred Saberhagen accomplishes a rare literary feat: he marries the intricate logic of a classical whodunit with the sweeping, high-stakes grandeur of epic fantasy. Published in 2011, this gripping narrative proves that Saberhagen’s imagination remained as razor-sharp as the legendary blades at the heart of his universe. Rather than relying solely on magical spectacle, the author crafts a cerebral puzzle, inviting readers to match wits with some of the realm's most cunning minds.

At the core of the novel is the formidable Stonecutter, the Sword of Siege—a weapon of terrifying capability, capable of cleaving diamonds and pulverizing mountains with nonchalant ease. Yet, Saberhagen treats this divine artifact not just as a tool of destruction, but as a catalyst that exposes the fragile motivations of mortals. The thematic heartbeat of the book explores the corrupting allure of absolute power and the lengths to which desperate factions will go to seize it. The Red Temple, the Blue Temple, and a clandestine network of rebels all converge in a frantic race for the blade, each representing a different facet of fanaticism, ambition, and rebellion. Through these competing forces, Saberhagen examines how power distorts ideology, turning noble causes into ruthless pursuits of dominance.

The author’s signature style shines brightly through the dynamic duo at the center of the investigation: the brilliant Magistrate Wen Chang and his earnest young physician assistant, Kasimir. Eschewing the trope of the brawny barbarian hacking his way through problems, Saberhagen leans heavily into a procedural, investigative rhythm. Wen Chang’s sharp intellect and Kasimir’s eager perspective inject the fantasy genre with a refreshing dose of ratiocination. Every clue matters, every alibi is scrutinized, and the pacing demands the reader's active participation. 

Adding emotional resonance and unpredictability to the narrative is Natasha, a mysterious young girl enlisted by Kasimir as a spy. Her presence introduces a simmering undercurrent of romantic tension and psychological suspense. As Kasimir falls under her spell, the narrative poses a timeless, unsettling question: can anyone truly be trusted in a world driven by greed and magical warfare? Natasha acts as a literary wildcard, keeping both the characters and the audience perpetually off-balance.

Saberhagen’s prose is economical yet evocative, balancing dense political machinations with moments of quiet character introspection. He understands that the magic of the Twelve Swords lies not just in what they can do to stone and steel, but in what they reveal about the human heart. Stonecutter's Story is a masterclass in genre-blending, offering a taut, mystery-box narrative wrapped in rich fantasy lore. For veterans of the Lost Swords saga and newcomers alike, this adventure is an irresistible summons to think, deduce, and survive.
