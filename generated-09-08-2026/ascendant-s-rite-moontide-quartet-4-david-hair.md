# Ascendant's Rite (Moontide Quartet, #4)

**Author:** David Hair  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

**A Thrilling Conclusion: Review of Ascendant's Rite by David Hair**

The epic conclusion to David Hair's Moontide Quartet has finally arrived, and it's a doozy. In Ascendant's Rite, Hair masterfully weaves together the complex threads of his fantastical world, bringing the fate of Urte to a climactic and heart-stopping conclusion. As the battle for power and magic reaches its crescendo, Hair's signature blend of politics, war, and mythology creates a rich tapestry that's sure to captivate fans of epic fantasy.

One of the standout features of Ascendant's Rite is its intricate plotting. Hair has a talent for crafting narratives that twist and turn, keeping readers guessing until the very last page. The stakes are higher than ever, as Alaron and Ramita struggle to recover the key to the Ascendants' magic and Queen Cera fights to claim the throne. Meanwhile, Seth Korion's Lost Legions are trapped between two massive armies, adding an air of desperation to an already perilous situation.

Hair's world-building is, as always, meticulous and immersive. The author's grasp of his created world is so complete that it's hard to imagine Urte as anything other than a living, breathing entity. From the sprawling cities to the treacherous landscapes of the Leviathan Bridge, Hair's depiction of this fantastical realm is nothing short of breathtaking.

But Ascendant's Rite is more than just a showcase for Hair's impressive world-building. It's also a thought-provoking exploration of the consequences of power. As the characters navigate the complex web of alliances and rivalries, they're forced to confront the darker aspects of their own morality. Will they prioritize their own ambition over the greater good? Or will they find a way to balance their desires with the needs of those around them?

Hair's writing is, as always, polished and evocative. His prose is economical yet expressive, conveying the emotions and motivations of his characters with ease. His dialogue is snappy and realistic, making it simple to become fully invested in the lives of his characters.

The conclusion to the Moontide Quartet is a fitting tribute to the epic fantasy genre. With its blend of politics, mythology, and action, Ascendant's Rite is a must-read for fans of series like George R.R. Martin's A Song of Ice and Fire and Patrick Rothfuss's Kingkiller Chronicle. Hair's masterful storytelling will leave you breathless and eager for more.

In short, Ascendant's Rite is a triumph of epic fantasy. With its intricate plotting, immersive world-building, and thought-provoking themes, it's the perfect conclusion to the Moontide Quartet. If you're a fan of the genre, or just looking for a compelling and epic read, don't miss out on this thrilling conclusion.
