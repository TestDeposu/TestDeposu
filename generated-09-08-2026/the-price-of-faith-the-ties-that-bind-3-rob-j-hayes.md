# The Price of Faith (The Ties That Bind, #3)

**Author:** Rob J. Hayes  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

The Price of Faith thrusts readers back into the bruised, politically charged world of the Dragon Empire, where loyalty is a currency and betrayal a constant shadow. Rob J. Hayes continues his Ties That Bind saga by splitting the narrative between two wounded souls—Thanquil Darkheart, the scarred inquisitor seeking redemption for a heretical loose end, and Jezzet Vel’urn, the disgraced noble fleeing an Empress’s wrath alongside the sly, unpredictable Drake Morrass. Their parallel journeys illuminate the novel’s central question: **what does it cost to believe in something when the world has already decided you’re expendable?** Hayes treats faith not as a lofty ideal but as a fragile, often painful bargain—one that forces characters to weigh personal survival against the ideals they swore to uphold.

Hayes’ prose remains sharp and unflinching, favoring tight, kinetic sentences that thrust the reader into the heart of each conflict. He balances brutal combat scenes with quieter, introspective moments, allowing the internal stakes to resonate as loudly as the clashing swords. The world‑building feels lived‑in; the Dragon Empire’s oppressive bureaucracy, the smell of incense in hidden chapels, and the ever‑present hiss of draconic politics all emerge through subtle details rather than exposition dumps. This economy of language mirrors the characters’ own scarcity of trust—every word feels weighted, every alliance a calculation.

What sets this installment apart is Hayes’ willingness to let his protagonists fail. Thanquil’s pursuit of judgment is tinged with doubt; his moral compass wobbles under the weight of past sins. Jezzet’s escape is less a heroic flight and more a desperate scramble, underscoring how desperation can warp even the noblest intentions. Drake Morrass, ever the insidious wildcard, serves as a reminder that survival often demands complicity with the very forces one despises. Through these intertwining arcs, Hayes explores the theme that faith is not a destination but a price paid daily—sometimes in blood, sometimes in shattered illusions, and always in the quiet moments when one must decide whether to keep believing or to let go.

The novel’s pacing is relentless yet deliberate; Hayes knows when to let tension simmer and when to ignite it, keeping readers hooked from the first whisper of conspiracy to the final, unsettling reckoning. Fans of grimdark fantasy will appreciate the moral ambiguity, while newcomers will find an accessible entry point into a saga that refuses to glorify power without acknowledging its corrupting echo.

In sum, *The Price of Faith* is a compelling meditation on the cost of conviction, delivered through Hayes’ signature blend of gritty action, psychological depth, and a world that feels both fantastical and eerily familiar. It’s a stark reminder that in the Dragon Empire, the most dangerous battles are fought not on the battlefield but within the soul.
