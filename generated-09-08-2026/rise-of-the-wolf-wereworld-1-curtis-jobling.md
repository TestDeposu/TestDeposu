# Rise of the Wolf (Wereworld, #1)

**Author:** Curtis Jobling  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

**Rise of the Wolf: A Howling Adventure for the Brave and the Believing**

Curtis Jobling’s *Rise of the Wolf*—the first installment in the *Wereworld* series—is a roaring debut that blends the political intrigue of *Game of Thrones* with the raw, untamed energy of a young hero’s awakening. Set in a world where Werelords rule as shapeshifting monarchs, the novel follows Drew, a seemingly ordinary boy who discovers he’s not just a werewolf but the lost heir to a throne stolen by Leopold the Lion, a tyrant who rules with claw and cunning. What begins as a personal revelation quickly spirals into a full-blown rebellion, as Drew must navigate a land of shifting loyalties, ancient grudges, and a kingdom on the brink of war.

At its core, *Rise of the Wolf* is a story about destiny and defiance. Drew’s journey mirrors the classic underdog narrative—he’s not the strongest, not the most cunning, but he’s the one with the heart of a wolf. Jobling crafts a protagonist who is both relatable and compelling, his struggles with self-doubt and fear grounding the fantastical elements in something deeply human. The novel’s strength lies in its ability to make the supernatural feel visceral, whether through Drew’s first, terrifying shift into his wolf form or the brutal, almost mythic battles that follow. Leopold the Lion isn’t just a villain; he’s a force of nature, a king who embodies the worst of power—arrogance, cruelty, and an unshakable belief in his own invincibility. His reign is one of fear, and Drew’s rise is the only thing that can break it.

Jobling’s writing style is a perfect match for the story’s tone—bold, rhythmic, and full of life. His prose crackles with energy, especially in the action sequences, where every clash between werebeasts feels like a dance of survival. The world-building is immersive, with Werelords and their shifting forms introduced in a way that feels both fresh and familiar, drawing on folklore while carving out its own mythos. The political intrigue is deftly handled, with factions vying for control and alliances shifting like the tides. Even the secondary characters—from Drew’s loyal friends to the cunning nobles—are vividly drawn, adding depth to the world beyond the main conflict.

What makes *Rise of the Wolf* particularly gripping is its balance of adventure and heart. The novel doesn’t shy away from the darker aspects of power, showing how easily loyalty can be bought, how quickly trust can be broken. Yet, there’s also a sense of hope, a belief that even the smallest spark can ignite a revolution. The book’s pacing is relentless, keeping readers hooked as Drew’s journey unfolds—from his initial disbelief at his heritage to the climactic moments where he must stand against a king who sees him as nothing more than a threat to be eliminated.

For fans of fantasy with a dash of *Ranger’s Apprentice* flair, *Rise of the Wolf* delivers a thrilling, character-driven adventure. It’s a story about finding your place in a world that wants to keep you down, about the power of belief, and about the howl that can unite a kingdom. Curtis Jobling’s debut is more than just a tale of werewolves and warriors—it’s a celebration of resilience, a reminder that even the smallest wolf can change the course of history.

If you’ve ever wanted a fantasy series that’s as fierce as it is fair, *Rise of the Wolf* is the perfect howl to answer.
