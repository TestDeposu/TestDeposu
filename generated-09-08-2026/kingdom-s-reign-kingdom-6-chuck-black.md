# Kingdom's Reign (Kingdom, #6)

**Author:** Chuck Black  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

Kingdom's Reign, the sixth installment in Chuck Black's Kingdom series, is a thrilling tale of good vs. evil, where the forces of darkness threaten to engulf the land of Arrethtrae. With the Dark Knight Lucius and his Shadow Warriors imprisoned in the Wasteland, the Prince returns to reclaim his rightful place on the throne, ushering in an era of peace and prosperity.

As the Prince rules from the royal city of Chessington, his ambassador, Cedric, travels the land, witnessing firsthand the benefits of the Prince's reign. However, not everyone is willing to swear allegiance to the King, and a sinister plot unfolds to free the Dark Knight and his minions. Cedric must ride against time to warn the Prince of the impending danger, setting the stage for an epic battle between good and evil.

Throughout the book, Black masterfully weaves a narrative that is both engaging and thought-provoking. His writing style is reminiscent of classic fantasy authors, with a unique blend of action, adventure, and spiritual themes. The world of Arrethtrae is richly detailed, with a complex history and politics that add depth to the story.

One of the standout features of Kingdom's Reign is its exploration of the theme of pride and greed. Black expertly portrays how these vices can corrupt even the noblest of individuals, leading them down a path of destruction. The character of Lucius, in particular, is a compelling example of how unchecked pride can lead to a downfall.

The author's use of allegory is also noteworthy. The Kingdom series is widely regarded as a Christian allegory, with the King and His Son representing God and Jesus Christ. Black handles this theme with sensitivity and nuance, avoiding heavy-handedness and instead opting for a more subtle approach. The result is a story that is both accessible to non-Christian readers and resonant with those familiar with the biblical narrative.

In terms of character development, Black excels at crafting relatable and well-rounded characters. Cedric, in particular, is a compelling protagonist, whose bravery and loyalty are inspiring. The supporting cast is equally well-developed, with complex motivations and backstories that add depth to the narrative.

If there is one area where Kingdom's Reign falls short, it is in the pacing. At times, the story feels a bit rushed, with key plot points resolved a bit too conveniently. However, this is a minor quibble in an otherwise engaging and well-crafted narrative.

In conclusion, Kingdom's Reign is a gripping tale of good vs. evil, with a richly detailed world and complex characters. Black's writing style is engaging, and his exploration of the theme of pride and greed is thought-provoking. While the pacing may be a bit uneven at times, the book is a compelling addition to the Kingdom series.
