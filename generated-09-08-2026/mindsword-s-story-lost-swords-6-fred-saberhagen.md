# Mindsword's Story (Lost Swords, #6)

**Author:** Fred Saberhagen  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

Fred Saberhagen’s *Mindsword’s Story*, the sixth installment in his celebrated *Lost Swords* series, demonstrates once again why the author remains a master of subverting high fantasy tropes. Published in 2011, this novel delves deep into the corrupting nature of absolute power, examining how human vulnerability distorts even the most noble intentions. While lesser fantasy series might rely on endless battles and simplistic good-versus-evil dynamics, Saberhagen crafts a narrative driven by psychological tension, moral ambiguity, and the terrifying allure of divine weaponry.

At the heart of the novel is the Mindsword, a Blade possessing the devastating ability to compel blind, absolute devotion from anyone within its influence. Saberhagen uses this artifact not merely as a magical plot device, but as a brilliant metaphor for obsession, manipulation, and the intoxicating nature of control. The premise is deceptively straightforward: Murat of Culm, aided by the chaotic influence of another legendary Blade, Coinspinner, recovers the Mindsword. Seeking to make amends for past transgressions and win the genuine affection of the striking Princess Kristin of Tasavalta, Murat resolves to keep the weapon safely sheathed. However, as any seasoned reader of Saberhagen knows, humanity and absolute power rarely coexist peacefully. The noble simplicity of Murat’s plan quickly unravels, poisoned by the ambitions of his advisors and the unpredictable desires of his own son.

Simultaneously, the narrative tracks Prince Mark, Kristin’s consort, whose prolonged absence in service to his father has strained his domestic life to a breaking point. Mark must now fight a desperate multi-front war—not just for his kingdom and his loyal friends, but for the heart and soul of his wife. Saberhagen brilliantly weaves these parallel struggles together, creating a tapestry of political intrigue and emotional turmoil. The true conflict in *Mindsword’s Story* is rarely about clashing steel; rather, it is fought in the quiet spaces of doubt, jealousy, and the desperate yearning for absolution. 

Saberhagen’s authorial style shines through his trademark pacing and sharply drawn characters. He avoids the purple prose that often bogs down epic fantasy, favoring a lean, propulsive narrative style that keeps the pages turning. He treats his characters with a profound empathy, refusing to divide them neatly into heroes and villains. Murat is neither a saint nor a monster, but a deeply flawed man trying to balance guilt with romantic aspiration, making his descent into complication all the more compelling. Similarly, Mark’s struggles ground the high-stakes magical elements in deeply relatable human emotions—neglect, love, loyalty, and betrayal. 

The integration of the Twelve Swords of Power has always been Saberhagen’s signature achievement, and the Mindsword stands out as one of his most psychologically complex creations. The terror of the Blade lies not in physical destruction, but in the theft of free will. By exploring how easily people surrender their autonomy—or seek to strip it from others—Saberhagen elevates *Mindsword’s Story* from a standard sword-and-sorcery adventure into a thought-provoking exploration of power dynamics and human relationships. 

For longtime fans of the *Lost Swords* universe, this novel is a welcome, twist-filled continuation that enriches the overarching mythos. For newcomers willing to jump into the middle of a sprawling saga, it stands as a testament to Saberhagen’s narrative prowess, offering a taut, intelligent fantasy experience where the greatest battles are fought within the human heart.
