# Hawkwood and the Kings (Monarchies of God #1-2)

**Author:** Paul Kearney  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

In the realm of epic fantasy, few authors have managed to craft a world as immersive and intricate as Paul Kearney's Monarchies of God series. Hawkwood and the Kings, a compilation of the first two books, Hawkwood's Voyage and The Heretic Kings, is a testament to Kearney's masterful storytelling and world-building prowess.

At its core, Hawkwood and the Kings is a tale of survival, rebellion, and the unyielding pursuit of power. The Western World is ablaze, as the Inceptines' ruthless crusade against heresy leaves destruction in its wake. Amidst the chaos, Richard Hawkwood, a grizzled sea captain, embarks on a perilous journey to transport refugees to the uncharted lands across the Great Western Ocean. This desperate quest serves as a catalyst for the events that unfold, weaving together the fates of Hawkwood and his crew with those of the Ramusian Kings, who must navigate the treacherous landscape of intrigue, excommunication, and civil war.

One of Kearney's greatest strengths as an author lies in his ability to balance the grand scope of his world with the intimate, personal struggles of his characters. The narrative is layered with complex moralities, as the lines between good and evil are constantly blurred. The Ramusian Kings, Lofantyr, Abeleyn, and Mark, are multidimensional figures, driven by a mix of noble and self-serving motivations. Their struggles to maintain power and survive the pontiff's purge serve as a powerful backdrop to the story, highlighting the brutal realities of war and the corrupting influence of power.

Kearney's prose is economical and evocative, conjuring vivid images of the world he has created. His writing is free from the flowery language and meandering descriptions that often plague epic fantasy novels. Instead, he opts for a more direct, visceral approach, which serves to heighten the sense of urgency and tension that pervades the narrative.

The world-building in Hawkwood and the Kings is nothing short of remarkable. Kearney's vision of a sprawling, medieval-inspired landscape is meticulously detailed, with a deep history that underpins the events of the story. The monastery city of Charibon, with its ancient traditions and mysterious artifacts, serves as a fascinating hub of learning and discovery, while the fallen Holy City of Aekir stands as a haunting testament to the devastating consequences of war.

Throughout the narrative, Kearney explores themes of faith, power, and the corrupting influence of both. The Inceptines' crusade against heresy serves as a stark reminder of the dangers of blind fanaticism, while the struggles of the Ramusian Kings highlight the difficulties of maintaining power in a world torn apart by conflict and intrigue.

In conclusion, Hawkwood and the Kings is a gripping and immersive epic fantasy that will appeal to fans of George R.R. Martin, Joe Abercrombie, and Mark Lawrence. Kearney's masterful storytelling, coupled with his vivid world-building and complex characters, makes for a compelling read that will keep you on the edge of your seat. With its richly detailed world, morally complex characters, and gripping narrative, Hawkwood and the Kings is an absolute must-read for anyone who loves epic fantasy.
