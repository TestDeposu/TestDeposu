# Three Trials (The Dark Side, #2)

**Author:** Kristy Cunning  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Unknown

**Three Trials: A Haunting Descent into Guilt, Redemption, and the Shadows of the Past**

Kristy Cunning’s *Three Trials*—the sequel to *The Dark Side*—is a chilling, psychologically layered thriller that strips away the veneer of innocence to expose the brutal, inescapable weight of guilt. Unlike many supernatural or mystery novels that rely on jump scares or contrived twists, Cunning crafts a story that lingers in the mind long after the final page, not through shock value, but through the raw, unflinching exploration of human frailty. This is a book that doesn’t just tell a story; it *unravels* one, peeling back layers of deception, moral ambiguity, and the haunting consequences of choices made in the name of survival—or worse, out of fear.

At its core, *Three Trials* is a meditation on the nature of justice, both divine and self-inflicted. The narrative follows characters who are trapped in a cycle of punishment, their sins—real or imagined—haunting them like spectral echoes. Cunning’s strength lies in her ability to blur the line between victim and perpetrator, forcing readers to question who truly deserves redemption and who is irredeemable. The prose is sharp, almost clinical in its precision, yet laced with a creeping dread that mirrors the psychological torment of her protagonists. There’s a deliberate restraint in the writing, a refusal to indulge in melodrama, which makes the moments of raw emotion all the more devastating.

The author’s style is a masterclass in atmospheric tension. Cunning doesn’t rely on overt horror tropes; instead, she builds unease through subtle details—the way a character’s reflection doesn’t quite match their movements, the way a voice whispers from an empty room, the way guilt manifests as a physical ache. This is a story where the scariest moments aren’t the monsters lurking in the dark, but the ones hiding in the corners of the human psyche. The pacing is meticulous, with each revelation coming at a calculated moment, ensuring that the reader is never left with false comfort. By the time the final confrontation arrives, it feels inevitable, a culmination of all the psychological and moral battles that came before.

What sets *Three Trials* apart is its refusal to offer easy answers. Cunning doesn’t shy away from the uncomfortable truth that some sins are too deep to forgive, and some people are too broken to save. The characters are flawed, often despicable, and their journeys are less about redemption and more about acceptance—of their own complicity, of the sins they’ve committed, and of the darkness they’ve unleashed. This isn’t a story where the good guys win; it’s a story where the survivors are left to grapple with the cost of their victories.

Themes of fate and free will are woven throughout, with characters caught in a web of their own making. Is their suffering punishment, or is it simply the natural consequence of their actions? Cunning challenges the reader to sit in the discomfort of these questions, to confront the idea that sometimes, the only justice is the one we impose upon ourselves. The result is a narrative that feels both deeply personal and universally relatable, a story about the shadows we carry and the price we pay for ignoring them.

For fans of psychological thrillers that prioritize depth over spectacle, *Three Trials* is a must-read. It’s a book that will unsettle you, haunt you, and leave you questioning the nature of guilt long after you’ve turned the last page. Kristy Cunning doesn’t just tell a story—she dissects the soul, and the experience is as visceral as it is thought-provoking.
