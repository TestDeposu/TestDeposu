# Angelbound (Angelbound Origins #1)

**Author:** Christina Bauer  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

**Angelbound: A Defiant Spark in the Shadows of Purgatory**

Christina Bauer’s *Angelbound* isn’t just a fantasy novel—it’s a rebellion in ink. Set in the morally gray underbelly of Purgatory, where the rules of Heaven and Hell are as fluid as the souls trapped between them, this debut novel carves out a space for a protagonist who refuses to be defined by the world’s expectations. Myla Lewis, a quasi-demon with a sharp tongue and sharper claws, is the kind of heroine who doesn’t just fight for survival—she fights *against* it, dismantling the very systems that seek to control her. Bauer’s world is rich with political intrigue, supernatural tension, and a love story that burns as fiercely as it complicates, making *Angelbound* a standout in the growing canon of YA fantasy that dares to be both dark and deeply human.

At its core, *Angelbound* is a story about identity, rebellion, and the cost of defiance. Myla has spent her life in Purgatory’s arena, a gladiator of souls where she battles for the right to enter the afterlife—or at least, that’s what she’s been told. But when the demons around her begin acting unnaturally, and the King of the Demons himself descends into her high school, Myla’s carefully constructed world starts to crack. The novel’s strength lies in its slow unraveling of truths: Why were the quasi-demons turned into slave labor? What really happened seventeen years ago? And why does her mother’s sorrow feel like a wound that won’t heal? Bauer weaves these questions into the fabric of the story, ensuring that Myla’s journey isn’t just about fighting—it’s about *knowing*. The revelation that Myla’s father is none other than Armageddon himself is a masterstroke, turning personal vendetta into a cosmic conflict. It’s a twist that elevates the stakes, forcing Myla to confront not just the demons hunting her, but the demons within her own bloodline.

Bauer’s prose is deft, blending the gritty realism of a teen protagonist with the lush, almost mythic quality of a world where angels and demons walk the same halls as humans. The dialogue crackles with wit and bite, particularly in Myla’s exchanges with Lincoln, the High Prince of the Thrax—a demon hunter who is, in many ways, the novel’s most compelling antagonist. Their chemistry is electric, charged with tension that goes beyond the romantic. Lincoln represents everything Myla is supposed to hate: a hunter, a symbol of the oppression that has shaped her life. Yet, their relationship is far from a simple love triangle. It’s a dance of power, trust, and betrayal, where attraction and antagonism collide in a way that feels raw and authentic. Bauer doesn’t shy away from the moral ambiguity of their dynamic, making their bond feel earned rather than forced.

The world-building in *Angelbound* is another standout feature. Purgatory isn’t just a place—it’s a character in its own right, a liminal space where the rules of Heaven and Hell are negotiated in blood and lies. The school setting is a brilliant twist, grounding the supernatural in the familiar. Purgatory High is less a typical teen school and more a microcosm of the larger conflict: a place where quasi-demons are treated as second-class citizens, where the line between friend and foe is as thin as a whispered secret. Bauer’s attention to detail—from the hierarchy of the demons to the political maneuvering of the angels—creates a world that feels lived-in and layered. It’s a setting that invites readers to ask: *What would I do if I were trapped here?*

Yet, for all its strengths, *Angelbound* isn’t without its challenges. The pacing in the early chapters can feel a bit sluggish, as Bauer takes time to establish Myla’s world and her relationships. Some readers might find the buildup to the larger conflict a bit slow, especially if they’re craving immediate action. However, this isn’t a flaw—it’s a deliberate choice. Bauer wants readers to *feel* the weight of Myla’s isolation before she’s thrust into the chaos. By the time the novel’s second act kicks in, the payoff is all the more satisfying.

Thematically, *Angelbound* is a celebration of defiance in the face of oppression. Myla isn’t just fighting for herself—she’s fighting for her people, for the quasi-demons who have been erased from history. Her arc is one of reclaiming agency, of refusing to be a pawn in someone else’s game. This resonates deeply in a world where marginalized voices are often silenced or sidelined. Bauer doesn’t offer easy answers, but she does offer a protagonist who refuses to accept them. Myla’s journey is one of questioning, of challenging the narratives she’s been fed, and of finding her own truth in the process.

Lincoln, meanwhile, serves as a fascinating counterpoint to Myla’s defiance. As a demon hunter, he’s the embodiment of the system that has oppressed quasi-demons for generations. Yet, his own past is shrouded in mystery, and his motivations are far from pure. Their relationship is a study in contrasts—Myla is impulsive and emotional; Lincoln is calculated and controlled. Their dynamic is one of the novel’s most compelling elements, as it forces both characters to confront their own biases and desires. When Lincoln’s past is finally revealed, it’s a moment that redefines the entire story, turning a simple love story into a battle for survival on multiple fronts.

Visually, *Angelbound* would be a stunning adaptation. Bauer’s world is ripe for the cinematic treatment, with its blend of high school drama and supernatural warfare. The arena fights, the political intrigue, and the slow-burn romance all lend themselves to a visually rich narrative. The contrast between the mundane and the monstrous—teachers who are actually angels, students who are demons in disguise—creates a sense of unease that lingers long after the last page.

Ultimately, *Angelbound* is a novel that refuses to be contained. It’s a story about breaking rules, about questioning the stories we’re told, and about the power of defiance in a world that seeks to control you. Myla Lewis is a heroine who doesn’t just fight for her place in the world—she fights to *reshape* it. In an era of fantasy that often prioritizes spectacle over substance, Bauer’s debut stands out for its emotional depth, its moral complexity, and its unapologetic protagonist. It’s a book that doesn’t just entertain—it *challenges*.

For readers who love fantasy with heart, rebellion, and a dash of supernatural intrigue, *Angelbound* is a must-read. It’s a story that will leave you questioning the world around you, and the stories you’ve been told about yourself.
