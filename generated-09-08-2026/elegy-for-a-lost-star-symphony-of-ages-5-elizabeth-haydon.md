# Elegy for a Lost Star (Symphony of Ages, #5)

**Author:** Elizabeth Haydon  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

"Elegy for a Lost Star" by Elizabeth Haydon is a masterful continuation of the Symphony of Ages series, weaving a narrative that propels the reader towards a catastrophic war that will shake the very foundations of the world. At the heart of this epic tale lies the theme of vengeance, a potent force that threatens to consume all in its path. The resurrection of the dragon Anwyn, a creature bound to the earth with unyielding hatred for Rhapsody, sets the stage for a confrontation that will test the bonds of friendship and loyalty among the Three - Rhapsody, Achylies, and Elyndor.

Haydon's skill as a storyteller is evident in the way she deftly weaves together multiple plot threads, each of which contributes to the escalating chaos that is to come. From the rebuilding of the Firbolg king's shattered home to the machinations of a ruthless despot, the sense of foreboding is palpable. Meanwhile, the stakes are raised with the introduction of a horribly deformed but magical being, imbued with an otherworldly power that will have far-reaching implications for the course of history. As the birth of the Child of Time draws near, the balance of power begins to shift, and the seeds of conflict are sown for a battle that will leave half of the world in ruin.

One of Haydon's greatest strengths as a writer is her ability to craft characters that are at once complex and relatable. Anwyn's transformation from a being trapped in a state of deathlike sleep to a creature consumed by hatred is a testament to Haydon's skill in creating nuanced and multidimensional characters. Achmed, the Firbolg king, is equally compelling, his efforts to rebuild his home a poignant reminder of the human cost of conflict. The bond between Rhapsody, Achylies, and Elyndor is put to the test as they navigate the treacherous waters of politics and war.

Throughout the book, Haydon's writing is characterized by its lyricism and imagination. Her world-building is rich and detailed, with a depth and complexity that is rare in fantasy literature. The writing itself is evocative and engaging, drawing the reader into a world of wonder and magic. The dialogue is sharp and witty, adding to the sense of tension and urgency that pervades the narrative.

If there is one weakness to the book, it is in the complexity of the plot. At times, the sheer number of subplots and characters can make it difficult to keep track of what is happening. However, this is a minor quibble in what is otherwise a masterful work of fantasy.

In conclusion, "Elegy for a Lost Star" is a gripping and epic tale that is sure to captivate fans of the series. Haydon's writing is engaging and imaginative, and her characters are complex and relatable. As the series hurtles towards its conclusion, the stakes are raised, and the tension builds towards a climactic confrontation that will be nothing short of monumental.
