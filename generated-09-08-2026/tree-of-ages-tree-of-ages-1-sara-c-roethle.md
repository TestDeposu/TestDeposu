# Tree of Ages (Tree of Ages, #1)

**Author:** Sara C. Roethle  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

**A Gritty, Glamorous Descent into the Shadows: *Tree of Ages* by Sara C. Roethle**

Sara C. Roethle’s *Tree of Ages* is a fresh, electrifying entry into the urban fantasy genre—a story that blends the raw, visceral energy of supernatural conflict with the sharp, self-aware wit of a protagonist who’s just as likely to question her own sanity as she is to take down a werewolf. At its heart, this novel is a tale of survival, betrayal, and the messy, often painful awakening of power, wrapped in a world where the line between hero and monster is as thin as a silver blade.

Xoe, our reluctant heroine, is the kind of protagonist we root for because she’s flawed in all the right ways. She’s not a chosen one with a destiny preordained—she’s a girl who stumbles into chaos, forced to confront the fact that her life, her family, and even her own body are far more complicated than she ever imagined. When her best friend’s life hangs in the balance, Xoe doesn’t just leap into action; she *adapts*, improvises, and sometimes, she fails spectacularly. Her journey isn’t about becoming invincible—it’s about learning to fight dirty, to trust (or not trust) the people around her, and to accept that some wounds run deeper than skin.

Roethle’s writing is a delightful mix of grit and glamour. The supernatural elements—werewolves, vampires, demons—are handled with a deft hand, avoiding the clichés that often bog down the genre. Instead, the world feels lived-in, with rules that are as brutal as they are logical. The werewolves aren’t just mindless beasts; they’re creatures of instinct and rage, their transformations as visceral as they are terrifying. The vampires, meanwhile, are more than just brooding seducers—they’re predators with their own hierarchies and secrets, and Xoe’s interactions with them, particularly with the enigmatic Jason, crackle with tension. Roethle doesn’t shy away from the darker aspects of these creatures, making their presence feel both thrilling and unsettling.

What truly sets *Tree of Ages* apart, though, is its focus on the *cost* of power. Xoe’s newfound abilities come with a price—physical, emotional, and moral—and Roethle doesn’t sugarcoat it. The novel explores themes of identity, sacrifice, and the blurred line between saving someone and becoming what you hate. There’s a raw honesty to Xoe’s struggles, a refusal to let her become a caricature of the "chosen one" trope. She’s not a warrior from the start; she’s a girl who learns to fight, and the process is messy, painful, and sometimes humiliating.

Roethle’s prose is sharp and engaging, with a knack for vivid descriptions that bring the world to life. The dialogue is snappy, the pacing is tight, and the world-building is immersive without being overwhelming. The novel balances action with introspection, giving Xoe’s internal monologues room to breathe while keeping the external threats ever-present. It’s a story that feels both grounded and fantastical, a rare blend that makes the supernatural elements feel tangible rather than fantastical.

One of the strongest aspects of *Tree of Ages* is its portrayal of the supernatural community. Roethle doesn’t treat these creatures as mere villains or love interests; they’re complex, flawed individuals with their own agendas, fears, and motivations. This adds depth to the story, making the conflicts feel more personal and the stakes higher. The relationships Xoe forms—with allies and enemies alike—are compelling, and the novel doesn’t shy away from exploring the trust (or lack thereof) that defines them.

In the end, *Tree of Ages* is a story about growing up in a world that’s far more dangerous than the one Xoe ever knew. It’s about the choices we make when faced with impossible odds, the people we trust, and the monsters we become in the process. Roethle’s debut is a fresh, gripping entry into urban fantasy, one that stands out for its boldness, its honesty, and its refusal to let its protagonist be anything less than human—even when she’s fighting against forces that are anything but.

If you’re looking for a supernatural tale that’s as much about the journey as it is about the battles, *Tree of Ages* delivers. It’s a story that will keep you on the edge of your seat, rooting for Xoe even as she questions everything she thought she knew. And in a genre often dominated by clichés, Roethle’s unique voice makes this book a standout.
