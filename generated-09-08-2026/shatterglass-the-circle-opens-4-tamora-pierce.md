# Shatterglass (The Circle Opens, #4)

**Author:** Tamora Pierce  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

In "Shatterglass," the fourth installment of The Circle Opens series, Tamora Pierce weaves a captivating tale of magic, mystery, and self-discovery. Set in a world where magic is an integral part of everyday life, the story follows Kethlun Warder, a young glassmaker whose life is forever changed after a freak accident infuses his glass magic with lightning. As Keth struggles to control his newfound powers, he must also confront his volatile temper, making him a challenging student for Tris, a skilled magician tasked with teaching him.

Pierce's writing style is as engaging as ever, with vivid descriptions of the world and its magical inhabitants. Her ability to craft relatable characters is once again on display, as Keth's frustrations and fears make him a sympathetic protagonist. Tris, as his teacher and mentor, provides a perfect counterbalance to Keth's impulsiveness, and their interactions are filled with warmth and humor.

One of the main themes of "Shatterglass" is the struggle for self-control and self-acceptance. Keth's journey is not just about mastering his magic, but also about coming to terms with his new identity and finding his place in the world. As he navigates the complexities of his powers, he must also confront the darkness within himself and learn to trust others. This theme is echoed in the mystery that unfolds, as Keth and Tris work together to uncover the truth behind a series of murders.

The mystery itself is expertly crafted, with Pierce dropping subtle clues and red herrings throughout the story. The pacing is well-balanced, with a mix of action, suspense, and quieter moments of character development. The world-building is, as always, meticulous and immersive, with a richly detailed magical system that adds depth and complexity to the narrative.

What sets "Shatterglass" apart from other fantasy novels is its thoughtful exploration of the consequences of power and the importance of responsibility. Keth's magic is not just a tool for solving the mystery, but also a metaphor for the dangers of unchecked power and the need for self-control. Pierce's message is clear: with great power comes great responsibility, and it is up to the individual to wield that power wisely.

Overall, "Shatterglass" is a compelling and engaging read, with a richly detailed world, complex characters, and a gripping mystery. Pierce's writing is, as always, engaging and accessible, making this book a must-read for fans of fantasy and mystery alike.
