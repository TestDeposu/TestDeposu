# The Song of the Quarkbeast (The Last Dragonslayer, #2)

**Author:** Jasper Fforde  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

In the enchanting world of Jasper Fforde's "The Song of the Quarkbeast", the second installment of "The Last Dragonslayer" series, magic is dwindling, and the Ununited Kingdom is on the cusp of a catastrophic transformation. This captivating narrative is a masterclass in Fforde's signature blend of wit, satire, and fantastical adventure, tailor-made for young readers.

At the heart of this mesmerizing tale is Jennifer Strange, a resourceful and determined fifteen-year-old who manages an employment agency for magicians. As the magic industry struggles to stay afloat, Jennifer's agency is the lifeline that keeps the sorcerers employed, even if it means taking on mundane tasks such as delivering pizzas by magic carpet or rewiring houses. Fforde's clever world-building shines through in these details, offering a humorous commentary on the commercialization of magic and the decline of a once-great art.

However, Jennifer's world is turned upside down when she begins experiencing visions that foretell the demise of the world's last dragon at the hands of an unnamed Dragonslayer. As the prophecy unfolds, all signs point to Jennifer herself being the chosen one, destined to wield Big Magic and seal the fate of the dragon. This central plot thread expertly explores the theme of destiny vs. free will, leaving readers pondering the consequences of Jennifer's actions and the true nature of her powers.

Fforde's writing style is a true delight, effortlessly balancing humor, action, and heart. His prose is peppered with clever wordplay, witty one-liners, and absurd situations that will leave readers grinning from ear to ear. The world-building is equally impressive, with a richly detailed landscape that is both fantastical and eerily familiar. The Ununited Kingdom, with its eccentric sorcerers, bizarre creatures, and magical mishaps, is a place where the ordinary and the extraordinary coexist in a swirling vortex of chaos.

One of the most striking aspects of "The Song of the Quarkbeast" is its ability to tackle complex themes and issues with a lighthearted touch. Fforde's exploration of the decline of magic serves as a clever allegory for the consequences of neglecting our environment, our traditions, and our sense of wonder. Through Jennifer's journey, we see the importance of embracing our unique talents and passions, even in the face of adversity.

In conclusion, "The Song of the Quarkbeast" is a captivating and hilarious fantasy adventure that will appeal to readers of all ages. With its unique blend of humor, magic, and heart, this novel is a must-read for fans of Jasper Fforde and anyone looking for a story that will transport them to a world of wonder and excitement.
