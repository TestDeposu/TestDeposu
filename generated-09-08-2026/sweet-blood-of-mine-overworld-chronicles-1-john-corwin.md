# Sweet Blood of Mine (Overworld Chronicles, #1)

**Author:** John Corwin  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

**Sweet Blood of Mine: A Snarky Hero’s Redemption in a World Gone to Hell**

John Corwin’s *Sweet Blood of Mine*—the first installment in the *Overworld Chronicles*—is a fresh, fast-paced fantasy romp that blends the humor of *The Secret History* with the high-stakes tension of *The Dark Tower*. At its core, this series is a story about identity, belonging, and the messy, often painful journey of becoming something greater than you ever imagined. But what sets it apart isn’t just its supernatural intrigue; it’s the razor-sharp wit of its protagonist, Justin Case, a demon spawn who’s equal parts lovable and infuriating—a nerdy, sarcastic everyman thrust into a world where he’s both the key to salvation and the most hated creature on Earth.

Justin isn’t your typical fantasy hero. He’s not a chosen one with a noble lineage; he’s a guy who spent his life obsessing over *Dungeons & Dragons* and pretending to be cool in high school. When he discovers he’s something *terrible*—a demon spawn with powers that make him a walking disaster—he’s less excited than he is horrified. The world of the *Overworld Chronicles* is a sprawling, dangerous place where vampires, lycans, sorcerers, and Templars (the holy knights of the supernatural world) all coexist in uneasy tension. And Justin? He’s the one thing they all want to destroy. The problem? He might be the only one who can stop the coming apocalypse.

Corwin’s writing is a delightful mix of sharp dialogue and dark humor, grounding the supernatural chaos in relatable, flawed characters. Justin’s voice is so distinct—equal parts self-deprecating and fiercely determined—that you can’t help but root for him, even when he’s being an idiot. His relationship with Elyssa, a Daemas (a type of angelic being) who’s sworn to kill him, is the emotional heart of the series. Their dynamic is electric, a perfect balance of tension, banter, and genuine care. It’s the kind of romance that feels earned, not forced, where the stakes are high enough that you’ll cheer when they finally get their happy ending.

The *Overworld Chronicles* also excels in world-building. Corwin doesn’t just drop readers into a fantasy realm; he makes it feel alive, with its own politics, factions, and hidden dangers. The Templars, in particular, are a fascinating force—religious zealots who see Justin as both a blight and a weapon, willing to burn the world to cleanse it of his kind. Meanwhile, the Daemas, the angels of the series, add a layer of divine intrigue, with their own agendas and moral ambiguities. The world feels vast and layered, with each book in the set (*Dark Light of Mine*, *Fallen Angel of Mine*, *Dread Nemesis of Mine*) introducing new threats, new allies, and deeper layers to the conflict.

What makes *Sweet Blood of Mine* stand out, though, is its willingness to embrace the messiness of its hero. Justin isn’t a paragon of virtue; he’s impulsive, stubborn, and often his own worst enemy. But that’s what makes him compelling. He’s not a hero because he’s perfect—he’s a hero because he *chooses* to be one, despite every reason not to. His growth isn’t about losing his quirks; it’s about learning to wield them. And that’s a rare and refreshing take in fantasy, where protagonists are often either flawless or irredeemable.

The series also plays with genre tropes in clever ways. The *Overworld Chronicles* isn’t just a typical demon-vs-angels story; it’s a tale of survival, redemption, and the cost of power. The villains aren’t just mindless monsters—they’re complex, motivated by their own beliefs and traumas. Even the rogue vampire Maximus, who kidnaps Justin in *Dread Nemesis of Mine*, feels like a full character, not just a one-dimensional threat. Corwin doesn’t shy away from the darker aspects of fantasy—betrayal, sacrifice, and the blurred line between hero and villain—but he balances it with enough humor and heart to keep the story from feeling too grim.

If there’s one criticism to be made, it’s that the pacing in some sections can feel uneven, particularly in the later books where the plot sometimes races ahead of character development. But this is a minor quibble in an otherwise engaging series. The world is rich, the characters are memorable, and the stakes are always high. By the time you reach the end of *Dread Nemesis of Mine*, you’ll be left wanting more—not just because the story is gripping, but because Justin’s journey feels *complete*.

*Sweet Blood of Mine* is more than just a fantasy adventure; it’s a story about finding your place in a world that wants to erase you. It’s about love that defies logic, about friendships forged in fire, and about the courage it takes to stand up when everyone else is running. And most importantly, it’s about a hero who isn’t born—he’s made. In a genre often dominated by chosen ones and prophecy, Justin Case is a breath of fresh air, a reminder that even the most unlikely of us can rise to the occasion.

If you’re looking for a fantasy series that’s equal parts funny, heartfelt, and action-packed, with a protagonist you’ll love to hate (and then love all over again), then *Sweet Blood of Mine* is the perfect choice. Just don’t expect a perfect hero—expect a mess, a miracle, and a story you won’t soon forget.
