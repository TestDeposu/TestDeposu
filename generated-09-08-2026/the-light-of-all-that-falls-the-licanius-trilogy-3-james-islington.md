# The Light of All That Falls (The Licanius Trilogy, #3)

**Author:** James Islington  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

In the grand tradition of epic fantasy, James Islington's Licanius Trilogy has been a behemoth of storytelling, weaving intricate plot threads, complex characters, and a sprawling world into a rich tapestry that has captivated readers. The final installment, The Light of All That Falls, is a masterclass in concluding a series, bringing together the various narrative strands in a satisfying and emotionally resonant climax.

At its core, The Light of All That Falls is a story about the nature of power, sacrifice, and the consequences of one's actions. As the forces of darkness gather for a final assault on the land of Andarra, the characters must confront their own demons and make impossible choices that will determine the fate of their world. Islington's exploration of these themes is nuanced and thought-provoking, raising questions about the morality of leadership, the cost of heroism, and the true meaning of sacrifice.

One of the standout features of Islington's writing is his ability to craft compelling characters, each with their own distinct voice and arc. Davian, Wirr, and Caeden, the trilogy's central protagonists, are complex and multi-dimensional, with rich inner lives and motivations that drive the plot forward. The supporting cast is equally well-developed, with even minor characters receiving careful attention and backstory.

Islington's world-building is another highlight of the series. The land of Andarra is meticulously crafted, with a deep history and politics that underpin the narrative. The magical system, based on a complex interplay of forces and energies, is similarly well-realized, with a clear set of rules and limitations that add depth and coherence to the story.

The pacing of The Light of All That Falls is relentless, with a narrative that hurtles towards its conclusion with barely a pause for breath. Islington's prose is descriptive and immersive, conjuring vivid images of the world and its characters. The action scenes are intense and well-choreographed, with a clear sense of tension and urgency.

If you're a fan of epic fantasy, particularly the works of Robert Jordan, Patrick Rothfuss, or Brandon Sanderson, then The Licanius Trilogy is a must-read. Islington's writing is reminiscent of these authors, with a similar focus on complex world-building, nuanced characters, and intricate plotting. The Light of All That Falls is a fitting conclusion to the series, bringing together the various narrative threads in a satisfying and emotionally resonant climax.

In short, The Light of All That Falls is a triumph of epic fantasy, a conclusion to the Licanius Trilogy that will leave readers breathless and satisfied. With its complex characters, nuanced themes, and intricate world-building, this series is sure to become a new favorite among fans of the genre.
