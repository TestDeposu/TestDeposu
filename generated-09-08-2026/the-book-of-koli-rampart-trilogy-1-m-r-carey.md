# The Book of Koli (Rampart Trilogy #1)

**Author:** M.R. Carey  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

**The Book of Koli: A Haunting, Heartbreaking Masterpiece of Survival and Sacrifice**

M.R. Carey’s *The Book of Koli*—the first installment in the *Rampart Trilogy*—is not just a story; it is a visceral, soul-shaking exploration of what it means to be human in a world that has turned against you. Set in a post-apocalyptic wasteland where nature itself has become a predator, the novel follows Koli, a boy who has spent his entire life believing the only rule worth knowing is this: *Never step beyond the walls of Mythen Rood.* But when the unthinkable happens—his village is overrun, his family is lost, and he is left to fend for himself—Koli must confront a brutal truth: *Everything that lives hates us.* And in this world, survival is not just about endurance; it is about the cost of being alive.

Carey’s prose is a masterclass in restraint and raw emotion. He doesn’t rely on flashy descriptions or overblown action; instead, he strips the narrative down to its essential, aching humanity. The world of *The Book of Koli* is a place where the land itself is hostile—choker trees with seeds that burst into deadly thorns, forests that whisper with unseen dangers, and the ever-present threat of the "shunned men," outcasts who will kill without hesitation. Yet, for all its bleakness, the novel never descends into nihilism. Carey balances horror with quiet moments of tenderness, particularly in his portrayal of Koli’s bond with his younger sister, Lira. Their relationship is the emotional core of the story, a beacon of love in a world that seeks to destroy everything it touches.

The novel’s strength lies in its themes of isolation and resilience. Koli is not a hero in the traditional sense; he is a survivor, shaped by loss and forced to make choices that will haunt him forever. Carey doesn’t offer easy answers or neat resolutions. Instead, he asks: *What would you do if the world had already condemned you?* The answer is never simple, and that’s what makes *The Book of Koli* so compelling. The journey Koli embarks upon is as perilous as it is enlightening, forcing him—and the reader—to question the nature of morality, family, and the will to endure.

Style-wise, Carey’s writing is deceptively simple. His sentences are sharp and precise, delivering each line with the weight of a blade. There’s a poetic quality to his descriptions, particularly when he paints the desolation of the wasteland or the quiet desperation of Koli’s internal monologue. Yet, beneath the surface, there’s a deep sincerity—a refusal to romanticize suffering, even as he makes the reader care deeply for his characters. This is not a story about triumph; it’s about the struggle to remain human in the face of annihilation.

The novel’s pacing is impeccable. Carey builds tension with surgical precision, ensuring that every moment feels urgent, every decision carries consequence. The reveal of the world’s true horrors is not just shocking—it’s devastating, and it sets the stage for the epic saga that follows. *The Book of Koli* is not just the start of a trilogy; it’s the beginning of a journey that will test the reader’s patience, their empathy, and their willingness to face the darkest corners of the human spirit.

For those who loved *The Girl with All the Gifts* or *Station Eleven*, *The Book of Koli* will resonate deeply. It’s a story about the fragility of life, the cruelty of fate, and the quiet acts of defiance that keep us going. Carey’s work is a reminder that even in the most desolate of worlds, there is still room for hope—though it may come at a price too heavy to bear.

If you’re looking for a book that will stay with you long after the last page, one that is as beautiful as it is brutal, then *The Book of Koli* is essential reading. It’s a testament to Carey’s storytelling prowess, a haunting fable about survival, and a story that will leave you breathless.
