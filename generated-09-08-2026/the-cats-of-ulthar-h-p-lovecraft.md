# The Cats of Ulthar

**Author:** H.P. Lovecraft  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

Feline Vengeance in the Shadow of Cosmic Indifference

H.P. Lovecraft is globally canonized as the master of cosmic horror, the architect of gelatinous nightmares, and the chronicler of human insignificance in the face of ancient, star-spawned deities. Yet, tucked away in the prolific machinery of his imagination lies a peculiar and delightful anomaly: a dark fairy tale that reverses his usual dynamic of helpless humans devoured by monsters, instead unleashing a merciless furry horde upon human cruelty. Written in June 1920, The Cats of Ulthar stands as a testament to the author's lifelong, ardent adoration of felines, transforming a personal obsession into a chilling, poetic myth of retribution.

The narrative unfolds through an unnamed narrator in a quaint, archaic town named Ulthar, a place where a bizarre and absolute law now reigns supreme: in Ulthar, no man may kill a cat. To understand the origins of this draconian statute, the reader is pulled backward into a grim tale involving an old cottager and his wife who possessed a sinister penchant for trapping and executing the neighborhood cats. The villagers, paralyzed by a superstitious and inexplicable fear of these mysterious creatures, dared not interfere. That is, until a caravan of exotic wanderers from the South—featuring a small, orphaned boy named Menes and his beloved black kitten—passed through the settlement. When the wicked old couple captures and murders the kitten, Menes unleashes a haunting, primal prayer to the heavens before the caravan departs. What follows is a nocturnal reckoning of mythic proportions, as every cat in Ulthar converges upon the cottage, exacting a silent, thorough, and terrifying justice.

Even in this brief, compact short story, Lovecraft’s signature stylistic brilliance shines with unmistakable radiance. Unlike the breathless, decaying madness of his later works like The Call of Cthulhu or At the Mountains of Madness, The Cats of Ulthar leans heavily into the cadence of a fable or a piece of ancient folklore. Lovecraft adopts a deliberately elevated, archaic tone—reminiscent of Lord Dunsany, who heavily influenced his early writing—which imbues the ordinary streets of Ulthar with an aura of timeless legend. Every sentence is meticulously weighed, rich with atmosphere, painting the foreign wanderers and the shadowy, creeping felines with a cinematic gravity that elevates a simple revenge tale into a grand moral reckoning.

Beneath the surface of this charmingly macabre anecdote lies a fascinating thematic exploration: the inherent power of the marginalized and the mysterious. To Lovecraft, cats were never mere domestic pets; they were enigmatic, alien beings possessed of secret knowledge and ancient dignity. In Ulthar, the villagers dismiss the cats as aloof oddities until their absence leaves a void filled with dread. When the felines finally rise, it is not with the chaotic rage of typical monsters, but with a terrifying, unified purpose that mimics the inexorable forces of nature itself. The story whispers a subtle warning to humanity: do not underestimate the silent watchers of the shadows, for they hold allegiance to powers far older and stranger than human laws.

For both longtime acolytes of the Mythos and newcomers dipping their toes into weird fiction, The Cats of Ulthar is an essential, bite-sized masterpiece. It offers a rare glimpse into the gentler, yet still delightfully dark, heart of Lovecraft’s universe—a place where cosmic justice wears whiskers, and the wicked do not escape the velvet-footed vengeance of the night.
