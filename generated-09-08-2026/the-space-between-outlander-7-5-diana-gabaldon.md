# The Space Between (Outlander, #7.5)

**Author:** Diana Gabaldon  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

Diana Gabaldon has long mastered the art of weaving dense historical tapestries with the ethereal threads of science fiction and romance. In her 2014 novella, The Space Between, set in the intricate universe of her acclaimed Outlander series, Gabaldon proves once again that her narrative prowess is just as potent in the short form as it is in her massive, multi-hundred-page tomes. Positioned chronologically as a bridge within the overarching saga, this self-contained story offers both longtime devotees and curious newcomers a masterclass in atmospheric storytelling, balancing the weight of history with the chilling whisper of the supernatural.

The primary theme of The Space Between is transformation, examined through the literal and figurative liminal spaces that separate individuals from their desires, their pasts, and their destinies. The narrative follows young Joan MacKimmie as she journeys toward Paris with the intention of taking vows as a nun. However, Joan’s flight into the sanctuary of the church is driven less by divine calling and more by mortal terror. Plagued by unsettling premonitions—voices that murmur of futures yet unwritten and visions that brand the soon-to-be-dead—Joan is a character trapped between the mundane world and a terrifying psychic reality. Her chaperone, the grieving widower Michael Murray, is similarly trapped in a state of suspension, mourning a lost spouse while unexpectedly finding his heart reawakened by his enigmatic charge. 

Gabaldon excels at exploring these emotional chasms. The "space" of the title is multifaceted: it represents the physical distance between Scotland and France, the generational divide between travelers of different eras, and the spiritual gulf between the living and the dead, time and eternity. Against this psychological backdrop, the author injects high-stakes peril with the re-emergence of the formidable, time-traveling antagonist, the Comte St. Germain. This collision of quiet, introspective character study and dark, looming menace elevates the novella from a mere transitional piece into a gripping, suspense-driven encounter.

True to her signature style, Gabaldon approaches historical fiction not as a dusty museum exhibit, but as a living, breathing sensory experience. Her prose is rich with period-accurate detail, capturing the sights, smells, and claustrophobic anxieties of eighteenth-century travel and Parisian intrigue. Yet, what truly anchors the narrative is Gabaldon's deep understanding of human vulnerability. She refuses to reduce her characters to mere pawns of the plot; instead, Michael and Joan's developing bond feels organic, tender, and earned, unfolding amidst the encroaching shadows of dark magic and historical turbulence. 

The pacing is brisk, a necessary constraint of the novella format, yet Gabaldon never sacrifices depth for speed. Every conversation carries subtext, and every scene hums with an undercurrent of suspense. The inclusion of elements tied to Claire Fraser—the legendary La Dame Blanche—serves as a delightful anchor for fans of the wider series, while remaining entirely accessible to those stepping into Gabaldon's world for the very first time. 

Ultimately, The Space Between is a triumph of mood and momentum. It reminds readers why Gabaldon remains a titan of historical fantasy. By shining a light on secondary characters who often operate on the periphery of the main narrative, she expands her universe horizontally and vertically, proving that even the smallest spaces in her fictional world are teeming with life, magic, and redemptive love.
