# The Scarab Path (Shadows of the Apt, #5)

**Author:** Adrian Tchaikovsky  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

The Scarab Path, the fifth installment in Adrian Tchaikovsky's sprawling Shadows of the Apt series, is a masterclass in epic fantasy storytelling. Set against the backdrop of a war-torn world, this novel delves into the complexities of power, loyalty, and the true cost of conflict.

Tchaikovsky's writing is akin to a rich tapestry, woven from threads of intricate world-building, complex characters, and pulse-pounding action. The Scarab Path is no exception, as the author expertly navigates the treacherous landscape of Collegium and the Wasp Empire. The war may have ended in a stalemate, but the consequences of the conflict are far from over. Cheerwell Maker, a brilliant and damaged protagonist, finds herself struggling to come to terms with the ghosts of her past, while the Wasp Empress seeks to reassert her dominance over the imperial cities.

One of the standout features of Tchaikovsky's writing is his ability to craft nuanced, multidimensional characters. Thalric, the former spymaster turned consort, is a prime example of this. His struggles with loyalty and power are deeply compelling, and his character serves as a perfect counterpoint to the Empress's ruthless ambition. As the story unfolds, it becomes clear that Thalric's knowledge of the Empress's true power is a ticking time bomb, waiting to unleash chaos upon the world.

Tchaikovsky's world-building is, as always, impeccable. The ancient city beyond the desert, with its secrets and mysteries, is a fascinating addition to the Shadows of the Apt universe. The author's use of mythology and folklore adds depth and richness to the narrative, drawing the reader into a world that is both familiar and yet, utterly alien.

The Scarab Path is also a novel about the consequences of war and the true cost of power. Tchaikovsky pulls no punches in his portrayal of the devastation and trauma that conflict inflicts upon individuals and societies. Cheerwell's struggles with her past are a powerful reminder of the long-lasting impact of war, and the difficulties of healing and recovery.

If there is one criticism to be made, it is that The Scarab Path can be a challenging read at times. Tchaikovsky's world is vast and complex, and the novel assumes a certain level of familiarity with the previous installments in the series. However, this is a minor quibble, and fans of epic fantasy will find themselves richly rewarded by the author's meticulous world-building and character development.

In conclusion, The Scarab Path is a gripping and thought-provoking addition to the Shadows of the Apt series. Tchaikovsky's writing is masterful, his characters are complex and nuanced, and his world-building is second to none. If you're a fan of epic fantasy, or simply looking for a compelling and immersive read, then The Scarab Path is an absolute must.
