# High Deryni (The Chronicles of the Deryni, #3)

**Author:** Katherine Kurtz  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

High Deryni, the third installment in Katherine Kurtz's iconic Chronicles of the Deryni series, is a masterful blend of fantasy, politics, and intrigue that will captivate fans of the genre. Published in 1973, this novel has stood the test of time, and its themes of prejudice, power struggles, and self-discovery remain as relevant today as they were upon its initial release.

At the heart of High Deryni is the struggle for acceptance and equality. The Deryni, a race of humans with extrasensory powers, have been marginalized and oppressed for centuries. The Church, fearing their abilities and viewing them as an abomination, has systematically persecuted the Deryni, forcing them to live in secret and hiding their true nature. This setup allows Kurtz to explore the dangers of prejudice, fear-mongering, and the corrupting influence of power.

One of Kurtz's greatest strengths as a writer is her ability to craft complex, nuanced characters. King Kelson, the young ruler of Gwynedd, is a perfect example. Half-Deryni himself, Kelson is torn between his duty to his kingdom and his desire to protect his people. His struggles to navigate the treacherous waters of court politics and Church interference are deeply relatable, making him a compelling and sympathetic protagonist.

Kurtz's world-building is another notable aspect of High Deryni. The Eleven Kingdoms are richly detailed, with a deep history that adds depth and complexity to the narrative. The author's use of magical realism, where the fantastical elements are woven seamlessly into the fabric of the world, creates a sense of authenticity that draws the reader in.

The pacing of High Deryni is well-balanced, with a mix of action, suspense, and introspection that keeps the reader engaged. Kurtz's writing style is descriptive and immersive, with a focus on character development and emotional resonance. While some readers may find the prose a bit dense or slow-moving at times, the payoff is well worth the investment.

In conclusion, High Deryni is a thought-provoking and engaging read that will appeal to fans of fantasy, science fiction, and historical fiction. Kurtz's exploration of prejudice, power struggles, and self-discovery is both timely and timeless, making this novel a must-read for anyone interested in complex, character-driven fiction.
