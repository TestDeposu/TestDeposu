# First Year (The Black Mage, #1)

**Author:** Rachel E. Carter  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

First Year opens with a gut‑wrenching betrayal: Ryiah watches the kingdom she thought she knew shatter when King Blayne reveals his cruelty, thrusting her into a covert rebellion where loyalty is a weapon and love a dangerous liability. Rachel E. Carter wastes no time plunging the reader into the tangled politics of Jerar, where the royal court glitter‑coats a simmering insurgency and the title “Black Mage” carries both awe and dread. At the heart of the turmoil stands Prince Darren—king’s brother, the realm’s most formidable sorcerer, and, unbeknownst to many, Ryiah’s husband. Their marriage, forged in political expediency, becomes the novel’s emotional furnace, testing the limits of trust as each spouse is forced to choose between the cause they swore to uphold and the person they cannot help but love.

Carter’s greatest strength lies in her ability to balance high‑stakes fantasy with an intimate, almost tactile romance. The prose is lean yet vivid, allowing the magic system to feel both mysterious and rule‑bound without bogging the narrative in exposition. Spellcraft is described with a kinetic energy that mirrors the characters’ internal conflicts—flares of dark energy crackle whenever Ryiah and Darren’s loyalties clash, turning every duel into a metaphor for their strained bond. This seamless integration of world‑building and character development keeps the pacing brisk; chapters end on hooks that feel earned rather than manipulative, urging the reader forward through court intrigues, secret meetings, and breathtaking magical showdowns.

The novel’s central theme—enemies‑to‑lovers forged in the fire of rebellion—resonates because Carter treats both sides of the conflict with nuance. Ryiah is not a naïve idealist; her determination to protect her family and topple a tyrant is tempered by moments of doubt that make her relatable. Darren, meanwhile, embodies the tortured archetype of the power‑wielder who believes his strength is the only means to preserve order, yet his vulnerability surfaces in quiet exchanges that reveal a man weary of the crown’s expectations. Their push‑pull dynamic is less a simple trope reversal and more a study of how love can both empower and blind individuals caught in larger machinations.

While the romance drives much of the emotional core, Carter does not sacrifice the broader stakes. The rebel movement feels authentic, with its own hierarchies, sacrifices, and moral ambiguities. The academy setting hinted at in the series title provides a fertile ground for exploring magical education, rivalries, and the discovery of latent abilities that will undoubtedly shape later installments. Readers who enjoy a blend of political intrigue, meticulous magic systems, and a slow‑burn romance that ignites amid peril will find First Year a satisfying launchpad for The Black Mage saga.

In sum, Rachel E. Carter delivers a compelling debut that marries the sweep of epic fantasy with the intensity of a personal love story. Her crisp, evocative style keeps the world immersive without sacrificing momentum, and the enemies‑to‑lovers dynamic feels fresh because it is rooted in genuine ideological conflict rather than mere attraction. Fans of series like The Shadow and Bone or The Kingkiller Chronicle will appreciate the careful balance of heart and hazard that defines this opening volume.
