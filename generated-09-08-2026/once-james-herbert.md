# Once...

**Author:** James Herbert  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

In the realm of fantasy and horror, few authors have managed to master the delicate balance between the two genres as skillfully as James Herbert. With "Once", Herbert weaves a complex narrative that delves into the darker aspects of human nature, while exploring the fascinating world of faeries, demons, and other supernatural beings.

At the heart of the story is Thom Kindred, an ordinary individual who finds himself thrust into a world of magic and wonder. As he navigates this new reality, Thom discovers that the faery stories of his childhood are, in fact, based on truth. However, this revelation comes with a terrible cost, as he soon realizes that the forces of evil are equally real and determined to destroy him.

Herbert's writing style is, as always, masterful. He effortlessly crafts a sense of atmosphere, transporting the reader to a world that is both wondrous and terrifying. His use of language is evocative, conjuring vivid images of the faeries, demons, and other supernatural beings that inhabit this realm. The author's skillful pacing ensures that the narrative flows smoothly, hurtling the reader towards a climactic confrontation between good and evil.

One of the most striking aspects of "Once" is its exploration of the human condition. Herbert delves deep into the complexities of human nature, revealing the darkest aspects of our psyche. The novel is a thought-provoking exploration of the nature of evil, and the ways in which it can corrupt even the strongest of wills. At the same time, the author celebrates the power of love and redemption, offering a glimmer of hope in the face of overwhelming darkness.

Throughout the novel, Herbert's use of eroticism and sensuality adds a layer of complexity to the narrative. The author's portrayal of the faeries is particularly noteworthy, as he imbues these creatures with a sense of sensuality and mystery. This aspect of the novel is sure to appeal to fans of fantasy and horror, who will appreciate the author's bold and unflinching approach to these themes.

If there is one criticism to be made, it is that the novel can be overwhelming at times. Herbert's world-building is meticulous, but this attention to detail can result in a narrative that feels dense and complex. However, this is a minor quibble, and one that is more than offset by the novel's many strengths.

In conclusion, "Once" is a masterpiece of fantasy and horror that is sure to appeal to fans of both genres. Herbert's writing is masterful, his world-building is meticulous, and his exploration of the human condition is thought-provoking and insightful. If you're looking for a novel that will transport you to a world of wonder and terror, then "Once" is the perfect choice.
