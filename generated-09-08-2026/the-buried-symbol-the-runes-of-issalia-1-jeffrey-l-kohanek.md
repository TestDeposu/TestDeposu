# The Buried Symbol (The Runes of Issalia, #1)

**Author:** Jeffrey L. Kohanek  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Unknown

In the ever-expanding landscape of epic fantasy, finding a novel that breathes fresh magic into a well-worn trope is a rare delight. Jeffrey L. Kohanek achieves just this in The Buried Symbol, the opening installment of The Runes of Issalia. Blending a rigid, stratified society with a forgotten, forbidden magic system, Kohanek crafts a compelling narrative that examines the heavy price of ambition, the danger of absolute institutional control, and the resilience of the human spirit. 

At the heart of the novel is its striking central theme: the dichotomy between inherent worth and assigned status. In the empire of Issalia, a person's value is strictly dictated by the Guilds, which rely on rare and powerful magic symbols. Those born without the aptitude for these runes are relegated to the bottom rungs of society, treated as little more than cogs in a relentless economic machine. Yet, Kohanek uses his protagonist to brilliantly subvert this deterministic worldview. The story explores how true power often lies not in the magical symbols stamped upon one's flesh, but in ingenuity, empathy, and the quiet courage to question a corrupt status quo. Through this lens, the book transforms from a standard magical academy adventure into a poignant critique of systemic oppression and elitism.

Kohanek’s writing style is brisk, accessible, and cinematic. He possesses a distinct talent for world-building without getting bogged down in exhaustive exposition. Instead of overwhelming the reader with heavy-handed history lessons, he reveals the dark underbelly of Issalia through character interactions, pacing, and environmental storytelling. The magic system itself—based on mysterious runes that grant extraordinary abilities—is inventive and visually striking, acting as both a coveted tool and a metaphorical shackle. The pacing is relentless, designed to keep the pages turning as the stakes escalate from personal survival to a much larger, continent-spanning conspiracy. 

While the narrative occasionally leans into familiar fantasy tropes, Kohanek’s earnest storytelling and well-crafted characters elevate the material. The protagonist is endearing precisely because he is flawed, vulnerable, and forced to rely on his wits rather than overpowering gifts. The supporting cast adds rich texture to the world, creating a dynamic interplay of loyalties and betrayals that keeps the reader guessing.

The Buried Symbol is a triumphant start to a sprawling fantasy saga. It offers a masterclass in blending classic heroic tropes with modern themes of rebellion and self-discovery. For readers hungry for intricate magic systems, underdog triumphs, and richly textured worlds where knowledge is both the ultimate weapon and the ultimate crime, this novel is an absolute must-read.
