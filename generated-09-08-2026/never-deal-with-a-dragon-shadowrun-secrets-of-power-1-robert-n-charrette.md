# Never Deal with a Dragon (Shadowrun: Secrets of Power, #1)

**Author:** Robert N. Charrette  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

Where Man Meets Magic and Machine: A Critical Look at Robert N. Charrette’s Never Deal with a Dragon

When Robert N. Charrette unleashed Never Deal with a Dragon upon the speculative fiction world in 1990—later republished in 2016 for a new generation of readers—he did more than just write a tie-in novel for a tabletop roleplaying game. He helped forge the foundational mythology of one of science fiction and fantasy’s most enduring crossover universes: Shadowrun. Set in a 2050 dystopia where elves, dwarves, and dragons share the urban sprawl with deckers, cyberware, and all-powerful megacorporations, Charrette’s novel stands as a masterclass in world-building and a striking exploration of identity in a digitized, enchanted age.

The central theme of Never Deal with a Dragon is the collision of the systemic, soulless corporate machine with the wild, unpredictable chaos of returning magic. In 2050, technology has advanced to the point where human consciousness can interface directly with global networks, yet ancient astral forces have reawakened to challenge our scientific dominance. Charrette uses his protagonist, Sam Verner, as the ultimate avatar of this thematic friction. Sam begins his journey as a sheltered corporate dependent, coddled by the monolithic Renraku conglomerate. He believes in the safety of the system. However, when his sister disappears and his reality fractures, Sam is violently ejected from his corporate womb into the neon-lit, rain-slicked underbelly of Seattle. 

Through Sam’s harrowing descent, Charrette explores the loss of innocence and the harsh necessity of adaptation. To survive in the shadows, Sam cannot rely on corporate policies or technological crutches; he must awaken to the dormant magical potential within himself. This transition from corporate cog to awakening mage serves as a brilliant structural metaphor for the novel's core thesis: that humanity cannot endlessly compartmentalize existence into pure logic or pure mysticism. The future belongs to those who can navigate the dangerous gray area where magic and machine bleed into one another.

Charrette’s writing style is brisk, atmospheric, and deeply cinematic. He possesses a rare talent for sensory immersion. The prose crackles with the kinetic energy of classic cyberpunk—the hum of cybernetics, the glare of holographic advertisements, the taste of cheap synth-caf—while seamlessly weaving in the archaic, visceral dread of awakened myth. Unlike later additions to the genre that sometimes lean too heavily into action-movie tropes, Charrette takes his time establishing the psychological weight of this transformed Earth. The pacing mirrors Sam’s own panic and disorientation; the narrative twists through corporate boardrooms, sprawling slums, and astral planes with equal narrative dexterity.

Furthermore, Charrette’s dialogue and characterization elevate the novel above standard media tie-in fare. The denizens of the Shadowrun universe—fixers, street samurai, and shamans—speak with a distinct, gritty cadence that helped define cyberpunk slang for decades. Yet, it is Sam’s internal vulnerability that anchors the high-concept world-building. We do not just see a cool future filled with magic and chrome; we feel the terror of a young man realizing that the rules of the world have been rewritten overnight.

Never Deal with a Dragon is more than a nostalgic relic of 1990s gaming culture or a mere franchise starter. It is a taut, inventive speculative thriller that poses a timeless question: when the gods and the corporations go to war over your soul, which side will you sell out to first? Charrette’s gripping debut answers that question with style, suspense, and a healthy dose of street-level magic, making it an essential read for fans of cyberpunk and urban fantasy alike.
