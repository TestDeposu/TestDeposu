# The Forgotten Sisters (Princess Academy, #3)

**Author:** Shannon Hale  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

Rating: 4.5 out of 5 stars

When Shannon Hale introduced readers to the rugged, quarry-dwelling mountain girl Miri in 2005, she crafted a modern classic of children's literature. With The Forgotten Sisters, the third installment in the beloved Princess Academy series, Hale returns to her signature enchanting style, delivering a masterclass in how to expand a fantasy world without losing the intimate heart of the original story. Far from being a tired continuation, this 2015 novel breathes vibrant new life into the trilogy, trading the familiar halls of the academy and the bustling cobblestones of Asland for a murky, unpredictable swamp that proves as challenging as it is atmospheric.

The narrative pivots on a brilliant role-reversal: Miri, once the hesitant student fighting for her education and a voice, now finds herself stepping into the shoes of the teacher. Sent by the king to a distant and desolate marshland, she is tasked with transforming three royal cousins—Astrid, Felissa, and Sus—into proper princesses. The comedic and cultural friction is immediate. Raised in the wild wilds of the swamp, these three sisters care infinitely more about tracking prey, fishing, and muddy survival than they do about court etiquette, history, or wearing silk. 

Hale’s greatest stylistic triumph in this book lies in her character work. The three sisters are not mere obstacles to Miri’s journey home; they are richly rendered individuals with distinct voices, deep-seated insecurities, and a fierce, untamed loyalty to one another. Through them, Hale explores the book's central theme: the tension between societal expectations of refinement and the raw, authentic power of one's upbringing. The novel asks a profound question wrapped in accessible middle-grade prose: What does it truly mean to be civilized, and who gets to define grace? For Astrid, Felissa, and Sus, discarding their swamp-born identity feels like an erasure of self. For Miri, who bridged the gap between Mount Eskel and the royal court in earlier books, the challenge is helping these girls find dignity in their roots while navigating the demands of the crown.

True to Hale's trademark plotting, the story is much more than a charming culture-clash comedy. As Miri digs deeper into her assignment, she uncovers a dark, long-buried secret regarding the royal family's sudden interest in these isolated cousins. The introduction of this political mystery elevates the stakes, transforming a cozy instructional retreat into a high-tension rescue and survival mission. Miri cannot rely on magic or brute force; she must utilize the very intelligence, empathy, and strength of character she cultivated back on Mount Eskel. 

Hale’s prose remains a beacon of middle-grade fantasy—luminous, grounded, and deceptively simple. She avoids overly florid descriptions, opting instead for sensory details that make the damp, mosquito-ridden swamp tangible, while keeping the emotional core razor-sharp. The dialogue crackles with wit, and the overarching message of female solidarity, sisterhood, and mutual empowerment rings loud and true on every page. 

The Forgotten Sisters is a triumphant capstone to a phenomenal trilogy. It honors the journey of Miri Latus while proving that true royalty isn't about sitting on a throne or wearing a crown—it is about standing up for those who have been forgotten, and having the courage to remain true to yourself no matter where life's currents take you. For fans of smart, empowering fantasy, Hale’s latest visit to this universe is an absolute must-read.
