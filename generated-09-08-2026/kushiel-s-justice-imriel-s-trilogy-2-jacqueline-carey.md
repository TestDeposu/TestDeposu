# Kushiel's Justice (Imriel's Trilogy, #2)

**Author:** Jacqueline Carey  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

Jacqueline Carey returns with Kushiel's Justice, the middle volume of the Imriel Trilogy, proving once again why she remains the undisputed queen of lush, sophisticated, and emotionally resonant high fantasy. Building upon the rich tapestry of her beloved D’Angeline universe, Carey weaves a tale where political maneuvering, divine intervention, and the agonizing friction between desire and duty take center stage. This is a novel that does not merely tell a story; it seduces the reader, wrapping them in velvet before striking with the swift, sharp blade of emotional truth.

At the heart of the narrative lies the central theme that defines the entire ethos of Terre d'Ange: the conflict between personal liberty and the heavy burden of responsibility. Blessed Elua’s foundational commandment, "Love as thou wilt," sounds liberating in theory, but in practice, it is a crucible for heartbreak. Imriel de la Courcel, the tormented Prince of the Blood whose lineage carries the heavy stain of his traitorous mother, finds himself trapped within this philosophical paradox. His forbidden, white-hot affair with his cousin Sidonie, the heir to the throne, is the ultimate expression of Elua’s freedom. Yet, the brutal realities of statecraft demand sacrifice. When Imriel must choose duty over passion—marrying an Alban princess to secure a political alliance—the novel transcends traditional fantasy to explore the profound grief of self-abnegation. Carey brilliantly exposes how the laws of gods and kingdoms often demand the crucifixion of the heart.

Carey’s literary style is as intoxicating as ever. Her prose is a masterclass in sensory world-building, dripping with baroque elegance, rich textures, and psychological depth. She treats language like a tapestry, threading together the decadent aesthetics of the D'Angeline court with the cold, misty landscapes of Alba. What sets Carey apart from her contemporaries is her refusal to rely on cheap tropes. The political intrigue in Kushiel's Justice is labyrinthine and intelligent, driven by characters whose motivations are messy, human, and achingly real. Imriel is a magnificent protagonist—vulnerable yet resilient, marked by trauma yet capable of profound love. As dark powers in Alba attempt to weaponize his past and his passions against him, Imriel’s journey becomes a harrowing quest for identity and redemption.

In Kushiel's Justice, Carey delivers a mesmerizing continuation of a legendary saga. It is a story that reminds us that love is never simple, justice is rarely swift, and the choices we make in the name of duty echo both in the halls of mortal men and the realms of the gods. For fans of epic fantasy imbued with romance, political suspense, and peerless prose, this novel is an absolute triumph.
