# Dragon Age: Asunder (Dragon Age, #3)

**Author:** David Gaider  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

Dragon Age: Asunder, penned by David Gaider, is a captivating addition to the Dragon Age series, delving into the tumultuous world of mages and templars in the realm of Thedas. Set against the backdrop of the Circle of Magi's destruction, this novel masterfully weaves a narrative of rebellion, power struggles, and self-discovery.

Gaider's writing style is akin to a masterfully crafted tapestry, rich in detail and depth. His world-building is meticulous, transporting readers to the majestic White Spire, where the intricate dance of politics and magic unfolds. The author's ability to breathe life into his characters is remarkable, making it easy to become invested in their journeys. Rhys, the protagonist, is a complex and intriguing figure, whose struggles to prove his innocence and navigate the treacherous landscape of the White Spire, are both poignant and captivating.

One of the most striking aspects of Asunder is its exploration of the mage-templar dynamic. Gaider skillfully exposes the deep-seated tensions and prejudices that simmer beneath the surface, threatening to boil over into chaos. The introduction of the seekers, a powerful and secretive segment of the templars, adds an air of mystery and foreboding, elevating the stakes and propelling the narrative forward.

The author's use of magic as a metaphor for power and oppression is thought-provoking, raising questions about the nature of freedom and control. The world of Thedas is one where magic is both revered and feared, and Gaider masterfully explores the gray areas in between. The mystical killer, invisible to all but Rhys, serves as a potent symbol of the unknown, highlighting the blurred lines between reality and the spirit world.

As Rhys embarks on his perilous journey into the western wastelands of Orlais, the narrative expands, incorporating a diverse cast of characters, each with their own motivations and secrets. The inclusion of Wynne, a heroine of the Blight, adds a welcome layer of depth, bridging the gap between the events of the novel and the larger Dragon Age universe.

Gaider's prose is engaging and accessible, making Asunder an enjoyable read for both fans of the Dragon Age series and newcomers alike. The pacing is well-balanced, with a mix of action, suspense, and introspection that keeps the reader invested in the story. The world of Thedas is richly detailed, with a history that is both complex and compelling.

In conclusion, Dragon Age: Asunder is a gripping and immersive novel that explores the complexities of power, magic, and identity. Gaider's masterful storytelling and well-developed characters make this a must-read for fans of fantasy and adventure. With its thought-provoking themes and engaging narrative, Asunder is sure to leave a lasting impression on readers.
