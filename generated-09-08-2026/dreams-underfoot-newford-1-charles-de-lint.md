# Dreams Underfoot (Newford, #1)

**Author:** Charles de Lint  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

**Dreams Underfoot: Where the Ordinary Bleeds into the Extraordinary**

Charles de Lint’s *Dreams Underfoot*—the first collection of stories set in his enchanting, gritty city of Newford—is a masterclass in weaving the mundane with the mythic. Here, a painter named Jilly captures fleeting faerie visions on canvas, a fiddle player named Geordie searches for his stolen love through the city’s labyrinthine alleys, and a mermaid named Katrina abandons the sea for a mortal man’s embrace. These aren’t fairy-tale escapades; they’re stories rooted in the lived reality of a city where magic is as much a part of the sidewalk as the rain, where the old and the new coexist in uneasy harmony.

What makes *Dreams Underfoot* so compelling is de Lint’s ability to make the extraordinary feel intimate. His Newford isn’t a sanitized fantasy realm—it’s a city with its share of squalor, struggle, and human frailty, yet one where the veil between worlds is thin enough to see through. The collection’s stories range from the tender (a paperjack reading fortunes in a dimly lit shop) to the haunting (a woman who hears the whispers of the dead in the walls of her apartment), each one a snapshot of a life where the supernatural isn’t just possible—it’s *alive*. And yet, de Lint never lets the magic overshadow the humanity. His characters are flawed, desperate, and deeply relatable, whether they’re Gemmins living in abandoned cars or a conjure man tending to his Tree of Tales, where every leaf holds a story.

The author’s style is a perfect blend of lyrical prose and earthy realism. His descriptions are rich with sensory detail—the scent of saltwater near the docks, the hum of a music club’s bassline vibrating through the streets—but they never veer into sentimentality. Instead, de Lint grounds his fantasy in the small, quiet moments that make life feel real. A lover’s argument in a dimly lit café, a child’s fear of the shadows under the bed, the quiet resilience of those who scrape by—these are the threads that weave his mythology. His writing is conversational yet poetic, as if he’s sitting across from you, spinning a tale with the warmth of a campfire storyteller.

What truly sets *Dreams Underfoot* apart is its refusal to simplify its world. Unlike many fantasy works that pit good against evil on grand scales, de Lint focuses on the personal—the way love can be both a blessing and a curse, how memory shapes identity, and how even the most ordinary lives can be touched by something greater. His Newford isn’t just a setting; it’s a character in its own right, a place where the past lingers in the cobblestones and the future whispers in the wind. The city feels alive because its people do, and de Lint captures that vitality with a tenderness that lingers long after the last page.

For readers who crave fantasy that feels fresh, human, and deeply rooted in the real world, *Dreams Underfoot* is a must-read. It’s a collection that reminds us that magic isn’t just out there—it’s in the cracks of everyday life, waiting to be discovered. And in de Lint’s hands, even the dirtiest, most forgotten corners of a city can become a place where dreams walk beside us.
