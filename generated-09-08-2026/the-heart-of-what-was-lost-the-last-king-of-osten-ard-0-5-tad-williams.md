# The Heart of What Was Lost (The Last King of Osten Ard, #0.5)

**Author:** Tad Williams  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

In "The Heart of What Was Lost," the latest installment in Tad Williams' epic fantasy saga, Osten Ard, Tad masterfully weaves together the intricate threads of a battle that will soon engulf the realm. This prequel, set in the aftermath of the events of "Memory, Sorrow, and Thorn," sets the stage for a catastrophic conflict that will shake the very foundations of the world.

At its core, "The Heart of What Was Lost" is a tale of desperation and survival. It's a story about the Norns, the dark cousins of the Sithi, as they flee for their lives from the armies of men bent on their destruction. Led by the cunning Queen Utuk'ku, the Norns must navigate treacherous landscapes and confront the darkness within themselves in order to reach their last stronghold, the mountainous fortress of Nakkiga.

Throughout the novel, Williams showcases his trademark ability to craft relatable and complex characters. We are introduced to Viyeki, a skilled Norn warrior who must confront his own doubts and fears in the face of an uncertain future. His struggles are palpable as he grapples with the prospect of extinction and the weight of his people's legacy. In contrast, the mortal soldiers Porto and Endri provide a fresh perspective, their naivety and bravery standing in stark contrast to the Norns' centuries-old wisdom.

One of the most striking aspects of "The Heart of What Was Lost" is its ability to balance action and introspection. Williams knows exactly when to inject a burst of energy and excitement, whether it be a thrilling battle scene or a heart-pounding chase through treacherous terrain. At the same time, he seamlessly integrates moments of quiet reflection, offering readers a glimpse into the motivations and emotions of the characters. It's a delicate balance that few authors have mastered, and Williams executes it with ease.

As with "Memory, Sorrow, and Thorn," the world-building in "The Heart of What Was Lost" is nothing short of magnificent. Williams draws upon a deep well of cultural and historical influences, crafting a rich tapestry that is both immersive and meticulously detailed. The world of Osten Ard is one of ancient magic, long-forgotten mythologies, and crumbling empires – and it's clear that Williams has poured his heart and soul into its creation.

But what truly sets "The Heart of What Was Lost" apart is its nuanced exploration of the human (or, rather, Norn) condition. At its core, this is a story about the consequences of trauma and the devastating impact that war and loss can have on even the strongest of individuals. Williams handles this theme with sensitivity and restraint, never shying away from the darkness but also refusing to succumb to cliché.

In short, "The Heart of What Was Lost" is a triumph of epic fantasy storytelling. It's a novel that will keep readers on the edge of their seats while also challenging them to reflect on the complexities of existence. Tad Williams has once again proven himself to be a master of the genre, and fans of "Memory, Sorrow, and Thorn" will rejoice at the introduction of this new cycle of Osten Ard.
