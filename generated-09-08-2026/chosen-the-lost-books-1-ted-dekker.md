# Chosen (The Lost Books, #1)

**Author:** Ted Dekker  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

**Chosen: A Masterclass in Mythic Imagination and Moral Urgency**

Ted Dekker’s *Chosen*—the first installment in *The Lost Books* series—is a sprawling, high-stakes epic that blends post-apocalyptic survival with cosmic mythology, all while delivering a relentless moral dilemma at its core: *What does it mean to be chosen?* Not by fate, but by the weight of a destiny you never asked for. This isn’t just another fantasy about chosen ones; it’s a meditation on sacrifice, the cost of power, and the fragile line between heroism and tyranny. Dekker, a master of psychological tension and theological undertones, crafts a world where the battle for the future isn’t just fought with swords and sorcery, but with the very fabric of history itself.

At the heart of *Chosen* is Thomas Hunter, a grizzled commander of the Forest Guard, a remnant of a world ravaged by an ancient evil. His army is dwindling, his enemies are relentless, and his faith in humanity is tested when he’s forced to recruit four teenagers—raw, untried, and utterly unprepared—for a mission that will determine the fate of two worlds. But here’s the twist: Thomas doesn’t know the full scope of their task. The real stakes are higher than he realizes. The four must recover the *Seven Lost Books of History*, artifacts said to control time, destiny, and even the laws of reality. And if the Dark One—a force of pure malevolence—gets them first, the consequences aren’t just cosmic; they’re *existential*. Because these books don’t just rewrite history—they rewrite *both* worlds, including ours.

Dekker’s genius lies in his ability to weave theological and philosophical questions into his storytelling. The *Books of History* aren’t just magical artifacts; they’re a metaphor for the dangers of absolute knowledge, the hubris of playing God, and the ethical void that opens when power is unchecked. The series’ premise echoes biblical and mythic traditions—think *The Da Vinci Code* meets *The Lord of the Rings*, with a dash of *The Matrix*’s existential dread—but Dekker infuses it with his signature intensity. His prose is lean, his pacing relentless, and his characters are driven by a mix of desperation and defiance. You don’t get to sit back and admire the world-building; you’re *thrown* into the chaos, forced to root for these flawed, human protagonists as they stumble toward their destiny.

The four chosen—each with their own backstory, flaws, and reasons for doubt—are the emotional core of the story. Dekker avoids the cliché of the "chosen one" trope by making their selection feel earned, not predetermined. Their journey isn’t about destiny; it’s about *choice*. Do they embrace the burden, or do they break under it? Dekker doesn’t offer easy answers. The moral ambiguity is palpable, especially in the way power corrupts even the most well-intentioned. The Dark One isn’t just a villain; it’s a force of entropy, a reminder that evil doesn’t need a face—it just needs an opportunity.

Visually, *Chosen* is a feast for fans of dystopian and fantasy worlds. Dekker’s settings are vivid, from the lush, overgrown forests of the Forest Guard to the decaying ruins of the past. The contrast between the two worlds—one thriving in nature, the other a shadow of what it once was—serves as a commentary on humanity’s relationship with progress and decay. The action is brutal, the stakes are personal, and the tension never lets up. Dekker doesn’t shy away from the darker aspects of survival, making the triumphs feel all the more hard-earned.

Yet for all its intensity, *Chosen* is ultimately a story about *hope*. Not the naive kind, but the stubborn, defiant kind that refuses to let the world end without a fight. The four chosen aren’t heroes because they’re perfect; they’re heroes because they *choose* to be. Dekker’s writing is unapologetically bold, his themes provocative, and his execution flawless. *The Lost Books* series is a testament to the power of mythic storytelling in the modern age—a reminder that the greatest battles aren’t fought with weapons, but with the stories we tell ourselves about who we are and what we’re willing to sacrifice for the future.

If you love fantasy that’s equal parts *The Last of Us* and *The Name of the Wind*, with a dash of *The Book of Eli*’s desperation, then *Chosen* is a must-read. Dekker doesn’t just tell a story; he *demands* your attention, your empathy, and your belief in the power of the ordinary to change the extraordinary. And in a world where the line between past and future is as thin as a page in one of those lost books, that’s no small feat.
