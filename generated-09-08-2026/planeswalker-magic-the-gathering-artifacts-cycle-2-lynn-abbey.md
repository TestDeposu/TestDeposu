# Planeswalker (Magic: The Gathering: Artifacts Cycle, #2)

**Author:** Lynn Abbey  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Unknown

**Planeswalker: A Masterclass in Mythic Storytelling and the Weight of Legacy**

Lynn Abbey’s *Planeswalker*—the second installment in *Magic: The Gathering’s* *Artifacts Cycle*—is a novel that doesn’t just expand the lore of the multiverse; it *redefines* what it means to wield power across dimensions. Set against the backdrop of the *Planechase* event, where planeswalkers like Nicol Bolas and Chandra Nalaar clash in a high-stakes game of cosmic survival, this book is a gripping blend of political intrigue, existential stakes, and the kind of mythic depth that makes *Magic*’s world feel alive.

At its core, *Planeswalker* is a story about **legacy, sacrifice, and the cost of immortality**. The narrative follows a group of planeswalkers—both legendary and newly ascended—who must navigate the brutal, zero-sum world of *Planechase*, where every move could mean the annihilation of entire planes. Abbey masterfully weaves together the perspectives of characters like **Jace Beleren**, the archmage whose brilliance borders on godhood, and **Nicol Bolas**, the self-proclaimed "God of Spells," whose ambition knows no bounds. The tension between these figures isn’t just about strategy; it’s about **what it means to be a planeswalker in a universe that demands everything from them**.

Abbey’s prose is **sharp, immersive, and effortlessly immersive**, blending the tactical precision of a chess match with the emotional weight of a tragedy. She doesn’t just describe battles—she makes the reader *feel* the weight of a spell cast, the terror of a plane collapsing into chaos, and the quiet desperation of a planeswalker who has outlived every mortal they’ve ever known. The dialogue crackles with wit and menace, whether it’s the sardonic banter of Jace or the chilling calm of Bolas, who speaks as if the fate of the multiverse is a mere afterthought.

What sets *Planeswalker* apart is its **unflinching exploration of power**. Abbey doesn’t shy away from the moral ambiguities of her characters. Nicol Bolas isn’t just a villain—he’s a man who believes he’s the only one capable of saving the multiverse, even if it means burning everything in his path. Jace, meanwhile, is a genius who has spent centuries playing the game, only to realize too late that the rules were never meant to be fair. The book forces readers to ask: **How far would you go to protect what you love?** And more importantly, **what happens when the thing you’re protecting is already doomed?**

The world-building is **rich without being overwhelming**, a testament to Abbey’s ability to drop readers into the heart of *Magic*’s lore while keeping the focus on the characters. The *Planechase* mechanic—where planeswalkers manipulate the flow of time and space—is handled with such creativity that it feels like a natural extension of the series, rather than an afterthought. The stakes are **cosmic**, the conflicts **personal**, and the emotional payoff **devastating**.

For fans of *Magic: The Gathering*, *Planeswalker* is a must-read—it’s the kind of book that doesn’t just expand the lore but *deepens* it, making the world feel more real, more dangerous, and more beautiful. For those new to the series, it’s an instant gateway into a universe where power is currency, and every decision could rewrite reality.

In the end, *Planeswalker* is more than just another entry in the *Artifacts Cycle*—it’s a **mythic tale of ambition, sacrifice, and the price of eternity**, told with the kind of prose that makes you believe in gods, spells, and the endless war between light and shadow.
