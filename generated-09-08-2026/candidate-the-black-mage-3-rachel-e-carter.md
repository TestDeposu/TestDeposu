# Candidate (The Black Mage, #3)

**Author:** Rachel E. Carter  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

**A Storm of Betrayal and Passion: *Candidate* by Rachel E. Carter**

Rachel E. Carter’s *Candidate*—the third installment in *The Black Mage* series—is a masterclass in high-stakes tension, where loyalty is a blade and love is both weapon and wound. This isn’t just a romance; it’s a political chess match where every move could mean the fall of a kingdom or the rise of a tyrant. Set against the backdrop of a crumbling empire, *Candidate* delivers the explosive finale to Ryiah’s arc, proving Carter’s knack for blending *romantasy* with the gritty realism of power struggles.

At its core, *Candidate* is a story about the cost of revolution. Ryiah, once a dutiful student at the Magic Academy, has become a rebel leader, her heart torn between her devotion to her family and her growing hatred for the regime that destroyed her world. Her marriage to Prince Darren—a man sworn to crush the rebellion—is the most dangerous game of all. Darren, the Black Mage, is a force of nature: powerful, brooding, and utterly devoted to his brother’s cause. But when Ryiah’s defiance forces his hand, their relationship spirals from reluctant alliance to something far more volatile. Carter doesn’t shy away from the moral gray areas here. Ryiah’s betrayal isn’t just personal; it’s a calculated strike against a system that has already shown it will burn everything—including its own—to maintain control. Darren, for his part, is a man of principle, but his love for Ryiah threatens to unravel the very foundation of his loyalty.

What makes *Candidate* so compelling is Carter’s ability to weave magic into the fabric of power. The spells here aren’t just tools; they’re extensions of character. Ryiah’s magic is tied to her emotions, making her battles as much internal as they are external. Darren’s, meanwhile, is cold and precise, a reflection of his disciplined nature. Their dynamic is electric, charged with the kind of tension that makes *enemies-to-lovers* tropes feel fresh rather than clichéd. There’s no easy resolution here—just the kind of morally complex choices that make the story linger long after the last page.

Carter’s prose is sharp and evocative, painting a world where magic is both awe-inspiring and terrifying. The political intrigue is meticulously crafted, with each faction feeling distinct and dangerous. The pacing is relentless, keeping readers on the edge of their seats as Ryiah and Darren navigate a world where trust is a luxury they can’t afford. By the time the final confrontation arrives, it’s not just a battle for survival—it’s a battle for the soul of an empire.

*Candidate* isn’t just the climax of Ryiah’s journey; it’s the culmination of Carter’s vision for a world where magic and politics are inseparable. It’s a story about the price of freedom, the weight of betrayal, and the dangerous beauty of love in the face of destruction. If you’ve followed Ryiah’s journey, you’ll find the payoff devastatingly satisfying. If you’re new to the series, *Candidate* is a perfect entry point—though be warned, it’s a storm you won’t want to walk away from.
