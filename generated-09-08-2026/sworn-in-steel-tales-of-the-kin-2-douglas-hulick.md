# Sworn in Steel (Tales of the Kin, #2)

**Author:** Douglas Hulick  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

**Sworn in Steel: A Blade’s Edge in the Shadows of Power**

Douglas Hulick’s *Sworn in Steel*—the second installment in *Tales of the Kin*—is a masterclass in dark, morally ambiguous storytelling, where loyalty is a currency as fleeting as blood and every shadow hides a knife. Three months have passed since Drothe, the reluctant Gray Prince, burned his way into the criminal underworld of the empire, only to discover that power in the streets is as brutal as it is temporary. Now, with a rival’s death casting suspicion over him, Drothe is caught between betrayal and survival, forced to navigate a world where trust is a luxury and every favor comes with a price.

Hulick’s prose is sharp as a dagger, weaving a narrative that feels both visceral and intricate. His world-building is immersive, painting a vivid picture of a fractured empire where the Despotate of Djan looms as a perpetual threat, its sands shifting with the weight of ancient grudges. The tension is relentless, with Drothe’s internal conflict—between vengeance, redemption, and self-preservation—driving the story forward. Hulick doesn’t shy away from the grim realities of power; in this world, a prince’s rise is as much a curse as it is an honor, and Drothe’s journey is one of learning that even the strongest alliances can unravel at the first sign of weakness.

Themes of betrayal and survival dominate, but Hulick elevates them with a nuanced exploration of morality. Drothe is no hero; he’s a man who has made choices that have left blood on his hands, and the story doesn’t flinch from the consequences. His alliance with a mysterious figure who offers a way out—at a cost—is a masterstroke, forcing Drothe into a desperate gamble across enemy lines. The stakes are high, the clock is ticking, and the question isn’t just whether he’ll survive, but whether he’ll be the same man when it’s over.

Hulick’s writing is both lyrical and brutal, with a rhythm that mirrors the pulse of a city on the edge of collapse. His characters are flawed, complex, and deeply human, even in their monstrous actions. The Gray Princes, with their code of honor and blood, are a fascinating study in power dynamics—where loyalty is earned through deeds, not words, and where a single misstep can mean the difference between a throne and a grave.

*Sworn in Steel* is more than just a sequel; it’s a deep dive into the cost of ambition and the fragility of alliances. Hulick keeps the tension high, the world rich, and the characters unforgettable, making it a standout in the world of dark fantasy. If you loved the morally gray depth of *The Blade Itself* or the political intrigue of *The Lies of Locke Lamora*, this is a book you won’t want to miss.
