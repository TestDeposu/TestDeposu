# Bloodhound (Beka Cooper, #2)

**Author:** Tamora Pierce  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

**Bloodhound: A Scent of Adventure and Defiance in Tamora Pierce’s Boldest Trilogy**

Tamora Pierce’s *Bloodhound*—the second installment in the *Beka Cooper* trilogy—is a masterclass in blending gritty realism with high-stakes fantasy, where the scent of danger lingers as strongly as the ink on the page. Here, Pierce doesn’t just write a mystery; she crafts a coming-of-age odyssey for a heroine who refuses to be sidelined, even when the world conspires to silence her. Beka Cooper, the sharp-tongued, quick-witted descendant of George Cooper (yes, *that* George Cooper), is no longer a rookie in the Provost’s Guard—she’s a seasoned investigator, but her career is as unstable as the city she calls home. Teaming up with the legendary Dog Goodwin and his scent-hound, Achoo, Beka is thrust into Port Caynn, a city where shadows hide more than just criminals. What begins as a routine mission quickly unravels into a labyrinth of corruption, where the line between justice and survival blurs.

Pierce’s genius lies in her ability to ground her fantasy in the raw, unfiltered texture of life. Tortall’s Lower City isn’t just a setting; it’s a character in its own right—noir, vibrant, and teeming with voices that echo the marginalized and the forgotten. Beka’s magic, tied to her ability to track scents and uncover hidden truths, mirrors her own journey: she doesn’t just *see* the world; she *smells* it, dissecting lies with the same precision she uses to follow a trail. The prose is lean, immersive, and packed with Pierce’s signature wit, where dialogue crackles with the energy of street-smart banter and internal monologues reveal a protagonist who’s as self-aware as she is stubborn. There’s no grand, sweeping romance here—just the quiet, relentless determination of someone who knows her worth and won’t back down, even when the odds are stacked against her.

At its core, *Bloodhound* is a story about agency. Beka isn’t waiting for permission to prove herself; she’s *taking* it. The mission to Port Caynn forces her to confront not just the corruption of the Rogue’s Court but the systemic barriers that try to keep women like her in their place. Pierce doesn’t shy away from the tension between loyalty and principle—Beka’s choices aren’t easy, and her relationships with her partners are tested in ways that feel earned, not contrived. The dynamic between Beka and Dog Goodwin, in particular, is a standout, blending mentorship with mutual respect, and Achoo’s presence adds a layer of heartfelt companionship that makes the stakes feel even higher.

What makes *Bloodhound* particularly compelling is how it bridges the gap between Pierce’s earlier works and the *Song of the Lioness* series. Beka is a precursor to the bold, independent heroines who would follow—fearless, flawed, and unapologetically herself. The book’s pacing is tight, the mystery is intricate, and the emotional payoff is satisfying without veering into melodrama. Pierce’s world-building remains as rich as ever, with Tortall’s history and politics woven seamlessly into the plot, giving readers a sense of place that’s both familiar and fresh.

For fans of *Song of the Lioness* or *Protector of the Small*, *Bloodhound* offers a delicious detour into a different facet of Pierce’s genius—one that’s darker, more urban, and deeply rooted in the idea that heroism isn’t about grand gestures but about the courage to keep going when the world tries to wear you down. It’s a book that rewards readers who love their fantasy with a side of grit, a dash of mystery, and a heroine who’s as unforgettable as she is indispensable.

If you’ve ever wanted to see where George Cooper came from—or if you’re simply craving a fantasy adventure where the protagonist’s street smarts and moral compass are her greatest weapons—*Bloodhound* is the perfect next read. Tamora Pierce doesn’t just write stories; she builds legacies, and Beka Cooper’s is one worth following.
