# Princess at Sea (Princess, #2)

**Author:** Dawn Cook  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2006

**Princess at Sea: A Swashbuckling Adventure Where Magic and Misadventure Collide**

Dawn Cook’s *Princess at Sea*—the second installment in the *Princess* series—is a delightful blend of high-seas adventure, royal intrigue, and the kind of magical whimsy that makes fantasy feel alive. Set against the backdrop of a world where magic is both a gift and a burden, this novel follows Tess, a young woman with untapped powers, as she finds herself thrust into a perilous mission: chaperoning Queen Contessa and her new husband on their honeymoon. What begins as a simple assignment quickly spirals into a high-stakes chase when pirates abduct the royal couple, demanding ransom. But Tess soon realizes the real treasure isn’t gold or jewels—it’s her own magic, which the pirates covet far more than they do the queen.

At its core, *Princess at Sea* is a story about discovery—both of one’s own abilities and the world beyond the familiar. Tess is no traditional heroine; she’s pragmatic, resourceful, and often frustrated by the limitations of her powers. Cook’s writing shines in her ability to craft a protagonist who feels deeply human, even as she navigates a world where magic is both a blessing and a curse. Tess’s journey isn’t just about saving the queen; it’s about learning to embrace her strengths, even when they’re unpredictable. The novel’s pacing is brisk, with a perfect balance of action, humor, and heart, making it an ideal read for fans of adventure stories that don’t take themselves too seriously.

Cook’s signature style—playful yet sophisticated—is on full display here. Her prose is crisp and engaging, with a knack for vivid descriptions that bring the pirate-infested seas and the opulent royal court to life. The dialogue crackles with wit, and the banter between Tess and her companions feels natural, never forced. There’s a lightness to the storytelling that makes even the most dangerous situations feel like an exciting game, rather than a grim ordeal. Yet, beneath the humor and swashbuckling fun, there’s a deeper theme: the idea that magic, like life itself, is messy, unpredictable, and often requires courage to wield.

The novel’s world-building is another standout feature. Cook has created a richly textured setting where magic is both revered and feared, and where the line between hero and villain is often blurred. The pirates aren’t just mindless thugs; they’re complex characters with their own motivations, adding layers to the story. Meanwhile, the royal court feels like a place of both grandeur and intrigue, with its own politics and secrets. Cook doesn’t shy away from exploring themes of power, identity, and the cost of ambition, all while keeping the tone accessible and enjoyable.

One of the most appealing aspects of *Princess at Sea* is its celebration of female agency. Tess is far from a damsel in distress; she’s a survivor who adapts to whatever challenges come her way. The novel doesn’t just feature strong women—it centers them, giving them room to grow, make mistakes, and ultimately triumph on their own terms. This is particularly refreshing in a genre where female protagonists are often sidelined or reduced to love interests. Tess’s journey is one of self-reliance, and Cook’s portrayal of her is both inspiring and relatable.

For fans of fantasy adventure, *Princess at Sea* is a must-read. It’s the kind of book that feels like a breath of fresh air—lighthearted yet meaningful, action-packed yet deeply character-driven. Cook’s ability to weave magic into the fabric of everyday life makes the story feel both familiar and entirely new. Whether you’re a longtime fan of the series or new to Tess’s world, this novel delivers the perfect mix of excitement, humor, and heart.

In the end, *Princess at Sea* is more than just a pirate adventure; it’s a story about finding your place in a world that doesn’t always understand you. And with Tess at the helm, it’s an adventure you won’t want to miss.
