# Stormlord Rising (Watergivers, #2)

**Author:** Glenda Larke  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

In the midst of a world ravaged by war and drought, Glenda Larke's Stormlord Rising stands as a testament to the power of resilience and determination. This second installment in the Watergivers series plunges readers into the depths of a desperate struggle for survival, where the very fabric of society hangs in the balance.

At its core, Stormlord Rising is a tale of transformation, as its protagonist, Shale, grapples with the weight of his newfound responsibilities. Betrayed and left for dead, Shale must navigate a treacherous landscape of shifting allegiances and ancient rivalries. His journey is one of self-discovery, as he confronts the harsh realities of his world and the true extent of his own abilities. Larke's masterful handling of Shale's character arc is a highlight of the novel, as she skillfully peels away the layers of his persona to reveal a complex and deeply human figure.

The world of the Quartern is a richly detailed and immersive environment, where the intricate politics of the rainlords and the mystical powers of the Watergivers are woven into a compelling narrative. Larke's writing is evocative and visceral, conjuring the arid landscapes and dusty cities of the Quartern with a vividness that is both captivating and unsettling. Her use of language is economical and precise, conveying the harsh realities of a world on the brink of collapse with a minimum of fuss.

One of the most striking aspects of Stormlord Rising is its exploration of the theme of power and its corrupting influence. As Shale navigates the treacherous waters of his new role, he must confront the darker aspects of his own nature and the temptations of unchecked power. This is a nuanced and thought-provoking portrayal, one that raises important questions about the nature of leadership and the responsibilities that come with great power.

Terelle, the other main protagonist, is equally well-drawn, her journey a poignant exploration of identity and belonging. Her search for her homeland and her people, the mysterious Watergivers, is a powerful metaphor for the human quest for connection and community. Larke's handling of Terelle's character is sensitive and empathetic, conveying the depth of her emotions with a subtlety that is both moving and authentic.

If there is a criticism to be made, it is that the supporting cast is not always as fully realized as the main protagonists. Some characters feel a little one-dimensional, their motivations and actions not entirely clear. However, this is a minor quibble in what is otherwise a gripping and engaging narrative.

In conclusion, Stormlord Rising is a gripping and immersive fantasy novel that explores the complexities of power, identity, and community in a richly detailed and immersive world. Glenda Larke's writing is evocative and visceral, her characters complex and deeply human. This is a must-read for fans of epic fantasy and anyone looking for a compelling and thought-provoking narrative.
