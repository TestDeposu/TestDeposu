# The Gap Into Conflict: The Real Story (The Gap Cycle #1)

**Author:** Stephen R. Donaldson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1991

The Gap Into Conflict: The Real Story, the first installment of Stephen R. Donaldson's The Gap Cycle, is a thought-provoking and deeply unsettling novel that delves into the complexities of human nature and the blurred lines between good and evil. Set in a richly detailed future where faster-than-light travel has become a reality, the story follows the lives of two complex characters: Angus Thermopyle, a notorious ore pirate and murderer, and Morn Hyland, a former police officer who becomes embroiled in Thermopyle's world.

From the outset, it is clear that Donaldson is a masterful storyteller, weaving a intricate narrative that is both gripping and unsettling. The world he creates is vivid and immersive, with a level of detail that is impressive without being overwhelming. The characters, too, are multidimensional and nuanced, with rich inner lives that are slowly revealed over the course of the novel.

One of the most striking aspects of The Gap Into Conflict is its exploration of the human condition. Donaldson's characters are flawed and often unlikeable, but they are also deeply human, with all the vulnerabilities and contradictions that this entails. Thermopyle, in particular, is a fascinating figure, a man who is both brutal and charismatic, with a deep sense of vulnerability and insecurity. Through his character, Donaldson raises important questions about the nature of evil and whether it is possible for individuals to change and redeem themselves.

The novel's central theme of conflict, both internal and external, is also noteworthy. Donaldson explores the ways in which individuals are shaped by their experiences and the choices they make, and how these choices can have far-reaching consequences. The conflict between Thermopyle and Nick Succorso, a rival pirate, is a powerful example of this, with each character driven by their own motivations and desires.

Throughout the novel, Donaldson's writing style is characterized by its lyricism and precision. His prose is economical and direct, yet also capable of conveying a deep sense of emotion and complexity. The pacing is well-balanced, with a narrative that is both fast-moving and reflective, allowing the reader to absorb the complexities of the story.

If there is a criticism to be made, it is that the novel's supporting characters are not always as fully fleshed out as the main protagonists. However, this is a minor quibble, and one that does not detract from the overall impact of the story.

In conclusion, The Gap Into Conflict: The Real Story is a gripping and thought-provoking novel that explores the complexities of human nature and the nature of conflict. With its richly detailed world, nuanced characters, and masterful storytelling, it is a must-read for fans of science fiction and fantasy. Donaldson's writing style is both precise and evocative, making this a novel that will linger in the reader's mind long after the final page has been turned.
