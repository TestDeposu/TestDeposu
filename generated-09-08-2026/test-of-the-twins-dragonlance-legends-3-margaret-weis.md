# Test of the Twins (Dragonlance: Legends, #3)

**Author:** Margaret Weis  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

Test of the Twins, the explosive final installment of the Dragonlance: Legends trilogy by Margaret Weis, brings one of fantasy literature’s most compelling sibling rivalries to a breathtaking and emotionally resonant climax. Co-authored historically with Tracy Hickman, Weis delivers a masterclass in epic fantasy pacing, character evolution, and thematic depth. In this concluding volume, the sprawling scope of Krynn narrows down to the ultimate, agonizing conflict between two brothers: Raistlin Majere, the ambitious mage seeking to usurp the Queen of Darkness, and Caramon, the stalwart warrior destined to stand in his way. 

At the heart of the novel lies a profound and timeless theme: the heavy burden of agency versus destiny, and the destructive nature of codependency. Weis expertly uses the magical and cosmic scale of the Dragonlance universe to mirror internal, deeply personal struggles. Raistlin’s relentless pursuit of godhood is not merely about power; it is the ultimate expression of his lifelong defiance of his own physical weakness and mortality. Yet, his ascent comes at the cost of the world's destruction, a bleak future that Caramon and the ever-charming kender Tasslehoff Burrfoot witness firsthand after being cast forward in time. 

This journey into a desolate, ruined future serves as one of the novel's strongest narrative devices. By physically confronting the apocalyptic consequences of Raistlin’s triumph, Caramon undergoes a necessary metamorphosis. He evolves from a passive, nostalgic protector into a man of grim, agonizing resolve. The tragedy of Caramon's arc is that to save his brother from himself, and to save Krynn, he must step into the terrifying role of executioner. Weis handles this emotional tightrope with incredible sensitivity, ensuring that the brothers' bond remains the beating heart of the narrative even as they stand on opposite sides of the Abyss.

Weis’s writing style in Test of the Twins is characterized by its cinematic clarity, brisk momentum, and emotional resonance. While many epic fantasy novels of the era relied heavily on sprawling world-building at the expense of character, Weis anchors every grand, mythic event in the intimate flaws of her protagonists. The prose is accessible yet evocative, seamlessly blending high-stakes sorcery, time-travel mechanics, and poignant dialogue. The introduction of alternate timelines allows the author to play with fate and consequence, heightening the suspense until the very last page. 

Furthermore, the supporting cast adds vital texture to the narrative. The tragic, unrequited devotion of the cleric Crysania, who follows Raistlin into the Abyss believing she can save his soul, highlights the corrupting allure of the dark mage. Meanwhile, Tasslehoff provides crucial levity and unexpected heroism, grounding the cosmic dread with characteristic kender innocence. 

Ultimately, Test of the Twins is more than just a satisfying conclusion to a beloved trilogy; it is a definitive milestone in 1980s fantasy fiction. Margaret Weis crafts a narrative that transcends the tropes of tabletop-inspired literature, offering a timeless exploration of sacrifice, brotherhood, and the high price of ambition. For anyone who has ever wrestled with the desire to protect someone from their own destructive path, Raistlin and Caramon’s final confrontation will leave an indelible mark long after the book is closed.
