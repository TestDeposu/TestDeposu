# Orconomics (The Dark Profit Saga, #1)

**Author:** J. Zachary Pike  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

When high fantasy meets Wall Street, the result is J. Zachary Pike’s brilliantly subversive debut, Orconomics. Published in 2014 as the opening salvo of The Dark Profit Saga, this novel does not merely poke fun at genre tropes; it dissects them with a butcher’s knife and runs them through a stock market crash. Pike takes the romanticized, sword-and-sorcery notion of heroic adventuring and forces it to look at its own balance sheet, creating a world where heroism is capitalism, dungeons are corporate-sponsored assets, and saving the world requires navigating soul-crushing red tape. 

At the center of this financial-fantasy nightmare is Gorm Ingerson, a disgraced dwarven hero whose liver-spotted dreams of peaceful alcoholism are violently interrupted by a stray act of charity. By helping a goblin, Gorm accidentally binds himself to a disastrous contract, plunging headfirst into a quest that is less about slaying dragons and more about surviving hostile takeovers. He is joined by a ragtag ensemble of deadbeat adventurers—misfits who are far more worried about their health insurance, debt accumulation, and retirement plans than the ancient, dark prophecies looming over the Freedlands. 

Pike’s central theme is a razor-sharp critique of market mania and corporate greed. In the world of Orconomics, dungeons are treated like real estate investments, heroes act as venture-backed influencers, and magical catastrophes are market corrections. By framing legendary quests through the lens of predatory lending and economic bubbles, the author highlights the absurdity of applying ruthless corporate logic to matters of life, death, and destiny. The satirical bite is constant, yet it never feels like a one-note joke. Instead, Pike uses humor as a Trojan horse to deliver a surprisingly intricate, high-stakes narrative about systemic corruption and individual redemption.

The author’s style is defined by its kinetic wit, relentless cleverness, and astonishingly deep world-building. Pike writes with the cadence of a cynical economist who grew up reading Tolkien and playing tabletop roleplaying games. The dialogue crackles with modern sarcasm nestled comfortably inside high-fantasy terminology, making financial jargon like collateralized debt obligations and fiscal insolvency feel right at home next to spells and broadswords. Yet, beneath the heavy layers of satire and laugh-out-loud misadventures, Pike maintains a genuine affection for fantasy traditions. The dark and the delightful walk hand in hand, balancing slapstick humor with genuinely grim threats, including a mad goddess hell-bent on fulfilling a suicidal prophecy.

Orconomics is an absolute triumph for readers fatigued by cookie-cutter fantasy or those simply looking for a clever twist on the genre. It proves that a story can be sidesplittingly funny while still delivering a biting, intelligent critique of the systems that rule our everyday lives. For anyone who has ever wondered how a hero pays off a mortgage after saving a kingdom, Pike’s masterpiece is required reading.
