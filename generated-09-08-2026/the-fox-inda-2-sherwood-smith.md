# The Fox (Inda, #2)

**Author:** Sherwood Smith  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2026

Sherwood Smith’s *The Fox*, the masterfully crafted second installment of the acclaimed *Inda* series, proves that epic fantasy can be both vast in geopolitical scope and intimately character-driven. While the publication date marks its enduring legacy, the novel loses none of its razor-sharp intensity. Smith expands upon the rich, complex tapestry established in the first book, delivering a narrative that thrives on tactical brilliance, cultural nuance, and emotional resonance. 

At the heart of *The Fox* is the overarching theme of displacement and the agonizing process of reinvention. Inda, the disgraced second son exiled from the kingdom of Choraed Elgaer, is a protagonist forged in the crucible of isolation and survival. Having fled his homeland before his thirteenth birthday, Inda is no longer just a royal runaway; he is a seasoned tactician. His time navigating the treacherous maritime world has culminated in the creation of a formidable marine fighting force designed to combat the Venn threat and outmaneuver ruthless pirates. Yet, despite his tactical genius on the water, Inda remains psychologically tethered to a homeland that has marched on without him—a brilliant exploration of how identity cannot be easily shed, even when survival demands it.

Smith’s authorial style is defined by her uncompromising patience and dedication to immersive worldbuilding. She does not spoon-feed her audience history or exposition; instead, she drops the reader directly into a living, breathing geopolitical landscape filled with intricate court politics, military strategy, and diverse cultures. The prose is lean yet evocative, prioritizing psychological realism over gratuitous spectacle. Every battle is rooted in logistics and human cost, and every character—from the beleaguered royals back in Choraed Elgaer to the hardened sailors on the deck—possesses distinct agency, flaws, and motivations. 

What makes *The Fox* particularly striking is its championing of kickass female characters who operate within a deeply stratified society without ever feeling like tokenized archetypes. Smith weaves gender, duty, and power into the very fabric of her kingdoms, ensuring that the political machinations occurring back in Inda’s homeland carry as much narrative weight as his naval skirmishes. 

For readers craving high fantasy that respects their intelligence, offers genuine diversity, and subverts tired tropes of chosen ones and easy redemptions, *The Fox* is an absolute triumph. It is a story about the heavy cost of command, the bitter-sweet nature of homecoming, and the resilience of the human spirit. Buckle up, because Sherwood Smith’s world will not let you go easily.
