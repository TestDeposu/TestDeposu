# The Pariah (Covenant of Steel, #1)

**Author:** Anthony Ryan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

With The Pariah, Anthony Ryan proves once again why he is considered a master storyteller of modern grimdark fantasy. Kicking off the Covenant of Steel series, this 2021 release dives headfirst into a world steeped in mud, blood, and moral ambiguity, offering a gripping narrative that will instantly captivate fans of John Gwynne and George R.R. Martin. 

At the heart of the novel is Alwyn Scribe, a protagonist forged in the crucible of a troubled kingdom. Raised as an outlaw, Alwyn is sharp-witted, fiercely capable with a blade, and bound by the rough camaraderie of forest thieves. Ryan crafts Alwyn not as a shining hero, but as a survivor navigating a brutal society. When a devastating betrayal shatters his world, Alwyn’s story pivots from petty thievery to a relentless pursuit of vengeance. This trajectory eventually thrusts him into the rigid, unforgiving life of a soldier within the king's army.

Ryan’s stylistic strength shines brightest in his gritty world-building and character dynamics. The author expertly avoids cartoonish morality; instead, he paints a richly layered landscape where every character operates in shades of gray. Alwyn finds himself fighting under the command of Lady Evadine Courlain, a compelling noblewoman haunted by terrifying visions of a demonic apocalypse. As Evadine gathers followers and faces the lethal political intrigues of the nobility, Alwyn becomes her shadow—a man torn between his instinct for self-preservation and a burgeoning sense of loyalty. 

The central theme of the novel examines the inescapable weight of one's past. Can a man born to the fringes of society ever truly change his stripes, or is he forever bound to be an outlaw? As arcane forces and human treachery close in around them, Alwyn is forced to confront this existential question amidst the chaos of war. 

Ryan’s prose is lean, propulsive, and relentlessly cinematic. He balances large-scale tactical military fantasy with intimate, character-driven moments of betrayal and retribution. The pacing rarely relents, keeping the reader hooked through sharp dialogue, brutal combat sequences, and simmering political tension. 

The Pariah is a masterclass in epic fantasy world-building and character evolution. It delivers a hard-hitting, visceral experience that establishes a captivating new foundation for the Covenant of Steel series, leaving readers hungry for the next chapter of Alwyn’s bloody crusade.
