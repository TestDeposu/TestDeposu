# King's Property (Queen of the Orcs, #1)

**Author:** Morgan Howell  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

In "King's Property", Morgan Howell crafts a gripping narrative that delves into the complexities of war, identity, and the human condition. The story follows Dar, a young woman born into hardship, who finds herself bound as a slave to a regiment of orcs in King Kregant's army. As Dar navigates the brutal campaign to conquer a neighboring country, she must confront the harsh realities of war and her own place within it.

One of the most striking aspects of Howell's writing is her ability to create a richly detailed world, steeped in the unique culture and language of the orcs. Dar's journey is not just one of survival, but also of discovery, as she learns to communicate with her captors and understand their customs. This nuanced portrayal of the orcs adds depth to the narrative, challenging the traditional fantasy trope of the "savage other".

Howell's prose is economical and evocative, conveying the gritty brutality of war without resorting to gratuitous violence. The action scenes are intense and well-choreographed, while the quieter moments reveal a deep understanding of Dar's inner world. The author's use of language is deliberate and measured, reflecting the harsh realities of Dar's environment.

At its core, "King's Property" is a story about resilience and the human capacity for adaptation. Dar's struggles to survive in a hostile environment serve as a powerful metaphor for the ways in which we all navigate adversity. As she faces treachery from both allies and enemies, Dar must draw upon a deep well of inner strength, forging a sense of identity that is both fragile and unbreakable.

The mystical gift that brings Dar dark, prophetic visions adds a layer of complexity to the narrative, raising questions about the nature of fate and free will. As the war escalates, Dar's visions become increasingly important, serving as a catalyst for her growth and transformation.

Throughout the book, Howell's characterization is superb, with Dar emerging as a fully realized and deeply relatable protagonist. The supporting cast is equally well-drawn, with even minor characters possessing a rich inner life.

If there is a criticism to be made, it is that the pacing can be uneven at times, with certain sections feeling a little rushed. However, this is a minor quibble in an otherwise compelling narrative.

Overall, "King's Property" is a gripping and thought-provoking read, one that will appeal to fans of fantasy and historical fiction alike. With its richly detailed world-building, nuanced characterization, and unflinching portrayal of war, this book is a must-read for anyone looking for a compelling and immersive story.
