# Den of Thieves (Ancient Blades, #1)

**Author:** David Chandler  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

**A Debut that Shines: David Chandler's "Den of Thieves"**

In 2011, David Chandler burst onto the high fantasy scene with "Den of Thieves," the inaugural installment of the Ancient Blades trilogy. This epic and captivating tale introduces readers to the world of Ness, a squalid Free City teeming with thieves, outlaws, and those who prey upon the weak. With its richly woven narrative, ingenious magic system, and well-defined characters, "Den of Thieves" is an exemplary debut that sets the stage for an unforgettable journey.

At its core, "Den of Thieves" explores the theme of consequence and the true cost of power. Our protagonist, Malden, is a skilled thief who has managed to eke out a meager existence in the depths of Ness. However, to join the esteemed guild of Cutbill, a powerful crime lord, Malden must steal the coronet of the Burgrave – a task fraught with perils and guarded by terrifying demons. This desperate endeavor leads Malden down a path from which there is no return, drawing him into a world of darkness and corruption.

Through Malden's eyes, Chandler expertly crafts a tale of complex moralities and gray areas. Our hero is neither purely good nor evil, but a product of his harsh environment. This nuanced approach makes him relatable and empathetic, as Malden navigates the underworld with a mix of wit, cunning, and desperation. His character is deftly woven alongside those of an outlaw knight and an ensorcelled lady, each with their own motivations and backstories, adding depth to the narrative.

One of the most impressive aspects of "Den of Thieves" is its magic system. Chandler's wild and ingenious approach to magic is unlike anything you've experienced before. It's a testament to his creative prowess that he manages to weave magic into the fabric of the world without making it feel contrived or overpowered. Instead, magic becomes an integral part of the setting, influencing the characters and their actions in meaningful ways.

Chandler's writing style is another notable highlight of the book. His prose is engaging, easy to follow, and replete with subtle humor. The pacing is brisk, hurtling the reader through a gripping narrative that refuses to let up. The story is as intricate as a can of worms, with threads of plot expertly woven together to keep the reader guessing.

The world of Ness, though grim and unforgiving, is a rich tapestry of history, politics, and culture. Chandler's attention to detail is impressive, from the intricate architecture of the city to the varied cast of characters that populate it. It's clear that he has invested a great deal of research and creativity into crafting this gritty, immersive setting.

The praise for "Den of Thieves" is well-deserved, as evident from the glowing endorsement by renowned author Dave Duncan. Chandler's characters are well-defined, his magic is ingenious, and his plot is intricately woven. It's a testament to his skill as a storyteller that he can balance humor, action, and drama without weakening the narrative.

In conclusion, "Den of Thieves" is an exceptional debut that sets the stage for an unforgettable trilogy. With its complex characters, ingenious magic system, and engaging story, this book is a must-read for fans of high fantasy. If you're looking for a gripping tale that will keep you on the edge of your seat, look no further than "Den of Thieves."
