# A Plague of Giants (Seven Kennings, #1)

**Author:** Kevin Hearne  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Stepping away from the urban fantasy playground that cemented his reputation with The Iron Druid Chronicles, Kevin Hearne dives headfirst into the expansive, treacherous waters of secondary-world epic fantasy with 2017's A Plague of Giants. The result is a sprawling, ambitious narrative that recalibrates the boundaries of traditional myth-making, offering readers a richly textured universe defined by the delicate, often violent interplay between ordinary humanity and colossal menace. 

At its core, the central theme of A Plague of Giants is the heavy, grinding cost of survival and how ordinary individuals are violently unmoored from their mundane existences to be forged into legends. Hearne brilliantly subverts the traditional "chosen one" trope by distributing his narrative weight across a diverse ensemble of distinct perspectives. There is Tallynd, a grieving mother and hardened soldier whose fierce maternal instincts transform her into a frontline bulwark against an unstoppable tide of fire-wielding giants. There is Dervan, a quiet scholar whose pursuit of historical truth drags him into a web of geopolitical espionage. And there is Abhi, a humble young hunter thrust onto a path of self-discovery who inadvertently stumbles upon a revolutionary magic capable of altering the war's trajectory. 

Instead of isolating these struggles, Hearne weaves them together into a tapestry of collective endurance. The thematic pulse of the novel beats strongest when highlighting empathy, resilience, and the sheer grit required to protect what one loves in the face of annihilation. The giants themselves are not merely mindless monsters; they represent an existential plague, an overwhelming force of nature that exposes the fragility of civilization and forces disparate kingdoms to confront their own internal divisions.

Stylistically, Hearne demonstrates remarkable versatility, shedding the breezy, wisecracking tone of his earlier works in favor of a more deliberate, classical epic cadence. Yet, his signature narrative charm remains entirely intact. The world-building is expansive and deeply immersive, anchored by a brilliantly conceived magic system built upon the "kennings"—mysterious, specialized connections to the natural world, ranging from shape-shifting bards to children who converse with astonishing beasts. 

Uniquely, the book employs a framing device wherein characters recount events to the historian Dervan, creating a layered storytelling structure that feels akin to sitting around a hearth listening to ancient myths being born in real-time. This structural choice elevates the prose, giving it a timeless, oral-tradition rhythm that enriches the lore without slowing the narrative momentum. Hearne balances intimate, character-driven emotional beats with sweeping geopolitical scope, ensuring that even amidst raging battles and magical upheavals, the reader remains deeply invested in the personal stakes of his protagonists.

A Plague of Giants is a triumphant launchpad for the Seven Kennings series. It showcases an author stretching his creative muscles, delivering a complex, vibrant world populated by fiercely human characters. For aficionados of epic fantasy craving fresh mythologies, inventive magic, and high-stakes heroism, Hearne’s latest offering is an absolute must-read that promises even greater spectacles to come.
