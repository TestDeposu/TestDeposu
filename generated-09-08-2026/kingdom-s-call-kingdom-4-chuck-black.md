# Kingdom's Call (Kingdom, #4)

**Author:** Chuck Black  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

Chuck Black returns to the allegorical realm of Arrethtrae with Kingdom's Call, the fourth installment in his celebrated Kingdom series. Known for his ability to translate timeless spiritual truths into pulse-pounding medieval fantasy, Black delivers a compelling narrative that serves as both an exciting adventure and a profound meditation on grace, redemption, and allegiance. 

At its core, the main theme of Kingdom's Call is the radical, life-altering power of unmerited mercy. The story centers on Sir Gavin, a dedicated Noble Knight whose worldview is anchored in strict adherence to the law and unquestioning loyalty to the established order of Chessington. When a mysterious peasant challenges this status quo and claims to be the Son of the King, Gavin reacts with the righteous indignation of a man who believes he is defending absolute truth. Yet, Black brilliantly subverts the traditional knight-versus-villain trope. The true turning point of the novel arrives not through the clashing of swords, but through an act of profound self-sacrifice when the condemned Prince saves the life of the very man sworn to destroy His followers. This pivotal moment forces Gavin, and by extension the reader, to confront the limitations of legalism when stacked against transformative love. 

Black’s authorial style remains brisk, accessible, and cinematic. Writing with a distinct episodic momentum that appeals strongly to both young adults and adult fantasy enthusiasts, he eschews flowery, overly dense prose in favor of direct, action-driven storytelling. His world-building relies heavily on effective allegory—a stylistic choice that calls to mind the classic works of C.S. Lewis and John Bunyan. Chessington and the broader land of Arrethtrae function as a mirror to our own reality, where moral choices carry eternal weight and loyalties are constantly tested. The dialogue is earnest, and the moral stakes are refreshingly clear-cut, providing a sturdy narrative framework where virtue is championed and cowardice is exposed. 

What makes Kingdom's Call particularly striking is how it examines the psychology of pride. Sir Gavin is not inherently evil; rather, he is blinded by his own righteousness and obedience to a code that lacks grace. Black expertly charts Gavin's internal fracture—the agonizing transition from arrogant certainty to humble surrender. The battle scenes are vividly rendered, but the true theater of war in this novel is the human heart. 

For readers who appreciate allegorical fantasy imbued with strong Christian themes, high-stakes medieval action, and a narrative heart centered on redemption, Kingdom's Call is a worthy and memorable journey. Chuck Black proves once again that classic tales of chivalry and sacrifice never lose their power to inspire.
