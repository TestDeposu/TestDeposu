# Infidel (The Lost Books, #2)

**Author:** Ted Dekker  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

**Infidel: A Daring Leap into the Shadows of Destiny**

Ted Dekker’s *Infidel*—the second installment in *The Lost Books* series—isn’t just a sequel; it’s a bold expansion of a mythos so vast and layered that it feels less like a continuation and more like an invitation into an alternate universe where fate is a battleground. Here, the line between past and future blurs, and the stakes aren’t just cosmic—they’re *personal*. Dekker, a master of blending apocalyptic dread with raw, visceral storytelling, crafts a narrative where four ordinary teenagers become the unlikely architects of salvation. But *Infidel* isn’t merely about their journey; it’s about the weight of legacy, the terror of forgotten history, and the question: *What if the darkest chapters of the past are still being written in the present?*

At its core, *Infidel* is a tale of *redemption through revelation*. Thomas Hunter, the grizzled commander of the Forest Guard, is a man haunted by loss—his world ravaged by an unnamed evil, his army dwindling, his faith in order fraying. His desperation to recruit new soldiers isn’t just tactical; it’s existential. He needs fresh blood, fresh hope, to stem the tide of annihilation. But the four teenagers he selects—each carrying their own scars, their own secrets—are far more than cannon fodder. They are the *keys*. Their mission isn’t just to survive; it’s to uncover the *seven lost Books of History*, artifacts so potent they can rewrite reality itself. And when Thomas learns the truth—that these books aren’t just tools but *living records* of a war that transcends their world—he’s thrust into a conflict that dwarfs his own struggles.

Dekker’s genius lies in his ability to weave *philosophical depth* into high-stakes action. The Books of History aren’t mere relics; they’re *testaments* to the cyclical nature of evil. The Dark One, the antagonist lurking in the shadows, isn’t just a villain—he’s a *corruptor of time*, a force that thrives on the ignorance of the past. By controlling these books, he ensures that history repeats itself, dooming both worlds to eternal suffering. This isn’t just a battle for survival; it’s a *clash of destinies*. The teenagers, each with their own moral ambiguities, must grapple with the ethical nightmare of altering history—what if their choices erase someone they love? What if they unknowingly doom an entire civilization? Dekker forces his readers to confront the same questions, making *Infidel* as much a *moral thriller* as it is a fantasy epic.

The author’s prose is *unflinching* in its intensity. Dekker’s writing is a storm of vivid imagery and relentless tension, where every sentence feels like a punch to the gut. His descriptions of the decaying world—crumbling cities, skeletal forests, and the ever-present specter of the Dark One—are suffocating in their realism. Yet, despite the bleakness, there’s a *flicker of hope* in the characters’ defiance. The teenagers aren’t heroes in the traditional sense; they’re flawed, terrified, and often selfish. But their humanity is what makes their journey compelling. Thomas, for all his strength, is a man drowning in grief; the recruits are survivors clinging to scraps of innocence. Dekker doesn’t romanticize their struggle—he *exposes* it, raw and unfiltered.

What sets *Infidel* apart from typical fantasy is its *interconnectedness*. This isn’t just a standalone story; it’s a thread in a much larger tapestry. The seven Books of History hint at a *greater conflict*, one that ties into the first novel (*The Lost Book of Souls*) and promises to unfold across the series. Dekker doesn’t just drop clues—he *weaves* them into the fabric of the narrative, making the reader feel like they’re uncovering secrets alongside the characters. The tension between the past and present is palpable, and the revelation that these books can *alter reality* adds a layer of dread that lingers long after the final page.

Yet, for all its ambition, *Infidel* isn’t without its flaws. Some readers may find the pacing uneven, particularly in the buildup to the climactic moments. The sheer scale of the world and its lore can be overwhelming, requiring a reader who’s willing to suspend disbelief and dive deep. But those who embrace Dekker’s style will find themselves *hooked*—not just by the action, but by the *moral complexity* of the story. The question isn’t just *who will win*, but *at what cost*.

Ultimately, *Infidel* is a testament to Dekker’s ability to blend *apocalyptic dread* with *human drama*. It’s a story about the power of memory, the terror of forgetting, and the courage it takes to rewrite one’s own destiny. In a world where the past is a weapon and the future is uncertain, these four teenagers—flawed, desperate, and unafraid—become the unlikely heroes of a battle that spans dimensions. And in Dekker’s hands, that’s not just a story. It’s a *warning*.
