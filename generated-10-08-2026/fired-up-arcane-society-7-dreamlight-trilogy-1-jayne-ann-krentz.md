# Fired Up (Arcane Society, #7; Dreamlight Trilogy, #1)

**Author:** Jayne Ann Krentz  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Jayne Ann Krentz has long mastered the delicate art of blending pulse-pounding romantic suspense with the speculative wonders of paranormal romance, and her 2009 novel Fired Up stands as a shining testament to this unique literary alchemy. As the inaugural installment of the Dreamlight Trilogy and the seventh entry in the sprawling, interconnected Arcane Society universe, this novel proves that Krentz's creative well is far from dry. She effortlessly transports readers into a meticulously crafted world where modern-day intrigue collides with centuries-old curses and psychic phenomena.

At the very heart of Fired Up lies a compelling exploration of generational legacy and the heavy burdens we inherit from our ancestors. The narrative is anchored by a fascinating historical premise: over three hundred years ago, a reckless competition between alchemist Sylvester Jones and Nicholas Winters forever altered the genetic destiny of the Winters family. This fateful act fractured their lineage, bestowing potent psychic abilities upon some while condemning others to terrifying descents into madness and hallucinations. Krentz uses this ancestral curse not merely as a convenient plot device, but as a profound thematic mirror for her protagonists. The curse serves as a physical manifestation of psychological dread—the fear of losing one’s mind, identity, and control to a destiny written long before birth.

Enter Jack Winters, the modern-day descendant bracing himself for the inevitable onset of the family madness, and Chloe Harper, a sharp, resilient private investigator with the rare ability to read dream energy. When Jack enlists Chloe to help him locate the mythical Burning Lamp—the legendary artifact said to hold the key to breaking the curse—the novel transforms into a high-stakes treasure hunt. Yet, the true treasure of the narrative is the emotional and psychological healing that occurs between the two protagonists. Krentz brilliantly weaves the theme of mutual salvation into the suspenseful plot. Neither character is a helpless damsel or an invincible savior; instead, they are equals navigating a minefield of danger, relying on trust, intellect, and an undeniable chemistry that threatens to overwhelm them.

Krentz’s signature style shines brightly throughout the pages of Fired Up. Known for her snappy dialogue, independent heroines, and brooding yet fiercely protective heroes, she delivers a masterclass in romantic tension. The pacing is relentless, balancing quiet moments of psychic discovery with adrenaline-fueled sequences where unseen enemies lurk in the shadows. The author excels at world-building without overwhelming the reader with exposition, seamlessly integrating the lore of the Arcane Society into a contemporary detective story. The atmosphere is thick with atmospheric mystery, where every shadow might hide a psychic trap, and the search for the Burning Lamp feels genuinely perilous.

Ultimately, Fired Up is a triumphant blend of romance, mystery, and speculative fiction. Krentz reminds her audience why she remains a New York Times bestselling staple, offering a story where love is not just a reward, but a vital, healing force capable of rewriting history. For fans of paranormal romance and suspense alike, this novel is an electrifying journey into the depths of the human psyche and the enduring power of connection.
