# Being a Green Mother (Incarnations of Immortality, #5)

**Author:** Piers Anthony  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1988

In Piers Anthony's captivating novel "Being a Green Mother," the Incarnations of Immortality series takes a fascinating turn with the introduction of Orb, a young woman with a rare gift - the magic of music that can manipulate even the most stubborn of hearts. When she discovers her destiny as the Incarnation of Nature, also known as the Green Mother, Orb embarks on a journey that combines the thrill of romance, mystery, and self-discovery.

At the heart of "Being a Green Mother" lies the intriguing theme of the delicate balance between good and evil, as embodied by the prophecy of Orb's marriage to the embodiment of Evil. This dichotomy is skillfully woven into the narrative, making it challenging for Orb, and the reader, to distinguish between true love and deception. Through this timeless conundrum, Anthony masterfully explores the complexities of the human condition, leaving us questioning the nature of morality and the true meaning of love.

One of the standout features of Anthony's writing in "Being a Green Mother" is his ability to create characters that are relatable, multidimensional, and often morally ambiguous. Orb's innocence, curiosity, and determination make her an endearing protagonist, while her vulnerabilities and doubts make her feel refreshingly human. Natasha, the charming and enigmatic suitor, on the other hand, raises the stakes as he navigates the blurred lines between truth and deception, further underscoring the series' penchant for moral ambiguity.

Anthony's prose is, as always, engaging and accessible, making "Being a Green Mother" an enjoyable read even for those who may not be familiar with the series. His wit and humor are woven seamlessly into the narrative, often through Orb's observations and interactions with the cast of characters. The pacing, too, is well-balanced, with moments of suspense and intrigue perfectly offset by lighter, more satirical interludes.

The themes of identity, morality, and the complexities of relationships are timeless and universally relevant. As Orb navigates her role as the Incarnation of Nature, she must confront her own sense of purpose and the consequences of her choices. Her struggles to balance her emotions, desires, and responsibilities will resonate with readers of all ages, making "Being a Green Mother" a thought-provoking and engaging read.

In conclusion, "Being a Green Mother" is a captivating and thought-provoking addition to the Incarnations of Immortality series. Anthony's signature blend of wit, humor, and philosophical depth makes this installment both entertaining and intellectually stimulating. With its intricate plot, memorable characters, and universal themes, "Being a Green Mother" is a must-read for fans of fantasy, romance, and philosophical fiction.
