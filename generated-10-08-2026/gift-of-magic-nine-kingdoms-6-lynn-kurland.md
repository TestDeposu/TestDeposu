# Gift of Magic (Nine Kingdoms, #6)

**Author:** Lynn Kurland  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

Stepping back into Lynn Kurland’s expansive Nine Kingdoms saga with Gift of Magic is akin to returning to a well-worn, comforting hearth, yet one where the shadows outside the window have grown alarmingly deep. Published in 2012, this sixth installment in the beloved fantasy series weaves together the familiar threads of romance, high fantasy, and intricate world-building, while pivoting sharply toward a mood of escalating peril. Kurland has long been celebrated for her gentle pacing and character-driven narratives, but in this volume, she deftly proves that sweetness and high-stakes tension can coexist to create a thoroughly captivating read.

At the heart of Gift of Magic lies the overarching theme of legacy—both the suffocating weight of inherited burdens and the liberating power of chosen purpose. The narrative continues to follow Sarah of Doìre, blessed (and sometimes burdened) with the unique sight of a dreamweaver, and Ruithneadh of Ceangail, a reluctant sorcerer grappling with the dark magic of his infamous father, Gair. Kurland uses their dynamic to explore the psychological toll of destiny. Ruith is not your typical swaggering fantasy hero; his self-doubt and careful hesitation make him deeply human. Paired with Sarah, whose steadfast intuition guides them through a labyrinth of dangerous runes, the duo embodies the theme that true strength is not born from absolute power, but from humility, partnership, and the willingness to face the unknown.

Kurland’s signature style remains a defining hallmark of the experience. Where contemporary fantasy often leans into relentless grit, rapid-fire action, and grimdark cynicism, Kurland embraces a more classic, lyrical cadence. Her prose is rich in atmosphere, focusing intensely on internal emotional landscapes, gentle banter, and the quiet spaces between monumental conflicts. Even as the plot drives Sarah and Ruith forward on a desperate race to collect and destroy Gair’s corrupted spells before rival mages exploit them, the story maintains a deliberate, reflective rhythm. This style allows the reader to truly inhabit the Nine Kingdoms, tasting the chill of the air, feeling the hum of latent magic, and investing heavily in the emotional evolution of the central couple. 

Yet, what elevates Gift of Magic above a standard romantic fantasy is the subtle shift in the author's tone regarding the antagonists. The synopsis hints that the real enemy is closer and more power-hungry than anticipated, and Kurland executes this twist with a slow-burn dread. She contrasts the external beauty of the fantasy realm with the creeping rot of corruption, illustrating how easily ambition can masquerade as necessity. The journey is populated by a colorful assortment of strange companions and unexpected foes, broadening the scope of the world and keeping the narrative vibrant and unpredictable.

Ultimately, Gift of Magic is a triumphant continuation of a saga that has captured fantasy romance readers for years. It rewards longtime fans with deeper lore and satisfying character growth, while offering a charming, magic-laden portal for anyone seeking an epic tale driven by heart as much as by sorcery. Kurland reminds us that even when the fate of the realm hangs in dire peril, the most potent magic of all remains trust, loyalty, and love.
