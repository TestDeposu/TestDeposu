# Pleasure (Shadowdwellers, #3)

**Author:** Jacquelyn Frank  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

**Pleasure (Shadowdwellers #3) by Jacquelyn Frank: A Whirlwind of Forbidden Desire and Divine Rebellion**

Jacquelyn Frank’s *Pleasure* is a masterclass in romantic tension—where faith, power, and raw, unapologetic lust collide in a way that feels both sacred and sinful. This third installment in the *Shadowdwellers* series doesn’t just deliver two standalone stories of forbidden love; it crafts them with the kind of intoxicating precision that makes you forget you’re reading a fantasy romance. Frank’s prose is lush yet controlled, her world-building immersive without overwhelming, and her characters—flawed, desperate, and achingly human—are the kind you’ll obsess over long after the last page.

At its core, *Pleasure* is a story about the war between duty and desire, where the line between devotion and damnation blurs with every stolen glance, every whispered promise. Sagan, a Sanctuary priest bound by vows of celibacy and order, is thrust into the wilderness, where the wildness of the Alaskan landscape mirrors the chaos within him. His encounter with Valera, a witch entangled in dark magic, isn’t just a romantic spark—it’s a rebellion. She represents everything he’s been taught to fear, yet she’s also the one person who understands the hunger he’s been forced to suppress. Their dynamic is electric, a dance of restraint and surrender where every touch feels like a betrayal and every kiss like a confession. Frank doesn’t shy away from the moral ambiguity here; Sagan’s struggle isn’t just about lust, but about whether his faith can survive the truth of his own body.

Then there’s Malaya and Guin, a power dynamic so charged it could ignite a revolution. Malaya, the Chancellor of the Shadowdwellers, is a woman of strength and cunning, bound by political marriages and the weight of her title. Guin, her devoted but powerless bodyguard, is a man who has spent years watching her—longing for her—while knowing he can never have her. His seduction isn’t just a conquest; it’s an act of defiance. He refuses to let her be taken by another, even if it means breaking every rule that keeps them apart. Their story is a study in obsession, where desire is both weapon and salvation. Frank writes their chemistry with a deft hand, making their clashes feel as inevitable as their eventual surrender.

What sets *Pleasure* apart isn’t just the romance, though—it’s the way Frank weaves in themes of power, sacrifice, and the cost of devotion. The Shadowdwellers world is rich with political intrigue, where alliances are fragile and loyalties can shift on a whim. Malaya’s arranged marriage isn’t just a plot device; it’s a commentary on the ways women are traded like commodities, and Guin’s desperation isn’t just personal—it’s a reflection of a system that keeps them apart. Meanwhile, Sagan’s journey forces readers to question what happens when the rules you’ve lived by no longer serve you. Is faith a cage, or a choice? Can love exist outside of sin?

Frank’s writing is a feast for fans of romantasy—her descriptions are vivid without being melodramatic, her dialogue sharp and full of subtext, and her pacing relentless. She excels at creating characters who feel deeply real, even in a world of magic and myth. The Shadowdwellers series has always been about the tension between light and dark, order and chaos, and *Pleasure* doubles down on that, delivering two stories that are as emotionally gripping as they are visually stunning.

If you love romance that doesn’t shy away from moral gray areas, where the heroes are as flawed as they are compelling, and where every kiss feels like a revolution, then *Pleasure* is for you. Jacquelyn Frank doesn’t just write love stories—she writes about the moments when love becomes the only thing that matters, even if it’s forbidden, even if it’s dangerous. And in a world where rules are meant to be broken, that’s the most dangerous kind of pleasure.
