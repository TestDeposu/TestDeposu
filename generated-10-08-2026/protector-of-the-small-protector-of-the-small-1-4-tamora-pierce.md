# Protector of the Small (Protector of the Small, #1-4)

**Author:** Tamora Pierce  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

Tamora Pierce has long been a master architect of fantasy, but in the Protector of the Small quartet—compiled here in a definitive ebook collection spanning First Test, Page, Squire, and Lady Knight—she achieves something truly remarkable. Set in the vibrant, medieval realm of Tortall, this epic narrative follows Keladry of Mindelan, the first girl to openly enter the gruelling, historically male-dominated training program for knighthood. While Pierce’s earlier works explored heroines gifted with wild magic or espionage prowess, Kel stands out precisely because she possesses no supernatural gifts. She is ordinary, grounded, and intensely human, making her triumphs all the more resonant.

At the heart of this omnibus lies a profound and brilliantly executed theme: the quiet, revolutionary power of resilience, systemic reform, and everyday integrity. Unlike her predecessors who fought dramatic, world-ending evils from the shadows, Kel’s initial battlefield is institutional bias and entrenched prejudice. Tortall is not ready for a female knight, and the road is paved with hazing, blatant sabotage, and hostile skepticism from instructors and peers alike. Yet, Pierce refuses to turn Kel into a grim, hardened anti-hero. Instead, Kel’s weapon of choice is stubborn, unshakeable fairness paired with an unwavering moral compass. She does not just fight to prove she belongs; she systematically changes the culture around her. She rescues abused animals, champions the downtrodden pages, and protects refugees, demonstrating that true chivalry is not about brute strength or grand romantic gestures, but about defending the vulnerable when no one else is watching. 

Pierce’s authorial style remains one of the crown jewels of young adult fantasy. Her prose is crisp, accessible, and deceptively simple, allowing the heavy emotional and political stakes of the narrative to land with maximum impact. What sets Pierce apart is her meticulous world-building and commitment to realistic progression. Kel’s journey is not a montage of easy victories; readers sweat, bleed, and study alongside her through years of rigorous training. We watch Kel grow from an anxious, rigid probationer into a pragmatic, empathetic leader and, finally, a battle-tested commander. 

Furthermore, Pierce excels at populating her universe with rich, multifaceted supporting characters. From the fiercely loyal mage Neal to the reformed bully Joren, the dynamics within the castle walls feel remarkably authentic, capturing the messy politics, deep loyalties, and growing pains of adolescence against a backdrop of impending war. The narrative seamlessly transitions from the intimate, claustrophobic halls of the royal palace to the sweeping, brutal realities of the battlefield, reflecting Kel’s own maturation from a defensive cadet into a visionary strategist. 

The Protector of the Small collection is more than just a nostalgic staple of fantasy literature; it is a timeless manual on leadership, courage, and moral fortitude. Keladry of Mindelan does not merely break glass ceilings—she builds a sturdier, kinder bridge for everyone who follows her. For both longtime devotees of Tortall and newcomers picking up the sword for the first time, this omnibus offers an exhilarating, emotionally stirring journey that proves true strength is forged not in the absence of fear, but in the stubborn refusal to compromise one’s values.
