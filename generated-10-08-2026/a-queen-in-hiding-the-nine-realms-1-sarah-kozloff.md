# A Queen in Hiding (The Nine Realms, #1)

**Author:** Sarah Kozloff  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

A Queen in Hiding marks a bold, cinematic entry into the crowded landscape of epic fantasy, establishing Sarah Kozloff as a formidable new voice in the genre. Released as the ambitious opening salvo of The Nine Realms quartet—a project designed for rapid-fire consumption with its unique four-books-in-four-months rollout—this debut manages to balance breakneck pacing with a surprisingly rich, deeply textured world. At its core, the novel explores a timeless yet potent theme: the arduous, often brutal transformation of a vulnerable child into an unyielding sovereign. 

The narrative centers on Cerulia, the orphaned princess of Weirandale, who is cast out, exiled, and hunted by the very forces meant to protect her. Where typical fantasy tropes might strand a royal heir in pampered obscurity, Kozloff thrusts Cerulia into the unforgiving margins of society. The central theme of the novel is not merely about reclaiming a birthright, but about the painful dismantling of innocence required to achieve true leadership. To survive, Cerulia must shed the expectations of courtly docility and master both the volatile magic humming in her blood and the harsh realities of guerrilla warfare. She is not a born warrior handed a legendary sword; she is a survivor forced to forge her own armor from the scraps of desperation and grit. 

Kozloff’s literary style is markedly visual, justifying the frequent comparisons to cinematic storytelling. Her prose is clean, direct, and propulsive, favoring momentum and atmospheric tension over exhaustive, doorstop-style world-building. She paints the kingdom of Weirandale with visceral strokes—its majestic peaks, its festering political corruption, and the haunting, mystical presence of spirits that actively meddle in mortal affairs. Instead of overwhelming the reader with a history text's worth of lore in the opening chapters, Kozloff unfurls the secrets of The Nine Realms organically through Cerulia’s harrowing journey of escape and resistance. 

The supporting cast elevates the narrative beyond a standard solitary-hero arc. Cerulia’s survival hinges on her ability to assemble a motley crew of rebels, outsiders, and loyalists who challenge her perspective and temper her youthful idealism. This ensemble grounds the fantastical elements in raw human emotion, exploring themes of loyalty, sacrifice, and the heavy moral compromises demanded by rebellion. The antagonists, meanwhile, embody the rot of a corrupt aristocracy, offering a satisfyingly despicable foil to Cerulia’s rising defiance. 

While the novel operates as the foundational first movement of a larger symphony, it successfully avoids feeling like a mere prologue. It delivers genuine stakes, emotional resonance, and a compelling narrative arc that leaves readers hungry for the next installment. Sarah Kozloff has crafted an invigorating start to a massive saga, proving that the classic tropes of royal betrayal and magical awakening still possess immense power when handled with modern pacing and emotional depth. A Queen in Hiding is a breathless, striking invitation to a realm you will not want to leave.
