# Sorcerer to the Crown (Sorcerer Royal #1)

**Author:** Zen Cho  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

Sorcerer to the Crown, the debut novel by Zen Cho, is a captivating tale that delves into the world of magic, mayhem, and the intricacies of the British elite. Set against the backdrop of a once prestigious Royal Society of Unnatural Philosophers, the story follows Zacharias Wythe, the Sorcerer Royal, as he embarks on a perilous journey to Fairyland to unravel the mystery behind England's dwindling magical stores.

Through Cho's masterful storytelling, we are introduced to a world where magic is not only a powerful force but also a highly politicized one. The Royal Society, once the bastion of magical excellence, has fallen into disrepute, and the appointment of Zacharias as the Sorcerer Royal has only added to the controversy. As a man of color in a predominantly white institution, Zacharias faces numerous challenges, and his determination to restore the Society's reputation is admirable.

One of the most striking aspects of the novel is Cho's use of language. Her writing is lyrical, witty, and engaging, with a keen eye for detail that transports readers to the world of 19th-century Britain. The author's command of language is impressive, and her ability to weave together elements of fantasy, adventure, and social commentary is nothing short of remarkable.

At the heart of the story is the theme of power and privilege. Cho skillfully explores the tensions between those who hold power and those who are marginalized, and the ways in which social norms can be both restrictive and liberating. The character of Prunella Gentlewoman, a woman with immense magical power, is a testament to the author's ability to craft complex and compelling female characters.

Through Prunella's journey, Cho sheds light on the limitations placed on women in a patriarchal society, where their abilities are often dismissed or undervalued. The author's portrayal of Prunella's struggles to assert her power in a world dominated by men is both poignant and thought-provoking.

The world-building in Sorcerer to the Crown is equally impressive, with Cho drawing inspiration from various mythologies and folklores to create a richly textured magical system. The author's use of historical and cultural references adds depth to the narrative, making the world feel both familiar and yet, utterly fantastical.

In conclusion, Sorcerer to the Crown is a captivating and thought-provoking novel that will appeal to fans of fantasy, adventure, and historical fiction. Cho's masterful storytelling, coupled with her unique voice and perspective, makes this debut novel a standout in the genre. With its blend of magic, mayhem, and social commentary, this book is sure to delight readers and leave them eagerly anticipating the next installment in the series.
