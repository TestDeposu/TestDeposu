# Scorpica (The Five Queendoms, #1)

**Author:** G.R. Macallister  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

G.R. Macallister’s *Scorpica*, the striking opening salvo in *The Five Queendoms* series, is a bold reimagining of epic fantasy that trades traditional patriarchal tropes for a sprawling, complex matriarchal landscape. Set across five distinct nations that have enjoyed half a millennium of relative peace, the novel immediately hooks the reader with a terrifying, high-concept premise: the sudden, inexplicable cessation of female births. As the "Drought of Girls" drags on for a decade, the foundation of this female-dominated world begins to crumble, transforming a society built on sisterhood and matrilineal succession into a desperate powder keg of paranoia, ambition, and survival. 

At its core, the main theme of *Scorpica* is the fragility of power and the lengths to which individuals—and nations—will go when faced with biological extinction. Macallister does not merely explore a world without girls; she examines the profound psychological and systemic shock waves that ripple through a civilization designed around female lineage. Without the promise of daughters to inherit thrones, tend sacred arts, and maintain bloodlines, the five queendoms quickly pivot from peaceful allies to bitter rivals. The narrative brilliantly dissects how easily ancient bonds dissolve when the existential clock starts ticking. Power, motherhood, politics, and dark magic become dangerously intertwined as leaders scramble to secure their legacies by any means necessary.

Macallister’s stylistic approach is ambitious and sweeping, reminiscent of grand-scale historical fiction infused with dark fantasy elements. Rather than anchoring the narrative to a single protagonist, she deploys a multi-POV structure that spans the breadth of the known world. We follow a diverse ensemble of women caught in the maelstrom: a warrior queen forced to transition from the vulnerability of the birthing bed straight back to the brutal defense of her crown; a healer hiding in the shadows to protect a daughter possessing a volatile, forbidden power; a desperate sovereign flirting with ancient, perilous magic to maintain her grip on reality; and a near-immortal sorcerer whose grand designs threaten to reshape the world entirely. 

Furthermore, the generation of "lastborns"—the young women who managed to slip into existence just before the drought began—serves as a poignant emotional anchor. These girls carry the crushing weight of their dying nations' traditions, treated simultaneously as sacred relics and tragic anomalies. Macallister weaves these disparate threads together with propulsive pacing, ensuring that the sprawling political machinations never overshadow the intimate, visceral stakes of her characters. 

The author’s prose is sharp, unflinching, and atmospheric, painting the five queendoms with distinct cultural strokes while maintaining a cohesive, immersive tone. She avoids easy moral binaries; her queens and commoners alike are flawed, driven by a potent mix of love, duty, terror, and ambition. The world-building is rich and tactile, blending visceral action with intricate courtly intrigue and subtle magic that feels both ancient and dangerous. 

For fans of character-driven epic fantasy who crave complex female characters, high-stakes political maneuvering, and a world order pushed to the absolute brink, *Scorpica* is a triumph. Macallister has crafted a darkly alluring universe where the absence of new life breathes terrifying new momentum into a genre ripe for reinvention.
