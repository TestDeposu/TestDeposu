# Eye of the Oracle (Oracles of Fire, #1)

**Author:** Bryan Davis  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

Eye of the Oracle launches Bryan Davis’s Oracles of Fire trilogy with a sweeping, three‑part saga that reaches from the mythic dawn of creation to the gritty streets of contemporary America. At its heart lies a single, resonant theme: the enduring power of faith to transform identity, even when that identity is buried beneath centuries of myth, bloodline, and betrayal. Davis follows the dragon‑blooded line—from the primordial guardians Makaidos and Thigocia, through the Arthurian era where Merlin’s enchanted wine grants dragons human immortality, to Bonnie Silver, a winged teen unaware that she is the living fulfillment of an ancient prophecy. Each epoch asks the same question: can a creature forged in fire and legend still hear the whisper of Elohim amid the clamor of war, slavery, and modern‑day slayers? The answer, Davis suggests, is yes—if the heart remains open to divine truth.

The novel’s structure mirrors its thematic concern. By dividing the narrative into three distinct books—“In the Beginning,” “Transformation,” and “Refiner’s Fire”—Davis invites readers to experience the same spiritual arc across wildly different settings. This triptych approach does more than showcase world‑building prowess; it reinforces the idea that faith is timeless, adaptable, and capable of bridging epochs. The shifts in tone are handled with a deft hand: the Flood‑era sections read like epic myth, heavy on lyrical description and solemn cadence; the Arthurian middle brings a chivalric, almost romantic flavor, replete with courtly intrigue and the warm wisdom of Merlin; the modern finale thrusts the story into a fast‑paced, urban‑fantasy rhythm, where Bonnie’s struggle feels immediate and personal.

Davis’s style is unmistakably his own: a blend of C.S. Lewis‑inspired allegory and high‑octane YA adventure. He writes with a clarity that makes complex theological concepts accessible without sacrificing depth. Descriptions are vivid yet economical—he paints the scintillating scales of a dragon, the hushed glow of Merlin’s blessed wine, and the stark neon of a city alley with equal precision. Dialogue serves both character development and exposition, often carrying a subtle didactic tone that never feels preachy because it is rooted in the characters’ genuine struggles and doubts. The pacing is brisk; action sequences are interspersed with quieter, reflective moments that allow the spiritual undercurrents to surface.

What truly sets Eye of the Oracle apart is its commitment to showing faith as an active, transformative force rather than a static belief system. Bonnie’s journey—from frightened orphan to courageous bearer of a prophetic legacy—mirrors the reader’s own potential to uncover hidden strengths through trust in something greater than oneself. The dragons, far from being mere beasts, become symbols of the divine spark within humanity, capable of both destruction and redemption depending on the choices of those who wield their power.

Fans of Davis’s Dragons in Our Midst series will find familiar ground in the rich lore and the moral earnestness that permeates the pages, while new readers will discover a stand‑alone entry point that invites them into a larger, interconnected universe. Eye of the Oracle is more than a fantasy prelude; it is a call to look inward, to recognize the “oracle of fire” that burns within each heart, and to let that flame guide one’s path through the ages.
