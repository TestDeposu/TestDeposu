# Passage (The Sharing Knife, #3)

**Author:** Lois McMaster Bujold  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

In Lois McMaster Bujold's "Passage" (The Sharing Knife, #3), the boundaries between cultures and worlds are once again skillfully explored through the eyes of Fawn Bluefield, a resourceful farm girl, and Dag Redwing Hickory, the noble Lakewalker patroller and necromancer. As they navigate the intricate web of their own relationship, the external forces of violence, prejudice, and the supernatural threaten to tear them apart.

Through Bujold's masterful storytelling, the reader is transported to a rich, detailed world where Lakewalkers possess a deep understanding of the natural world and a unique mastery of their own magical abilities. The author's attention to every aspect of this fantasy realm, down to the smallest cultural and linguistic nuances, creates a palpable sense of immersion in this world that is both familiar and yet, utterly unique.

Bujold's character development is nothing short of phenomenal. Dag, the stoic Lakewalker, and Fawn, the vibrant farm girl, each bring with them their own personal demons and insecurities, which they gradually confront and learn to overcome. The romantic tension between them is palpable, making their interactions both poignant and authentic. Their love serves as a beacon of hope in a world ravaged by the devastating powers of the malice – a force of pure evil that seeks to destroy all that is good and pure.

Bujold's use of language is a true masterpiece. With her elegant prose and precise dialogue, she conjures an atmosphere that is both lyrical and suspenseful, drawing the reader inexorably into the world of her characters. She has an uncanny ability to craft compelling narratives that seamlessly blend elements of fantasy, adventure, and coming-of-age, all while maintaining a deep emotional resonance.

The author's exploration of themes such as prejudice, power dynamics, and personal identity serves as a powerful commentary on our own world. Her thought-provoking questions encourage the reader to reflect on their own place within the social hierarchies and relationships they find themselves in. By skillfully navigating these complex issues, Bujold reminds us that it is through understanding and empathy that we may create a more harmonious world.

A key aspect of "Passage" is its ability to subvert and challenge our expectations of traditional fantasy tropes. By presenting a world where women play roles traditionally ascribed to men, and vice versa, the story subtly critiques societal norms and encourages us to consider different perspectives. This is perhaps most evident in Fawn's development as a character, whose quiet strength and resourcefulness gradually supplant her initial inexperience, as she navigates the challenges of her relationship with Dag.

Ultimately, "Passage" (The Sharing Knife, #3) stands as a testament to Lois McMaster Bujold's skill as a master storyteller, world-builder, and character craftsman. With its richly detailed world, memorable characters, and thoughtful exploration of complex themes, Bujold reminds us why she remains one of the most respected writers in the speculative fiction genre.
