# The Shining City (Rhiannon's Ride #2)

**Author:** Kate Forsyth  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2006

In The Shining City, the second installment of her acclaimed Rhiannon’s Ride series, Kate Forsyth proves once again why she is a master storyteller of dark, lyrical fantasy. Building upon the ferocious momentum of *Medio Medicus*, Forsyth plunges her readers back into a vividly constructed world where ancient myth collides with treacherous court politics. This novel is not merely a sequel; it is a deepening exploration of resilience, identity, and the intoxicating, often destructive power of love.

At the heart of the narrative is Rhiannon, a fierce half-human protagonist who was raised by the savage satyricorn tribe and later found salvation in the skies upon the back of a winged horse. Forsyth’s thematic focus in *The Shining City* centers on captivity and liberation—not just physical, but psychological and emotional. Imprisoned in the foreboding Sorrowgate Tower awaiting trial for murder and treason, Rhiannon’s wild spirit is tested as never before. Forsyth brilliantly contrasts the brutal, open expanse of Rhiannon’s past with the claustrophobic stone walls of her cell, mirroring the internal battle the protagonist faces between her untamed instincts and the rigid demands of human civilization.

Forsyth’s writing style is enchanting, seamlessly blending dark gothic elements with high-fantasy intrigue. The nights in Sorrowgate Tower are heavy with atmosphere; Rhiannon is haunted not only by her impending doom but by the malevolent ghost of a dead queen who hungers to possess her mortal shell. These ghostly visitations and prophetic dreams are rendered in lush, chilling prose that wraps around the reader like a shroud. Forsyth has a distinct knack for sensory world-building, making the chill of the stone tower, the weight of injustice, and the bitter sting of perceived betrayal entirely palpable.

As the narrative unfolds, the emotional core of the story shifts toward vulnerability. Lewen, the handsome apprentice-witch who convinced Rhiannon to stay in the human world and hone her magical talents, begins to grow distant. Forsyth handles this romantic subplot with nuanced complexity, avoiding trite tropes in favor of exploring jealousy, insecurity, and the corrosive nature of courtly manipulation. When Rhiannon suspects that a royal princess has cast a spell to steal Lewen’s heart, the novel transforms into a high-stakes psychological thriller wrapped in a magical cloak. 

What makes *The Shining City* truly striking is its refusal to offer easy resolutions. Forsyth’s characters are flawed, desperate, and fiercely human—even when they are dealing with gods, ghosts, and forbidden magic. Rhiannon is no passive damsel waiting for rescue; her vow to win back her lover and orchestrate a daring escape drives the narrative with relentless tension. 

Ultimately, Kate Forsyth has crafted a compelling continuation that expands the borders of her fantasy realm while keeping the intimate, beating heart of her protagonist front and center. *The Shining City* is a dark, mesmerizing tale of survival against impossible odds, destined to captivate both returning fans of the series and new readers alike.
