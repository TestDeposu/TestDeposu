# Gene Of Isis (Mystique Trilogy, #1)

**Author:** Traci Harding  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

**A Gateway to the Divine: Traci Harding’s *Gene of Isis* and the Mythic Resonance of Time**

Traci Harding’s *Gene of Isis*—the first installment in her *Mystique Trilogy*—is a literary alchemy, weaving together the threads of history, mysticism, and female agency into a tapestry that feels both timeless and urgently relevant. At its core, the novel is a meditation on the cyclical nature of power, the weight of inherited destiny, and the quiet rebellions of women who refuse to be confined by the eras they inhabit. Harding’s prose is lush yet precise, her storytelling a seamless blend of historical depth and mythic grandeur, making *Gene of Isis* less a traditional time-travel adventure and more a spiritual odyssey across centuries.

The novel introduces three women—Mia Montrose, Ashlee Granville, and Lillet du Lac—each bound by blood, prophecy, and an unshakable connection to the *Gene of Isis*, a divine lineage said to hold the key to a dimension of light. Mia, a modern academic, is the most grounded of the trio, her skepticism a foil to the supernatural forces pulling her toward an ancient destiny. Ashlee, a Victorian clairvoyant, navigates the stifling constraints of 19th-century society with the quiet defiance of a woman who knows her gifts are both her curse and her salvation. Lillet, a 13th-century Cathar priestess, embodies the fierce resilience of those fighting for faith and freedom in the face of annihilation. Their stories intersect not just in time but in purpose, each woman a thread in a larger tapestry of resistance, revelation, and rebirth.

Harding’s strength lies in her ability to ground the fantastical in the tangible. The novel’s historical settings—from the oppressive elegance of Victorian England to the brutal siege of Montségur—are rendered with meticulous care, ensuring that the supernatural elements never feel like mere escapism. Instead, they emerge organically from the cultural and spiritual landscapes of each era. The *Gene of Isis* itself is not just a plot device but a living force, a symbol of feminine power that has been suppressed, sought after, and ultimately reclaimed. Harding’s exploration of this theme resonates deeply in an era where the erasure of women’s contributions to history and spirituality is still a persistent issue. By centering these women—each marginalized in their own way—she offers a counter-narrative, one that celebrates their agency and the sacredness of their struggles.

The prose is another standout feature. Harding’s writing is evocative without being florid, her descriptions immersive yet never overwhelming. She has a knack for capturing the essence of a moment—whether it’s the quiet dread of a woman sensing a vision or the raw desperation of a besieged fortress—without resorting to melodrama. Her dialogue is sharp and authentic, giving each character a distinct voice, from Mia’s dry academic wit to Lillet’s fierce, uncompromising spirit. The pacing is well-balanced, with each time period receiving its due attention without feeling rushed, and the transitions between eras are seamless, allowing the reader to fully inhabit each world before being pulled into the next.

What makes *Gene of Isis* particularly compelling is its refusal to simplify its themes. Harding doesn’t shy away from the complexities of faith, sacrifice, or the cost of destiny. The novel grapples with the idea of predestination versus free will, asking whether some women are truly chosen or whether they simply recognize their own power when it’s finally given to them. The character of Albray, the enigmatic Sion knight who guards the Grail lineage, serves as a fascinating counterpoint—a figure of authority who is neither wholly benevolent nor malevolent, but a necessary guide in a journey that is ultimately the women’s to claim.

The book’s title itself is a promise, and Harding delivers on it. *Gene of Isis* is not just about unlocking a gateway to another dimension; it’s about unlocking the potential within these women, and by extension, within all women who have been told their voices don’t matter. It’s a story about the enduring power of the feminine divine, about the ways in which history—both personal and collective—can be rewritten when those who have been silenced finally speak.

For readers who crave mythic storytelling with depth and substance, *Gene of Isis* is a revelation. It’s a novel that feels like a lost relic, one that has been waiting for its time to be rediscovered. Harding’s blend of historical fiction, fantasy, and spiritual inquiry makes it a standout in the genre, a book that lingers long after the final page, inviting reflection on the threads that connect us across time and the stories we are all still writing.
