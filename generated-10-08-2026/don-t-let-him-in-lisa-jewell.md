# Don't Let Him In

**Author:** Lisa Jewell  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

Master of domestic suspense Lisa Jewell returns with Don’t Let Him In, a chilling and expertly paced psychological thriller that reaffirms her undisputed reign over the genre. Published in 2025, this instant #1 New York Times bestseller wastes no time grabbing the reader by the throat and refusing to let go. Blending acute emotional observation with the breathless dread of a slow-burn nightmare, Jewell dissects the terrifying vulnerability of modern romance and the hidden monsters that often lurk behind polished smiles and charming façades.

At the heart of the narrative is Nick Radcliffe, a man who radiates substance, good taste, and quiet sophistication. To Nina Swann, a newly widowed woman navigating the desolate landscape of grief, Nick feels like a miraculous second chance at happiness—a warm light cutting through the shadows of loss. But psychological thrillers thrive on the friction between perception and reality, and Nina’s sharp-eyed adult daughter, Ash, isn’t buying the fairy tale. To Ash, Nick’s perfection is unnerving; he is too slick, too rehearsed, too good to be true. Her instinctive unease sets off a clandestine investigation, unspooling a disturbing trail of coincidences that hint at a man living a fragmented existence. 

Meanwhile, in a neighboring town, a parallel domestic rhythm begins to fracture. Martha lives a seemingly idyllic life with her infant daughter and her devoted husband, Alistair. Yet, peace is a fragile construct in a Lisa Jewell novel. Alistair’s work trips become increasingly frequent and protracted, leaving Martha alone with a gnawing, visceral intuition that something is fundamentally wrong. As Nina, Martha, and Ash unknowingly march toward a violent convergence, the title’s titular warning resonates with deafening clarity: Don’t let him in.

What elevates Don’t Let Him In far above standard genre fare is Jewell’s signature stylistic brilliance. She possesses a rare empathy for her characters, rendering them with such psychological depth and flawed humanity that their eventual terror feels intensely personal. She understands that the most effective scares do not stem from monsters in the dark, but from the quiet betrayals committed by the people we invite into our homes and our beds. The narrative structure—alternating perspectives that gradually draw tighter like a noose—builds an agonizing momentum. Freida McFadden aptly calls the book gripping, shocking, and masterful, and that praise is well-earned; Jewell orchestrates her plot twists with the precision of a master surgeon, withholding information just long enough to maximize the psychological impact when the truth finally shatters the illusion.

In Don’t Let Him In, Jewell explores the dark theme of identity theft of the soul—how easily a practiced predator can weaponize empathy, loneliness, and trust. It is a cautionary tale for the modern era, wrapped in an addictive, unputdownable package. For both longtime devotees of psychological suspense and newcomers looking for a masterclass in tension, this novel is an absolute triumph that will have you double-checking your door locks long after the final page.
