# The Cross-Time Engineer (Conrad Stargard, #1)

**Author:** Leo Frankowski  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

**The Cross-Time Engineer: A Time-Traveling Odyssey of Genius, Folly, and Historical Firepower**

Leo Frankowski’s *The Cross-Time Engineer* (the first in the *Conrad Stargard* series) is a genre-defying masterpiece—a high-stakes, fast-paced adventure that blends alternate history, military strategy, and the sheer absurdity of a modern-day engineer thrust into the crucible of the 13th century. At its core, this novel is a meditation on ambition, the fragility of history, and the terrifying weight of unintended consequences. Conrad Schwartz, a disgraced but brilliant engineer, doesn’t just stumble into the past; he *crashes* into it with the force of a runaway locomotive, and from that moment on, his life becomes a desperate, often hilarious, and occasionally tragic attempt to reshape the world around him.

The premise is simple yet explosive: Conrad, suffering from a hangover in modern Poland, wakes up in 1231—only to realize he’s been stranded in a time where his knowledge of steam engines, industrialization, and military tactics would either make him a god or a martyr. The challenge? Poland is a backwater kingdom, the Mongols are poised to devastate Europe, and the Church is already suspicious of "heretical" innovations. Frankowski doesn’t just ask, *"What if a modern man could change history?"* He answers with a novel that’s equal parts *Foundation*, *The Name of the Rose*, and *Game of Thrones*—but with the intellectual rigor of a history buff and the adrenaline rush of a heist movie.

What makes *The Cross-Time Engineer* so compelling is its balance between grand historical stakes and intimate character drama. Conrad isn’t just a time-traveling savior; he’s a flawed, stubborn, and often self-destructive protagonist whose greatest strength—his genius—becomes his greatest weakness. He’s a man who sees the future clearly but struggles to navigate the present, whether it’s convincing medieval nobles to adopt his ideas or avoiding the Inquisition’s noose. Frankowski’s writing is sharp, witty, and relentlessly engaging, with a knack for blending technical detail (Conrad’s tinkering with early steam engines is both fascinating and hilarious) with brutal, high-stakes action. The battle scenes are visceral, the political intrigue is razor-sharp, and the humor—particularly in Conrad’s interactions with his medieval peers—keeps the novel from feeling like a dry history lesson.

The novel’s strength lies in its exploration of power and legacy. Conrad isn’t just trying to save Poland; he’s trying to *invent* it, to force it onto a trajectory that would make it a superpower. But history resists such grand designs. The Mongols still come. The Church still burns heretics. And Conrad’s own hubris often works against him. Frankowski doesn’t glorify his protagonist; Conrad is a man who believes he can outthink fate, only to learn that the past is as unpredictable as the future. The result is a story that’s as much about the limits of human control as it is about the thrill of defying them.

Frankowski’s style is a delight—a mix of dry humor, rapid-fire dialogue, and deep historical immersion. He doesn’t shy away from the brutality of medieval life, but he also doesn’t treat it with the solemnity of a textbook. Instead, he uses it as a playground for Conrad’s schemes, whether he’s inventing a primitive version of the assembly line to produce weapons or trying (and failing) to convince a skeptical bishop that education is the key to civilization. The result is a novel that feels both timeless and fresh, a blend of classic adventure storytelling with a modern twist.

For fans of alternate history, military strategy, and time-travel narratives, *The Cross-Time Engineer* is a must-read. It’s a book that rewards readers who love complex plots, clever twists, and protagonists who are as compelling as they are flawed. And while the series spans three novels, this first book stands alone as a thrilling, thought-provoking adventure—a story about a man who tries to bend history to his will, only to discover that sometimes, the best thing he can do is survive.
