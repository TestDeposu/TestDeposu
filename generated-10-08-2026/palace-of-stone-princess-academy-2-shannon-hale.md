# Palace of Stone (Princess Academy, #2)

**Author:** Shannon Hale  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

When Shannon Hale introduced readers to the isolated mountain quarry and the industrious young women of Mount Eskel in her Newbery Honor-winning *Princess Academy*, she crafted a masterclass in coming-of-age fantasy. In its eagerly awaited 2012 sequel, *Palace of Stone*, Hale pulls her beloved protagonist, Miri, out of her familiar element and drops her straight into the churning, politically charged heart of the capital city, Asland. The result is a striking, deeply relevant expansion of a fantasy world that proves revolutions are rarely built on fairy tales alone.

The core brilliance of *Palace of Stone* lies in its exploration of a sophisticated thematic shift. In the first book, the narrative focused on self-worth, education, and sisterhood within a confined, albeit challenging, space. Here, Hale broadens her lens to tackle systemic inequality, the friction between tradition and reform, and the heavy price of social change. Miri arrives in Asland to help her friend Britta prepare for her royal wedding and to study at the prestigious Queen's Castle. Yet, the glittering marble and bustling commerce of the capital cannot mask the glaring disparity between the privileged city dwellers and the impoverished commoners. 

Hale brilliantly juxtaposes Miri’s nostalgic longing for her humble mountain home with the intoxicating, dangerous allure of revolutionary ideas circulating among the city’s youth. Miri finds herself caught in an ideological tug-of-war. Is true progress achieved by uprooting the existing power structures entirely, or is there value in preserving the foundational traditions that shaped who she is? Through Miri’s internal conflict, Hale avoids easy answers, respecting her young readers—and the characters themselves—enough to grapple with the messy, often contradictory nature of political awakening.

True to Hale’s signature style, the prose is lyrical yet grounded, driven by sharp emotional intelligence and understated wit. She has a rare gift for writing fantasy that feels intimately human. The magic system—specifically the quarry-speech, the psychic resonance between people who share the same stone—remains a potent metaphor for community, empathy, and interconnectedness. Even when the setting shifts from the rugged mountain to the opulent palace, the magic retains its earthy, tactile quality, anchoring the grand political drama in the personal bonds between friends.

Furthermore, Hale’s character development remains top-tier. Miri is not a flawless warrior-princess, but a thoughtful, occasionally insecure, and fiercely loyal young woman whose intellectual curiosity drives her forward. Her romantic entanglements—navigating a new crush in the city alongside her enduring ties to her mountain past—feel authentic to the turbulence of youth, never overshadowing the primary stakes of the narrative. The friendships forged in the academy continue to be the beating heart of the series, showing that collective solidarity is the ultimate weapon against oppression.

*Palace of Stone* is far more than a standard middle-grade or young adult sequel; it is a compelling allegory about civic duty, the ethics of rebellion, and the responsibilities of a voice that has finally been heard. Shannon Hale weaves a tapestry of suspense, heart, and social consciousness that will leave readers breathless. For anyone who has ever wondered what happens after the princess is chosen and the fairy tale supposedly ends, this novel delivers a dazzling, thought-provoking answer.
