# Beggars in Spain (Sleepless, #1)

**Author:** Nancy Kress  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

In a future where the relentless pursuit of human optimization collides with our deepest evolutionary insecurities, Nancy Kress’s seminal science fiction work, Beggars in Spain, asks a chilling question: What happens when the biological haves no longer need the rest of humanity? Adapted from her Hugo and Nebula Award-winning novella, this novel is not merely a speculative romp about genetic engineering; it is a sharp, fiercely intellectual sociological crucible that examines how society treats those who possess an unfair advantage.

At the narrative’s pulsing heart is Leisha Camden, a woman engineered to be a member of the "Sleepless"—a growing class of genetically modified humans who require zero sleep. Gifted with extraordinary intelligence, enhanced health, and an extra third of a lifetime freed from the necessity of rest, Leisha and her kind represent the ultimate leap forward. Yet, rather than being embraced as the vanguard of a golden age, the Sleepless find themselves cast out into the periphery of a fearful society. They are envied, resented, and eventually targeted by a rising tide of political repression and mob violence that threatens to tear the species apart.

Kress’s greatest triumph in this novel lies in her refusal to rely on cheap space-opera thrills. Instead, she anchors her speculative premise in rigorously explored sociological and economic consequences. The style is marked by a compelling dialectic—a sharp, conversational, and philosophically dense sparring match of ideas. Through Leisha and her contemporaries, Kress interrogates the very bedrock of the modern social contract. What do we owe one another? Does a meritocracy inevitably breed cruelty? If one group of people possesses biological superiority, do they owe anything to the "beggars" left behind in the sleep-bound world? 

The title itself serves as a brilliant thematic anchor, echoing an economic parable used within the text to explore the ethical decay of a world divided strictly between producers and consumers, the optimized and the obsolete. Kress paints this tomorrow not with broad, dystopian strokes, but with the quiet, unsettling plausibility of a headline you might read ten years from now. The tension between the genetically altered elite and normal humans feels intensely modern, echoing contemporary debates surrounding bioethics, economic inequality, and the fear of the "other."

Leisha Camden emerges as a remarkably nuanced protagonist—caught between the insular, protective culture of her fellow Sleepless and her desperate desire to build a bridge to the "Sleepers" who hate her. Through her eyes, the novel exposes the tragedy of human tribalism. Kress exposes how easily fear mutates into hatred, and how the promise of a utopian future can be easily subverted by the petty insecurities of the present. 

Beggars in Spain is a masterclass in speculative fiction. It is a thought-provoking, elegantly crafted novel that challenges the reader to look past the shiny veneer of technological progress and examine the moral cost of our deepest ambitions. For anyone drawn to intelligent science fiction that treats its audience with respect, Kress’s brilliant exploration of biological advantage and human nature is an essential, unforgettable journey.
