# Children of Fire (The Chaos Born, #1)

**Author:** Drew Karpyshyn  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

When a master storyteller known for crafting some of the most immersive universes in modern gaming and tie-in fiction steps into the realm of original epic fantasy, the result is nothing short of electrifying. With Children of Fire, the inaugural volume of The Chaos Born series published in 2013, Drew Karpyshyn successfully transitions his proven knack for relentless pacing, high-stakes action, and complex morality into a brand-new, wholly original world. Known previously for his work on blockbuster franchises like Mass Effect and Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic, Karpyshyn brings a distinctly cinematic lens to traditional epic fantasy, proving that his narrative ambitions extend far beyond the stars.

At the heart of Children of Fire lies a compelling exploration of corruption, legacy, and the cyclical nature of power. The novel’s mythology is anchored in antiquity: the tale of Daemron, a mortal champion chosen by the gods to fight the demonic spawn of Chaos. Gifted with three formidable talismans—a sword, a ring, and a crown—Daemron’s immense power ultimately poisoned his soul. Consumed by pride, he betrayed the Immortals, forcing them to make the ultimate sacrifice to seal him and the forces of Chaos behind a magical barrier known as the Legacy. 

Karpyshyn’s central theme examines how the sins and ambitions of the past inevitably bleed into the present. As the Legacy begins to fade and the ancient threat of Daemron stirs once more, the narrative shifts to the modern era, where four children are born of suffering and strife. Each child embodies a fragmented aspect of Daemron’s terrifying potential—wizard, warrior, prophet, and king. Bound by a mysterious connection that transcends bloodlines, these youths stand at a perilous precipice. They are the linchpin of existence; their choices will either reinforce the fading barrier or shatter it entirely, unleashing unmitigated vengeance upon the mortal realm.

What distinguishes Karpyshyn’s style in Children of Fire is his ability to balance sweeping, grandiose world-building with gritty, character-driven storytelling. As noted by Publishers Weekly, the novel breathes realism and an overshadowing menace into classic mythic archetypes, marrying the high-stakes wonder of traditional fantasy with the cynicism and emotional complexity demanded by modern readers. There is a dark, biting edge to his prose—aptly described by bestselling author Tracy Hickman as a "dark-chocolate fantasy"—that keeps the pages turning with compulsive readability. 

Rather than relying solely on dry exposition, Karpyshyn lets his world unfold through the crucible of intense action and personal turmoil. The magic system and the ancient lore feel earned and weighty, deeply integrated into the cultural psychology of the people who live in the shadow of the Legacy. The four central figures are not idealized, untouchable heroes from the outset; they are scarred, modern individuals shaped by hardship, making their eventual convergence all the more gripping. 

Children of Fire is a rousing, fast-paced quest fantasy that satisfies fans of traditional epic world-building while delivering the visceral thrills and sharp character dynamics of contemporary speculative fiction. Karpyshyn has crafted a masterful, doom-laden tapestry that firmly establishes him alongside luminaries of the genre like Brandon Sanderson and Peter V. Brett. For readers seeking a dark, intricately layered adventure filled with impending dread, ancient magic, and reluctant heroes, this explosive first installment is an absolute must-read.
