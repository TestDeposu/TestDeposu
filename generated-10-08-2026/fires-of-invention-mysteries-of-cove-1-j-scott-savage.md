# Fires of Invention (Mysteries of Cove, #1)

**Author:** J. Scott Savage  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Unknown

Deep within the jagged, coal-rich heart of a mountain hides Cove, a subterranean city carved out of necessity, where fire and invention are not merely tools of convenience, but the very lifespans of civilization. In Fires of Invention, the opening installment of J. Scott Savage’s acclaimed Mysteries of Cove series, the author constructs a high-octane steampunk universe that brilliantly marries Jules Verne-esque mechanics with the breathless urgency of young adult survival fiction. 

Savage wastes no time immersing the reader in a claustrophobic yet wondrous world. Cove is a society forged in fear, perpetually threatened by the dreaded Scourge—monstrous subterranean creatures that lurk in the unmapped dark beyond the city's blast doors. To keep the darkness at bay, the citizens rely entirely on the Guild of Engineers, masterminds of steam, gears, and combustion. At the center of this gripping narrative is 12-year-old Trent Colkon, an aspiring inventor whose imagination far outpaces the rigid, archaic rules of his society. Alongside his resourceful sister, Jael, Trent stumbles upon a forgotten history and a series of mechanical secrets that challenge everything Cove believes about its past—and its precarious future.

What elevates Fires of Invention above standard middle-grade and YA genre fare is Savage’s masterful handling of pacing and world-building. The author's style is kinetic and tactile. He doesn't just describe a machine; he makes you hear the rhythmic chug of pistons, smell the ozone and coal dust, and feel the searing heat of the forge. The technology of Cove feels gloriously tangible, breathing life into a setting that could have easily felt dreary. Instead, through Trent’s eyes, the subterranean city pulses with an infectious sense of wonder and rebellious curiosity.

The central theme of the novel examines the dangerous friction between tradition and innovation. Cove’s elders enforce strict conformity, arguing that straying from established engineering protocols invites destruction from the Scourge. Trent’s journey, however, champions the defiant spirit of inquiry. He embodies the idea that true survival does not stem from blind obedience to a stagnant past, but from creative adaptation and the courage to ask "what if?" Savage uses this dynamic to craft a thoughtful allegory about critical thinking, reminding young readers that the greatest threats to a society are often not external monsters, but internal stagnation and the suppression of truth.

Characterization is another bright spot. Trent is an endearing protagonist—imperfect, endlessly inventive, and driven by a fierce loyalty to his family. His dynamic with Jael provides a brilliant emotional anchor, showcasing sibling cooperation that is both realistic and fiercely protective. When the plot plunges them into mortal peril, their reliance on one another grounds the high-stakes mechanical action in genuine heart.

Fires of Invention is a triumphant launch to a series that treats its audience with immense respect, offering brains as well as brawn. It is a thrilling, gear-turning adventure that will leave readers desperate to grab the next wrench and plunge back into the depths of the mountain. J. Scott Savage has engineered a modern classic of steampunk fantasy, proving that the brightest fires are often ignited by the youngest minds.
