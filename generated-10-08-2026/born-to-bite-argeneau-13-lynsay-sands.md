# Born to Bite (Argeneau, #13)

**Author:** Lynsay Sands  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

Title: Lethal Husbands and Immortal Laughs: A Critique of Lynsay Sands’s Born to Bite

For readers weary of brooding, self-serious immortals who spend their endless nights gazing mournfully at the moon, Lynsay Sands offers a deliciously chaotic antidote. In Born to Bite, the thirteenth installment of her wildly popular Argeneau series, Sands returns to the trademark blend of sultry paranormal romance and laugh-out-loud domestic comedy that has cemented her status as a staple of the genre. Published in 2010, this installment proves that even after more than a decade of blood, bites, and bonding, the Argeneau family can still deliver a fresh cocktail of danger and desire.

The central theme of Born to Bite revolves around the perilous intersection of mortality, matrimony, and mystery. At its core, the novel explores the fragility of human life when intertwined with ancient immortal power—specifically, through the ominous lens of a historical Bluebeard trope. When a breathtaking immortal man goes through three wives who tragically fail to remain undead, the vampire establishment naturally raises an eyebrow. Enter the Argeneau family’s rugged, chopper-riding Enforcer, dispatched to investigate whether these tragic demises are mere coincidence or something far more sinister. 

Sands expertly anchors her supernatural stakes in very human anxieties. The mystery element injects a palpable sense of urgency into the narrative, transforming what could easily be a standard romance into a high-stakes whodunit. As the Enforcer digs deeper into the mystery of the thrice-widowed immortal, the novel questions the nature of trust, the baggage of past relationships, and the lengths to which one will go to protect the vulnerable. 

Where Sands truly shines, however, is in her distinct authorial style. While contemporary paranormal romance often leans heavily into dark, gothic aesthetics, Sands champions a lighter, effervescent tone. Her prose is breezy, conversational, and relentlessly witty. She has a rare talent for puncturing moments of intense sensual tension with disarming humor, grounding her ageless, god-like vampires in quirky, highly relatable domestic habits. The Argeneaus may possess fangs and telepathic abilities, but they argue, meddle, and worry about family dinners just like any ordinary, albeit eccentric, clan. 

This juxtaposition of the extraordinary and the mundane creates a uniquely comforting reading experience. Fans of Charlaine Harris’s Sookie Stackhouse series will find a kindred spirit in Sands’s work. Both authors share an appreciation for quirky small-town or insular dynamics, strong-willed protagonists, and a refusal to take the supernatural too seriously. The romance in Born to Bite is undeniably steamy—Sands never skimps on the heat between her protagonists—yet it is always balanced by genuine warmth and comedic timing. 

Of course, long-running series face the dual challenge of satisfying loyal veterans while remaining accessible to newcomers. Born to Bite navigates this skillfully by leaning heavily into the self-contained mystery of the plot. While series enthusiasts will delight in catching up with the sprawling Argeneau lore and familiar character dynamics, the central investigation provides a strong enough narrative spine for a first-time reader to sink their teeth into. 

In summary, Lynsay Sands has crafted a fast-paced, entertaining romp that satisfies both the craving for passionate romance and the itch for a good mystery. Born to Bite is vibrant, humorous, and delightfully addictive. For anyone looking to escape into a world where danger is hot, immortals are deeply flawed, and laughter is always just a page away, this book is an absolute must-read.
