# Beauty

**Author:** Sheri S. Tepper  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1991

In Sheri S. Tepper's enchanting and poignant novel, Beauty, the celebrated science fiction author masterfully weaves a rich tapestry of fairy tale, fable, and ecological warning. Released in 1991, Beauty stands as a testament to Tepper's skill in crafting immersive, thought-provoking stories that delve into the intricacies of love, loss, hope, and despair.

On the surface, Beauty appears to be a traditional retelling of the classic "Sleeping Beauty" narrative, with a wicked aunt's curse afflicting the protagonist, a young woman named Beauty, on her sixteenth birthday. However, as the story evolves, it becomes clear that Tepper's exploration is far more complex and nuanced. The world she creates is a place both enchanting and frightening, replete with magical creatures, treacherous landscapes, and a deep, abiding connection to the natural world.

At its core, Beauty is a novel about the delicate balance between human existence and the environment. Tepper posits a future where the natural world is on the brink of collapse, and the consequences of our actions have far-reaching, disastrous effects. Her world is not unlike our own, a stark reminder that the choices we make today will determine the fate of our planet tomorrow. This potent eco-feminist message is woven throughout the narrative, as Beauty navigates a world where the lines between magic and reality are increasingly blurred.

Tepper's writing style is a key element in the success of Beauty. Her prose is evocative and descriptive, conjuring vivid images of the fantastical landscapes and creatures that populate her world. Her use of language is precise and economical, conveying the weight of emotions and the intensity of human experience. Through Beauty, Tepper masterfully explores the complexities of love, revealing it to be a multifaceted and capricious emotion that can both heal and harm.

One of the most striking aspects of Beauty is its exploration of the relationship between women and power. Throughout the novel, Tepper critiques patriarchal societies and the ways in which they devalue and dismiss feminine perspectives. Her female characters are multidimensional and richly drawn, imbued with agency and a deep understanding of the interconnectedness of all living things. Beauty herself is a compelling protagonist, navigating a complex web of relationships and alliances as she strives to save her world from the brink of collapse.

In Beauty, Sheri S. Tepper has crafted a novel that is both a love letter to the natural world and a warning about the consequences of our actions. With its lush, descriptive prose and nuanced exploration of human emotion, Beauty is a must-read for fans of science fiction, fantasy, and eco-feminist literature. Tepper's writing is a true masterpiece, weaving together the threads of fairy tale, fable, and ecological warning to create a truly unforgettable narrative.
