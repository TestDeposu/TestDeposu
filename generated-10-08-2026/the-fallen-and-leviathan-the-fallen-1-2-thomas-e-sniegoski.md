# The Fallen and Leviathan (The Fallen, #1-2)

**Author:** Thomas E. Sniegoski  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

Title: Walking the Line Between Heaven and Hell: A Review of Thomas E. Sniegoski’s The Fallen and Leviathan

In the crowded landscape of young adult urban fantasy, stories featuring ordinary teenagers burdened with extraordinary destinies are a dime a dozen. Yet, Thomas E. Sniegoski’s omnibus edition, combining the first two installments of his gripping series, *The Fallen and Leviathan*, manages to carve out a distinct and hauntingly atmospheric niche. Originally published as part of *The Fallen* quartet, this bind-up invites readers into a morally gray cosmos where the celestial and the terrestrial collide, driven by a protagonist who is refreshingly flawed and remarkably grounded.

At the heart of the narrative is Aaron Corbet, a young man who appears entirely normal until the eve of his eighteenth birthday triggers a terrifying metamorphosis. Sniegoski cleverly eschews the pristine, halo-wearing tropes of traditional angel lore. Instead, Aaron discovers he is a Nephilim—the offspring of an angel and a mortal—imbued with powers that terrify him as much as they fascinate. More importantly, he learns he is a figure of pivotal prophecy, tasked with the monumental burden of uniting angels, mortals, and ancient cosmic Powers, some of which are violently determined to annihilate him before he can fulfill his potential.

The central theme of the narrative pivots on identity and the burden of choice. Sniegoski explores what it means to be caught between two worlds—neither fully human nor fully divine. Aaron’s internal struggle mirrors the overarching cosmic conflict: a war not just between good and evil, but of free will versus predestination. The author does not present a sanitized universe where heroes wear white and villains wear black. The angels Aaron encounters can be cold, dogmatic, and unsympathetic, while his own demonic heritage constantly tempts him with power and belonging. This blurring of lines elevates the story above standard teen-hero fare, transforming a standard coming-of-age tale into a deeper meditation on integrity, sacrifice, and the assertion of one's own humanity in the face of insurmountable destiny.

Sniegoski’s writing style is fast-paced, cinematic, and remarkably visceral. He possesses a distinct talent for world-building that relies on urban decay juxtaposed with ethereal majesty. The prose is unpretentious, prioritizing propulsive momentum and character-driven tension over florid exposition. Action sequences are choreographed with a kinetic energy that makes the pages turn themselves, yet the author wisely punctuates the supernatural chaos with quiet, poignant moments of adolescent angst and familial longing. Aaron’s vulnerability is his greatest asset; despite his growing cosmic abilities, he remains fundamentally relatable, worrying about his foster family, his friends, and his place in a world that suddenly feels entirely alien.

*Leviathan*, the second half of this bind-up, successfully expands the scope of the universe introduced in the first book, deepening the mythology and raising the narrative stakes. Sniegoski introduces new threats and complicates old alliances, ensuring that the momentum never lags. The mythology draws cleverly from apocryphal lore, giving the story an ancient, weighty texture that grounds the supernatural elements in rich, historical mythos. 

For readers seeking an engaging blend of dark urban fantasy, high-stakes mythology, and a deeply sympathetic protagonist who refuses to be neatly categorized by fate, *The Fallen and Leviathan* is an absolute must-read. Sniegoski delivers a thrilling ride that proves the battle for the soul of the universe is often fought in the turbulent heart of a teenager just trying to figure out who he is.
