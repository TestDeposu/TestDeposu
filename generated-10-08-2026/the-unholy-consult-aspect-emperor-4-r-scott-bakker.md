# The Unholy Consult (Aspect-Emperor, #4)

**Author:** R. Scott Bakker  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

R. Scott Bakker's masterful conclusion to the Aspect-Emperor series, The Unholy Consult, is a searing, unflinching portrayal of the darkest aspects of human nature. In a world ravaged by war, violence, and madness, Bakker's trademark philosophical and psychological depth propels us into the heart of existential despair, leaving us questioning the very fabric of morality and the nature of reality itself.

As I devoured the final installment of this groundbreaking series, I was struck by the sheer audacity of Bakker's vision. Like a masterfully crafted mirror, The Unholy Consult reflects our own darkest instincts, forcing us to confront the abyss that stares back at us with unflinching clarity. This is no easy read; rather, it is a harrowing, unflinching descent into the depths of human depravity, where the comforting façade of civilization is stripped away, revealing the primal, animalistic drivers that lurk within.

Bakker's prose, as always, is a tour-de-force. His writing is a mesmerizing dance of language, weaving together philosophical treatises, epic battles, and intimate character studies with effortless ease. His world-building is nothing short of breathtaking, crafting a rich tapestry of myth and magic that draws us inexorably into the heart of his fictional universe.

At the core of The Unholy Consult lies the complex, enigmatic figure of Kellhus, the Aspect-Emperor. His is a mind-bending, almost incomprehensible presence that seems to defy the very fabric of reality. Like a shapeshifter, Kellhus weaves in and out of allegiances, manipulating the strings of power with calculated precision. And yet, even as we are drawn into his orbit, Bakker skillfully subverts our expectations, revealing the dark chasm that lies beneath Kellhus's seemingly omniscient surface.

Throughout the series, Bakker has demonstrated a radical willingness to confront the darker aspects of human nature, stripping away the comforting illusions that shield us from the abyss. The Unholy Consult is no exception, plunging us into a world of violence, cruelty, and despair. Yet, paradoxically, this is also a work of profound hope, a testament to the resilience of the human spirit even in the face of unimaginable horror.

One of the most striking aspects of The Unholy Consult is its use of character relationships as a kind of psychological mirror. Sorweel, the Believer-King of Sakarpus, and Serwa, the enigmatic daughter of the Aspect-Emperor, form a bond that is at once tender and terrifying. Their interplay serves as a microcosm for the larger conflict, reflecting the fragmented, often contradictory nature of human experience.

Bakker's masterful handling of these character relationships underscores the series's central theme: that morality is a fluid, context-dependent construct, subject to the whims of power and circumstance. As the world of the Aspect-Emperor careens towards its catastrophic conclusion, the reader is forced to confront the realization that even the most well-intentioned actions can have devastating consequences.

Ultimately, The Unholy Consult is a testament to the enduring power of fantasy as a tool for exploring the human condition. Like George R. R. Martin's A Song of Ice and Fire, Bakker's series has earned its place among the modern canon of epic fantasy, thanks to its unflinching portrayal of the darker aspects of human nature. This is no easy series to read, but one that will leave you changed, forever altered by the unflinching gaze of the abyss.

In the end, Bakker's work is a stark reminder that our capacity for both good and evil is limitless, and that the choices we make in the shadows can have far-reaching consequences that will haunt us for eternity. It's a haunting, searing, and unflinching final installment to a groundbreaking series, and one that will be etched into the minds of readers for years to come.
