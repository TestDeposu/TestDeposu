# Working for the Devil (Dante Valentine, #1)

**Author:** Lilith Saintcrow  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

**A Devil's Bargain: Lilith Saintcrow's Seductive Ride into the World of Dante Valentine**

In the realm of urban fantasy, few authors have mastered the art of weaving dark, edgy tales as effortlessly as Lilith Saintcrow. Her debut novel, "Working for the Devil," is a gripping ride into the world of necromancer-for-hire Dante Valentine, a heroine as complex as she is deadly. This 2007 release, the first in the Dante Valentine series, marks the beginning of a thrilling journey that combines elements of horror, mystery, and dark humor.

From the onset, it's clear that Saintcrow has crafted a protagonist unlike any other in the genre. Dante Valentine is a force to be reckoned with, a necromancer who can raise the dead with eerie precision and dispatch her targets with ruthless efficiency. When the Devil himself calls upon her services, Dante must navigate a treacherous web of demonic politics and ancient power struggles.

With the Devil's promise of immunity as her only guarantee, Dante must face the impossible: eliminating a rogue demon. Vardimal Santino, a malevolent entity with the ability to cheat death, becomes the focal point of her quest. How do you kill something that defies mortality? Saintcrow raises the stakes in an expertly crafted narrative that hurtles the reader toward an action-packed climax.

One of Saintcrow's greatest strengths lies in her ability to infuse her characters with a depth that feels refreshingly honest. Dante, for all her bravado, struggles with self-doubt and the weight of her own morality. This authenticity makes her all the more compelling, and her relationships with other characters are equally nuanced. The supporting cast is equally well-developed, from demonic underlings to human allies, each bearing secrets of their own.

Saintcrow's writing is a sensory experience, conjuring the dark, gritty urban landscapes and the macabre world of supernatural politics. Her prose is unflinching and seductive, pulling the reader into a realm of mystery and magic. The pacing is relentless, propelling Dante through a journey that blends dark fantasy and horror, often blurring the lines between good and evil.

"Working for the Devil" marks a triumphant entry into the urban fantasy genre for Saintcrow. With its intoxicating blend of the supernatural and the mundane, this novel establishes a foundation for a captivating series that would go on to captivate fans worldwide. While the book can be somewhat rough around the edges – a result of its debut status – the raw talent on display is undeniable.

If you're a fan of dark, fast-paced fantasy with complex characters and a narrative that refuses to be contained, then "Working for the Devil" is an absolute must-read. Lilith Saintcrow's mastery of the genre is evident from the very start, and her unique voice will leave you eagerly anticipating the next installment in the Dante Valentine series.
