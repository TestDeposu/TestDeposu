# Burning Chrome (Sprawl, #0)

**Author:** William Gibson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

William Gibson needs very little introduction. As the visionary architect of cyberpunk, the man who coined the term cyberspace, and the literary force behind the seminal masterpiece *Neuromancer*, Gibson forever altered the DNA of modern science fiction. Yet, despite a decades-long career that inspired everything from cinematic milestones like *The Matrix* to countless dystopian landscapes, Gibson famously left one frontier unexplored: the medium of comic books. That is, until now. 

Released in 2020, this striking graphic novel presentation—linked to the evocative legacy of his foundational universe—brings the master’s unparalleled storytelling directly to the illustrated page. Nominated for the 2017 Eisner Award for Best Limited Series, this work proves that Gibson’s transition to the sequential art format is not just a novelty, but a vital, mandatory read for any devotee of speculative fiction. 

At the heart of the narrative lies a chilling and grandiose premise: political leaders have utterly destroyed the world, and now they seek a new reality to corrupt. To achieve this, they turn to humanity’s final, desperate gamble—Splitter, a colossal machine designed to puncture the fabric of time and allow ruthless elites to travel into the past to cement their absolute power in the future. Armed with his trademark dark sarcasm and unyielding militaristic pragmatism, Gibson guides the reader from the toxic, ruined peripheries of the present straight into the heart of a war-torn Berlin in 1945. Here, RAF officer Naomi Givens finds herself thrust into an impossible war, forced to face heavily armed soldiers hailing from a cruel and unthinkable tomorrow. The fate of multiple epochs hangs in the balance, creating a high-stakes thriller that seamlessly bridges historical fiction with high-concept sci-fi.

What makes this work truly sing is Gibson’s unmistakable style. Long celebrated for his ability to fuse gritty technological speculation with deep historical and philosophical anxieties, Gibson applies those exact sensibilities to a landscape of temporal warfare. Much like Philip K. Dick’s explorations of alternative histories in works like *The Man in the High Castle*, Gibson asks us to look at the fragile nature of time, memory, and political corruption through a cynical, razor-sharp lens. His narrative voice is dripping with pragmatic grit, treating apocalyptic stakes with the weary exhaustion of soldiers who have seen too much. 

Collaborating with veteran comic artist Butch Guice—whose impressive pedigree includes defining work on Superman and the Flash—Gibson’s bleak, atmospheric world-building takes on a visceral, breathing life. Guice matches Gibson’s narrative pessimism with stunning visual panels that contrast the claustrophobic devastation of 1945 Europe with the sterile, imposing coldness of a dystopian future. The result is a seamless synthesis of word and image, drawing praise from critics across the board, with *BoingBoing* hailing it as spectacular and *Ars Technica* recommending it as essential reading for fans of thought-provoking science fiction and espionage thrillers alike.

Whether you are a die-hard cyberpunk purist tracing the lineage of the Sprawl, a fan of mind-bending temporal mechanics, or a newcomer eager to witness a legendary author conquer a brand-new medium, this graphic novel delivers on every front. It is a triumphant, razor-sharp collision of past and future, brought to life by a master at the absolute peak of his observational powers.
