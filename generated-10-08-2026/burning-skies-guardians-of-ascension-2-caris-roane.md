# Burning Skies (Guardians of Ascension, #2)

**Author:** Caris Roane  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

In Burning Skies, the second installment of her acclaimed Guardians of Ascension series, Caris Roane plunges readers back into a high-stakes realm where celestial warfare and consuming passion collide. Published in 2011, this novel solidifies Roane’s reputation as a daring voice in paranormal romance, deftly blending the grit of apocalyptic fantasy with the intense heat of erotica. If the first book established the mythology of the winged Guardians and their eternal war against the death vamps, Burning Skies tests the resilience of that universe through the crucible of a deeply fated, slow-burn romance.

At the heart of the novel is the central theme of redemption through surrender. Roane explores this brilliantly through Marcus Amargi, a fierce vampire warrior currently living in exile. Marcus is a man at war with his own destiny, stubbornly resisting his rightful place among the Guardians. Yet, his internal fortress is severely breached not by an army of the undead, but by Havily Morgan. An immortal possessing extraordinary gifts and residing in the vibrant realm of Second Earth, Havily has long occupied Marcus’s dreams. She is the woman destined for him, the one whose very blood he craves with a desperate, primal hunger. 

Roane’s thematic exploration centers on the idea that true power is not found in isolation or stubborn independence, but in the terrifying vulnerability of the soul-deep bond. For Marcus and Havily, coming together is not merely a romantic milestone; it is a tactical necessity. As their bloodthirsty enemies unleash terrifying sky-blazing weapons that threaten to reduce their worlds to ash, the couple must unite body and soul. The external fires mirroring the internal heat of their passion is a heavy-handed metaphor, but Roane executes it with enough visceral energy to make it sing. 

Stylistically, Roane thrives on sensory overload and heightened emotional stakes. Her writing style is unapologetically lush and cinematic, leaning heavily into the tropes of romantic suspense. She paints her supernatural landscapes with broad, fiery strokes, ensuring that every shadow feels menacing and every touch feels electric. The prose moves at a relentless pace, balancing the frantic choreography of battle scenes with the equally intense, steamy dynamics of her protagonists. Roane understands the assignment of the genre: she offers escapism rooted in high drama, ensuring that the emotional stakes feel just as catastrophic as the literal fires consuming the skies.

While the plot relies on familiar paranormal romance conventions—the tortured alpha warrior, the powerful yet yielding heroine, and the looming apocalyptic threat—Roane’s world-building elevates the material. She creates a tangible hierarchy of danger and desire, making the reader feel the physical weight of Marcus’s wings and the shimmering magic of Havily’s abilities. The chemistry between the leads is palpable, driven by a yearning that feels genuinely earned amidst the chaos of war.

Burning Skies is a thrilling, scorching addition to Roane’s ongoing saga. It delivers everything fans of the genre crave: danger, high-concept fantasy lore, and a romance that literally sets the world on fire. Roane proves that even when the skies are burning, love remains the most volatile, and vital, force of all.
