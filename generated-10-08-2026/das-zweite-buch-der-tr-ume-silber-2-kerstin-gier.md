# Das zweite Buch der Träume (Silber, #2)

**Author:** Kerstin Gier  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

Unlocking the Subconscious: Kerstin Gier’s Triumph in Das zweite Buch der Träume

When Kerstin Gier burst onto the international young adult literary scene with her wildly imaginative Ruby Red trilogy, she proved that she possessed a rare literary alchemy: the ability to blend high-stakes supernatural mythology with the acutely relatable, laugh-out-loud awkwardness of teenage life. With Das zweite Buch der Träume (The Second Book of Dreams), the middle installment of her acclaimed Silber trilogy published in 2014, Gier returns to the surreal corridors of the human subconscious, delivering a breathless, witty, and masterfully paced continuation that surpasses the foundations laid in the first volume. 

At its core, the central theme of Gier’s narrative is the thin, permeable membrane separating our waking anxieties from our nocturnal fears, and how the secrets we keep in the dark inevitably bleed into the light. Liv Silber, our sharp-witted and resilient protagonist, thought she had come to terms with the labyrinthine rules of dream-travel, where corridors stretch endlessly and doorways lead into the deeply guarded psyche of others. But Gier escalates the psychological stakes immediately. When the mysterious gossip blogger "Secrecy" begins publishing Liv’s most deeply buried, intimate secrets, the sanctity of the dream world is violently compromised. 

Gier uses the metaphysical mechanics of lucid dreaming as a brilliant allegorical canvas for the chaos of adolescence. Just as Liv must navigate the shifting loyalties of her newly minted, chaotic patchwork family—complete with an intriguingly sinister grandmother—and the enigmatic behavior of the brooding Henry, her dream life mirrors this emotional turbulence. The appearance of a dark, menacing figure stalking the endless dream corridors, coupled with her sister Mia’s sudden, alarming descent into sleepwalking, transforms the narrative from a clever romantic fantasy into a gripping psychological thriller. 

What elevates Gier’s work above standard young adult fare is her signature stylistic brilliance. Her prose is effortlessly conversational, crackling with sharp, self-deprecating irony and sparkling dialogue that keeps the pages turning at a breakneck speed. Gier has an uncanny ear for how teenagers actually speak—or at least how they wish they could speak, armed with the kind of quick-witted comebacks we all wish we had conjured in the moment. Yet, amidst the humor and the swoon-worthy romantic tension, Gier expertly weaves a pervasive sense of dread. The transition from the sunlit, albeit stressful, reality of London high school life to the eerie, unpredictable hallways of the dream corridors is handled with cinematic fluidity. 

Furthermore, Gier’s exploration of trust and paranoia forms the beating heart of the novel. By trapping her characters in a realm where their subconscious desires and darkest shames are accessible to outside forces, she forces them—and the reader—to confront uncomfortable truths. Who can truly be trusted when even your own sleeping mind can betray you? 

Das zweite Buch der Träume is a triumph of pacing, world-building, and character chemistry. It takes the whimsical premise of dream-jumping and grounds it in genuine emotional stakes, wrapped in the cozy, addictive storytelling blanket that only Kerstin Gier can provide. It is a dazzling, suspenseful, and thoroughly entertaining read that leaves the reader eagerly pressing forward into the final frontier of the trilogy.
