# The Forgetting Moon (The Five Warrior Angels, #1)

**Author:** Brian Lee Durfee  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

In "The Forgetting Moon" by Brian Lee Durfee, we embark on an epic fantasy journey where the very fabric of truth is turned upside down. Set in a world where prophecies are nothing but lies and magic exists beneath the surface, Durfee crafts a narrative that is both captivating and thought-provoking. The book introduces us to a vast array of complex characters, each with their own motivations and backstories, and masterfully weaves their tales together to create an unforgettable story.

At the heart of the narrative is Gault, an elite Knight Archaic of the invading army of Sør Sevier, who is struggling to reconcile his duty with his growing disillusionment with the crusade. The Angel Prince, Aeros, drives the Sør Sevier army forward with his prophetic fervor, but Gault's doubts create a sense of tension that sets the stage for a much larger conflict. Meanwhile, within the kingdom of Gul Kana, the eldest son of the fallen king rules with increasing paranoia, while his sisters, Jondralyn and Tala, forge their own paths amidst the treacherous politics of the court.

One of the standout features of "The Forgetting Moon" is its complex cast of characters. Each character has a unique voice, and Durfee delves deep into their motivations and backstories, creating a rich tapestry of people that is both believable and relatable. Hawkwood, the assassin, is a particular standout, as his tumultuous relationship with Jondralyn adds a layer of depth to the story that is both romantic and deadly.

The world-building in "The Forgetting Moon" is also noteworthy. Durfee creates a vivid and immersive world, full of magical creatures and mysterious locations, that is both breathtaking and eerie. The Five Isles are a place of wonder, where magic is believed in but never seen, and hope is a fragile thing that is easily extinguished. The author's writing is evocative and atmospheric, conjuring up images of mist-shrouded mountains and dark, foreboding forests.

However, it's Durfee's masterful handling of the theme of deception that really sets "The Forgetting Moon" apart. In a world where prophecies are lies and magic is a mere myth, the truth is a fleeting concept that can be manipulated and distorted to suit one's purposes. This theme is woven throughout the narrative, as characters struggle to discern reality from fiction, and the consequences of deception are catastrophic.

Throughout the book, Durfee explores the consequences of living in a world that is built on lies. Characters who cling to their delusions, believing in false prophecies or distorted truths, find themselves entangled in complex webs of deceit that threaten to destroy everything they hold dear. Meanwhile, those who seek the truth must navigate a treacherous landscape of conspiracy and betrayal, where even the most seemingly trustworthy allies may prove to be false.

In "The Forgetting Moon", Durfee has created a true epic fantasy, full of complex characters, atmospheric world-building, and thought-provoking themes. His writing is masterful, weaving multiple storylines together to create a narrative that is both captivating and unpredictable. Fans of epic fantasy will devour this book, but be warned: nothing is as it seems in the Five Isles, and the truth is a luxury that few can afford.
