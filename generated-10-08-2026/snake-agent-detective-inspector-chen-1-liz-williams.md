# Snake Agent (Detective Inspector Chen, #1)

**Author:** Liz Williams  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

Step into Singapore Three, a neon-drenched metropolis where the bureaucratic machinery of modern policing grinds against the timeless bureaucracy of the afterlife, and you will find Liz Williams’s brilliantly inventive 2013 novel, Snake Agent. As the inaugural entry in the Detective Inspector Chen series, this genre-bending tour de force effortlessly fuses the gritty realism of a police procedural with the flamboyant world-building of comic fantasy, science fiction, and dark horror. Williams has not merely constructed a setting; she has conjured a sprawling, multicultural crossroads where ancient Chinese mythology and high-tech futurism coexist in a state of chaotic, mesmerizing harmony.

At the heart of the narrative is Detective Inspector Wei Chen, a refreshingly weary, round-faced cop whose beat just happens to straddle the mortal plane and the terrifying expanse of the underworld. Chen is an exceptional protagonist—grounded, world-weary, and possessing an unflappable pragmatism that is sorely needed when your caseload involves missing souls rather than missing persons. Adding a delightful layer of domestic and supernatural friction to Chen’s life is his wife, a demon who complicates his daily routine just enough to remind readers that his job follows him home. When Pearl Tang, the teenage daughter of a prominent industrialist, dies of anorexia only for her soul to be mysteriously waylaid at the very gates of Hell, Chen is thrust into a case that defies standard jurisdiction.

The central theme of Snake Agent explores the commodification of the soul and the inescapable weight of human suffering within rigid systems—both earthly and infernal. Williams uses the investigation as a satirical yet poignant lens to examine how corporate greed, familial expectation, and personal demons can transcend death. Pearl’s tragic fate is not just a supernatural kidnapping; it is a manifestation of the modern world's relentless pressures, filtered through a mythological prism. As Chen delves deeper into the abyss to rescue the girl, the novel poses profound questions about redemption, agency, and the blurry boundaries between the damned and the saved.

Williams’s authorial style is a masterclass in atmospheric world-building and tonal balance. She writes with a lush, sensory prose that captures the humid, sensory-overload of Singapore Three while seamlessly integrating complex mythological lore without overwhelming the reader. What could easily become a discordant mess in lesser hands is instead harmonized by Williams’s sharp wit, brisk pacing, and noir sensibilities. The dialogue is snappy, the bureaucratic absurdities of hell provide biting dark comedy, and the stakes consistently feel visceral and high. 

The crowning achievement of the novel, however, is the dynamic between Chen and his reluctant partner: a literal demonic detective. Their reluctant partnership forms the emotional and narrative backbone of the book, offering a brilliant subversion of the classic buddy-cop formula. As they navigate the treacherous, shifting landscapes of the underworld, the novel accelerates into a thrilling test of survival where getting into Hell is merely an inconvenience, but finding a way back out proves to be the ultimate test of character.

Snake Agent is a dazzling, darkly funny, and utterly unique triumph of speculative fiction. Liz Williams has crafted a world so vibrant and characters so compelling that readers will find themselves eagerly turning pages, terrified of the dark yet completely unwilling to leave. For fans of boundary-pushing urban fantasy and supernatural noir, this novel is an absolute must-read.
