# Fists of Justice (Schooled in Magic, #12)

**Author:** Christopher G. Nuttall  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

Stepping back into the sprawling, ever-evolving universe of Christopher G. Nuttall’s long-running *Schooled in Magic* series is always a bittersweet exercise in comfort and escalation, but with the twelfth installment, *Fists of Justice*, Nuttall boldly pivots away from the familiar halls of Whitehall. Instead, he trains his narrative lens on a daring social experiment that has quietly brewed in the background of previous conflicts: Heart’s Eye University. This time, the beloved overarching legacy of Lady Emily is thrust into the crucible of institutional reform, ideological friction, and a chilling domestic conspiracy.

At the heart of this latest installment is Adam of Beneficence, a protagonist who instantly grounds the narrative with a deeply relatable sense of inadequacy and ambition. Adam wanted nothing more than to wield the arcane, to join the ranks of the respected and the powerful. Yet, cursed with a complete lack of magical aptitude, his apprenticeship hit a brick wall. Desperate to find his place in a world dominated by spellcasters, he finds himself enrolled at Heart’s Eye University. Once a bastion of pure magic, the facility has been dramatically reinvented as a collaborative melting pot where magical practitioners and mundane individuals are expected to study side-by-side, fusing science, industry, and the arcane to forge a unified future. 

It is a utopian vision, but Nuttall is far too seasoned a speculative fiction author to let idealism survive contact with human nature. The central theme of *Fists of Justice* is the agonizing, often volatile friction between tradition and progress. Heart’s Eye is a powder keg. The magical apprentices look down upon their mundane peers with deeply ingrained arrogance and fear, while the mundane students harbor bitter resentments toward a privileged caste that has ruled society from the shadows for centuries. Compounding this volatile student body is a bewildered and deeply divided faculty, hopelessly unequipped to bridge the cultural chasm. 

Nuttall’s stylistic fingerprints are all over this dynamic. Known for his brisk pacing, straightforward prose, and a distinct penchant for socio-political world-building, the author uses this academic micro-society to mirror real-world anxieties regarding integration, technological advancement, and the radicalization of the disenfranchised. He does not rely on flowery prose or overly dense literary devices; rather, Nuttall favors pragmatic storytelling that prioritizes pacing and ideological debate. When Adam stumbles upon a nefarious, well-organized plot operating within the university’s bowels—one that threatens to reduce Lady Emily’s grand vision to literal cinders—the narrative accelerates from a campus drama into a breathless, high-stakes thriller.

What makes *Fists of Justice* particularly striking for long-time fans of the series is its thematic maturity. While earlier books often relied on external demonic threats or sweeping imperial wars to drive the plot, this novel turns inward. The enemy here is not just an assassin or a rogue dark wizard, but prejudice, institutional incompetence, and the sheer terror of change. Adam’s journey is compelling precisely because he lacks superpowers. He cannot blast his way out of trouble with a fireball or deflect a curse with a flick of a wrist; he must rely on his wits, his observational skills, and his unique perspective as someone caught between two worlds.

Ultimately, Christopher G. Nuttall delivers a gripping, thought-provoking chapter that revitalizes the *Schooled in Magic* mythos. *Fists of Justice* proves that the most dangerous battlegrounds are not always found on distant frontiers, but in the classrooms and corridors where the future is being painfully negotiated. For readers who love institutional fantasy, underdog triumphs, and intricate plots that challenge the very fabric of society, this twelfth installment is a thoroughly engaging and satisfying read.
