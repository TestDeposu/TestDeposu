# The Rebels of Gold (Loom Saga, #3)

**Author:** Elise Kova  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

**The Rebels of Gold: A Spark of Fire in the Kingdom of Thorns**

Elise Kova’s *The Rebels of Gold*—the third installment in *The Loom Saga*—is a masterclass in weaving political intrigue, slow-burn tension, and morally complex romance into a dark fantasy tapestry that feels both fresh and deeply familiar. If you’ve ever craved the razor-sharp wit of Leigh Bardugo’s *Six of Crows*, the fiery rebellion of Jennifer L. Armentrout’s *Throne of Glass*, or the morally gray allure of Sarah J. Maas’s *Crescent City*, this book will leave you breathless. Kova doesn’t just deliver a story; she crafts a world where every word carries weight, where loyalty is a currency, and where love—if it ever arrives—is as dangerous as it is intoxicating.

At its core, *The Rebels of Gold* is a tale of betrayal, survival, and the seductive power of deception. Sage, our protagonist, is a rebel with a knife for a conscience and a reputation for being ruthless. Her mission to infiltrate the palace of Aermia should have been straightforward: infiltrate, sabotage, escape. But when her own team turns on her, she’s left with nothing but her wits—and the cruel, golden-eyed prince Tehl Ramses, who believes he’s captured a spy. What follows is a cat-and-mouse game of wits, where Sage’s cunning clashes with Tehl’s icy arrogance, and where the line between prisoner and prisoner-taker blurs into something far more volatile.

Kova’s strength lies in her ability to make her characters feel *alive*—flawed, contradictory, and utterly compelling. Sage is no saint; she’s a survivor who’ll burn the world down if it means staying alive. Tehl, on the other hand, is a prince who’s seen too much to trust easily, but whose icy exterior cracks just enough to reveal a man haunted by duty and a war he can’t win. Their dynamic is electric, a slow burn that simmers with tension, where every glance, every sharp remark, feels like a spark waiting to ignite. It’s the kind of enemies-to-lovers trope that doesn’t rely on clichés but instead thrives on the friction between two people who *should* hate each other but can’t look away.

The world-building is equally immersive, dripping with the kind of lush, dangerous detail that makes you want to lose yourself in it. Dragons soar over battlefields, royal intrigue twists like a serpent, and the looming threat of war hangs over everything like a storm cloud. Kova’s prose is sharp and evocative, with a rhythm that pulls you forward—whether it’s through Sage’s razor-witted banter or Tehl’s brooding introspection. The romance isn’t just a subplot; it’s the heart of the story, a battle of wills that feels as real as it does romantic.

What makes *The Rebels of Gold* truly stand out, though, is its refusal to sugarcoat. This isn’t a fairy-tale romance; it’s a story where trust is a luxury, where every alliance could be a blade in the back, and where love, if it’s earned, is hard-won. The morally gray characters—both protagonists and antagonists—make the world feel gritty and real, where no one is purely good or evil, just people fighting for what they believe in, even if it destroys them.

For fans of dark fantasy with a dash of rebellion, a sprinkle of romance, and a whole lot of danger, this book is a must-read. Elise Kova doesn’t just tell a story; she crafts an experience—one where every page feels like a step closer to the fire, and where the only way out is through.
