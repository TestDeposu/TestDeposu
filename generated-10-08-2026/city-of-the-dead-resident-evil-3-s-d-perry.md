# City of the Dead (Resident Evil, #3)

**Author:** S.D. Perry  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

Shadow and Bone: S.D. Perry’s Descent into the City of the Dead

When adapting interactive media into prose, authors often stumble, falling into the trap of merely transcribing gameplay mechanics onto the page. However, with City of the Dead, S.D. Perry achieves something remarkably cinematic, proving once again why she is the undisputed queen of video game novelizations. While the provided synopsis amusingly mistakes Raccoon City for a Colombian cave system and Lara Croft for Leon Kennedy and Claire Redfield, true fans of the survival horror genre know the harrowing reality: this is the definitive, blood-soaked retelling of Resident Evil 2. Perry takes the chaotic, button-mashing panic of the PlayStation classic and distills it into a suffocating, atmospheric nightmare that stands tall as a thrilling pulp horror novel in its own right.

At the heart of City of the Dead lies the central theme of systemic corruption and the chilling hubris of unchecked corporate science. Raccoon City is no longer just a backdrop; it is a rotting organism, betrayed from within by the Umbrella Corporation. Perry masterfully explores the psychological toll of this apocalypse on her protagonists. Leon S. Kennedy, a rookie cop facing his very first day on the job only to find the world has ended, embodies the loss of innocence. Alongside him, Claire Redfield represents desperate familial devotion, searching relentlessly for her missing brother amidst a sea of the damned. Through their alternating perspectives, Perry examines how ordinary people adapt when the rule of law is replaced by the primal law of survival. The city becomes a purgatory where morality is tested, and every shadowed corridor threatens to swallow hope whole.

Perry’s writing style is brisk, visceral, and unyielding. She understands that horror thrives in the details—the metallic tang of copper in the air, the guttural, inhuman groans echoing through rain-slicked asphalt, and the horrific squelch of biological mutations tearing through flesh. Unlike the sparse text boxes of the 1998 video game, Perry’s prose breathes life into the claustrophobia of the Raccoon City Police Department. The pacing is relentless, mimicking the breathless terror of running low on ammunition while something heavy drags its claws against the floorboards just around the corner. She handles the action sequences with surgical precision, ensuring that the kinetic thrills of dodging Lickers and Tyrants translate powerfully to the printed page without ever losing the grounded dread of the genre.

Furthermore, Perry manages to flesh out the lore, giving characters like Leon, Claire, and even the doomed Ada Wong a psychological depth that elevates the source material. We feel Leon’s growing disillusionment with the authority he swore to uphold, and Claire’s fierce, unyielding compassion in a world that has discarded empathy entirely. The monsters they face are not just mindless obstacles, but grotesque monuments to human arrogance, making every victory feel hard-won and fragile.

City of the Dead is more than a mere tie-in; it is a masterclass in how to translate interactive terror into a page-turning literary experience. S.D. Perry honors the legacy of Resident Evil while crafting a dark, suspenseful thriller that will keep both die-hard fans and newcomers turning pages deep into the night. If you dare to step into the fog-shrouded streets of Raccoon City, prepare yourself—because once Perry locks you inside, there is no easy way out.
