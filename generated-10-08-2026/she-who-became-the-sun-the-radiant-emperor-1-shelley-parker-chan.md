# She Who Became the Sun (The Radiant Emperor, #1)

**Author:** Shelley Parker-Chan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

She Who Became the Sun, the debut novel by Shelley Parker-Chan, is a mesmerizing and epic tale of fate, power, and identity set against the backdrop of 14th-century China. This stunning work of fantasy reimagines the rise of the Ming Dynasty's founding emperor, weaving a complex and captivating narrative that explores the intricacies of war, love, and betrayal. With its richly detailed world-building and compelling characters, this book is a must-read for fans of historical fantasy and adventure.

At the heart of the story is Zhu, a young girl born into a famine-stricken village, who is given a fate of nothingness by the gods. However, when her brother, Zhu Chongba, who is destined for greatness, dies, she seizes the opportunity to claim his identity and forge a new path for herself. As she navigates the treacherous landscape of war-torn China, Zhu must confront her own morality and the true cost of her ambition. Through Zhu's journey, Parker-Chan masterfully explores the themes of fate, identity, and the social constructs that govern our lives.

One of the most striking aspects of Parker-Chan's writing style is her ability to craft a narrative that is both deeply personal and epic in scope. The world she creates is meticulously researched and vividly realized, with a keen attention to historical detail that immerses the reader in the time period. At the same time, the characters that populate this world are multidimensional and nuanced, with rich inner lives that drive the plot forward. Zhu, in particular, is a fascinating and complex protagonist, whose struggles to reconcile her desire for greatness with the harsh realities of her world are both deeply relatable and utterly compelling.

Parker-Chan's prose is also noteworthy for its lyricism and precision. Her writing is infused with a poetic sensibility that brings the world of 14th-century China to life in vivid and evocative language. From the dusty yellow plains to the majestic grandeur of the imperial court, the settings in this novel are meticulously crafted to transport the reader to another time and place. At the same time, the action scenes are fast-paced and suspenseful, with a keen sense of tension and drama that propels the plot forward.

Throughout the novel, Parker-Chan also explores the social and cultural norms of the time period, shedding light on the limited options available to women in a patriarchal society. Zhu's decision to disguise herself as a man in order to claim her brother's fate is a powerful commentary on the restrictive nature of gender roles and the ways in which women have been marginalized throughout history. At the same time, the novel celebrates the resilience and determination of women in the face of adversity, offering a powerful and inspiring vision of female empowerment.

Overall, She Who Became the Sun is a stunning achievement, a novel that combines stunning world-building, compelling characters, and a gripping narrative to create a truly unforgettable reading experience. With its richly detailed settings, complex characters, and thought-provoking themes, this book is sure to appeal to fans of fantasy, adventure, and historical fiction. As a debut novel, it is a remarkable achievement, and Parker-Chan is undoubtedly a talent to watch in the years to come.
