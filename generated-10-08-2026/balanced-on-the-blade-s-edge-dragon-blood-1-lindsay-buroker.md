# Balanced on the Blade's Edge (Dragon Blood, #1)

**Author:** Lindsay Buroker  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

Steampunk meets epic fantasy in Lindsay Buroker’s 2014 novel, Balanced on the Blade's Edge, a briskly paced and entertaining opener to the Dragon Blood series. Buroker crafts a distinct world where traditional sword-and-sorcery elements collide with high-altitude military aviation, frost-bitten penal colonies, and ancient magic. The result is a lighthearted yet adventurous narrative that prioritizes snappy dialogue, high-stakes survival, and slow-burn chemistry over dense, brooding world-building.

At the heart of the novel is a classic, highly effective character dynamic: the collision of two opposing forces. On one side stands Colonel Ridge Zirkander, an irreverent, hot-headed fighter pilot whose martial prowess is matched only by his utter disdain for diplomatic protocol. Buroker uses Ridge to inject humor and grounded realism into the military aspects of the plot. He is far from the stoic, brooding fantasy hero; instead, he is a man of action who suffers the consequences of his own loose tongue. 

On the other side is Sardelle Terushan, a displaced sorcerer waking from a three-hundred-year stasis only to find her people exterminated and her homeland overrun by the descendants of her enemies. Sardelle embodies the novel's central theme: the heavy burden of history and the challenge of starting over in a world that has learned to fear your very existence. Forced to masquerade as a common prisoner in the freezing fortress Ridge now commands, Sardelle must navigate a delicate dance of deception. 

Buroker’s writing style is accessible, witty, and fast-paced. She avoids the trap of overly florid prose, favoring instead a cinematic approach that keeps the pages turning. The juxtaposition between Ridge’s modern military pragmatism and Sardelle’s ancient, magical heritage fuels much of the narrative tension and comedic relief. The inclusion of a sentient soulblade adds a layer of classic fantasy charm, providing banter and historical context that enriches the lore.

While the novel operates within familiar tropes—the reluctant military leader, the secret sorcerer hiding in plain sight, and the inevitable romantic tension—Buroker executes them with such charm and enthusiasm that it is difficult not to be swept along. Balanced on the Blade's Edge is a thoroughly enjoyable read for fans of romantic fantasy, steampunk adventures, and character-driven plots. It successfully sets the stage for a sprawling series, leaving readers eager to see how Ridge and Sardelle will navigate the dangers of a changing world.
