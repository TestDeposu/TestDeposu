# Midnight Falcon (The Rigante, #2)

**Author:** David Gemmell  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2001

Midnight Falcon, the second installment in David Gemmell's The Rigante series, is a gripping tale of revenge, loyalty, and the unyielding bonds between a father and son. Set in a world of ancient magic and brutal warfare, this novel is a masterclass in epic storytelling, with Gemmell's signature blend of action, drama, and fantasy.

At the heart of the story is Bane, the illegitimate son of the Rigante king, Connavar, also known as Demonblade. Born out of treachery and rejected by his father, Bane is forced to navigate a treacherous world where his unique heritage makes him an outcast among his own people. With his striking eyes, one tawny brown and the other emerald green, Bane is a constant reminder of his father's infidelity, and his presence is a threat to the very fabric of the Rigante society.

Gemmell's portrayal of Bane is nothing short of brilliant. The young warrior's journey from an outcast to a gladiator is both heartbreaking and inspiring, as he faces unimaginable challenges and emerges stronger with each triumph. Bane's quest for revenge against the swordsman who destroyed his life is a driving force behind the plot, and Gemmell expertly weaves together the threads of his story, creating a narrative that is both intense and emotionally resonant.

One of the most striking aspects of Midnight Falcon is Gemmell's exploration of the complex relationship between Bane and his father, Connavar. The dynamics between the two are fraught with tension, guilt, and a deep-seated sense of betrayal. Connavar, the mighty Demonblade, is a figure of awe and reverence, but his treatment of Bane is a stark reminder of his own flaws and weaknesses. Through their interactions, Gemmell raises important questions about the nature of fatherhood, loyalty, and the consequences of one's actions.

Gemmell's writing style is, as always, a joy to behold. His prose is lean and muscular, with a keen sense of pacing that propels the reader through the story. The world-building is meticulously detailed, with a richly imagined landscape of ancient forests, snow-capped mountains, and bustling cities. The characters, too, are multidimensional and complex, with even the most minor players possessing a depth and nuance that adds to the overall richness of the narrative.

The themes of Midnight Falcon are timeless and universal, speaking to fundamental human emotions that transcend genre and setting. Gemmell's exploration of the human condition, with all its attendant complexities and contradictions, is a hallmark of his writing, and this novel is no exception. As the armies of the Stone prepare to march on the lands of the Rigante, the fate of human and Seidh alike hangs in the balance, and the reader is left wondering what the future holds for Bane, Connavar, and the people they love.

In conclusion, Midnight Falcon is a triumph of epic storytelling, with a narrative that is both gripping and emotionally resonant. Gemmell's masterful handling of the plot, combined with his signature style and deep understanding of the human condition, makes this novel a must-read for fans of fantasy and adventure. With its richly imagined world, complex characters, and timeless themes, Midnight Falcon is a true classic of the genre, and a testament to Gemmell's enduring legacy as one of the greatest fantasy writers of all time.
