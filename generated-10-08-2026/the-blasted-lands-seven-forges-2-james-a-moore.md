# The Blasted Lands (Seven Forges, #2)

**Author:** James A. Moore  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

**The Blasted Lands: A Grimdark Masterpiece Where War Forges the Future**

James A. Moore’s *The Blasted Lands*—the second installment in *Seven Forges*—is a sprawling, brutal epic that cements itself as a standout in the grimdark fantasy genre. Where many tales of war and prophecy feel like grand but hollow spectacles, Moore’s work is visceral, morally complex, and relentlessly immersive. This is a world where gods are not distant deities but forces of destruction, where empires crumble under the weight of their own ambition, and where survival is less about heroism and more about sheer, unyielding will.

At its core, *The Blasted Lands* is a story of collapse and rebirth. The once-great empire of Fellein stands on the brink of annihilation, its people divided between those who cling to tradition and those who embrace radical change. The death of the emperor has left a power vacuum, and the new commander, a hardened warrior with a reputation for ruthlessness, becomes the linchpin of Fellein’s survival. But his victory is not just against external enemies—the Sa’ba Taalor, a relentless and fanatical foe—but against the creeping corruption of the past. The Blasted Lands themselves, a cursed wasteland once home to an ancient civilization, are not merely a setting but an active participant in the conflict. Their ruins whisper of forgotten horrors, and the remnants of their people—now awakened—hold secrets that could either save Fellein or doom it forever.

Moore’s prose is sharp, unflinching, and rich with atmosphere. He avoids the pitfalls of overly descriptive fantasy, instead favoring a lean, efficient style that keeps the momentum high. His worldbuilding is meticulous, with a deep sense of history and lore that makes the Blasted Lands feel like a living entity. The gods here are not benevolent or abstract—they are seven brutal forces, each embodying different aspects of war, decay, and rebirth. Their influence is palpable, shaping the fate of nations and individuals alike. When the Silent Army rises, not as a noble force but as a disciplined, almost mechanical response to the coming storm, it’s clear that Moore is less interested in traditional heroism than in the cold mechanics of power.

The political intrigue is as gripping as the battlefield action. The Sa’ba Taalor are not mindless invaders but a people driven by a fanatical belief in their own destiny, making their conflict with Fellein a clash of ideologies as much as armies. Meanwhile, the refugees fleeing the war become pawns in a larger game, their suffering a constant reminder of the human cost of greatness. Moore doesn’t shy away from the brutality of war—there are no easy victories, no clear heroes, and no neat resolutions. The Blasted Lands is a story where survival is earned through blood and cunning, where loyalty is a liability, and where the only certainty is that the world will keep breaking—and someone will have to pick up the pieces.

What makes *The Blasted Lands* truly compelling is its refusal to offer easy answers. The empire’s new commander is not a traditional hero; he is a pragmatist, willing to make choices that will haunt him. The ancient forces stirring in the Blasted Lands are not just threats—they are inevitabilities. Moore’s world feels like a living organism, one that breathes, decays, and regenerates. The tension between tradition and change, between the old gods and the new, is the heart of the story, and Moore handles it with a master’s touch.

For fans of grimdark fantasy, *The Blasted Lands* is a must-read. It’s the kind of epic that doesn’t just tell a story but immerses you in it, making you feel the weight of every battle, the desperation of every choice, and the inevitability of every consequence. It’s a world where war is not just a conflict but a force of nature, and where the only way forward is through the ashes of the past.
