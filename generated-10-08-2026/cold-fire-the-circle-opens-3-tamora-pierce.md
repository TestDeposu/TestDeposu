# Cold Fire (The Circle Opens, #3)

**Author:** Tamora Pierce  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

In "Cold Fire," the third installment of "The Circle Opens" series, Tamora Pierce masterfully weaves a tale of mystery, magic, and self-discovery set against the backdrop of a city plagued by arson. The story follows Daja, a young and powerful mage, as she visits the port city of Kugisko with her mentor, Frostpine. What promises to be a peaceful winter getaway quickly turns into a desperate fight against mysterious fires that threaten to engulf the city.

Pierce's world-building is as vivid as ever, with the city of Kugisko coming alive through her descriptive prose. The author's use of sensory details effectively transports readers to the bustling port, where the smell of smoke and saltwater fills the air. The magical system, too, is expertly woven into the narrative, with Daja's unique abilities as a smith-mage playing a crucial role in the story.

At its core, "Cold Fire" is a tale of friendship and trust. Daja's relationship with Bennat Ladradun, a local firefighter, is particularly compelling. The two form a strong bond as they work together to combat the fires, but their friendship is soon tested by the revelation that the blazes are not accidental. Pierce handles their complex emotions with sensitivity, exploring the themes of loyalty, betrayal, and the blurred lines between truth and deception.

One of the strengths of Pierce's writing is her ability to craft relatable, well-rounded characters. Daja, in particular, is a wonderful protagonist – smart, resourceful, and determined. Her struggles to navigate the complexities of adult relationships and her own sense of identity are deeply human, making her a joy to read about. The supporting cast is equally well-developed, with Bennat's tragic past and inner turmoil adding depth to the narrative.

The pacing of the story is well-balanced, with Pierce expertly ratcheting up the tension as the mystery unfolds. The plot twists are clever and unexpected, keeping readers on the edge of their seats as they try to piece together the clues alongside Daja. The author's use of magic as a tool for both creation and destruction adds an extra layer of complexity to the story, highlighting the responsibility that comes with power.

If there's one criticism to be made, it's that the story feels slightly more predictable than some of Pierce's other works. The identity of the firestarter, while not immediately obvious, is not entirely surprising either. However, this is a minor quibble in an otherwise engaging and immersive narrative.

In conclusion, "Cold Fire" is a gripping and emotionally resonant read that will appeal to fans of fantasy, mystery, and adventure. Pierce's masterful storytelling and well-developed characters make this a compelling addition to "The Circle Opens" series. With its themes of friendship, loyalty, and self-discovery, this book is sure to leave readers eagerly anticipating the next installment.
