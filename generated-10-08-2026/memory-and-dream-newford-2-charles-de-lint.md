# Memory and Dream (Newford, #2)

**Author:** Charles de Lint  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

Canvas and Consequence: The Enchanting Haunts of Charles de Lint’s Memory and Dream

Urban fantasy often trades in neon and grit, treating the fantastical as an overlay upon the modern world. But in the hands of master storyteller Charles de Lint, the genre becomes something deeply intimate, tactile, and profoundly human. Memory and Dream, the second installment in his celebrated Newford series, stands as a shimmering testament to this craft. It is a novel that does not merely visit the borders of the Otherworld; it invites that wild, untamed magic to bleed directly onto the canvas, exploring the perilous intersection where art and reality collide.

At the heart of the narrative is Isabelle Copley, a young woman in the bohemian city of Newford who catches the eye of the brilliant, reclusive, and ultimately tyrannical master painter, Vincent Rushkin. Under his rigorous tutelage, Isabelle discovers a dangerous, breathtaking talent: her paintings do not merely depict subjects; they grant them life. But creation comes with a devastating price. Rushkin’s cruelty births a tragedy so profound that it forces Isabelle to sever ties with her gift, burying her memories, emotions, and dreams beneath two decades of suffocating denial. 

When the past finally claws its way back into the present—sparked by a haunting message from beyond the grave—Isabelle is forced into a dark reckoning. She must shatter her self-imposed delusions, pick up her brushes once more, and confront the monstrous legacy of her old mentor. 

De Lint’s thematic focus in Memory and Dream is as ambitious as it is delicate. The novel serves as a profound meditation on the sheer power of art. It examines how creation requires vulnerability, how love and loyalty can warp into cruelty and greed, and how the human spirit fractures under the weight of unheeded trauma. The author treats the artistic process not as a polite hobby, but as an act of conjuring—a summoning of spirits that demands moral responsibility from the creator. 

Stylistically, de Lint is operating at the absolute peak of his powers. His prose effortlessly weaves a multi-voiced, time-shifting narrative across a twenty-year span, grounding the fantastical in the richly textured, bohemian atmosphere of Newford. What sets de Lint apart from his contemporaries is his effortless folkloric scholarship. He roots his magical realism in deep, mythic traditions without ever sliding into academic pretension or stiffness. Instead, he evokes the creative community with such vibrant, sensory detail that readers will find themselves half-believing that fantastic creatures truly lurk just out of sight, waiting for a chink in the facade of everyday life. 

This particular edition is further elevated by a 2014 afterword penned by the author, offering invaluable insight into how the act of making art has personally shaped his writing and defined this specific story. 

Booklist rightly called Memory and Dream a mandatory acquisition for any self-respecting fantasy collection, and Publishers Weekly praised its evocative, atmospheric world-building. It is a riveting, emotionally resonant triumph of urban fantasy. For anyone who has ever wondered about the cost of imagination, or the courage it takes to face the ghosts of our own making, de Lint’s masterpiece remains an essential journey.
