# The Last Coin (Christian Trilogy, #1)

**Author:** James P. Blaylock  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

The Price of Eternity: A Review of James P. Blaylock’s The Last Coin

Two thousand years ago, a bargain was struck in the shadows of Jerusalem, paid in thirty pieces of silver. While the traitor who pocketed them is long gone, the coins themselves endured, scattered across the corridors of time like ticking metaphysical time bombs. In The Last Coin, the opening volume of his acclaimed Christian Trilogy, World Fantasy Award-winning author James P. Blaylock takes this chilling premise and anchors it not in the grand, sweeping arenas of high fantasy, but in the peculiar, sun-bleached, and slightly decaying topography of the Southern California coast. The result is a brilliant masterclass in modern fabulism—a novel where cosmic conspiracy collides with mundane domesticity in a wonderfully idiosyncratic dance.

Blaylock, often celebrated alongside figures like Tim Powers as a pioneer of secret history and steampunk sensibilities, treats the supernatural not with grim, humorless solemnity, but with a uniquely American brand of oddball folk magic. The narrative unfurls through a mosaic of seemingly unconnected lives: Andrew Vanbergen, whose struggling attempts at operating an ordinary inn attract an aggressively strange clientele; the indomitable Aunt Naomi, whose prized family heirloom is an antique silver spoon bearing an uncannily ancient engraving; and the deeply unsettling Mr. Pennyman, an old man driven by a singular, obsessive compulsion, currently sitting just five silver coins shy of actual immortality. 

At its core, The Last Coin explores the corrosive nature of greed, guilt, and the ultimate human folly: the desperate desire to cheat death. The thirty coins of Judas are not merely historical relics; they are conduits of supernatural weight, warping the reality and morality of anyone unfortunate enough to possess them. Blaylock uses this theological anchor to examine how grand, epoch-spanning conspiracies manifest in the rust, dust, and coastal fog of ordinary neighborhoods. He extracts a profound, unsettling magic from familiar, everyday things—old silverware, mundane storefronts, local eccentricities—proving that the miraculous and the monstrous are often hiding just beneath the surface of the mundane world.

Stylistically, Blaylock’s prose is lyrical yet grounded, weaving a tapestry of weird fiction, farce, and philosophical inquiry. Long before modern bookshelves were flooded with fast-paced, high-concept thrillers involving revisionist scriptures and biblical cover-ups, Blaylock was already subverting the genre with wit, literary depth, and an appreciation for the peculiar. His Southern California setting feels palpably real, bathed in a melancholic yet vibrant coastal light that contrasts sharply with the dark, ancient malice of the silver coins. 

The Last Coin is a triumph of imagination that confirms Blaylock as a singular American fabulist. It is a book that rewards the curious reader with unforgettable imagery, sharply drawn characters, and a narrative voice as rare as the cursed currency at its heart. Whether you are drawn to intricate metaphysical mysteries or simply appreciate literary fantasy written with immense style and eccentric charm, this journey to the coast is one you will not soon forget.
