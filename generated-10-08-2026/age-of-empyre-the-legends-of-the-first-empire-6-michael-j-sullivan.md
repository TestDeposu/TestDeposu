# Age of Empyre (The Legends of the First Empire, #6)

**Author:** Michael J. Sullivan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

History, as written by the victors, is rarely the whole truth. Over the course of six ambitious novels, Michael J. Sullivan has deconstructed the foundational myths of Elan, stripping away layers of divine propaganda to expose the messy, brutal, and profoundly human struggle that birthed a new world. With Age of Empyre, the grand finale of the Legends of the First Empire series, Sullivan delivers a masterclass in epic fantasy conclusion-writing, tying off decades of myth and millennia of lore with emotional resonance, narrative precision, and satisfying finality. 

At its core, Age of Empyre is a profound exploration of legacy, unity, and the heavy cost of paradigm shifts. The central theme of the novel examines how societies transition from the age of gods and monsters into the age of mortals. The Fhrey, long worshiped as immortal and infallible deities, are laid bare in all their tragic hubris, driven to desperate measures as their monopoly on power crumbles. Conversely, the Rhunes—mankind—rise not through inherent magical superiority, but through resilience, adaptability, and the radical idea of cooperation across divided tribes. Sullivan demonstrates that history is not forged by solitary legends destined by fate, but by ordinary individuals who choose to stand together against insurmountable odds when a door opens and the darkness presses in.

Sullivan’s authorial style remains one of the most reliable and comforting treasures in modern fantasy, yet it is elevated here by the sheer scale of the climax. Known for his brisk pacing, witty dialogue, and deeply empathetic character work, Sullivan eschews the grimdark nihilism currently popular in the genre in favor of classic, character-driven optimism. Even in the face of annihilation—with an army of dragons advancing and the fate of the living resting quite literally with the dead—the narrative never loses its heart. His prose is clean, transparent, and entirely unpretentious, allowing the complex plotting and emotional stakes to take center stage without getting bogged down by purple descriptions or convoluted world-building jargon. 

What makes Age of Empyre particularly striking as a finale is how it recontextualizes everything that came before it. Fans of Sullivan’s broader Elan corpus—specifically the Riyria Revelations—will find themselves experiencing a profound sense of historical vindication. The lies unmasked throughout this series are dismantled piece by piece, revealing the true architects of civilization. Yet, Sullivan achieves a rare narrative feat: while this book serves as a vital historical bridge for long-time readers, it operates with absolute emotional independence. You do not need to know the distant future of Elan to feel the crushing weight of the sacrifices made in this volume.

The pacing in this concluding installment is relentless. With Imaly plotting the overthrow of the fane and a mystic and a Keeper racing against time to preserve the future of two races, the tension builds steadily into a thunderous crescendo. Sullivan balances grand-scale military strategy with intimate, character-defining moments with the practiced hand of a veteran storyteller. No loose thread is left dangling, and no character’s arc is treated as an afterthought. 

Age of Empyre is a triumph of traditional epic fantasy executed with modern emotional intelligence. It reminds us why we fall in love with secondary worlds in the first place: to watch flawed, courageous people stare into the abyss and decide to build a better dawn. Sullivan delivers a bittersweet, triumphant, and utterly unforgettable farewell to an era, cementing the Legends of the First Empire as a defining modern epic fantasy series.
