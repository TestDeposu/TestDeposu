# A Game of Proof (The Trials of Sarah Newby #1)

**Author:** Tim Vicary  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

**A Game of Proof: A Legal Thriller That Strips Away the Glamour of Justice**

Tim Vicary’s *A Game of Proof*—the first in *The Trials of Sarah Newby* series—is a razor-sharp legal thriller that doesn’t just challenge the reader’s assumptions about guilt and innocence; it forces them to question the very fabric of trust. At its core, this is a story about a mother’s worst nightmare: her own son, Simon, stands accused of a series of brutal rapes and murders. But what makes this thriller so compelling isn’t just the high-stakes courtroom drama or the psychological tension of a parent facing their child’s alleged crimes. It’s the raw, unflinching exploration of how far a woman will go to defend someone she loves—even when the evidence seems damning.

Sarah Newby is no typical barrister. She’s a self-made woman, a single parent who left school at fifteen and clawed her way into the legal profession through sheer determination. Her rise is a testament to her resilience, but it also hints at the sacrifices she’s made along the way. The novel’s brilliance lies in how Vicary uses Sarah’s background to deepen the emotional stakes. She’s not just a lawyer defending her son; she’s a woman who has spent years proving she’s capable of more than her circumstances allowed. Now, she must confront whether her ambition has come at the cost of truly knowing Simon—or if his lies and secrets have always been part of a different kind of game.

Vicary’s writing is precise, immersive, and relentlessly gripping. He doesn’t rely on sensationalism; instead, he crafts a narrative that feels both intimate and explosive. The legal proceedings are meticulously detailed, but never dry—they’re woven into a personal saga that keeps the reader hooked. The courtroom scenes are electric, with Vicary’s understanding of legal strategy making the arguments feel authentic, whether Sarah is cross-examining witnesses or defending her client against overwhelming evidence. Yet, the real power of the novel lies in its psychological depth. Vicary doesn’t just ask, *Is Simon guilty?* He asks, *How much of a mother’s love is blind?* And the answer is never straightforward.

What sets *A Game of Proof* apart is its refusal to offer easy answers. Sarah’s journey is one of doubt, not just for her son but for herself. She uncovers evidence that seems to point to Simon’s guilt, forcing her to confront the possibility that she’s been deceived for years. The novel’s tension isn’t just in the courtroom but in the quiet moments—when Sarah questions whether she ever truly knew her son, or if she’s now willing to believe the worst to protect her own reputation. Vicary’s portrayal of Sarah is nuanced; she’s not a flawless heroine, but a woman grappling with the weight of her choices, her fears, and her love.

The legal thriller genre often thrives on moral clarity, but Vicary subverts that expectation. *A Game of Proof* is less about a battle between justice and injustice and more about the messy, human cost of pursuing it. The novel’s strength lies in its ability to make the reader feel the moral ambiguity, the fear, and the desperation of a mother who must decide how far she’s willing to go—not just for her son, but for herself.

Vicary’s style is a perfect blend of grit and sophistication. He grounds the high-stakes drama in the gritty reality of Sarah’s life, from her working-class upbringing to the pressures of her career. The dialogue crackles with authenticity, whether it’s the sharp exchanges in the courtroom or the raw, tense conversations between Sarah and Simon. The pacing is tight, with each revelation driving the story forward, but never at the expense of character development. By the time the final verdict is delivered, the reader isn’t just invested in the outcome—they’re invested in Sarah’s emotional journey.

For fans of legal thrillers, *A Game of Proof* stands alongside the best of the genre—works by John Grisham, Scott Turow, and Michael Connolly—but with a distinctly British edge. Vicary’s understanding of the legal system is sharp, and his ability to balance courtroom drama with personal stakes makes this more than just another procedural. It’s a story about the lengths we go to for those we love, the lies we tell ourselves, and the moments when the truth becomes too painful to face.

If you’re looking for a legal thriller that doesn’t just keep you on the edge of your seat but also challenges your assumptions about guilt, innocence, and the bonds of family, *A Game of Proof* is a must-read. It’s a gripping, emotionally charged debut that proves Vicary is a master of the genre—one who knows how to make the law feel personal, and the personal feel impossible to ignore.
