# The Lady of the Sorrows (The Bitterbynde, #2)

**Author:** Cecilia Dart-Thornton  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

Step into the shimmering, perilous twilight of a world where ancient myth breathes, bleeds, and casts a long, haunting shadow. In The Lady of the Sorrows, the second installment of Cecilia Dart-Thornton’s acclaimed Bitterbynde Trilogy, the author deepens the rich, atmospheric enchantment that first captivated readers in her debut. This is not merely a sequel; it is an immersive descent into a grand tapestry of folklore, where the boundaries between the mortal realm and the dark, unseelie Otherworld dissolve into a waking nightmare.

At the heart of Dart-Thornton’s narrative style is a lush, poetic lyricism that sets her apart in the realm of contemporary epic fantasy. Rather than relying solely on rapid-fire action, the author crafts her story with the meticulous patience of a master weaver. Every page is saturated with sensory details—the scent of damp moss and woodsmoke, the haunting strains of forgotten melodies, and the electric chill of supernatural malice. Dart-Thornton dips deeply into the well of British Isles mythology, breathing new life into traditional legends of faerie lore, monstrous hounds, and ancient curses. She treats folklore not as a mere backdrop, but as a living, breathing entity that dictates the fates of her characters.

The central theme of the novel pivots on identity, memory, and the crushing weight of destiny. Our nameless, mute heroine—now bearing the heavy mantle of the Lady of the Sorrows—continues her poignant quest to unearth the truth of her forgotten past. Her journey is one of quiet resilience. Thrust into the glittering yet treacherous halls of the royal court, she finds herself isolated, yearning for the enigmatic ranger Thorn while a darker fate closes in around her. Dart-Thornton excels at exploring the vulnerability of her protagonist, contrasting her internal grace against the external brutality of a world plunging into chaos. 

As the King-Emperor and his men wage a desperate war against the malevolent unseelie, a mounting psychological dread permeates the court. The shadow of the brutal Lord Huon and his monstrous Wild Hunt looms large, transforming the narrative into a tense psychological thriller wrapped in high fantasy. The central question—is the Lady herself the ultimate target of these nocturnal horrors?—drives the plot forward with a quiet, relentless momentum. 

For readers who cherish immersive world-building, poetic prose, and high-stakes epic fantasy rooted in authentic myth, The Lady of the Sorrows is an unforgettable experience. Dart-Thornton proves that true fantasy power lies not just in clashing swords, but in the haunting resonance of a well-told legend.
