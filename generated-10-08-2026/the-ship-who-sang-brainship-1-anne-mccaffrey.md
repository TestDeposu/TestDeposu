# The Ship Who Sang (Brainship, #1)

**Author:** Anne McCaffrey  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2026

In "The Ship Who Sang," Anne McCaffrey's beloved Brainship series is reinvigorated with a new installment that promises to captivate both old and new fans alike. This latest entry is a testament to McCaffrey's enduring legacy in the science fiction and fantasy genres. Co-authored by Mercedes Lackey and Veronica Giguere, this novel seamlessly blends action, suspense, and humor, making it an unforgettable read.

At the heart of the story is Brainship Tia, a sentient spaceship, and her trusted Brawn, Alex. Their partnership is put to the test when they accept a contract to test a prototype singularity drive, which comes with a catch - the drive's tendency to make "random" jumps. As they navigate the dangers of space, Tia and Alex find themselves entangled in a web of intrigue, confronting abandoned research stations, and unraveling the mysteries behind the singularity drive's erratic behavior.

McCaffrey's signature style shines through in the novel's fast-paced narrative, which masterfully balances character development with plot progression. The author's world-building is, as always, meticulous and immersive, drawing readers into the vast expanse of the Brainship universe. The characters of Tia and Alex are skillfully crafted, their banter and camaraderie adding a lighthearted touch to the story.

One of the most striking aspects of "The Ship Who Sang" is its exploration of the themes of identity, loyalty, and the blurred lines between human and artificial intelligence. Tia, as a sentient spaceship, grapples with her own sense of self, raising questions about the nature of consciousness and what it means to be alive. This introspection adds depth to the narrative, making the story more than just a space-faring adventure.

The writing is engaging, with a keen sense of pacing that propels the reader through the story. Lackey and Giguere's collaboration brings a fresh perspective to the series, while remaining true to McCaffrey's original vision. The result is a novel that feels both nostalgic and new, a testament to the timeless appeal of the Brainship series.

In conclusion, "The Ship Who Sang" is a thrilling addition to the Brainship series, offering a captivating blend of science fiction, adventure, and humor. With its richly detailed world-building, memorable characters, and thought-provoking themes, this novel is sure to delight both old and new fans of the series.
