# Elven Star (The Death Gate Cycle, #2)

**Author:** Margaret Weis  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1990

In "Elven Star," the second installment of The Death Gate Cycle, Margaret Weis masterfully weaves a rich tapestry of intricate world-building, compelling characters, and a gripping narrative that explores the destructive nature of prejudice and the redemptive power of unity.

Weis's world of Pryan is a masterclass in creative storytelling, where the relentless sunlight and abundant rainfall have given rise to a sprawling jungle that has forced humans and elves to inhabit the treetops, while the dwarves dwell in the shadows below. This unique environment serves as a potent metaphor for the rigid social stratification that has led to generations of discord and hatred among the three main races.

At the heart of the story is an unlikely trio of heroes: an elf, a human, and a dwarf, each with their own distinct voice and perspective. Weis skillfully navigates the complexities of their relationships, expertly conveying the tension, mistrust, and ultimately, the tentative bonds that form between them as they embark on a perilous quest to save their world from the Titans.

Weis's writing is characterized by a keen sense of pacing, effortlessly shifting between moments of high-stakes action, introspective character development, and witty dialogue. Her prose is evocative and immersive, conjuring vivid images of Pryan's lush jungle landscapes and the treacherous politics that govern the treetop cities.

One of the most striking aspects of "Elven Star" is its thought-provoking exploration of prejudice and its corrosive effects on society. Weis pulls no punches in her portrayal of the deep-seated biases that have led to the fragmentation of Pryan's cultures, and the devastating consequences that have resulted from these divisions. Through her characters' struggles and triumphs, she offers a powerful message about the importance of empathy, cooperation, and understanding in the face of overwhelming adversity.

If there is a criticism to be made, it is that the supporting cast of characters feels somewhat one-dimensional at times, serving primarily to advance the plot or illustrate a particular theme. However, this is a minor quibble in an otherwise engaging and well-crafted narrative.

In conclusion, "Elven Star" is a captivating and thought-provoking addition to The Death Gate Cycle, offering a rich and immersive world, compelling characters, and a gripping story that explores the destructive nature of prejudice and the redemptive power of unity. Weis's writing is engaging, evocative, and expertly paced, making this a must-read for fans of fantasy and adventure.
