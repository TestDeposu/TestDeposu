# Someplace to Be Flying (Newford, #5)

**Author:** Charles de Lint  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

Where does the magic of a modern metropolis truly reside? For master urban fantasist Charles de Lint, it lives not in distant, Tolkien-esque realms or forgotten medieval forests, but in the gritty, neon-lit alleys, trailer parks, and hidden corners of Newford. In his mesmerizing 1998 novel, Someplace to Be Flying—reissued in 2013 with a special author's afterword—de Lint once again proves why he is the unrivaled architect of contemporary urban fantasy. This rich, enthralling novel invites readers into a world where ancient mythologies breathe right alongside the asphalt and exhaust of the modern city, creating a breathtaking tapestry of human vulnerability and primordial power.

At its core, Someplace to Be Flying is a profound exploration of family, belonging, and the invisible threads that bind us to one another and to the earth. De Lint weaves a narrative centered on the misfits, the marginalized, and the orphans of the modern world. We are introduced to a makeshift family of choice—street folk who gather around the warmth of a fire by Moth’s trailer in a local junkyard. These are characters who carry heavy, damaged pasts and complex personal relationships, yet they find a fierce, resilient grace in one another. 

Against this deeply human backdrop, de Lint superimposes a vibrant, dangerous pantheon of ancient folklore, drawing heavily on Native American and Old European traditions. The novel introduces us to the "animal people" living secretly among us: the punky, delightfully dangerous Crow Girls who infiltrate the heart and change the narrative forever; Raven, the enigmatic keeper of the world's ways; Coyote, the handsome trickster whose good intentions invariably spark dire consequences; and the seductive Fox, haunted by past indiscretions. Standing against them are the vicious, amoral Cuckoos, sparking a high-stakes, apocalyptic conflict over an artifact of unimaginable power capable of destroying the entire world.

What elevates Someplace to Be Flying from a standard fantasy thriller into a work of literary art is de Lint’s signature style. His prose is unfailingly elegant, lyrical, and deeply empathetic. He possesses a rare gift for transforming the mundane into the magical at every turn, making the impossible feel as natural as drinking your morning coffee. His dialogue is entirely believable, anchoring the fantastical elements in a gritty, emotional truth. As critics have rightly noted, de Lint makes you believe as many as six impossible things before breakfast. 

Unlike many urban fantasies that rely solely on cheap thrills or tired tropes, de Lint’s work succeeds because he cares as deeply for his human characters as he does for his mythological tricksters. He is a folklorist of the highest order, seamlessly blending ancient myth into a modern setting without ever letting the seams show. The result is a book that transcends genre boundaries—captivating hardcore fantasy enthusiasts while remaining completely accessible and enthralling to readers who might normally steer clear of the genre.

Someplace to Be Flying is a soaring, imaginative triumph. It reminds us that magic is never truly gone; it is simply waiting around the corner, hiding in the laughter of the streets, the smoke of a junkyard fire, and the beating wings of forgotten legends. Charles de Lint has crafted a timeless masterpiece of urban myth-making that will linger in your soul long after the final page is turned.
