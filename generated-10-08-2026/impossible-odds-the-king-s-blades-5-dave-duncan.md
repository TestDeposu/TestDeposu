# Impossible Odds (The King's Blades, #5)

**Author:** Dave Duncan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Impossible Odds, the fifth installment in Dave Duncan’s “The King’s Blades” series, thrusts readers back into a world where swords are as much a matter of fate as they are of steel. The novel opens with a grim decree: the deposed Grand Duke Rubin must be restored to his throne, and the task falls to a ragtag trio of fledgling Blades—Ranter, whose quick wit is matched only by his social clumsiness; Ringwood, whose eagerness borders on recklessness; and Bellman, a mediocre swordsman whose latent talent for espionage makes him an unlikely but vital asset. Accompanied by the prescient White Sister Trudy, they embark on a perilous quest that pits them against an army of the dead and forces them to confront the unsettling truth that the duke they swore to protect is not the noble liege they imagined.

At its core, the book explores the theme of loyalty tested by illusion. Duncan interrogates what it means to serve a cause when the very figurehead of that cause is shrouded in deception. The Blades’ oath—once a proud symbol of honor—becomes a crucible that reveals each character’s inner strengths and weaknesses. Ranter learns that bravado cannot substitute for genuine understanding; Ringwood discovers that impetuosity must be tempered with patience; Bellman finds that his perceived inadequacy in combat is outweighed by his capacity for insight and subterfuge. Through their individual arcs, Duncan underscores that true heroism lies not in flawless skill but in the willingness to confront one’s limitations for a greater purpose.

Duncan’s style remains a hallmark of restrained yet evocative storytelling. He balances brisk, kinetic action with moments of quiet introspection, allowing the reader to feel the weight of each sword swing as well as the tremor of doubt that follows. Battle scenes are rendered with a crisp, almost cinematic clarity—clashing steel, the smell of blood and iron, the grim resolve of fighters who know they may not see dawn—yet never at the expense of character depth. Duncan’s dialogue crackles with a dry wit that often surfaces amid the grimmest circumstances, providing relief without undermining tension. His prose is economical; every sentence seems to pull double duty, advancing plot while revealing nuance about the world’s politics, religion, and the lingering shadows of past wars.

The novel’s pacing is deliberate, mirroring the Blades’ own journey from raw recruits to hardened warriors. Duncan spends ample time establishing the oppressive atmosphere of Ironhall, the training grounds where hope and desperation intermix, before thrusting the protagonists into the wider world’s chaos. This gradual build‑up makes the eventual confrontation with the undead army feel earned rather than gratuitous. Moreover, the author’s handling of the supernatural—raising questions about necromancy, destiny, and the cost of defying death—adds a layer of philosophical depth that elevates the story beyond a straightforward sword‑and‑sorcery romp.

What makes Impossible Odds particularly striking is its refusal to glorify war uncritically. Duncan constantly reminds us that the blades wielded by his heroes are instruments of both protection and destruction, and that the line between savior and tyrant can blur with a single misjudged strike. The revelation about Duke Rubin’s true nature forces the Blades—and the reader—to reconsider the motivations behind their quest: Is it loyalty to a person, loyalty to an ideal, or simply the inertia of sworn duty? By leaving these questions open, Duncan invites reflection long after the final page is turned.

In sum, Impossible Odds is a compelling blend of gritty combat, character‑driven drama, and moral inquiry. Dave Duncan’s seasoned voice shines through, delivering a tale that honors the traditions of epic fantasy while pushing its boundaries with thoughtful nuance. Fans of the series will find familiar comforts in the camaraderie of the Blades, while newcomers will be drawn in by the stark honesty of Duncan’s storytelling and the relentless, yet hopeful, march of his unlikely heroes toward an uncertain destiny.
