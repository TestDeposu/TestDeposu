# Sword-Maker (Tiger and Del, #3)

**Author:** Jennifer Roberson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1989

Step into the freezing, wind-scoured peaks of a world where steel sings and magic demands a terrible price. In Sword-Maker, the third installment of Jennifer Roberson’s acclaimed Sword-Dancer saga, the author deepens the mythos of her iconic pairing, Tiger and Del, shifting the focus to a harrowing, soul-deep exploration of solitary survival and reluctant heroism. Published in 1989, this volume finds Roberson at the absolute peak of her storytelling powers, effortlessly blending high-stakes sword-and-sorcery with profound psychological intimacy.

At its core, the main theme of Sword-Maker is the agonizing tension between independence and connection. Tiger, the swaggering, multi-disciplinary master of both Northern and Southron sword skills, finds himself physically separated from Del yet inextricably bound to her memory. Roberson uses this physical distance not to weaken their dynamic, but to test its foundation. Tiger is a man defined by his freedom, yet his pursuit of the monstrous hounds of hoolies and the rumored dragon terrorizing the mountain village of Ysaa-den becomes a metaphor for his own internal reckoning. He must confront the reality that true mastery—whether of the blade or of one's own destiny—requires more than solitary prowess; it requires vulnerability. 

Roberson’s authorial style remains one of the defining treasures of 1980s fantasy. Eschewing the sprawling, overly ornate world-building of some of her contemporaries, she favors a lean, muscular prose style that mirrors the lethal efficiency of her protagonists. Every action sequence is choreographed with breathtaking precision, smelling of ozone, sweat, and cold iron. Yet, Roberson never sacrifices character for spectacle. The dialogue crackles with sharp wit and underlying emotional weight, establishing a rhythm between the characters that feels genuinely lived-in. 

When the narrative ultimately drags Tiger to the ominous heights of Dragon’s Lair and pits him against the legendary wizard Chosa Dei, the book transcends traditional fantasy tropes. It ceases to be merely a quest to slay a beast and transforms into a philosophical battle of wills, forcing Tiger to face an adversary capable of unmaking reality itself. 

Sword-Maker is a masterclass in epic fantasy pacing and character development. Roberson delivers a striking, atmospheric adventure that honors the traditions of sword-and-sorcery while elevating them through rich emotional resonance. For fans of fierce heroics, intricate magic, and unforgettable character chemistry, this perilous mountain ascent is an absolute must-read.
