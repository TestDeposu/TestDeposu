# Burning Lamp (Arcane Society, #8; Dreamlight Trilogy, #2)

**Author:** Amanda Quick  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

Paranormal romance meets Victorian-era intrigue in Amanda Quick’s captivating Burning Lamp, the second installment in the mesmerizing Dreamlight trilogy and the eighth book within the broader, beloved Arcane Society universe. Known for her seamless blend of historical detail and supernatural suspense, Quick delivers a novel where psychic abilities, looming madness, and simmering passion collide against the atmospheric backdrop of 19th-century London. 

At the heart of the narrative is a brilliant and haunting exploration of a central theme: the heavy, inescapable burden of legacy and the corrupting nature of power. The novel revolves around the infamous Winters Curse, a supposed hereditary madness tied to a mysterious artifact known as the Burning Lamp—a creation of Nicholas Winters, one of the founding fathers of the Arcane Society. For generations, the male descendants of the Winters line have inherited not only extraordinary psychic talents but also a terrifying descent into hallucinations and psychological ruin. 

Quick uses this curse not merely as a supernatural plot device, but as a poignant metaphor for fear, isolation, and the struggle for self-determination. Griffin Winters, a notorious crime lord plagued by nightmares and convinced his mind is fracturing, embodies the tragedy of a man fighting his own biology. His journey is one of reclaiming agency over a destiny that seems pre-written by his ancestors. 

When Griffin’s path crosses with the resourceful and enigmatic Adelaide Pyne—who unwittingly holds the missing piece of the puzzle, the lamp itself—the story shifts into a high-stakes game of survival. Quick’s signature writing style shines brightest in the dynamic between these two protagonists. Adelaide is no shrinking violet of the Victorian era; she possesses a sharp intellect and a quiet strength that anchors Griffin’s volatile energy. Their developing romance is not a sudden, convenient spark, but a slow-burning combustion fueled by shared danger and mutual vulnerability. As they test the boundaries of their psychic abilities together, Quick illustrates how intimacy and emotional connection can serve as a shield against chaos.

The author’s prose is effortlessly readable, balancing dark, Gothic undertones with witty banter and brisk pacing. While the historical setting feels rich and textured, Quick never lets the period details bog down the narrative momentum. Instead, she keeps the focus sharply on the external threats—shadowy forces within and outside the Arcane Society who will stop at nothing to possess the lamp—and the internal battles of her characters. 

Burning Lamp is a striking testament to Amanda Quick’s mastery of romantic suspense. It offers readers a thrilling escape into a world where the supernatural is treated with grounded realism, and where love is the most potent magic of all. Whether you are a devoted follower of the Arcane Society arc or a newcomer looking for a taut, atmospheric romance with a dash of the uncanny, this novel proves to be a brilliantly entertaining read that lingers long after the final page is turned.
