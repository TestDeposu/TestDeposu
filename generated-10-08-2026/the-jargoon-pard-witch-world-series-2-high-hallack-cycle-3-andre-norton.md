# The Jargoon Pard (Witch World Series 2: High Hallack Cycle, #3)

**Author:** Andre Norton  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

In The Jargoon Pard, Grand Master of fantasy Andre Norton returns to the sprawling, endlessly inventive tapestry of her beloved Witch World, specifically anchoring her narrative in the mythic sub-cycle of High Hallack. Originally published to expand upon a universe that has captivated readers for decades, this novel encapsulates Norton’s signature literary strengths: a profound sense of ancient mystery, the heavy burden of destiny, and a deep-seated reverence for the fragile balance between humanity and the natural, often untamed world.

At the narrative's emotional and psychological core is the theme of bodily and spiritual autonomy versus manipulation. Young Kethan is a classic Norton protagonist—marginalized, burdened by a royal lineage he scarcely wants, and immediately besieged by predatory figures in his own court. His mother, Lady Heroise, and her conniving Wise Woman, Ursilla, view him merely as a puppet for political ascendancy, while his cousin Maughus represents petty, vindictive malice. Norton brilliantly uses this claustrophobic aristocratic intrigue to set up the central Faustian bargain of the book. When Kethan receives the mysterious leopard-skin belt adorned with a carved jargoon stone, it offers an alluring escape from his powerlessness. 

However, Norton refuses to write a simplistic tale of empowerment. The magic of the jargoon pard is seductive, dangerous, and transformative. As Kethan’s body begins to morph into a shape more lethal than any human weapon, the book evolves into a striking exploration of identity. The metamorphosis is physical, but the true battle is fought in the moral crucible of Kethan’s soul. He must navigate a treacherous, crooked path between light and darkness, discovering that true strength does not lie in dominating others or surrendering to wild, predatory instincts, but in mastering the self. This internal struggle elevates the novel from a standard portal-and-artifact quest into a poignant coming-of-age allegory about the costs of survival and power.

Norton’s authorial style in The Jargoon Pard is both lyrical and economical. Eschewing the bloated world-building exposition common in modern epic fantasy, she drops the reader directly into the atmospheric lore of Arvon and High Hallack. Her prose relies heavily on tactile sensory details—the chill of stone corridors, the oppressive weight of political expectation, the sleek, hypnotic texture of the enchanted pelt, and the feral thrill of the hunt. There is an archetypal fairy-tale quality to her storytelling, where objects carry ancient weight and every shadow in a palace holds a potential threat or a hidden ally. 

While the book functions as a standalone adventure within the broader High Hallack cycle, it resonates deeply with long-time fans of the Witch World canon. Norton’s fascination with animal shapeshifting, ancient curses, and the resilience of outsiders shines on every page. She captures the melancholy beauty of a world steeped in forgotten magic, where the past constantly bleeds into the present.

Ultimately, The Jargoon Pard is a rewarding, atmospheric read that showcases Andre Norton at the height of her evocative powers. It speaks directly to anyone who has ever felt cornered by circumstance and yearned for the strength to break their chains—reminding us, however, that the wildness we unleash to save ourselves may demand our very humanity in return. A striking and hypnotic addition to any fantasy lover's bookshelf.
