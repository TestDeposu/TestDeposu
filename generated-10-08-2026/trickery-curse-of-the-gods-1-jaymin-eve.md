# Trickery (Curse of the Gods, #1)

**Author:** Jaymin Eve  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Unknown

**Trickery: A Mythic Masterpiece Where Gods Play Dice with Mortals**

Jaymin Eve’s *Trickery*—the first installment in *The Curse of the Gods*—is a dazzling, fast-paced fantasy that reimagines the ancient world through the lens of divine deception, mortal defiance, and the razor’s edge of survival. Set in a realm where the gods are as unpredictable as they are powerful, this novel doesn’t just weave a tale; it spins a web of intrigue so tight you’ll find yourself questioning every shadow, every whispered oath, and every goddamn miracle.

At its core, *Trickery* is a story about the cost of power—and the price of believing in it. The protagonist, a young woman named **Lyssa**, is no ordinary thief. She’s a *trickster*, a survivor who’s spent her life outsmarting the gods themselves, who see mortals as pawns in their eternal games. When Lyssa stumbles upon a prophecy that could either save her people or doom them all, she becomes the unwitting target of divine wrath, political schemers, and her own growing belief that maybe—just maybe—she’s more than a fleeting spark in the gods’ fire.

Eve’s prose is sharp as a dagger, laced with the kind of wit and bite that makes every line feel like a punch to the gut. Her worldbuilding is rich but never overwhelming, grounding the mythic in the gritty. The gods here aren’t lofty, distant figures; they’re petty, cruel, and endlessly entertaining, much like the best villains in any story. Their voices crackle through the narrative, their whimsy as dangerous as their power. And Lyssa? She’s a protagonist who refuses to be a damsel—she’s cunning, ruthless, and deeply flawed, which makes her victories all the more satisfying.

What sets *Trickery* apart is its unflinching exploration of **faith, free will, and the illusion of control**. The gods in this world don’t just watch mortals—they *play* with them, bending fate like a bowstring. Lyssa’s journey isn’t just about outsmarting them; it’s about deciding whether to break the cycle or become another pawn in their grand design. Eve doesn’t offer easy answers. Instead, she forces readers to ask: *How far would you go to defy the gods? And what happens when you realize they’ve already won?*

The pacing is relentless, with twists that feel earned rather than contrived. Betrayals sting, alliances shift like sand, and the stakes escalate with the precision of a well-tuned blade. By the time the final pages land, you’ll be left breathless—not just from the action, but from the sheer audacity of the story’s vision.

For fans of **gritty fantasy, morally complex protagonists, and gods who treat mortals like chess pieces**, *Trickery* is a must-read. It’s a book that doesn’t just entertain; it challenges, provokes, and leaves you hungry for the next move in the game.

**Final Verdict:** A bold, brilliantly written debut that redefines what it means to defy the divine.
