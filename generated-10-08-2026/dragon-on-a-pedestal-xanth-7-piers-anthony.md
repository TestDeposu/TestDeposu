# Dragon On A Pedestal (Xanth, #7)

**Author:** Piers Anthony  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1983

Piers Anthony’s 1983 addition to the sprawling fantasy realm of Xanth, Dragon On A Pedestal, is a quintessential entry in a series that has long thrived on unchecked imagination, relentless punning, and a distinctively quirky approach to quest-fantasy. Clocking in as the seventh installment, this novel embraces the chaotic charm that made Anthony a household name among young adult fantasy readers of the era, while unapologetically showcasing his trademark literary eccentricities.

At the heart of Dragon On A Pedestal lies a deceptively simple yet high-stakes narrative engine: a missing toddler and a loose leviathan. When three-year-old Ivy, daughter of King Dor and Queen Irene, wanders deep into the perilous jungles south of the Gap, she falls victim to a wandering forget-whorl—a localized fragment of amnesia left behind by the breaking of the great forget-spell that once sealed the infamous Gap. As Ivy toddles obliviously toward danger, her mind wiped clean of identity, she inadvertently heads straight into the path of the escaped Gap Dragon, a creature on the prowl for a substantial meal. Adding to the catastrophe, Good Magician Humfrey, the ultimate source of wisdom in Xanth, has accidentally overdosed on the Fountain of Youth and reverted to a helpless infant, leaving the realm entirely devoid of its usual problem-solving oracle.

The central theme of the novel pivots around vulnerability, memory, and the resilience of innocence. By infantilizing both the wisest figure in the land—Magician Humfrey—and the most innocent—little Ivy—Anthony strips Xanth of its institutional safety nets. The adults are forced to scramble, negotiate with bizarre creatures, and confront their own anxieties. Yet, it is Ivy’s perspective, framed by absolute childlike naivety in the face of lethal monstrosities, that anchors the emotional stakes. The forget-whorls serve as a clever thematic device, exploring the fragility of identity and how memory shapes one’s perception of reality. Without memory, the horrors of the Xanth jungle are merely curious novelties to a toddler, creating a darkly humorous and tense juxtaposition for the reader.

Anthony’s authorial style is on full, unbridled display here, characterized by an exhaustive dedication to wordplay, environmental personification, and systemic world-building. Xanth is a living, breathing ecosystem of linguistic literalism, where every landscape feature, creature, and plant is born from a groan-worthy pun. For some readers, this relentless barrage of humor is an acquired taste—a whimsical carnival ride through surrealist comedy. For others, it can occasionally threaten to overshadow the plot's genuine urgency. However, Anthony manages to balance the levity of the jokes with a surprisingly swift narrative pace. The search parties navigating the hazards of the southern jungle provide a classic, episodic adventure structure that keeps the pages turning.

What makes Dragon On A Pedestal stand out in the overarching Xanth canon is its focus on family dynamics and generational vulnerability. King Dor and Queen Irene are not merely epic heroes; they are terrified parents navigating every parent's worst nightmare within a magical landscape that turns every bush and beast into an active hazard. This grounds the fantasy in a relatable emotional core, even as the narrative detour-dances through absurd encounters and anatomical riddles.

Ultimately, Dragon On A Pedestal is a fun, nostalgic, and vividly imaginative romp that delivers exactly what Piers Anthony fans crave. It is a world where danger and silliness coexist in equal measure, proving that even when a realm is facing apocalypse-level threats from escaped dragons and amnesia-inducing weather patterns, there is always time for a clever quip. For returning readers, it is a comforting visit with old, delightfully eccentric friends; for newcomers, it is a quintessential 1980s fantasy trip that refuses to take itself too seriously.
