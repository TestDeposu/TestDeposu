# Power That Preserves (The Chronicles of Thomas Covenant the Unbeliever, #3)

**Author:** Stephen R. Donaldson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1983

Power That Preserves thrusts Thomas Covenant back into a Land on the brink of annihilation, where the once‑noble Lords of Revelstone are besieged and the ancient enemy Lord Foul feeds on every setback. Donaldson’s third installment deepens the moral quagmire that defines the series: Covenant’s reluctant heroism is less about wielding power than about confronting the corrosive temptation to use it for selfish ends. The novel’s central theme hinges on the paradox that true preservation often requires surrender—of pride, of certainty, even of the self‑loathing that has defined Covenant since his first summons. As the Land’s magic wanes under Foul’s creeping influence, the protagonists—giant, Bloodguard, and the tormented woman Covenant wronged—forge a fragile alliance that tests whether compassion can outlast despair.

Donaldson’s style remains unmistakably dense and lyrical, weaving archaic diction with stark, psychological introspection. He balances epic battle scenes with lingering interior monologues, allowing readers to feel the weight of each decision as much as the clang of swords. The prose is deliberately challenging, rewarding close reading with layers of mythic resonance and philosophical inquiry. Yet this very richness can be a double‑edged sword; newcomers may find the narrative’s opacity daunting, while seasoned fans will appreciate the way Donaldson refracts classic fantasy tropes through a lens of existential doubt and moral ambiguity.

Power That Preserves does not offer easy victories; instead, it insists that the struggle to preserve what matters is itself a form of power—one that demands continual, painful renewal. In doing so, the novel cements the Chronicles as a work where fantasy becomes a conduit for probing the human condition, and where the line between savior and damned is perpetually redrawn.
