# Between Two Fires

**Author:** Christopher Buehlman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

The year is 1348, and the world is already ending. Before the human race can even process the agonizing, indiscriminate devastation of the Black Death, a far more terrifying apocalypse is quietly unfolding in the shadows. This is the brutal, bleeding canvas of Christopher Buehlman’s Between Two Fires, a masterwork of medieval dark fantasy that redefines the boundaries of historical horror. 

At its core, Buehlman’s narrative explores the timeless, agonizing theme of redemption in the face of absolute despair. The story follows Thomas, a disgraced and battle-weary knight whose soul is as scarred and mutilated as his body. Drifting through a plague-ravaged France, Thomas encounters a young orphan named Delphine. Innocent yet unnervingly perceptive, Delphine carries a burden far heavier than her years: she insists that the pestilence wiping out humanity is merely a prelude to a greater war. The gates of Hell have broken open, Lucifer’s fallen angels are rising for a second assault on Heaven, and Earth has become the blood-soaked battleground. Convinced that she holds a divine mandate to reach Avignon and confront the darkness, Delphine enlists the reluctant, faithless knight as her protector. 

What follows is a macabre pilgrimage through a depraved landscape where the horrors of human cruelty are chillingly eclipsed by the supernatural. Buehlman’s style is nothing short of transcendent. He writes with the grit and realism of a seasoned historical novelist, grounding his fantastical elements in the mud, blood, and rotting stench of fourteenth-century Europe. The prose is lush, visceral, and uncompromisingly dark, yet it is consistently pierced by moments of aching poetic beauty and stark grace. 

Buehlman does not merely tell a story of good versus evil; he meticulously examines the fragile, stubborn nature of human faith when God seems to have abandoned His creation. Thomas and Delphine’s journey is a profound theological road trip, a descent into madness and damnation that paradoxically uncovers the lingering embers of grace. The juxtaposition of grotesque body horror—risen dead, grotesque demons, and weeping saints—with deeply human vulnerability creates a haunting, atmospheric tension that lingers long after turning the final page. 

Between Two Fires is a triumph of dark imagination. It is a terrifying, breathtaking epic that proves horror and hope can occupy the exact same broken heart. For readers brave enough to walk the road to Avignon, Buehlman offers an unforgettable descent into the fire.
