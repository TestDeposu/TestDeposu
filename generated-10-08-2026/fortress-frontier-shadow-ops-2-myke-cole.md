# Fortress Frontier (Shadow Ops, #2)

**Author:** Myke Cole  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

When Myke Cole burst onto the contemporary fantasy scene with *Control Point*, he introduced readers to a military-industrial complex grappling with the terrifying, uncontainable advent of human magic. With *Fortress Frontier*, the second installment in the *Shadow Ops* series, Cole returns to the bloody trenches of a world at war with itself, proving that military fantasy can be both brutally realistic and kinetically thrilling. 

If the first novel was about the shattering of the mundane world, *Fortress Frontier* is about the desperate, grinding reality of holding the line amid the fallout. The narrative’s beating heart remains Colonel Alan Bookbinder. Unlike the hyper-trained, battle-hardened operatives typical of the genre, Bookbinder is an anomaly: an aging army bureaucrat whose initiation into magic was as unwanted as it was abrupt. Thrust onto the literal and figurative front lines in command of the besieged Forward Operating Base Frontier, Bookbinder represents the ultimate theme of the novel—the agonizing intersection of bureaucracy and raw, apocalyptic power. 

Cole’s background as a security contractor and government employee permeates every page, lending the novel a gritty, procedural authenticity that sets it apart from high fantasy epics. The author does not merely use military terminology as window dressing; he explores the logistical nightmares of supplying a supernatural army, the grinding fatigue of command, and the moral rot that seeps in when the rules of engagement are rewritten by fire and blood. FOB Frontier feels uncomfortably real—a dusty, isolated crucible where soldiers are pushed past their breaking points, surrounded by monsters both supernatural and bureaucratic.

Thematic friction defines the core of the story. Cole brilliantly juxtaposes the rigid, procedural mindset of the traditional military with the chaotic, highly individualistic nature of magic. Bookbinder’s leadership is tested not just by the horrors outside the base's perimeter, but by the politics and mistrust within. The introduction of Oscar Britton—the fugitive whose cataclysmic powers triggered the base's current peril—creates a compelling ideological and tactical clash. The reluctant alliance between Bookbinder and public enemy number one forces the narrative to grapple with uncomfortable questions about necessity versus morality. When the world is ending, how much of your humanity can you afford to sacrifice just to survive another day?

Stylistically, Cole’s prose is lean, muscular, and relentlessly fast-paced. He approaches action scenes with the precision of a tactical report, yet infuses them with visceral, cinematic terror. There is no romanticizing of war here; magic is depicted not as a wondrous gift, but as a dangerous, volatile utility that scars the soul just as deeply as it incinerates the enemy. The dialogue snaps with authentic military banter and the exhausted gallows humor of people living on borrowed time. 

What makes *Fortress Frontier* a triumph is its refusal to offer easy answers. Cole strips away the glamorous veneer of superheroics, replacing it with mud, blood, and the crushing weight of responsibility. It is a story about ordinary men and women asked to do the impossible, led by a man who would rather push paper than command armies, yet somehow finds the steel to stand in the breach.

For readers craving a military sci-fi/fantasy hybrid that treats its worldbuilding with uncompromising seriousness and its characters with empathetic grit, *Fortress Frontier* is an essential read. Myke Cole has firmly established himself as a master of contemporary military fantasy, delivering a high-stakes thriller that leaves you smelling the cordite and feeling the chill of a world teetering on the edge of the abyss.
