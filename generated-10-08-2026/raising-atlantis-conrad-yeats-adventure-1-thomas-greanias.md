# Raising Atlantis (Conrad Yeats Adventure #1)

**Author:** Thomas Greanias  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2005

Buried beneath two miles of Antarctic ice lies the ultimate secret of human history: the legendary lost city of Atlantis. In his 2005 bestselling debut novel, Raising Atlantis, author Thomas Greanias plunges readers headfirst into a high-octane thriller that marries speculative archaeology with global apocalyptic stakes. This is the novel that launched the Conrad Yeats Adventure series, and it does so with a breathless momentum that has earned comparisons to masters of the genre like Clive Cussler and James Rollins. 

At the heart of the narrative is Dr. Conrad Yeats, a brilliant authority on megalithic architecture whose expertise becomes the key to unlocking a subterranean metropolis. Greanias constructs his protagonist not merely as an action hero, but as an intellectual warrior. Yeats is joined by an uneasy coalition: his estranged father, General Griffin Yeats, representing the unyielding weight of the military-industrial complex, and Serena Serghetti, a linguistic genius and Conrad’s former lover. The interpersonal friction between these three adds a much-needed human anchor to a plot that moves at a breakneck, cinematic pace. 

Greanias’s authorial style is defined by maximalism and relentless pacing. He takes real-world historical anomalies, ancient mythologies, and fringe scientific theories, blending them into a cocktail of high-concept fiction. The pacing rarely drops below a sprint; chapters end on sharp hooks, propelling the reader forward through a labyrinth of government conspiracies, ancient technology, and supernatural dread. Where some authors might bog down the narrative with dense archaeological exposition, Greanias prefers the adrenaline-fueled approach, ensuring that every historical revelation is immediately tied to a physical threat. 

The central theme of Raising Atlantis explores the arrogance of human ambition set against the timeless, indifferent power of the earth. Atlantis in this novel is not merely a lost civilization of aesthetic marvels and advanced machinery; it is a Pandora’s box. The discovery beneath the ice forces humanity to confront its own origins and its potential extinction. Greanias plays with the eternal fear that our ancestors possessed knowledge so vast it ultimately destroyed them—and that modern civilization is hurtling toward the exact same precipice. 

While purists of literary fiction might balk at the larger-than-life plot twists and occasional reliance on action-thriller tropes, Raising Atlantis never pretends to be anything other than a supremely entertaining rollercoaster. It is a book designed to be devoured in a single weekend, offering an escape into a world where ancient mysteries still hold the power to reshape reality. For fans of techno-thrillers, lost-world myths, and relentless suspense, Greanias’s debut remains a thrilling excavation of the imagination.
