# Beyond Sanctuary

**Author:** Janet E. Morris  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

**Beyond Sanctuary: A Swirling Storm of Power, Betrayal, and the Undying**

Janet E. Morris’s contribution to *Beyond Sanctuary*—the seventh volume in the sprawling, ever-evolving *Myth Adventures* shared-world anthology—is a masterclass in how to weave political intrigue, supernatural dread, and visceral action into a tale that feels both timeless and urgently alive. Set against the crumbling grandeur of Sanctuary, a city fractured by war, betrayal, and the slow, creeping return of the dead, Morris’s story is a perfect microcosm of the anthology’s strengths: richly layered worldbuilding, morally complex characters, and a relentless sense of stakes that make every page turn.

At its core, *Beyond Sanctuary* is a story about power—who wields it, who craves it, and what happens when the dead refuse to stay buried. The Emperor’s death has left the city in chaos, with factions vying for control while the original Stepsons, those legendary warriors of old, return to reclaim their legacy. But the real threat isn’t just the clashing armies or the scheming nobles; it’s the witch, her dark pact, and the army of undead rising from the shadows. Morris excels at making the supernatural feel visceral, grounding the horror in the political and personal. The dead aren’t just mindless husks—they’re echoes of a city’s sins, a reminder that some wounds never heal. This duality—between the living’s desperate struggle for dominance and the undead’s silent, inevitable return—creates a tension that simmers beneath the surface of every scene.

Morris’s prose is sharp, economical, and steeped in atmosphere. She has a knack for describing a moment with just enough detail to make it feel immediate, whether it’s the rain-slicked streets of Sanctuary or the cold grip of a sword in a dying hand. Her characters are equally compelling: flawed, ambitious, and often ruthless, but never one-dimensional. The witch, in particular, is a fascinating study in moral ambiguity—her magic is both a tool and a curse, her motives as layered as the city she serves. Morris doesn’t shy away from the grim realities of power; her world is brutal, and her characters pay for their choices, often in blood.

What makes *Beyond Sanctuary* so compelling is how it blends the personal with the epic. The Stepsons’ return isn’t just about reclaiming a throne; it’s about legacy, honor, and the cost of vengeance. Meanwhile, the witch’s struggle is as much about survival as it is about defying the very forces that raised her. Morris weaves these threads together seamlessly, ensuring that even as the stakes grow larger, the emotional core of the story never wavers. The result is a narrative that feels both intimate and grand, a snapshot of a world on the brink of collapse—and the desperate, desperate people who refuse to let it fall without a fight.

For fans of the *Myth Adventures* series, this volume delivers the same sense of shared history and interconnected storytelling that makes the anthology so special. Morris’s story fits effortlessly into the larger tapestry, expanding on themes of empire, decay, and the inescapable weight of the past. Yet, it also stands on its own as a gripping, self-contained tale of survival, sacrifice, and the unyielding march of time—even for the dead.

If you love fantasy that balances political intrigue with supernatural horror, morally complex characters, and a world that feels both vast and intimate, *Beyond Sanctuary* is a must-read. Janet E. Morris doesn’t just tell a story; she immerses you in it, making you feel the cold rain on your face, the weight of a sword in your hand, and the creeping dread of a world that refuses to stay buried.
