# A Cast of Stones (The Staff and the Sword, #1)

**Author:** Patrick W. Carr  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

From Drunkard to Deliverer: A Masterclass in Christian Epic Fantasy

Patrick W. Carr’s 2013 debut novel, A Cast of Stones, arrives not with a whisper, but with the clashing of steel, the whisper of ancient prophecies, and the heavy weight of destiny. Set within a vividly textured medieval world that bristles with hidden dangers, this first installment of The Staff and the Sword series earned its 2014 Carol Award for Speculative Fiction by blending high-stakes political intrigue with a deeply personal narrative of redemption. 

At the heart of the story is Errol Stone, a down-on-his-luck roustabout and habitual drunkard from the backwater village of Callowford. When Errol is casually enlisted by a church messenger to deliver urgent missives to a hermit priest in the hills, he anticipates nothing more than a quick coin. Instead, he plunges headfirst into a deadly game of cat-and-mouse, hunted by ruthless assassins through a landscape steeped in peril. Forced on the run alongside the priest and a ragtag band of travelers, Errol discovers he has inadvertently stumbled into a quest that holds the fate of the entire kingdom in the balance.

The central theme of A Cast of Stones revolves around divine selection, hidden heritage, and personal redemption. The kingdom has been protected for millennia by the heirs of the first king, but that sacred dynasty is drawing to its inevitable close. Now, the secretive process of selecting a new monarch begins amidst shadows and looming threats. Yet, the true brilliance of Carr’s thematic exploration lies in Errol’s parallel transformation. The overarching question of the novel is not merely who will inherit the throne, but whether a deeply flawed, grief-stricken outcast can cast off the stains of his past and rise to become the hero his people desperately need. 

Carr’s authorial style is defined by its confident, up-tempo pacing and an acute eye for homely, life-as-lived details that anchor his fantasy realm in gritty reality. Rather than relying solely on sweeping grandiosity, Carr constructs a believable world through rich characterization and tactile descriptions. Both main and secondary characters are fully realized, flawed, and endearing, keeping readers guessing as loyalties are tested and dark motives are slowly unveiled. The evolution of Errol from a self-loathing drifter into a capable, courageous protector is meticulously plotted, making his hard-won victories feel genuinely earned rather than magically bestowed.

For fans of epic Christian fantasy, A Cast of Stones is a triumphant introduction to a talented new voice. It seamlessly marries gripping suspense, small-town drama, a subtle hint of romance, and grand prophetic stakes into a cohesive narrative that refuses to let go. Carr delivers a riveting adventure that proves the most powerful stones cast are not just those of ancient ritual, but the foundational changes forged within a redeemed human heart.
