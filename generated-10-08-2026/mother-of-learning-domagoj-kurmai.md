# Mother of Learning

**Author:** Domagoj Kurmaić  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

Time is a flat circle, but for Zorian Kazinski, it is a loaded weapon. In Domagoj Kurmaić’s breakout speculative fiction phenomenon, Mother of Learning, the author takes the classic time-loop trope—a narrative device popularized by cinema and genre fiction alike—and elevates it into an intricate, sprawling masterclass of progression fantasy and world-building. 

At its core, the novel explores the theme of forced metamorphosis. Zorian begins his journey as an insufferable, cynical mage-in-training at the prestigious Cyoria Royal Academy of Magic. He is profoundly anti-social, resentful of his family, and primarily concerned with escaping his overbearing mother and securing a quiet, comfortable life. But fate, as it turns out, has cruelly other plans. Thrust into a mysterious one-month time loop that resets every time he dies, Zorian is forced to live the same brutal month over and over again. 

Kurmaić masterfully uses this repetitive structure not to bore the reader, but to examine how pressure transforms carbon into diamond. The central theme shifts seamlessly from mere survival to the acquisition of knowledge and personal growth. Without the threat of permanent consequences initially, Zorian learns to shed his abrasive isolationism. He realizes that to break the loop, he cannot rely solely on his own meager magical talents; he must forge alliances, understand the intricate politics of a continent on the brink of war, and delve into esoteric magical arts—from shaping mind magic to mastering ancient rituals. 

The author’s style is intensely pragmatic, methodical, and deeply rewarding for analytical readers. Kurmaić approaches magic not as a soft, mysterious force of unexplained miracles, but as a hard science. Every spell has rules, limitations, and costs. The prose is clean, fast-paced, and relentlessly plot-driven, favoring tactical problem-solving and psychological realism over flowery descriptions. When Zorian encounters a problem, he doesn't overcome it through sudden, unearned bursts of chosen-one destiny; he fails, resets, researches, trains obsessively, and tries again. This gives the narrative an addictive, game-like progression rhythm that keeps the pages turning at a breathless pace.

Furthermore, the world of Mother of Learning feels remarkably lived-in. Kurmaić populates his universe with a rich tapestry of factions, including powerful noble houses, secretive spider colonies, aggressive barbarians, and ancient primordial evils. As the loops accumulate, the scope of the story broadens magnificently, transforming from a localized school-day mystery into an epic, high-stakes conspiracy involving the fate of the entire world. 

Mother of Learning is a triumphant testament to the power of meticulous plotting and character evolution. It takes a deeply flawed, reluctant protagonist and places him in the ultimate crucible of time, crafting a narrative that is as intellectually stimulating as it is wildly entertaining. For fans of intricate magic systems and brilliant strategic storytelling, this is an absolute must-read.
