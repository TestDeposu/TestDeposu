# Dog Wizard (Windrose Chronicles, #3)

**Author:** Barbara Hambly  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

**A Love Spell Across the Void: Barbara Hambly’s *Dog Wizard* and the Timeless Magic of Defiance**

Barbara Hambly’s *Dog Wizard* (2011), the third installment in her *Windrose Chronicles*, is a spellbinding return to the world of Joanna Sheraton—a woman who, against all odds, became a bridge between two realms: the mundane and the magical. Where Hambly’s earlier books in the series (*The Dragon’s Dilemma* and *The Dragon’s Spell*) established the stakes of Joanna’s journey—from reluctant heroine to reluctant queen—*Dog Wizard* refines the narrative into a poignant meditation on love, sacrifice, and the cost of defiance. Here, the story doesn’t just unfold; it *resonates*, weaving together political intrigue, personal tragedy, and a love so fierce it transcends the boundaries of time and space.

At its core, *Dog Wizard* is a tale of exile and redemption. Joanna and Antryg, the wizard who stole her heart, fled their perilous world to reclaim a quiet life on Earth, only to be torn apart by fate. When Joanna is abducted by a figure cloaked in dark sorcery, Antryg must confront the very Council that once condemned him to death—now his only hope for survival and reunion. The tension here isn’t just about magic or war; it’s about the *weight of choices*. Antryg, once a rebel, must now ally with those who saw him as a threat, forcing him to question whether loyalty to Joanna justifies betraying the principles that once defined him. Hambly’s genius lies in her ability to make these moral dilemmas feel *personal*, not abstract. The stakes aren’t just cosmic—they’re intimate, bleeding into the quiet moments between lovers, the fear of loss, and the stubborn hope that love can outlast even the Void itself.

Hambly’s prose is as sharp as it is lyrical, a hallmark of her writing that makes the *Windrose Chronicles* feel both timeless and urgently modern. Her descriptions of the magical world—its architecture, its politics, its creatures—are vivid without being overwrought. The Void, that liminal space between worlds, becomes a character in its own right, a place of both terror and wonder. Yet it’s Joanna’s voice that truly shines. Hambly crafts her as a protagonist who is neither passive nor infallible; she’s a woman shaped by trauma, capable of both brilliance and vulnerability. Her relationship with Antryg is the emotional heart of the series, and in *Dog Wizard*, Hambly deepens it with a tenderness that feels earned. There’s no grand romanticism here—just the quiet, aching reality of two people who love each other so deeply that they’d burn the world to keep the other safe.

The political intrigue is equally compelling, though it serves the personal story rather than overshadowing it. The Wizard’s Council, once a monolith of order, is now fractured, with factions vying for power in the absence of their former ruler. Hambly’s world-building is meticulous, but she never lets it bog down the narrative. Instead, she uses it to heighten the tension—every political maneuver feels like a step closer to Joanna’s rescue, every betrayal a knife twist in Antryg’s resolve. The villain of this tale isn’t just a faceless evil; he’s a man who understands the power of love—and seeks to weaponize it. This adds a chilling layer to the story, reminding readers that in Hambly’s universe, magic is never purely benevolent, and love can be both a shield and a weapon.

What makes *Dog Wizard* particularly striking is how it balances the grand and the intimate. The series has always been about Joanna’s journey from outsider to someone who wields influence, but in this book, Hambly shifts the focus slightly—to the *cost* of that influence. Joanna’s actions in previous books set in motion events that now threaten to consume her. Antryg’s return isn’t just about saving her; it’s about saving *them*, as a couple, from the consequences of their choices. There’s a melancholy beauty to this, a recognition that some loves are doomed from the start, yet we keep reaching for them anyway.

Hambly’s style is another of the series’ strengths. She writes with a dry wit and a keen eye for the absurd, even in the most dire circumstances. There’s a moment in *Dog Wizard* where a minor character delivers a line so perfectly understated that it could have come from a classic comedy of manners. Yet the tone never undercuts the gravity of the story. Instead, it gives the reader a moment to breathe, to remember that even in a world of wizards and wars, there’s room for humor—and for the quiet, stubborn hope that love, in all its messy, beautiful complexity, can endure.

For fans of Hambly’s work, *Dog Wizard* is a satisfying continuation of Joanna and Antryg’s story, but it also stands on its own as a tale of love, sacrifice, and the lengths we’ll go to protect the ones we love. It’s a book that reminds us that magic, whether in the form of spells or the unspoken bonds between people, is never just about power—it’s about *connection*. And in a world where Joanna and Antryg are constantly pulled between two realities, that connection is the one thing that keeps them from being lost in the Void forever.

If you’ve ever loved a story where the personal and the political collide, where love feels like both a weapon and a vulnerability, and where the magic isn’t just in the spells but in the people who wield them, then *Dog Wizard* is a book you’ll want to read. It’s a testament to Barbara Hambly’s ability to weave together high fantasy, deep emotion, and a narrative that lingers long after the last page.
