# In the Cities of Coin and Spice (The Orphan's Tales, #2)

**Author:** Catherynne M. Valente  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

To read Catherynne M. Valente is to surrender willingly to a labyrinth, one built not of stone and mortar, but of ink, breath, and relentless imagination. In the Cities of Coin and Spice, the second and concluding half of The Orphan’s Tales, Valente picks up the thread of her monumental narrative tapestry and weaves it into something even more dizzying, opulent, and profound. Following her acclaimed debut, In the Night Garden, this volume deepens the mythology of the nameless girl whose eyelids are inked with secret histories, offering a reading experience that feels less like turning pages and more like unrolling an ancient, jewel-encrusted scroll.

At the heart of Valente’s work lies a fierce, unwavering devotion to the art of storytelling itself. The primary theme running through the veins of this magnificent epic is the sheer, world-building power of narrative—how stories sustain us, define us, and construct the very architecture of our reality. Like Scheherazade holding death at bay with a thousand and one nights, Valente’s characters tell tales within tales, nesting narratives like Matryoshka dolls. We drift effortlessly from the Lake of the Dead to the City of Marrow, encountering ghost cities of spice, hedgehog warriors, winged skeletons, and women forged entirely of glass and gears. Yet, despite the dizzying proliferation of the fantastic, Valente never loses sight of the emotional core grounding her wanderers. Her fantastic elements—singing manticores, mutilated unicorns, loyal leopards, and sparrow calligraphers—are never mere window dressing; they are the vocabulary through which she explores longing, grief, identity, and survival.

Valente’s style is nothing short of intoxicating. It is maximalist prose at its absolute finest, dripping with sensory details that demand to be savored rather than rushed. Every sentence is meticulously polished, fragrant with spices, heavy with the weight of ancient myth, and glittering with metallic sheen. She borrows the cadence of traditional fairy tales and folklores from across the globe, blending them into a seamless, polyphonic chorus. It is a prose style that some might find overwhelmingly rich, yet for the adventurous reader, it is a feast. Complementing her labyrinthine sentences are the exquisite illustrations of Michael Kaluta, whose ethereal artwork serves as the perfect visual anchor for Valente’s sprawling dreamscapes. 

In the Cities of Coin and Spice is not a book one simply reads; it is a book one inhabits. Valente proves that fantasy can be both brutally dark and overwhelmingly beautiful, serving as a reminder that stories are the only true magic we have left. Open it anywhere, and you will find yourself irrevocably lost in its enchanting, endless weave.
