# Immortal Plague (The Judas Chronicles #1)

**Author:** Aiden James  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Unknown

History is rarely written by the survivors, and almost never by those who watched empires rise, crumble, and bleed from the very front row. In Immortal Plague, the explosive opening salvo of Aiden James’s acclaimed Judas Chronicles, the reader is plunged into a relentless, high-octane reimagining of the world’s darkest historical epochs, filtered through the tortured consciousness of perhaps the most infamous figure in human mythology: Judas Iscariot. 

Stripping away centuries of pious myth and religious dogma, James introduces us to a Judas who is neither a one-dimensional villain nor a saint in disguise, but something far more compelling: a weary, reluctant immortal cursed to walk the earth forever as punishment for his ultimate betrayal. James anchors his narrative in a profound and gripping central theme: the heavy burden of redemption and the agonizing weight of endless time. While most supernatural thrillers treat immortality as an enviable prize of power and leisure, Immortal Plague reframes it as a psychological purgatory. Through Judas, James explores what happens to a soul when it is denied the sweet release of death, forced to witness humanity repeat its most cyclical, bloody atrocities over and over again. The immortality depicted here is not a gift; it is a disease, a creeping plague of memory and guilt that festers across millennia.

Aiden James’s authorial style is defined by its kinetic energy, cinematic pacing, and a gritty, unflinching realism that grounds the supernatural in the mud, blood, and sweat of the past. James rejects flowery, archaic prose in favor of a punchy, modern narrative voice that cuts straight to the bone. His world-building does not rely on massive, lore-heavy info-dumps; instead, the past feels lived-in, tactile, and claustrophobic. Whether painting the suffocating, plague-ridden alleys of medieval Europe or the tense, shadowy corridors of the modern world, James crafts atmospheric settings that act as characters in their own right. The action sequences are choreographed with a filmmaker’s eye—fast, brutal, and utterly absorbing—ensuring that the reader's adrenaline never flags. 

Yet, what truly elevates Immortal Plague above standard genre fare is James’s commitment to moral gray areas. The historical thriller genre often relies on clear-cut divisions between heroes and villains, but James dances effortlessly in the shadows. The modern-day narrative running parallel to the historical flashbacks creates a dual-timeline puzzle that keeps the pages turning at a blistering pace. As ancient sins collide with contemporary threats, the stakes escalate from personal penance to global catastrophe, proving that the past is never truly dead—it is merely waiting for its moment to strike again.

For fans of dark historical fantasy, supernatural conspiracies, and complex antiheroes in the vein of Anne Rice’s Lestat or Highlander, Immortal Plague is an absolute must-read. Aiden James has crafted a thrilling, thought-provoking, and darkly addictive entry point into a saga that promises even greater depths of blood and revelation to come.
