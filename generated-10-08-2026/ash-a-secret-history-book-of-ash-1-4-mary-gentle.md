# Ash: A Secret History (Book of Ash, #1-4)

**Author:** Mary Gentle  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

**Ash: A Secret History – Where War Meets Myth and the Soul Pays the Price**

Mary Gentle’s *Ash: A Secret History*—the sprawling, genre-defying epic that won the British Science Fiction Association Award in 2000—is not merely a novel about war. It is a brutal, poetic meditation on the cost of survival, the weight of legacy, and the dangerous allure of power. Set in a world where the Ottoman Empire’s advance has reshaped Europe, Gentle crafts a story that blurs the line between historical fiction, fantasy, and dark fantasy, weaving together the lives of soldiers, scholars, and a woman who may be more than she seems.

At its heart, *Ash* is the story of Ash, a mercenary commander whose life has been defined by violence. She is a warrior in every sense—skilled with sword and gun, hardened by battle, and utterly devoted to her company. But Gentle’s genius lies in her refusal to let Ash be a one-dimensional figure of steel and blood. Beneath the armor, there is a woman haunted by the deaths she cannot prevent, the oaths she cannot keep, and a past she barely understands. Her leadership is not just tactical; it is moral, flawed, and deeply human. When she is thrust into a conflict that threatens to unravel the very foundations of Europe, she must confront not only the Infidel armies but also the secrets of her own lineage—secrets that suggest she may be more than mortal.

Gentle’s prose is lush yet precise, a style that balances the grit of war with the mythic weight of her world. Her descriptions of battle are visceral, immersive, and unflinching, yet they never descend into gratuitous violence for its own sake. Instead, each clash is a moment of consequence, a turning point in the lives of her characters. The world-building is equally impressive, blending historical detail with fantastical elements in a way that feels organic rather than forced. The Ottoman Empire is not just a backdrop but a living, breathing adversary, its culture and politics explored with depth and nuance.

What makes *Ash* truly compelling, however, is its exploration of power and legacy. Ash is not just fighting for survival; she is fighting for a future she may not deserve. The novel’s themes of inheritance—both biological and cultural—are woven into every layer of the story. The Infidel threat is real, but so too is the threat of Europe’s own corruption, its greed, and its willingness to sacrifice its own people for the sake of empire. Gentle forces her readers to ask: What does it mean to be a leader? What does it mean to carry the weight of history on your shoulders?

The novel’s structure—spanning four books—allows Gentle to develop her characters and world with patience and care. Ash’s journey is not a linear one; it is a spiral, where each battle, each betrayal, and each revelation pulls her deeper into a mystery that may destroy her. The supporting cast is equally rich, from the cunning scholar who sees patterns in the chaos to the soldiers who follow Ash not out of loyalty, but necessity. Even the Infidel armies are given depth, their motivations and beliefs presented with a fairness that elevates the story beyond mere good vs. evil.

Yet for all its strengths, *Ash* is not without its challenges. The pacing can be uneven, particularly in the early books, as Gentle lays the groundwork for her world and characters. Some readers may find the political intrigue slow-moving compared to the action, but those who stick with it will be rewarded with a story that grows in ambition and emotional resonance. The fantasy elements, while intriguing, can sometimes feel like they exist on the periphery, waiting for a grander reveal that never fully materializes in the first four books. Still, Gentle’s ability to weave myth into history is a testament to her skill as a storyteller.

Ultimately, *Ash: A Secret History* is a novel for those who crave depth, complexity, and a story that refuses to be contained by genre. It is a tale of war and wisdom, of sacrifice and survival, where the line between hero and villain is blurred by the weight of history. Gentle’s writing is both a celebration of the human spirit and a warning of its fragility. If you seek a story that is as much about the soul as it is about the sword, then *Ash* is a masterpiece waiting to be discovered.
