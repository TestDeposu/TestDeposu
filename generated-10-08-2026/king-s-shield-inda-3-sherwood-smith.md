# King's Shield (Inda, #3)

**Author:** Sherwood Smith  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2026

In Sherwood Smith's latest masterpiece, "King's Shield," the acclaimed Inda series reaches new heights as our hero Inda returns home after a decade of living under a new name and fighting against the pirate scourge. This epic fantasy novel is a must-read for fans of military fiction, courtly politics, and diverse characters, set within the richly detailed Sartorias-deles universe.

The premise of "King's Shield" is deceptively simple: Inda, now a skilled and feared pirate raider, learns that his home country is about to be attacked from the sea by an ancient enemy. His friend Evred, now the king, sees Inda's martial prowess and wants him to become the Royal Shield Arm – the person responsible for defending the entire kingdom from land and sea.

However, this is no ordinary task. Inda must learn how to conduct a land war, a skill he's never mastered, while facing an enemy outnumbering his force ten to one. The stakes are high, and the outcome far from certain. Smith masterfully ramps up the tension, making it impossible to put the book down as Inda navigates the treacherous landscape of politics, betrayal, and war.

One of the hallmark strengths of Smith's writing is her ability to create complex characters that feel fully realized. Inda, with his troubled past and strategic mind, is a compelling protagonist, while Evred's transformation from a powerless prince to a wise and just king is both inspiring and nuanced.

Smith's world-building is equally impressive, showcasing the intricate politics and cultures of the Sartorias-deles realm. From the treacherous waters of the Aalkahara to the majestic architecture of the royal palace, every detail feels meticulously crafted, drawing the reader into a rich and immersive world.

Another notable aspect of "King's Shield" is its exploration of themes that are both timely and timeless. Smith delves into the complexities of war, exploring the psychological toll on soldiers, the weight of responsibility, and the true cost of victory. This is no simplistic tale of good vs. evil; Smith presents a nuanced, morally ambiguous narrative that challenges readers to confront their own assumptions.

Throughout the book, Smith's prose is crisp, engaging, and effortlessly readable. Her writing is a masterful blend of action, drama, and character-driven storytelling, never shying away from the brutal realities of war. This is a book that will appeal to fans of military fiction, fantasy epics, and historical fiction alike.

In short, "King's Shield" is a triumph for Sherwood Smith, cementing her reputation as a masterful storyteller. This book is a gripping and immersive ride that will leave readers eagerly anticipating the next installment of the Inda series. For fans of epic fantasy, military fiction, and complex world-building, "King's Shield" is a must-read.
