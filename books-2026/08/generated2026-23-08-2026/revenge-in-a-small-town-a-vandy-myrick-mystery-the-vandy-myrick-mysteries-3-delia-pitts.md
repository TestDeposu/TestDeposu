**Author:** Delia Pitts  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-12-01 APL

The promise of a mystery that weaves together past and present - always a tantalizing prospect, and one that Delia Pitts seems determined to master in *Revenge in a Small Town*. As the third installment in The Vandy Myrick Mysteries, this upcoming novel has the unenviable task of not only living up to its predecessors but also subverting the expectations that come with a well-established series.

One can't help but wonder, though - will Pitts's narrative be able to balance the demands of a decades-old mystery with the more immediate concerns of Vandy Myrick's investigation? The synopsis hints at a complex web of secrets and scores to be settled, but it's anyone's guess how these threads will be woven together. Perhaps it's a deliberate choice, this vagueness, meant to pique our interest and leave us speculating about the twists and turns that lie ahead.

And yet, despite the uncertainty, there's something undeniably compelling about Vandy Myrick herself - a Black woman private investigator in a small New Jersey town, still reeling from the loss of her daughter and trying to rebuild her life. It's a setup that echoes the likes of Tana French's Dublin Murder Squad series, where the personal and professional lives of the detectives are inextricably linked. Will Vandy's own trauma inform her approach to the case, or will it prove to be a liability in the face of the Bartlett family's secrets?

### The Weight of Unspoken Histories

The more I ponder the synopsis, the more I'm struck by the weight of unspoken histories that seem to be at play here. The Bartlett twins' concern for their mother's safety, Merna's own cryptic remarks about stories she could tell and scores she could settle - it all suggests a deep-seated unease that's been simmering for decades. But what exactly is the nature of this unease, and how will Vandy's investigation bring it to the surface?

One thing's for certain: Pitts has her work cut out for her in terms of crafting a narrative that's both propulsive and thoughtful. It's a delicate balancing act, one that requires a deep understanding of the characters and their motivations. I'm reminded of Laura Lippman's *What the Dead Know*, where the past and present collide in unexpected ways, forcing the protagonist to confront the darker aspects of her own history.

But I digress - or do I? Perhaps it's precisely this willingness to digress, to follow the threads of a narrative wherever they may lead, that will make *Revenge in a Small Town* such a compelling read. Only time will tell, of course, but for now, I'm willing to take a chance on Delia Pitts's latest offering.

And now, if you'll excuse me, I have more pressing matters to attend to - namely, selecting the floral arrangements for my upcoming book launch. It's a daunting task, to be sure, but someone's got to do it.
