**Page Count:** Unknown

We are expected to collectively swoon over a preacher's daughter being hauled out of a railway car like a sack of turnips by a so-called brooding outlaw. Groundbreaking. *Bloom Town: Genesis* wants us to buy into the tired-ass trope that a woman needs a literal armed abduction across the scorched 1852 desert to realize her domestic setup in Iowa was aggressively mid. But let us look past the BookTok algorithm for a hot second and examine the bones of this premise. Ally North is giving us the classic forced-proximity dynamic, wrapped in petticoats and coated in frontier dust. 

### Frontier Rebellions And Other Capitalist Psyops

What fascinates me—and by fascinates, I mean induces a heavy sigh that rattles my coffee mug—is how female autonomy in historical fiction is so rarely granted without a man, or in this case a female outlaw playing the masculine archetype, dragging the protagonist into it. Abby Proctor cannot simply pack a bag and leave her oppressive husband because she possesses a spine. No. She requires a catalyst named Joey “JT London” Taylor to intercept her transit. Men—and fictional tough-girl proxies who perform traditional outlaw machismo—love nothing more than a damsel who needs to be unmoored by force. 

Yet, I see the glimmer of something sharper hiding beneath the sand dunes. The synopsis hints at a systemic conspiracy stretching all the way back to the secrets Abby left behind in Iowa. Good. Let us hope North uses this geography not just as a picturesque backdrop for sapphic longing, but as an indictment of mid-nineteenth-century patriarchal confinement. If this book pivots entirely into yearning while ignoring the structural violence that put Abby on that train in the first place, I am throwing my reading glasses across the room. We have read this exact brand of sapphic outlaw pulp before. Think Casey McQuiston meets a gritty, unwashed version of *True Grit*, engineered specifically to go viral on a Tuesday afternoon. 

### The Gallow’s Shadow And Compulsory Obedience

The ideological tension here hinges on a choice between a suffocating marriage and a literal hanging offense. Iconic. Nothing screams feminist liberation quite like choosing between domestic subjugation and an outlaw headed for the gallows. But let us be real for a second—North is playing the hits. The brooding captor with a heart of gold is a literary ancestor to every billionaire romance lead, just wearing a Stetson and smelling faintly of horse manure. 

And yet. The prose in the synopsis promises a rejection of the religious patriarchy that feels genuinely cathartic, assuming North does not cop out in the final chapters and force a tragic martyrdom. Give me messy women making feral choices in the desert. Do not give me sanitized historical revisionism where queer survival looks like a glossy photoshoot. If Abby chooses the gallows-bound outlaw, she better be doing it because she burns the whole corrupt system down with her. I am exhausted by stories where female freedom is just a smaller cage shared with a hotter warden. 

Let us see if North delivers a subversion of the frontier narrative or just another aestheticized romp for the algorithms. I have to go hand-bind a chapbook for a friend's upcoming release.
