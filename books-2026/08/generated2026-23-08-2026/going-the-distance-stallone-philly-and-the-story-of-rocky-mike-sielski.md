A love letter to Philadelphia, the city of brotherly love, and the birthplace of a cinematic icon – *Rocky*. Mike Sielski's *Going the Distance* promises to deliver the definitive account of the film's making, and I, for one, cannot wait to dive into the story behind one of the most beloved underdog stories in movie history. The fact that *Rocky* turns 50 this year only adds to the allure of this book. Yet, the synopsis remains vague about the specific details that Sielski will reveal – a clever marketing ploy, perhaps, but one that leaves me with more questions than answers.

—What exactly does Sielski mean by "fresh details and anecdotes" that will surprise even the film's biggest fans? Are we talking about behind-the-scenes drama, or will he shed new light on the creative process that brought *Rocky* to life? The vagueness is almost palpable, and I find myself wondering if Sielski is simply teasing us with promises of exclusivity, or if he truly has uncovered something groundbreaking.

But, I must admit, the prospect of reading about Sylvester Stallone's rise from an aimless kid to a Hollywood powerhouse is nothing short of captivating. The fact that Sielski has conducted extensive research and gathered insights from the likes of Irwin Winkler and Bill Conti only adds to the book's credibility. That’s certainly an opinion. One that I'm willing to bet on, given the author's intimate knowledge of Philadelphia's culture and history.

In many ways, *Going the Distance* reminds me of books like *The Kid Stays in the Picture*, Robert Evans's memoir about the making of *The Godfather*. Both share a common thread – the story of an underdog's rise to fame, and the unrelenting passion that drives them to succeed. And, just as Evans's book is more than just a behind-the-scenes account of a film's making, Sielski's *Going the Distance* promises to be a dramatic and fascinating ride in its own right.

Or so I hope. At this point, I'm willing to take a chance on Sielski's book, if only because the story of *Rocky* is one that continues to captivate audiences to this day. The fact that it's been 50 years since the film's release only adds to the nostalgia factor – a factor that Sielski is no doubt counting on to drive interest in his book.

But, what about the book's themes? How will Sielski explore the emotional connection between *Rocky* and the city of Philadelphia? Will he delve into the psychological aspects of Stallone's character, and the ways in which he drew inspiration from his own life experiences? These are questions that I hope *Going the Distance* will answer, and ones that will ultimately determine the book's success.
