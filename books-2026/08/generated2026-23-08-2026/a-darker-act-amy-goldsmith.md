What if the line between reality and performance is just an illusion waiting to be shattered? *A Darker Act* seems to be asking this very question, weaving a chilling romantic folk horror narrative inspired by the legend of the Wild Hunt. The official synopsis is sparse on details, but it's enough to pique my interest - and raise a few red flags.

### The Haunting of Hazel Fletcher

We're introduced to Hazel Fletcher, a senior who lands the lead in her high school theater group's production of a doomed romance inspired by local legends of a fearsome huntsman. The story takes a darker turn when Hazel reunites with her castmates on a camping trip, only to find themselves confronting the consequences of their previous performance. It's here that the synopsis gets frustratingly vague, but I'll take a stab at interpreting the themes.

It appears that *A Darker Act* is exploring the idea of performance as a form of invocation, where the lines between reality and fiction become blurred. The Edgemoor Players' production seems to have awakened something malevolent, which now threatens to destroy them. This raises questions about the power of storytelling and the responsibility that comes with it. Are the players merely actors, or have they become vessels for something darker?

But what really caught my attention was the mention of Roman Cavalero, the enigmatic leading man. His character seems to embody the idea of the "tortured artist," and I'm curious to see how his relationship with Hazel unfolds. Will it be a typical romance, or something more complex, perhaps even toxic? The synopsis doesn't give much away, but I'm willing to bet that Roman's character will be a pivotal part of the narrative.

And then there's the setting - a ruined church deep in the woods. It's a classic horror trope, but one that can be incredibly effective when done well. I'm hoping that Amy Goldsmith will use this setting to create a sense of claustrophobia and isolation, heightening the tension as the players realize they're being hunted.

I need a vacation after reading this, just thinking about the atmosphere Goldsmith is likely to create. But I digress.

### The Wild Hunt

The Wild Hunt is a fascinating mythological concept, and I'm excited to see how Goldsmith incorporates it into her narrative. The idea of a ghostly procession, led by a supernatural huntsman, is both captivating and terrifying. It's a great way to explore themes of mortality, the power of nature, and the dangers of meddling with forces beyond human control.

But, I have to ask, what's with the vagueness of the synopsis? Is Goldsmith trying to create a sense of mystery, or is this just a case of not wanting to give too much away? Either way, it's left me with more questions than answers.
