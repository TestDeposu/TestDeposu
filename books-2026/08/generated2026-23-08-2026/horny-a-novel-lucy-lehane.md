Look at this August heat beating against the windowpane while the cicadas outside create a ceaseless, maddening drone that makes coherent thought feel like wading through wet cement. Yet here I am, staring down Lucy Lehane’s upcoming *Horny: A Novel*, a title so aggressively juvenile it makes me want to launch my coffee mug across the living room rug. December 2026 cannot come fast enough simply so we can stop talking about whatever this is.

### The Architecture of the Absurd

We have a deceased protagonist sharing a physical vessel with a squatter poltergeist, now forced to room with a dimension-hopping, soul-reading unicorn who apparently looks like a supermodel. And people wonder why kids today have trouble grounding themselves in reality when this is the literary pabulum being pumped out for consumption. It feels like someone took a blender to a Terry Pratchett manuscript and a midnight romance paperback found behind a dumpster. Speaking of which, why did the third-grade science fair board have to be made out of foam core that warps the second you slap school glue on it? We spent forty dollars on baking soda and vinegar volcanoes last year, only for the dog to chew the papier-mâché base to pieces while I was downstairs trying to get five minutes of quiet, and now we are doing a friction-coefficient ramp using Hot Wheels tracks that keep sliding off the linoleum because nothing in this house ever stays put. 

Lehane leans entirely into the high-concept chaos. The synopsis reads like a mad-libs session engineered by a marketing algorithm chasing the exact demographic that made Gail Carriger and TJ Klune rich. 

### The Mechanics of Friction

Opposites-attract tropes usually rely on stubbornness and wit. Here, we get interdimensional virginity mixed with post-mortem career anxiety. 

### A Predictable Collision

The friction between a cynical dead girl and a pristine mythical creature lacks subtlety. Lehane will undoubtedly give us three hundred pages of slamming doors, accidental eye contact, and heavy-handed metaphors about learning to love your inner darkness. It smells like every mediocre urban fantasy published since 2010. My editor is breathing down my neck about word counts and deadlines, but the science fair project takes precedence and I still need to find where the hot glue gun went.
