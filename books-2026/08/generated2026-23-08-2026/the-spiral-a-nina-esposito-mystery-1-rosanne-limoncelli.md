**Author:** Rosanne Limoncelli  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-27 APL

The perpetual masquerade of trust – who to confide in, who to shun – a dance as old as humanity itself, and one that *The Spiral* promises to masterfully exploit. Set in the crime-ridden, graffiti-scarred New York City of 1979, this psychological thriller by Rosanne Limoncelli weaves a dark murder mystery that threatens to ensnare its protagonist, Nina Esposito, at every turn.

In this world of leering strangers and suspect intentions, Nina's struggle to discern predator from protector will doubtless be a potent metaphor for the universal struggle to find trust in a society that often seems hell-bent on devouring its most vulnerable members. And yet – can we truly trust Limoncelli to deliver a narrative that rises above the familiar tropes of the noir-thriller genre? The fact that *The Spiral* is being touted as perfect for fans of Tana French and Stieg Larsson only serves to heighten my skepticism – will this be a bold, innovative take on the genre, or merely a rehashing of the same old themes and plot devices?

One can't help but feel that the success of *The Spiral* will hinge on its ability to convincingly portray the inner workings of Nina's psyche – her motivations, her fears, her demons. Will Limoncelli prove equal to the task of crafting a protagonist whose psychological complexity and emotional depth can sustain the reader through the twists and turns of the narrative? Only time will tell, but for now, I remain decidedly... unimpressed.

Literally.

But what's truly got me scratching my head is the utter lack of specificity in the synopsis – we're told that *The Spiral* is a 'dark murder mystery', but what does that even mean in this context? Is it a police procedural? A serial killer thriller? A character-driven exploration of the human psyche? The vagueness is maddening, and I find myself wondering if the publishers are deliberately withholding information in order to pique our interest, or if they simply don't know how to market this thing.

And speaking of marketing, have you seen the latest attempts to rebrand the *Sweet Valley High* series as 'edgy' and 'subversive'? It's like they're trying to sell us a nostalgia-tinged dream of '90s teenage angst, minus the actual angst. I mean, who needs genuine emotional depth when you can just slap a trendy new cover on an old favorite and call it a day? Not me, that's for sure.

But I digress – back to *The Spiral*. Assuming that Limoncelli can deliver on her promises, I predict that this book will be a major player in the thriller genre come awards season. The setting, the premise, the protagonist – it all sounds like a recipe for success. But will it live up to the hype? Only time will tell.

And now, if you'll excuse me, I have to go deal with the washing machine, which has just flooded the laundry room. Because of course it has.
