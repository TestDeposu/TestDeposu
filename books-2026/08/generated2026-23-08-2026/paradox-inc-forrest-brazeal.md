Every single time humanity manages to invent something that fundamentally threatens the fabric of our continued existence—splitting the atom, engineering microscopic pathogens, or inventing the algorithm that convinced my uncle that birds are federal surveillance drones—some techbro in a fleece vest looks directly into a Ring doorbell camera and asks how we can monetize the fallout. Now, Forrest Brazeal is stepping up to skewer this exact brand of techno-optimistic psychosis in *Paradox Inc.*, an upcoming oral history that imagines what happens when Silicon Valley gets its grubby, venture-backed hands on a "consumer temporal interface." We are promised a world where the ultra-wealthy use personal time machines not to prevent historical atrocities or save dying civilizations, but to steal limited-edition 1990s snack chips and murder their past incarnations for social media clout, which honestly feels less like a satirical extrapolation and more like a leaked memo from a Tuesday morning product meeting at Meta.

### Snacks, Surveillance, and Sociopaths

Brazeal appears to be leaning heavily into the Douglas Adams playbook here, trading interstellar bureaucracy for the soul-crushing banality of modern startup culture—though I swear to you, reading about these corporate titans reminds me painfully of trying to explain to my neighbor’s golden retriever why it cannot eat the drywall, except the retriever has better hair and significantly more empathy than anyone funding a temporal bubble. 

Billionaires dragging the panopticon into the fourth dimension. 

Andy Weir compares the whole mess to a bittersweet ride, which gives me a tiny, fragile spark of hope that beneath all the sardonic jabs at venture capitalists, someone actually remembers to care about the people getting crushed under the wheels of progress. Because comedy about late capitalism usually makes me want to throw my coffee mug through the drywall, but maybe—just maybe—this one finds the human pulse beneath the algorithms.
