**Author:** Elizabeth Klehfoth  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

The air in this August heat is thick with the kind of humidity that clings to the spine like a bad decision—perfect for the kind of story that thrives on suffocating tension, the kind where the real estate mogul’s wife vanishing into thin air isn’t just a plot device but a *lifestyle choice* for the kind of people who believe tragedy is just another kind of performance art. *Every Pretty Thing* isn’t just another *Gossip Girl* knockoff—it’s the kind of book that smells like a summer blockbuster that’s been sitting too long in the sun, its pages curling with the promise of scandal but the weight of something far more insidious: the idea that privilege isn’t just a shield, but a *game*.

Charlie Fairchild, the orphaned heiress of a family name that’s been whispered about in the same breath as the *Jeffrey Epstein* scandal (because of course it has), is the kind of protagonist who exists in the same social stratum as the girls in *The Secret History*—only with the added layer of being a *child* in a world that treats her like a pawn. The A’s aren’t just a secret society; they’re the modern equivalent of the *Sorbonne’s infamous 1990s murder ring*, but with more TikTok aesthetics and less actual bloodshed. The Game they’re playing isn’t just a scavenger hunt—it’s a psychological experiment designed to see how far the elite will go before they realize they’ve already lost. And yet, the synopsis doesn’t even *try* to sell it as anything more than a gimmick. Where’s the bite? Where’s the *substance*? This isn’t *The Turn of the Screw*—it’s *The Bachelor* meets *American Psycho*, and that’s not a compliment.

The real tragedy here isn’t that Grace Fairchild disappeared—it’s that her daughter is being groomed to replace her. Charlie isn’t just a victim; she’s a *product*, and the A’s are the brand that’s going to sell her to the highest bidder. The synopsis doesn’t even *acknowledge* the irony that the same people who would gaslight a child into believing her mother’s disappearance was her own fault are now treating her like a *glamour girl* in a psychological thriller. But then again, irony is the last thing *Entertainment Weekly* cares about when they’re hyping up another *it-girl* tragedy.

The thematic weight here is all surface—family secrets, elite betrayal, the kind of drama that makes *Keeping Up with the Kardashians* look like a documentary. But where’s the *meat*? Where’s the *depth*? The synopsis doesn’t even attempt to grapple with the fact that Charlie’s trauma isn’t just a plot point—it’s the *fuel* for the entire story. And yet, it’s treated like a side effect, something to be *overcome* rather than *examined*. This isn’t *The Girl on the Train*—it’s *The Real Housewives of New England*, and that’s not a genre I’m dying to revisit.

The A’s aren’t just a secret society—they’re a *metaphor*, and a lazy one at that. They’re the kind of group that would stage a murder mystery at their annual fundraiser, where the "victim" is always someone who *deserved it*. But the synopsis doesn’t even *try* to make it more than that. It’s the literary equivalent of a *Chanel* perfume ad—beautiful, empty, and designed to make you feel like you’re missing something you can’t quite name.

And yet, despite all this, I can’t help but wonder if there’s a kernel of something here. The way the past and present collide isn’t just a trope—it’s a *promise*. But the synopsis doesn’t deliver. It doesn’t *commit*. It’s the literary equivalent of a *Netflix* series that’s been greenlit but hasn’t even started filming yet. And that’s a shame, because if this were written by someone with the *gravitas* of *Gillian Flynn* or the *psychological precision* of *Tana French*, it could have been something truly terrifying.

But no. This is just another book that’s going to get hyped up as *the next big thing* before it even hits the shelves. And that’s not a surprise—it’s a *trend*. The elite, the secrets, the games—it’s all been done before, and done better. The only thing missing here is the *edge*. The only thing missing is the *risk*.

And so, I find myself sighing as I pull up the contract for the film adaptation—because of course there’s going to be one. The question isn’t whether this will be made into a movie; it’s whether it’ll be as hollow as the book itself. #BookTok #EliteDrama #SummerReading
