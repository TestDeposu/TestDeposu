**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Look, I watch people beat each other unconscious for a living inside a cage. I understand striking angles, defensive posture, and how a fighter reacts when they take a brutal hit to the liver. So forgive me if I roll my eyes when a fantasy synopsis throws five dudes at one protagonist and expects me to buy the physical dynamics. Five-on-one? In a clinch, numbers usually dictate the casualty count, myth or no myth. Yet here we are, looking at *Red* by Izzy Barry—or is it Kiera McKenna, given the promotional whiplash at the top of the press release? Branding confusion aside, let us talk about the tactical realities of taking on a crew of ancient, winged apex predators on the Isle of Skye. 

### Footwork, Fang, and Five-Against-One Brawls

Isobel Rhodes walks into a dusty archive looking for a paycheck and stumbles into a reverse harem fever dream featuring the Sìol Dòmhnaill brothers. And honestly? The logistical nightmare of five guys trying to crowd around one human woman while defending ancient relics stresses me out more than a botched weight cut. How does the cage control work here? Who maintains the perimeter while everyone is busy being "fierce and unyielding"? Barry—or McKenna, whoever is actually steering this hype train—promises sizzling romantic tension and mythic stakes. But give me some dirty striking. Give me actual friction in the grappling exchanges, not just endless lingering over chiseled jaws and draconic heat. If you are going to write combatants who can shift into mythical fire-breathing monsters, they better throw hands with the same kind of explosive violence you see in a championship title fight. Otherwise, the stakes are just soft-focus melodrama disguised as a paranormal turf war.

### The Problem with Prophesized Punch-Ducking

Every combat sports analyst knows that relying purely on destiny gets you knocked out cold in the first round. You need actual technique, grit, and situational awareness. Yet the synopsis leans hard into the oldest trope in the book: the reluctant heroine who has zero interest in mates until she is suddenly forced to choose between her old life and a blazing inferno of supernatural destiny. Groundbreaking. We have seen this exact style of matchmaking in books like *A Court of Thorns and Roses* or whatever grimdark romantasy Sarah J. Maas cooked up this week. But let us be real. If an ancient harp starts playing and five dragon shifters trap you on a mist-drenched Scottish island, your first instinct should be a double-leg takedown or an improvised weapon, not falling backward into their collective arms. 

Yet I am still pre-ordering. Why? Because the genre is starved for high-stakes aggression, and if Barry delivers even half the atmospheric punch she’s selling, I will happily consume every single page before the sequel drops. I just need the physical confrontations to hit as hard as the emotional ones. 

I need to go block three people on Instagram.
