**Author:** Everina Maxwell  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-12-01 APL

What's so compelling about *Call Me Traitor*, the latest offering from Everina Maxwell, the same author who brought us *Winter's Orbit*, a romance that's been name-dropped by none other than *The New York Times*? As a pop-culture connoisseur on the night shift, I'm intrigued by the prospect of a slowburning sapphic adventure, particularly one rooted in dark academia and forbidden love.

—And let's get straight to the point: the setup is tantalizing. We're presented with five individuals bearing exile brands on their hands, individuals who by all logical reasoning should have met their maker, yet they're still kicking. It's the kind of mysterious premise that sets my analytical gears in motion. But then, we're introduced to En, a weapon masquerading as a soldier, trained to withstand excruciating pain, and bound by loyalty to her archmagi commander. Tamol, on the other hand, is the soldier En begrudgingly has to pair up with to achieve their mission objective.

En is a character with depth, at least according to the synopsis. Her backstory and motivations are clearly alluded to, painting a picture of a stoic assassin driven by duty and obligation. Then there's Tamol, whose 'flightiness' and 'cowardice' En so readily dismisses. It's as if Maxwell has crafted a classic love-to-hate relationship, the same kind that captivated readers in *Annie on My Mind* or *The Lady's Guide to Petticoats and Piracy*. But let's not kid ourselves – this is *Call Me Traitor*, and it's aiming for something far more nuanced than surface-level angst.

What really gets my pulse racing, though, is the thematic depth Maxwell seems to be exploring. The tension between duty and desire, the societal expectations placed upon women, the dark underbelly of academia — it's a sizzling cocktail of elements that will undoubtedly leave readers invested in the story. I'm reminded of authors like Patricia A. McKillip, who've woven similar intricate narratives that subvert and challenge the status quo.

Yet we're also faced with an enigmatic empire that seems all too willing to crush anyone who dares to challenge its authority. It's a classic case of oppression vs. revolution, the kind of binary setup that leaves me pining for the days of *The Handmaid's Tale*. And then, of course, there's the monstrous entity lurking in the shadows of the Far Peninsula, an antagonist as unpredictable as the plot. At this point, I'm salivating at the mere prospect of being immersed in such a rich, atmospheric world.

But the question remains: will Maxwell successfully navigate this complex web of characters and storylines without losing readers along the way? Only time will tell. One thing's for certain, though, *Call Me Traitor* looks like it's going to be a wild ride — and I couldn't be more thrilled.
