**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-09-29 APL

They pay me seventy-five dollars an hour to slash adjectives and bleed out clichés, yet I am sitting here reading the jacket copy for Kate Golden's *Cursed City* and wondering if the marketing department simply threw a dart at a board of urban fantasy buzzwords. "Stenciled edges." Right. Because artisanal paper goods magically fix structural pacing issues. But let us look past the decorative trade paperback gimmick and examine the text itself—or rather, the recycled tropes masquerading as a sophomore novel.

### The Anatomy of a Second-Semester Slump

We are expected to care about Viv Abbot returning to Harker Academy for Deviant Defense. Harker—what an utterly uninspired name for a supernatural boarding school. It sounds like a mid-tier spin-off of *The Cruel Prince* crossed with a rejected script from the *Shadowhunters* writers' room. Yet here we are. Viv must rescue her reformed Brood demon boyfriend, Reid, by teaming up with his violent brother, Deacon. 

Groundbreaking. Truly. 

And by groundbreaking, I mean we have officially entered the era of the mandatory morally gray sibling swap. The synopsis leans hard into the "embrace her darkness" routine. Why do heroines in contemporary romantasy always have to inherit a latent dark side just because they love a monster? It is lazy shorthand for actual character development. Instead of building a complex psychological profile, the author gives us a bounty on Viv's head and a ticking clock. 

### The Aesthetics of Searing Grift

Let us discuss the prose promises hidden in this blurb. The text tells us that "temptation cuts deeper than any blade." Does it? Because that sentence cut me right across my patience barrier. And what about this "White Stag" guiding them through Astera's seedy underbelly? Sounds like a literal fauna-themed Uber driver for edgy teens. The synopsis is positively dripping with vague threats—enemies closing in from all sides, a city that thrives on lies, a betrayal that strikes at the heart. It is a masterclass in saying absolutely nothing while hitting every single beat of the modern supernatural romance checklist. 

Do not get me wrong. This book will sell three hundred thousand copies before lunch on release day. Readers eat up this brand of academy-setting melodrama with a spoon, especially when the publisher dangles sprayed edges like keys in front of a toddler. But as an editor staring down the barrel of another manuscript reliant on secret bounties and familial angst, I am exhausted. Golden is leaning heavily on the momentum of book one, treating the actual plot mechanics of *Cursed City* as an afterthought behind the aesthetic packaging. 

If the interior prose matches the derivative safety of this synopsis, we are in for a long semester. I predict a mid-book training montage, at least three breathless hallway confrontations with Deacon, and a third-act revelation that the White Stag cannot be trusted. Colour me shocked. 

Yet, as I stare at my rent bill and calculate how many split infinitives I must fix today just to afford groceries, I realize something profound about the industry. We are all trapped in the machine. Producing content for a readership that demands the exact same plot recycled with new foil stamping. 

I am officially entering my villain era.
