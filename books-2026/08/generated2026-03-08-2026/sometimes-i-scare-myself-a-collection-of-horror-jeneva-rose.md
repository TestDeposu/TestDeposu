# Sometimes I Scare Myself: A Collection of Horror

**Author:** Jeneva Rose  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-09-08 APL

**What to Expect from *Sometimes I Scare Myself*: Jeneva Rose’s New Horror Collection Drops in September 2026**

Jeneva Rose’s name is synonymous with the kind of horror that lingers long after the last page. A *New York Times* bestselling author whose work has been praised for its psychological depth, eerie atmosphere, and relentless dread, Rose has spent years perfecting the art of making readers question their own sanity. Now, with *Sometimes I Scare Myself: A Collection of Horror*—her first standalone anthology—she delivers three standalone tales that promise to unsettle, intrigue, and haunt in equal measure. Set for release on **September 8, 2026**, this collection isn’t just a return to form; it’s an evolution, a chance for Rose to stretch her storytelling muscles across three distinct yet thematically linked narratives.

If you’ve devoured Rose’s previous works—like *The Last Time I Lied* or *The Only Good Indians*—you’ll recognize her signature blend of slow-burn tension and explosive horror. But *Sometimes I Scare Myself* isn’t just a rehash of her signature style; it’s a fresh exploration of the ways fear can manifest in the mundane. Whether it’s a dinner party gone wrong, a house that whispers secrets, or a cursed roadside shop that traps its victims in paranoia, Rose takes familiar settings and twists them into something far more sinister.

### **Three Stories, Three Kinds of Terror**

The collection opens with *Never Kill the Medium*, a tale that begins with the kind of setup any horror fan would recognize: a group of friends, a birthday dinner, and a séance gone awry. But where most stories would lean into the supernatural, Rose subverts expectations, weaving in themes of guilt, denial, and the terrifying reality that some doors—once opened—cannot be closed. The medium’s death isn’t just a plot device; it’s a catalyst, a moment where the line between the living and the dead blurs in ways that will leave readers questioning whether the horror is real or if they’ve simply lost their grip on reality.

Next is *The Nightmare House*, a slow-burn descent into domestic dread. The Dalton family moves into what seems like the perfect home—until the house itself begins to resist. Newly postpartum and emotionally vulnerable, Kristin Dalton becomes the first to hear the whispers, the creaks, the unnatural stillness of a home that has claimed every previous resident. Rose excels at making the ordinary terrifying, and here, the horror isn’t just in the supernatural but in the psychological unraveling of a woman already fragile. The question isn’t just *what’s in the house*, but *what’s wrong with her*—and the answer is far more unsettling.

The collection closes with *All Curses Are Final*, a high-stakes, paranoia-driven thriller that begins with a seemingly harmless roadside stop. A hand-painted warning, a mysterious item, and a storm that traps a group of friends in a remote cabin—this is the setup for a story that will have readers flipping back to see if they missed any clues. As bodies begin to pile up and trust erodes, Rose masterfully turns the cabin into a pressure cooker of suspicion, where no one is safe and no one can be trusted. The curse isn’t just on the items; it’s on the people who bought them, and the realization comes too late.

### **Why This Collection Matters**

Rose has always been a master of atmospheric horror, but *Sometimes I Scare Myself* feels like a deliberate step into more experimental territory. While her novels often rely on deep character studies and layered narratives, these three stories are tighter, more self-contained, and—dare we say—more *accessible* in their immediate impact. That doesn’t mean they’re any less terrifying; if anything, their brevity makes their chills more potent.

For fans of Rose, this collection is a must-read. For newcomers, it’s the perfect introduction to her work—three distinct flavors of horror, each with its own flavor of dread. Whether you’re drawn to the psychological horror of *Never Kill the Medium*, the cosmic terror of *The Nightmare House*, or the paranoid descent of *All Curses Are Final*, there’s something here for every kind of horror lover.

Mark your calendars for **September 8, 2026**. By then, Jeneva Rose will have given us another reason to stay up late, turn off the lights, and wonder—just for a moment—if we’re alone in our fear.
