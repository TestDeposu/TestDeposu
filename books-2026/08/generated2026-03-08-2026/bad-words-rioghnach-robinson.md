# Bad Words

**Author:** Rioghnach Robinson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-06 APL

**What to Expect from *Bad Words* by Ríoghnach Robinson: A Literary Feud That Burns Bright**

When a book arrives with the kind of buzz that makes critics and readers alike lean in—*Bad Words*, Ríoghnach Robinson’s debut novel, is already shaping up to be one of the most talked-about releases of 2026. Part razor-sharp romance, part searing exploration of art, ambition, and the cost of being seen, this novel doesn’t just promise a story—it promises a reckoning. And if the early praise is any indication, it’s a reckoning we won’t soon forget.

At its heart, *Bad Words* is a will-they-won’t-they romance with teeth. It follows Parker Navarro, a writer whose career was once destined for greatness—until a single, devastating review by literary critic Selina Chan reduced his debut to the flop of the decade. Four years later, both are at a crossroads: Parker, desperate to reclaim his voice, and Selina, torn between professional duty and the undeniable pull of the man who once haunted her. When their paths collide again—this time in a viral feud that ignites the literary world—what begins as a public battle soon fractures into something far more dangerous: a quiet, aching understanding of the spaces between words.

What makes *Bad Words* so compelling isn’t just the tension of their feud, but the way Robinson dissects the very act of creation. This isn’t a story about love or hate—it’s about the messy, beautiful, and often brutal intersections of truth, art, and vulnerability. Parker and Selina are both artists, both people who have staked their identities on being seen, and their clash forces them to confront the question: *Can you separate the work from the person?* The answer, as Robinson reveals, is never as simple as it seems.

The early buzz is electric. Emily Henry, whose own romances have redefined the genre, calls it “a book that could only ever have been written by Ríoghnach Robinson,” praising its “clever voice and incisive social commentary” that elevate it beyond a typical romance. Carley Fortune, another *New York Times* bestseller, goes further, calling it “genre-redefining” and “easily one of my favourites of the year, if not the decade.” And Mary H.K. Choi, whose work is known for its sharp wit and emotional depth, describes it as “full of blistering barbs, incisive takedowns, and searing gazes”—a perfect encapsulation of the novel’s tone.

What’s most exciting about *Bad Words* is how Robinson blends the intellectual rigor of literary fiction with the emotional punch of contemporary romance. This isn’t a story about passive observers—it’s about two people who have spent their lives performing for the world, only to realize that the most dangerous truths are the ones they’ve never spoken aloud. The result is a narrative that’s as intellectually stimulating as it is deeply human, a rare feat in a genre often dismissed as frivolous.

If you’ve ever wondered what happens when two brilliant, stubborn artists collide—when the things they say to each other in public are nothing compared to what they’re too afraid to admit in private—*Bad Words* is your answer. It’s a novel that doesn’t just entertain; it challenges, provokes, and lingers long after the last page. And with Robinson’s voice already established as one of the most distinctive in contemporary fiction, this debut is one to watch closely.

Mark your calendars for October 6, 2026—because *Bad Words* isn’t just a book. It’s an event.
