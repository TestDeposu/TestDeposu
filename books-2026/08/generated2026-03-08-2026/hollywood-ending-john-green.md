# Hollywood, Ending

**Author:** John Green  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-09-22 APL

**John Green’s *Hollywood, Ending*: A Love Story for the Age of Attention**

John Green’s return to fiction is one of the most eagerly awaited literary events of the year—and *Hollywood, Ending*, his first novel since *The Anthropocene Reviewed*, promises to be as sharp, tender, and culturally resonant as anything he’s written before. Set against the glittering, cutthroat world of Hollywood, this dual-narrative love story explores the tension between public spectacle and private longing, all while dissecting the modern obsession with attention. If you’ve ever wondered what it’s like to be young, ambitious, and adrift in a world that demands you be *seen*—or what it takes to find love in a place where trust is a luxury—this is the book for you.

At its heart, *Hollywood, Ending* follows Kai Laramie and Juniper Castillo, two rising stars cast in the buzzed-about biopic *Andy Warhol Never Gets Old*. For them, the film isn’t just a career launchpad; it’s a crucible. As their lives collide on and off screen—amid the chaos of a movie blowing up overnight—they’re forced to navigate the fragile balance between authenticity and performance, between the person they are and the person they’re expected to be. Green, who has long been a master of blending humor, heart, and social commentary, weaves these dual perspectives with his signature wit, creating a story that’s as much about the industry that consumes us as it is about the people who dare to resist it.

What makes this novel particularly compelling is its timely examination of the attention economy. Warhol’s own life—his fascination with fame, his blurring of art and celebrity—serves as a mirror to the modern experience, where every like, every share, every viral moment can feel like a transaction. Green, who has spent years reflecting on fame, privacy, and the search for meaning (*Looking for Alaska*, *The Fault in Our Stars*, *Turtles All the Way Down*), brings that same intellectual depth to this new project. But where his earlier works often focused on the internal struggles of adolescence, *Hollywood, Ending* expands that lens to the external pressures of a world that demands constant visibility. It’s a story about the cost of being seen—and the courage it takes to love someone when the world is watching.

For fans of Green’s work, this novel offers a fresh but familiar voice. If you’ve loved his dual-narrative storytelling (*Paper Towns*), his sharp social observations (*The Anthropocene Reviewed*), or his ability to make the mundane feel profound (*Let It Snow*), you’ll find much to cherish here. Yet *Hollywood, Ending* also marks a bold new direction, venturing into the world of Hollywood itself—a setting Green has only glimpsed before, most notably in *The Art of Starving*, his 2015 memoir about his time as a struggling writer in Los Angeles. This time, though, he’s crafting a full-fledged fictional exploration of the industry’s allure and its dangers, all while keeping the emotional core of his storytelling intact.

The dual POV structure ensures that no character feels one-dimensional. Kai and Juniper’s relationship unfolds with the same nuance Green brings to his most intimate portrayals of young love, but here, their bond is tested by the very forces that shape their world: the studio executives, the paparazzi, the algorithmic demands of modern fame. It’s a story about the audacity to love in a world that rewards performativity over authenticity, and about the quiet rebellions that make such love possible.

With *Hollywood, Ending*, Green doesn’t just return to fiction—he redefines it. This isn’t just a love story; it’s a meditation on the price of being seen, the value of privacy, and the rare, beautiful moments when two people find a way to connect despite everything. If his past work has taught us anything, it’s that Green’s fiction doesn’t just entertain—it challenges, it comforts, and it lingers long after the last page. *Hollywood, Ending* will be no exception.

Mark your calendars for September 22, 2026—this is one return you won’t want to miss.
