**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

What is it about humanity that we find so mesmerizing, even in the face of utter alienation? In *Bound by Fate*, South Korean author Dolki Min's debut novel, we're invited to explore the complexities of human existence through the disorienting lens of an extraterrestrial. This enigmatic novel promises to subvert our expectations, and based on the tantalizing synopsis, I'm convinced it will be a game-changer.

Or is it just another variation on the "stranded alien" trope? —A la *The Left Hand of Darkness*, this iteration is notable for pairing the sci-fi staple with a shapeshifting alien, who adopts various identities to lure in unsuspecting prey. And what a deliciously dark concept it is. The tension is palpable as our cephalopod-like protagonist stalks their quarry through the city, armed with an unsettling array of hunting tools, including a dating app—because, of course. Yet, the true horror lies not in the alien's monstrous nature, but in the mirror it holds up to our own species' predilections for consumption.

But what of the human condition? How does Min, in conjunction with translator Victoria Caudle, aim to illuminate our existential plight through this unsettling narrative? —I'm reminded of the visceral power of Kazuo Ishiguro's *Never Let Me Go*, where the blurred lines between life and death serve as a cautionary tale about the commodification of human experience. Similarly, *Breaking Bad*'s Walter White, though morally reprehensible, forces us to confront the dark underbelly of American society. Will Dolki Min's novel join this pantheon of thought-provoking works, or falter in its attempt to dissect our peculiarities? Only time (and, hopefully, a competent editor) will tell.

What if we're being sold a bill of goods, and *Bound by Fate* is merely a glossy facade, devoid of substance? —Perhaps the comparisons to *Squid Game* and *Under the Skin* are nothing more than a cynical marketing ploy. I, for one, am willing to take the bait. There's something to be said for a novel that can juggle multiple tonalities—shocking, horrifying, hilarious, intimate—and still manage to leave a lasting impression. As the venerable critic, James Wood, once astutely observed, "A good novel is a novel that you can no longer imagine living without."

What will the aftermath be like, after we've devoured this peculiar feast? Will we emerge scarred, changed, or simply sated? —The answer, much like the novel itself, remains a tantalizing mystery. For now, I'm hooked. Give me a glimpse of humanity, warts and all, and I'll be there with bells on. Bring on the line drawings, too—the more unsettling the visuals, the better.

The glare on my monitor is starting to give me a headache, but I'm too captivated to look away. As the words blur on the screen, I'm left to ponder the abyss that separates us from our alien "other." What do we really know about the Other, anyway?
