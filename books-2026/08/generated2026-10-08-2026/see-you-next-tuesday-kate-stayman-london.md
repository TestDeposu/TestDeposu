**Author:** Kate Stayman-London  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-09-08 APL

Darling, forgive me if I sound slightly jaded, but my trust fund didn’t pay for three degrees in comparative literature just so I could pretend contemporary political romance possesses the existential gravity of a Camus essay. Yet, here we are. Kate Stayman-London is attempting to weaponize *The West Wing* nostalgia for our current, deeply exhausting historical moment with *See You Next Tuesday*. But is this actually a critique of institutional machinery, or merely bourgeois escapism wrapped in bipartisan bunting? 

### The Dialectic of the Copy-Room

Consider the archetypes. Anya Katz—Type A, morality thicker than my grandmother's Swiss bank accounts—versus Gabe Hunt, the golden boy whose ethical compass clearly points toward whatever room has the best lighting. Classic Hegelian friction. Thesis meets antithesis. But does the synopsis give us any real ideological conflict? No. They agree on their politics. How dreadfully sterile. Where is the actual danger? Where is the intellectual blood sport? Instead, we get a decade of greasy takeout and late-night phone banks. A decade! One wonders if they ever pause to consider the ontological absurdity of American democracy, or if they are simply too busy engaging in what I can only describe as high-stakes professional foreplay. 

And yet, the structural ambition here is rather fascinating. Spanning ten years of elections—from glamorous celebrity fundraisers to devastating electoral losses—the book attempts to map the erosion of the human soul against the timeline of a campaign. Politics changes you. But does it destroy you? Or does it merely offer a shiny, highly photogenic excuse to avoid looking inward? I suspect Gabe and Anya use the adrenaline of public service as a prophylactic against genuine intimacy. How deliciously modern. 

### The Cult of the Sizzling Banter

We must talk about the dialogue. The blurbs promise "breakneck banter." One shudders. Nothing ages faster than attempts at rapid-fire, Aaron Sorkin-esque repartee written in the 2020s. Emily Henry and Ali Hazelwood have already thoroughly monetized this exact brand of academic and professional bickering, pushing the trope into the literary stratosphere. Stayman-London must do something truly feral with these pages to justify another enemies-to-lovers arc across ten calendar years. If they simply bicker about poll numbers while falling into each other's arms every time a primary goes south, I shall be writing a very strongly worded letter to my portfolio manager. 

Ultimately, this text stands as a litmus test for our collective exhaustion. Do we read political romance to process our dread, or to numb it? I am placing my bets on numbness. But packaged with enough gloss to distract us from the impending collapse of late-stage capitalism. 

Ouch. I just bit my own cheek.
