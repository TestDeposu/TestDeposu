**Author:** Aiden Thomas  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-09-08 APL

The forthcoming *Espíritu*, the second installment in Aiden Thomas's *Cemetery Boys* series, promises to be a haunting, genre-bending exploration of the human (and spectral) experience. The official synopsis, replete with tantalizing hints and enigmatic allusions, has left this ardent literary enthusiast with more questions than answers, and I, for one, can barely contain my excitement.

Aided by the eerie, dreamlike atmosphere conjured by Thomas's writing, we're introduced to Julian—a charismatic, ghostly protagonist whose recent transformation has afforded him an unsettling new perspective on the world. It's this singular gift that has Julian oscillating between fascination and terror, for it's not just the unseen entities he's begun to perceive that trouble him, but also the faint, flickering "dark spots" that seem to affix themselves to those around him. One can't help but wonder: what does it mean to be alive, to be seen, and to be perceived in this liminal realm?

The character of Angel, a nonbinary bruje with an unsettling capacity for "healing the living and releasing the dead," serves as a fascinating counterpoint to Julian's own struggles. As an expert in the macabre traditions of the brujo world, Angel's presence injects an air of unease, their dark, troubled past an unsettling reminder that even those with the power to mend and release can be consumed by an insatiable hunger for control. But can we truly trust a being with such unfathomable power, or will Julian's desire for validation lead him down a path from which there's no return?

As an aficionado of dark, atmospheric fiction, I am drawn to Thomas's mastery of the brujo world, a realm that, in the hands of a lesser writer, might devolve into saccharine, feel-good fantasy. And yet, I am equally intrigued by the author's decision to introduce Angel, a figure whose complexity threatens to upend the delicate balance between Julian's own experiences and the brujo community. It's this fraught, often toxic interplay between characters that has piqued my interest, and I'd wager Thomas's expert handling of this delicate material will yield a narrative that's both visceral and affecting.

I am reminded, rather, of the atmospheric, mystical world-building in Victor LaValle's *The Changeling*, where myth and reality blur in a dazzling display of literary pyrotechnics. Or the masterful, genre-subverting exploration of Latinx identity in Carmen Maria Machado's *Her Body and Other Parties*. Thomas's *Espíritu*, with its intricate dance between human and ghostly realms, promises to push the boundaries of the paranormal romance further, exploring the fraught, often fraught spaces between love, identity, and mortality.

And what of Julian's precarious fate, torn between an existence as a ghostly conduit for the spirits and his all-too-human desire for connection with Yadriel, his brujx boyfriend? Can we truly know, or will the shadows only continue to deepen, drawing Julian inexorably toward a destiny from which there's no return?

I am eager to discover the truth, to unravel the enigmatic threads that bind this narrative together.
