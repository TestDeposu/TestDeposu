**Author:** Elena Armas  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-11-17 APL

Listen, I grade thirty-five persuasive essays a day on why cell phones shouldn't be allowed in the cafeteria, so when I look at a piece of writing, I smell sincerity. Or the lack of it. Elena Armas is stepping out of her cozy contemporary lane and straight into the moody, stenciled-edge halls of *Alma Vampires*. And honestly? As someone who spends her days convincing thirteen-year-olds that reading is actually cooler than scrolling on TikTok, I am vibrating with pure, unadulterated caffeine and excitement. 

### From Ivy League Desks to Immortal Danger

Let us talk about the absolute whiplash of Valle Cepeda's syllabus. One day you are stressing over a macroeconomics midterm, and the next you own a multi-million-dollar fashion empire because of a mother you never even knew existed. Talk about a glow-up with a high body count. And Hadrian Cruz? The brooding, calculating vampire who is supposedly just playing a corporate game until a hundred-year-old blood debt comes due? Sign me up for this extra credit assignment immediately. 

### The Adolescent Symphony of Power and Trust

Why does this premise make teachers like me feel so wildly inspired? Because it captures the exact hormonal, volatile essence of being a teenager magnified by a thousand. Think about how high schoolers feel when their entire identity shifts in a single Tuesday homeroom period. Val goes back to Icarus University feeling manipulated by everyone, surrounded by shifting power dynamics—a classic coming-of-age metaphor wrapped nicely in fangs and designer clothes. She wants autonomy. She wants answers. But she has to rely on a literal monster just to survive her own inheritance. 

And let us be real for a second. The synopsis is delightfully vague on the actual mechanics of this blood debt. Is it a cursed family heirloom? A supernatural corporate merger? Who knows! But we are eating it up anyway. 

If this book channels even half the delicious, angsty tension of Tracy Wolff’s *Crave* or the sharp academic vibes of Ninth House, it is going to absolutely dominate the reading charts this winter. Armas knows how to write chemistry that makes your face hot—trust me, I have confiscated enough paperbacks under desks to recognize elite romantic tension when I read it. 

I just got a brain freeze.
