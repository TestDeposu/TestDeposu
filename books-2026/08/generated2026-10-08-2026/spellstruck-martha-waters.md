Rushing to get my thoughts down on *Spellstruck* before my subway stop arrives — and I'm already hooked on the premise. A historical romance with a dash of magic, set in an alternate 1920s London, where the use of magic has been outlawed? It's like Martha Waters is speaking my language. But, I have to say, the synopsis feels a bit... vague. I mean, what exactly are these "accidents" that are striking the magical speakeasies? And how does Vesper Holloway's family club fit into the larger picture? Yet, despite the lack of detail, I'm intrigued by the idea of a magical underworld operating in secret.

As I ponder the themes of *Spellstruck*, I'm reminded of authors like Naomi Novik and Susanna Clarke, who have mastered the art of blending fantasy and historical fiction. The idea of a hidden world of magic, existing just beneath the surface of everyday life, is a compelling one — and I'm curious to see how Waters explores this concept. And, of course, the romance between Vesper and Maxwell Ashbridge is a major draw. I love a good enemies-to-lovers story, and the fact that Max has a personal vendetta against the magical elite adds an extra layer of tension to their relationship. But, what really gets me is the chemistry between them — it's like the sparks are flying, even in the synopsis.

### The Magic of Subversion
Now, I know some readers might be thinking, "Oh, great, another fantasy romance with a strong female lead and a brooding love interest." But, I think Waters is onto something more nuanced here. The fact that Vesper's family club is operating outside the law, and that Max is tasked with enforcing the ban on magic, raises interesting questions about power and rebellion. It's like Waters is asking us to consider the consequences of suppressing certain aspects of human nature — and whether the benefits of conformity are worth the cost. That’s certainly an opinion. And, as someone who devours thrillers like they're going out of style, I appreciate the potential for danger and suspense that this setup provides.

As I see it, *Spellstruck* has the potential to be a real game-changer in the fantasy romance genre. With its unique blend of history, magic, and romance, it's like Waters is throwing down the gauntlet — challenging readers to rethink their assumptions about the genre. And, let's be real, the comparison to *USA TODAY* bestselling author Emily Krempholtz is no small thing. It's like the literary world is already buzzing with anticipation. But, will *Spellstruck* live up to the hype? Only time will tell. For now, I'm just excited to see where Waters takes us on this wild ride.

### A Dash of Magic
One thing that's got me curious is the role of magic in the story. Is it just a plot device, or does it serve a deeper purpose? I mean, what does it say about Vesper and Max that they're drawn to each other, despite being on opposite sides of the law? And, how does the magical underworld reflect the societal tensions of the time? These are the kinds of questions that keep me up at night, wondering about the possibilities. Yet, as much as I want to dive in and start analyzing the themes and characters, I'm also aware that I'm working with limited information. The synopsis is just a tease, a hint of what's to come.

In the end, it's all about the execution. Can Waters deliver on the promise of *Spellstruck*, or will it fall flat? I'm willing to take the risk, because the potential payoff is just too great. And, who knows, maybe I'll even find myself swept up in the magic of it all.
