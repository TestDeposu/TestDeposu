**Author:** Katie Cotugno  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Look, I watch people beat each other unconscious for a living. I understand the sweet science of trading leather, absorbing punishment, and figuring out who actually has a chin when the championship rounds start. So when I look at a synopsis for a psychological thriller, I am looking for one thing: a fighter who can take a punch and keep walking forward. 

Does *Daisy* by Katie Cotugno look like it has a granite jaw? Or is it just another glass-jawed hype job? Let us break down the tape.

### The Tale of the Tape and the Body Blows

Cotugno wants us to believe that this modern Long Island socialite—our girl Daisy Buchanan—is dodging psychological body shots two summers after Gatsby caught a bullet. But what is the actual striking game here? The synopsis throws a wild, telegraphed right hook: Jordan Baker turns up dead in the pool. Classic. 

Yet, as any seasoned cornerman will tell you, you cannot win a fight on a single flash knockdown. The blurb leans heavily on vague paranoia. Is someone watching her? Or is it just the gin? Look, I am no stranger to a heavy night out after a brutal sparring session, but leaning on the "am I hallucinating or is this a conspiracy" trope is the literary equivalent of throwing naked haymakers. It is sloppy footwork. 

And yet, I respect the stamina. The setup positions Daisy as a counter-puncher caught in a clinch she did not ask for. People keep dropping dead around her. That is not just bad luck; that is a terrible defense. If your inner circle has a mortality rate higher than a bare-knuckle fighting circuit, you need to change your camp immediately. 

### The Championship Rounds

The thematic angle here—if we can pierce the guard of all those glossy East Egg descriptors—is survival after the bell rings. Cotugno is attempting to drag Fitzgerald's romanticized golden girl out of her plush corner and throw her into a gritty street fight. But will the prose have any actual power behind it, or are we just shadowboxing with ghosts? 

If you want a gritty, high-stakes psychological beatdown done right, look at how Gillian Flynn or Paula Hawkins operate. They do not just hint at trouble; they put their characters in a chokehold from page one. Cotugno needs to deliver real, bone-crunching suspense here, or this whole enterprise is going to get knocked out in the first round by readers who demand actual narrative gravity instead of just name-dropping iconic literature like a casual fan wearing a vintage boxing tee they bought at a thrift store. 

My official prediction? The pacing will be propulsive, exactly as promised—fighters love a fast starter—but the real test is whether Daisy has the stamina to go the distance once the police start landing clean counters. 

Rent in this city just went up another two hundred bucks for a studio that smells faintly of old gym socks, and yet somehow people expect me to give up my lifestyle to work a desk job answering emails about Q3 metrics. Not happening. I would rather take a left hook from heavyweight contender than stare at a spreadsheet all morning.
