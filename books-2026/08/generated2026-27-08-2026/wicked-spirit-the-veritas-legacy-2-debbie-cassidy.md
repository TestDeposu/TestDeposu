The syllabus at Nightsbridge Academy remains entirely soaked in the same tired lethality we have watched publisher marketing departments recycle since the mid-two-thousands. Debbie Cassidy is back for round two of *Wicked Spirit*, dragging us straight back into the administrative nightmare where failing a pop quiz apparently gets you dissolved into mist. 

### The Curriculum of Endless Bruises

We are expected to marvel at a protagonist who discovers she is not merely a garden-variety sorcerer, but a "Veil Walker." What does that even mean? The synopsis hides behind capitalized nouns and vague threats about the spirit world clawing at ankles. It is the literary equivalent of a parent trying to convince a six-year-old that Brussels sprouts taste like candy because they are green and vaguely crunchy. Yet, the audience eats it up. They line up around the block for another round of mandatory hunt duty and suspicious faculty members who look like they skipped their anger management milestones. 

Speaking of children refusing to eat their vegetables, my daughter spent forty-five minutes yesterday trying to negotiate a peace treaty with a piece of broccoli by hiding it inside a napkin origami swan. Kids will invent entire geopolitical strategies just to avoid dealing with discomfort. Adults do the exact same thing when they pick up fantasy romance novels featuring men named Sterling Damascus. We wrap ourselves in the comforting delusion that if we just survive the trial, if we just endure the toxic supervision of someone we shouldn't trust, the universe will finally hand us a gold star and validate our emotional damage. 

### The Mechanics of Compulsory Danger

Sterling Damascus. Honestly, the man sounds like a high-end kitchen appliance designed to slice through interpersonal boundaries with ruthless efficiency. We get the standard slow-burn friction, the forced proximity tropes, and a magical system that punishes characters for wanting things. Power has consequences. Desire has consequences. We get it. Every single YA and NA author since Sarah J. Maas learned how to type has beaten this exact drum until it turned to dust. 

Give me a narrative about someone learning to sit quietly in a room without setting things on fire. Give me a protagonist who breaks a generational cycle by going to therapy instead of punching a demon in the face. But no. We need active hunt duty. We need vanishing classmates and tinctures that reveal a monster's truest self because subtlety apparently died in the third quarter of two thousand and fifteen. Cassidy knows her demographic, and she is feeding them raw meat wrapped in barbed wire. 

I have to go outside right now because Barnaby managed to climb past the second branch of the weeping willow and he is currently staring down at me with the absolute moral certainty of a creature who regrets nothing.
