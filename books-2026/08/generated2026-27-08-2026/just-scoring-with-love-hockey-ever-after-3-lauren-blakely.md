Some marketing department somewhere clearly had a catastrophic administrative collapse, because the synopsis provided for *Love ad Lib*—marketed mysteriously under a completely different hockey umbrella—promises ice rinks and slapshots while delivering a *Downton Abbey* on steroids with a grumpy British lord. And honestly? I refuse to accept this bureaucratic erasure of athletic sweat. If I am strapping in for a romance, I demand emotional accountability, clear communication before the third-act crisis, and absolutely zero games played with people's fragile hearts. 

### The Architecture of Pretension

Fake dating is the literary equivalent of a safety net woven entirely out of barbed wire and withheld text messages. Yet, we devour it because the yearning tastes like sugar on bruised tongues. Libby Fletcher is currently starring in the tragicomedy of a housing crisis mixed with career freefall. Enter Lord Henry Foxbrooke—a man whose very surname sounds like it smells of damp tweed and ancestral guilt. But let us discuss the real menace here: poorly packed suitcases in modern television dramas. Why do characters in prestige period pieces always pack twenty steamer trunks for a weekend trip, while I cannot even find a matching pair of socks without questioning my life choices? We deserve structural integrity in our romantic protagonists, not just chiseled jawlines and generational debt.

### Sparks and Square Footage

The proximity trope remains undefeated. Give me forced bed-sharing or give me absolute silence. This estate sounds suffocatingly large, yet miraculously small enough to force two reluctant souls into proximity until their emotional walls crumble like stale shortbread. Real affection requires vulnerability, not just snappy banter tossed across a grand dining table. Give me a protagonist willing to apologize sincerely over burnt toast. 

I need to sign divorce papers in twenty minutes, which is infinitely less exhausting than explaining emotional availability to someone who communicates exclusively through brooding eyebrow movements.
