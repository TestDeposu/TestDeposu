Another sugar-dusted corpse in Savannah. Because what else would occupy the humid afternoons of a woman whose primary vocation involves buttercream and minor demonology? *Rituals and Rolling Pins* promises the exact brand of low-stakes comfort that makes me long for a hostile takeover just to feel a pulse. Yet I find myself perusing the synopsis of this eleventh installment with the detached curiosity of someone watching a superyacht burn from a private helipad. 

Let's face it. 

### The Aesthetics of Confectionary Homicide

The premise relies on the tired intersection of artisanal baking and amateur sleuthing—a genre combination roughly as surprising as a margin call from Goldman Sachs. Our protagonist, a hedgewitch named Katie, apparently multitasks between kneading sourdough and stumbling over true-crime podcasters in the marshlands. A druid standing over a corpse. Groundbreaking. One might expect a searing critique of modern media sensationalism, but instead, we get a cozy little romp involving a spellbook club. The juxtaposition of ritualistic murder and pecan buns feels less like dark satire and more like an episode of the Costa Concordia disaster viewed through a pastel Instagram filter. 

Wait. Actually, the inclusion of a podcaster victim suggests a faint, desperate pulse of cultural relevance. 

### The Granola-Coated Void of Suburban Witchcraft

We are expected to care about the social dynamics of a magical bakery where the stakes never rise above a soggy pastry. The thematic architecture here is entirely hollow. It trades on the exhausted trope of the quirky community rallying together, armed with wooden spoons and righteous indignation, to outsmart local law enforcement. Give me Cormac McCarthy or give me silence. Still, there is an undeniable architectural efficiency to these cozy mysteries. They march toward their predictable resolutions with the mechanical precision of a Swiss timepiece—or the liquidation of a failing hedge fund. 

The synopsis breathlessly informs us that Katie must "follow the trail of crumbs to the real killer." A culinary pun. In the year of our lord two thousand and twenty-six. The audacity of this literary laziness is almost admirable. 

### Predictive Insolvency in Narrative Form

This book will sell a quarter-million copies to people who exclusively drink chamomile tea while wearing woolen cardigans in eighty-degree weather. It will spawn five more sequels before the decade closes. It offers zero existential dread, zero moral ambiguity, and absolutely no genuine blood. It is pure, unfiltered literary empty calories. 

Good grief, the nib is gone. The toddler has dropped my limited-edition Montblanc Meisterstück somewhere into the shredder again.
