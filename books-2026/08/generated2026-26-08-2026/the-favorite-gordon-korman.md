**Author:** Gordon Korman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Sweating through linen in this suffocating August heat while reviewing plebeian literature is my personal circle of Dante’s inferno. Yet here we are, staring down Gordon Korman’s upcoming manuscript, *The Favorite*. One glance at the lineage—or lack thereof—makes my aristocratic eye twitch violently. A billionaire descends upon some dusty hamlet in Oklahoma like a deus ex machina wrapped in designer fleece. Groundbreaking. Truly, the Habsburgs managed their succession crises with far more decorum. 

### The Commodification of Progeny

Consider the vulgarity of the premise. Four wretched offspring, including twins because lowbrow storytelling always relies on biological duplication for cheap dramatic friction, forced into a gladiatorial arena for the ultimate prize. And what is this glittering crown? To become a brand ambassador for *Zzazz*. Good grief. The very name tastes like cheap corn syrup and cheap polyester. Korman essentially takes the royal primogeniture model, strips away centuries of divine right, and drags it straight through the corporate mud. 

### Bloodlines Versus Boardrooms

Harper, Dylan, Lacey, and Bobby Coleridge. Such pedestrian names for children allegedly possessing billionaire blood. But wait, the synopsis casually mentions a three-legged rat named Ollie. A rat. In high society, we maintain drawing rooms free of vermin, yet this wretched rodent apparently plays a pivotal role in the sibling bloodbath. Make it make sense, as the peasants say on their digital communication networks. One observes a glaring thematic reliance on populist tropes. Soccer games, Monopoly smackdowns, and pizza-eating challenges. How utterly base. Where is the fencing? Where is the acquisition of obscure European art. Instead, we get a carbohydrate-heavy gluttony contest designed to appeal to the absolute lowest common denominator. 

Korman clearly fancies himself the Tolstoy of the tween demographic—giving us *War and Peace* for kids who consume Tide Pods. Yet the sociological implications here are fascinatingly bleak. This isn't just sibling rivalry. It is the capitalist commodification of familial affection, packaged neatly for mass consumption. The children are willing to sacrifice their dignity for global jet-setting and star-studded parties. Absolute garbage behavior. No notes. 

Expect this low-rent *Succession* knockoff to dominate the bestseller lists simply because contemporary readers possess the literary palate of a feral raccoon. They will eat it up. Personally, I would rather inherit a title burdened by insurmountable death duties than read another sentence about this wretched *Zzazz* enterprise. 

I must flee this stifling study immediately, seeing as I have an exclusive wine tasting event at the country club in an hour.
