**Author:** Penn Cole  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Gods and their bloody offspring running a penal colony of mortals. How original. We are staring down the barrel of yet another divine-hierarchy fantasy where the protagonist is forced into a civil war simply because Mom bit the dust and left a pile of unanswered homework. Penn Cole returns to the ring with *Burn of the Everflame*, and based strictly on the publisher's synopsis, we are promised the usual literary cardio. Diem Bellator wants out of her miserable routine—don't we all—and suddenly finds herself shoulder-deep in court politics, divine lineage, and tactical maneuvering. 

### The Footwork of a Novice

Let us look at the striking mechanics promised here. The synopsis drops Diem right into a civil war, expecting us to believe she dodges divine right hooks and political jabs without getting her jaw shattered in round one. It is all well and good to say she must master the rules of power and romance, but what is her actual reach? If you are going to write a combatant stepping onto the mat against immortal offspring, you need to show proper defensive posture. Instead, we get the literary equivalent of a fighter keeping their chin completely exposed while circling away from the power hand. 

Actually, strike that. Maybe the lack of specific combat choreography is precisely the point. Cole is not writing a gritty sports drama about footwork and liver shots; this is pure, unadulterated romantasy spectacle where a bruised ego hurts worse than a broken rib. 

### The Politics of the Chin Check

Power dynamics in these stories always follow the same sloppy clinch. A mortal girl enters the dark underbelly of the ruling class, absorbs a flurry of blows from corrupt elites, and somehow clinches a victory through sheer emotional stubbornness. Where is the counter-punch? Where is the tactical exploitation of an opponent's over-extended guard? The synopsis relies entirely on vague buzzwords about civil war and hidden mysteries, leaving us guessing whether Diem has a single strategic bone in her body or if she is just absorbing punches until the plot saves her from a technical knockout. 

Sarah J. Maas and Rebecca Yarros have practically built an entire arena out of this exact blueprint—girls with hidden potential getting tossed into deadly trials while falling for someone who definitely has too many dark secrets and cheekbones that could cut glass. Yet, if Cole refuses to tighten up the defensive mechanics of her plot, this anticipated release is going to get countered hard in the third round. You cannot just throw a protagonist into a civil war without establishing her striking distance.
