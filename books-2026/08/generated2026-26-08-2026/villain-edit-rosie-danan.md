The house is finally quiet, save for the rhythmic, ominous hum of the refrigerator and the faint, ghostly creak of floorboards upstairs where I desperately hope two small humans are actually falling asleep instead of plotting drywall destruction. 

And so I turn my exhausted gaze to the publishing world's latest offering, *Villain Edit*, because apparently, we are still aggressively mining the corpse of late-stage capitalism and reality television for romance tropes. Rosie Danan is back with an upcoming paranormal rom-com that asks a truly staggering question: What if a vampire—yes, an actual immortal creature of the night who is supposed to be terrifying—was forced to audition for a dating show in a Southern California retirement community? 

Breathe. Just breathe. 

Because nothing screams "healthy, emotionally mature adult communication" quite like a centuries-old bloodsucker being exploited by a desperate network producer for ratings while wearing a hot mic. We are swimming in a cultural swamp where every single interpersonal dynamic must be manufactured, edited, and weaponized for our collective entertainment, and now we are apparently romanticizing the exact machinery of that exploitation under the guise of a cute meet-cute. Why do we insist on finding our happily-ever-afters inside a panopticon? 

Give me a break. Honestly, I am so profoundly exhausted by modern fiction that tries to make toxic institutional settings look quirky. Remember when people just fell in love over burnt toast and shared trauma in a quiet room without three camera operators hiding behind fake ficus trees? Alyssa Cole and Talia Hibbert understand how to write actual human affection with real stakes, whereas this premise reads like it was generated by a sleep-deprived algorithmic dartboard thrown at a whiteboard of trending Netflix docuseries. 

Yet here we are. 

We are supposed to cheer for Harper Calloway as she tries to whip a reluctant, blood-shy vampire into an advertiser-friendly bachelor within three weeks, as if emotional vulnerability can be fast-tracked by a production schedule and a ring light. The synopsis tells us he doesn't even fit the dark fantasy, which is the most cliché, teethless subversion imaginable—pun entirely intended. We take every sharp edge off our monsters until they are just polite, marketable boyfriends who happen to have fangs. 

Tragic. 

Hard pass. 

I have to go convince a tiny dictator that water is not an acid meant to melt their flesh, and that taking a bath is somehow not a human rights violation orchestrated exclusively by me.
