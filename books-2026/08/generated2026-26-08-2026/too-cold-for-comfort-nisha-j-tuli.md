The tundra of the human heart - a vast, unforgiving expanse where the frostbite of past wounds can leave one numb, and yet, a spark of attraction can set the whole landscape ablaze. Nisha J. Tuli's latest offering, *Too Cold for Comfort*, promises to be a steamy romance that will thaw even the coldest of hearts.

In the frozen wilderness of northern Canada, photographer Aadya Williams finds herself face-to-face with the gruff Reid Doerksen, the owner of the Northern Wilderness Polar Bear Lodge. Their forced proximity, thanks to an overbooked lodge, sets the stage for a battle of wills, with Aadya's free-spirited nature pitted against Reid's tightly wound reserve. But as the storm clouds gather, and the snow begins to fall, the boundaries between them start to blur.

And yet, I find myself wondering - what lies beneath Reid's gruff exterior? Is he a wounded soul, hiding behind a mask of indifference, or is he simply a man who has learned to shield himself from the world? The synopsis hints at a deeper complexity, but I'm left wanting more. How will Tuli peel back the layers of Reid's character, revealing the vulnerabilities that lie beneath?

As I ponder this, my mind wanders to the likes of Kristan Higgins and her ability to craft complex, emotionally nuanced characters. Will Tuli follow in her footsteps, or will she forge her own path? The uncertainty is tantalizing, like a whispered promise of a winter's night.

But, I must confess, my attention span is currently being tested by the antics of my youngest, who insists on watching an endless loop of *Paw Patrol*. The adventures of Marshall and Chase are all well and good, but I find myself longing for the quiet, introspective world of *Too Cold for Comfort*. The contrast between the two is jarring, like a splash of cold water on a sweltering summer day.

And yet, as I return to the world of Aadya and Reid, I'm struck by the sense that this is a story about more than just romance - it's about the human need for connection, for understanding, and for healing. The frozen landscape of northern Canada serves as a backdrop for a deeper exploration of the human condition.

I call bluff. If Tuli can deliver on the promise of this synopsis, *Too Cold for Comfort* will be a true masterpiece of the romance genre. But, if the characters remain one-dimensional, and the plot meanders into predictable territory, I fear it may fall flat.

As I close this review, I'm reminded that I must explain to my teenager why the Wi-Fi has been temporarily disabled. It seems our router has decided to stage a rebellion, and I'm left to negotiate a peace treaty.
