What's the point of retelling a legend if you're just going to reimagine it with a 21st-century twist? That's the question I keep asking myself as I read through the synopsis of *Keeper of the Grail*. I mean, I get it - the Arthurian legend is ripe for reinterpretation, but do we really need to transport a modern-day waitress to medieval England to make it interesting? And yet, I'm drawn to the idea of Vera, a queen who's as much a fish out of water as I am in my own life.

The more I read about Vera's journey, the more I'm reminded of *The Mists of Avalon*, Marion Zimmer Bradley's feminist retelling of the Arthurian legend. But whereas Bradley's work was a slow-burning, introspective epic, *Keeper of the Grail* seems to be more of a swashbuckling adventure, complete with magic potions, dashing knights, and daring maidens. It's a curious blend of genres, to say the least - but I suppose that's what makes it intriguing.

And then, of course, there's the matter of Vera's incredible gift of healing. I'm not sure what to make of this plot point - is it a convenient deus ex machina, or a genuine attempt to subvert the traditional Arthurian narrative? I'm inclined to think the former, but I suppose I'll have to wait and see how it plays out.

But what really catches my attention is the conflict brewing between the Knights of the Round Table and the High Council of Mages. It's a classic struggle between tradition and progress, with Vera caught in the middle. I can already imagine the dramatic showdowns, the heart-wrenching romances, and the snarky one-liners that will ensue.

And yet, despite my growing interest in the plot, I'm still skeptical about the whole thing. I mean, can a modern-day queen really navigate the complexities of medieval politics and magic? It's a bit too convenient, a bit too... tidy. I want to see some real tension, some real conflict, not just a shallow retelling of the Arthurian legend with a 21st-century twist.

But I suppose that's the point of a retelling - to challenge our assumptions, to subvert our expectations. And who knows? Maybe *Keeper of the Grail* will surprise me, will make me see the Arthurian legend in a whole new light. Maybe Vera will turn out to be more than just a fish out of water, more than just a convenient plot device.

Oh, who am I kidding? I'm still going to be cynical about the whole thing. But hey, at least it'll be an interesting ride.

### The Art of Retelling

You know what's even more annoying than a poorly executed retelling of a classic legend? A poorly executed kids' cartoon. I mean, have you seen *Steven Universe*? It's like the writers took every tired fantasy trope and mashed them all together into a mess of confusing plotlines and poorly developed characters. And don't even get me started on the "twists" - it's like they thought they were being clever, but really they were just being lazy.

But I digress. *Keeper of the Grail* has its work cut out for it, if it wants to live up to the standards set by its predecessors. And I'm not just talking about *The Mists of Avalon* - I'm talking about all the other retellings, all the other reinterpretations of the Arthurian legend.

Can it do it? I'm not holding my breath.
