The smell of cinnamon rolls always distracts me from the pile of manuscripts on the counter. But here we are, looking at Kimberly Ann Johnson’s upcoming *Erotic Seasons*. 

### Finding the Missing Keys
The synopsis asks if you are at a threshold where what used to feel good doesn't make sense anymore. Honestly, between managing a household, refereeing sibling arguments over who licked the wooden spoon, and trying to finish a chapter before the dough over-proves, my entire life feels like one giant threshold. Johnson previously wrote *The Fourth Trimester*. Now she is moving past motherhood and into a broader conversation about female desire across every single life stage. 

### Rethinking the Lifespan
Let us talk about the archetypes. Maiden, mother, virgin, crone. They sound like characters you would find in an Agatha Christie village drama. Yet Johnson wants to reframe them for modern women dealing with perimenopause, shifting marriages, and changing nervous systems. Alexandra Roxo praises the book as a wise woman sitting by the fire. Wait, let me rephrase that. Is a fireside chat actually what readers want when their bodies feel confusing, or do they want concrete anatomical maps? Perhaps the fireside metaphor is entirely too cozy. Actually, strike that. Give me all the cozy wisdom I can get while I wait for the oven timer to shriek. 

Literally. 

### The Somatic Promise
As someone who usually analyzes the psychological motivations of fictional poisoners in mid-century country houses, shifting to somatic experiencing and sexological bodywork feels like a distinct gear change. The synopsis leans heavily on the idea that women lack the language to communicate these shifts. It promises a revolutionary reckoning. Yet the premise relies entirely on buzzwords about anatomical patterns and monthly rhythms. Will it deliver practical insight, or will it read like a textbook wrapped in a wellness blog? 

The school bell waits for no one, and the flour is currently coating my favorite reading glasses.
