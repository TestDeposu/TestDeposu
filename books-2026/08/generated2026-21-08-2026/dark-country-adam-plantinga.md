**Author:** Adam Plantinga  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-27 APL

What's the point of a serene existence if it's just going to get blown to smithereens by the past? Kurt Argento's attempt at a peaceful life in Barton, South Dakota, sounds like a recipe for disaster. A former Detroit cop turned church handyman, Argento's got the kind of baggage that never truly gets left behind. And then he meets Majed, a seventeen-year-old Syrian refugee with a price on his head.

The plot thickens, or so the synopsis would have us believe. Two men with hard eyes are after Majed, and Argento steps in to protect him. But what's the real story here? Is Majed just a target, or is he carrying something valuable? The synopsis is frustratingly vague, leaving me with more questions than answers. I'm reminded of the countless times I've read a thriller that promised the world but delivered a shallow, predictable plot. Take, for instance, that one book by Harlan Coben – you know, the one where the protagonist's family is kidnapped, and he has to navigate a complex web of lies to rescue them. Yeah, that one. It was a total snooze-fest.

But I digress. Back to *Dark Country*. The premise has potential, I'll give it that. A grizzled ex-cop protecting a young refugee from unknown assailants? It's a classic trope, and one that can be done well if executed properly. I'm thinking of books like *The Terminal List* by Jack Carr – now that's a thriller that knows how to deliver. But can Adam Plantinga pull it off? I'm not convinced.

And what's with the title, *Dark Country*? Is it a reference to the American heartland, or something more sinister? The synopsis doesn't give us any clues, leaving me to wonder if the title is just a cheap attempt to sound edgy. I mean, come on, *Dark Country*? It sounds like a rejected title for a bad horror movie.

But despite my reservations, I'm willing to give *Dark Country* a chance. Maybe, just maybe, it'll surprise me and turn out to be a gripping thriller that keeps me on the edge of my seat. Maybe the characters will be well-developed, and the plot will twist and turn in unexpected ways. Maybe.
