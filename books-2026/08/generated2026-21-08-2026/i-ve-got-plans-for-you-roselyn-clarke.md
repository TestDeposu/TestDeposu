The shrieks of laughter outside are reaching a pitch that suggests someone is about to weaponize the garden hose against a sibling, and honestly, good for them. I am sitting by the patio doors, squinting at a synopsis that smells entirely of sunscreen, teenage malice, and the kind of high-stakes cruelty that only happens when privilege and boredom intersect. Roselyn Clarke is giving us *I've Got Plans for You* this coming October, and I have immediate, violent opinions about it.

### The Architecture of Petty Warfare

We are looking at a classic dynamic here. Rory and Celeste. Two girls whose friendship is built on mutual blackmail and a shared geographical destiny. But let us pause for a second. Actually, no. Let us rewind. I said earlier this feels like a fresh spin on the toxic-best-friend trope, but looking closer at the premise, it is actually deeply derivative of every straight-to-streaming thriller churned out since Gillian Flynn changed the literary landscape. Yet, I will read it in one sitting while eating stale crackers. 

The mechanics of their arrangement—keeping the peace through mutually assured destruction—provide a delicious little engine for narrative tension. Middle school secrets. Soccer co-captain boyfriends. A college transition hanging over their heads like a guillotine. Clarke understands the claustrophobia of adolescent social circles. When you are eighteen, a beach house isn't a vacation. It is a locked combat zone.

### The Weather as a Co-Conspirator

And then the storm hits. How original. A weather event trapping scheming teenagers in a isolated house is a trope older than the hills. But efficiency matters more than originality in genre fiction. If a squall knocks out the Wi-Fi and leaves a bunch of sociopathic teenagers to turn on each other with psychological warfare, I am seated. 

The blurb drops names like Veronica Bane, which tells us precisely the flavor of blood we are tasting here. Sharp, mean-girl dialogue mixed with body count anxieties. Will someone end up dead? Probably. Will the alibis be paper-thin? Absolutely. 

### Why the Frenemy Trope Refuses to Die

We love watching pretty girls ruin each other's lives because the alternative is thinking about real-world economic collapse. Give me petty blackmail over mortgage rates any day of the week. Clarke seems poised to deliver a fast-paced, morally vacant afternoon read that requires zero actual thought, which is precisely what the market demands right now. 

I am going to hide in my study for exactly ten minutes of uninterrupted silence.
