**Author:** Emily Gray  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-11-17 APL

Ah yes, *By the Light of the Stars*. Another monumental contribution to the literary canon where a girlboss in a tiara suddenly realizes that absolute monarchy might just be slightly problematic. Groundbreaking. Emily Gray is apparently dropping this space opera in late 2026, and I am already preemptively exhausted by the sheer audacity of the recycled tropes. 

### The Mandatory Riot Against the Patriarchy (In Space)

We begin with a pristine lunar colony because apparently, future dystopias must maintain immaculate housekeeping before they fall apart. Princess Lyrii Thana is dutiful. She is wearing the gilded chains of her birthright. And by the gods, she is ready to marry an austere commander from a Sisterworld just for the vibes—well, no, for geopolitical stability. How deeply thrilling. 

Then comes the obligatory assassination attempt. Because no YA-adjacent sci-fi romance can function without an inciting incident that literally drops a handsome rogue directly into the heroine's lap. Enter Kade Maxwell. He is mysterious. He is a space pilot. He is giving major Han Solo-meets-TikTok-boyfriend energy, and I am rolling my eyes so hard I might detach my retinas. 

### The Predictable Epiphany Pipeline

Of course, she has to trust him because she has no Uber. And OF COURSE they stumble upon a secret refugee spaceship—because nothing screams systemic critique quite like stumbling into a poorly hidden proletariat metaphor. Suddenly, her entire worldview is shattered. Who could have possibly predicted that the ruling family was built on lies? Not Lyrii, apparently, who possesses the critical thinking skills of a damp piece of toast until a hot guy flies her past a marginalized demographic. 

And then we get the eternal internal monologue of doom. Is she loyal? Is she terrified? Or is she just extremely distracted by his jawline while her arranged wedding looms like a ticking clock of marital oppression? Groundbreaking literary deconstruction of late-stage capitalism and dynastic rule, truly. It is giving Sarah J. Maas meets every generic Kindle Unlimited space romance churned out by an algorithm in a basement. 

### The Inevitable Subversion of Absolutely Nothing

Let us be completely real here. We all know how this manuscript ends. She is going to choose herself. She is going to reject the system, smash the patriarchy, and probably fly off into the sunset with Kade while a civilization collapses behind them. But hey, at least she got a boyfriend out of the systemic collapse of her home planet. Priorities! 

My tea is ready to be steeped. I am going to brew a cup of lapsang souchong and ponder my existential dread.
