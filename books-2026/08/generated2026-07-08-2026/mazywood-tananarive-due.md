**Author:** Tananarive Due  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-09-22 APL

Do you know what it is like to be trapped in a stifling box while giants loom outside?  Well, Johnny—a filmmaker returning to his family's California mountain lodge—is about to find out.  And honestly, reading about a secluded retreat right now sounds both heavenly and terrifying when your own apartment feels like a brick oven.

### Thematic Analysis of Rage and Illusion

Old Hollywood has always traded in illusions, yet Due appears to be stripping away the varnish to expose something rotten underneath. And who can blame her? The synopsis boldly invokes *All the Sinners Bleed* and *Erasure*—lofty comparisons, to be sure, but Due has the pedigree to back it up after the brilliant devastation of *The Reformatory*. 

Consider the generational weight here. Mazelle Washington's career spanned the 1920s to the 1940s, brushing shoulders with royalty like Lena Horne and Clark Gable. Yet, beneath that shimmering public facade, she nurtured a childhood secret born from a dark wish that came true. Fascinating in theory. A dangerous gamble in execution. Will the narrative actually unpack the systemic exploitation of Black artists during the Golden Age, or will it lean entirely into monster-in-the-woods horror? The synopsis leaves that balance tantalizingly vague. 

### Anticipatory Preview

Fifty years after Mazelle's death, her filmmaker grandson brings his family right into the jaws of a multi-generational curse. A monster lurks outside Mazywood. Classic setup. But because the plot details remain frustratingly sparse—typical for marketing departments terrified of spoiling their own twists—we are left to obsess over the atmosphere. 

Expect a masterclass in claustrophobia. Due excels at historical ghosts that refuse to stay buried, and tracing a single malignant seed of rage from the roaring twenties into the modern day requires immense structural discipline. I just hope the third act doesn't collapse under the weight of its own ambition. Time will tell. 

Is any family history anything more than a haunted house we inherit?
