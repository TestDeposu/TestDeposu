But, as I sit here, sipping on a lukewarm cup of coffee, I'm more concerned with the literary sauce that's simmering on my desk.  Specifically, *Lucy Kline, Necromancer*, a book that promises to mix the Nancy Drew magic with the occult, and I have to admit, it's a combination that has piqued my interest.  After all, *The Name of the Rose* by Umberto Eco showed us that historical mysteries can be murderously intriguing.

— But, let's get down to the nitty-gritty. The premise is straightforward: Lucy's twin sisterLouisa went missing, was presumed dead, but now another girl who looks just like her is MIA. The plot thickens when Lucy starts experiencing ghostly nightmares. It's as if the author, Tom O'Donnell, is attempting to craft a whodunit with a dash of paranormal activity. The comparison with *Buffy the Vampire Slayer* isn't exactly misplaced, given the supernatural elements. However, I'm more intrigued by the "Nancy Drew-style murder mystery" label, which suggests a more cerebral, puzzle-solving approach.

And yet, the more I ponder the synopsis, the more I realize that it's still a rather...vague endeavor. We don't know much about the world this book inhabits, beyond the obvious: necromancy, ghostly apparitions, and a protagonist struggling with her sister's disappearance. Where does this story take place? Is it a contemporary setting, or is it a period piece? I'm not being picky; I'm simply saying that for a book that promises to "binge," I want a lot more substance.

Still, there are aspects that could make this a compelling read. The author seems to be tapping into the nostalgia of old-fashioned mysteries, while infusing them with modern thrills and chills. I can almost see the plot unfolding in front of me: Lucy's growing obsession with uncovering the truth, her descent into madness (not unlike *The Shadow of the Wind*'s Daniel Sempere), and the blurring of reality and fantasy. It's a tantalizing prospect, one that I would love to see fleshed out.

But, we live in an era where the supernatural is more mainstream than ever, what with the *Chilling Adventures of Sabrina* and *Stranger Things* dominating the screens. *Lucy Kline, Necromancer* will, in all likelihood, be yet another attempt to capitalize on this trend. I'm not sure if it will stand out from the crowd, or if the author has something truly unique to offer.

A bold prediction: this book will either be a masterclass in blending genres or a lackluster attempt at conjuring up a compelling narrative. Only time will tell.
