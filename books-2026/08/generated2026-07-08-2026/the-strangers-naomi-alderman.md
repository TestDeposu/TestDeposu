**Author:** Naomi Alderman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-09-22 APL

Naomi Alderman, whose previous work *The Power* shattered gendered power dynamics with the elegance of a well‑cut diamond, now invites us into a world where grief and the unknown collide like two rivers in flood.

She opens with a camera, a simple device that captures a creature that feels like a myth made flesh. The mimmoth—low, flat‑faced, trunk‑like nose—appears in the back garden of her parents’ home, a quiet intruder that soon spreads across continents. How does a creature that refuses to be captured, that refuses to eat human food, become a symbol of humanity’s fragile place in the ecosystem? The question lingers, like a perfume that refuses to fade.

Alderman’s voice is both memoir and speculative fiction, a blend that feels like a glass of aged port: sweet, complex, with a hint of bitterness. She wrestles with her mother’s death, a grief that is as much a character as the mimmoths. The narrative oscillates between the personal and the planetary, a duality that feels like a train’s carriage split between a luxury lounge and a sleeper compartment. The tension between the two is palpable—yet it is also the book’s greatest strength.
