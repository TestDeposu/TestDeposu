**Author:** Richard Osman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-09-15 APL

We Chase Shadows, the second entry in Richard Osman's *We Solve Murders* series, promises to be a globetrotting whodunit with all the hallmarks of a classic detective tale—think Poirot meets *Killing Eve*, with a dash of Scandinavian noir. I can already anticipate the sun-baked Italian hills, the sleek, modern trappings of Barcelona, and the quaint English pubs that will serve as the trio's unwitting hub. But what of the story itself?

A body is found on the steps of a villa in the Italian hills, a victim with a threatening note that sets the tone for a mystery that will span continents. Our intrepid detectives, Amy, Rosie, and Steve, are back together and ready to take on the case. Ah, but this is no straightforward whodunit—there's something more at play, something that will keep them (and, by extension, the reader) guessing. The plot twists, it seems, will be as plentiful as the suspects.

One can't help but think of Tana French's Dublin Murder Squad series when reading the synopsis of *We Chase Shadows*. The same blend of local flair, intriguing characters, and a deep dive into the complexities of detective work is on offer. Osman, it seems, is aiming for the big leagues—impressing, at the very least, with his globe-trotting ambitions. But what of the execution? Will he deliver on his promises?

The stakes, it seems, will be higher than ever before, with danger closing in on the trio in ways both personal and professional. We're promised a thrilling ride, full of twists and turns, and I'll be lying if I say I'm not intrigued. But what kind of stakes are we talking about? The personal lives of the detectives, perhaps? The very fabric of their agency? The suspense is killing me—literally.

The world of *We Solve Murders* seems to be expanding, with each new installment offering fresh locales and complexities. Osman is clearly playing the long game, building a series that will appeal to fans of mystery fiction, not just in the UK but worldwide. I'm reminded of the success of M.C. Beaton's Agatha Raisin series, with its mix of humor, charm, and small-town mysteries.

Fascinating in theory, yes. But can Osman sustain this level of tension and intrigue? Will the mystery be satisfying, or will we be left with too many loose ends? The plot thickens, indeed. And, as I sweat on this sweltering August bus ride, I can feel my excitement building. Will *We Chase Shadows* be a worthy follow-up to *The Word is Murder*, Osman's breakout novel?

As I scribble my thoughts on the back of a soggy notebook (air conditioning? what air conditioning?), a stray phrase comes to mind: 'a story that will keep you guessing.' And so it will, I have no doubt. But what of the shadows themselves? Are they hiding more than just a single killer?

I'll be watching, eagerly, on September 15. But will it be too late to join the party?
