**Author:** J.D. Robb  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-09-08 APL

And let me tell you something about this synopsis.  J. D.  Robb is throwing hands.

Look at the setup. A biotech exec named Larry Chu gets turned into mincemeat inside a high-security building by a lone, unarmed assailant who hits with superhuman force. Fascinating in theory. But as someone who spends twelve hours a day dodging flying sheet pans and watching brawls break out over the last rack of ribeyes, I have questions about the biomechanics here. How do you mangle a body without a weapon unless you are dropping an anvil from the rafters? Pure rage is a great narrative fuel—trust me, I see it every weekend when the front-of-house staff forgets an entire table's order—but physics still applies, even in a futuristic New York. 

Lieutenant Eve Dallas thinks the violence is deeply personal. Fine. She understands trauma. But let us talk about the mismatch between the combat styles hinted at in this text. You have a suspect raging out like an untrained heavyweight throwing wild haymakers in a street fight, yet somehow bypassing high-tech security and manipulating Cayman Island bank accounts like a slick submission grappler. Which is it? Is this an unhinged brawler or a calculated assassin with a massive adrenaline spike? J.D. Robb is playing coy with the vagueness here, dropping hints about a shadowy corporate entity while leaning hard into the emotional pyrotechnics. 

Predicting how this plays out? This feels straight out of a classic Patricia Cornwell or Karin Slaughter playbook, where the visceral brutality on the slab is just a mask for corporate greed wearing a bloody sweater. Yet, if the killer is just another angry suit with deep pockets and a fancy exoskeleton, I will be bitterly disappointed. Give me a proper matchup. Give me a striker who can actually back up that kind of structural damage. 

Time will tell if Dallas can actually land a clean takedown on this mystery brawler, or if the plot gets bogged down in corporate spreadsheet jargon. But right now, between flipping burgers and wiping down stainless steel, I just want to know who hits harder.
