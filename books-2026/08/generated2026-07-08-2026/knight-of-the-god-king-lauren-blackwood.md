**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-20 APL



Let us address the premise, shall we? Calliope wants to be a magical, music-summoning knight. Fascinating in theory. But of course, the universe—and a parasitic, ungrateful family—must conspire to ruin her ambitions. Blackwood is leaning hard into the pressured-caretaker dynamic, a trope I usually devour like leftover pizza at midnight. And yet, the synopsis plays things painfully close to the chest regarding the exact mechanics of this musical magic. Will it be lyrical incantations? Instrument-based warding? Vague hand-waving set to a lute? Time will tell. 

### Thematic Analysis of Courtly Chaos

Power dynamics and institutional rot form the beating heart of this narrative. Calliope is not just fighting monsters in The Dark; she is actively propping up a fraudulent god-king whose immortality is entirely dependent on her own magic. A masterclass in workplace exploitation. Truly, we have all worked for a boss who takes the credit while we do the heavy lifting, though hopefully your supervisor was not a mortal impostor sentencing you to death. 

And then, naturally, we have Beric. The handsome, prickly squire who gets snubbed immediately upon introduction. Music to my chronically tired eyes. I am infinitely weary of instant-love triangles and hollow yearning. Give me genuine mutual aggravation that slowly morphs into fierce, protective partnership. If Blackwood handles their forced proximity with the sharp-witted banter I am praying for—and I am praying hard, given how sweat is currently pooling on my keyboard—this could easily scratch the exact same itch left by recent romantic fantasies from authors like Margaret Rogerson or Raybearer-era Jordan Ifueko. 

### Anticipatory Preview and Verdict

The synopsis promises dark deeds, royal intrigue, and a demonic entity that sounds wonderfully threatening, even if the phrasing remains delightfully opaque. Blackwood is playing her cards close to the vest. Will the central conflict rely too heavily on the annoying cousin's blackmail? Possibly. High-stakes political fantasy often trips over petty domestic subplots. But I am choosing optimism. Or perhaps that is just the caffeine talking as my desk fan blows lukewarm air directly into my left eyeball. 

Either way, watching Calliope and Beric take fate into their own hands while the literal fabric of their kingdom unravels sounds like the exact kind of high-stress escapism required to survive a brutal summer afternoon. One can only hope the romance avoids the modern plague of miscommunication tropes and leans into a genuinely healthy, communicative alliance against tyranny. 

Is it October yet? Or will I completely dehydrate before I ever find out if the fake god gets what's coming to him?
