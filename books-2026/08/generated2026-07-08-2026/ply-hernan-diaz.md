**Author:** Hernan Diaz  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-09-29 APL

The musty smell of old books and the faint hum of the air conditioner are my only comforts as I dive into the synopsis of Hernan Diaz's upcoming novel, *Ply*.  The Pulitzer Prize-winning author of *Trust* has turned to the future with a novel that promises to examine the place of technology in the American imagination.  But, I ask you, can he deliver?

The story follows an orphan, a "pincher" who steals electricity from the grid to sell it on the black market, and her journey into a rich art and music scene. It's a high-risk life, but one that brings her into contact with a colossal scientific invention that could change the very fabric of reality. Fascinating in theory. Yet, the synopsis is frustratingly vague, leaving me with more questions than answers. What is the nature of this invention? How does it impact the protagonist's life and the world at large? The lack of specificity is, frankly, annoying.

But, I suppose that's the point. *Ply* is meant to be a novel of ideas, built from a story of people. It's a Dickensian odyssey, a family drama, and a scientific thriller all rolled into one. It's a bold gamble, one that could pay off if executed correctly. After all, Diaz has proven himself to be a masterful storyteller with his previous novels. And, if the praise from David Mitchell is any indication, *Ply* is a novel that will break the skin with its fine sentences and swerving plot.

As I sit here, surrounded by the musty smell of old books, I am reminded of other authors who have tackled similar themes. Margaret Atwood's *The Handmaid's Tale* comes to mind, with its exploration of a dystopian future and the impact of technology on society. And, of course, there's the obligatory nod to Dickens himself, whose novels of social commentary and critique of the human condition are a clear influence on Diaz's work.

But, I digress. *Ply* is a novel that promises to be a major achievement from a major contemporary writer. It's a novel that will question the place of technology in the American experiment, and one that will, no doubt, leave readers pondering the implications long after they finish reading. Will it live up to the hype? Time will tell.

As I close the synopsis and let out a deep sigh, I am left with one lingering thought: what does it mean to be human in a world that is increasingly driven by technology? Is it possible to find a sense of self and connection with others in a world that is constantly changing? *Ply* promises to explore these questions and more, and I, for one, am eager to dive in and find out.

Will *Ply* be the next big thing in literary fiction? Hardly. But, it's definitely a novel that will spark conversation and debate. And, who knows, maybe it'll even break the skin with its fine sentences and swerving plot.
