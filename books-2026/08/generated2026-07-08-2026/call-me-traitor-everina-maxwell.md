Yet my mind isn't on the soil, but rather on Everina Maxwell’s upcoming *Call Me Traitor*.  And honestly?  I am already aggressively protective of these characters before I have even read a single chapter.

### An Anticipatory Preview and Thematic Analysis

Let us look at the setup. En is literally a walking weapon with battle spells carved into her skin, bound by magical loyalty to the archmagi. But then there is Tamol—flighty, cowardly, silver-tongued, and possessing a dangerous affinity for magic. Naturally, Tamol saves En’s life, and naturally, En starts falling for her right before discovering that Tamol is the exact traitor she was sent to hunt down. Deliciously messy. Opposites attract combined with the assassin-falls-for-her-target trope? Sign me up immediately. 

Thematically, the synopsis leans heavily into the institutional cruelty of an unforgiving empire versus the idealism of youth. Years ago, these exiled students had a vision for a better kingdom. They lost. They were discarded to die in the deadly, unpredictable wilderness of the Far Peninsula. Yet the empire refuses to let the past stay buried, because something monstrous and ancient lurks in that wilderness, waiting. 

### Tropes and Expectations

We are looking at dark academia roots mutating into survival fantasy. We get the classic found family gone wrong motif, a slow-burn romance, and forbidden devotion. Maxwell previously gave us *Winter’s Orbit*, a book I adored for its tender character dynamics against a backdrop of rigid political machinery. If you liked the quiet, desperate longing in T. Kingfisher’s work or the bureaucratic fantasy weight found in Katherine Addison’s *The Goblin Emperor*, this new sapphic adventure should sit comfortably on your radar. 

### A Critical Eye on the Blurb

Still, I have complaints about the marketing copy itself. The synopsis leans a bit too heavily on the vague "something monstrous in the wilderness" trope without giving us actual meat. What is the monster? Is it magical wildlife, or a forgotten god? Vague threats frustrate me. But I forgive it because the emotional core looks solid. 

Can an obedient, magic-bound soldier choose love over systemic oppression without getting them both killed? Time will tell. But if anyone hurts En or Tamol, I am personally driving out to the publisher's office with my heavy-duty garden trowel.
