**Author:** Marshall J. Moore  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-09-22 APL

It is ninety-five degrees outside, the pastures are parched to a crisp, and I am sitting here reading press releases about fictional mad scientists.  Marshall J.  Moore’s *The Henchperson's Guide to Unionizing* arrives next month, promising a darkly comedic look at the laborers of the gothic underworld.

Consider the sheer audacity of it. We have gravediggers, familiars, and disgruntled brides—classic victims of unchecked, monopolistic evil—finally realizing that their aristocratic employers are nothing more than exploitative land-hoarders. Frankly, it is about time someone applied a bit of historical materialism to the classic monster trope. For centuries, these literary villains have treated their assistants as disposable organic waste, discarding limbs and life force without a single thought for fair compensation or workplace safety. 

### The Politics of the Lab Bench

Look closely at the synopsis. Igor spends his nights robbing graves and stitching body parts together, all while keeping his boss from electrocuting himself. Unpaid emotional labor of the highest order. And what do they get for their troubles? Driven out of town by pitchfork-wielding mobs, relocating repeatedly because their bosses refuse to secure proper zoning permits for their unholy experiments. A classic allegory for corporate negligence. Ravenwald Manor becomes a makeshift commune of the oppressed. 

And yet, I worry the book might shy away from actual structural critique in favor of quirky, marketable whimsy. Sarah Beth Durst compares it to *The Spellshop*, which signals a decidedly cozy aesthetic. Can a narrative about impending armageddon truly bite the hand that feeds it while aiming for the whimsical bestseller lists? Hardlikely. We are likely looking at a softer, more palatable rebellion—less *The Jungle*, more Christopher Moore with fangs. 

### Environmental Exploitation at Ravenwald

As an eco-critic watching our own local fields turn to dust during this relentless drought, I find myself hypersensitive to how these narratives treat the land. Ravenwald Manor is described as isolated, a dumping ground for mad science and dark magic. Who cleans up the toxic run-off from these resurrection experiments? Does the Doctor properly dispose of discarded biological matter? The synopsis stays frustratingly vague on the ecological footprint of necromancy. 

Time will tell if Moore uses this gothic sandbox to genuinely skewer the capitalist extraction models of modern industry, or if we are just getting another round of quirky banter between a witch and a cat. But sitting here sweating through my shirt while the grid threatens to fail, I find a grim sort of solidarity in Igor’s plight. We are all just overworked laborers keeping the lights on for tyrannical bosses who care nothing for the ecosystem they are actively destroying. 

Will the proletariat of the gothic underworld actually seize the means of reanimation, or will they settle for a mild human resources seminar and a pizza party?
