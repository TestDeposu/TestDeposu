**Author:** Nisha J. Tuli  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-05-05 APL

Tuli's *Storm Breaker* trilogy.  The premise, much like the carefully curated art that surrounds me, promises a complex dance of power, obedience, and forbidden love.

Poet Graves, the nineteen-year-old protagonist, is a product of the ruling elite in New Manhattan, her life a carefully constructed facade. The weight of her secrets, particularly the one that sets her apart from the rest – her ability to control the storms that ravage the wasteland – threatens to upend this delicate balance. It's a dangerous gamble, one that could have far-reaching consequences, not just for Poet, but for those around her.

The setup is undeniably intriguing – a dystopian dark academia narrative, replete with brutal trials designed to expose weakness, and a forbidden romance that promises to upend the status quo. It's a familiar trope, one that echoes the works of Veronica Roth and Suzanne Collins, yet the unique blend of elements – the storm caller aspect, in particular – hints at a freshness that could set *Storm Caller* apart from its contemporaries.

But, as is often the case, the devil lies in the details. The synopsis, while tantalizing, remains frustratingly vague. What are the true stakes of Poet's power? How does her relationship with Rook, the mysterious outsider, evolve? And what of the trials themselves – what secrets do they hold, and how will Poet navigate the treacherous landscape of Amery Academy?

These are questions that only the full narrative can answer, and I, for one, am eager to see how Tuli chooses to develop these threads. The inclusion of two bonus scenes from the hero's POV is a nice touch, one that promises to add depth to the narrative.

In many ways, *Storm Caller* feels like a spiritual successor to the likes of *Fourth Wing*, with its blend of dystopian politics and forbidden romance. It's a potent combination, one that has proven successful time and again in the world of YA fiction. Yet, I remain cautious – the line between homage and imitation is a fine one, and it's unclear, at this point, which side of that line *Storm Caller* will fall.

Still, I'm drawn to the promise of this narrative, the way it seems to dance on the edge of chaos, threatening to spill over into something greater. It's a tantalizing prospect, one that has me wondering – will *Storm Caller* be the next big thing in YA fiction, or will it succumb to the pressures of its own ambition?
