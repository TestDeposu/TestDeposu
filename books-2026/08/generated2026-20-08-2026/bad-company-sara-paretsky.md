**Author:** Sara Paretsky  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-11-10 APL

The August heat baking the asphalt outside my floor-to-ceiling glass is oppressive enough to melt a lesser executive, but inside this corner office, the AC is cranked to arctic, freezing my nerves just enough to read another pitch. Sara Paretsky is handing us *Bad Company* this November, and honestly, the market is starving for a protagonist who isn't a twenty-something influencer solving cold cases from a ring light. 

### The Pensioner Spy Trope Needs an Upgrade

We are looking at Lily Sedako—a seventy-something CIA retiree in Chicago whose quiet routine gets shattered by an anonymous package. A magazine clipping. A forgotten photo from Hayalistan, a former USSR territory where her past went catastrophically sideways back in 2002. Someone wants her exposed. Someone wants her dead. 

Wait. Let me backtrack on my previous critique about tired tropes because Paretsky actually has the gravitas to pull this off without making it look like a *Red* movie ripoff. 

Or maybe the manuscript will completely bomb because the premise relies entirely on decades-old geopolitical fallout that modern readers might need a Wikipedia tab open to comprehend, which is totally mid and a massive vibe-kill for the airport paperback demographic. 

### Geopolitics Meet Geriatric Retribution

The thematic architecture here is fascinating. Paretsky uses aging not as a quiet descent into twilight, but as an absolute flex of hard-won tradecraft. We are talking about love, systemic corruption, and brutal revenge filtered through the lens of a body that remembers every bone-jarring betrayal from Central Asia. It gives off major John le Carré energy crossed with Lee Child's calculated precision, minus the cheap testosterone. The contrast between mundane retirement tasks and clandestine Cold War residue slaps hard, honestly. 

Yet, the synopsis plays it remarkably safe with the antagonist's motivation—reducing a potentially rich geopolitical conspiracy to a vague "someone is trying to expose Lily" beat. Give me teeth. Give me moral ambiguity where the CIA looks just as dirty as the mountain tribes. 

### The Bottom Line on the Market

If Paretsky leans into the psychological toll of outliving your own classified sins, this book prints money. If she gets bogged down in bureaucratic exposition about 2002 coups, it's going to stall out faster than a tech startup in this economy. Either way, my assistant needs to fix my schedule before the next partner call, and I desperately need to find a decent dry cleaner in Manhattan who doesn't ruin custom silk shirts during a brutal summer downpour.
