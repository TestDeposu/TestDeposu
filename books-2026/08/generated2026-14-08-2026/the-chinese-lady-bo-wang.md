Velvet velvet everywhere, and not a single genuine syllable left in the parlor. They compare Bo Wang’s *The Chinese Lady* to Amy Tan’s *The Joy Luck Club* as if every piece of Asian-diaspora fiction must be packaged in the same bruised silk. How dreadfully unimaginative. Yet, I find myself tracing the edge of my Sèvres teacup, utterly seduced by the structural ambition promised here. A life fractured into titles. Little Sparrow. Julia. Afong Moy. Madame Moy. China. The nomenclature alone suggests a linguistic violence that excites the poet in me far more than any tidy chronological plot ever could. 

### The Currency of Human Curiosities

Look at the mechanics of this premise. In 1834, reduced to an oddity because of bound feet—those lotus feet molded into architectural tragedies for the male gaze. And P.T. Barnum enters the frame, because of course he does. America loves a sideshow. But the structural conceit is what demands our attention. We do not start with her voice. No. We hear her through the echoes of those who owned, named, and commodified her. A mother’s sale in Canton. A captain’s patronizing renaming in New York. The museum's dehumanizing moniker. 

Wait. Let me retract my praise for that structure. It is a tired trick. Why must we endure the chorus of oppressors before we are granted the privilege of the victim's interiority? It is an artistic indulgence that flirts too closely with the very exploitation it purports to condemn. Let her speak from page one. We do not need the white captain's wife to translate the agony for us. 

### The Weight of the Name

And yet, the promise of San Francisco’s Chinatown at the terminus offers a sharp, bitter fruit. The reclamation of a stolen identity. Wang is attempting a monumental feat—filling the silent chasms of archival history with the breath of imaginative prose. Will it soar? Or will it buckle under the heavy-handed marketing of historical trauma porn designed to win literary prizes on the backs of broken bones? 

My daughter just burst into the study to inform me that her science fair is tomorrow, not next week.
