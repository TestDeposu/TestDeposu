**Author:** Isabela Livino  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-09-01 APL

Fans of Silvia Moreno-Garcia and Gothic stalwarts like *Mexican Gothic* should pay attention to Isabela Livino's upcoming *An Immaculate Deception*, though I have serious reservations about how well this Pernambuco-set period piece will handle its psychological mechanics once the dust settles from that carriage crash. The setup is practically begging for a heavy-handed metaphor about patriarchal control, pitting a religious girl who reads Dante against a fiancé who morphs from a charming poet into a silent, omnipresent stalker overnight—a dynamic I usually adore because it mimics the suffocating feeling of watching a once-promising bond curdle into bitter resentment, yet I worry the author relies far too heavily on stale haunted-house tropes like vanishing jewelry, missing book pages, and creeping perfumes to do the heavy lifting that genuine character chemistry should be handling instead. 

Honestly, why do writers always trap pregnant protagonists in decaying mansions with creepy doctors named Lobo? 

It is completely uninspired. 

Or perhaps it is brilliant. 

Let us be entirely fair to Livino for a moment. Setting 1870s Gothic horror against the humid, Catholic-saturated backdrop of rural Brazil offers a deliciously claustrophobic texture that separates this release from the standard gloomy English moors we have all read a thousand times. And Madalena's impending motherhood—born from a terrifying ultimatum issued by her own mother—creates an immediate, desperate relational tension that makes her reliance on Leandro agonizing to witness. A grueling exercise in patience. That is what watching these two interact in the synopsis feels like, and I desperately hope the full manuscript gives them actual conversational chemistry rather than just endless staring matches in mirror-less rooms. 

Suffocating.
