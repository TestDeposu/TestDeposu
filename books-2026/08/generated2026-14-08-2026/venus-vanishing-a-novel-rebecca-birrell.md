**Author:** Rebecca Birrell  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-27 APL

Power structures love a good uniform until the people wearing it decide to pick up a paintbrush instead of a measuring tape. Rebecca Birrell’s *Venus, Vanishing* drops us straight into the chaotic, neon-drenched underbelly of late-twenties Berlin—a place where the ruling class thought they could commodify rebellion right up until the floorboards collapsed. We are looking at a narrative engine built on the friction between high society patronage and underground survival. And honestly, it is exhausting watching contemporary publishers shove every historical fiction manuscript into the exact same marketing box. Comparisons to *Portrait of a Lady on Fire* and *The Safekeep* are thrown around like confetti at a mayoral parade, as if every single novel featuring queer desire and historical surveillance needs the exact same lineage to justify its existence on a nightstand. Why can't a book just stand in the gutter on its own without needing a slick cinematic pedigree attached to its collar? 

Speaking of structural collapse, I spent three solid hours yesterday trying to explain to a municipal zoning board why converting our community garden into yet another luxury parking lot for high-tech autonomous delivery vans is a literal dystopian nightmare, only to have the head commissioner check his crypto portfolio halfway through my speech. It makes you want to tear your hair out and scream into a void of concrete and Wi-Fi signals. But back to Berlin, where the stakes actually involve fascism knocking on the door rather than just aesthetic zoning laws.

Art theft as a weapon of the marginalized always plays well in a market obsessed with institutional subversion. 

Betrayal.
