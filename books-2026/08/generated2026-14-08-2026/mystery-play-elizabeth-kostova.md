**Author:** Elizabeth Kostova  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-13 APL

Kostova's back, and it's time to see if she can recapture the lightning in a bottle that made her a household name with her debut, *The Historian*. I'm thinking 2006-esque epic, Gilded Age glamour, with dashes of horror and, most definitely, Dracula. And yeah, because we all need more of that in our lives. But let's get to the nitty-gritty, shall we? 

—The fact that Elizabeth Kostova is revisiting the Dracula lore is fascinating, but the real draw here is the new protagonist, Jay Turner. This guy's got a solid reason to be paranoid: a partially burned newspaper with a name on it, "Gael Brogan," and a package containing a creepy leather-bound book at his doorstep. And we all love a protagonist with secrets, don't we? 

It's a little too convenient that Gael Brogan is some 19th-century actor who played the part of Dracula, don't you reckon? Still, you gotta hand it to Kostova: she knows how to spin a narrative that has you hooked from the get-go. But what if she—*The Mystery Play* turns out to be just another exercise in nostalgia, another attempt to cash in on Dracula's timeless appeal? That would be a real shame, seeing how this book does feel like it's tapping into the zeitgeist. 

I mean, the whole "ancient evil and its dramatic followers" thing is, at the very least, a tantalizing setup. Think *The League of Extraordinary Gentlemen* meets a dash of *Penny Dreadful*, and you've got a solid premise on your hands. Kostova's got a lot of work ahead of her if she wants to make this book stand out, though. Let's be real, folks: the real test will come when she tries to make Gael Brogan more than just a curiosity.

### The Play's Dark Shadows

But here's the thing: *The Mystery Play* feels like it's trying too hard to be something greater than it is. This book is a thriller, yes, and a good one at that. And yeah, sure, I'd totally read a Stephen King novel about an actor who goes to far in his portrayal of Dracula. But what if this isn't just some clever rehash of the classic, what if it's something more... something more... well, that's the million-dollar question, isn't it?

### The Theater of the Absurd

Still, as a fan of *The Historian*, I won't lie: I'm a little skeptical. Kostova's got a real knack for crafting atmosphere, for making you feel like you're right there within the world she's so meticulously created. And, man, does she love her period settings. It's a lot like *The Alienist* meets *The Night Circus* with a dash of *Dracula* thrown in for good measure. Okay, so it may be a bit on the nose at times, but that's all part of the fun, isn't it?

But, let's get real, *The Mystery Play* better bring the heat. I've read it all before, from *The Historian* to *Dracula's castle* to—okay, you get the idea. This has got to be something more, more original, more *wow*. Otherwise, it's just another tired Dracula adaptation, and we've been there, done that, and gotten the t-shirt to prove it.

### The Final Curtain

I think what I'm really worried about is how much *The Mystery Play* relies on nostalgia and familiarity with the original Dracula lore. Don't get me wrong, the idea of it is solid. The problem lies in execution. Kostova's got this, I genuinely believe that. But until I see some real, tangible tension in these pages, I'll be keeping my expectations on the low side. I mean, let's face it: this is a high-risk, high-reward situation. Either this book blows me away, or I'll be the one getting a bad rap about being too cynical.
