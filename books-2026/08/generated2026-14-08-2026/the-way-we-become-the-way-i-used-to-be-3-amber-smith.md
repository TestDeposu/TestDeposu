**Author:** Amber Smith  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Look at this synopsis. Just stare at it for a second. We are staring down the barrel of another installment in *The Way I Used to Be* universe—a series that apparently refuses to die despite having wrung every possible ounce of melodramatic trauma from its protagonist like a damp dishrag—and honestly? The publisher’s marketing copy is recycling the exact same blurbs from a decade ago. Seriously. They are still leaning on a quote from *The Boston Globe* regarding a debut novel published when some of these prospective readers were still losing their baby teeth. Make it make sense, or at least pay my invoice on time so I can afford decent coffee instead of this instant cardboard sludge. 

### The Corpse-Revival Economy

We are looking at an exercise in prolonged literary purgatory. The premise relies entirely on the commodification of adolescent suffering, stretching a linear narrative into an indefinite cinematic universe of woe because the YA market loves nothing more than a protracted cry. The synopsis tells us absolutely nothing new—it literally pastes the foundational blurb of the very first book right at the top like a lazy safety blanket. Are we getting character growth, or just another three hundred pages of performative melancholy designed to farm tears on TikTok? I am leaning heavily toward the latter. Amber Smith is about to give the BookTok girlies a collective aneurysm, and the algorithm is going to eat it up like feral raccoons at an overturned dumpster. 

### The Aesthetic of Infinite Pain

The thematic trajectory is painfully obvious—no pun intended, or perhaps completely intended—because the infrastructure of trauma fiction rarely builds new rooms; it just repaints the same depressing hallway. We get the standard-issue dichotomy of before versus after, the shattered identity, the obligatory first-love wreckage. Yet, the prose in the synopsis sounds like a discarded draft from a creative writing seminar populated entirely by ghosts. Words like capsizes and buries are doing all the heavy lifting while the actual stakes remain frustratingly opaque. Give me some stylistic friction. Give me a sentence that doesn't read like a copy-pasted trigger warning with a bow on it. If I have to read one more paragraph about a girl hiding a secret in her heart like a tragic little porcelain doll, I am going to throw this laptop out the window and take up woodworking. Or crime. 

Predicting how this holds up is like guessing the expiration date on a carton of heavy cream left in a broken fridge—you know it is going to curdle, you just want to know how foul the smell gets before someone finally cleans the damn thing out. Compare it to the endless churn of *The Perks of Being a Wallflower* clones or the more recent Colleen Hoover-industrial complex; it is trauma porn wrapped in pastel typography, engineered to trigger maximum parasocial grief. Eden deserves better. I deserve better. My landlord definitely deserves better than the check that is currently bouncing while I sit here editing this sophomore-slump fanfiction masquerading as high art. 

I have to go finish painting a terribly depressing portrait of a toaster engulfed in existential dread.
