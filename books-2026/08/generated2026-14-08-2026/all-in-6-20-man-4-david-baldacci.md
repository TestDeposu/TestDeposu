**Author:** David Baldacci  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-11-10 APL

The kitchen counters are still sticky from the flour explosion we called an after-school baking session, and the humidity outside is clinging to the windowpanes like a bad habit. Yet here I am, thinking about *All In*. David Baldacci is dragging Travis Devine out of the country, straight into the glittering harbors of Australia and a storm of billion-dollar secrets. 

### Shadows in the Harbour

Power plays that could shatter the world order? Groundbreaking. We have traded the cramped federal investigations for an Australian dynasty so obscenely wealthy they make nation-states look like mom-and-pop corner stores. But let us talk about the romance angle for a second, because the synopsis hints that a love story might actually be a complete lie. And honestly? Good. Give me heartbreak wrapped in weaponized data over a predictable happily-ever-after any day of the week, especially on a sticky August evening when the AC is humming like a dying jet engine. 

Speaking of domestic catastrophes, why did the third book in that cozy baking series by the pseudonym-happy author down the street involve a gluten-free murder weapon? It makes zero sense. You cannot strangle a man with a sourdough boule unless it has been left on the counter for three weeks and hardened into a literal brick—which, come to think of it, is precisely how my eldest approaches middle school geometry homework. 

Wait. Let me rephrase that entirely. Actually, scratch the notion that a bogus romance elevates a spy thriller. Fake dating tropes belong in fluffy rom-coms with pastel covers, not in the gritty, bullet-riddled domain of the Five Eyes alliance and rogue Russian mobsters. Baldacci is throwing too many genre ingredients into the bowl here: international journalism, billionaire dynasties, compromised prisons, and a missing analyst. It risks tasting like a badly mixed batch of snickerdoodles where someone accidentally dumped salt instead of sugar. 

### The Currency of Information

Information as currency is hardly a novel concept in twenty-first-year espionage fiction—we have read this exact maneuver in half the titles cluttering the airport bookstores right next to the thriller shelves bearing Daniel Silva's name. Yet Devine remains a compellingly bruised protagonist. A former Army Ranger wading through manufactured truths and glass-and-steel canyons. Will the execution match the grandiosity of the global stakes? Probably not entirely. But we will all preorder it anyway, hoping for a sharp punch to the gut before page three hundred. 

My daughter's papier-mâché volcano science project is currently on fire in the backyard because someone decided three ounces of lighter fluid was a valid substitute for baking soda and vinegar.
