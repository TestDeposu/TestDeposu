—Another fantasy epic about a 'chosen one' with magical abilities and a penchant for saving the world, how original. The official synopsis for *Spirit Lands*, the third installment in Maiya Ibrahim's *The Spice Road Trilogy*, has me questioning the state of YA fantasy today. We're presented with Imani, a 16-year-old with the affinity for iron and a reputation for being the next great Shield, tasked with finding her supposedly dead brother who's been accused of stealing the nation's coveted spice.

And, of course, she's not going alone – she's accompanied by other Shields, including Taha, a powerful beastseer who can control the minds of falcons. It's the same old formula we've seen time and time again in YA fantasy: a young, gifted protagonist on a quest to save the world from destruction, accompanied by a cast of supporting characters with their own unique abilities. It's a tired trope, and I'm not convinced that Ibrahim's take will bring anything new to the table.

But, I suppose that's the problem with fantasy series these days – they're all starting to blend together. Imani's character, in particular, sounds like a carbon copy of the usual YA heroine: fiercely independent, yet emotionally vulnerable; skilled in combat, yet struggling with her own demons. It's a familiar character arc, and one that I'm not sure Ibrahim will be able to subvert or add anything interesting to.

One thing that does pique my interest, however, is the world-building. The hidden desert city of Qalia, with its secret spice magic and Arabian folklore-inspired setting, sounds like a rich and immersive environment. I'd love to see Ibrahim explore the intricacies of this world, delving deeper into the mythology and politics that govern it. Perhaps, if done well, this could be the book's saving grace.

Yet, I'm still skeptical. The synopsis is vague, and I'm not convinced that Ibrahim will be able to deliver on the promises she's making. The themes of family, loyalty, and power are all well-worn in YA fantasy, and I'm not sure that *Spirit Lands* will bring anything new or interesting to the table.

As I sit here, exhausted and overwhelmed by the sheer number of YA fantasy series vying for my attention, I'm left wondering if *Spirit Lands* will be worth my time. Will it be a sweeping epic, full of complex characters and moral nuance? Or will it be just another tired retread of the same old fantasy tropes? I suppose only time will tell.
