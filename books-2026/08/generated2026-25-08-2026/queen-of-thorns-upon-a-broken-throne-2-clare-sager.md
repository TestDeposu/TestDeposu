Megan Collins' latest novel, *The Heart of the Matter* is not our focus today. Instead, we have *Queen of Thorns*, the upcoming release by Clare Sager, and I'm left wondering if I'm about to dive into a twisted game of cat and mouse or a poignant exploration of the human heart.

The story revolves around Rosie Lachlan, a young woman who has received a life-saving heart transplant and is now convinced that she's meant to be with the donor's husband, Morgan Thorne. As she begins to correspond with Morgan via an anonymous service, the lines between reality and fantasy blur, and Rosie finds herself entangled in a web of obsession and deceit. But what drives this fixation? Is it the desire for a happy ending, or something more sinister?

As I ponder the intricacies of Rosie's psyche, I'm reminded of the works of Gillian Flynn and Paula Hawkins, who have mastered the art of crafting complex, flawed female protagonists. Rosie's character seems to embody the same sense of vulnerability and determination, making her a compelling and relatable figure.

The synopsis hints at a darker underbelly to Morgan's character, with rumors surrounding his late wife's death. This raises questions about the nature of truth and the reliability of narrators. Will Rosie's obsession cloud her judgment, or will she uncover the secrets that Morgan may be hiding? The tension between these two characters is palpable, even in the brief synopsis, and I'm eager to see how their relationship unfolds.
