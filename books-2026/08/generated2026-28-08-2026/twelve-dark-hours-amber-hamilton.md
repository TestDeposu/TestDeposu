**Author:** Amber Hamilton  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-11-03 APL

Amber Hamilton is back at it, dragging us right back into the corridors of Gossemort Academy with *Twelve Dark Hours*. Or at least, she will be by late next autumn. We are expected to swallow another round of dark academia romantasy, as if the publishing market hasn't already suffocated us with leather-bound mediocrity for the past four years. Right off the heels of *Seven Deadly Thorns*, we get Vesper Fontaine and Lonan Duval sharing a top spot, a dark secret, and apparently, mandatory proximity while the kingdom plays war. How original. 

### The Architecture of Compulsory Proximity

What a tired little cage for two supposed intellectual equals. The synopsis leans heavily on the classic enemies-to-lovers crutch, because heaven forbid a woman in modern fantasy get to focus on her education without a brooding rival breathing down her neck. Wait. Let me rephrase that—actually, scratch the dismissal. Maybe Hamilton is attempting something genuinely subversion-heavy with this academy turned military camp. When the state demands that students become lethal weapons of Light magic, the personal rivalry between Vesper and Lonan suddenly morphs into a much sharper critique of institutional control. 

Or perhaps it’s just another excuse for some forced nocturnal survival games. 

### The Tyranny of the Fated Mate

And then, of course, the crown prince drops in like an entitled plague to ruin everyone's solstice. He claims Vesper as a bloodmate. Oh, joy. Nothing screams radical autonomy quite like a royal stranger unilaterally assigning your biological destiny while locking the doors behind him. The synopsis frames this forced blood-tether as the ultimate catalyst for the Ridding—three ruthless trials designed to test loyalty to the Light. But let us look at the actual thematic subtext here: a patriarchal power structure literally locking women inside a building until dawn to prove their compliance through violence. 

Vesper has to choose between what she knows and what fate offers. Except, if fate is a crowned jerk who traps you in a magical cage, is it really a choice? Or is it just coercion wrapped in sparkling prose? We are supposed to care about the ticking clock, about the danger lurking around every corner. Yet, I find myself infinitely more exhausted by the sheer predictability of the romantic trapdoor waiting at the bottom of these trials. If Hamilton leans into the horror of losing one's agency to a fated bond—turning that tired romance trope into a psychological cage match—she might actually redeem the genre. If she plays it straight and has Vesper swoon while dodging monsters? Pass the hemlock. We traded the pink-covered fairy tale fluff for blood-soaked darkness, yet the female protagonist is STILL expected to swoon over her royal oppressor. Progress is a myth. 

Anyway, I have to go finish painting a terribly depressing portrait of a woman staring blankly at a locked door while her coffee goes completely cold.
