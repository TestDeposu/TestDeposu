My landlord is currently threatening eviction over eighty-four dollars in unpaid library fines, yet the publishing industrial complex expects me to consume yet another sports-romance manuscript built entirely on neurological pseudoscience. *The Rivalry Reset* by Amy Lea drops in 2026, resting its entire narrative superstructure on the cinematic absurdity of selective retrograde amnesia induced by a minor rink mishap. 

### Pathological Puck-Drop Parasomnias

Let us examine the epistemological fragility of the premise. A professional athlete takes a tumble on the ice, sustains blunt-force craniotrauma, and instantaneously metamorphoses from a misogynistic gremlin into a golden retriever-coded simp who is hopelessly infatuated with his former nemesis. Groundbreaking. Emily Henry and Ali Hazelwood must be shivering in their designer loafers. But my roommate's biophysics textbook makes it abundantly clear that localized concussions do not selectively erase interpersonal professional hostility while leaving syntax and long-term memory intact—unless the human brain is literally a wetware chalkboard that can be wiped clean with a damp sponge whenever the plot demands a manufactured enemies-to-lovers narrative arc. 

And yet, I am strangely seated. 

Actually, no, I take that back. The premise is entirely derivative trash that insults basic human neurobiology. 

### The Neurotic Physics of Modern Rom-Coms

The thematic architecture relies heavily on the worn-out trope of forced proximity mediated by corporate media obligations. Marnie Miller is presumably supposed to represent the quintessential under-appreciated working woman navigating a male-dominated broadcast ecosystem—because heaven forbid a romance novel feature a protagonist whose primary obstacle isn't institutional misogyny or a lack of basic communication skills. The text leans aggressively into the razor-thin binary between romantic revulsion and erotic fixation, a literary device that has been flogged to absolute death since the dawn of paperback fiction. 

Parker Landry’s sudden cognitive reset functions merely as a narrative cheat code. It allows the author to bypass the arduous psychological labor of genuine reconciliation by substituting genuine emotional growth with chemically induced Stockholm syndrome-lite. Instead of confronting the structural toxicity of their on-air rivalry through mature discourse, the characters are thrust into a low-stakes farce where one half of the dynamic lacks the cognitive architecture to hold a grudge. It is fundamentally lazy. 

Still, the marketing copy hits all the right algorithmic dopamine receptors. If you enjoyed the snappy banter of *Set on You*, you will likely inhale this absolute garbage fire of cognitive impossibility the second it hits shelves, completely ignoring the blatant violation of medical science just to watch two attractive fictional people yell at each other before inevitably kissing in a maintenance closet. 

My upstairs neighbor has decided 2:00 AM is the optimal time to practice the Bach Cello Suites, and the rhythmic scraping of the bow against the strings is completely destroying my ability to parse further literary pretense.
