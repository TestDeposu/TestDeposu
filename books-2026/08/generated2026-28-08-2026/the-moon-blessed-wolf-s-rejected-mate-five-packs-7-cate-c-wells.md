God forbid someone chop these bell peppers into uniform strips while I sweat through a sleeveless shirt in this stifling August humidity, because apparently, I also need to sit here and psychoanalyze Cate C. Wells and her relentless obsession with canine shifter mating dynamics. *The Moon Blessed Wolf's Rejected Mate*—or *The Stone Wolf's Rejected Mate*, seeing as the synopsis can't even decide on its own goddamn mineral classification for the hero—promises us the exact same brand of industrial-grade emotional devastation we have all mainlined since Ruby Dixon and J.R. Ward figured out how to monetize pheromones and daddy issues. We are looking at North Border, which sounds less like a functional pack territory and more like a dilapidated coal-mining town where everyone smells like pine needles and repressed trauma, and frankly, I am exhausted just thinking about the labor required to unpack this power dynamic. The setup is practically dripping with structural irony: our nameless heroine is sneaking around after shift work for illicit snogs with a guy whose wolf is apparently asleep at the wheel because he hasn't even triggered her heat yet, which raises serious physiological questions about how biology works in these five packs. And then—classic genre choreography—they get caught, he panics like a cowardly suburban dad caught cheating at a PTA mixer, he publicly disowns her in front of the entire community just to save face, and she is supposedly destroyed. Groundbreaking. Truly a sociological masterpiece of internal misogyny and institutional pack politics. Tell that to someone who cares. Wait, actually, let me walk that back entirely because Wells usually delivers on the actual mechanics of groveling, and if Clay Pulley doesn't spend at least seventy percent of this novella weeping on the floor of a damp cabin begging for forgiveness while society judges him, I am going to throw these chopped bell peppers directly at the drywall. 

Trash.

### The Geography of Bad Decisions

No, wait, let me actually look at the structural implications of this textually vague blurb. Wells is trading on the exact same bruised-ego real estate that made *The Stone Wolf* tick, leaning hard into the tired myth that biological inevitability always trumps free will, even though the whole point of a rejected mate trope is watching a stubborn alpha realize he is entirely replaceable until he proves otherwise. 

Oh, damn it. 

The superglue is completely dried up inside the neck of the ceramic vase on the mantelpiece, and if I don't wedge these jagged shards back together and wipe away the epoxy residue before anyone walks through that front door, I am going to have to spend the rest of the evening listening to a very loud, very annoying interrogation about why I am so clumsy with inherited pottery during the worst heat wave of the summer.
