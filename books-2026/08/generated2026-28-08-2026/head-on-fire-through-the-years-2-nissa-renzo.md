**Author:** Nissa Renzo  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

The synopsis for *Head on Fire*—the sequel to whatever *Through the Years* was—is so deliberately vague it might as well be a haiku written by a sleep-deprived intern. "Following Scottie and Stan" is the kind of line that could apply to a dozen different genres, from a grimy, character-driven drama to a YA rom-com where the emotional stakes are about as deep as a TikTok comment section. But since we’re dealing with a BookTok sensation, the safest bet is that this is going to be another one of those books where the emotional labor is outsourced to the reader, and the payoff is a slow-burning, cathartic mess that feels like crying into a pillow while scrolling through old photos.

The first book in the series—*Through the Years*—was presumably the kind of thing that made people weep in public restrooms, the kind of story where the emotional core is so tightly wound that it could snap like a rubber band. If that’s the case, then *Head on Fire* is either going to be a masterclass in escalation or a slow-motion train wreck of unresolved tension. The title itself is a red flag—it’s the kind of phrase that could mean anything: a literal fever dream, a metaphor for emotional turmoil, or just a really dramatic way of saying "the sequel is going to be intense." But given the author’s BookTok pedigree, the intensity is likely to be of the *It Ends with Us* variety—where the stakes are personal, the pacing is deliberate, and the reader is left with the distinct impression that they’ve just been emotionally manipulated into caring.

The synopsis doesn’t even attempt to hint at the thematic throughline, which is either a sign of confidence or a sign that the publisher is too afraid to commit to anything. Is this about grief? Is it about the passage of time? Is it about two people who are so deeply entangled that their lives feel like a single, shared narrative? Or is it just another installment in the "slow-burn enemies-to-lovers" trend that’s been drowning the market for the past decade? The lack of specificity is frustrating, but then again, maybe that’s the point—maybe the entire book is about the ambiguity of memory, the way time distorts our perceptions, and how we cling to the past like it’s a lifeline. If so, then it’s already playing into the same themes as *The Time Traveler’s Wife* or *A Little Life*, but with the emotional rawness of something like *The Song of Achilles*—if Achilles had been a queer, emotionally stunted mess who still made you cry.

But here’s the thing: the synopsis doesn’t even *try* to sell the book. It doesn’t promise anything. It doesn’t even give us a sense of tone. Is this going to be dark and brooding, like *The Fault in Our Stars* but with more existential dread? Or is it going to be lighter, more hopeful, like *Red, White & Royal Blue* but with a grieving protagonist? The vagueness is almost intentional, as if the author is banking on the fact that readers will fill in the blanks with their own expectations. And in a market where emotional storytelling is king, that might just work.

Yet—here’s where I have to pivot—maybe the lack of specificity is a feature, not a bug. Maybe the entire point of *Head on Fire* is to be a book that feels like a letter from a friend, raw and unfiltered, where the emotional beats are left to the reader’s imagination. If that’s the case, then it’s already ahead of the curve, playing into the same kind of intimate, confessional storytelling that’s been dominating BookTok for years. But then again, if it’s just another installment in the "slow-burn emotional wreck" genre, it might as well be a rehash of *The Love Hypothesis* but with more angst.

The real question is whether this sequel will live up to the hype—or if it’s just going to be another book that gets lost in the shuffle. Given the author’s track record, I’d wager on the former, but only because the alternative is too depressing to consider. Either way, if it’s half as good as the first book, it’s going to be a mess of feelings, and that’s exactly what the market wants right now.
