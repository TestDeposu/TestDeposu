**Author:** Shauna Lawless  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-09-10 APL

Isolde is wandering into the stronghold of the Descendants, which sounds suspiciously like every other "chosen one" arrival trope I’ve had to choke down this year. *Son of the Serpent*—the latest from Shauna Lawless—promises us magical gifts, visions of fire, and the usual existential dread of being an immortal who just can’t seem to stay out of historical wreckage. Why do they always have visions? Why can’t they just have a nice, quiet hobby like knitting or taxidermy? Honestly, if I have to read one more synopsis about a character learning to harness their hidden powers while everything burns, I might actually lose my mind. 

The synopsis leaves quite a bit to the imagination, doesn't it? It’s a series of breathless promises—kings losing crowns, Anglo-Norman lords, and vague threats of destruction. It feels like a template dragged and dropped into medieval Ireland. But I suppose we’re here for the atmosphere. Lawless clearly has a knack for that, if the blurbs from Anthony Ryan and the *Irish Independent* are to be believed. She’s leaning into that *The Last Kingdom* grit, which is fine, I suppose, if you enjoy watching people lose their heads for the sake of a border dispute. 

### The Burden of Being Prettily Doomed

Cuan, that poor, cursed nephew of the High King, is busy playing the part of the tortured warrior. Revenge? Check. A mysterious stranger offering a "new path"? A classic literary bait-and-switch. I’m betting this stranger is either a complete fraud or a deity with a terrible sense of humor. The trope of the "warrior so close to the king" being in danger is as old as the hills—or as old as the crumbling plaster on my ceiling. It’s supposed to feel high-stakes. Yet, I’m just staring at my word count, wondering if Cuan is actually going to be interesting or just another brooding silhouette against a sunset. 

I’m being forced to churn out this critique for a book that hasn't even hit the shelves because the machine demands constant anticipation. Is this art? Or is this just a frantic scramble to stay relevant in a market that devours authors whole? I suspect *Son of the Serpent* will be perfectly adequate. It will satisfy the readers who want their historical fiction with a side of supernatural spice. It will likely sell well enough to keep Lawless in ink for another cycle. 

But I crave something messier. I want a protagonist who makes a genuinely stupid mistake and doesn't have a prophecy to justify it. Instead, we have "working together or risking destruction." A universal ultimatum. How refreshingly dull. 

The radiator is clanking again, sounding like a disgruntled ghost trapped in the pipes, and I truly wish it would either fix itself or just explode so I can finally stop typing.
