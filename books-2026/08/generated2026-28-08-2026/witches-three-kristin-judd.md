**Author:** Kristin Judd  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Chewing on a lukewarm turkey sandwich while the guy two cubes down aggressively clears his throat into a napkin is no way to process historical trauma. Yet here we are. Kristin Judd’s upcoming *Witches Three* lands on shelves in 2026, and the official synopsis already has me spiraling faster than the office copier on a Monday morning. 

### Shadows Across the Centuries

Three women. Three distinct epochs of absolute, unadulterated dread. Mary Hatley in 1650 London, Clara Brooks fleeing the Salem madness in 1692, and July Hathaway hiding out on a dreary Pacific Northwest island in 1959. 

And why does every single historical fiction author insist on stretching a family curse across three distinct timelines like cheap taffy? It is exhausting. We get it. Patriarchy bad, herbs good, historical persecution leaves a mark. I am just so tired of books relying on the exact same structural gimmick popularized by Kate Quinn or half the Reese’s Book Club picks from 2021. Give me a single, claustrophobic narrative that actually breathes instead of constantly jumping eras just when things are getting mildly interesting. Mary’s apothecary shop in London sounds fascinating—until the book abruptly rips us away to colonial Massachusetts to deal with more puritanical shouting. 

### The Weight of Unresolved Angst

Yet I will read it. Every single page. Because the emotional stakes outlined in this blurb hit too close to home when you are staring down an Inbox overflowing with passive-aggressive spreadsheet requests. 

The synopsis promises a legacy that is both a curse and a gift. Groundbreaking. Truly, nobody has ever written about the heavy burden of motherhood intersecting with societal rejection before. But Judd’s focus on the isolation of these outcasts—from Mary escaping her childhood farm to July trapped as an unwed mother in the fifties—taps into a very specific brand of fury. It is the fury of being cornered by systems designed to crush you. 

Speaking of systems designed to crush you, why did whoever designed this office layout place the microwave directly adjacent to the ventilation intake for the men's room? It defies basic human decency, much like the entire premise of the Salem witch trials. We are expected to just sit here and inhale lukewarm electromagnetic waves while contemplating the historical oppression of women healers across four centuries. 

At least July gets a remote island. I would trade my entire 401k for a remote island right now, preferably one completely devoid of coworkers who chew with their mouths open. 

Ultimately, *Witches Three* is going to break my heart. It will undoubtedly lean too hard into lyrical prose—that dangerous publisher buzzword that usually means five pages describing a fern—and leave the actual mechanics of the plot hanging. But I am a sucker for stories about women who refuse to stay in their designated historical boxes. 

Now if you will excuse me, I need to go find where the dog hid my only good pair of dress shoes.
