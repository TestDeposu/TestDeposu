**Author:** L.L. Starling  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Running from corporate espionage is so 2012, giving heavy *Fyre Festival* energy before the tents even arrived. Yet here we sit, clutching our unblemished first-edition hardcovers while the literary world holds its collective breath over L.L. Starling’s upcoming *Otherworld (The Chronicles of Between, #2)*. A masterclass in tension. The official synopsis drops Sam and Declan right back into the bureaucratic meat-grinder they fought so desperately to escape. 

### The Aesthetics of Bureaucratic Carnage

Blood on the linoleum. Missing superiors. A sinister shadowy cabal named after the alphabet because apparently, original nomenclature died in the eighties. The premise trades pastoral whimsy for a locked-room massacre, pushing our erstwhile field agents from globetrotting operatives into reluctant corporate sleuths. 

And what a delectable pivot. 

Gone are the breezy operational logistics. Instead, we are promised a psychological autopsying of Sir James Aiken's past. Who authorized the Alphabet Club? More importantly, why do we care when the pacing looks entirely unhinged? This is elite-tier gaslighting wrapped in a hardback. One moment you are negotiating retirement packages with a stolen memory key; the next, you are stepping over bodies in London HQ like you just caught a stray L train at rush hour. 

### The Ontology of Coercion

Power dynamics in speculative fiction rarely taste this bitter. Starling strips away the glamour of agency work—literally—leaving Sam and Declan shackled to a corpse-strewn desk by pure bureaucratic hostage-taking. They cannot leave. They will not leave. They must interrogate their own institutional Stockholm syndrome while digging through the dusty filing cabinets of a man who clearly deserved a treason charge and a shallow grave. 

Yet the real artistic triumph here lies in the central ethical rot. Do they want to save Sir James? Do any of us? It is giving massive structural rot with a side of absolute yikes. The thematic weight rests entirely on this delicious ambiguity. Loyalty forged in the furnace of corporate exploitation always leaves third-degree burns. 

One worries the plot might drown in its own acronyms. Secret societies named after letters feel awfully close to a mid-tier airport thriller found crumpled in a VIP lounge at Heathrow. But if Starling maintains the razor-sharp character dynamics of the previous installment, this sophomore outing will effortlessly transcend the genre gutter. 

I simply must go finalize the imported delphiniums and the string quartet repertoire for my upcoming book launch.
