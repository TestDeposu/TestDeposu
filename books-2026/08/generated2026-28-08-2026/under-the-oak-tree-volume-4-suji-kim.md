Flour is currently dusting the kitchen counter, and my eight-year-old has just tried to smuggle a raw chocolate chip past my left ear. Yet my mind remains entirely trapped in the Duchy of Croyso. Suji Kim is giving us the backstory we have all begged for in *Under the Oak Tree: Volume 4*, and I refuse to care about the rising dough for at least ten more minutes. 

### The Mud and the Smithy

We are looking at forty-four side-story chapters plus three bonus drops. And honestly? This collection of origins is going to completely ruin standard romantic fantasy for anyone who reads it. Forget the polished courts of Sarah J. Maas or the sweeping, high-altitude politics of *Fourth Wing*. Riftan Calypse starts his journey in absolute squalor. A bruised peasant boy sweating over a blacksmith’s anvil, listening to a wretched stepfather, haunted entirely by the red-haired daughter of a duke. 

Wait. Let me rephrase that last bit entirely. Actually, I take it back. The synopsis makes it sound like a classic, sweeping lower-class-hero-saves-the-day trope, but knowing Suji Kim’s psychological depth, this isn't going to be a clean upward trajectory. It’s going to be brutal. It will likely be uncomfortable. 

### Scars Woven Into Armor

A grub should keep to the ground. That single, cruel sentence defines the entire architecture of Riftan’s youth. But pain is a brilliant motivator. And becoming the continent's mightiest hero doesn't happen just because a boy decides to sneak onto a mercenary wagon. It requires a slow, agonizing erasure of self. The Remdragon Knights aren't just an unorthodox order; they are an anvil. And Riftan is the iron taking every single blow. 

The thematic core here relies on obsession masking as quiet devotion. Is it romantic? Absolutely not. It borders on a sickness. And that is precisely why we are all pre-ordering it. We crave the ugly, desperate edges of human attachment—the kind that makes a man walk through literal armies just to prove he is worthy of standing in the same sunlight as a red-haired noblewoman who barely knows he exists. 

Give me more gritty, lowborn grit over pristine fantasy lords any day of the week. Kim understands that true power is forged in the dirt, not handed down by bloodlines. 

Oh, wonderful. That distinctive sound of shattering ceramic just echoed from the pantry, and I am entirely certain the toddler has found the mixing bowls.
