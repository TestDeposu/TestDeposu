### Asphalt, Trauma, and Excess Baggage

K.D. Edwards is giving us an American road trip in *The Misfit Caravan*, and I am already preemptively exhausted by the logistics. Packing two luxury RVs for a bunch of ancient, traumatized magical beings sounds less like a healing journey and more like a municipal nightmare that needs an immediate zoning permit and a community board intervention. We are looking at a narrative engine built entirely on forced proximity and high-horsepower anxiety. It reminds me of why municipal budget meetings always devolve into shouting matches over parking ordinances—everyone brings their historical grievances to the asphalt, and nobody wants to empty the grey water tank. 

### The Illusion of Found Kinship

The synopsis leans heavily on the architecture of chosen family. Because nothing screams domestic bliss quite like fleeing a trail of murdered fae while towing a multi-ton recreational vehicle across state lines. Let us be entirely serious for a moment. This genre has become hopelessly obsessed with the "found family heals all wounds" trope to an almost comical degree. It is like watching a poorly managed community garden where everyone insists on planting invasive perennials right next to the prize-winning tomatoes because they think shared suffering constitutes a root system. 

### The Infrastructure of Betrayal

Rune Saint John is apparently heading straight for Sanctuary City to uncover lies stitched into the lives of the people he loves most. Groundbreaking. Nothing like discovering that your domestic safety net is actually a poorly woven hammock held together by administrative negligence and buried secrets. I predict the middle three hundred pages of this book will consist entirely of brooding in cramped dining nooks while someone refuses to pass the salt because of a centuries-old blood feud. It is practically a suburban HOA meeting with better casting and higher body counts.
