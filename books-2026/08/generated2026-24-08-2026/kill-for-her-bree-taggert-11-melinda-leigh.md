**Author:** Melinda Leigh  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-07-05 APL

The discovery of human remains on the long-abandoned Taggert family farm – a gruesome reminder of the dark legacy that haunts Sheriff Bree Taggert. In *Kill For Her*, Melinda Leigh weaves an intricate narrative that threatens to upend Bree's carefully constructed reality, forcing her to confront the brutal night that has haunted her for most of her life.

The echoes of her father's heinous crime – the murder of her mother, followed by his own demise – still linger, a constant reminder of the trauma that Bree has struggled to keep at bay. And now, with the unearthing of two more sets of remains, the past comes flooding back. The investigation, which determines that the murders took place thirty years prior, throws Bree's father into the spotlight as a suspect, compelling her to re-examine the events of that fateful night. But it's not just her father's actions that come under scrutiny – an unlikely occupant of the Taggert farm, who claims to possess secrets about Bree's past, becomes an improbable suspect. Yet, as he vanishes into thin air, and Bree's niece is kidnapped, the stakes are raised, and the old case is rekindled.

It's a cat-and-mouse game, with Bree seemingly having antagonized a ruthless killer, who will stop at nothing to keep their identity and motivations hidden. The parallels to Laura Lippman's *What the Dead Know* are striking – both novels featuring a complex web of secrets and lies, with the past threatening to destroy the present. But while Lippman's work is marked by a more nuanced exploration of the human psyche, Leigh's writing often veers into the realm of the sensational. The result is a narrative that feels more akin to a jigsaw puzzle, with pieces being fitted together to create a larger picture, rather than a thoughtful exploration of the characters' inner lives.

And yet, it's this very sensationalism that might just prove to be the novel's greatest strength. In an era where true-crime podcasts have become the norm, *Kill For Her* taps into our collective fascination with the darker aspects of human nature. It's a thriller that promises to keep readers on the edge of their seats, even as it raises questions about the reliability of memory and the devastating consequences of trauma.

But what of the supporting cast? How will Adam, Bree's brother, fare in the midst of this chaos? And what secrets might the occupant of the Taggert farm have been hiding? The synopsis, unfortunately, remains tight-lipped on these matters, leaving us to wonder if Leigh has done enough to flesh out her characters beyond their surface-level motivations. The plot, too, feels somewhat predictable, with the kidnapping of Bree's niece serving as a contrived plot twist.

Still, there's something to be said for a narrative that wears its heart on its sleeve, even if that heart is a bit of a mess. *Kill For Her* is a novel that promises to deliver on its thrills, even if it doesn't quite live up to its full potential. It's a bit like the Hindenburg disaster – a spectacle that's equal parts captivating and terrifying, with the potential to leave readers scarred, but ultimately, it's a wild ride that's worth taking.
