Staring out at the tarmac from the first-class lounge while nursing a remarkably overpriced espresso, I find myself in a bizarrely charitable mood. Perhaps it is the altitude. Or perhaps the literary gods have finally smiled upon us. Matthew Stewart is back with *Everything You Need to Know: How Philosophy Can Set You Free*, and frankly, the intellectual zeitgeist desperately needs a swift kick in the backside. 

### The Iconoclasm We Deserve

Let us be entirely honest. Modern philosophy has become a thoroughly neutered academic circle-jerk. We are drowning in high-impact journals that nobody reads and tenure-track navel-gazing. But Stewart? He treats the grand lineage of human thought like a bar fight at 2:00 AM—messy, vital, and aggressively alive. 

And thank goodness he is finally knocking Plato off his insufferable pedestal. For centuries, we have groveled at the feet of ancient Greek elitism as if the Forms were handed down on stone tablets by some cosmic hall monitor. Descartes? Total fraud with a clever marketing campaign. Bring on the underdogs. Giving Giordano Bruno and Pierre Bayle their rightful spotlight is the kind of historiographical flex that makes my cold heart flutter. 

### Breaking Out of the Ivory Tower

Philosophy is not some exclusive country club for people with tweed jackets and crippling emotional detachment. It is instinctual. It is as raw as a cat licking its own fur—metaphorically speaking, of course. 

Stewart dares to pitch the discipline not as an accumulation of dusty facts, but as an active, disruptive practice. Like starting a revolution or making aggressive noise in a quiet library. The synopsis promises a global, prehistoric-to-present sweep condensed into a mere six chapters. Is that hopelessly ambitious? Absolutely. Will it piss off traditionalists clinging to their canonized hierarchies? God, I hope so. We need more literary terrorism in the intellectual sphere. Think *A Little History of the World* meets a turbocharged punk manifesto, written by someone who actually survived graduate school without losing their soul. 

### The Global Shuffle

Weaving Eastern and Western traditions into a singular narrative without sounding like a pretentious yoga retreat brochure is a Herculean task. Can Stewart pull it off across metaphysics, epistemology, and political philosophy in a handful of pages? Who cares if the execution gets a bit sloppy in the middle chapters; the sheer audacity of the framing is breathtaking. We are talking about a total ideological glow-up for a subject that academia tried its hardest to bury under footnotes. 

Expect the Twitterati to clutch their pearls over the irreverence. Expect the old guard to sneer at the funny illustrations. But for those of us exhausted by the sheer mediocrity of contemporary public discourse, this text looks like a lethal dose of adrenaline. It is fiercely iconoclastic. It is delightfully unhinged. 

I must run. I have to go select the artwork for the lobby of our new corporate headquarters.
