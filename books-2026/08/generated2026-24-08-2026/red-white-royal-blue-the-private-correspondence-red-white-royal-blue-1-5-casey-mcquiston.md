What's next for the saccharine power couple of *Red, White & Royal Blue*? The official synopsis of Casey McQuiston's upcoming novella, *Red, White & Royal Blue: The Private Correspondence*, reads like a glossy promotional pamphlet, touting "gorgeous designed edges" and "floral endpapers" as if the very aesthetic of the book is what will propel readers through its pages — but we know better. We're here for the dirty laundry, the whispered secrets, and the occasional bout of existential dread that can only come from being in love with a prince.

And what better way to excavate these tender moments than through the intimate, handwritten correspondence between Alex Claremont-Diaz and Prince Henry? It's a clever move, one that recalls the epistolary charm of *The Color Purple* or *Dracula*, but with the added bonus of being able to fetishize the physical act of writing itself. Who needs the cold glow of a screen when you can have the tactile thrill of paper and ink?

But let's not get ahead of ourselves. The real question on everyone's mind is: what's the narrative arc of this novella? Are we getting a straightforward, chronological retelling of Alex and Henry's relationship, or is this going to be a more fragmented, impressionistic exploration of their inner lives? The synopsis hints at a "twenty-year" span, which suggests that we might be in for a more sprawling, epic treatment of their love story.

One thing's for certain: Casey McQuiston is playing to her strengths here. The *Red, White & Royal Blue* series has always been about the performative aspects of love and identity, and what better way to explore those themes than through the carefully curated, deeply personal letters that Alex and Henry exchange? It's a chance for McQuiston to dig deeper into the psychological nuances of her characters, to excavate the hidden vulnerabilities and desires that make them tick.

And yet, despite the promise of this setup, I have thoughts. Most of them bad. Specifically, I'm worried that this novella might be too focused on the aesthetic trappings of its epistolary format, rather than the actual emotional depth of its characters. Will we get a genuine exploration of Alex and Henry's inner lives, or will this just be a series of cute, superficial missives strung together with a few well-placed sentimental moments? Only time will tell.

Still, I'm willing to give McQuiston the benefit of the doubt. After all, she's proven herself to be a master of the romance genre, and her fans will undoubtedly devour this latest installment with gusto. And who knows? Maybe *Red, White & Royal Blue: The Private Correspondence* will surprise us all, revealing a more nuanced, more vulnerable side to Alex and Henry's relationship. Maybe it'll be a game-changer for the series, a bold new direction that will leave readers breathless.

Or maybe it'll just be a bunch of sappy love letters. Either way, I'm here for it.
