**Author:** Alex T. Singer  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-20 APL

What's the real horror here - the angels, or humanity's insatiable greed? *Angel Country* promises a darkly wondrous cosmic horror tale that will leave readers questioning the true nature of monstrosity.

In this alternate early 1900s, scientist Ada Franklin finds herself resurrected in her own grave, her lab abandoned, and her research partner gone - along with her life's work. The prototype designed to enable human-angel communication has the potential to reshape the world, and whoever controls it may gain the power not only to communicate with angels, but to command them. It's a tantalizing prospect, and one that raises more questions than answers.

But what's truly terrifying - the angels themselves, or the way humanity responds to them? The synopsis hints at a world warped by celestial horror, where angelic contact remakes both flesh and mind. It's a classic trope, one that's been explored in everything from *The Starving Saints* to *Ring Shout*. Yet, there's something about the way *Angel Country* approaches this idea that feels fresh, that feels...off.

Perhaps it's the way the story seems to blur the lines between good and evil. Ada's pursuit of her former research partner is driven by a desire to reclaim her work, but as she gets closer to her goal, she begins to suspect that humanity's greed may be the true horror. It's a morally complex tale, one that asks difficult questions about the nature of power and the human condition.

But can *Angel Country* deliver on its promises? The synopsis is tantalizing, but it's also frustratingly vague. What does this world look like, exactly? How do the angels interact with humanity, and what are the consequences of their contact? The answers to these questions will make or break the story.

And then, of course, there's the matter of Ada herself. What drives her? What motivates her to pursue her former partner, even in the face of unimaginable horror? Is she a hero, or a villain? The synopsis doesn't give us much to go on, but I suspect that Ada's character will be a major part of the story's appeal.

As I sit here, pondering the possibilities, I find myself drawn to the work of authors like Jeff VanderMeer and China Miéville. Their ability to blend the surreal and the terrifying is unmatched, and I suspect that *Angel Country* will be similarly unsettling. But will it be more than just a fever dream mash-up of alternate history, dark fantasy, and eldritch horror? Only time will tell.
