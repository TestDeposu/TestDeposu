**Author:** Ann Liang  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

A collection of 29 stories from 30 authors, all centered around the theme of choice - how delightfully vague. The Lost Life of Sylvia Song, a title that promises mystery, intrigue, and a dash of the unknown. And yet, the synopsis reads like a laundry list of disparate story ideas, each one more intriguing than the last, but ultimately leaving me with more questions than answers.

What exactly does it mean to explore the choices made under "ordinary and extraordinary circumstances"? Are we talking about the everyday decisions that shape our lives, or the grand, sweeping choices that define us as individuals? And what of the authors' visions - are they united in their exploration of this theme, or do they diverge wildly, like a flock of birds scattering in different directions?

I'm reminded of the great Jorge Luis Borges, who once wrote, "The only possible morality is to accept the universe." But what does that mean, exactly, in the context of this anthology? Are we being asked to accept the universe, or to challenge it, to push against the boundaries of what's possible?

The stories themselves seem to span a wide range of genres and styles - from the humorous to the paranormal, the bittersweet to the retellings of classic fairy tales. It's a veritable smorgasbord of literary delights, and yet, I'm left wondering if the whole is greater than the sum of its parts.

But, of course, that's the thing about anthologies - they're always a bit of a crapshoot. Will the disparate voices and styles come together to create something cohesive, something greater than the sum of its parts? Or will it all fall apart, like a house of cards in a gust of wind?

I'm thinking of Italo Calvino's Cosmicomics, a collection of short stories that explored the origins of the universe. Like The Lost Life of Sylvia Song, it was a wildly inventive, genre-bending work that defied easy categorization. But whereas Calvino's stories were united by a shared sense of wonder, a sense of awe at the vastness of the universe, I'm not entirely sure what unites the stories in this anthology.

And yet, I'm drawn to it, like a moth to flame. There's something about the sheer audacity of this project, the willingness to take risks and push boundaries, that's undeniably compelling. I'm reminded of the great avant-garde movements of the early 20th century, the Dadaists and the Surrealists, who sought to challenge the very notion of what art could be.

So, I'll take a chance on The Lost Life of Sylvia Song. I'll dive in, headfirst, and see where it takes me. And if it all falls apart, well, that's just part of the fun.
