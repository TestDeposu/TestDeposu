**Author:** Andie Burke  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-09-22 APL

Outside the panoramic windows of this sleek, high-speed train, the August sun bakes the golden fields of the European countryside into a shimmering mirage of heat. Across the laminate table, my children are aggressively kneading a sticky lump of sourdough dough—our current mobile baking experiment is testing my patience—while my mind wanders far from our destination. I am pondering Andie Burke’s upcoming novel, *So It Grows*. Fascinating in theory. A cozy small-town business-rivals romance about plant shops and mac and cheese restaurants. But what does it truly tell us about the human condition under capitalism? Hardly a simple question. 

Let us dissect the premise. Felicity inherits a botanical sanctuary, only to be ambushed by a trendy, carbohydrate-heavy culinary neighbor named Nicolas. And what a delightfully absurd juxtaposition—delicate greenery versus glorious, melted cheese. Yet beneath the culinary kitsch lies a profound existential battle. A greedy developer threatens their sunlight with a towering parking garage. Architectural tyranny in a quirky Kansas corner. But is this merely a localized squabble over photons, or an allegory for the relentless march of modern gentrification? Time will tell. Or perhaps it is just an excuse to smash two stubborn egos together until sparks fly. 

Burke clearly leans into the classic enemies-to-lovers framework, a literary dynamic that depends entirely on sharp banter and simmering tension. Yet the synopsis drops heavy hints about hidden battle scars and old secrets. A dangerous gamble for a cozy narrative. Does the romance buckle under the weight of contrived melodrama, or does it genuinely earn its emotional catharsis? One worries the plot mechanics might get bogged down by the third act, much like my children's over-worked dough. But if anyone can balance heat and heart without tipping into saccharine exhaustion, it is a *USA Today* bestselling author. Think Emily Henry meets *Book Lovers*, but with chlorophyll and pasta. 

The thematic core here revolves around roots—both literal and psychological. Felicity wants to plant them, while the impending storm and old ghosts threaten to uproot everything. A neat metaphor, albeit a trifle obvious. But romance readers do not crave avant-garde subtlety; they crave the delicious agony of two obstinate people realizing they are looking at their own future. Will they cut and run? A tired trope, frankly. Yet I find myself strangely invested in how Burke handles the fallout of the developer's plotline. 

As the train hurtles past another sun-drenched vineyard, and my youngest successfully flours the entire tray table, I remain cautiously optimistic. Will *So It Grows* redefine the contemporary romance landscape, or is it merely fast-food fiction dressed up in botanical drag?
