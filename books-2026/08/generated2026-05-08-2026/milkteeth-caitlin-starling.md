**Author:** Caitlin Starling  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-20 APL

Fascinating in theory, the prospect of a vampire broodmother struggling to control her hunger in *Milkteeth*, Caitlin Starling's upcoming novel, has all the makings of a captivating tale. The notion of a world governed by secrecy, where even the smallest revelation can prove fatal, raises the stakes and promises intense suspense.

As I sit here, trying to stay focused on the sweltering heat outside, I find my mind wandering to the parallels between *Milkteeth* and other works in the vampire fiction genre, such as *The Passage* by Justin Cronin or *The Strain* by Guillermo del Toro. The use of a maternal figure, in this case, the broodmother Beatrice, adds a layer of complexity to the narrative, highlighting the tension between nurturing and destruction.

The synopsis hints at a struggle to reconcile Beatrice's vampiric nature with her maternal instincts—A delicate balancing act, indeed. But what if this inner conflict ultimately proves to be a weakness, rather than a strength? Perhaps it's a clever ruse, used to lull the reader into a false sense of security, only to be subverted by the author's true intentions.

We're told that Beatrice's world will be disrupted by the addition of a third, hungrier child. But how will the introduction of this new character impact her existing relationships? Will it be a catalyst for growth, or a further source of tension within the brood? The possibilities are endless, and it's exhilarating to consider the ways in which Starling might choose to explore this narrative thread.

Yet, the reliance on vampire lore and the 'mothers' who are both nurturing and monstrous echoes the problematic dynamics found in certain works of feminist horror. Will *Milkteeth* subvert or reinforce these tropes? Hardly. Only time will tell.

Beatrice's decision to seek the aid of a mortal OBGYN creates an interesting dynamic, pitting her vampiric existence against the scientific understanding of the human world. This tension will likely serve to further complicate her relationships with her children, as well as the broader vampiric community. A fragile web of secrets is at play, and one misstep could spell disaster.

Time will tell if Caitlin Starling has crafted a nuanced exploration of maternal horror or merely capitalized on tired tropes. I'm predicting a darker, more complex take on the vampire genre, comparable to the work of Tananarive Due or N.K. Jemisin. But we'll have to wait until October 20th to find out.

As the air conditioning struggles to keep up with the sweltering heat, I find my own sense of unease growing. Will *Milkteeth* be a slow burn, building tension incrementally, or a faster-paced ride, full of twists and turns? And what secrets lies hidden in the depths of Beatrice's world?
