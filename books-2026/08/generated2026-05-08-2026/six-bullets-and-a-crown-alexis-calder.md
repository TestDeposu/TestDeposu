**Author:** Alexis Calder  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Oh, *Six Bullets and a Crown*—because nothing says "summer reading" like a desert oasis built on the bones of a dead god, a cult that wants to resurrect it, and two protagonists who are basically the literary equivalent of a Tinder date gone wrong—one’s a wide-eyed amateur, the other a gunslinger whose magic revolvers make him sound like a character from a *John Wick* fanfic written by a guy who’s never held a gun. And yet, here we are, because the synopsis is so broad it could be describing *any* romantasy with a dash of Western grit and a side of "oh no, the magic is cursed."

Let’s start with the *setting*—Serpent City, a desert oasis built on the bones of a dead god. Hardly. This is the kind of vague, atmospheric setup that makes you wonder if the author’s ever actually been to a desert, or if they just Googled "spooky city" and hit "I'm feeling lucky." The bones of a dead god? That’s a hell of a foundation for a city, but also, how do you even *build* on that? Do they just stack the ribs like Lego? And why is it an oasis? Is the god’s corpse hydrating? The synopsis doesn’t say, and that’s the problem—it’s so abstract it could be describing *any* fantasy world where the stakes are "big magic" and the city is "weirdly picturesque." Where’s the grit? Where’s the *dirt*? Because if we’re talking desert, we should at least get some sand in our teeth, some heat haze, some characters who aren’t just wandering around like they’re in a *Disney* movie.

Now, the *characters*—Ever Kaid, whose father is kidnapped by a cult, and Ford Sterling, a gunslinger with god-touched revolvers. Oh, *god-touched revolvers*—because nothing says "romantasy" like a man with guns that shoot holy lead. This is the kind of thing that makes you think of *The Witcher* meets *Django Unchained*, but with less actual Django and more "let’s pretend this is a Western but with magic." Ford’s magic revolvers make him invincible, yet also "may spell his demise"—because of course they do. That’s the whole point of magic in these stories, isn’t it? It’s a double-edged sword, like a romance that’s doomed from the start. And Ever? She’s the classic "chosen one" who has to solve her father’s clues to stop the ritual. But here’s the thing—if the synopsis is to be believed, Ever isn’t the only one after the crown. So why is she the focus? Is this a "chosen one" story, or is it a "two people who don’t want to be together but have to save the world" story? The synopsis doesn’t say, and that’s the problem—it’s so vague it could be describing *any* duology where the love interest is a brooding gunslinger and the protagonist is a girl with a mystery to solve.

And then there’s the *themes*—trust, betrayal, impossible odds. Hardly. These are the bread and butter of romantasies, the kind of tropes that make you roll your eyes but also can’t help but read on because, let’s be honest, we all love a good "will they, won’t they" with a side of apocalypse. But the synopsis doesn’t give us much to work with. Is this a story about the cost of power? The cost of love? The cost of digging too deep into the past? Or is it just a story about two people who have to work together to save the world, despite their differences? The synopsis doesn’t say, and that’s the problem—it’s so broad it could be describing *any* fantasy romance where the stakes are high and the chemistry is even higher.

But here’s the thing—I can’t help but think this is going to be a *Crescent City* knockoff, because why not? It’s got the same kind of "dark fantasy meets Western" vibe, the same kind of "chosen one" protagonist, the same kind of "brooding love interest with a dark past." And if it’s not, then it’s at least going to be a *Raiders of the Lost Ark* meets *Django* mashup, which is fine, but let’s be real—this is the kind of thing that gets lost in the shuffle of "another fantasy romance with a gunslinger and a magic artifact." The synopsis doesn’t give us much to go on, but it does give us enough to know that this is going to be a story about two people who have to trust each other, despite their differences, to save the world. And if that’s the case, then I can’t wait to see how it plays out—because, let’s be honest, we all love a good "will they, won’t they" with a side of apocalypse.

But will it be any good? That’s the question. Because if it’s just another "chosen one" story with a brooding love interest and a magic artifact, then it’s going to get lost in the shuffle. And if it’s something more, something deeper, something that actually explores the themes of trust and betrayal and the cost of power, then it might just be worth reading. But for now, all we have is a synopsis that’s so vague it could be describing *any* fantasy romance, and that’s not exactly a vote of confidence.

So, here’s my prediction—*Six Bullets and a Crown* is going to be a *Crescent City* wannabe, a *Raiders* knockoff, a *Django* pastiche, all rolled into one big, messy romancy that’s going to leave you feeling like you’ve been hit by a bullet from Ford Sterling’s god-touched revolver. And if that’s the case, then I can’t wait to see how it plays out—because, let’s be honest, we all love a good "will they, won’t they" with a side of apocalypse.
