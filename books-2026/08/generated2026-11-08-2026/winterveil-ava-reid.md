**Author:** Ava Reid  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-09-15 APL

The sweat dripping down my neck while I try to dice three bell peppers in this stifling August humidity is a crime against domesticity, yet here I am, aggressively roasting vegetables for the week while dissecting Ava Reid’s upcoming September release, *Winterveil*. 

And what a premise we have been handed. Four teenage criminals drafted into an alternate Cold War military academy. It is a delightfully cynical setup. We get the disgraced dynastic politician, the ruthless strategist, the pageant queen turned world-changer, and the secretive soldier. Reid clearly knows how to build a high-concept pressure cooker. Think *The Atlas Six* meets *Red Queen*, but soaked in geopolitical dread and teenage angst. 

Give me healthy communication in fiction for once in my life. Please. I am begging authors to let teenagers talk about their feelings instead of staring across a war room while suppressing a five-year-long yearning. But will these characters actually sit down and use "I" statements when their loyalties are tested? Unlikely. They will probably communicate exclusively through loaded eye contact across map tables and tactical weapon cleanings. And honestly? I will eat it up. Because if there is one thing I demand from a YA dystopia, it is mutual vulnerability disguised as enemies-to-lovers tension. 

Speaking of culinary disasters, why is red bell pepper juice chemically designed to linger on plastic cutting boards forever? I scrubbed that exact board three times yesterday after making shakshuka, and it still smells like roasted paprika. It is deeply offensive. My kitchen sponge is gaslighting me. 

Back to the book at hand. The synopsis promises forged romances and a potential apocalypse. Classic Reid fashion. Yet I am struck by how aggressively vague the official blurb remains. They are "precocious teenage criminals with dark skills." What does that even mean? Did they hack the Pentagon? Did they steal the crown jewels? Or did they just commit grand tax evasion with severe attitude? The marketing team refuses to tell us, opting instead for vague stylistic vibes. We are left grasping at literary smoke. 

Yet I trust Reid implicitly. Her previous work possesses a razor-sharp bite that avoids the usual YA pitfalls of toothless world-building. If anyone can make an alternate-universe Cold War academy feel claustrophobic and electrically romantic, it is her. I just hope the romance has actual emotional stakes rather than endless lingering glances in drafty armories. 

Oh, look at the time. I have to go stop the toddler from trying to feed the cat vanilla bean ice cream again. Apparently, we cannot eat frozen dairy products for dinner, despite what the three-year-old’s aggressive lobby on the matter suggests.
