**Author:** Liv Andersson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Little Red House by Liv Andersson is a tantalizing proposition, a diabolically twisty thriller that promises to peel back the layers of dark family ties and long-buried secrets. Alma Katsu, author of *The Hunger*, calls it "diabolically twisty," and one can almost smell the musty scent of New Mexico's desert landscapes where the serial killer prowls in the shadows.

But what does it say about our collective psyche that we're so enamored with the notion of the "tainted inheritance," the idea that some wounds run so deep, they're impossible to heal? We've seen it in *Sharp Objects* by Gillian Flynn, where the protagonist's past comes back to haunt her in the form of a gruesome, small-town mystery. We've seen it in *The Silent Patient* by Alex Michaelides, where the inner workings of a painter's troubled mind are laid bare for all to see.

Yet, in *Little Red House*, it's not just about the tangled web of family dynamics or the killer's warped logic – it's about the suffocating grip of a mother's toxic legacy and the ways in which it can both destroy and define us.

Or is it?

Actually, scratch that – it's about absolutely nothing. We get a vague setup, a series of unsolved murders in New Mexico, and a narrator who receives a strange, dilapidated house as an inheritance. The setup is tantalizing, yet frustratingly vague. We get glimpses of Connie's toxic relationship with her mother, the cruel games she played, but nothing concrete. The plot promises to be a thrilling ride, but the more we learn, the more we realize that Andersson is setting up a series of tropes, not a cohesive narrative.

Literally.

But I digress – it's clear Andersson knows how to build tension, how to create a sense of foreboding that follows us through the pages like a predator stalking its prey. The writing is crisp, the pacing sharp, and the imagery evocative, conjuring up images of a desert landscape that's both beautiful and deadly.

Hard pass.

As I see it, *Little Red House* is a study in contrasts, a exploration of the darker aspects of motherhood and the ways in which our pasts can shape us into the people we become. It's a cautionary tale about the dangers of inheriting someone else's demons, and the ways in which we can both perpetuate and break free from toxic patterns.

Or is it just a bunch of tired tropes strung together?

One thing's for sure – I'm hiding in the pantry for five minutes of peace, sipping on some lukewarm coffee and trying to shake off the sense of disquiet that's been following me since I stumbled upon this book.
