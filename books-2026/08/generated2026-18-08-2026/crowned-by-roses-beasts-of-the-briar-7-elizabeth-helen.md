**Author:** Elizabeth Helen  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

The allure of a lush, dark fairy tale – it's a siren's call I've fallen prey to time and time again. *Crowned by Roses*, the latest offering from Elizabeth Helen, promises to be just that: a rich, atmospheric fantasy that will transport readers to a world of ancient magic and whispered secrets. And yet, as I scan the synopsis, I find myself growing increasingly irritable – what's the point of teasing a world so vivid, so full of potential, only to leave me with so many unanswered questions?

A kingdom prospering at the cost of its surrounding woods, a curse that's been simmering for centuries, a young protagonist with a mysterious connection to the very heart of the forest... it's all so deliciously familiar, and yet, so frustratingly vague. I'm reminded of *The Wrath and the Dawn* by Renée Ahdieh, with its lush, Middle Eastern-inspired setting and its brooding, mystical atmosphere – but where Ahdieh's world-building was meticulous, deliberate, Helen's seems almost... lazy. The woods are dark and cursed, the kingdom is peaceful and prosperous – but what does that really mean? What are the stakes, the consequences, the underlying tensions that drive this world forward?

And then, of course, there's Cerys herself – a protagonist with a traumatic past, a dash of magic in her veins, and a determination to save her home. It's a familiar archetype, to say the least – I'm thinking of *The Girl of Fire and Thorns* by Rae Carson, with its own plucky, magic-wielding heroine – but what sets Cerys apart? What unique perspective, what compelling voice, will she bring to this narrative?

As I ponder these questions, my mind begins to wander – I find myself thinking of *Adventure Time*, of all things, and its own exploration of the tension between nature and civilization. The show's creators, Pendleton Ward and Dentsu Aegis Network, managed to craft a world that was both whimsical and profound, full of characters that were at once relatable and strange – can Helen do the same? Can she balance the darkness of her world with a sense of wonder, of magic, that will keep readers entranced?

But I digress – or do I? The truth is, I'm not sure what to expect from *Crowned by Roses*, and that uncertainty is both thrilling and infuriating. Will it be a lush, atmospheric fantasy that will transport me to another world, or a shallow, predictable retread of familiar tropes? I suppose I'll just have to wait and see – but for now, I'm left with more questions than answers, and a growing sense of impatience.

And speaking of impatience – I really need to pack these lunches. Three of them, to be exact, and I have a feeling they'll all come back uneaten. Ah, the joys of parenthood.
