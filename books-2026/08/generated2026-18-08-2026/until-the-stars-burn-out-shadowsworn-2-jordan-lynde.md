**Author:** Jordan Lynde  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-12-29 APL

The binder holding the advance sheets smells faintly of bergamot and expensive glue, resting quite comfortably against the vellum lining of my blotter. Jordan Lynde returns to us with *Until The Stars Burn Out*, the promised continuation of *Until the World Falls Down*, a national bestseller that somehow bypassed every salon on the Upper East Side yet managed to shift plenty of units. One shudders at the demographics. 

### The Architecture of Despair

We are subjected once again to Nell and Enver, fresh from the labyrinth and presumably exhausted by their own branding. The premise trades in recycled velvet. A fading magic, a lost relic, and the perennial curse that requires a woman's gentle patience to mend. It reads less like mythic fantasy and more like the infamous 1992 Windsor Castle fire—slow, predictable, and entirely insured. Enver’s control is slipping, you see. The shadows devour him. Or perhaps the editors devoured the plot structure before the manuscript ever hit the bindery. 

And then there is the touch. Every stolen kiss brings physical proximity that somehow destabilizes arcane metaphysics. A curious piece of magical engineering, that. One wonders if the thermodynamic laws of Lynde’s universe apply strictly to labial tension. The synopsis leans heavily on the sort of devastating intimacy usually reserved for cheap paperbacks bought at railway kiosks during a delayed departure to Zurich. 

### Shadows and Stock Footage

Familiar faces resurface. The dangerous fae makes a curtain call. Naturally. Because introducing a novel antagonist would require actual imagination rather than reheating the same literary gruel that populated the first volume. 

The prose promises a race against time and across realms. How exhausting. We are promised impossible choices that will shatter hearts for eternity. Eternity is a rather long time to spend mourning a character who speaks entirely in brooding ellipses and aggressive cheekbones. The emotional stakes are telegraphed with the subtlety of a falling chandelier at the Paris Opera. Nell must choose. Enver will break. The paper will absorb it all, patiently waiting for the paperback release in three months. 

I shall require the page proofs removed from my sight before the morning post arrives. The courier is already late with the delivery of bespoke Italian leather furniture.
