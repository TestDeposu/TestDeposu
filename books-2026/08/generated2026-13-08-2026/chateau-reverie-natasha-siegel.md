**Author:** Natasha Siegel  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-27 APL

The humidity clinging to the silk of my collar today is nothing short of an absolute tragedy, a suffocating August blanket that mirrors the precise agony of staring at yet another derivative historical fantasy being shoveled onto the market by desperate publishers. *Chateau Reverie* arrives late next year courtesy of Natasha Siegel, demanding we care about a rococo parlor game masquerading as high art. And yet, Samantha Shannon is blubbering about being enchanted. How precious. We are promised a haunted house, a fable, and a romance during the twilight hours of the French Revolution—because apparently, the guillotine simply wasn't bloody enough without a touch of magical realism thrown in for the TikTok demographic. 

### The Currency of Suppressed Trauma

Genevieve de Vienne and Leander Sterne walk into a cursed architectural wet dream with empty pockets and heavy baggage. Or rather, Genevieve wants to obliterate the memory of a former lover while Leander merely desires basic autonomy. They must survive three days of transactional amnesia. Bidding secrets for desires sounds awfully like a graduate seminar in comparative literature, minus the stale coffee and career despair. Siegel previously gave us *As Many Souls as Stars*, which apparently prepared her to write about ethereal auctions where people trade their deepest shames like baseball cards. It is deeply unserious, low-key giving me brain rot just reading the logline, yet executed with enough rococo window dressing to hook the Erin Morgenstern sycophants. 

Actually, scratch that completely. The prose is allegedly "lush and transportive," and if Siegel leans hard enough into the decadent horror of the late eighteenth-century aristocracy, I might actually forgive the sheer absurdity of a magical house playing landlord to amnesiacs. 

### The Aristocratic Audit of the Damned

We are expected to marvel at the peril of seven attendees trapped in a sentient estate where winning means losing your humanity. How dreadfully original. One shudders at the structural integrity of a plot that relies entirely on people trading secrets they supposedly cannot remember. How does one bid a secret when memory is the price of admission? A glaring logical paradox, yet the V.E. Schwab crowd will eat it up without chewing. They adore aestheticized suffering wrapped in velvet ribbons and candle wax. 

We shall see if Siegel delivers an actual deconstruction of French aristocratic rot or merely another pastel-tinted YA-crossover dressed up in lace. My Rolex is losing five seconds an hour and the jeweler cannot look at it until Tuesday, which is a profound inconvenience.
