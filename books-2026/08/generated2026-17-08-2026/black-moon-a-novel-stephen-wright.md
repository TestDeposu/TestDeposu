**Author:** Stephen Wright  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-20 APL

The air tonight is humid enough to choke a horse, and the city below looks like a circuit board melting under a neon fever-dream while I sit here trying to muster a single ounce of genuine excitement for Stephen Wright’s upcoming *Black Moon*. Another fictionalized historical pirate romp. Oh, joy. Smoke and mirrors, mostly. We get it, women in corsets can hold cutlasses just as well as men—groundbreaking stuff for the fall release cycle. The promotional machinery is already screaming about a wild, debauched, and violent ride based on Anne Bonny, as if every second historical fiction author isn't currently cashing in on the exact same rebellious-woman-on-the-high-seas meta-trend. 

### The Aristocratic Pipeline to Scurvy

Look, the elevator pitch involves a poor Irish orphan named Meeks—because of course her name is Meeks before she becomes a total badass—who marries into the aristocracy just so her war-obsessed husband can promptly kick the bucket, leaving her free to catch the eye of a pirate captain. Groundbreaking trajectory. Truly, what a vibe check for the patriarchy. You start in a grim reformatorio, you lose your posh husband to some European skirmish, and suddenly you are swashbuckling because your hustle game is just that immaculate. The synopsis reads like a mad libs generator designed specifically for readers of George Saunders and Percival Everett who desperately need a palate cleanser after reading too many depressing contemporary literary novels about divorce. 

And yet. The publisher comparisons to Maggie O'Farrell give me a tiny sliver of hope that the prose won't read like absolute garbage truck juice. If Wright actually leans into the feral, blood-soaked absurdity of the eighteenth century instead of giving us sanitized historical tourism with modern feminist talking points awkwardly stapled to the dialogue, this might actually slap. Or it will be a pretentious mess of purple prose about ambition and cunning. Usually, when a blurb uses words like exuberant epic and starry-eyed in the same breath, I reach for the industrial-strength ibuprofen. 

### Aesthetic Bloodlust on the Horizon

We are promised a protagonist with an insatiable appetite and boundless intelligence, which is code for an author trying desperately to write a chaotic-neutral antihero who gets away with literal murder because she has snappy one-liners and tragic backstory lore. It is giving high-concept grift. Still, sitting here listening to the distant sirens bouncing off the skyscrapers, I kind of want to watch someone burn down the European aristocracy and sail into the sunset. If Wright commits the bit and makes it genuinely unhinged—think *Flint* meets Cormac McCarthy without the theological despair—I might actually crack the spine. But let's be real. It will probably win a bunch of awards, dominate the book-tuber feeds for a week, and then get adapted into a painfully mediocre prestige streaming series that gets canceled after two seasons. Anyway, I need to patch a hole in my favorite, impossibly worn tweed jacket before the AC freezes me out here.
