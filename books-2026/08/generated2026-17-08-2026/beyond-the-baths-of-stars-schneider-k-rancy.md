**Author:** Schneider K. Rancy  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-11-03 APL

What if the fates that bind us are not the gods, but our own making? *Beyond the Baths of Stars*, Schneider K. Rancy's forthcoming novel, dares to ask this question by reimagining Homer's *The Odyssey* in the sweltering landscape of South Florida. Eurick Ulysse, the protagonist, is a young man trapped in a life that seems predetermined by poverty, class, and the expectations of his immigrant parents. His existence is a monotony of dead-end jobs and unaffordable college aspirations, a never-ending cycle that threatens to consume him.

And then, Ana María Cortez appears, a free-spirited siren who crashes into Eurick's life like a stormy sea. Her world is one of privilege and excess, a realm where the rules that govern Eurick's existence do not apply. As they embark on a wild joyride through the streets of Miami, Eurick finds himself torn between the thrill of rebellion and the desperation of his circumstances. Will this one night of freedom be enough to set him on a different path, or will it only serve to remind him of the life he can never have?

This is where the synopsis falls short, leaving us with more questions than answers. What does it mean to be trapped, exactly? Is it the external circumstances that bind us, or is it our own perception of those circumstances? The vagueness is frustrating, but it also hints at a deeper exploration of the human condition. Rancy's prose promises to be a dizzying, surreal ride, full of strange encounters and surreal landscapes.

But what of Eurick's character? Is he a modern-day Odysseus, or just a lost soul adrift in a world that seems determined to keep him down? The comparison to Homer's classic is intriguing, but it also raises the stakes. Can Rancy's novel live up to the weight of that legacy? The themes of class, identity, and the search for freedom are timely and urgent, but they are also familiar. Will *Beyond the Baths of Stars* offer anything new, or will it simply retread old ground?

Literally.

One thing is certain: Rancy's novel will be a visceral, unflinching portrayal of life on the margins. The writing promises to be lyrical and evocative, conjuring the sights and sounds of South Florida with vivid precision. Fans of authors like Junot Díaz and Gabriel García Márquez will likely find much to appreciate in Rancy's style, which blends the gritty realism of contemporary fiction with the mythic, dreamlike quality of magical realism.

But I'm still not convinced. The synopsis is too vague, the themes too familiar. I need to see more, to know that Rancy's novel is more than just a clever reimagining of a classic. I need to feel the weight of Eurick's desperation, the thrill of Ana's rebellion. Until then, I remain skeptical.
