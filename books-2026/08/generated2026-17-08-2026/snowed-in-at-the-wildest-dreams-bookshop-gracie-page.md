**Author:** Gracie Page  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

The snow’s already falling in the synopsis, isn’t it? That’s the problem with these cozy romcoms—you don’t need to wait for the weather to ruin the mood. The Wildest Dreams Bookshop series, or at least this first installment, is promising a *Snowed In at the Wildest Dreams Bookshop* that smells like overpriced latte and the faintest hint of emotional labor. Ivy, an art student who’s clearly been through the mill, is trading her dorm for a seaside bookshop, which is either a brilliant escape or a metaphor for her own creative burnout—we’ll have to wait and see. Trip, the relentlessly cheerful tour guide, is the kind of character who makes you wonder if his optimism is a personality trait or a defense mechanism. The synopsis leans hard into the grumpy x sunshine trope, which is fine, but it’s also the kind of setup that either plays out beautifully or feels like a gimmick by page 150.

The seaside setting is the real star here, though. Fox Bay sounds like a place where the air smells of salt and regret, where the bookshop is both a sanctuary and a character in its own right. The Wildest Dreams Bookshop isn’t just a backdrop—it’s a living entity, a place where dreams are sold and maybe even found. That’s the kind of atmospheric detail that makes a romcom feel like more than just a plot device. But then again, the synopsis also mentions "forced proximity," which is a trope so overused it’s starting to feel like a plot twist in itself. Ivy and Trip are stuck together, which is either a brilliant way to force emotional growth or a lazy way to avoid writing actual dialogue. The synopsis doesn’t give us enough to judge, but I can already predict the pacing will be uneven—long stretches of charming banter followed by sudden, awkwardly timed revelations.

And let’s talk about the "depth" part of the synopsis. Trip has hidden depths, Ivy is more hopeful than she seems—this is the kind of vague character description that makes you want to roll your eyes. Depth in a romcom is usually just a fancy way of saying "we’ll give them a backstory later." But if Gracie Page can pull it off like Emily Henry does with her emotional beats, then maybe this will be more than just a fluffy escape. The synopsis also mentions that this is the first Fox Bay love story, which is a clever way to set up a series. If the next book delivers the same kind of charm, then we might be in for something special. But if it’s just another installment in the endless stream of small-town romcoms, then we’re all just waiting for the next snowstorm to pass.

But here’s the thing—I don’t actually care if this book is good or bad. I care about the way it’s marketed. The synopsis is all about the vibe, the atmosphere, the *feeling* of the book. And that’s fine, because that’s what sells. But it’s also why I’m already tired of reading about people who are just waiting for their grumpy x sunshine to save them. It’s not that I don’t enjoy a good romcom—it’s that I’ve seen this movie before. The snow, the bookshop, the forced proximity—it’s all just a gimmick until the characters start talking.

And speaking of talking, I need to stop writing this review. I have a small forest of houseplants that are probably dying right now, and I can’t let that happen. I’ll just leave it at this: if you want a book that smells like snow and secondhand romance, then this might be your thing. But if you want something with a little more bite, then maybe you should stick to something with a little more bite. Like a thriller. Or a memoir. Or anything that doesn’t involve a man who’s too cheerful for his own good.
