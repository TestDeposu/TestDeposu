**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-20 APL

The flour is currently settling into every single groove of my kitchen counter while two sugar-crazed children argue loudly about whether chocolate chips belong in oatmeal cookies—they do, obviously—which means I am in the absolute prime mental state to dissect a YA fantasy synopsis about royal runaways and shadowy spy orders. Braidee Otto is returning with *Daughter of the Tempest*, the sequel to *Songbird of the Sorrows*, and honestly, I am staring at this plot summary with the exact same skepticism I reserve for store-bought pie crust. We have the classic split narrative—one girl fleeing across mountains with a brooding, dangerous prisoner she is reluctantly falling for while he teaches her magic, and the other trapped in a hostile palace disguised as a servant, forced to ally with a petulant prince who has a terribly convenient redemption arc written all over his aristocratic face. 

Wait. Actually, let me walk that back entirely. 

The forced-proximity servant trope with Prince Kallias might actually be the only thing keeping this book afloat if the fugitive-in-the-mountains dynamic drags on for too long. 

Splitting up the dynamic duo of Aella and Nyssa right out of the gate is a bold narrative gamble—one that often breaks momentum in middle-grade and YA sequels because readers just want the friends back together sharing sarcasm over campfires—yet Otto seems determined to double down on the isolation torture. Enemies-to-lovers with a mysterious prisoner who holds dark secrets? Groundbreaking. I mean, we have seen this exact choreography play out in every Sarah J. Maas and Jennifer L. Armentrout novel published since 2018, complete with the obligatory high-stakes games and vague prophesied storms brewing inside our misunderstood heroine. But readers eat this up like half-baked brownie batter straight from the bowl. 

Predictable.

The Empyrieos Games sound suspiciously like every other tournament trope designed to manufacture artificial tension while the main plot catches its breath. 

Yet, I will probably pre-order it anyway because I need to know if Nyssa actually gets to slap Prince Kallias. 

I have to go because ten fingers just slammed down on the middle C key at maximum volume and someone is crying over spilled sprinkles.
