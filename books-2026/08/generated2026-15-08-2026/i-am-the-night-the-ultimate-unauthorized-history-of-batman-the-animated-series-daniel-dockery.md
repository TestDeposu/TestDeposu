Cold fluorescent hum overhead, lukewarm sludge in a styrofoam cup, and an eight-minute timer blinking mercilessly on the monitor. God, I hate pitch season. Yet here we are, pretending a marketing blurb for Daniel Dockery’s *I Am the Night: The Ultimate Unauthorized History of Batman: The Animated Series* constitutes actual literature worth analyzing on a fifteen-minute breathing pause. 

### Bureaucracy in the Batcave

According to the synopsis, we are promised the gritty, behind-the-scenes war over whether the brooding vigilante could have a recycling bin. Riveting stakes. Truly, the pulse-pounding drama of municipal waste management in Gotham. Let's just rip the band-aid off. 

At least Paul Duni and Bruce Timm actually understood noir pacing, unlike the endless corporate sludge currently clogging the arteries of modern superhero cinema. Or wait—did the synopsis say Duni? No, it just mentions Timm and an anonymous legion of bruised animators fighting network executives over onscreen kissing quotas. 

My left eyelid has been twitching since 3:00 AM. 

Anyway, the thematic underpinnings here practically write themselves, provided the author avoids the sycophantic hero-worship that usually plagues unauthorized pop-culture oral histories. Think less Peter Biskind's *Easy Riders, Raging Bulls* and more of a Wikipedia printout padded with quotes from tired storyboard artists who just wanted to sleep. Actually, scratch that—Dockery usually knows his stuff, so maybe we will get some genuine insight into how a children's afternoon block accidentally birthed a timeless aesthetic. 

### The Illusion of Depth

Or maybe we won't. Look at the text itself. It leans heavily on nostalgia, propping up the corpse of 90s animation as a golden standard while conveniently ignoring the clunky filler episodes involving Clock King. I shouldn't judge a book by a press release written by an exhausted publicist who probably hasn't slept in a week either, but here we are. Writing about a book that isn't out yet based entirely on three paragraphs of breathless hype is a special kind of purgatory. I have three hundred words left to hit before my shift manager glares at my desk again. 

The synopsis tells us Batman is eternal. Sure. Everything is eternal if you slap enough trademark lawyers on it. 

The local independent bookstore has run out of my favorite literary magazine.
