As I savor the last crumbs of a mediocre croissant on this overpriced, underwhelming train ride, my mind whisks me away to *Level Up for Love*, the sophomore effort from Lenora Woods. The synopsis boasts a winning combination: contemporary second-chance romance and fantasy, set against the backdrop of Dungeons & Dragons. It's a match made in heaven, or at least in the annals of geeky literary fiction.

Reagan Riley and Wesley Locke, two former college rivals, find themselves forced together in a new campaign – and, of course, their real-world baggage comes crashing to the surface. I'm reminded of the excellent *The Hating Game* by Sally Thorne, where the banter is as witty as the chemistry between the leads. Will *Level Up for Love* deliver a similar dose of tension and romance? I'm inclined to say yes, and I'm not alone in my enthusiasm – the book's endorsement from M. Stevenson, author of *Behooved*, is a testament to its potential.

But what really gets me excited is the way Woods interweaves the D&D campaign with the main love story. It's a bold move, one that demands deft handling to avoid feeling like a gimmick. One can almost imagine the author meticulously crafting an intricate narrative, à la *The City & The City* by China Miéville, where the fantastical elements are woven so seamlessly into the fabric of reality that it's hard to discern where one ends and the other begins.

Yet, upon closer inspection, the synopsis raises more questions than answers. What, exactly, is the nature of Reagan and Wesley's game design opportunity in the real world? Is it a challenge they must complete together, or is it a chance for them to finally outdo each other? The vagueness here is a bit of a nonstarter, and I fear it may be a misstep – after all, one of the most compelling aspects of *Roll for Romance*, the author's previous work, was its clear and concise storytelling.

But let's not get ahead of ourselves. Woods has managed to pique my interest, and I'm willing to give her the benefit of the doubt. I predict that *Level Up for Love* will emerge as a strong contender in the romance-fantasy genre, one that will appeal to fans of *Uprooted* by Naomi Novik, with its deft blend of fantasy and character-driven drama.

### Narrative Pivotal Points

One way to interpret this romance-fantasy hybrid is as a nuanced exploration of what it means to be a "winner" in life. Reagan and Wesley's game design rivalry has long since cooled, but the embers still burn, refusing to be extinguished by their newfound affection for each other. Is it possible to overcome past hurts and find a way to compete with someone you love? Woods might be onto something profound here.

### D&D as a Metaphor

Or maybe I'm reading too much into it. Perhaps the D&D campaign is simply a convenient plot device, a way for Woods to explore themes of control and agency in a fantastical setting. In that case, Reagan's struggles with Vadrien, the merciless vampire lord, take on a whole new significance – no longer just a battle of wits, but a metaphor for the existential crises we face in our own lives.

### Artistic Ambition

Whatever the case, Lenora Woods has demonstrated a keen understanding of the power of genre-bending. She's not afraid to take risks, to challenge her readers and push the boundaries of what's expected in a romance-fantasy novel. And that, my friends, is something to get excited about.

I'm already mentally rewriting this review for the umpteenth time, questioning everything from my initial enthusiasm to my more recent reservations.
