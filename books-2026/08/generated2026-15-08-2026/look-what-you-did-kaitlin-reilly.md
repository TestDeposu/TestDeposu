**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-09-15 APL

Steam is hissing from the group head of this ancient, recalcitrant espresso machine, threatening to scald my forearm while I wipe down a counter covered in spilled oat milk. Perfect soundtrack for digesting yet another promotional blurb about dead teenagers and small-town rot. Kaitlin Reilly’s debut, *Look What You Did*, is slated to drop in September 2026, and the synopsis reads like an algorithm was fed every paperback published since *One of Us Is Lying* and told to regurgitate a fresh corpse. 

### The Anatomy of a Dead Golden Boy

We get the usual suspects. A dead golden boy named Will Ashford—because why do the fictional popular kids always sound like a country club prospectus? A girl named Mackenzie who is profoundly relieved he checked out. And naturally, a text message. Always with the anonymous texts. Because God forbid a thriller rely on atmosphere or prose when a glowing rectangle can just deliver exposition. 

Actually, wait. Let me walk that back. Maybe the text message cliché is earned here. The synopsis implies a psychological excavation rather than a standard whodunnit, leaning into buried trauma and the kind of claustrophobic dread that actually makes a thriller worth the paper it’s printed on. 

### Small Towns and Secret Bodies

The thematic core here isn't the murder itself. It never is. It’s the rot underneath the manicured lawns. Reilly is playing in the same sandbox as Kathleen Glasgow and Megan Lally, where adolescent grief gets weaponized and nobody's hands are clean. 

And honestly, I am typing this between pulling double shots for sleep-deprived commuters who treat me like furniture, just trying to hit my mandated word count so I can afford rent this month. The premise promises a deep dive into repressed memory and municipal hypocrisy. Yet I’ve seen this exact plot structure forty times this year alone. A girl keeps a secret. The secret comes back via smartphone. Another girl dies. Rinse, repeat, cash the YA royalty check. 

Yet I’ll probably read it anyway. I am a sucker for stories where the prom king turns out to be a monster and the girls have to burn the town down to survive. 

My shift ends in twenty minutes, provided the espresso machine doesn't explode. Then I have to run home and rescue the family cat from the top of the giant oak tree in the front yard again, because the stupid creature refuses to learn physics.
