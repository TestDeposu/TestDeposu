**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-11-17 APL

Thunderstruck, the latest release by Lissa Layland, promises to deliver a smart, sexy debut romantasy infused with Norse mythology. The premise—American exchange student Althea Leos gets caught up in a love triangle with Norse gods Thor and Loki—is tantalizing enough to pique the interest of even the most jaded reader.

—But what exactly are we getting with this book? It seems like a predictable academic romance with a dash of fantasy.

Accordingly, the synopsis lists love triangle, academic romance, slow-burn romance, and Norse mythology as tropes. One can't help but think of *The City of Brass* by S.A. Chakraborty as this book's distant cousin. Both novels deal with academic romances and fantastical elements, albeit with vastly different settings and tones.

The inclusion of Norse mythology seems like a bold move, but one that risks feeling superficial. We're told Althea stumbles upon a bridge to Asgard, but what exactly happens in this immortal realm remains a mystery. It's unclear whether Layland will delve into the mythology with any depth or if it'll be reduced to superficial references.

Meanwhile, let's examine the character of Althea Leos, described as Type A. What might this mean in the context of her interactions with Thor and Loki? We're told she's "love-drunk" and "angrily follows the elusive Professor Dahl home." This juxtaposition of passion and anger could potentially create a compelling narrative, but only if Layland chooses to explore these complexities.

The dynamic between Althea, Thor, and Loki promises to be the real focus of this novel. Will we see a genuine exploration of their relationships or a tired rehashing of typical slow-burn romance tropes?

As a feminist critic, I have to ask: what does this love triangle between a mortal and two Norse gods say about female agency? Does Althea truly have control over her own narrative or is she trapped in a predetermined fate?
