**Author:** Barbara Truelove  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-09-08 APL

My knees are currently molding into the cracked pleather of seat forty-two while some absolute goblin two rows up blasts TikTok at four in the morning, which means it is prime time to dissect Barbara Truelove’s upcoming *She's a Doll* before my phone battery drops to zero and I have to resort to staring blankly at the highway median like a lobotomized Victorian child. 

### The Porcelain Pipeline of Vengeance

Truelove apparently decided that standard psychological thrillers are for cowards. Instead, we get an eighteen-inch ceramic homicide enthusiast possessing an antique plaything to settle a score with a guy named Kyle. Because nothing screams righteous retribution quite like structural integrity issues and brittle limbs. Yet the synopsis leans hard into that hyper-marketed brand of capital-R Rage that every publishing house is currently shoving down our throats to appease the book-tok demographic. 

### Small-Town Gaslighting and Lacy Sleeves

And what a predictable suburban backdrop we have here. An idyllic town where the locals refuse to believe that a promising young man—probably named something aggressively basic like Chad or Kyle—could possibly be a murderer. Groundbreaking. Truly a sociological critique of our era. Lucy has to roll up her lacy little sleeves and murder him herself because the local constabulary is apparently too busy eating donuts and ignoring structural misogyny to notice an eighteen-inch porcelain murder-toddler rolling down Main Street. One must wonder if Truelove is actually doing anything subversive here, or if this is just another mid-tier IP mill-product designed to be optioned by a streamer before the galley proofs even dry. 

### Genre Slop and Word-Count Sadism

Look, I am typing this on a frozen screen while inhaling the distinct chemical aroma of toilet deodorizer wafting from the back of the bus just to make rent this month, so forgive me if my patience for quirky posthumous vengeance narratives is running dangerously low. This whole enterprise smells like a corporate boardroom brainstorm where someone yelled, *What if Barbie was John Wick, but make it feminist?* We are drowning in high-concept gimmickry. Authors refuse to write about actual human behavior anymore without wrapping it in a supernatural metaphorical onesie just to secure a blurb from the *LA Times*. 

Truelove probably delivers a fun, unhinged ride—comparable to Grady Hendrix crashing into a Christopher Moore novel after three too many espresso martinis—but let’s be entirely real here. The synopsis reads like a pitch deck written by an algorithm that ingested every horror-comedy published since 2018. But hey, it beats watching the scenery blur past the grease-smeared window. 

I am going to go sit on a park bench and aggressively people-watch.
