**Author:** Paula Brackston  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-11-10 APL

Look at this synopsis for *Hecate Cavendish and the Angel of Death*. Just look at it. Another Victorian heroine tending to a medieval map and a pile of chained books, because apparently every single historical fantasy published this decade must borrow its aesthetic from the same Pinterest board dedicated to dusty academia and sepia tones. 

And yet, here we are. 

My tea is stone cold—had it sitting by the window for hours while staring at the empty chair across the garden—and the weight in my chest feels entirely permanent, but at least Paula Brackston is offering up a conclusion to a trilogy that stubbornly refuses to die quietly. Or maybe she isn't. The description insists on cramming every single narrative cliché into a tiny blender: we have an endangered cathedral job, a best friend walking down the aisle, a brooding Welsh warrior looking for closure, and a phantom serial killer roaming the cobblestones of Hereford like an extra from a rejected penny dreadful. 

Except I said earlier this would be a tight, focused character study on grief. I was wrong. It sounds like an absolute narrative traffic jam where romance, municipal murder mysteries, and bureaucratic library politics crash violently into one another with the grace of a collapsing bookshelf. 

Messy.

One can practically hear the editorial boardroom discussions now—make sure she has a quirky ghost family, but don't forget the Inspector who needs her to solve the bloody murders because the local police are utterly useless without a psychic twenty-something whispering to spectral entities in the dark. It reads less like Susanna Clarke and more like a Sunday evening television drama designed to be watched while half-asleep on the sofa with a blanket over your knees. But Brackston usually delivers a certain cozy charm, even when the plot machinations feel borrowed from better gothic traditions, so perhaps the sheer emotional weight of finality will rescue the proceedings from becoming a ridiculous parade of supernatural busywork. 

Though I genuinely wonder how anyone finds the time to help a lovelorn Welsh spirit when a savage murderer is actively terrorizing the zip code. 

Priorities, Hecate. 

The heart breaks daily, yet the world keeps demanding we read about chained libraries and phantom vengeance. 

Must go mediate an argument over whose turn it is to empty the dishwasher.
