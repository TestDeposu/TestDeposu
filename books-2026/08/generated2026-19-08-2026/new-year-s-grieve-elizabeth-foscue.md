A country house mystery for the *Truly Devious* and *Knives Out* crowd, how quaint. *New Year's Grieve*, Elizabeth Foscue's latest, promises a romp of a whodunit, replete with all the makings of a classic genre piece: a sprawling estate, a snowed-in cast of suspects, and a plucky protagonist determined to unravel the mystery of her grandfather's untimely demise. Yet, as I read through the synopsis, I couldn't help but feel a creeping sense of déjà vu – haven't we seen this all before?

Let's get the obvious out of the way: Jojo, our intrepid heroine, is a veritable amalgamation of every "feisty" YA protagonist to have ever graced the pages of a *Twilight*-esque thriller. Her "maddening ex-boyfriend," Fritz, is, of course, the requisite love interest cum suspect, and their tortured past is already telegraphing a romance rekindled amidst the mayhem. It's a tired trope, one that's been done to death in the likes of *Gossip Girl* and *Pretty Little Liars*. And don't even get me started on the "horrible relatives" – I'm sure they'll all be suitably one-dimensional and cartoonishly evil.

But, I suppose that's the point – *New Year's Grieve* is, by all accounts, a genre piece, content to revel in the tropes and conventions of the country house mystery. And, if done well, it could be a fun, if forgettable, romp. After all, who doesn't love a good whodunit? The problem lies in the fact that the synopsis is so maddeningly vague – what, exactly, sets *New Year's Grieve* apart from its genre brethren? Is it merely a rehashing of the same old tropes, or is there something more at play?
