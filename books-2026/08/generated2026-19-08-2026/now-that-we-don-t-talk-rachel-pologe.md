**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-20 APL

A commitment-phobe and a nemesis stuck together for Hanukkah – sounds like the perfect setup for a holiday romance, replete with forced proximity and unresolved feelings. *Now That We Don't Talk* has all the makings of a compelling, emotionally resonant debut. But can it live up to the promise of its premise?

— The setup is undeniably clever, leveraging the festive atmosphere of Hanukkah to bring together two people with a complicated history. Norah Gold, our protagonist, is a self-proclaimed bolter, always running from emotional situations. And then there's Henry Cole, the boy who broke her heart in high school. Their mothers are now dating, and they're forced to spend the holidays together – it's a recipe for disaster, or perhaps, a chance for redemption.

But what really draws me in is the character psychology at play. Norah's avoidance of emotional intimacy is a classic coping mechanism, one that's both relatable and heartbreaking. And Henry, with his condescending attitude and custom suits, seems like the perfect foil to Norah's chaos. Their dynamic is already fraught with tension, and I can only imagine how the close quarters of a Hanukkah celebration will exacerbate their feelings.

For what it's worth, the author's use of Hanukkah as a backdrop is a refreshing change of pace from the usual Christmas rom-coms. It adds a unique cultural layer to the story, one that I hope will be explored in depth.

— As I read through the synopsis, I couldn't help but think of authors like Taylor Jenkins Reid and Emily Henry, who excel at crafting emotionally charged, character-driven romances. *Now That We Don't Talk* seems to be cut from the same cloth, with its focus on complex relationships and personal growth.

But, I have to admit, the synopsis is a bit vague when it comes to the actual plot. We're told that Norah and Henry will have to confront their past and work through their feelings, but the specifics are left unclear. I'm hoping the book will deliver on its promise of "holiday chaos" and "complicated feelings," but for now, I'm left with more questions than answers.
