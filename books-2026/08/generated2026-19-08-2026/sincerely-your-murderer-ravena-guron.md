**Author:** Ravena Guron  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-13 APL

The air in this August heat is thick with the kind of humidity that makes even the most disciplined reader’s spine prickle—like the moment you realize you’ve left your first-edition *The Great Gatsby* in the backseat of your car, now warped by sun and the slow, inevitable decay of time. And yet, here we are, because *Sincerely, Your Murderer* arrives like a text from a stranger you can’t ignore: a promise of chaos, a dare to engage. Ravena Guron’s latest—already being compared to the kind of YA thrillers that make bookstores smell like popcorn and regret—isn’t just another countdown to doom. It’s a slow-burning letter bomb, addressed to readers who still believe in the thrill of the unsolved, the way a mystery can curl around your ribs like a question mark.

The premise is so *obviously* a setup—seven days, seven sins, seven events that spiral into a murder—that it’s almost a shame it’s not more. Almost. Because the real genius here isn’t the structure; it’s the *psychology* of the threat itself. A letter that doesn’t just announce a death but *names* it, *dates* it, *assigns* it—this isn’t just a killer’s manifesto. It’s a performance. And performances, as any fan of *One of Us Is Lying* or *The Final Five* knows, are all about the audience. Who is this letter for? Is it for Kay, the protagonist, or for *us*, the readers, who are being dragged into the same game? The synopsis doesn’t say. And that’s the problem—because the vagueness here isn’t just a lack of detail; it’s a *choice*. Guron is playing with the idea that the scariest part of a threat isn’t the violence, but the *uncertainty* of who it’s aimed at. Is this a personal vendetta? A social experiment? A digital ghost from Kay’s past? The synopsis leaves it all hanging, which is either a masterstroke or a cop-out, depending on whether you trust the author to deliver.

But let’s be honest—this isn’t the first time we’ve seen a countdown to murder. The trope is as old as *Clue* itself, and yet, Guron’s name carries weight because she’s done it before, and done it *well*. *This Book Kills* and *Catch Your Death* prove she understands the balance between escalating tension and character-driven stakes. The question is: can she make this one feel fresh? The synopsis doesn’t give us much to go on beyond the obvious—Kay’s return, the letter, the escalating events—but if we’re being generous (and we are, because first editions deserve it), we can assume she’ll lean into the psychological warfare. The letter isn’t just a countdown; it’s a *game*, and games, as any teenager who’s ever played *Among Us* knows, are about trust. Who can you believe? Who’s feeding you misinformation? Who’s actually in on the joke?

Yet—and here’s where the pivot happens—what if the real twist isn’t who the killer is, but *why* they’re doing this? The synopsis doesn’t even hint at motive, which is either a sign of brilliance or a sign that Guron is banking on the reader’s own paranoia to fill in the gaps. Because let’s face it: in a world where social media thrives on performative outrage, where people stage their own "murder mysteries" for clout, the line between art and artifice is thinner than ever. Is this letter a prank? A cry for help? A test? The fact that we don’t know—*that we’re supposed to care*—is what makes this premise so *slippery*. It’s the kind of thing that would make Karen M. McManus nod approvingly, because she knows how to make a reader’s skin crawl with the weight of their own assumptions.

But here’s the thing about summer reads: they’re supposed to be *light*, even when they’re heavy. And while *Sincerely, Your Murderer* promises the kind of relentless pacing that makes you lose track of time (and maybe even your phone), the real test will be whether it can hold its own against the kind of literary sloppiness that turns a promising premise into a forgettable mess. The synopsis is so barebones it’s almost *deliberately* vague, which could mean one of two things: either Guron is playing with form, or she’s too busy counting her words to count her ideas. Either way, it’s a gamble—and gambles, as any book lover knows, are what make the summer season so damn exciting.

And now, because the world is a cruel place and my dry cleaner in Manhattan is currently on strike, I find myself wondering if this book will be worth the wait—or if it’ll just end up like last year’s *True Crime* obsession, gathering dust on a shelf while I’m left holding the receipt for a $20 latte I didn’t need.
