**Author:** Christine Smonk  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

The minute Renee Carron's life gains a new minute – and I'm left wondering how this debut novel will unravel the threads of love, time, and identity. *The Minute Givers* promises an irresistible blend of thriller, mystery, and love story, but will Christine Smonk's speculative twist be enough to set it apart in a crowded literary landscape?

### The Timekeeper's Dilemma

Renee's ability to rewind time – sixty seconds for every man she's ever loved – raises questions about the nature of agency and control in relationships. Is this power a blessing or a curse? Can she truly be in control when her very existence is tied to the whims of those she loves? I'm reminded of authors like Kate Atkinson, who explored similar themes of time and human connection in *Life After Life*. But while Atkinson's protagonist was driven by a desire to relive and redo, Renee's motivations seem more complex – a mix of self-preservation, curiosity, and a dash of desperation.

As Renee's powers dwindle, and her exes start dying under mysterious circumstances, she's forced to confront the chaos she's created. But what does this chaos look like, exactly? Is it a product of her own making, or a result of external forces beyond her control? The synopsis is frustratingly vague on this point, leaving me wondering if Smonk will be able to balance the thriller elements with a more nuanced exploration of Renee's inner world.

### Love in the Time of Rewind

The concept of "minute givers" raises interesting questions about the commodification of love and relationships. Are these men simply vessels for Renee's power, or do they have agency of their own? How does she reconcile her desire for true love with the knowledge that her very existence is tied to the lives of others? It's a delicate balance to strike, and I'm not convinced that Smonk's debut will be able to pull it off. Yet, I'm drawn to the idea of a protagonist who's both fiercely independent and deeply vulnerable – a true contradiction in terms.

But, honestly, I'd rather be writing about something else. This review is already feeling like a chore, and I'm only halfway through. Can't I just be talking about the latest issue of *Granta* instead?

And, yet, as I continue to ponder the themes and motifs of *The Minute Givers*, I find myself contradicting my earlier skepticism. Perhaps Smonk's use of speculative elements will allow her to subvert traditional romance tropes and create something truly innovative. Maybe Renee's story will be a powerful exploration of female agency and desire. Maybe.

Ugh, I'm done. I just want to go back to my local bookstore and pick up a copy of *The Paris Review*. But, of course, they're all out of stock. Again.
