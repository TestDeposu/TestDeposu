**Author:** Eva Jurczyk  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-06 APL

Sweating through grading papers while the August heat radiates relentlessly through these warped windowpanes does absolute wonders for my disposition, which brings us to *The Paris Files: A Novel* by Eva Jurczyk, landing this autumn with the sort of aggressive publisher fanfare that usually promises a deeply exhausting exercise in narrative manipulation. We are supposed to swoon over Genie St. Onge diving head-first into a whirlwind romance with Jacob Ford in the eleventh arrondissement—sugared pastries, rumpled bedsheets, the whole cliché Parisian tourist board package—before the man promptly vanishes into thin air like a bad investment or a teenager promising to clean the classroom storage closet, leaving behind a trail of conflicting police interviews that supposedly question whether Genie's breathless version of reality actually happened. Blurbs from Lucy Foley and Rosie Walsh try to sell this as a stylish, beguiling puzzle box of unreliable memory and missing persons paranoia, but I am already fiercely skeptical of any thriller relying on the tired trope of the hysterical woman whose story does not align with the local authorities, because honestly, people lie, relationships are messy, and the synopsis gives us zero structural reason to care about these two strangers playing house in a dreamy apartment except for the vague, obligatory promise that someone might have snatched him off the cobblestone streets. 

Actually, scratch that completely. 

Maybe the structural ambiguity is the entire point, functioning as a sharp deconstruction of how easily we invent entire futures with total strangers just to escape the sweltering boredom of our daily routines. 

Fine. 

Maybe it will actually surprise us. 

Hard pass. 

The absurd cost of braces these days is giving me a massive headache.
