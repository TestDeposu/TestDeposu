**Author:** Madison Tyler Nelson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-06 APL

Madison Tyler Nelson is dropping *Portrait of a Haunting* in late 2026, and based on the publisher’s boilerplate, we are getting another round of hereditary trauma masquerading as high-stakes supernatural horror. Look. If you are going to put a protagonist in a chokehold with her own bloodline—in this case, an infamous artist mother who used a lover's claret for her final canvas—you better deliver some actual striking force in the prose. Otherwise, it is just narrative shadow-boxing. 

### The Footwork of Trauma

Tatum Alexander spends her life dodging the Sight like a washed-up heavyweight dodging a right hook in the twelfth round. And why? Because her mother turned domestic violence into an avant-garde palette. Groundbreaking stuff, really. 

Yet, let us pause for a second. The coven-and-best-friend safety net is pure narrative padding. Actually, strike that. I am completely revising my stance on the coven angle because Manhattan witches hiding from municipal property taxes while managing ghost drama sounds like the kind of chaotic energy this genre desperately lacks. 

### Canvas and Concussion

The synopsis leans hard into the "price must be paid" trope. A dead best friend. An eternally perplexing mother—honestly, what does that even mean, is she doing avant-garde interpretive dance in a maximum-security prison yard?—and a boyfriend who refuses to tap out. The pacing here needs absolute precision. If Nelson lets the middle rounds drag with endless exposition about spectral etiquette, the reader is going to throw in the towel faster than a glass-jawed bantamweight. 

We are looking at a classic pedigree play here. Think *The Picture of Dorian Gray* if Oscar Wilde spent too much time scrolling through BookTok occult trends. The thematic mechanics rely entirely on whether Nelson treats the blood-painting motif with genuine visceral dread or just uses it as edgy window dressing for a standard supernatural whodunit. 

### The Championship Rounds

The climax hinges on a decades-old secret. Naturally. Because no modern gothic thriller can survive without a dusty skeleton rattling in the family closet. But give me some kinetic energy. I want to see these characters actually take a hit and keep moving forward instead of endlessly whining about their generational curses. 

My kingdom for a protagonist who does not need a support group of Manhattan spellcasters to throw a punch at destiny. 

Still, I have to pack three lunches that will inevitably return uneaten.
