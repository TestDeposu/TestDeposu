**Author:** Will Shindler  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

The ghosts of trauma, how they haunt us still. *Chasing Demons* promises to be a thrilling exploration of the dark forces that shape our lives, but will it deliver more than just a tired retread of familiar horror-thriller tropes? The premise, while intriguing, feels all too familiar – a man with a traumatic past, a series of gruesome murders, and a dash of the supernatural. If I had a nickel for every time I've read this premise, I'd be able to buy a decent cup of coffee by now.

The setup, at least, shows some promise. Joel Sampson, our protagonist, has a compelling backstory – a massacre at a Young Persons' Mental Health Institute, a life rebuilt as a schoolteacher and devoted father. The return to his hometown of Tayborough, however, feels like a contrived attempt to stir up old demons (no pun intended). And what's with the convenient murder of his former therapist? It's a plot device that screams "inciting incident," but will it be enough to propel the story forward?

One of the most interesting aspects of the synopsis is the blurring of lines between reality and the supernatural. As Joel delves deeper into the mystery, he becomes convinced that something otherworldly is at play. It's a bold move, but will the author be able to pull it off without resorting to cheap tricks and convenient plot twists? The comparison to authors like Joe Hill or Paul Tremblay comes to mind – both masters of the horror-thriller genre, but will *Chasing Demons* be able to stand alongside their works?

But what really gets my goat is the vagueness of the synopsis. What exactly are these "dark forces" that have shadowed Joel's life? Is it a literal entity, or just a metaphor for his own trauma? And what's the significance of the 23-year gap between the original massacre and the present-day murders? The lack of clarity is frustrating, to say the least. It's like trying to grasp a fistful of smoke – the harder you squeeze, the more it slips through your fingers.

As I read through the synopsis, I couldn't help but think of the infamous "Black Dahlia" murders – a gruesome and highly publicized crime that remains unsolved to this day. The parallels between the two are striking, but will the author be able to capture the same sense of dread and unease that permeates the real-life case?

In the end, it all comes down to execution. Can the author deliver a story that's both terrifying and thought-provoking? Or will it devolve into a mess of clichés and predictable plot twists? I'm not holding my breath, but I am curious to see how it all plays out.

Now, if you'll excuse me, I need to go yell at my gardener about the hydrangeas. They're looking a bit wilted, and I won't stand for it.
