**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-06 APL

The synopsis for *Vengefully Matched* arrives like a poorly sealed jar of pickles—bright, tangy, and immediately suspicious of what’s been left out. The premise is a classic rom-com setup, the kind that thrives on opposites attracting, but the execution risks collapsing under the weight of its own contradictions. Priscilla Hart, the runaway bride, is framed as a chaotic force of nature—mischievous, magical, and draped in designer heels—while Agatha Vanderbilt Voisin is the polar opposite, a prim, proper witchery council president who, by all accounts, should find Priscilla’s antics *infuriating*. Yet the synopsis doesn’t linger on the tension between them; it skips past the friction like a bad plot twist, assuming the reader will accept that two such polar opposites can coexist in the same sentence, let alone the same plot.

The idea of a fake dating scheme to clear Priscilla’s name is a trope so well-worn it’s practically a spell itself—think *The Hating Game* meets *Bridgerton*, but with more hexes and fewer historical costumes. The problem isn’t the premise; it’s the vagueness. Where’s the chemistry? Where’s the *real* conflict? The synopsis mentions "late-night arguments" and "slow-burn attraction," but it doesn’t tell us what those arguments are about—just that they exist. Is this a battle of wills, or a battle of wits? Is Agatha’s patience a facade, or is Priscilla’s chaos actually charming? The synopsis doesn’t say. It just assumes we’ll believe that two people who can’t stand each other will somehow fall in love, which is the kind of lazy storytelling that makes me want to throw a spell of my own—preferably one that erases the entire book from existence.

And let’s talk about the villain. The synopsis drops a cryptic line about "something wicked brewing in Hallowcross," but it doesn’t specify what that something is. Is it a rogue witch? A cursed artifact? A political conspiracy within the Witchery Council? The lack of detail is frustrating, like reading a *Studio Ghibli* script where the villain’s backstory is left to the imagination—except in *Ghibli*, the ambiguity is part of the magic. Here, it feels like a cop-out, a way to avoid committing to a real threat. If the book is going to lean into the witchy rom-com genre, it needs to give us something more than a placeholder for danger.

I respectfully disagree with the publisher’s hype.

The synopsis also hints at a thematic tension between reputation and redemption, but it doesn’t explore how that plays out. Priscilla wants to rebuild her reputation, but is she doing it for herself, or is she trying to prove something to Agatha? Agatha, meanwhile, is the council president—does she have her own reasons for helping Priscilla, or is this just a PR move? The synopsis doesn’t answer these questions, and without them, the emotional core of the story feels thin. It’s like reading a *Jane Austen* novel where the love triangle is never actually fleshed out—you know it’s there, but you can’t quite see the strings pulling it.

The ADHD tangent: I can’t even. Why does every sapphic rom-com these days have to be about witches? It’s like the genre is stuck in a loop of *The Craft* nostalgia, where every magical girl is either a rebel or a victim, and the only way to resolve the conflict is through a grand gesture of love. Where’s the nuance? Where’s the *messiness*? *Vengefully Matched* seems determined to be another entry in this trend, but without the depth of something like *The Song of Achilles* or the subversion of *The House in the Cerulean Sea*. It’s not that the premise is bad—it’s that it’s been done before, and done better.

Literally.

The book’s release date is 2026, which means it’s either a masterstroke of timing or a desperate gamble. If the trend for witchy sapphic rom-coms is still hot, this could be a sleeper hit. If not, it might get lost in the shuffle like another *BookTok* fad that fizzled out faster than a cursed candle. The synopsis doesn’t give us enough to care, but then again, neither does most of the genre these days.
