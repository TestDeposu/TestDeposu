**Author:** Sophie Li  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-20 APL

Sweating through a heavy linen shirt while the August heat radiates off the pavement outside my open window does little to temper my profound exhaustion regarding the tired clichés of the college romance genre, yet Sophie Li’s upcoming graphic novel *Last Night I Dreamed of Home* somehow manages to catch my eye anyway. Perhaps it is the promise of emotional honesty in a medium that so frequently relies on exaggerated facial expressions and neon-colored bubble tea aesthetics to do the heavy lifting of character development. Alice wants to party like the North Americans on television—a wonderfully specific, mildly cynical nod to media consumption and cultural displacement—while Haoran carries the crushing, guilt-ridden weight of parental sacrifice on his shoulders alongside a secret desire to draw comics instead of staring at spreadsheets all day. And honestly, who among us hasn’t wanted to throw away a pragmatic future for the sweet, terrifying uncertainty of an artistic calling, especially when the alternative is a corporate cubicle and a slow death of the soul?

Wait. Let me rephrase that entirely because I am making it sound like an indie drama about existential dread when the publisher is explicitly marketing a sweetly satisfying romantic comedy. 

Actually, scratch that optimism. 

The synopsis leans heavily on the forced-proximity playbook—stuck elevators, burnt cooking, late-night caffeine runs—which usually signals a painful reliance on tropes that insult the reader's intelligence rather than challenging it, meaning Li walks a razor-thin line between a genuinely touching cross-cultural coming-of-age story and a paint-by-numbers YA graphic novel that belongs on a TikTok shelf rather than a serious critic's desk. Think *Heartstopper* meets a quieter, more academic iteration of *Everything I Never Told You*, though that might be giving the premise too much credit given how vaguely the synopsis glosses over the actual stakes of that mysterious creative opportunity. 

Sigh.

The rising cost of color film for my analog camera is getting entirely out of hand, and quite frankly, paying eighteen dollars for a single roll of Portra 400 just to capture the harsh, blinding afternoon light on my balcony is draining my wallet faster than buying iced lattes to survive this miserable late-summer humidity.
