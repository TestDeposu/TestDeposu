**Author:** Sara Raasch  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-12-29 APL

My favorite character was just murdered in chapter four by a cheap, manufactured shock-value trope, and now I am standing fifty feet from a bass speaker that is actively vibrating my ribcage into dust while some guy in a shredded Misfits shirt screams poetry about garbage cans. God, I love literature. I am also supposedly contractually obligated to write a thousand words on *Lightning & Thunder* by Sara Raasch just to pay rent, because capitalism demands a sacrifice and apparently my sanity wasn't enough. 

### The Mythological Industrial Complex

And what are we getting? More divine nepotism. Because Percy Jackson didn't already pave every damn inch of this road with gold-plated demigod angst. Raasch is throwing us headfirst into a modern pantheon where the descendants of gods act less like majestic arbiters of fate and more like a corrupt corporate board room. Yet the synopsis leans hard into the *Thunderbolts* energy—a crew of morally bankrupt, deeply unhinged vigilantes doing state-sanctioned dirty work. It is peak chaotic neutral garbage, and frankly, I am parasocially obsessed. 

### The Aesthetic of the Disaster Bisexual

Let’s talk about the character dynamics, or rather, the heavily marketed homoerotic tension. We have Pollux “Lux” Keraunos, a cyber-crime vigilante who is literally described as a disaster bisexual with a savior kink, which is an exquisite masterclass in pandering to Twitter booktokers right before they descend into feral madness. And his foil? Erik Thorson. A Viking-wannabe BFF who is smoking hot and actually possesses a functional moral compass. The juxtaposition here is delicious. It’s high-concept urban fantasy colliding with low-brow enemies-to-lovers romcom tropes. If Sara Raasch doesn't deliver agonizing, slow-burn emotional devastation wrapped in snappy one-liners, I am throwing my e-reader directly into the mosh pit. 

### The Structural Gamble

We need to critique the premise itself. A tyrannical Hero Council? A missing brother? A mandatory training arc disguised as an assassination plot? It is aggressively tropey. It’s giving YA lit-agent checklist stamped onto a mythology textbook. But sometimes you want a thoroughly predictable literary McDouble. You want the structural predictability of a romance novel where the grumpy hacker slowly realizes the golden retriever boy has a heart of gold. Or maybe I am just projecting because the author of my current reading material clearly hates joy and anyone with a pulse. 

### The Editorial Abyss

Look, I am just trying to hit a word count here so I can afford an overpriced IPA and forget the aforementioned fictional massacre. The synopsis is vague in that breathless, hand-waving way that screams the middle hundred pages are going to be a pacing nightmare of unnecessary political exposition about divine taxation or council bureaucracy. But let's be honest. We aren’t reading this for the intricate world-building of a modern Olympus. We are reading it for the unhinged banter and the sheer audacity of giving a Zeus descendant a cyber-crime aesthetic. 

Anyway, I have to go now because I am late for an underground poetry reading in a damp basement that will inevitably be terrible, and my e-reader battery is blinking red.
