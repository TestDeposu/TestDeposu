**Author:** Sarah Hawley  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-12-08 APL

Another late-stage YA fantasy trilogy promises to grind whatever is left of our collective sanity into cheap glitter. Sarah Hawley’s *Warrior of the Shards* arrives at the end of 2026, dragging the carcass of the *Shards of Magic* series across the finish line. Because nothing says high-stakes political maneuvering like a protagonist whose primary obstacle is her own hyper-ventilating remorse. 

Princess Kenna of Blood House has made a choice. And like every mediocre monarch in a sub-par grimdark clone, that choice immediately detonates the peace, gets her captured, and hands the keys to the kingdom to two faeries "crueler and more ambitious than even the late tyrant king." Truly, the Fyre Festival of governance. What an absolute shock. 

### The Currency of Guilt

The synopsis leans heavily on the tired trope of the angsty royal who worries her "humanity" is slipping away because she has to make hard choices in a civil war. Oh, spare me. Hundreds of faeries are dead, the Accord is shattered, and Kenna is sitting in a dungeon crying about whether she still has a soul. This isn't dark fantasy. It is an extended wellness retreat for people who find Abercrombie too abrasive. Real power requires cold, calculated ruthlessness—not a multi-chapter internal monologue about the loss of one's precious internal moral compass. If your kingdom is collapsing because of a single strategic misstep, you deserve the guillotine, not a redemption arc.

Yet we are expected to buy into this manufactured anguish. Hawley wants us to believe that Kenna’s "guilt" is a weapon, a heavy burden that proves she cares. But angst is not an aesthetic. It is an endurance test for the reader. 

### The Romance Crutch

And then, naturally, we have the anchor. The synopsis makes sure to mention that Kenna has found "someone who loves her just as fiercely as she loves him." Because a bloody peasant rebellion and a fractured throne simply cannot function without a mid-war soulmate subplot to lower the intellectual bar. Nothing dilutes a high-stakes political collapse quite like whispered sweet nothings between torture sessions. It is the literary equivalent of a corporate merger brokered over artisanal sourdough. 

### The Inevitable Horizon

I predict three hundred pages of padded imprisonment, predictable betrayals, and an ending where the power of mutual affection saves the realm from the terrifyingly generic super-villain faeries. No actual structural critique of power. Just safe, sanitized suffering designed for readers who think reading Sarah J. Maas counts as studying Machiavelli. 

My driver is waiting outside idling a V12 engine that burns more cash per minute than these authors make in royalties, and I am entirely too late for a painfully dull charity gala where I have to force down artisanal sea salt canapés and pretend to care about corporate philanthropy.
