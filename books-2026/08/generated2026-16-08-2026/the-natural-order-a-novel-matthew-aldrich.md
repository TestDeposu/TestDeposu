**Page Count:** Unknown

The book synopsis has a certain... allure to it. *The Natural Order*, a novel by Matthew Aldrich, promises an unflinching look at our dystopian future. Paul Whitmore, the world's wealthiest man, has cracked the code to eternal life—a notion that should excite us, yet the author's warning signs are waving frantically like a reckless driver on a highway.

The setup is straightforward: a billionaire with a foolproof plan to stop aging, an extremist group hell-bent on foiling his plans, and a tense reunion with his estranged son. It's a familiar, yet intriguing combination—think *The Road* meets *The Immortalists* with a dash of *Black Mirror*. The stakes are clear: the future of humanity hangs in the balance, and the protagonist's fragile family bond is put to the test.

However, I find myself questioning the vagueness of it all. What does this "method" really entail? How does it work? These unanswered questions leave me with an unsettling feeling, as if the author is deliberately withholding crucial information. It's like being shown a fascinating but incomplete blueprint—enough to pique my curiosity, but not enough to fully commit to the project.

This is where *The Natural Order* starts to feel like a Frankenstein's monster of themes and ideas. We have our familiar dystopian warning signs, our tech-obsessed billionaire, and our family drama. It's a mix that should resonate with fans of authors like Kim Stanley Robinson and Margaret Atwood, but somehow it feels... unrefined. Like a still-learning screenwriter trying too hard to make a point.

Yet, despite my reservations, I can see the sparks flying. There's a potential richness here—this is a novel that could grapple with issues of technology, longevity, and the human condition in ways that would make for a thought-provoking and unsettling read. Aldrich has set the stage for a thought-provoking exploration of what it means to be human in a world where our mortality is a distant memory.

I'm not yet convinced that *The Natural Order* will deliver on its promise, but I'm willing to give it a shot. With the right execution, this could be a haunting and humane novel that lingers in the reader's mind long after the final page is turned.
