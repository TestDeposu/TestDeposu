Steam hisses from the commercial wand behind me, drowning out the miserable screech of another customer ordering a half-caf macchiato with far too many syllables. I am wiping down a sticky stainless-steel counter for the fourth time this hour, glaring at the dark puddles left by the morning rain while waiting for the espresso machine to stop groaning. And yet, I am expected to sit here and dissect *The Invisible Roommate* by Timothy Janovsky. Joyous. Truly, nothing screams literary terror quite like a premise crossing HGTV house-hunting with H.G. Wells on a budget. 

### The Sub-Basement of Romance Tropes

The synopsis tells us we are getting an autumnal love story where a pop star escapes fame on a crumbling Chesapeake Bay island and accidentally shacks up with an invisible graduate school drop-out. Because nothing says gothic dread quite like forced proximity in a damp Victorian fixer-upper. Wait. Let me correct myself immediately. Maybe the dampness is actually doing heavy lifting here. Maybe the mold is sentient. Or maybe I am just desperately searching for a phantom slice of dread in a book that sounds aggressively engineered for a TikTok book club. 

And look, I usually love a good monster feature. Give me Cronenberg body horror or Shirley Jackson's slow-acting psychological rot any day of the week. But marrying the classic tale of isolation with a reality dating show hook—*Love is Blind* meets biological invisibility—feels less like a descent into the macabre and more like a fever dream hatched in a sterile writers' room. Can we talk about the sheer absurdity of hiding from the local police while conducting groundbreaking invisibility research in a rental? Actually, let us not talk about it. I have three hundred words left to write before my shift mercifully ends, and my caffeine-addled brain refuses to unpack the jurisprudence of spectral trespassing.

### Autumnal Bleakness Versus Manufactured Quirp

The thematic stakes here allegedly hinge on vulnerability and secrets as Halloween approaches. But where is the blood? Where is the psychological devastation of watching your own body slowly vanish from the earthly plane? Instead, we get a cute autumnal backdrop. Give me actual ghosts. Give me a protagonist losing his mind to isolation rather than a pop star whining about chart rankings. Though, to be fair, maybe Janovsky is slipping a sharp little critique of surveillance capitalism under the rom-com rug. Or maybe I am projecting my deep, abiding hatred for open-plan living onto a fictional Victorian house. 

Let us be entirely honest with ourselves for a fraction of a second. I am only tapping away at this keyboard because my rent skyrocketed by twenty percent and the tips this week have been abysmal. If I wanted to read about rich men crying in remote locations, I would read standard literary fiction. Yet here we are. Waiting for the invisible shoe to drop. 

The new taxes on luxury imports are seriously impacting my wine collection.
