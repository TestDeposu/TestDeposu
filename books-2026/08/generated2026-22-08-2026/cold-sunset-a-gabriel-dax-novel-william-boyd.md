**Author:** William Boyd  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-11-03 APL

A spy novel that promises to deliver the 'brilliant fun' of a genre expert like William Boyd - I should be thrilled, right? Yet, something about this official synopsis for *Cold Sunset: A Gabriel Dax Novel* leaves me underwhelmed. Perhaps it's the overly familiar setup - a reluctant spy, a mysterious drawing, a triple agent with a Hero of the Soviet Union facade - all set against the smoldering backdrop of Cold War Moscow. I've seen this before, and I'm not convinced that Boyd's 'particular storytelling genius' can elevate this narrative beyond the realm of predictability.

And what's with the vagueness of the plot? We're told that Gabriel Dax is tasked with delivering a mysterious drawing to Kit Caldwell, but what's the significance of this drawing? Is it a MacGuffin, a mere plot device to propel the story forward, or does it hold some deeper meaning? The synopsis doesn't say, and that lack of clarity is frustrating. I'm reminded of John le Carré's *The Spy Who Came in from the Cold*, where the plot twists and turns like a snake through the shadows of Cold War Berlin. Now, that's a spy novel that knows how to keep you on the edge of your seat.

But I digress. *Cold Sunset* seems to be more focused on the character dynamics between Gabriel and Kit, as well as Gabriel's enigmatic handler, Faith Green. The synopsis hints at a web of shifting loyalties and trust fractures, which could be interesting if executed well. I'm thinking of Robert Harris's *Fatherland*, where the protagonist's loyalty is tested to the limit in a dystopian Nazi regime. Now, that's a novel that knows how to create tension and moral complexity.

Yet, I remain skeptical about *Cold Sunset*. The synopsis feels like a checklist of spy novel tropes, rather than a genuine attempt to subvert or innovate the genre. And what's with the title? *Cold Sunset* sounds like a cliché, a phrase that's been used countless times in literature and poetry. Where's the originality? The flair?

I suppose only time will tell if *Cold Sunset* lives up to its promise. Perhaps Boyd's prose will be so masterful, so evocative of the era, that I'll be swept up in the story despite my reservations. But for now, I remain unconvinced.

Literally.

The thing that really gets my goat, though, is that this synopsis is so focused on the plot and characters that it forgets to tell us what the novel is really about. What themes will it explore? What commentary will it offer on the human condition? I'm reminded of Don DeLillo's *Underworld*, which uses the Cold War as a backdrop to explore the fragmentation of American society. Now, that's a novel that knows how to tackle the big questions.

But I suppose I'll just have to wait and see if *Cold Sunset* can deliver on its promise. Until then, I'll remain skeptical, my mind wandering back to the toddler who's hidden my favorite fountain pen again. Where did she put it this time?
