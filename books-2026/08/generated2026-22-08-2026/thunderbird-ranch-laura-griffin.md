**Author:** Laura Griffin  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-11-10 APL

Flour is currently dusting every single flat surface in my downstairs domain—including my favorite woolen cardigan, which is just fantastic—because I thought it would be a charming domestic endeavor to let the seven-year-old measure out baking soda while I tried to parse Laura Griffin’s upcoming procedural, *Thunderbird Ranch*. And look. When a Texas shindig concludes with a corpse and a bunch of ungrateful heirs pointing fingers at each other like they are starring in a low-rent regional theater production of *The Little Foxes*, we are not exactly treading into uncharted literary territory, are we? Yet there is something delicious about a messy family dynamic when you are ankle-deep in sticky dough and wishing for a glass of something heavy enough to forget that the school bake sale requires organic, gluten-free, emotion-regulated cupcakes by tomorrow morning. 

The synopsis sets up a classic closed-circle-of-suspects thriller—or semi-closed, considering our frantic protagonist is sprinting through a creek bed covered in actual homicide evidence while trying to remember why she agreed to date into this nest of vipers in the first place—and honestly, the vibe screams Shari Lapena meets late-night Lifetime movie marathon, minus the self-awareness. 

My youngest just dumped an entire carton of chocolate chips directly onto the floor, scattering them across the hardwood with the chaotic energy of a rogue feline, and I am choosing to stare straight ahead at my laptop screen rather than scream into the empty mixing bowl. 

Murder.

Griffin is leaning hard into the wealthy-dynasty-in-financial-ruin trope, which feels about as fresh as yesterday’s sourdough starter, but sometimes you just want a straightforward whodunit where everyone is lying through their veneered teeth and the local detective has to wade through enough passive-aggressive Texas charm to choke a mule. Kate Graham—our beleaguered badge-holder—has her work cut out for her with Faye McCann and her disgruntled offspring, and I sincerely hope the manuscript avoids the lazy trap of making the outsider girl the actual killer just because she happened to be jogging through a creek in a bloody panic. 

Predictable.

Give us a real twist, Laura. Or at least give us a sharp-tongued matriarch who uses a shotgun as a walking stick before the final chapter. 

I have to go because the oven timer is screaming at me like a banshee and I need to grade a stack of middle-school persuasive writing essays on the structural decline of modern culture.
