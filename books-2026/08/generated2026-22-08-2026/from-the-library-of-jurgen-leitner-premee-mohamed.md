**Author:** Premee Mohamed  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-27 APL

The air-conditioning in this office is broken again—somehow the humidity outside has seeped through like a bad horror movie plot, and now the synopsis for *From the Library of Jurgen Leitner* feels like it’s been left in the backseat of a car on a summer afternoon. It’s *thick* with the kind of dread that doesn’t just lurk in the margins but *breathes* on the page, like a library book that’s been sitting too long in the stacks. Premee Mohamed and Jonathan Sims—two names that already carry the weight of *The Magnus Archives*’ slow-burn cosmic horror—have crafted something that smells like old parchment and bad decisions, the kind of thing that makes you wonder if you should’ve stayed in bed with the AC blasting instead of reading this.

The premise is *deliciously* vague, which is either a genius move or a sign that the authors are playing a long game with the reader’s patience. Hugh Franklin and Sebastian Everett—two men who, despite working in the same cursed institution decades apart, are bound by forces they don’t yet understand. One is a translator, the other an acquisition specialist, both drawn to the library like moths to a flame that isn’t quite real. The books themselves aren’t just dangerous—they’re *alive*, and not in the cozy, *Practical Magic* way. There’s a starving cookbook. A scroll that *breezes*. A hardback that *demands* you dig, dig, dig—like something out of a Lovecraftian fever dream, but with the kind of bureaucratic precision that makes you think of *The Library of Babel* meets *The Terror*. And yet, the synopsis doesn’t *tell* you what happens—it just *hints*, like a librarian who’s already seen too much and doesn’t want to look at you too long.

The real hook here is the *structure*. Parallel timelines, two men who never meet but are inextricably linked by the library’s rules—it’s the kind of narrative trick that works best when it feels inevitable, like the way a bad dream loops back on itself. But the synopsis doesn’t give you enough to *grab onto*. Where’s the payoff? Is this a slow descent into madness, like *Annihilation*’s cosmic horror, or is it something more personal, like *The Fisherman*’s quiet dread? The way it’s framed—“free, these books are more than simply unnerving—they’re deadly”—suggests a tone that’s equal parts *Lovecraftian* and *folk horror*, the kind of thing that would make *The Witch*’s atmosphere feel like a Sunday afternoon tea party by comparison. But without more detail, it’s hard to tell if this is going to be a *House of Leaves*-style labyrinth or a *Mexican Gothic*-style slow burn.

And then there’s the *character work*. Hugh Franklin, the translator, sounds like the kind of guy who’s spent too long in archives, his skills honed by books that don’t just *teach* but *corrupt*. Sebastian Everett, the acquisition specialist, is the kind of character who’d make *The Magnus Archives*’ own Elias Ash look like a choirboy—arcane ambitions, a willingness to pretend, a job that’s more about *survival* than scholarship. The way their stories are woven together, decades apart, feels like it’s borrowing from *The Haunting of Hill House*’s temporal play, but with the *cosmic* weight of *The Only Good Indians*. The question is: are they victims, or are they complicit? The synopsis doesn’t say, and that’s the kind of ambiguity that either makes or breaks a horror novel.

The biggest risk here is that the vagueness becomes *too* much. If this is going to work, it needs to *pull the reader in* with the kind of specificity that makes you *feel* the weight of the library’s rules. Right now, it’s all *implied*—the deadliness of the books, the library’s true nature, the fate of Hugh and Sebastian—but without more, it’s easy to imagine this becoming the literary equivalent of a *Twilight Zone* episode that never lands. Compare it to *The Fisherman* or *The Only Good Indians*, and you can see the potential, but without that *sharp* focus, it might just feel like a *really* expensive haunted house attraction.

And then there’s the *summer* factor. August in this office is *oppressive*, the kind of heat that makes you want to curl up with something that’s equal parts *chilling* and *escapist*. This synopsis has that potential—if it delivers on the promise of a library that’s not just a setting but a *character*, one that’s as alive and breathing as the books it contains. But right now, it’s just *hinting*, like a librarian who’s already seen too much and doesn’t want to look at you too long.

The draft on my screen is starting to blur from the heat, and I can’t tell if that’s the AC or the way the synopsis is making my skin prickle. Either way, I’ll be watching for this one—if it lives up to the promise of *The Magnus Archives* and Mohamed’s own *Nebula-winning* chops, it could be the kind of horror that sticks with you like a book you can’t put down, even if you know it’s going to *ruin* you. But if it’s just another *cosmic horror* with too many loose ends, it’ll end up like a half-read book on a summer night, left in the sun to warp.
