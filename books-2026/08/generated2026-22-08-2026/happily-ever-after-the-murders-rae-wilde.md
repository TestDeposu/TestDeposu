Two elderly women, Ida and Jean, on the run for a double murder they committed four decades ago – this is the stuff of which dark, twisted fairy tales are made. And yet, Rae Wilde's *Happily Ever After the Murders* promises to be so much more than that. A mashup of *The Golden Girls*, *Bonnie and Clyde*, and *Thelma and Louise*, this novel seems to be a wildly entertaining, genre-bending romp through the American heartland.

But what's really at the heart of this story? Is it the cat-and-mouse game between Ida and Jean and the detective hot on their heels? Or is it something deeper – a commentary on the societal expectations that have held these two women back for so long? The fact that they're a lesbian couple, forced to keep their relationship a secret for fear of ostracism, adds a layer of poignancy to their tale. It's a reminder that, even in the 1990s, there were still so many barriers to overcome.

And then, of course, there's the fact that they're on the run – a scenario that's both thrilling and terrifying. I'm reminded of the film *Thelma and Louise*, in which two women embark on a similarly ill-fated road trip. But whereas that movie was a commentary on the toxic masculinity that can drive women to desperation, *Happily Ever After the Murders* seems to be more about the power of female friendship and the unbreakable bonds between two women who've been through hell together.

But I'm getting ahead of myself. The real question is – can Wilde pull off this ambitious, genre-bending narrative? Can she balance the dark humor and wit with the deeper themes and emotions? I think she can. I think she's going to create a novel that's both a wildly entertaining page-turner and a powerful commentary on the human condition.

And yet...I'm also reminded of another book, one that promised the world but ultimately fell flat. *The Girls* by Emma Cline – remember that one? It was supposed to be this big, sweeping novel about female friendship and the darker side of human nature. But in the end, it just felt like a bunch of shallow, pretentious navel-gazing. I'm hoping that *Happily Ever After the Murders* won't suffer the same fate.

But I digress. The real test of this novel will be its ability to balance tone and theme. Can Wilde make us laugh and cry and cheer for Ida and Jean, even as we're aware of the darker forces driving them? I think she can. I think she's going to create a novel that's both a wildly entertaining romp and a powerful commentary on the human condition.

And speaking of wildly entertaining romps...have you seen that kids' cartoon *Adventure Time*? I mean, what's up with that show? It's like they took every weird, quirky element from the 1980s and mashed them all together into this bizarre, fantastical world. I mean, I love it, but it's just so...out there. Anyway, I was watching it with my kid the other day, and I started thinking about how it's kind of like *Happily Ever After the Murders* – both are these wildly entertaining, genre-bending narratives that are also secretly deep and meaningful.

But I'm getting off track. The point is – *Happily Ever After the Murders* has the potential to be a truly great novel. It's got the perfect blend of dark humor, wit, and heart. And if Wilde can pull it off, I think we're in for a real treat.
