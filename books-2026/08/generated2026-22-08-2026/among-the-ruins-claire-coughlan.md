**Author:** Claire Coughlan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-12-08 APL

The eternal struggle of the working mother, forever torn between the demands of career and family — a theme that echoes through the decades, and one that *Among the Ruins* promises to tackle with unflinching honesty. Set against the backdrop of 1970s Dublin, this atmospheric mystery follows Nicoletta Sarto, a women's editor at the *Irish Sentinel*, as she becomes embroiled in a complex web of secrets and lies surrounding the death of Helen Leonard.

But what's truly at stake here? Is it merely a question of uncovering the truth behind Helen's demise, or is it something far more insidious? The synopsis hints at a pervasive sense of danger lurking in the shadows, waiting to pounce on Nicoletta's every move. And yet, we're given precious little to go on, leaving me to wonder if the true horror lies not in the external threats, but in the suffocating societal expectations that Nicoletta must navigate.

The parallels to Paula Hawkins's *The Girl on the Train* are already beginning to form in my mind — the same sense of claustrophobic tension, the same blurring of lines between truth and deception. But where Hawkins's protagonist was a hot mess of instability, Nicoletta appears to be a more measured, calculating individual, driven by a fierce determination to uncover the truth. And yet, it's precisely this determination that may prove to be her undoing.

Let's face it — the constraints placed on women during this era were nothing short of suffocating. The pressure to conform, to prioritize domesticity above all else, must have been overwhelming. And Nicoletta, with her twin baby girls at home, is no exception. Her decision to pursue a career, to take on a role that's traditionally male-dominated, is a bold one, but it's also a decision that comes with a price.

But what of Helen Leonard herself? The enigmatic aunt, whose death sets off a chain reaction of events that will change Nicoletta's life forever. What secrets did she take to her grave, and why did she leave everything to her nurse, a mysterious woman who's now vanished into thin air? The more I think about it, the more I'm convinced that Helen's story is the true heart of this novel, the key to unlocking the mysteries that Nicoletta is so desperate to uncover.

And yet, despite my growing excitement, I'm also left with a sense of trepidation. Will *Among the Ruins* prove to be a genuine game-changer, a novel that challenges the status quo and pushes the boundaries of the genre? Or will it succumb to the same tired tropes and conventions that have come to define the mystery genre? Only time will tell, but for now, I'm willing to take the risk.

Literally.

But as I sit here, pondering the possibilities, I'm reminded of the countless other novels that have tackled similar themes with varying degrees of success. *The Silent Companions*, *The Witch Elm*, *The Last Mrs. Parrish* — all of these novels have explored the darker side of human nature, the secrets that we keep, and the lies that we tell. And so, the question remains: what sets *Among the Ruins* apart from the rest?

I need to go find where the dog hid my only good pair of dress shoes.
