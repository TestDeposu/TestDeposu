**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Flour is currently dusting every single flat surface in my kitchen because my eight-year-old insisted we bake snickerdoodles while I caught up on upcoming releases. And honestly? Emily Thiede's *Gilded Wings* looks like the exact kind of high-octane sugar rush I need right now. 

### Sugar, Spies, and Scaly Things

The premise leans heavily into historical-adjacent fantasy, invoking *The Buccaneers* and *Newsies* like some glorious, chaotic period-piece fever dream. Yet, let us be entirely real for a second. Why must every YA heroine run away from an unwanted betrothal to become a factory worker? Can a girl just stay home and eat a scone for once? Give me a protagonist who embraces an arranged marriage for the tax benefits. Still, the idea of wearing jeweled, vibrant attire strictly as a tactical defense against nocturnal dragon attacks is wildly inventive. I will forgive a thousand cliches for that singular bit of world-building. 

Speaking of world-building, I am experiencing severe flashbacks to that wretched middle-grade dragon series my youngest brought home last month—the one with the pastel covers and the prose so utterly devoid of caffeine that I actually fell asleep sitting upright against the radiator. That book traumatized my reading soul. 

### The Clandestine Charm of the Newsie

Enter Gentry. A street-wise, devilishly handsome newsie hiding a dark secret. Because of course he is. And naturally, he mistakes our runaway heroine for a pickpocket who conned him out of something priceless. It is a dance as old as time—or at least as old as every mid-2010s YA paperback collecting dust on my overcrowded lower shelves. But add cat-sized, thieving miniature dragons called infaerna going missing? And factories bursting into flames? Suddenly I am paying attention. 

And look, I am already deeply invested in Gentry and Stella's inevitable undercover infiltration among the wealthy elite. Do not talk to me about pacing or predictability. I do not care if the plot twists are visible from space. I want the banter. I want the high-society dresses that double as armor. I want the absolute architectural nightmare of a city on the brink of structural collapse. Thiede clearly knows how to craft a swoony dynamic—anyone who read *The Last Bloodcarver* can attest to her penchant for dangerous magic and snappy dialogue—and I am planting my flag firmly in this camp. 

Just let the central mystery actually make sense by chapter twenty. That is all I ask. 

Wait. The smoke alarm is not just chirping. It is performing a full aria. My daughter's papier-mâché volcano for her planetary science project has somehow caught the stray heat of the oven and is actively, brilliantly on fire.
