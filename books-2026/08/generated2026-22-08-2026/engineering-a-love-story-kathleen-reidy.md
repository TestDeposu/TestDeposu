**Author:** Kathleen Reidy  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-06 APL

—In the grand tradition of women's fiction, where love and career are perpetually at odds, *Engineering a Love Story* promises to deliver a witty, warm exploration of what it means to be a smart woman in a male-dominated field. But as I read the synopsis, I couldn't help but wonder: how much of this is truly new?

Chelsea Lefevre, our protagonist, is a software engineer turned tech advisor, who finds herself alone after a mortifying slip-up. Her journey into the 21st century is marked by a series of safe, calculated decisions, until she meets Seth Murphy, a magnetic sales executive who threatens to upend her carefully constructed life. It's a familiar setup, reminiscent of Jennifer Weiner's *Good in Bed* or Emily Giffin's *Something Borrowed*, where the protagonist's personal growth is inextricably linked to her romantic relationships.

But what sets *Engineering a Love Story* apart, I hope, is its thoughtful exploration of the tech industry's misogyny. As someone who's witnessed firsthand the ways in which women are marginalized in STEM fields, I'm eager to see how Kathleen Reidy tackles this issue. Will Chelsea's experiences be a nuanced, thought-provoking commentary on the systemic barriers that prevent women from advancing in tech? Or will it devolve into a simplistic, "girl power" narrative?

### The Privilege of Risk

And then there's the question of risk. Chelsea's decision to pursue a secret affair with Seth is framed as a bold, empowering move, but I worry that it reinforces the notion that women must choose between their careers and their personal lives. What about the women who can't afford to take risks? The ones who are already marginalized, already struggling to make ends meet? Do they get to have love stories too?

You know what really gets my goat? The way kids' cartoons always portray female characters as being obsessed with romance. I mean, take *The Little Mermaid*, for example. Ariel gives up her entire life, her family, her voice, for a guy she's known for like five minutes. It's like, hello, girl, you're a mermaid! You have the entire ocean at your fingertips! Why are you settling for some bland, human dude?

But I digress. *Engineering a Love Story* has the potential to be a thought-provoking exploration of what it means to be a woman in a male-dominated field. I just hope it doesn't rely on tired tropes or simplistic solutions.

As I read the synopsis, I couldn't help but think of the women who've come before Chelsea – the ones who've fought for equality, for recognition, for the right to be taken seriously. Will *Engineering a Love Story* honor their legacy, or will it reinforce the status quo?
