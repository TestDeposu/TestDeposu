**Author:** Ashley Audrain  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

The Things You'll Never Know - a title that reverberates with the crushing weight of secrets left unspoken. And yet, it's this very premise that draws me in, a moth to the flame of Ashley Audrain's unflinching examination of motherhood, desire, and the performative identities we don to conceal our true selves.

As I delve into the synopsis - no, scratch that - as I grapple with the sparse, tantalizing details Audrain has chosen to share, I'm struck by the eerie sense of disconnection that pervades this narrative. A mother, Fiona, lies on the hallway floor, her body taken away by the ambulance, yet she remains, a spectral presence haunting the lives of her loved ones. It's a bold, disorienting choice, one that defies the conventions of traditional storytelling. And I'm intrigued.

But what of Fiona's family, those who are left to pick up the pieces of her life? Her husband, Adam, her children, her best friend - all seem to be hiding something, their faces masks that conceal the truth. It's a hall of mirrors, where reflections distort and reality blurs. Audrain's exploration of the tension between our public personas and private selves is a timely one, a scathing critique of the societal expectations that suffocate us.

And then, of course, there's the diary - Fiona's secret confessional, where the stunning betrayal she had been hiding is finally revealed. But what does this betrayal entail? Is it a revelation that will shatter the fragile bonds of family, or a truth that will set them free? Audrain's vagueness is a clever ploy, a deliberate withholding of information that only serves to heighten the sense of anticipation.

Or does it? Perhaps I'm just frustrated by the lack of concrete details, the absence of a clear narrative thread to cling to. Maybe I'm just not patient enough, too accustomed to the instant gratification of plot reveals and tidy resolutions. But that's the thing about Audrain's writing - it's not about providing answers; it's about posing questions, probing the dark recesses of the human experience.

Yet. And yet. As I ponder the themes of *The Things You'll Never Know*, I'm reminded of the works of Ottessa Moshfegh, another author who excels at excavating the complexities of female desire, the ways in which we perform our identities to appease the expectations of others. There's a kinship here, a shared concern with the ways in which societal pressures can suffocate us.

Literally.

But what of Fiona's ultimate question - will we find who we really are, before we die? It's a query that hangs in the air, a challenge to the reader to confront their own mortality, their own sense of self. And it's here, in the face of this existential uncertainty, that Audrain's writing truly comes alive.

My youngest just painted the family cat blue.
