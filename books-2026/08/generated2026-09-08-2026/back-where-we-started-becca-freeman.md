**Author:** Becca Freeman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-13 APL

Ugh, fine. Let’s talk about *Back Where We Started*—because nothing says "emotional whiplash" like a Hollywood star getting dragged back to her small-town roots to confront the ghost of her first love. And by "ghost," I mean a guy who probably still lives in the same crappy apartment he did in high school, judging by the synopsis’s *deliciously* vague descriptions of "different paths."

The premise is *so* 2023, it’s almost funny—except it’s not, because the formula works. Katie, our overachieving actress, has traded Dutch Ridge for the glitz of Hollywood, where she’s presumably getting paid to pretend to be someone else. But here’s the kicker: she’s still *there*—in every street, every memory, every time she sees Drew’s face pop up like a bad flashback in a slasher movie. The synopsis leans *hard* into the "small town vs. big city" trope, which is fine, but it’s the *psychological* tension that’s actually interesting. Katie’s success feels hollow—she’s got the accolades, but the hollow-eyed exhaustion of someone who’s spent years chasing a dream that’s now just a job. And Drew? Oh, Drew’s *definitely* not the president he once dreamed of being. Maybe he’s a teacher. Maybe he’s a barista. Maybe he’s still waiting for her to come back, like some kind of literary *Groundhog Day* but with more self-help books and fewer time loops.

The thematic meat here is the *addictive nature of achievement*—which, let’s be real, is just a fancy way of saying "burnout." Katie’s life is a glittering cage, and the second she steps back into Dutch Ridge, the bars start to rust. The synopsis doesn’t spell it out, but I can *feel* the unspoken question: *Was the dream ever really hers, or was it just a performance?* And Drew—oh, Drew’s the wild card. Is he the stable anchor she never knew she needed, or is he just another reminder of what she left behind? The synopsis hints at him being "happy," which is *so* suspicious. Happy people don’t make for good second-chance romance material. Happy people are the ones who move on. The real tension lies in whether Drew’s happiness is *earned* or just a front, like one of Katie’s Oscar-worthy performances.

Consider me obsessed. This feels like a *The Love Hypothesis* meets *The Kiss Quotient*—but with the emotional gut-punch of *Normal People*, if Paul and Marianne had ever actually left Dublin. The synopsis doesn’t give us enough to go on, which is *deliberate*, because the beauty of these books is in the *implied* drama. Katie’s Hollywood life is a gilded cage, and Drew’s small-town existence is the quiet rebellion. But here’s the thing: the best second-chance romances aren’t about reuniting with the past—they’re about deciding whether to *stay* or go. And this one? It’s going to force Katie to ask herself if she ever really left Dutch Ridge at all.

The only problem? The synopsis is *too* clean. No messy breakups, no red flags, just a vague sense of "they were once great, now they’re complicated." Where’s the *real* conflict? Where’s the moment that makes you lean in, heart racing? Because right now, it’s all just setup. And setup is *boring*—unless you’re setting up a horror movie, and even then, you’d want more than a "promise to her sister" to justify the dread.

I’ll admit, I’m curious. Maybe this is the rare second-chance romance that doesn’t rely on clichés. Maybe Drew isn’t the guy who’s been pining for her all these years, but the one who’s *changed*—and that change is the real obstacle. Or maybe Katie’s Hollywood success is a front, and she’s just as lost as she was in high school. The synopsis doesn’t say. And that’s the problem with *Back Where We Started*—it’s promising a lot, but it’s not *showing* anything yet.

I’m going to go eat cold ramen for the third time today. Maybe it’ll give me the emotional distance to properly dread this book’s inevitable emotional payoff.
