**Author:** Katie Jung  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-09-22 APL

The anticipation is almost palpable, like the whispered secrets of a Gothic mansion on a stormy night. *You're the One That I Haunt*, the latest offering from Katie Jung, is an enchanting enigma, waiting to be unraveled. As I peruse the official synopsis, I am struck by the tantalizing prospect of a love triangle that defies the boundaries of life and death.

And what a delicious conundrum it is! Libby Lakoski, our beleaguered heroine, finds herself entangled in a web of emotions, torn between her lifelong crush, Carter Coldwell, and his handsome, mischievous cousin, Trip—a ghostly presence who only she can discern. Yet, their objectives seem at odds: Libby wishes to prevent the wedding, while Trip's "unfinished business" necessitates its completion. It is a dilemma that will keep readers on the edge of their seats, pondering the intricacies of Libby's psyche and the Coldwell curse.

But can we trust the synopsis to reveal the full depth of Jung's narrative? Alas, it leaves us with more questions than answers. Why, for instance, does Carter agree to marry a British diamond heiress if his heart lies elsewhere? What dark secrets is the curse hiding? And how will Libby reconcile her feelings for Trip, given that his very existence is a product of unfinished business? These queries are merely a sampling of the many enigmas that *You're the One That I Haunt* poses, inviting us to speculate about the twists and turns that lie ahead.

I am reminded of the masterful blend of romance, mystery, and the supernatural that has characterized the works of authors like Holly Black and Leigh Bardugo. There is an unmistakable air of atmospheric tension to Jung's writing style, conjuring images of a decaying mansion in New York City's shadows. Will she succeed in weaving a tale that rivals these modern greats? I am cautiously optimistic, sensing that *You're the One That I Haunt* may be the perfect vehicle to explore the complexities of love, loyalty, and the human condition.

Still, one cannot help but ponder the implications of Jung's narrative choices. Does the presence of a ghostly character necessarily invite comparisons to *The Fault in Our Stars* or *The Time Traveler's Wife*? Perhaps, but I would argue that Jung's handling of the genre is more akin to *The Mortal Instruments* series, where the supernatural is a mere backdrop for the exploration of complex relationships.

As my time to depart for Aspen's literary festival draws near—my flight departs three hours from now—my imagination is captivated by the prospect of embarking on this thrilling adventure. I predict that *You're the One That I Haunt* will be an unforgettable read, oscillating between the gothic and the romantic, the paranormal and the poignant. If Jung masterfully navigates this delicate balance, *You're the One That I Haunt* may indeed become a modern classic.
