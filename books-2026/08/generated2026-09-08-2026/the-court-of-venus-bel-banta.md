**Author:** Bel Banta  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-09-29 APL

What's the deal with all these young adult fantasy novels featuring magical courts and ancient prophecies, anyway? *The Court of Venus* by Bel Banta is the latest addition to this ever-growing list, and I'm both excited and skeptical about its potential. As a middle-school teacher, I'm always on the lookout for books that showcase positive representation of youth, and this one seems to tick a few boxes.

### The Unseen Magician

Bianca Mortlake, the protagonist, is a Moonborn magician – a rare breed, apparently. I love how her abilities are shrouded in mystery, even to herself. It's a great way to create tension and curiosity in the reader. But what really caught my attention is the fact that Bianca has been living a quiet life, away from the magical court. It's not every day you see a YA protagonist who's not eager to jump into the fray. This could be a great opportunity to explore themes of identity, duty, and the weight of expectations.

Now, let's talk about Roland, the infuriatingly alluring Venusborn magician. I'm getting some serious *A Darker Shade of Magic* vibes from this character – you know, the whole "rivals-to-lovers" trope. It's a bit predictable, but I'm willing to give it a chance. What I'm more interested in is how Roland's character will challenge Bianca's perceptions of herself and her place in the world.

### Courtly Intrigue and the Politics of Power

The magical court in *The Court of Venus* seems like a powder keg of intrigue and backstabbing – literally. Two queens have already met their demise, and Bianca's return could be the spark that sets off a chain reaction of events. I'm reminded of the complex power dynamics in *The Knight and The Moth*, where alliances are forged and broken with ease. It'll be fascinating to see how Bianca navigates this treacherous landscape, especially with Roland by her side.

But here's the thing: the synopsis is a bit too vague about the actual plot. What's the central conflict that Bianca and Roland need to resolve? Is it a murder mystery, a magical tournament, or something else entirely? I'm not sure, and that's a bit frustrating. I hope the book itself will provide more clarity on this front.

### The Astrology of Character

One aspect that really stands out to me is the use of astrology as a way to determine magical abilities. It's a clever way to create a sense of depth and history in the world-building. I'm curious to see how the different planetary influences will shape the characters and their relationships with each other.

Overall, I'm cautiously optimistic about *The Court of Venus*. It's got all the makings of a compelling YA fantasy novel, with a strong protagonist, complex world-building, and a dash of romance. But I need to see how it all comes together.
