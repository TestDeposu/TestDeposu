**Author:** Marlee Bush  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Marlee Bush is back with *Darling, You're Next*, and frankly, I am already sweating through my cardigan. As someone who treats book characters like my own flesh and blood—worrying over their choices at Sunday dinner and yelling at them through the pages—this synopsis gave me immediate whiplash. Maren Kemp walks into my life as a kindred spirit. She is bruised by grief, sitting in a support group, trying to glue her life back together. And then Topher Kowalski appears. Patient. Attentive. Too good to be true. 

### Why This Friendship Circle Is Raising Red Flags

We all know a Topher. Or at least, we have all dated a version of him in our twenties before our married friends staged an intervention over Pinot Grigio. Yet Bush leans into a much darker, sharper psychological terror here. The setup relies on classic tropes—the dead ex-girlfriend whose shadow looms over a new relationship like a tacky velvet painting you cannot get rid of. But what fascinates me as a reader is the emotional manipulation of safety. Maren thinks she has found a soft place to land. Instead, she is walking into a trap disguised as a comfort zone. 

And let us talk about the comparisons. The blurbs mention Freida McFadden and Gillian Flynn, which sets a wildly high bar. Can Bush actually deliver the sharp, domestic claustrophobia of *Gone Girl* combined with the breakneck pacing of *The Housemaid*? Time will tell. But the thematic core of the book—how past relationships haunt our current beds, how memory can be gaslit into nonexistence, and how easily we ignore warning signs just because someone holds our hand in a grief group—feels intensely sharp. 

### Stepping Into Someone Else's Ending

The synopsis admits that past and present begin to blur. Two women. One man. One story with too many gaps. I am inherently suspicious of vague promotional copy, but those gaps are where literary thrillers live or die. If Bush fills those spaces with genuine psychological dread rather than cheap twists, we are in for a brutal ride. I just want to sit Maren down on my porch, pour her a massive glass of iced tea, and demand to know why she is ignoring the exact same habits she noticed in poor Abby. 

Predicting the ending right now? Topher is entirely innocent of murder, but guilty of something infinitely weirder, or he is a textbook sociopath who rehearses empathy in front of a mirror. Either way, I am pre-ordering it. 

Now, if you will excuse me, I need to go ice my foot because I just stepped on a stray Lego brick my nephew left in the hallway.
