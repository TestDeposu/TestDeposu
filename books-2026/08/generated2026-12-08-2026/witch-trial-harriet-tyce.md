Yet, as I sit here, sipping on a lukewarm coffee, I find myself wondering if the execution will live up to the hype — or if it will succumb to the pitfalls of sensationalism, exploiting the very real fears and anxieties that underpin our society. Can Tyce balance the scales of justice and horror, or will *Witch Trial* devolve into a lurid, exploitative spectacle?

And what of the characters, these teenage girls — Eliza, Isobel, and the murdered Christian — whose lives are reduced to a series of soundbites and headlines in the court of public opinion? Are they more than just archetypes, mere pawns in a game of judicial cat-and-mouse, or do they possess a depth, a complexity that will elevate the narrative beyond the realm of pulp fiction? And it is this, I think, that Tyce is attempting to tap into — the deep-seated fears that we all harbor, the darkness that lurks just beneath the surface of our everyday lives.
