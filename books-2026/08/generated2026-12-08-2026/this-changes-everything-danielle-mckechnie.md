**Author:** Danielle McKechnie  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-09-08 APL

The notion that an as-yet-unreleased novel about love and heartache can genuinely subvert our expectations is what sells us the dream. And yet, the official synopsis of *This Changes Everything* by Danielle McKechnie teases us with its vagueness: "a funny, sexy, and heartfelt romance for readers of Yulin Cuang, Rebecca Serle, and Emily Henry." What does it mean by 'funny', 'sexy', and 'heartfelt'? How does it compare to the sassy banter and saccharine romance of *The Hating Game* or the angsty melodrama of *Maybe in Another Life*? We'll just have to wait and see.

I've got no problem with a healthy dose of ambiguity, but the lack of concrete details here hints at a certain superficiality. Romance novels that genuinely tackle the complexities of love, loss, and the existential crises of early adulthood are the ones that stick with us – *The Rosie Project*, *Attachments*, *Conversations with Friends*; those ones know how to dig deep. Does *This Changes Everything* have the mettle to tackle these weighty themes or will it succumb to clichéd tropes and shallow characterizations?

From the get-go, the protagonist Ellie McCain is saddled with an inordinate amount of pressure. We're supposed to feel for this aspiring actress torn between two loves – her childhood sweetheart and the guy who got away – as she navigates the cutthroat world of Hollywood. It's a relatable predicament, to be sure, but I'm not convinced that Danielle McKechnie will bring enough nuance to the table. After all, we've seen this exact same narrative play out in any number of lesser rom-coms.

—But what if I'm wrong? What if *This Changes Everything* surprises us all by subverting our expectations and plumbing the depths of human emotion? Maybe it's exactly the kind of rom-com that will leave us all feeling, well, changed. I mean, we've seen *The Kissing Booth*'s saccharine take on young love work wonders for Netflix viewers, perhaps Danielle McKechnie can work similar magic here.

On the surface, the narrative of *This Changes Everything* bears an uncanny resemblance to the tropes we've come to associate with young adult fiction. Yet the blurb promises something more: a thoughtful, deeply human exploration of love that knows no boundaries. Does this mean that the author has a more expansive vision for the characters and their relationships, or is this just marketing speak? Can we really believe that this will be a truly transformative read?

And then there's the question of the titular "everything". What exactly is Ellie's world turned inside out? Her love life? Her sense of identity? Her dreams of stardom? I want *This Changes Everything* to tackle these questions head-on and emerge with some profound insights – not just another fluffy romance. Am I expecting too much?

Or am I just projecting my own cynicism onto this book? Maybe *This Changes Everything* will be exactly what I need to remind me of the power of romance, the beauty of love's messy, complex messiness. Who knows? The truth is, I'm too jaded to care – and I'll probably end up devouring it anyway.
