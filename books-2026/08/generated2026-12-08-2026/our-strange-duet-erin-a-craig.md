**Author:** Erin A. Craig  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-09-01 APL

Our Strange Duet, the latest endeavour from Erin A. Craig, promises to reimagine Andrew Lloyd Webber's *Phantom of the Opera* from the perspective of Christine Daaé. But— and I'm thinking here, what does it mean to reimagine a classic like this without, I don't know, actually changing anything?— it seems to me that Craig is merely rearranging the furniture in a room we've all been to before. Still, I'm intrigued.

This is going to be an opera-singer's coming-of-age story, with Christine torn between two men: Raoul, her devoted admirer, and the Phantom, the mysterious masked figure who shapes her voice. And what does it mean to be shaped by someone else's passion, someone else's obsession?— that's a question only the book can answer. Yet, already I'm sensing the tropes at work here: the love triangle, the artistic passion driving the plot... it's all a bit too familiar. But Craig is a New York Times bestselling author, after all. One wouldn't be surprised if she has a few tricks up her sleeve.

Now, Christine Daaé sounds like an interesting protagonist. Her voice is a gift she's only just learning to appreciate; she's caught between a safe, warm love and a darker, more complicated obsession. And we all know which way this is going to go, don't we?— I mean, the way it goes with every Phantom retelling. But that's not to say I won't be interested in seeing how Craig handles it. She has to bring something new to the table if she wants to keep this story feeling fresh.

But still, the more I think about it— and this is just a hunch— I'm convinced that *Our Strange Duet* will be a bit of a train-wreck, a mess of clichéd characters and overwrought drama. I mean, have you read any other authors dealing with similar themes and characters?— I'm thinking of books like *The Night Circus* or *The Thirteenth Tale*. These are authors who understand how to make the familiar feel new and exciting again. Craig, on the other hand— I just have a feeling she's going to phone it in.

Which raises the question: can you really call this a reimagining if you're only scratching the surface of the original story?— Or is it just a rebranding, some shallow attempt to cash in on a classic? And yet, and yet... I still want to read it. I'll give her the benefit of the doubt. This could be something special.

Now that I'm thinking about it— but honestly, I'm getting a bit tired of this analysis— I'm starting to wonder if this book is more than just a retelling of *Phantom of the Opera*. Maybe it's something deeper, a exploration of the complicated relationship between creation and creator. After all, the Phantom himself is a musician, a composer who shapes the voice of Christine Daaé. That's some meta stuff right there.

But still, that doesn't change the fact that I'm not exactly blown away by the synopsis— yet. And that's the problem. This book feels like a setup, a stage upon which all the usual characters will strut their stuff. Where's the innovation? The real risk-taking? Craig needs to take a few more chances if she wants to make this book truly memorable.

Anyway, I have a feeling about this book, and it's not exactly positive. I'm thinking of all the times I've read books like this, all the disappointment that comes with them. But hey, maybe I'm just being cruel. Maybe Craig will surprise me, and *Our Strange Duet* will be something amazing.

— I should probably get some coffee if I'm going to finish writing this review.
