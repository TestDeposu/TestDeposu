**Author:** Hallie Shepherd  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-09-22 APL

A fascinating prospect lies before us with *Call Off the Dark*, the debut thriller by Hallie Shepherd. The premise is already generating a mix of excitement and trepidation, much like a tempest brewing on the horizon. It begins with the heart-wrenching loss of Tyler, a soulmate cut down in his prime, leaving behind his bereaved partner Helen. In a desperate attempt to escape the suffocating grip of grief, Helen marries Wade, a near-stranger, and abandons her polished life in Seattle for a tranquil existence in rural Washington.

As Helen struggles to come to terms with her new reality, her husband's brother Isaac appears, raising more questions than answers. Isaac, we're told, has been deceased for years, yet Wade insists they're in grave danger. Here's where *Call Off the Dark* starts to take a dark turn, a thrilling labyrinth where Helen's fragile psyche hangs in the balance.

One cannot help but think of the eerie similarities between this setup and the works of Megan Abbott, a master of subverting our expectations and blurring the lines between reality and madness. The atmosphere is already piqued, and I'm left wondering if this is a slow-burning descent into chaos or a rapid-fire thrill ride. Keep your expectations on the floor before publication day, because the truth often lies in the gray areas.

But, we have a nagging sense of something missing, a gap in the narrative that cries out for an explanation. The vagueness surrounding Isaac's return from the dead and the danger they supposedly face is a glaring omission. It's as if the narrative is deliberately holding back, forcing me to infer and piece together the puzzle in my mind. A deliberate choice, I'm sure, but does it serve the story, or does it simply pander to the reader's morbid curiosity?

Literally.

And yet, with each passing sentence, I find myself drawn back in, eager to unravel the tangled web of relationships and uncover the truth behind Isaac's reappearance. This is a hallmark of Shepherd's skill as a storyteller – she's expertly crafted a situation where nothing is as it seems, and the reader is forced to question everything. The result is a mesmerizing, albeit uncomfortable, exploration of human emotions and the fragility of the human psyche.

As I delve deeper into the synopsis, I begin to see echoes of *The Woman in the Window*, a cinematic thriller where the lines between paranoia and reality are repeatedly blurred. But where A.J. Finn's protagonist is confined within her own walls, Shepherd's Helen is thrust headfirst into a treacherous new reality, one where survival is dependent on trusting the wrong people.

I must admit, I'm both enthralled and unnerved by the prospect of this novel. Will it ultimately prove to be a riveting, twisty ride or a meandering, predictable slog? The only way to find out is to wait and see.
