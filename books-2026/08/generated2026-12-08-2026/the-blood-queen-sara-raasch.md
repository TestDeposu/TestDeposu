**Author:** Sara Raasch  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-06 APL

Cold brew stains another legal pad while I stare down yet another fantasy duology conclusion promising the earth and likely delivering recycled cardboard. *The Blood Queen* by Sara Raasch and Beth Revis drops this coming October, bringing an end to the *The Crimson Throne* sequence — because apparently, nobody is allowed to write a standalone story anymore when they can milk a dual-perspective cash cow. 

### The Burden of Infiltration

The synopsis drops us straight into the exhausting tropes of underground espionage. Alyth and Samson are infiltrating the fae court. Groundbreaking stuff. We get the classic hidden identity panic — Samson has a secret identity that will get him executed on sight, which is the absolute baseline requirement for modern romantasy protagonists. If your male lead does not possess a lethal heritage that makes him public enemy number one, are you even trying to capture the BookTok demographic? Yet, the premise hints at a delicious structural conflict regarding female power. The Faerie Queen has been missing for years, yanked out of existence by the mysterious High Blade, leaving a collection of useless fae princes sitting around doing absolutely nothing. Useless men in high-fantasy courts? Honestly, that might be the most realistic world-building detail Raasch and Revis have ever conceptualized. 

### Romance as the Last Standing Pillar

We are told that Alyth and Samson can only depend on each other and their love. Slop. Absolute romantic essentialism designed to feed the readers who think codependency is a viable survival strategy. But let us look past the marketing gloss. The inclusion of a betrayed-from-within dynamic — discovering the High Blade is someone they never suspected — saves this from being a total structural snooze-fest. It requires actual psychological betrayal rather than standard external punching. If the co-authors lean into the paranoia of courtly politics instead of drowning the narrative in purple prose about cheekbones and leather corsets, this might actually survive the genre's current downward spiral. Think Sarah J. Maas meets a caffeine-addicted editor who just wants the characters to make smart tactical choices for once in their miserable fictional lives. 

### The Spectacle of the Red Cap Army

A bloodthirsty Red Cap army threatening both mortal and fae worlds sounds like an excuse for high-impact cinematic carnage. But I am inherently skeptical of villains who are supposedly intent on world-destruction without a coherent socio-political ethos. Violence for the sake of aesthetic grit bores me. I want structural critique disguised as high fantasy. Give me institutional rot. Give me an antagonist who actually challenges the systemic apathy of these pathetic fae princes rather than just swinging a bloody weapon around like an overgrown toddler. 

Expect this to debut on every bestseller list regardless of its actual literary merit. The machinery is too oiled, the target audience too ravenous for emotionally high-stakes escapism. Whether it justifies its own page count remains to be seen through the haze of cheap espresso and impending deadlines. 

The subway rumble is vibrating right through my noise-canceling headphones again.
