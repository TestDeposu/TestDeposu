**Author:** Alexis Patton  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-09-01 APL

The afternoon heat through these floor-to-ceiling glass windows is currently doing absolute violence to my vintage Bordeaux, and quite frankly, I am in no condition to be parsing promotional copy. Yet here we are. Staring down the barrel of *Us Defiant Few*, the final installment of a trilogy that has thoroughly drained my adrenal glands since late August began sweating down the neck of the city. 

### The Amnesia Industrial Complex

Every single dystopian manuscript hitting desks this season reads like the opera *Tosca* staged inside a CrossFit box. The synopsis for Alexis Patton's upcoming finale leans entirely on the most exhausting trope known to modern publishing: the selective memory wipe. Oh, the man she loves doesn't remember her? How original. One expects nothing less than a full structural collapse of logic when an author relies on the Fëdorov case method of romantic amnesia. Khalani Kanes is apparently running for her life through subterranean corridors—because apparently, nobody is allowed to see grass anymore in contemporary fiction—while dodging a lover holding a blade. Give me strength. Or at least give me a functioning vintage corkscrew that doesn't require manual torque in this humidity.

### Architectural Pathology of Apollo

The promise of returning to "Apollo" feels less like a narrative homecoming and more like a structural threat. We are promised brutal alliances and ruthless training, which is industry shorthand for three hundred pages of characters doing push-ups in the dark while exchanging stilted dialogue about trauma. The synopsis hints at impossible truths and a crisis of perception regarding Khalani's own mind. How terribly chic. We are diving headfirst into the unreliable narrator pool wearing lead boots. Rebecca F Kuang walked so these grimdark heroines could sprint blindly into concrete walls. The prose insists that "only those ablaze will remain," which sounds suspiciously like a marketing team frantically throwing matches at a damp pile of genre tropes. 

### The Price of Admission

Deluxe editions with printed edges are arriving in mailboxes like glossy designer invitations to events nobody actually wants to attend. They look gorgeous on the mahogany shelving, matching the spine of every other blood-soaked paperback currently flooding the market. But I am fundamentally exhausted by the aestheticization of suffering. Patton is undoubtedly delivering the exact brand of high-octane misery that readers devour between sips of iced espresso, but my nerves simply cannot sustain another protagonist questioning her own sanity beneath the earth. Let the sun burn the whole underground city down for all I care. Just keep the air conditioning running and spare me another chapter of existential dread before the autumn vintage arrives.

The new taxes on luxury imports are seriously impacting my wine collection.
