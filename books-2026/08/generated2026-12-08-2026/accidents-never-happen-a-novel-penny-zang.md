**Author:** Penny Zang  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Staring down yet another lukewarm cup of stale roast while my grease-pencil sketches of inverted monuments slide off this groaning, warped mahogany disaster of a desk. Penny Zang wants us to care about Baltimore in 1985—as if historical pastiche is an adequate substitute for actual visceral stakes. *Accidents Never Happen* leans hard on the familiar gothic apparatus, trading genuine transgressive grit for moody window dressing, complete with a literary ghost story that spirals out of control because characters simply refuse to possess basic operational common sense. 

### The Aesthetics of Decay and Cheap Sherry

Edgar Allan Poe as a structural crutch. How delightfully predictable. We get the standard-issue mourning tropes—dead roses, weeping poets, and an atmosphere dripping in performative melancholy. Zang attempts to graft nineteenth-century romanticized despair onto a working-class neighborhood fighting economic entropy, but the juxtaposition feels less like a deconstruction of myth and more like a high-concept Pinterest board manifested into prose. And honestly? The killer leaving a bottle of sherry at every crime scene is giving me major theater-kid energy. It is peak serial killer dramaturgy, absurdly cringe, and entirely too neat for a world supposedly rotting from the edges inward. 

### Narrative Architecture and the Vanishing Father

Disappearances are currency in contemporary suspense. First the patriarch vanishes into thin air, leaving behind a crumbling tavern and massive debt, and then the reclusive sister follows suit because—plot convenience demands it. Mad is left holding the empty bag, frantically racing against a ticking clock while trying to dodge suburban whispers. Zang is clearly gunning for the atmospheric territory staked out by *Long Bright River* or *The Last House on Needless Street*, but those texts earned their psychological dread through prolonged structural claustrophobia. Here, the synopsis promises a breathless hunt that risks trading genuine character interiority for cheap plot-twist gymnastics. Will the missing father be the puppet master, or just another red herring designed to pad out the word count before the inevitable fiery climax? Probably the latter. The literary industrial complex loves a neat thematic symmetry that ties paternal abandonment directly to a madman's calling card. 

### Subverting the Heroic Trajectory

Nobody in this narrative gets to just fix their life without a corpse showing up to complicate the accounting books. Mad is a classic exemplar of the exhausted protagonist—the trope of the put-upon young woman carrying the emotional and financial labor of an entire failing business while the men in the periphery either vanish or murder people. It is tedious. It is exhausting. Frankly, I am tired of heroines whose primary character arc is enduring catastrophic systemic failure while sleuthing on the side. Give me an unreliable narrator who sets the bar on fire herself instead of waiting around for a Gothic specter to do it. If Zang wants to really shock the reader, subvert the Poe obsession—make the girls realize the dead poet was just a miserable hack who wrote fanfiction about grief. 

My wrist hurts from holding this damn charcoal stub, and the deadline for my gallery proposal is looming like an existential guillotine. I have to go scavenge for a decent cup of matcha in this wretchedly gentrified neighborhood before my last remaining nerve endings completely combust.
