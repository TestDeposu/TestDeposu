**Author:** Megan Mostyn-Brown  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-09-22 APL

The quiet weight of a completed spreadsheet and the steady hum of an empty office lingering in the back of my mind leave me uniquely unbothered by the prospect of reading a horror novel about hereditary cannibalism. Yet the premise of Megan Mostyn-Brown’s upcoming *The Need* manages to spark something far more interesting than mere dread: a profound exhaustion regarding how contemporary fiction insists on dressing up domestic dysfunction in monstrous literalism. We are told this electric debut features a rare genetic disorder where individuals must consume human flesh to survive, splitting families into those who view it as a curse and those—like the haute couture Armstrong dynasty of New York City—who treat their apex-predator status with fashionable pride, all while a protective mother flees across the map with a son who cannot resist his bloody impulses and a daughter who simply wants a normal high school existence, complete with TikTok and homecoming dances, which honestly sounds like standard teenage rebellion even without the dietary restrictions. 

Teens wanting a normal life while their mother harbours dark secrets feels aggressively well-trodden. 

### Inheritance as an Entree

One can already trace the lineage of this narrative back through Paul Tremblay’s quiet family terrors and the slick, high-society savagery found in Bret Easton Ellis or modern prestige television, leaning heavily on the metaphor of generational trauma as something literally passed down in the blood like a cursed heirloom. But the synopsis trades in a certain kind of high-concept gloss that leaves the actual texture of the prose entirely hidden behind blurb quotes from Joe Hill and S.A. Cosby, forcing us to wonder if the book will rely on genuine psychological friction or just lean entirely on the shock value of its central conceit. 

Boring.
