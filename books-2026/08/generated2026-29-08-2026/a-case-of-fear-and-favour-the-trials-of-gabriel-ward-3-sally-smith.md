**Author:** Sally Smith  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Staring at yet another Regency romance blurb while my landlord bangs on the radiator for the third time this week does wonders for one's literary perspective. *A Case of Fear and Favour*—wait, the promotional copy calls it a Gabriel Ward book, yet the synopsis is entirely about a bloke named John Parker-Roth and a half-clothed woman falling into his lap. Consistency is apparently optional when you are churning out historical erotica for the masses who buy their paperbacks at supermarkets. 

### The Horticulture of Cliché

The thematic architecture here rests on the tired juxtaposition between botanical detachment and uncontrollable physical urges. John loves his flora. He wants a calm, level-headed female. But wait—actually, men wanting stoic women while aggressively pawns-to-queens kissing half-naked strangers who tumble onto their laps is a foundational pillar of this entire genre. Make it stop. Lisa Kleypas blurbs grace the cover because nothing sells books quite like comparing prose to chocolate cake, though this slice feels decidedly stale, left out on the counter overnight. 

### Horticultural Hysteria and Other Lies

The marketing leans heavily into the "heat and hilarity" angle, a phrase publishers use when they mean the plot is tissue-thin and the characters possess the emotional depth of a puddle in a London alleyway. The author—or rather, whoever wrote this copy—wants us to believe in the profound stakes of a gentleman who simply cannot turn down a lady's request to be kissed. Groundbreaking stuff. Truly a masterclass in psychological realism. 

Let us be completely honest about the artistic merit here. This is absolute trash, but it slaps. 

### The Editorial Post-Mortem

Expect the usual forced banter, the predictable proximity trope, and enough bodice-ripping energy to satisfy the readers who treat these paperbacks like disposable napkins. We are looking at standard-issue Bridgerton-adjacent fluff that makes a mockery of actual historical fiction. The prose equivalent of empty calories. The oysters at lunch were entirely subpar, ruining my creative flow.
