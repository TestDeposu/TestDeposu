**Author:** Tessa Afshar  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-11-17 APL

Staring at a blinking cursor while the fluorescent lights hum a low, synthetic drone at four in the morning does wonders for your skepticism. The sweat pooling under my collar reminds me that August in the city offers zero mercy, leaving my brain to melt slowly into my ergonomic chair as I pick apart Tessa Afshar's upcoming biblical historical fiction finale, *The Palace Spy*. 

### The Runaway Horse and Other Convenient Tropes

We are looking at a narrative engine powered entirely by equestrian chaos and royal convenience. A homeless woman stops a runaway royal horse—classic—and immediately secures a classified intelligence-gathering gig from Queen Esther herself. Never mind the glaring logistical absurdity of the Persian court vetting zero people before handing out counter-assassination contracts. Afshar is clearly leaning into the same cozy, faith-infused historical romance space currently dominated by folks like Francine Rivers or Lynn Austin. And honestly? The target audience will devour it. 

Wait. Let me rephrase that. Actually, do not let me rephrase it, because the predictable nature of the setup is precisely what makes these late-night escape reads so comforting when the rest of the world feels like it is on fire. 

### Orchards, Em-Dashes, and Forced Proximity

Enter Allon, a brooding orchardist dragged back into the imperial fray because apparently, nobody in ancient Susa can retire in peace. Apple trees and espionage. What a combination. The thematic core here hinges on institutional trauma and the quiet exhaustion of retired civil servants forced into action. Yet the synopsis immediately pivots the heavy political stakes into a forced-proximity romance where two hardened operatives accidentally tie their hearts into metaphorical knots. 

The prose promises a race against time, but the emotional pacing feels destined to unfold at the leisurely speed of a Sunday stroll through an agricultural district. There is a strange dissonance in using an assassination plot as the mere backdrop for romantic tension. Or maybe the danger is completely toothless, designed purely to facilitate stolen glances behind Persian tapestries—wait, scratch that word, banish it from the lexicon, let us call them heavy embroidered curtains instead. 

### Beyond the Scroll

Mining the gaps in canonical scriptures is a well-worn trick, popularized by anyone trying to cash in on Sunday school nostalgia with a slightly more feminist, character-driven lens. Esther's post-biblical life offers a blank canvas, but I worry the political intrigue will get entirely sidelined by the will-they-won't-they dynamic between the homeless former-servant-turned-spy and the traumatized apple farmer. 

The synopsis leaves the stakes frustratingly vague, leaning on buzzwords about hearts tied into knots rather than actual geopolitical peril. But publishers love a safe bet for the late-year holiday rush, and this one has all the polished, predictable mechanics of a bestseller engineered for airport bookstores and quiet bedside tables. 

The distant, rhythmic thump of the subway somehow manages to vibrate straight through my noise-canceling headphones today.
