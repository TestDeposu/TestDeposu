**Author:** Kameron Hurley  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Forty-one hours. That is the exact amount of time assigned to a decaying worldship before it obliterates two innocent planets, perfectly mirroring the structural panic of a poorly managed corporate merger during a hostile takeover. Or perhaps the final minutes aboard the *Costa Concordia*. Kameron Hurley returns us to the grimy, bruised mechanics of *The Stars Are Legion* in *These Savage Stars*, demanding that we look at galactic geopolitics through the barrel of a smoking gun. And what a painfully predictable barrel it is. 

### The Architecture of Domestic Catastrophe

We are handed a tired pitch. Kill Bill meets Andor, they say, as if mashing two entirely disparate cultural properties together somehow constitutes an original artistic identity. Yet the premise relies entirely on an exhausting brand of matrimonial hostility. Reyka, a retired spy who should honestly just stay retired, must board the doomed vessel Satvi to rescue a kidnapped noble offspring. Her adversary? The estranged wife she supposedly already killed once. Because nothing screams middle-aged resentment quite like having to murder your spouse a second time because she joined a militant extremist cell. 

Suburban marital therapy disguised as a grimdark space opera. How endlessly chic. 

### The Tyranny of the Suicide Squad

The synopsis leans heavily on the tired trope of the all-female suicide mission, treating women's bodies as disposable shock troops for the amusement of readers who prefer their empowerment covered in grease and synthetic blood. Give us actual structural critique, not just cynical aesthetics wrapped in Hugo-winning credentials. Hurley clearly knows how to build a decaying hulk of a spaceship—the worldbuilding in her previous outings proved she has an eye for industrial rot. But reducing a planetary extinction event to a bitter ex-spouse dispute diminishes the cosmic stakes into a petty domestic squabble. 

One imagines the diplomatic negotiations over chamomile tea would have been far more efficient. But efficiency sells no paperbacks, does it? 

We are asked to root for desperate women racing against a ticking clock while ignoring the sheer absurdity of the geography. Forty-one hours. A ticking clock device so aggressively cliché it belongs in a Jerry Bruckheimer workshop from 1998. Everything rests on whether Reyka can outrun her own marital baggage before gravity does the heavy lifting for her. 

The business class lounge was far too crowded today, ruining my concentration.
