**Author:** Matthew Hubbard  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-12-22 APL

Champagne flutes do not shatter themselves, yet Matthew Hubbard’s *Drop Dead Handsome* arrives with the kinetic force of a dropped Baccarat vase. We are looking at a December 2026 release date, which feels remarkably far away for a manuscript already generating the kind of breathless chatter usually reserved for a disgraced British peer's tell-all memoir. 

### The Architecture of Sashes and Spite

Southern Tennessee pageantry meets the high-octane panic of a college admissions office. Parker Ryland finds his meticulously curated dossier branded as—and this is genuinely offensive—"boring." One thinks immediately of the Fyre Festival catering tent. A tragedy of mediocrity. Naturally, the only logical antidote to an uninspired academic profile is subverting the patriarchy in an evening gown and a sash. 

And then there is Dean. The student body president. Nerdy, undeniably charming, armed with a clipboard and a dossier of pageant lore. 

### Subversion Versus Sequins

Let us pause for a moment. Actually, scratch that. Let us not pause at all, because the premise of a boy weaponizing the local pageant circuit against antiquated gender ideals is either going to be brilliantly satirical or as toothless as a boarding school faculty meeting. The synopsis leans heavily on the aesthetic of painted toenails and flying sparks. Yet, one suspects the actual prose might lean into the same earnest, overly processed sentimentality that plagued the later seasons of *Glee*. 

Hubbard wants us to believe in the gravity of a high school crown. But high school pageants possess the existential weight of a Gwyneth Paltrow wellness summit. 

Sparks fly. Paint splatters. Someone is sabotaging our protagonist. This reads less like a high-stakes thriller and more like the plot of a forgotten Disney Channel Original Movie that was hastily re-written after a focus group in Paramus threw a tantrum. Casey McQuiston comparison in the blurb? Please. Let us reserve that particular crown for writers who actually understand the mechanics of wit rather than trading exclusively in manufactured whimsy. 

Sigh. 

Or perhaps I am being too harsh on a book that has not even materialized on velvet-lined shelves yet. Maybe Hubbard will deliver a blistering social critique wrapped in taffeta and tulle. Let us pivot entirely: the setup is actually genius. The collision of small-town bigotry and teenage ambition offers a pristine canvas for sharp, biting prose—assuming the author avoids the temptation to wrap every emotional beat in cotton candy. 

The synopsis leaves us guessing about the mechanics of the sabotage, which is frankly lazy marketing. Who is ruining the evening gowns? The track team? The faculty advisor? We get no names, no motives, just vague threats and glitter. 

We wait. We watch. We pour another vintage. 

Except I cannot linger on this prose any longer because I desperately need to review the architectural plans for my new villa in Tuscany.
