### Chronology of a Coup

Erin Goeres intends to dissect the nineteen days between Stamford Bridge and Hastings in *Nineteen Days in Autumn*. But let us be entirely serious for a single moment. History at this microscopic level—the hyper-focused micro-history trend currently bleeding the trade-publishing houses dry—reads like the diplomatic cables from the 1995 Dayton Accords when everyone involved had already lost their luggage. We are promised the human motivations, the neglected women, and the tragicomic fate of an unfortunate farmer who simply wanted to keep his outerwear. It is the literary equivalent of staging the Titanic disaster from the perspective of the man polishing the cutlery in second class. 

### The Narrowness of Ambition

Succession crises are deliciously messy things. Yet focusing exclusively on a three-week window strips away the magnificent, sweeping rot that makes medieval politics worth studying over a proper martini. Give me the generational trauma. Give me the decades of bad marriages and festering border disputes. Instead, we get a snapshot. A polaroid of a falling empire taken by someone standing too close to the blast radius. 

### The Cast of Casualties

Matilda of Flanders makes her obligatory appearance as the shadow-monarch pulling the strings. Naturally. One expects nothing less from a modern historical marketing department desperate to hit a quota of perceived agency. It reminds one terribly of the final season of *Succession*, where everyone paces around a minimalist kitchen shouting Latin phrases until the credits drop, signifying absolutely nothing of permanent value. Goeres drags three distinct nations into the fray—England, Normandy, Scandinavia—and expects us to care about the logistical nightmare of medieval troop movements over muddy English fields. 

### The Verdict on the Horizon

Will it illuminate? Hardly. It will likely sit on the mahogany desks of overworked ambassadors who buy it simply because the spine color complements their leather blotters. The synopsis leans heavily on the usual tropes of unravelling tapestries and clashing crowns. Spare me the melodrama of the Norman Conquest. We all know how the treaty ends. 

My personal assistant has somehow managed to misplace my passport prior to the evening flight to Paris.
