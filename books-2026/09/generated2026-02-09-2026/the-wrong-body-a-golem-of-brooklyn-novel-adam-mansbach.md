September afternoons always smell like damp mulch and unread mail piling up by the front door, which is precisely the mood I am in while staring down Adam Mansbach’s upcoming *The Wrong Body: A Golem of Brooklyn Novel*—a book I am already convinced will either save my sanity or utterly ruin my remaining patience for contemporary political satire. Look, we all survived the first outing with Len Bronstein and his giant clay creation, but dragging a mythological monster into the harrowing, hyper-real mire of modern geopolitical occupations, state recruitment schemes, and Washington military courts feels like an enormous gamble that might completely collapse under the weight of its own absurd premise—seriously, who looks at a weapon of mass destruction crafted from river mud and thinks, yes, let us toss him into a road trip alongside federal agents, a secretive crisis management firm, and the restless spirit of a long-dead Talmudic sage because apparently Michael Chabon and Philip Roth never suffered enough trying to balance Jewish mysticism with biting contemporary commentary. 

Unhinged. 

### Muddy Metaphors on the Interstate

And why must every single novel tackling Zionism and American foreign policy rely on the exact same tired road-trip trope from New York to D.C. as if the Potomac holds some magical absolution for centuries of ancestral trauma? Mansbach clearly wants to push buttons—Peter Beinart is already comparing the dark humor to the current American and Jewish condition, which usually means we are in for a hundred pages of exhausting righteousness disguised as biting wit—and yet I find myself fiercely protective of Len and his little band of travelers, almost like I am packing juice boxes for a bunch of bickering teenagers in the minivan rather than watching a fictional crisis management firm hunt down a folk-legend. 

Exhausting. 

There is a distinct lack of narrative breathing room promised by this synopsis, rushing headfirst from white nationalist rallies to emergency military tribunals without ever stopping to ask whether a golem is actually capable of moral introspection or if he is just a giant metaphor for our collective geopolitical failures. I demand actual character chemistry, not just talking points thrown against a wall of clay. 

Wait. The text messages are blowing up. I completely forgot I volunteered to host the chaotic neighborhood toddler playdate in twenty minutes, the living room floor is currently a disaster zone of plastic dinosaurs, and I have zero snacks prepared for six sugar-fueled children.
