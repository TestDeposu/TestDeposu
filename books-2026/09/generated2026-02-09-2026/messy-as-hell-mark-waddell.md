**Author:** Mark Waddell  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Another office-place satire dressed up in the tattered, neon-lit skin of corporate horror. Because what literature truly lacks in our current epoch is yet another junior custodial worker stumbling upon the literal underworld behind a mahogany door. How delightfully inventive. One might as well exhume the corpse of the 2008 financial crisis and hand it a mop. 

Mark Waddell’s upcoming *Messy as Hell* asks us to believe that a multinational enterprise can securely house infernal biohazards on the thirteenth floor while employing a protagonist named Sam whose primary motivation is swooning over a ruthless, charming CEO. Groundbreaking. We are apparently meant to brace ourselves for a madcap caper involving daredevil friends, secret crushes, and a suspicious emotional-support rabbit named Mr. Bun-Bun. This reads less like a narrative and more like the script notes for the Fyre Festival documentary. I’ll take that with a mountain of salt.

### The Architecture of Corporate Despair

The synopsis leans heavily on the tired trope of the working-class everyman unmasking white-collar villainy. Except this time, the white-collar villainy literally smells of sulfur and features a signed contract spelling doom for humanity. How quaint. We are swimming in a toxic slurry of Douglas Adams trying desperately to channel Chuck Tingle, crossed with the frantic energy of the Costa Concordia captain trying to parallel park a cruise ship. 

Yet the real offense here is not the premise itself. It is the predictability of the emotional architecture. Sam will peek behind the door. Sam will regret it. Sam will learn that his corporate idol is actually a monster—who could possibly have foreseen such a betrayal in a book featuring a character literally named Dark Enterprises?—and he will save the day with his trusty bucket and broom. Happiness is guaranteed by page three hundred. And that is precisely what turns my stomach. Where is the nihilism? Where is the crushing, inescapable weight of late capitalism grinding human aspirations into dust without a single plucky rabbit to lighten the mood? 

### The Cult of Competence

We are force-fed this dreary notion that a plucky janitor can outwit infernal bureaucracy. Give me a break. A real corporate hellscape does not feature homicidal assistants you can outrun; it features quarterly performance reviews that strip your soul away incrementally until you welcome the sulfur. Calvin is losing his mind over a secret project? Please. We have all lost our minds over lesser spreadsheets, and nobody is writing a witty urban fantasy about our descent into madness. 

The prose promises a high-stakes romp through cubicle farms and demonic contracts. But let us be entirely honest about what this is. It is disposable genre fiction packaged for readers who think *The Office* lacked enough demons. And speaking of unnecessary revivals, I am already dreading the inevitable sequel where Sam takes middle management. 

My tailor is waiting at the Carlyle to fit me for the gala.
