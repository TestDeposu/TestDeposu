**Author:** Lana Harper  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

The subway’s fluorescent glow bleeds through the gaps in the newspaper, casting a sickly yellow over the synopsis of *Firstdawn*—Lana Harper’s next move in the demonic love triangle chess game. And Christ, if this isn’t the kind of thing that makes you want to hurl your coffee into the tracks. Danica Marsden, Chicago’s most jaded PI, gets yanked into a hellscape where the only thing more intoxicating than the half-demons chasing her is the fact that she’s *literally* not human anymore. Shahara and Rhavin, those two serpentine heartthrobs, are basically the literary equivalent of a vampire romance where one of the leads is a feral witch and the other’s got a god complex—except here, the stakes are cosmic and the foreplay is *definitely* going to involve blood.

The synopsis doesn’t *tell* you this is a book about identity theft on a supernatural scale—it *shows* you by way of Danica’s fractured reflection, her therapy bills, and the way her dreams bleed into her waking life like a bad acid trip. Harper’s got a knack for making the mundane feel like a pressure point before the world ends. Danica’s barfights and one-night stands aren’t just backstory; they’re the last gasps of a woman who’s spent her life pretending she’s in control. And then—*poof*—she’s not. The moment her reflection flickers, you know this isn’t going to be a cozy paranormal romance. This is *The Girl with All the Gifts* meets *American Gods*, but with the erotic tension of *The Devil’s Daughter* and the existential dread of a woman realizing she’s been lied to her whole life.

The half-demons, Shahara and Rhavin, are the kind of characters that make you want to scream at the page: *Why do they have to be so damn charming?* Shahara’s lethal beauty is the kind of thing that would make a lesser writer lean into cliché, but Harper’s got the guts to make her feel like a predator who’s also a victim of her own lineage. Rhavin, meanwhile, is the golden boy with a secret—because of course he is. Their dynamic isn’t just a love triangle; it’s a power struggle disguised as romance, the kind that makes you think of *The Lies of Locke Lamora*’s political maneuvering but with more skin and less wit. And the fact that they’ve been vying for Danica’s love for *centuries*? That’s not just worldbuilding—it’s a middle finger to the whole "fated mates" trope. These guys have been playing the long game, and Danica’s just the latest pawn.

The real question is whether Harper can pull off the balance between the gritty, grounded PI protagonist and the mythic, high-stakes demonic court. Because right now, the synopsis reads like a love letter to *The First Fifteen Lives of Harry August*, where the protagonist’s past is a labyrinth and the present is a ticking clock. But where *Firstdawn* might stumble is in the execution of Danica’s arc—will she be a reluctant heroine, or will she double down on her cynicism and burn it all down? The synopsis hints at both possibilities, but it’s the kind of ambiguity that could either elevate the book or leave readers feeling like they’re chasing ghosts.

And let’s not forget the aesthetic—stenciled edges, interior cover art that’s presumably *divine*. This isn’t just a book; it’s a statement. The kind of thing you’d want to display on a shelf next to *The Night Circus* and *The Shadow of the Wind*, but with the edge of something darker, something that doesn’t just entertain but *haunts*. Harper’s got a reputation for blending eroticism with horror, and if *Firstdawn* delivers on the promise of Danica’s descent into the infernal, it’ll be the kind of book that lingers like the taste of something forbidden.

But here’s the thing—you can’t read this on a crowded subway. Not without risking a panic attack or a spontaneous fight with the guy in the trench coat who’s been staring at you too long. The world Harper’s building is one where the line between human and demon is thinner than a razor’s edge, and Danica’s the kind of protagonist who’ll make you root for her even when she’s being a mess. And if she’s half as compelling as the synopsis suggests, you’ll be waiting for the next installment like it’s the only thing keeping you from losing your mind.
