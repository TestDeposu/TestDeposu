September air smells like rot and wet wool, though nobody wants to admit the summer is truly dead. I sit here surrounded by piles of unread proofs while my own grandchildren treat me like a piece of antique furniture they forgot to dust, demanding ancient tales they never actually listen to anyway. They just want noise. They want blood and shadows without paying any attention to the old blood on the hearthstones. 

Maria Tureaud is about to drop *Beneath, It Sleeps* upon us in 2026, and I already know I will despise the modern gloss slapped over real suffering. An eighteenth-century Irish Gothic tale about a runaway accountant hiding in the notorious Hellfire Club. Dublin brothels, wicked gentlemen, and the sudden intrusion of an ancient *púca*. Because apparently, we are still recycling the same tired folklore tropes until every drop of terror is wrung completely dry. 

### Ledgers and Bloodstains

The premise leans heavily on the domesticity of fear—running from a powerful duke only to land in the lap of Dorcas Kelly, the city's most infamous madam. Lotty Boyle is supposed to be our eyes and ears, an accountant turned spy. Yet everyone loves a plucky female protagonist who stumbles into supernatural conspiracies because human greed is apparently too mundane for the current literary market. Why must every historical fiction piece turn into a monster mash by chapter three? It is exhausting. It is the exact same intellectual laziness I saw last year when everyone lost their minds over that dreadful Irish witch novel that shall remain nameless—the one where the protagonist spent three hundred pages staring at damp peat while the author desperately tried to invent a new adjective for "grey."

### The Old Sins Wake

God forbid a story just be about the crushing weight of eighteenth-century debt and male cruelty without needing an elemental power waging war against the Devil. We always have to dig up some primeval entity sleeping beneath the floorboards. Subtlety died somewhere around 2012, buried under a mountain of high-concept pitches designed for streaming adaptations rather than actual readers who appreciate a well-turned sentence. The locals chatter about banshees, Lotty has nightmares of shapeshifters, and the dark magics collide in a predictable display of pyrotechnics. 

My offshore accountant is demanding a secure phone call.
