**Author:** Darby Kane  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

The Mediterranean glare is simply ruthless on Morocco-bound calfskin, yet I persist through the literary drivel publishers insist on floating past my transom. Darby Kane’s upcoming *Don't Blame the Wife* crosses my desk wrapped in the usual breathless marketing hyperventilating—comparing it to *Succession* and *The Guest List* because apparently, corporate boardrooms and blood-spattered seaside shindigs are entirely interchangeable tropes to the common reading public. Lmao, make it stop. 

### The Architecture of Vapid Affluence

We are subjected once again to the tired, utterly derivative milieu of wealthy people behaving monstrously. Groundbreaking. Walter Benedict is dead in a Rhode Island pool, his wife Amery—whose name drips with the sort of aggressively expensive vowels that require trust funds to pronounce—skated away from jail time like a rich girl bossing her way through an etiquette crisis. Ten years later, she gathers the greedy spawn for a celebratory anniversary of a corpse. But wait. An explosive documentary looms. The plot hinges on a reality-tv-adjacent bribe: solve the murder of dear old Walter, win a twenty-eight-million-dollar oceanfront estate. Winner take all, baby. No cap.

### Wealth Pornography as a Blood Sport

What fascinates me here—if one can pry open a sliver of intellectual curiosity beneath the yacht’s canopy—is the unabashed weaponization of domestic architecture as a gladiatorial arena. Kane is clearly leaning hard into the contemporary obsession with hyper-wealthy dysfunction, treating family trauma like an immersive escape room designed by sociopaths. Amery doesn't just want the truth; she wants a demolition derby of ethics. She yearns for her children and their parasites to descend into feral backstabbing just to secure the deed to Waverly. It is base, cynical, and utterly lacking in the Shakespearean heft of actual dynastic collapse. Yet, the sociology of the leisure class demands this sort of public flaying. We love watching the monied class eat their own until the cholesterol gets them. Or in this case, a pool filter.

### The Problem of Manufactured Vagueness

The synopsis reads like a pitch meeting at a streaming service desperate for a weekend binge-watch before the algorithm resets its parameters. We get zero texture on the children, zero interiority beyond the broad strokes of greed, and a premise that stretches credulity thinner than my vintage Hermès caftan. If Amery has been planning this calculated psychological massacre for a decade, why now? Because a true-crime internet mob got bored? Please. The stakes feel simultaneously inflated and profoundly hollow—a twenty-eight-million-dollar carrot dangled by a woman who views her offspring as malfunctioning Labradoodles. One expects cardboard cutouts rather than breathing characters. 

Still, one must admit the sheer audacity of the subtitle-esque threat baked into the promotional copy: "This will teach people to blame the wife." Iconic behavior, honestly. If the prose matches the campy, venomous bite of that closing line, the plebes might actually buy it at airport terminals worldwide.
