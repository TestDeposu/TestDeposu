### The Architecture of the Gimmick

The setup relies heavily on a classic speculative bait-and-switch. Dre Archer gets plucked from the Squares—where human history supposedly ended—to nanny a five-year-old prodigy named Albie. The catch? Albie is convinced the year is 1884, two centuries out of sync with reality, and his grand Victorian manor operates as a strict historical reenactment society. Corsets, comportment lessons, and absolute denial of the 22nd century. 

Writers love throwing characters into artificial cages—Kazuo Ishiguro did it with devastating grace in *Never Let Me Go*, and Daphne du Maurier practically patented the oppressive architectural anxiety in *Rebecca*. Yet the synopsis leans hard into Russian doll-like revelations. I can already smell the third-act twist. Someone is watching through a two-way mirror. Someone is harvesting data. 

### What Makes Us Tick

The promotional blurbs—courtesy of heavyweights like Rumaan Alam and Charles Yu—insist this debut is principally concerned with the great, unanswerable question of what makes us human. That is publisher-speak for philosophical window dressing. Every speculative novel written in the shadow of climate collapse and digital saturation now tackles our pathetic yearning for the analog world. We stare at glowing rectangles all day, so naturally, novelists write about corsets and candlelight as the ultimate resistance. 

Harrison is a playwright by trade. Dialogue will likely snap and crackle, carrying the entire weight of the narrative while the world-building does the heavy lifting in the background. But I worry about the mechanics. When a protagonist asks "What is my job, really?", the reader is implicitly promised an intellectual payoff that rarely survives the landing. 

If the prose stays as fluid as Yu claims, the journey through this bizarre Victorian time-bubble might justify the inevitable techno-paranoia tropes. Or it will collapse under the weight of its own cleverness. Either way, I am trapped writing about a 2026 publication date while September winds rattle the windowpane. 

I have to go finish painting a terribly depressing portrait.
