**Page Count:** Unknown

Another Wisconsin cliff, another tragic drop, another fictional universe where professionals willingly chase urban legends instead of admitting that people simply fall when gravity does its job. *Creature of the Night* arrives soon to pad out an already bloated genre, dragging its cryptozoologist protagonist into the dreary expanse of Door County because some dead guy allegedly heard a screeching oversized chicken with glowing red eyes. Groundbreaking literature. Absolutely bussin, no cap. 

### The Aesthetics of Boredom

Annelise Ryan relies on the oldest crutch in the publishing book—dropping a charismatic pseudo-scientist into an isolated geographical pocket with a local myth to decode. We get the usual atmospheric window-dressing. Water. Cliffs. Cold winds. It reads like Joe Abercrombie stripped of all genuine nihilism and replaced with cozy suburban sleuthing. The thematic underpinnings here seem desperately obsessed with the friction between rational empirical data and human folklore. Or perhaps the author just wanted an excuse to write the word Mothman while coking up another generic mystery arc. 

And yet, watching protagonists pretend to face mortal peril while trapped in a cozy matchmaking dynamic with their new business-partner-slash-boyfriend screams absolute predictability. The stakes are nonexistent. Nobody important ever dies in these mid-list paperbacks because the algorithm demands sequels until the copyright holder keels over. Actually, wait. I take that back completely. Maybe the genre is shifting toward actual tragedy. No, who am I kidding? This will end with a mildly spooky campfire revelation and a lukewarm cup of coffee at a local diner. 

### The Cryptid Industrial Complex

We are living through a cultural exhaustion loop where every regional ghost story must be dragged into a multi-book franchise. Give me a protagonist who breaks down from existential dread in a sterile boardroom instead of traipsing through the woods looking for red-eyed mutants. Real monsters wear custom Italian suits and bankrupt pension funds with a single signature. But no, let us pretend a man-sized insectoid is the greatest threat facing modern jurisprudence. 

My assistant is violently signaling from the mahogany doorway that the quarterly philanthropic foundation statement is due on the wire before the European markets open.
