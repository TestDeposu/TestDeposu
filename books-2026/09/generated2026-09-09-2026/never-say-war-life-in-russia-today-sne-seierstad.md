**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Between you and me, the sheer volume of geopolitical nonfiction landing on my oak dining table lately is giving me mild heart palpitations, especially when the youngest is currently midway through murdering a *Chopin* nocturne two rooms over with the dedication of a medieval torturer. 

Ah, *Never Say War: Life in Russia Today*. Åsne Seierstad is stepping back into the ring with what promises to be a deeply human lens on a country currently choking on its own paranoia. Between you and me, I bought extra tea just to brace for the emotional toll of reading about war widows, taxi drivers, and silenced artists. 

### The Kitchen Table Chronicles

Think Svetlana Alexievich meets George Packer, but filtered through the crisp, fearless boots of a Scandinavian war reporter who clearly prefers talking to real humans over schmoozing with bureaucrats in Moscow hotel lobbies. The synopsis drops hints about a canvas stretching from the freezing Arctic north down to the metropolitan cultural elites, which instantly makes me wonder if the pacing will feel a bit scattershot. Can one slim volume genuinely hold the agony of a soldier fleeing the Ukrainian front alongside the quiet, desperate defiance of banned writers? 

Books like this always rely heavily on the invisible thread of human connection, turning strangers into pseudo-friends you worry about long after you close the back cover. 

Brutal.

### The Neighborhood Drama

Speaking of high-stakes domestic chaos, our neighbor Mrs. Gable has decided to repaint her entire Victorian porch a violent shade of mustard yellow that clashes so aggressively with the hydrangeas it physically hurts my retinas to water the geraniums, yet somehow she expects everyone on the block to admire her bold artistic statement during our morning garbage-can retrievals. But back to Seierstad and her brave dispatching from behind the iron curtain, because watching everyday people navigate totalitarian absurdity feels painfully relevant when half my neighborhood is fighting over property lines. 

Read it.

I have to go supervise a terrifyingly loud piano practice.
