**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

The idea that sleep is a performance hack—like a secret weapon tucked into the back of your closet—isn’t exactly revelatory. But here’s the thing: the way *Sleep Intelligence* frames it, you’d think we’ve been sleepwalking through our own exhaustion for decades, and only now, with the precision of a Swiss watchmaker, has someone decided to measure the exact seconds we’re wasting. The synopsis leans hard into the "high-performance" angle, which is fine if you’re a CEO who’s already convinced their 4 a.m. coffee is a superpower. But what about the rest of us? The people who aren’t just "ambitious professionals" but also, say, parents, artists, or anyone who’s ever stared at a ceiling fan and wondered why their brain feels like a deflated balloon?

The six-principle framework sounds impressive—chronotype alignment, REM sleep protection, strategic napping—but it’s the kind of thing that makes you wonder if the author has ever actually *lived* in a world where "optimal sleep" isn’t just a luxury but a survival tactic. The mention of "sleep leaders" is particularly rich. Leaders? Like, the kind who can schedule their REM cycles while also dealing with existential crises? Or is this just another corporate buzzword for people who can afford to outsource their sleep to a neuroscientist?

And yet—here’s where I pivot—maybe the real genius of this book isn’t in the science (which, let’s be honest, isn’t exactly groundbreaking) but in the way it weaponizes guilt. "You’re not performing at your best because you’re not sleeping like a lab rat!" It’s the kind of thing that makes you feel like a failure just for existing in a world that doesn’t revolve around your circadian rhythm. But then again, if you’re the kind of person who’s already convinced that sleep is a weakness, this might be the book to make you feel like you’re finally getting it right.

The synopsis is so vague it’s almost insulting. "Identify your chronotype"—great, so I’m supposed to take a quiz and suddenly become a high-functioning machine? And "protect REM sleep"—because nothing says "adulting" like scheduling your dreams like a spreadsheet. The real question is: who is this for? The people who already know they need to sleep but don’t know how to make it happen? Or the people who think they’re too important to sleep at all?

I can’t help but think of *The Sleep Revolution* by Arianna Huffington—because, let’s face it, this is just the next iteration of the same tired message, repackaged with a PhD and a six-step plan. Huffington at least had the decency to make it sound like a revolution. This feels more like a corporate wellness seminar with a side of self-help.

But then again, maybe that’s the point. Maybe the book isn’t about sleep at all—it’s about selling the idea that you’re not doing enough. And in a world where productivity is worshipped, who could resist?

I’m supposed to be writing this review, but honestly, I’d rather be anywhere else. The idea of sitting down with a book about sleep when I’ve already spent the last three hours staring at the wall is almost comical. Yet here I am, forced to engage with another self-help manual that’s going to tell me I’m doing it wrong. Not in this lifetime.

I’m going to go drink absinthe and pretend I’m in 1920s Paris, where at least the existential dread comes with a side of absinthe. #SleepIsOverrated #CorporateWellnessFail #PhDOrNotPhD
