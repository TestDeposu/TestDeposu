**Author:** Kat Cho  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Look at the marketing machinery churn out another modernized mythological retread, as if we haven't suffered enough urban fantasy paperbacks with neon-soaked Seoul covers littering the front tables of independent bookstores. *House of Forgotten Souls* arrives via Kat Cho with all the subtlety of a sledgehammer to a Venetian mosaic. We get the standard supernatural predator hiding in plain sight—a gumiho living off the life force of morally ambiguous men because, naturally, contemporary misandry makes for convenient caloric intake. Groundbreaking stuff. 

### The Currency of Viscera

The prose relies heavily on transactional intimacy. You lose a bead, you lose your soul, you hold someone's literal life essence in your palm—it is all terribly literal, robbing the folklore of its actual psychological rot. Cho positions her protagonist in a sprawling concrete labyrinth, hunting the kind of men no one will miss, which is a tired trope that tries desperately to pass for feminist empowerment. But let us be entirely honest: this is pure YA comfort food slathered in K-pop aesthetics and high-concept gloss. 

Actually, scratch that completely. I take back every ounce of my skepticism regarding the conceptual framework. 

The interplay between the immortal predator and the collateral-damage human boy who intercepts her fox bead hits a genuinely delicious nerve of high-stakes melodrama. There is something intoxicating about the sheer structural audacity of binding a mythical entity’s survival to an absolute civilian who stumbles into the brush. 

### The Architecture of Ruin

Centuries-old feuds crashing headfirst into the awkward fumbling of teenage affection always yields fantastic narrative carnage. The young shaman stepping in to fix a supernatural mess only to blow up the geopolitical landscape of local spirits is peak dramatic irony. We are looking at a kinetic, high-octane narrative engine that begs for immediate consumption over an overpriced espresso. It serves up the same frenetic, low-key unhinged energy that made *Wicked Fox* a sensation, though dialed up to absolute god-tier melodrama. 

The aesthetic execution on the page will likely salvage whatever structural predictability lingers in the synopsis. Cho knows how to construct an atmospheric backdrop where neon reflections bleed into ancient shadows, marrying modern apathy with visceral, folkloric terror. One simply cannot deny the kinetic appeal of a universe where domestic tranquility is permanently undermined by a glowing, stolen orb of metaphysical mortality. 

My private flight to London has been delayed by an exact hour, leaving me stranded here with a surplus of time and zero patience left for mediocre prose.
