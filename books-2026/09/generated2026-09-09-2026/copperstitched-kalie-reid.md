Somebody just dropped their half-eaten pretzel on my boot while the train lurched violently toward Chambers Street, and I am supposed to sit here drafting thoughts about *Copperstitched* because apparently someone decided my afternoon needed more anxiety. I hate writing these previews. I am literally just typing fast on my notes app to hit the word count so my editor stops emailing me every four minutes. 

### Maps, Metals, and Extortion

Kalie Reid is walking straight into the high-stakes corporate espionage trap with this one—except everyone is wearing velvet corsets and arguing over blueprints instead of signing NDAs. We get it. Magic guilds are the new tech startups. And the Cartographer's Guild sounds like a logistical nightmare run by people who probably hoard fountain pens. But the premise here isn't about geography; it's about blackmail. You poison the patriarch, you corner the successor, and suddenly everyone is playing high-stakes poker with rendered copper. 

What fascinates me—and by fascinates me, I mean I am squinting at this synopsis while holding onto a greasy metal pole for dear life—is the weaponized bureaucracy. Fantasy usually gives us glowing swords and ancient prophecies. Here? We have artisanal metals and deadly HR violations. The Coppersmiths holding an antidote over Saskia's head feels deliciously petty. It reminds me of the corporate ruthlessness in *The Atlas Six* mixed with the moody atmospheric dread of Holly Black’s adult work. 

### The Chronically Exhausted Childhood Friend

Enter Nicholas. The distractingly charming childhood friend. Because of course he is. You cannot have a cozy institutional sabotage plot without a guy who leans against doorframes and makes witty remarks while the building literally burns down around them. Reid is leaning hard into the friends-to-lovers pipeline, and honestly? Good. We need the emotional stakes to ground whatever convoluted copper-magic lore she is about to dump on us in chapter three. If Nicholas doesn't betray her or at least hide a massive, universe-shattering secret in his jacket pocket, I will eat my other boot. 

Anyway, I digress. The synopsis gives us just enough vagueness to keep us guessing whether this book is actually a slow-burn political thriller or just an excuse for two stressed twenty-somethings to make intense eye contact across map tables. I suspect the pacing is going to breakneck—people do not write books titled *Copperstitched* expecting quiet tea parties. They want ticking clocks. They want leveraged assets. They want toxic corporate culture translated into sword-and-sorcery stakes. 

Look out for the forced proximity trope working overtime here. When you give a fictional protagonist a ticking clock—poisoned dad, zero margin for error—and force her to team up with the one boy who knows all her childhood secrets, you are engineering absolute narrative chaos. And honestly? I am here for the disaster. Assuming the prose doesn't drown in exposition about guild taxes, Reid might actually deliver a fun, sharp-edged ride that doesn't take itself too seriously. 

The train is finally slowing down, my knees are locking up, and I am officially done staring at this screen. I am going to go sit on a park bench and aggressively people-watch.
