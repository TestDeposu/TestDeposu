The engine is off, the air conditioning is wheezing a pathetic lukewarm breath, and the digital clock on the dashboard insists it is still three minutes before dismissal. Because God forbid the bell ring on time and let a mother have five consecutive minutes of cognitive silence. 

Instead, I am staring down Avina St. Graves and *Pink Ink*, a premise that aggressively demands we suspend every ounce of worldly sense for the sake of gothic academic sleaze. 

### The Curation of Carnage

The pitch is loud. Gothikana meets *Dexter*, which is publishing shorthand for *we figured out how to monetize your weirdest nocturnal Pinterest boards*. And honestly? I am entirely here for it. Let the pearl-clutchers gasp over sleeping with faculty. At least Professor Maddox Shaw brings a creative hobby to the tenure track. Most academics I encounter at school board mixers barely manage a passive-aggressive sigh over the budget cuts, let alone curate macabre artistic murder scenes with visual motifs. 

Yet my youngest child has somehow developed an obsession with a cartoon blue heeler that defies all laws of animation, forcing me to listen to thirty minutes of high-pitched Australian barking on repeat before I even opened this synopsis. It builds a distinct kind of psychological fortitude. If you can survive an entire afternoon of aggressively wholesome canine capitalism, reading about a film student recreating a serial killer's artwork for a class assignment feels positively relaxing. 

### Aesthetic Over Ethics

St. Graves knows her audience. They do not want moral redemption. They want brooding artists who make your pulse race while dodging secret societies that control the local art scene. 

And look at the marketing copy. "There’s no line Maddox won’t cross to keep *me*." Sublime. Pure, unadulterated pulp perfection. Never mind that the synopsis relies entirely on the kind of dramatic irony that makes English majors twitch. How do you recreate a secret murder scene for a film project without knowing the victim? Did she just find a corpse in the quad and yell, *Action*? The vagueness of the plot mechanics is astonishing. But who cares about logic when there are dark academia vibes on the line?

This book will either be a magnificent, unhinged fever dream of artistic obsession or a complete structural disaster. I am blindly, stubbornly betting on the former. 

The minivan side door just slid open with the exact frequency of a gunshot, and three backpacks are currently being hurled at the back of my head like small boulders. Time to close the tab, pretend I was doing something responsible, and hand out juice boxes.
