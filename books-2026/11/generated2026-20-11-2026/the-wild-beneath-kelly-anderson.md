**Author:** Kelly Anderson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-08-04 APL

Bells are ringing down the hall right now and someone’s child is aggressively practicing the recorder outside my door like it’s a personal vendetta against my sanity. God forbid anyone wants a quiet forty-minute planning period to lose themselves in something—anything—that doesn't involve teenage apathy or administrative mandates. Enter Kelly Anderson’s *The Wild Beneath*. Please. Drop me into the Pacific Northwest immediately. Let a tsunami take my current reality because I am ready to read a book where people actually listen to the stars instead of screaming across the cafeteria about energy drinks.

### Tsunami-Sized Metaphors and Boyfriends Left Behind

Annie MacLeod grows up on a sailboat. Classic maritime isolation trope. And then—bam. A natural disaster alters the trajectory of existence. It is deeply exhausting to watch characters grapple with profound emotional tidal waves when I spent my morning breaking up a physical altercation over a stolen mechanical pencil. Yet, the synopsis promises a wordless man who hums. A literal human tuning fork. Does he hum in tune? Is it a C-major chord? Because if I have to listen to one more seventh-grader hum aggressively during a silent reading block, I might also walk into the ocean. But for Annie, this mysterious Walker provides an instant, elemental connection that somehow supersedes her childhood sweetheart, Evan. 

### The Ultimate Choice Between a Mortgage and a Myth

Six years pass. Annie has buried the past. Haven't we all? Yet the otherworldly hum returns, demanding a reckoning. Honestly, relatable. I too have buried my past, usually beneath a mountain of ungraded essays and lesson plans I wrote at two in the morning. Anderson is clearly playing with themes of primal instinct versus terrestrial responsibility. Do you choose the safe, predictable Evan on dry land, or do you chase a weird, silent beach-dweller who vibrates? It is giving heavy *The Shape of Water* meets Alice Hoffman energy. Give me all the magical realism. Strip away the mundanity of standard contemporary fiction and hand me something drenched in saltwater and weird acoustic phenomena. 

I refuse to entertain any middle-ground critique here. This book is either going to be an absolute masterpiece of atmospheric longing or a complete narrative shipwreck, but frankly, I do not care which, provided it takes me three thousand miles away from this building. If the oceanic lore holds up, Anderson might just deliver the kind of transportive magic that makes sitting through a faculty meeting bearable. 

I have three minutes to compose myself, swallow my seventh cup of lukewarm coffee, and hand-deliver a handwritten manuscript markup to a terrifyingly strict editor who thinks split infinitives are a sign of moral decay.
