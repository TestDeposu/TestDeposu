**Author:** Alex Kiester  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

The November damp has settled deep into the upholstery today, smelling faintly of rotting birch leaves and yesterday’s damp wool, which matches my disposition precisely—dour, gray, and aggressively unimpressed. Look at us, staring down another literary season saturated with the exact same tired parlor tricks. Alex Kiester—bolstered by that tiresome #1 *New York Times* moniker because illiteracy apparently sells by the pallet—returns in 2026 with *A Girl To Kill*. Another thriller built upon the corpses of young women for casual consumption. Groundbreaking.

### The Pedigree of Paupers

Kat Howard is yet another financially embarrassed scholarship student dragging her pauper’s pedigree through the hallowed, ivy-strangled quadrangles of Ashbrook College. Because apparently, fiction writers cannot comprehend academia without throwing poor people into Gothic architecture to hustle. Then enters Theo Hart, the resident wunderkind who monetized his little sister’s immolation via a memoir called *Growing Up Scarface*. Oh, spare me the melodrama. An eighteen-year-old literary sensation? Please. This is pure cringe, a laughable caricature of artistic precocity cooked up by someone who clearly peaked during their sophomore seminar in creative writing.

### Con Artists in Cashmeres

Naturally, these two starving bohemians set their sights on Madelyn St. Clair—an oil heiress whose very surname reeks of old money and new money crimes. They want to swindle her. Groundbreaking stuff, truly. It is the same tired *The Talented Mr. Ripley* cosplay we have been enduring since Patricia Highsmith breathed her last, updated for TikTok-brained Zoomers who think reading a book constitutes an act of supreme philanthropy. And why do we care? Because the synopsis asks that insufferably trite question: *why are we so obsessed with dead girls?* Newsflash: the author is banking on that exact obsession while pretending to deconstruct it. Pick a lane, darling. 

### Multi-Perspective Mediocrity

Three distinct points of view. Because two perspectives were simply not enough to drag out a premise thinner than cheap gruel. We are promised shifting layers and a shocking truth. My prediction? The heiress is in on the grift, the dead sister isn't actually dead, and the scholarship student has a secret trust fund. It is aggressively predictable garbage disguised as high-brow suspense. Donna Tartt did this with actual prose and genuine pedigree in *The Secret History* decades ago. Kiester is merely dumpster-diving in the ruins of superior literature. 

Excuse me. I must authorize a multi-million dollar wire transfer for a philanthropic endeavor.
