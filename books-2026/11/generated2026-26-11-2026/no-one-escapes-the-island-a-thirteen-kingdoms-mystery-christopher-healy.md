**Author:** Christopher Healy  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Another locked-room parlor game disguised as juvenile fiction. Christopher Healy insists on dragging us back to the Thirteen Kingdoms, as if *No One Leaves the Castle* did not already exhaust every conceivable fairy-tale trope. Now we get *No One Escapes the Island*. How original. A billionaire recluse named Calumnius Murk invites high-society types to a remote outcrop—because nothing screams artistic innovation quite like a plot stolen directly from the doomed passenger manifests of the *Titanic*.

### The Aristocratic Cage

Lila is thirteen. Let that sink in. A royal princess running homicide details while her parents issue royal decrees demanding she cease her sleuthing. Yet she persists. Naturally. Because children in modern literature possess sharper investigative instincts than entire Interpol divisions. The synopsis leans hard into the classic Christie-meets-Dungeons-and-Dragons aesthetic. Adam Gidwitz blurbs it with breathless enthusiasm. But corporate wealth disparity wrapped in velvet cloaks feels a bit too close to reality lately. Who needs a fictional island resort when you can just read the flight logs from Little St. James? 

Wait. Let us reevaluate that cynicism. Perhaps the adolescent detective angle provides a necessary, serrated edge against the bloated opulence of the leisure class. Watching a precocious royal dissect the rotting foundations of plutocracy might actually offer some minor comedic value. I will protect this character with my life. Or at least until the third chapter. 

### The Illusion of Suspense

The premise relies entirely on an enclosed ecosystem of morally bankrupt nobles. Murk made his fortune by stepping on people. A literal titan of industry hiding behind expensive canapes and azure waves. And surprise—someone turns up dead. The synopsis offers zero clues about the actual mechanics of the murder. Just vague threats and high-stakes posturing about losing everything. This is the literary equivalent of a teaser trailer for a streaming series that will ultimately get canceled after one season. 

We are expected to marvel at the sheer audacity of a thirteen-year-old outsmarting generational wealth. Yet the formula feels remarkably predictable. Shady characters. A body in the study. A ticking clock. It is the literary equivalent of a billionaire's submersible trip to the Titanic—expensive, ill-advised, and destined to implode under the weight of its own hubris. Healy writes well enough, but I grow fatigued by authors who rely on genre mashups as a substitute for genuine invention. 

I am going to sit in my mahogany-lined study in absolute, expensive silence.
