**Author:** Rebecca Ross  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Another manuscript. Another doomed academic future sacrificed at the altar of a brooding boy’s redemption arc. It is exhausting, really, watching the literary world continually demand that women dismantle their brilliance just to save some tragic man from his own shadow. Rebecca Ross is spinning another yarn in *A Bittersweet Spell*, and while the endorsements from Ava Reid and Danielle L. Jensen promise a delicious concoction of whimsy and mystery, I am sitting here side-eying the premise so hard my neck hurts. Because heaven forbid a female scholar gets to keep her tenure without a knife at her throat. 

### The Currency of Stolen Futures

Lorne St. Clair hid away curating magical antiques, a cute little aesthetic for a man who apparently traumatized a brilliant woman a decade ago. And now he needs her help. OF COURSE HE DOES. Because female intelligence in fantasy is rarely treated as an autonomous force; instead, it is mined as a literal utility service for men who fucked up their own lives. Avalene Vale was declared dead, hiding on the outskirts while her academic career rotted in the dirt, all because Lorne begged her to flee with cold steel pressed against her jugular. SMH. The sheer audacity of showing back up on her doorstep like an Uber Eats driver for archaic translation services is peak male entitlement, magical or otherwise. 

Ross usually delivers that swoon-worthy, atmospheric prose—think *Divine Rivals* minus the typewriter ink—yet I am praying Avalene gives this man absolute hell instead of melting into his arms by chapter three. We are neck-deep in a cultural fatigue regarding the reformed villain trope, and frankly, I need my fictional heroines to possess a bit more venom and a lot less amnesia about who brought the knife. 

### The Enchantment Trap

The manuscript itself is the only element holding my interest hostage. An enchanted script written in an ink only rare mages can read? Fine. I respect the world-building mechanics there. Books about dangerous books are my specific kryptonite, giving off major *A Study in Drowning* vibes where the text bites back. Yet, I worry the romance will completely overshadow the structural critique of an academic system that discarded a woman the moment a man panicked. The synopsis leans heavily into the fragile hope of second chances—give me a break, the man committed assault—and glosses over the actual institutional violence Avalene endured. 

Expect breathless pacing, atmospheric whimsy, and plenty of longing glances over glowing parchment. But if Avalene forgives him too fast, I am literally going to throw my espresso cup across the room. We demand better character arcs in this economy. 

Wait. Did I just hear someone rattle the pantry doorknob? If that is my nephew looking for snacks during my designated five minutes of peace, I am staging a permanent sit-in right here between the canned tomatoes and the artisanal olive oil.
