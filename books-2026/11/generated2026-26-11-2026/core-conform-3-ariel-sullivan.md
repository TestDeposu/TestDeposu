**Author:** Ariel Sullivan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Scraping dried acrylic off my thumb while the radiator bangs its death rattle against the chill of this November dusk—god, I am already exhausted by the sheer predictability of yet another YA dystopia trading on tired tropes. We are looking at the third installment of Ariel Sullivan’s *Conform* series, titled *Core*, slated for 2026, and based on the provided synopsis alone, it is giving peak derivative panic. 

### The Architecture of Clichés

Look at the setup. Emeline wakes up in the clouds. Literally in the clouds. Because nothing says overthrowing an oppressive regime quite like literal altitude and a forced procreation contract with a boy named Collin—who is apparently the youngest member of the Illum, whatever aggressively sleek, dystopian aesthetic that implies. It is giving *Hunger Games* meets Marie Lu’s *Legend*, but filtered through a late-capitalist blender of romanticized state-mandated breeding. 

The synopsis breathlessly informs us that "The game is simple: Who loves whom more. The winner lives and the loser dies." Absolutely tragic. Peak literary cringe. This epistemic rot of turning interpersonal affection into a gladiatorial death match has been beaten to an absolute pulp since Suzanne Collins published her little arena experiment fifteen years ago. Yet publishers keep greenlighting this exact brand of emotional hostage-taking because they think teenage girls will consume literally any garbage wrapped in a matte-black cover with metallic font. 

### The Epistemological Void

And the vagueness of the synopsis! We get zero concrete world-building. Just vague murmurings about the rebellion falling, secret-keeping elites, and a truth that will "shake things to the very core." Groundbreaking. Utterly revolutionary. How utterly pedestrian to promise shocking discoveries about the "true nature" of a revolution without offering a single structural hint as to what makes this society tick beyond a vague mandate to couple up or perish. Sullivan is leaning entirely on lazy, plot-driven signposts—secret allies, sudden betrayals, high-altitude paranoia—rather than building a persuasive, breathing sociological framework. 

Yet, as an artist currently staring down a half-finished canvas of rotting fruit because rent is due in five days, I recognize the hustle. Why bother subverting the chosen-one narrative or deconstructing the tired mechanics of the YA romance-industrial complex when you can just slap a high-concept death game onto a stale rebellion plot and call it a bestseller? 

We are trapped in a Sisyphean loop of recycled tropes where every protagonist must be a reluctant revolutionary caught between her treasonous heart and her oppressive overlords. Emeline has to decide where her loyalties lie—newsflash, she is going to pick the rebels and shatter the system, because she is a YA heroine and narrative inertia demands nothing less. The discourse around this book will undoubtedly be insufferable on TikTok, driven by fanbases arguing over shipping dynamics while ignoring the total lack of prose-level ambition. 

I am going to throw this press release into the recycling bin, pull on my heaviest wool coat, and wander the freezing city streets at midnight seeking inspiration because staring at this press release is rotting my brain cells.
