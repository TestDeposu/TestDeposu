**Author:** Nita Prose  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Free Wi-Fi and the dull hum of an industrial heater that smells suspiciously of singed dust. God, I hate Tuesdays. Some corporate suit left half an oat-milk carton on the linoleum over there, weeping its lukewarm dregs onto the floor while I stare down this press release for *Emerald Lake* by Nita Prose. 

### The Grand Architectural Deception of Octogenarians

Jane Weber is eighty-four, decaying, and sitting on a pile of local trauma that apparently stayed comfortably refrigerated until her grandkids tripped over a corpse. Groundbreaking. Truly avant-garde suburban horror. We are supposed to care about the "long-buried secrets" of a woman who probably hoards Werther's Originals and refuses to update her Windows 95 desktop. Prose gave us *The Maid*, which played like a twee Agatha Christie cover band performing in a daycare center. Now we are plunging headfirst into the brooding, melancholic waters of a lake that sounds like a masterclass in beige psychological thriller tropes. 

### Shadows, Woods, and Other Lazy Tropes

Look at the synopsis. Just stare at it. A mysterious boy in the shadows between the water and the woods. What does that even mean? Did he live inside a pinecone? Was he rendered entirely in CGI vibes? It is the literary equivalent of a jump scare featuring a stray cat. We get a police officer named Clair Mundy—because apparently every town needs an investigative cipher with a vowel-challenged name—digging into a local legend about a swamp-witch ghost. 

And yet. 

There is something aggressively bankable about this. Shirley Jackson this is not; it reads more like Stephen King stripped of all nicotine-stained grit and repackaged for a book club that drinks boxed Pinot Grigio while complaining about lawn ornaments. The thematic machinery here relies on memory as an unreliable narrator, which is just a fancy way of saying nobody in this town bothered to file a missing persons report in 1955 because they were too busy churning butter or whatever repressed mid-century nonsense senior citizens love to reminisce about. 

### The Inevitable Nostalgia Industrial Complex

Give me actual dread. Give me something that doesn't smell like a heavily marketed autumn candle from Bath & Body Works. This book will sell three million copies by Thursday afternoon because middle-aged women on Instagram love a geriatric protagonist grappling with historical guilt while staring stoically at a body of water. It is grief porn with a side of rustic escapism. The synopsis leans so hard into atmospheric hand-waving—wilderness, memory, dangerous myths—that the actual plot is hiding behind a smoke machine fueled by pure middle-brow pretension. 

I would rather drink the mop water in the janitor's closet than read another seventy pages about a tragic summer romance that culminates in a decaying femur poking out of the moss. But who am I kidding? The ending will involve a poignant sunset, a tearful reconciliation, and a cheap emotional payoff that leaves everyone feeling cleansed and emotionally stable. Utterly tragic. 

I need to get back to the studio apartment before the light dies entirely and finish blocking out the acrylic eyes on this wretched, screaming portrait of my landlord.
