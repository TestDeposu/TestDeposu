Sweater weather has officially settled over the city, stripping the trees bare and turning my morning espresso into a survival necessity, yet I am sitting at my mahogany desk utterly vibrating with the kind of incandescent rage that only a YA trilogy conclusion can manufacture. Kate Golden is about to unleash *Infernal City*, the explosive final installment of the Harker Academy series, and frankly, my blood pressure hasn't been this compromised since the catastrophic closing night of the Maria Callas centenary at La Scala. 

### The Architecture of Despair

Viv Abbot is back, trapped beneath a sealed-off Chasm in the blistering depths of the underworld—never mind that it is currently freezing enough outside to shatter diamonds. She faces seven deadly trials to save the Half City. Seven. Because apparently, Dante Alighieri’s structural blueprint was simply too subtle for contemporary fantasy publishing. And who is suffering beside her? Deacon Graveheart. Naturally. The synopsis practically drips with the forced proximity of two people who have been denying their palpable chemistry since book one, treating emotional repression like an Olympic sport. 

### Subterranean Clichés and Architectural Crimes

Let us address the aesthetic trappings because we must. The publisher is pushing a deluxe first-edition trade paperback featuring stenciled edges and captivating interior cover art. They are trying to distract us. They are dressing up narrative bankruptcy in custom sprayed edges, hoping we will not notice that the plot relies on the exact same tired gauntlet tropes we have been choking on since the golden age of Wattpad. It is the Fyre Festival of literary concessions—shiny, heavily marketed, and fundamentally built on structural fraud. Viv must confront her new existence as a demon while Deacon relives his exile trauma. Oh, the humanity. 

Yet the real casualty here is pacing. While our star-crossed duo is busy playing underworld dodgeball against seven arbitrary obstacles, an election for mayor of Astera looms alongside a world-ending plot by the High Thane. This is precisely what happens when an author tries to juggle municipal politics and demonic purgatory in the same breath. It is the literary equivalent of serving Dom Pérignon with a side of gas-station jerky. 

### The Stakes of Sub-Par Prose

Will the Half City survive? Does anyone genuinely care when the narrative architecture is this bloated? Golden is leaning entirely on the crutch of high-stakes exhaustion, throwing magical elections and apocalyptic threats at the wall simply to see what sticks. We deserve better world-building. We deserve prose that does not read like a discarded screenplay for a straight-to-streaming supernatural drama. But do not worry about the structural integrity of the plot, darlings. As long as the edges of the book are pretty enough to photograph for an autumn flat-lay on Instagram, the readership will happily swallow the poison. 

My tailor is currently loitering in the foyer, endlessly checking his gold pocket watch because I am dangerously late for a mandatory fitting for the winter charity gala.
