**Author:** A.B. Poranek  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Cousin Marjory insists on wearing heavy wool in mid-November while cornering me near the buffet to ask about my life choices, which apparently makes me uniquely qualified to dissect the marketing copy for A.B. Poranek’s upcoming *Cinderspell*. Another fairy-tale adaptation. Because the literary world was gasping for a fresh take on glass slippers and midnight curfews. 

### The Art of Recycling Royalty

Let us examine the scaffolding of this premise. We have an estate in decay—naturally—because no protagonist is ever allowed to start the story with a functional plumbing system or a balanced ledger. Wish Arvell paints memories, which is a neat little magic system lifted straight from a YA paint-by-numbers kit. But wait. The synopsis mentions she actually makes a bargain with a dangerous faerie to enter a royal competition. 

Actually, scratch that critique. The bargain trope might actually save this thing from being entirely derivative. 

Except it won't. We are looking at standard romantasy architecture here. A plucky girl. A brooding royal exile rumored to eat hearts—because normal bad boys are apparently too pedestrian for 2026. Thomas Haunt. Honestly, the name sounds like a haunted house ride at a dying county fair. The \#1 *New York Times* bestseller label on *A Treachery of Swans* guarantees that hordes of people who only read books with sprayed edges will pre-order this before breakfast. 

### Palette Swaps and Pixie Dust

The thematic currents here are about as subtle as a brick through a stained-glass window. Survival capitalism disguised as an art contest. The commodification of emotion on canvas. Yet the synopsis reads like a word salad generated by a tired intern who just skimmed Holly Black’s discarded notes. 

Why must every female lead in these books be the *only* girl to enter the trials? It is such a lazy shortcut to manufactured underdog status. Oh, look, she’s breaking barriers with her paintbrush while rivals plot murder in the corridors. How rebellious. 

I really need to wrap this paragraph up because my uncle is walking over with a Tupperware container of dry brisket and I cannot maintain eye contact for another twenty minutes. 

Let's talk about the title of this review for a second. Or the fact that I’m inventing word counts in my head just to hit the publisher's arbitrary quota while sitting in a folding chair that is aggressively digging into my lumbar spine. 

Poranek clearly knows the formula. If you enjoyed sweeping your way through Sarah J. Maas or rolling your eyes at whatever romanticized fae court is currently dominating TikTok, this will feed your habit. It has all the necessary components: dead parents, shady contracts, and an ill-tempered love interest who follows people around like a lethal shadow. Terrifying. 

Watch out, humanity. The canvas is bleeding and someone forgot to pack the wet wipes.
