**Page Count:** Unknown

Ah, yes, another publishing cycle where we expect marginalized creators to bleed out their daily micro-aggressions onto glossy paper just so a romance-starved public can learn basic empathy—because nothing screams "healthy relationship dynamic" quite like reading a memoir written by someone whose primary romantic prospect involves explaining to a date that wheelchair access is a non-negotiable prerequisite for getting a second coffee. 

*If I Were You, I’d Kill Myself* is poised to do what Imani Barbarin does best: take the soul-crushing, bone-deep exhaustion of dealing with able-bodied delusion and package it into a weaponized, neon-lit punchline that will inevitably be ignored by the exact people who need it most, resting comfortably on bedside tables right next to *Such a Fun Age* and whatever frantic, trauma-mined essay collection is currently topping the bestseller lists. 

### The Art of Smiling Through the Pity-Party

We are expected to applaud the sheer resilience of a human being simply trying to exist in a public square without some over-caffeinated stranger walking up to whisper, "I could never do what you do," as if existing with cerebral palsy is a Cirque du Soleil audition rather than a Tuesday morning trip to the grocery store. 

Brilliant. 

### Romance is Dead and So Is My Patience

And because my current emotional bandwidth requires finding someone who views me as an equal partner rather than a charity case or a porcelain doll destined to shatter the second life gets complicated, my tolerance for books that expose the sheer, unadulterated horrors of how society treats human bodies is hovering around absolute zero. 

Exhausting. 

### The Verdict on the Anticipated Masterpiece

Barbarin will no doubt deliver a masterclass in cultural critique, wielding comparisons to Samantha Irby and Roxane Gay like a switchblade wrapped in velvet, slicing right through the saccharine garbage tropes of inspirational misery porn—yet I will still have to sit across from a date who tells me I am "brave" for putting my shoes on and pretend I am not actively calculating the velocity required to launch my water glass across the room. 

Fascinating. 

I have to piece together a shattered vase before anyone notices.
