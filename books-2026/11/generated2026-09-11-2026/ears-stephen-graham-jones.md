**Author:** Stephen Graham Jones  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Listen, I am currently wiping oat milk splash-back off my apron for the fourth time today, staring at a blank document because I am contractually obligated to hammer out three hundred words about Stephen Graham Jones’s upcoming *Ears* just so I can afford my rent. It is deeply mid, this whole routine. But here we are. 

### The Real Estate of Dread

We are looking at a haunted house narrative that actually looks like it might hurt, which is a rare, gorgeous flex in a genre currently bloated with toothless grief-metaphors. Jones isn't playing around with polite, atmospheric dampness here. He’s throwing a disgraced former teacher named Mr. Morning Gun into the dead-property meat grinder. The premise is brilliantly sleazy and desperate—a guy who checks on vacant houses just to scrape by, stumbling into a gig where a girl in lavender footie pajamas refuses to let her family home get flipped. And honestly? Based on the synopsis alone, this is giving us peak exploitation-gothic vibes fused with blue-collar squalor, a direct descendant of Jack Ketchum’s bleakest domestic nightmares colliding with Shirley Jackson’s claustrophobia. 

### Lavender, Plumbing, and Other Bad Omens

What actually gets my cold, caffeinated heart pumping is the sheer audacity of the mechanics here. A ghost child saving a squatter from a fatal confrontation, only to drag him down into a blood-soaked transactional hellhole of hidden crawlspaces and—wait for it—the apocalyptic flush of a toilet. Yes, you read that right. We are getting ontological terror wrapped up in domestic infrastructure. It is aggressively absurd and yet totally terrifying because it’s Stephen, the guy who can make a pair of rusted scissors or a basketball shoe feel like instruments of the apocalypse. The text promises a reckoning that starts with bathroom plumbing, and frankly, if that isn't peak late-stage capitalism horror, I do not know what is. No cap, the vibe is straight-up cursed architectural violence.

### Belonging as a Meat-Grinder

Let's talk about the subtext before the book even drops, because the synopsis practically bleeds desperation. Belonging and isolation aren't just themes here; they are physical traps. Morning Gun survives on the margins, which means he is already a ghost long before he meets the kid in the lavender pajamas. The house isn't just haunted; it’s aggressively territorial. It wants to consume whatever broken thing crawls into its floorboards to find answers. We are looking at a masterclass in psychological claustrophobia, a narrative designed to make you check your own locks twice and stare suspiciously at your hallway closet. It’s gritty, it’s nasty, and it completely bypasses the polite, sanitized ghost stories currently dominating bestseller lists. 

I have thirty more minutes on this shift before I can legally scream into the walk-in freezer, so I am going to go brew a dangerously strong espresso and stare blankly at a blank page.
