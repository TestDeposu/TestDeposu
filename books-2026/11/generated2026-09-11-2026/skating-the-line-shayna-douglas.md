**Author:** Shayna Douglas  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

*Skating the Line* is going to completely dominate the conversation when it drops in 2026, and honestly, we deserve a win after the absolute literary dumpster fire that was last season’s YA contemporary crop. Remember when everyone collectively pretended that a book about sentient glue sticks was high art? I certainly do, and my grading pencils have not stopped trembling since I had to confiscate three copies of that nonsense behind the gymnasium. Thank goodness Shayna Douglas is bringing us back to solid ground with proper athletic tension and genuine academic peril. 

### The Rink as a Crucible of Despair

Marley Hayes is facing the ultimate adolescent nightmare: GPA eligibility thresholds. And because life lacks basic narrative mercy, her ex-girlfriend’s homewrecker—pardon me, the legendary Élodie Leblanc—is transferring right into her locker room. 

Youth sports fiction lives and dies by forced proximity tropes. But putting two fierce competitors in charge of a youth ringette team is a stroke of absolute genius. Think *Heated Rivalry* meets the relentless, soul-crushing energy of trying to explain why borrowing a mechanical pencil without asking is a social contract violation of the highest order. 

### The Anatomy of Petty Grudges

And let us talk about the baggage. Élodie represents inherited legacy and effortless grace, while Marley is fighting tooth and nail for every single inch of ice time. It is class warfare on skates. 

Readers love a good enemies-to-lovers arc. Yet this one actually feels earned because the stakes are tangible—grades, draft picks, and the agonizing humiliation of having to share a bench with the girl who stole your first love. Douglas understands that teenagers do not just experience mild annoyance; they experience Shakespearean levels of betrayal over stolen mixtape songs and defensive zone turnovers. 

Expect sharp banter. Expect accidental hand-brushes near the penalty box. Expect me to confiscate at least four dog-eared copies from back-row readers by the third week of November. 

Hold on a second. I need to go pry my favorite gold-nibbed fountain pen out of Timmy’s sticky, jam-covered fingers before he tries to write his spelling test in strawberry preserves again.
