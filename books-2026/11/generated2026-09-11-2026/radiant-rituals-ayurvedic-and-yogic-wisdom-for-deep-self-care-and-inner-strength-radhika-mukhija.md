Everyone at the kitchen table is currently drowning in notifications while I try to preserve what little family dinners we have left. Radhika Mukhija clearly understands this modern exhaustion in *Radiant Rituals*, promising an escape from the relentless grind that has turned our living rooms into satellite corporate offices. But can a book featuring functional nutrition and trauma-informed mindfulness actually save a household from burning out, or is it just another pretty manual destined to gather dust next to the juicer we used twice in 2021? I am aggressively skeptical of anyone selling inner peace through seasonal rhythms when my oldest kid has college applications due and the dog just ate a sneaker. Yet Mukhija’s personal pivot—stepping back from demanding corporate trenches after a family illness—lends a gritty, lived-in authenticity to the prose that traditional wellness gurus completely lack. 

### The Corporate Detox Illusion

We are drowning in productivity hacks disguised as self-care. *Radiant Rituals* attempts to slice through this noise by replacing hustle culture with ancient Ayurvedic frameworks, though I worry it demands a level of quiet contemplation that simply does not exist in a house with three teenagers and a barking terrier. 

Exhaustion.

### Surviving the Grind

The thematic core relies heavily on reclaiming bodily wisdom through intuition and cyclical rest. Realistically, my intuition tells me to scream into a pillow when the Wi-Fi drops during a remote workday. If Mukhija can genuinely bridge the gap between ancient yoga traditions and the chaotic reality of contemporary parenting without sounding like a sanitized wellness retreat brochure, she deserves a medal. Or at least a quiet weekend away from us. 

Doubtful.

My teenage son’s driving lesson awaits.
