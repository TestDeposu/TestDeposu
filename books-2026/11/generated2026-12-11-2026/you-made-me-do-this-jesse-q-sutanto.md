**Author:** Jesse Q. Sutanto  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Another day, another digital parasite weeping over a slightly smaller engagement rate. Jesse Q. Sutanto is apparently diving headfirst back into the algorithmic meat-grinder with *You Made Me Do This*, a title that sounds less like a psychological thriller and more like an exhausted text message sent to a mediocre contractor at midnight. 

### The Digital Panopticon of Mid-Tier Complacency

The setup is a masterclass in modern banality. We have Meredith Lee, a washed-up creator who refuses to go quietly into the retail sector, deciding that "Stalking Lite"—stealing an iPad, naturally—is a valid career pivot. And why not? Identity theft is the new hustle culture. Sutanto relies on the tired trope of the unhinged mentor-mentee dynamic, recycling the exact same suburban paranoia that populated Liane Moriarty’s early drafts and every domestic suspense novel currently rotting on a Target endcap. 

Except, wait. Let me correct myself immediately. I called it tired, but honestly, watching wealthy women weaponize ring lights against each other is the only spectator sport left that doesn't require a stadium. 

Actually, let's pause. Why am I even analyzing a book that isn't out yet based on three paragraphs of publisher-mandated synopsis? I am quite literally staring at these words trying to stretch a thousand characters out of a premise that belongs in a discarded TV pitch from 2018. But the bills demand blood, or at least a few clever sentences about parasocial obsession.

### The Aesthetics of Manufactured Despair

The thematic payload here is painfully obvious. We are meant to gasp at the horrors of the curated life. Aspen Palmer’s existence is falling apart because her husband finds her repulsive and sponsors are ghosting—truly, the modern apocalypse. Who needs nuclear war when you can just lose your brand deals? Sutanto is poking fun at the hollow architecture of momfluencer culture, treating ring lights and hashtag strategies as lethal weapons. 

Yet the execution smells entirely of algorithmic safety. Give us real grit. Give us an influencer who snaps and streams her own descent into madness on TikTok Live while shilling for a brand of organic bone broth. Instead, we get standard-issue corporate suspense dressed up in pastel fonts. 

Let me stare at a blank canvas until inspiration strikes.
