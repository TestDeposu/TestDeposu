**Author:** Valerie Norton  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Steam hisses from the espresso machine behind me while I stare at a digital galley sheet for Valerie Norton’s debut, *The Darkness Beneath*. Everyone in publishing is currently losing their collective minds over historical fantasy mashups. Yet my espresso grinder is jammed, and my soul feels remarkably soft today. Truly, I am in a frighteningly benevolent mood. Normally, I would shred a synopsis that leans this heavily on *The Mummy* nostalgia. But let us be fair. Nostalgia is the warm milk of contemporary fiction, and we are all freezing. 

### The Architecture of Desperation

The premise relies on a classic transaction—the disgraced explorer trading her survival for a wealthy scholar’s resurrection project. Aris Nycta gets thrown into scorching fighting pits because her ex-lover framed her. Classic Tuesday. Killian Lance wants to wake the dead using the lost city of Nemethet. Because grieving rich boys never learn. But wait. Let me correct myself on second thought—maybe the ex-lover betrayal is actually the most interesting piece of psychological bait here. Or maybe it is just lazy plotting designed to force two opposing archetypes into a damp jungle together. We have all read this trajectory a thousand times. The snarky thief. The sheltered academic. They bicker over rations. They find common ground. They accidentally wake an ancient evil that has a weird obsession with the female lead. 

### Shadows and Marketing Strategies

And yet, the marketing copy leans hard into the action-packed magic of *The Adventures of Amina al-Sirafi* alongside the romance-heavy intrigue of *What the River Knows*. That is a heavy cross for a debut novel to bear. Can a new voice actually deliver the swashbuckling weight of Shannon Chakraborty combined with the brooding academic tension of Rayner? Doubtful. Most debuts drown in their own world-building lore. They spend forty pages describing masonry before anyone punches a monster. Yet Norton gives us an ancient city hidden beneath the sea with a lingering malice. Actually, scratch that—is it beneath the sea, or is it a desert ruin? The synopsis mentions harrowing jungles, dangerous waters, AND a city hidden beneath the sea. Make up your mind, blurb writer. Geography matters when you are trying to evoke Brendan Fraser fighting Imhotep. 

### The Anticipatory Verdict

I predict the middle three chapters will drag horribly while our protagonists discuss their parental trauma over campfire embers. Then the third act will violently accelerate into neon-lit magical chaos. I am oddly okay with this. The cynicism usually wins, but today I am choosing to root for the doomed crew in the fighting pits. I hope the ancient evil is genuinely terrifying and refuses to monologue. Monsters should never explain their motivations. They should simply consume. 

Now I have to rush out of here and haul myself across town for the opening night of a five-hour experimental Lithuanian documentary about decaying industrial drywall, and my feet already hurt.
