**Author:** Joss Wood  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-08-13 APL

Solstice Springs is beckoning again, and frankly, my nervous system requires *Pumpkin Lane* to arrive right this instant before I completely unravel from the sheer weight of carrying everyone else's sorrow through another grueling autumn. Joss Wood clearly understands the medicinal properties of small-town romance, constructing a universe where you can just escape your own crushing domestic reality by diving headfirst into a world where pumpkins apparently reign supreme and ghosts actually give a damn about local gossip—which sounds infinitely more comforting than sitting in a quiet, dark house staring at a wall while trying to figure out how to breathe properly after a year that has felt like a slow-moving truck crash. 

Escape. 

Poppy McMaster escaping her own wedding through a bathroom window in a ruined gown is a magnificently theatrical trope that feels right out of a Sophie Kinsella novel crossed with a Hallmark movie on third-strength espresso, and honestly, we love a messy, panicked heroine who chooses chaos over a lifetime of quiet misery. But wait. Let me backtrack on that—is chaos really what I want right now, or do I just want a predictable, safe sanctuary where the biggest problem is running into your ex-fiancé's brooding older brother who also happens to be your childhood hero? 

Actually, the brooding older brother trope is completely overdone and frankly exhausting, practically ripped straight from every second-chance contemporary romance published since 2018, and I am already deeply tired of men who look like lumberjacks and brood in dark corners of inherited inns while harboring secret, tortured devotion for women who smell like cinnamon. Yet Wood’s promise of sharp banter and "spice that can fog up a window" almost redeems the blatant predictability of the setup, because sometimes a tired reader doesn't want high-concept literary fiction that demands intellectual labor; sometimes you just need a book that reads like a warm mug of sugary caffeine infused with autumn aesthetics and low-stakes emotional damage. 

The synopsis promises a cozy town with invested ghosts and nosy neighbors, leaning heavily into that specific brand of quirky escapism that readers ate up in *Summerween*, though I remain intensely skeptical of whether a book can genuinely balance humor, healing, and spooky magic without devolving into saccharine mush. But who cares about literary merit when the kitchen ceiling is dripping again and I have to go scream at the landlord for the fourth time this week?
