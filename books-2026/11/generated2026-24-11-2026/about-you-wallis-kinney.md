**Author:** Wallis Kinney  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

The rain hammers the cheap plastic awning over my head like a metronome set to *The Shining*, and I’m here—*here*—because my cousin’s wedding was a disaster, my ex sent a text that read *"I miss your taste in horror movies"*, and now I’m stuck in a room full of people who probably think I’m either a fraud or a cultist, depending on how much pumpkin spice I’ve had. But let’s talk about *About You*, because Wallis Kinney’s synopsis is doing that thing where it smells like a witch’s brew—equal parts comforting and *questionable*.

First, the *vagueness*—oh, the *vagueness*. We get a hedge witch, a sister, a coven, a man from the past, a dark tome, and a birthday that coincides with a witch’s new year. That’s a lot of *potential* for something, but also a lot of *potential* for something that feels like it’s been cobbled together from a Pinterest board of *"dark academia meets cozy witchcraft"*. The synopsis leans hard into the *"classic Hades and Persephone"* angle, which is either a smart nod to a well-loved myth or a lazy way to signal *"romantic tension + forbidden knowledge"* without doing the work. Because let’s be real—if this were actually a retelling of the Persephone myth, we’d get *some* actual mythological weight, not just the *"underground magic + emotional manipulation"* vibe that’s been doing the rounds since *The Raven Boys* tried to sell us on *"dark academia"* as a genre.

Kate’s solitude is framed as a *choice*—she’s the *"perfect"* hedge witch, content in her cottage, her black cat, her herbs. But here’s the thing: solitude as a *choice* is a trope so old it’s practically a spell itself. It’s the *"I don’t need anyone"* narrative that’s been worn thin by every brooding protagonist since *The Notebook* decided to make loneliness look like a fashion statement. The real tension here, if it exists, will come from whether Kinney can make Kate’s isolation feel *earned*, not just a backdrop for the *"oh no, she’s not alone anymore"* plot twist. Because right now, it reads like a checklist: *"Check, witch lives alone. Check, sister forces her to socialize. Check, man from past shows up. Check, dark magic appears. Check, trust issues."* It’s the literary equivalent of a buffet where every dish is *"dark academia"* and *"emotional whiplash"*.

And then there’s Matthew Cypher—*"a practitioner of forbidden magic"* who’s *"no ordinary lost soul."* Oh, *fantastic*. Another *"dark, brooding, morally gray"* male character who’s basically a walking *"I’m not like other guys"* energy. The synopsis doesn’t even *try* to give him depth beyond *"he’s dangerous and he knows magic."* That’s not a character, that’s a plot device. Where’s the *nuance*? Where’s the *"but he also has a soft spot for stray cats"* or *"he’s actually a victim of the same system he exploits"*? Because right now, he’s just a placeholder for *"the guy who complicates things."* And if this book leans into the *"forbidden magic"* angle without giving Matthew any *real* complexity, it’s going to feel like *The Witcher*’s lovechild with a Hallmark movie—all style, no substance.

The dark tome, written in her mother’s handwriting? *Oh, please.* This is the *"family secrets"* trope in its most basic form, and it’s been done so many times it’s practically a spell itself. The *"mother’s dark magic"* angle is a cheap way to introduce tension, but it also risks feeling like a cop-out. If Kinney wants to make this more than just *"witch discovers mom was evil"*, she needs to give the mother’s legacy *weight*. Was she *forced* into dark magic? Did she *choose* it? Or is this just another *"my mom was a witch and she’s dead now"* narrative that’s been mined for all it’s worth? The synopsis doesn’t even *hint* at the emotional core of this—just the *"oh no, dark magic"* reveal.

But here’s where the synopsis *almost* works: the *"Halloween season"* framing. Kinney’s name-dropping of *Practical Magic* and *Witch of Wild Things* isn’t just fluff—it’s a *deliberate* nod to the cozy witchcraft subgenre that’s been dominating shelves for years. And if *About You* can balance the *"dark"* and the *"cozy"* without falling into the *"too dark for cozy, too cozy for dark"* trap, it could be something special. The problem? The synopsis doesn’t *show* that balance—it just *tells* us there’s a *"celebration of Halloween"* and a *"love letter to anyone who drinks pumpkin spice in August."* That’s not a theme, that’s a marketing angle.

I need to patch a hole in the drywall from the last time I tried to explain to my roommate why *"dark academia"* isn’t a genre, it’s a *mood disorder*.
