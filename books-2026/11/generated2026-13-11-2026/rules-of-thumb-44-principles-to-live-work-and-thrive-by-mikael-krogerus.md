The radiator is hissing a frantic, rusty SOS against the sub-zero chill of dawn, and I am staring at forty-four supposed blueprints for human sanity. Mikael Krogerus is coming back at us with *Rules of Thumb: 44 Principles to Live, Work, and Thrive By*. God help us all. 

Another pocket-sized manual for the bewildered. As if Parkinson’s Law and the Pareto Principle are going to somehow pay my half of the internet bill when the landlord starts banging on the door with a wrench. 

### The Architecture of Unproven Truths

There is a distinct kind of hubris in packaging cognitive biases and glorified shower thoughts into something small enough to slide into a canvas tote bag next to half-smoked clove cigarettes and a receipt for instant ramen. Krogerus wants to save us from the modern grind using the IKEA Effect and Murphy’s Law—as if knowing that things will inevitably go wrong stops them from going wrong in the most aggressively stupid way possible. 

The synopsis promises wisdom over MBAs. A bold claim for a collection of truisms that sound suspiciously like what your chronically online uncle retweets after three gin and tonics. Yet, there is a weird, haunting poetry to this reductionism. We crave order because the kitchen floor is literally sticky with someone else's spilled oat milk and we cannot afford a rug. We collect mental models like loose change in a damp coat pocket, hoping one of them is the magic password to unlock a life where meetings actually end on time and the existential dread stops vibrating behind our eyeballs at 3:00 AM.

### Against the Cult of Efficiency

We are talking about a cultural obsession with micro-optimizing our suffering. *Rules of Thumb* sits comfortably in the lineage of *Atomic Habits* and every other productivity grift that tries to turn human existence into a linear algebra problem. Except this one is allegedly whimsical. Whimsical illustrations. How charming. Give me a break. You cannot draw a cute little cartoon over the sheer, crushing weight of late-stage structural collapse and call it a paradigm shift. 

Or maybe you can. Maybe that is the entire point. 

Krogerus is not offering a philosophical revelation; he is offering a security blanket made of recycled buzzwords and folk wisdom. We will buy it anyway. We will underline sentences about the Pareto Principle while ignoring the fact that 100% of our current anxiety is stemming from the twenty-four bucks left in our checking account. It is intellectual comfort food for the terminally exhausted bourgeoisie who still pretend they are bohemian artists while freelancing copywriting for supplement brands. 

I need to pack three lunches that will inevitably return uneaten.
