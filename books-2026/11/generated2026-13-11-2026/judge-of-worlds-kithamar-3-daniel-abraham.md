**Author:** Daniel Abraham  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Silk sheets, a morning espresso brewed precisely to extraction standards, and the quiet dread of another mediocre season of contemporary fiction. Yet Daniel Abraham refuses to bore us. *Judge of Worlds* arrives on the horizon as the final structural beam in a colossal architectural experiment. Three massive books, one singular municipal beast, twelve months of absolute carnage. Joe Abercrombie called the earlier construction a fascinating weave, but I suspect this concluding volume will feel less like craftsmanship and more like a slaughterhouse floor after the midnight bell. 

### The Mortar and the Massacre

It is no small artistic vanity to think you can murder a deity and expect the plumbing to keep working. The synopsis tells us the Thread has been severed. What does that even mean in a practical sense? Vague metaphysical poetry draped over a dead body. But Abraham has always excelled at the gritty mechanics of civilization gasping for breath while its throat is slowly slit. Byrn a Sal steps into the spotlight as the inaugural monarch of a broken epoch, hunted by ghosts that refuse to stay neatly tucked into footnotes. We are promised secret archives exposing the rot at the center of civilization. Groundbreaking. Or perhaps just standard genre furniture dressed up in expensive prose. 

Actually, scrap that entirely. The archives are probably just a MacGuffin for dull political maneuvering. Why do we pretend historical revelations possess narrative thrust when characters merely stand in damp basements reading parchment? 

Release day can't come fast enough. 

### Shadows in the Corridors

Power corrupts, friendship fractures, and nobody wears a white hat. It is deliciously cynical. We are wading deep into *The First Law* territory here, mixed with the grim bureaucratic dread of China Miéville at his most hungover. Halev Karsen and Byrn stumbling through a massive conspiracy hits all the right notes for readers who prefer their protagonists thoroughly bruised and morally compromised. They are total frauds, obviously. High-status dumpster fire masquerading as statesmanship. 

We watch the machinery of empire grind bones into fertilizer while society clinks wine glasses in the upper terraces. That hits close to home when one spends evenings dodging patrons at private galas who treat human misery like an aesthetic choice. Abraham understands that cities are just mausoleums with better zoning laws.
