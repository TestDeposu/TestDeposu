My shoulders are burning. The tines of this rusted red metal rake keep catching on soggy, half-rotted oak leaves that refuse to break down, packing into a dense, wet sludge that feels heavier than a cinder block. Yet here I am, scraping damp earth in the freezing November wind while thinking about Trang Thanh Tran’s upcoming novel, *The Summoning of Tess Pham*, because apparently, my brain refuses to rest. 

### Roots That Won't Stay Buried

An ancient demon preying on a family since the war. Three teenagers trapped in a remote Vietnamese forest with a six-hour countdown. It sounds like the kind of high-octane horror that Stephen Graham Jones or Paul Tremblay would tear through with absolute glee. Grief is already an excavation project, isn’t it? You spend months digging through old boxes, unearthing awkward memories, wishing you could burn the whole archive. And Tran is taking that internal wreckage and weaponizing it into a literal ancestral entity that demands a sacrifice. 

Or maybe the demon is just a metaphor for adolescent guilt. Actually, scratch that—metaphors in horror are often a cop-out. I hate when a book spends three hundred pages promising a flesh-eating monster only to reveal it was just depression all along. Give me the monster. Let the demon have teeth. 

### The Weight of Unpaid Tuition

Speaking of things that drain the life out of you, my youngest son’s travel soccer club just sent another invoice for winter indoor training. Four hundred dollars for turf rental so nine-year-olds can kick a neon plastic ball into a warped net while parents stand in an unheated warehouse shivering in puffer jackets. It costs a small fortune just to keep kids active these days, and for what? So they can learn how to fake an injury and yell at a teenager holding a yellow flag? Anyway, Tess Pham has bigger problems than club dues, specifically a vanishing grandmother and blood-stained forest altars. 

Let us be completely honest about the synopsis. It relies on a very familiar YA survival blueprint. Best friends pushed to the absolute breaking point. Secrets buried in the mud. Tensions boiling over when the cell service drops. But Tran proved with *She Is a Haunting* that she has zero interest in playing it safe with cozy ghost stories. She writes about rot—familial, historical, and deeply emotional. 

### Six Hours to Daybreak

The synopsis gives us a neat little box: six hours until dawn, three girls, one angry deity. I predict the pacing will either collapse under the weight of the flashbacks or race so fast we barely get to know why Tess is harboring a secret crush on her friend. No, wait. The crush is crucial. The best horror always couples terror with agonizing teenage yearning because the human heart is stupid enough to crave romance while running for its life. 

The rake handle just splintered right into my palm. A tiny, perfect sliver of gray wood. Exactly what I deserve for standing in the freezing yard overthinking a book that doesn't even hit shelves until 2026. 

Now I need to go figure out why the kitchen faucet has been making that low, groaning whale noise every time someone washes a coffee mug.
