My fourth-grader is currently screaming into a pile of damp, decaying gourds because the artisanal caramel apple she demanded cost eighteen dollars and tasted like wet sand, which feels like a depressingly accurate microcosm for the late-stage capitalist pastoralism we are all forced to perform every November. And yet, amidst this olfactory assault of cinnamon spray and screaming offspring, my mind wanders back to the galley sheets sitting on my reclaimed oak desk—specifically, Barbara Truelove’s forthcoming *The Secret Lives of Zombie Wives*. 

### Domesticity as Corpse Paint

Truelove promises an unhinged collision of Martha Stewart and *Shaun of the Dead*. Or perhaps *Dawn of the Dead* meets Goop. Honestly, the genre-mashing is aggressively trendy—almost too aggressively catered to the BookTok-meets-The-Paris-Review demographic that currently dominates our discourse. We are looking at Patricia, an influencer-grade trad wife who happens to be a reformed corpse. But wait—did the synopsis not state earlier that she finds the roaming zombie horde genuinely beautiful rather than menacing? Let us backtrack. The ideological coherence of her undead awakening remains entirely obscured by the marketing copy, leaving us to guess whether Truelove is writing a biting satire of patriarchal domesticity or just a gore-soaked romp that capitalizes on trad-wife aesthetics because some algorithm dictated it. 

### The Algorithmic Rot

There is a profound epistemological exhaustion in watching contemporary horror try to monetize every micro-trend before the ink has even dried on the manuscript. We have traded genuine transgressive shock for hyper-fixated brand synergy. Truelove is attempting to deconstruct the hyper-manicured prison of domestic femininity—complete with antifungal meds and ring lights—by having her protagonist eat her husband’s brains on a livestream. It is camp, it is violently literal, and it is peak mid-wit lit-fic realness, bestie. Yet I fear the commentary on influencer capitalism might get bogged down by the sheer velocity of the jokes. 

### The Gourd-Stuffed Horizon

One expects the biting irony of Ottessa Moshfegh crossed with the splatter-punk sensibilities of Alma Katsu, yet the synopsis leans so hard into the gimmick that one worries the actual narrative architecture will collapse under the weight of its own premise. Truelove has sketched a vivid, albeit thinly veiled, metaphor for the quiet desperation of women performing wholesome perfection for an audience of ghouls. But whether she sustains this conceptual high-wire act across an entire novel remains to be seen, especially given how vague the promotional apparatus is about the middle act. 

Anyway, the raw oysters we forced down at the overpriced orchard bistro halfway through our family outing were completely, offensively warm and entirely subpar, completely ruining my creative flow and leaving me questioning every life choice that led to me standing in a muddy field watching my spouse argue with a teenager over organic cider.
