**Author:** Lilly Lu  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

The espresso is entirely too bitter this morning, much like the general trajectory of my investments, yet I find myself staring at a digital galley sheet for Lilly Lu’s upcoming *Death Wish: A Supernatural Love Story* instead of looking at my art broker's emails. We are apparently still doing the millennial-pink typography for anything involving immortals with baggage. It is the architectural equivalent of putting chrome trim on a brutalist concrete parking garage. 

### Lopsided Vessels And Dead-End Leases

The premise hinges on a century-old reaper taking a pottery class. Yes, a celestial bureaucrat who ferries souls decides to throw clay on a wheel because her bowls are lopsided. What next? A demon with existential dread taking up sourdough baking during a transit strike? It feels like the specific brand of whimsy cooked up by someone who spent lockdown scrolling through artisanal TikTok accounts while ignoring their therapist's voicemails. 

### The Eternity Of Unresolved Tension

Lu’s synopsis leans heavily into the tired tradition of sapphic yearning where nobody communicates for three hundred pages because they are too busy brooding across a crowded cafe. Amory is a jaded reaper. Clementine is a vampire who missed her own execution date. It reads precisely like the script of the 1997 Venice Biennale installation that nearly bankrupted my uncle—pretentious, overly reliant on fog machines, and devoid of actual narrative velocity. 

Yet, there is a distinct cultural pathology at play here. We cannot simply let the dead stay dead. We must romanticize the administrative burden of the afterlife. Give us bureaucratic ennui, but make it queer. Give us a murderous maker running rampant because apparently immortality lacks enough middle-management disputes. 

One suspects the prose will rely heavily on metaphors about things being brittle before they snap. Or perhaps things being fired in a kiln until they harden. The subtlety of a sledgehammer wrapped in velvet. It reminds me entirely of the third act of *Tosca*, minus the structural integrity and the decent soundtrack. 

### The Final Kiln Burn

Will I pre-order the hardcover? Absolutely not. My coffee is gone, the gallery doors open in twenty minutes, and I have a bespoke charcoal suit that does not pair well with supernatural domesticity. 

I need a double pour of that rare 1998 bourbon my sommelier managed to pry out of Kentucky before the tariffs hit.
