**Author:** Wendy Smolen  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Decades of synchronized breathing. It is the sort of claustrophobic devotion that brings to mind the final, disastrous hours aboard the *Costa Concordia*, where everyone simply followed the crowd right off the edge. Wendy Smolen invites us into this exact suffocating parlor in her upcoming fiction debut, *The Hive*. Five tumultuous decades of weddings, funerals, and shared air. 

### The Architecture of Enclosure

Look at the promise here. A self-contained universe of women who are sharp, vain, and wounding in the exact same breath. Poetry requires space to breathe, yet this synopsis implies a room with all the windows nailed shut. Do me a favor. Imagine spending sixty years revolving around the axis of other people's expectations only to have a charming outsider—what a delightfully pedestrian catalyst—fracture the facade. 

Wait. Actually, scratch that notion of pedestrian intrusion. Perhaps an outside catalyst is precisely the wrecking ball required when a collective ego becomes too dense to pierce. 

Smolen appears to lean heavily into the friction between the singular pronoun and the plural possessive. Can there be a me within the us? The phrasing is a touch derivative of every mid-century suburban drama ever pressed into paperback, yet the execution might salvage the cliché. Critics suggest a polished prose style. We shall see if the verse holds weight when the cracks widen. 

### The Peril of the Chorus

The premise hinges entirely on the duality of sisterhood. It enriches the voice; it endangers the soul. Yet, one must wonder if fifty years of friendship leaves room for genuine discovery, or merely a protracted rehearsal of old grievances. When Littie asks how one finds oneself after a lifetime of definitions, the question demands lyrical sharpness. If the prose lacks bite, the book will read like a very long, very polite coroner’s report on a dead social circle. 

My evening is already ruined because the caterer for my weekend poetry salon just cancelled abruptly.
