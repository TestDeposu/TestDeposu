**Author:** Nora Carmody  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Bleary-eyed and staring at a flickering terminal at four in the morning, watching the clock inch closer to dawn while the stale coffee tastes like copper and regret. That is the exact headspace required to parse Nora Carmody’s upcoming 2026 release, *Heiress of Storms*. Oh, they are trying to feed us the same stale fantasy tropes again. We have a princess with a magical bond, an ancient evil entity with a dramatic moniker, and a tragic romance that defies logistical common sense. Sarah J. Maas walked so a thousand identical YA heroines could sprint off cliffs on the backs of majestic creatures. Except my patience for royal angst evaporated around three thirty when I realized the entire plot hinges on a missed shot. She missed. One job, Zara. One shot to defeat the global threat, and now we are subjected to a multi-book exercise in collateral damage.

### The Geography of Regret

Look at the premise. The antagonist is literally called the Devourer. How terrifying. How deeply original. Yet we are expected to care about Zara's divided loyalties between her royal obligations and her imprisoned paramour, Talon. And honestly? No, wait. Scratch that. I take back the cynicism about the romance. Maybe the dynamic between the horse and the newly bonded eagle represents a genuinely fascinating exploration of animalistic instinct versus human grief. Or maybe it is just another vehicle for melodramatic longing under a blood-red sky. Let us be real. It is probably the latter. Carmody relies entirely on the synopsis-speak of capital-D Drama. Talon escapes captivity into a falling empire, because apparently empires fall quite casually on Tuesdays in these books. We get undead Eagle Riders. Let that sink in. Undead. Eagle. Riders. My keyboard is sticky with espresso and I am laughing out loud in a silent office because the absurdity of necromantic avian warfare has officially broken my brain. 

### Feathers and Foreboding

The thematic weight here rests on sacrifice. Always sacrifice. The synopsis warns us that Zara will lose more than she can imagine. What does that even mean in the grand tradition of contemporary fantasy? Does she lose a hand? Her magic? Her favorite horse? Because based on these marketing beats, the greatest sacrifice will be my remaining ounces of sanity. We are staring down the barrel of a trope-heavy landscape where motherly secrets dictate genetic magical destinies. Groundbreaking. Groundbreaking stuff. Yet I will probably preorder it anyway. That is the sickness of the midnight shift. You consume trash because the quiet is too loud. 

I am going to put on a Coltrane vinyl and forget about this book.
