The screech of the D train braking hard enough to rattle my teeth is a great backdrop for breaking down Brynne Weaver's upcoming bloodbath, *Hunting Season*. Look, I did not ask my editor for this specific assignment—I was promised an easier contemporary release where people just yell at coffee shops, but here we are, counting down the days until 2026. Give me the comfort of a reliable murder-romance any day of the week. Give me unhinged fictional characters solving their problems with extreme violence while I dodge elbows in aisle three. 

### The Anatomy of a Cape Carnage Bloodfeud

What are we even looking at here? The synopsis plays it predictably cagey. Harper Starling wants blood. Nolan Rhodes is battered, captive, and apparently the wrong target for her vendetta. But let us be real for a second—do we care about the logic of the clues? Absolutely not. We are here for the kinetic friction of two feral people forced into proximity. Weaver practically owns the dark romantic comedy niche right now, sitting comfortably alongside authors like HD Carlton and Raven Kennedy, trading sweet nothings for sharp objects. 

Wait. Let me back up for a second. Actually, scratch that earlier thought about the plot making sense. The synopsis admits the clues do not add up, which usually means the third act is going to require a tactical suspension of disbelief thicker than the winter coats currently suffocating half the passengers in this subway car. 

### Why We Fall for the Grumpy Psychopaths

The tropes are doing heavy lifting here. Second-chance romance mixed with enemies-to-lovers? Inject it straight into my veins. Add a dash of forced proximity and a mutual "touch him/her and die" pact, and you have got a masterclass in emotional devastation disguised as pulp fiction. Don't sleep on this one. The psychological underpinnings of two wounded apex predators realizing their shared enemy is pulling the strings? Delicious. It scratches that exact itch for obsessive, high-stakes devotion that contemporary romance has been desperately lacking between all the mundane billionaire fluff. 

Every choice is a risk, or so the marketing copy screams at us from the screen. Yet the real risk is whether Weaver can stick the landing on a trilogy finale without rushing the body count. Small town romance usually means neighbors bringing casserole, but in Cape Carnage, they bring tactical knives. I am genuinely invested in seeing how Harper breaks her sacred vow. 

Honestly, trying to stretch these two paragraphs out to satisfy an arbitrary word count while the person next to me breathes garlic-infused air into my ear is its own kind of psychological torture. 

The new luxury import taxes are officially out of control, and if they hike the tariffs on my favorite French reds one more time, I am going to start hunting local politicians the way Harper hunts her ex.
