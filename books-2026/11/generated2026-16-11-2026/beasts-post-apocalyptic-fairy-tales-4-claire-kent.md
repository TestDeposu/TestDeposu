Staring at a blinking cursor at four in the morning while the radiator bangs like a trapped animal is a special kind of purgatory. The fluorescent overhead hums its lobotomy tune. Caffeine wears off, leaving behind only the metallic taste of dread and a terminal lack of inspiration. Yet here we are, staring down the barrel of *Beasts*, the fourth installment of that post-apocalyptic fairy tale anthology series edited by Claire Kent slated for 2026. Because apparently, what humanity needs right now in the middle of these freezing, bleak November nights is another bleak, dystopian spin on Sleeping Beauty or whoever else hasn't been dragged through the mud yet. 

### Dystopian Scraps and Familiar Tropes

Anthologies are notorious literary roulette wheels—you spin, you hope for something resembling coherent world-building, and you usually end up with a mixed bag of absolute bangers and utter garbage. Looking strictly at the promotional copy for this upcoming collection, the tonal whiplash is already giving me a migraine. We pivot from medical horrors in Katie French’s "Plan B"—where a hospital cleaner stumbles into some grim-dark ethical violations for the sake of sick family members—to Ariele Sieling’s sci-fi *Ugly Duckling* riff, titled "Tailless." And honestly? The juxtaposition of bio-engineering with childhood fables feels awfully tired. Give us something genuinely transgressive instead of just slapping a post-apocalyptic coat of spray paint on Hans Christian Andersen and calling it a day. 

### The Genre-Blending Soup

Sarah Dalton brings a zombie-slaying Maid Marian into the mix alongside a degenerate Robin, while Marijon Braden decides a cranky, ancient genie ruins Aladdin's whole vibe. It's giving me severe *Fables* meets *Once Upon a Time* fatigue, but filtered through a grittier, pseudo-grime lens that screams mid-tier Kindle Unlimited fodder. Jamie Campbell flips *Snow White* from the Evil Queen's perspective because moral ambiguity is apparently the only trick left in the bag for modern genre fiction. Groundbreaking. Truly avant-garde. H.S. Stone tosses us into the woods with Hansel and Gretel running from scavenger hunts, and Zoe Cannon rounds it out with a captive scholar curing a cursed prince with a smooch. It is a wildly disjointed blueprint. 

### Final Verdict on the Mess

Will this sell a bajillion copies to exhausted commuters looking for cheap escapism? Absolutely. Does it reek of algorithmic trend-chasing where editors sat around a whiteboard shouting things like let's make Little Red Riding Hood a cyborg sniper? Hard yes. If you liked books like *The Book of Eels* meets Margaret Atwood's dystopian rejects minus the actual prose style, you will probably devour this on your commute. But sitting under these flickering fluorescent lights while the autumn wind howls outside the frosted glass, I am profoundly exhausted by the industrial-scale recycling of cultural mythology.
