The radiator beside this rattling window just clanked its death knell for the autumn season, a fittingly dismal soundtrack for anyone trying to concentrate while the ghost of a betrayed narrative arc still haunts their living room. Because how is an author supposed to trust anyone anymore after the way Chapter Nine of that *other* recent release completely dismantled basic causality just for cheap shock value? But we are supposed to look forward now. We are expected to turn our gaze toward *Waxing and Waning*, a forthcoming sapphic urban fantasy by J.N. Howell that lands on the precipice of next December, trading the dying amber leaves outside for the frost-laced backdrop of Southern Ontario and Quebec. 

### The Curriculum of Chaos

Consider the baseline premise. Delilah Ali operates as a Magical Medical Practitioner—a profession that sounds distinctly exhausting—while saddled with an incompetent intern she actively plans to fail by summer's end. Yet, systemic bureaucratic competence rarely survives contact with the supernatural. And enter Darcy. A vampiric ex-partner who crashes back into the frame with a newborn infant and a desperate plea to untether the child's magic before a werewolf pack descends. It is peak chaotic bisexual energy mixed with high-stakes urban folklore, leaning heavily into the kind of messy relational dynamics that Gideon the Ninth fans consume like morning espresso. 

### The Anatomy of an Exigent Mess

The synopsis reads like a masterclass in trope collision, blending found family dynamics with the high-octane panic of running for one's life. Yet, wait—actually, looking closer at the structural setup, the exposition relies entirely too much on vague, hand-waving jeopardy. What does "untethering a baby's magic" even entail? Is it open-heart surgery with runes, or just a really aggressive baptism? The text leaves us hanging with corporate buzzword levels of ambiguity, which honestly gives me major ick while trying to evaluate the world-building mechanics. One moment we are examining the epistemological weight of late-stage capitalist magical bureaucracy, and the next we are expected to care about some feral werewolves crashing the party because—boom, lore reasons. 

### The Prognosis for Winter

If Howell manages to balance the snarky academic antagonism between Delilah and her doomed intern with the emotional whiplash of a reawakened vampire romance, this might actually slap. Or it will completely implode under the weight of its own synopsis-speak. We have seen this exact geographic and atmospheric blueprint before in contemporary speculative fiction—think Hailey Piper meets a colder, moodier Iron Widow—where rural Canadian geography becomes a character unto itself, freezing the arterial stakes of the plot until everything shatters at once. 

I am going to abandon this draft, go brew a very smoky cup of lapsang souchong, and sit entirely in the dark to ponder my profound existential dread.
