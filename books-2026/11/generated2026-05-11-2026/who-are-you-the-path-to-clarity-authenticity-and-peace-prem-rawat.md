The golden light of November is thinning, hitting the velvet armchair just right, though the chill in the air makes the prospect of yet another spiritual primer feel like an unwelcome draft. Prem Rawat is back. *Who Are You?* promises, or rather threatens, to strip away the artifice of modern existence. It suggests a stripping back of the ego, a classic move for a man who has spent decades curating a following that spans borders. The premise is painfully familiar—the radical pivot from the material to the metaphysical. We are to look beyond the labels, beyond the bank accounts, into the abyss of the self. Groundbreaking.

### The Illusion of Depth

The synopsis is a masterpiece of carefully constructed vagueness. It promises everything—AI, folk tales, aviation, the Divine—without promising a coherent structure. Is it a memoir? A philosophical tract? A sermon dressed in a dust jacket? It reeks of that perennial self-help malaise where the author assumes his anecdotal inventory equals universal truth. One wonders if he truly believes that a mid-flight reflection or a quaint story from his youth in India provides the clarity he claims to offer. Perhaps it does. Actually, no, it is likely just another iteration of the same tired, human-centric mysticism that finds a comfortable home on the nightstands of people who find *The Power of Now* too taxing.

The conceit of placing the reader at the "center of their life" feels suspiciously like a marketing pivot rather than a radical philosophical shift. It sounds exactly like something Eckhart Tolle or even a less cynical Deepak Chopra would pitch to a boardroom of burnt-out executives. I suppose there is a market for this. A very large, very exhausted market.

### A Predictable Inward Journey

I find myself irritated by the sheer audacity of claiming to be free of "rules" while simultaneously establishing a rigid, pre-packaged itinerary for the reader’s internal life. The irony is as sharp as the frost on the windows today. I initially thought this might be a fresh take on the nature of identity, perhaps leaning into the cold, calculated nihilism required to actually understand the self in a digital age. I was wrong. It is clearly going to be a warm, fuzzy blanket of platitudes designed to stifle any genuine intellectual discomfort.

The inclusion of "AI" in the list of thematic touchstones is particularly transparent. It is a desperate, clumsy reach for relevance in a year where every author is terrified of being replaced by a neural network. It feels cheap. It feels like a desperate attempt to bridge the gap between ancient wisdom and the inevitable obsolescence of the human soul. Actually, wait. Perhaps that is the point. Maybe the attempt to reconcile a flight-pilot’s perspective with the cold logic of an algorithm is precisely the kind of intellectual vertigo we deserve. No, I take it back. It is just lazy writing. 

I think I shall leave this on the shelf of some airport bookstore, nestled between the manifestos of disgraced tech moguls and diet regimens that promise eternal youth. I need something with more bite. I am going to read *The Economist* instead.
