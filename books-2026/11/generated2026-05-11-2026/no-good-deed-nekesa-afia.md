**Page Count:** Unknown

Paris in November is usually a cloying exercise in performative melancholy, all damp cobblestones and people clutching their coats like they’re shielding a fragile ego. Yet, here I am, tasked with dissecting *No Good Deed*—a book that hasn’t even hit the shelves, based on a synopsis that reads like it was churned out by a caffeinated intern in a publishing basement. Nekesa Afia is back with Louise Lloyd, dragging her across the Atlantic because apparently, Harlem didn't have enough corpses to keep our girl busy. 

### A Tired Waltz Through Cliché

Is it too much to ask for a mystery that doesn't involve the “dark underbelly” of a nightclub? Honestly, the trope of the *former showgirl* solving crimes in a jazz-infused haze is so well-worn it’s practically translucent. We get it. She has rhythm, she has pluck, and she has access to the VIP lounge. Groundbreaking stuff, really. The synopsis leans heavily on the “glamorous new life” vs. “family duty” binary, a dichotomy so basic it makes me want to scream into a silk scarf. It’s the literary equivalent of eating flavorless toast while listening to a scratchy Edith Piaf record—vaguely atmospheric but entirely hollow.

The romance element? I’m feeling particularly susceptible to a good swoon today, which makes me want to root for Rafael, but the description of their relationship is pure, unadulterated mid-tier drama bait. Oh, look, the piano player is making things complicated. How utterly pedestrian. It’s giving *Murder on the Orient Express* but with the intellectual rigor of a tabloid gossip column. Why must we rely on the same tired song-and-dance? I need depth, not just a series of events held together by glitter and loose ends. 

### The Aesthetics of Superficiality

I’m currently staring at my screen, wasting precious, finite brain cells trying to inflate this critique to meet a word count that feels entirely arbitrary—a pathetic little hurdle for a critic of my stature. The industry is currently drowning in these period-piece mysteries that prioritize vibes over actual structural integrity. If I have to read one more protagonist who has to “outwit everyone around them,” I might just retire to a monastery. It’s like the author is trying to balance high-brow existential dread with the sheer, brain-dead trashiness of a daytime soap. It’s a total mess, fam. But, if you’re looking for something to read while nursing a lukewarm gin and tonic, this will undoubtedly satisfy the masses who find Tana French too taxing for their delicate sensibilities. 

I’ll hold my breath until December 2026, though I suspect the air will remain disappointingly stale. The writing here feels less like a mystery and more like a costume party where everyone forgot their lines and just started improvising. 

Hold on. There is a disgusting, sticky lump of Hubba Bubba matted into the back of someone’s hair in front of me and I have to go deal with it.
