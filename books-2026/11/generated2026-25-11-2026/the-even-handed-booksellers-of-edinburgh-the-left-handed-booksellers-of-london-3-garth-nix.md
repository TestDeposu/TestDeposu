The wind is biting today, rattling a discarded crisp packet against the brickwork of this alley with the kind of rhythmic, hollow persistence that makes one question the point of it all. November light is a funeral shroud draped over the city, and here I am, wasting my breath—and my eyesight—on the promise of *The Even-Handed Booksellers of Edinburgh*. Another installment in a sequence that insists on dressing up magical gatekeeping in the finery of 1983 London. Why must we always look backward? Or, in this case, North, to Scotland. 

### Genealogy as a Lethal Weapon

Susan Arkshaw is ostensibly the protagonist, yet her primary function remains the pursuit of a missing patriarch. A tired trope, really. If I have to read one more story where a young woman defines her existence through the absence of a man—even a potentially mythical one—I might just lose my lunch. The synopsis suggests her identity is tied to dusty archives, a reading room ticket, and a coat of arms. It’s all very *The Ninth House* meets a fever dream of British boarding school malaise, though with significantly more stabbings. Does Susan actually possess any agency, or is she merely a meat-puppet for the plot? She’s dragged into this "Old World" chaos because of who her father might be, which feels suspiciously like the literary equivalent of a participation trophy. 

### Aesthetics Over Substance

Merlin is "outrageously attractive." Groundbreaking. Garth Nix has spent two books painting these booksellers as a mixture of high-concept librarians and glorified bouncers, yet the aestheticization of their violence is what really grates. They police the boundary between the mundane and the mythic with a silver hatpin, which is frankly a bit camp for my taste. I suppose this is where the "Even-Handed" shift comes in—a geographical pivot to Edinburgh to presumably rinse and repeat the same cycle of magical investigation. It’s all pure vibes and zero stakes. Why bother building a world if you’re just going to let it erupt into the same predictable, violent nonsense? 

Writing this drivel is a cosmic joke. I’m sitting here, counting the words just to ensure I meet some arbitrary quota imposed by people who probably think a "deep read" is a cereal box ingredient list. It’s mid-shelf fluff masquerading as prestige fantasy. If I see one more blurb about "mystical entities" or "secret histories," I’m going to scream into the wind until my lungs collapse. We’ve seen this before in the works of V.E. Schwab or even the lighter side of Susanna Clarke, but whereas they find something genuinely uncanny in the shadow, Nix seems content to just polish the brass on a sinking ship. 

Susan needs to stop looking for her father and start looking for a personality that doesn't involve being a damsel in a magical combat boot. But who am I to judge? I’m just a woman standing in an alley, freezing, while the world outside these pages continues its slow, inevitable decline into absolute mediocrity. Now, leave me be; I have a vintage dress that needs dyeing in the bathtub and it won’t turn charcoal on its own.
