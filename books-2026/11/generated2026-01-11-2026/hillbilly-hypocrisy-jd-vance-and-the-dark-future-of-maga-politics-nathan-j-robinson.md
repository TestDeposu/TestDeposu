The grand salon is suffocatingly hot today, smelling faintly of stale champagne and the expensive desperation of people who believe owning real estate makes them statesmen. Across the room, someone is breathlessly discussing the upcoming literary autopsy of the modern American right—*Hillbilly Hypocrisy* by Nathan J. Robinson. Because nothing says contemporary political discourse quite like a thousand-page grift about another billionaire-backed grifter. 

### The Aesthetics of Absolute Fraud

Look at the premise. We are meant to be shocked—truly, deeply scandalized—that a man who wrote a maudlin, heavily mythologized memoir about rust-belt trauma might pivot directly into an authoritarian bootlicker. Color me entirely unsurprised. Robinson attempts to unpack the slippery ideological gymnastics of JD Vance, wondering aloud if our prospective overlord is an opportunistic shape-shifter or a true believer in the techno-feudalist project. 

Por qué no los dos, darling? 

It is utterly exhausting. We are served the exact same narrative arc recycled from every mid-tier op-ed page since 2016. Vance sits comfortably at the nexus of Peter Thiel’s blood-transfusion paranoia and Elon Musk’s ketamine-addled galaxy-brain manifestos. Yet the synopsis reads like a panicked memo drafted by someone who still thinks institutional norms are coming back from the dry cleaners. 

### The Techno-Feudalist Circus

Robinson frames the emerging post-Trump ecosystem as a motley crew of Christian nationalists and tech overlords intent on gutting regulations. Groundbreaking. Next, they will tell us that water is wet and that Davos is populated by ghouls. The real problem with this brand of political writing is its naive insistence that hypocrisy is a fatal bug rather than the entire business model. Vance didn't mutate by accident; he evolved the exact way a parasitic organism adapts to a dying host. 

Think of it as *Dark Money* meets *Hillbilly Elegy* on a bad bender. But let us be entirely honest about the literary trajectory here. We are drowning in urgent, deeply-researched warnings designed exclusively to be stacked on walnut coffee tables in Brooklyn lofts while the republic quietly burns to a crisp. Nobody reading this tract is going to have their mind changed. They want their priors validated with a well-turned phrase and a scathing jab at Silicon Valley bros. 

And frankly, the prose better snap. If I have to read one more earnest, hand-wringing paragraph about the sanctity of democratic institutions while tech oligarchs buy up the postal service, I am going to throw my Limoges ashtray through the French doors. 

My driver is waiting downstairs with the engine running, and I am already late for a table at a Michelin-starred steakhouse where the dry-aged ribeye costs more than a semester at Ohio State.
