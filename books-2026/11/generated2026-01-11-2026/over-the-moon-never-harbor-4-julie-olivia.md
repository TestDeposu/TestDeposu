The penthouse is entirely too quiet this afternoon, save for the hum of the climate control keeping the humidity away from the first editions. We are meant to discuss *Over the Moon*, though one must immediately question the commercial audacity of altering a title from *A Curse of Blood and Wolves*—as if the target demographic of affluent suburban escapists can be trusted to handle a lexical shift without hyperventilating. 

### The Architecture of Forestal Cliches

Ruby Bellerose occupies a fairy-tale cottage on the periphery of Maple Hollow, which is code for an author realizing she cannot afford an original spatial metaphor. We are treated to the perennial trope of the reclusive woodsman. Rafe is brooding. Rafe is mysterious. Rafe is constructed entirely from surplus lumber and the marketing notes of three different bestselling paperback franchises. 

One suspects the psychological depth here rivals the casting choices of the Fyre Festival. 

### The Tyranny of the Fated Mate

And yet, the machinery grinds forward. The synopsis leans heavily on the architecture of forced proximity and paranormal seasoning, attempting to elevate what is essentially a grocery store checkout impulse purchase into high art. Elena Armas and Kandi Steiner provide their obligatory blurbs, praising the "swoon-worthy tension." Tension. Right. The kind of manufactured friction one finds in a showroom kitchen designed by an algorithm that has never actually boiled an egg. 

### The Marketing Industrial Complex

Let us speak plainly about the genre's descent. We are promised a darker, moodier cousin to Dream Harbor—a stylistic pivot reminiscent of the moment Gloria Vanderbilt attempted mustard yellow. Small-town charm grafted onto supernatural lupine mythology. It is derivative. It is safe. It trades on the exhausted cultural memory of *Once Upon a Time* and repackages it for readers who think reading darker fiction means turning off the bedside lamp. 

The prose—judging by the relentless parade of exclamation-pointed editorial praise—will undoubtedly rely on adjectives that do the heavy lifting of entire paragraphs. Yearning. Atmospheric. Delicious. The literary equivalent of a truffle-oil drizzle over a frozen pizza. 

The oysters delivered at noon were lukewarm, utterly devoid of the brine and crisp salinity one expects from an import arriving before midday, and frankly, this entire exercise has left me entirely disinclined to investigate what lurks in the woods.
