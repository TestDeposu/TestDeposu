**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Darling, another suburban satire dressed in a faux-designer cardigan about to drop in 2026. Sue Hincenbergs serves up *Very Bad Neighbors*, a literary amuse-bouche that smells entirely of Trader Joe’s reserve pinot and concealed carry permits. We are asked to swallow the oldest chestnut in the pantry: the reformed assassin seeking domestic tranquility in a zip code that requires a homeowners association. Groundbreaking. Truly, it’s giving the 2005 cinematic masterclass *Mr. & Mrs. Smith* mixed with whatever whispered panic happens during the silent auction committee meetings at private academies in Greenwich. 

### The Architecture of Astroturfed Domesticity

Let us examine the synopsis like a flawed vintage pulled from a dubious cellar. Soph Kowalski trades the obsidian aesthetic of living with a literal hitman for a fresh start across the country. And why? Because every middle-market thriller requires a relocation trope to manufacture artificial friction. The premise rests entirely on a structural paradox. You cannot live in the shadows while simultaneously objecting to police tape on the begonias. 

Hincenbergs constructs a sleepy hollow where someone is trying to execute a neighbor. Naturally, Soph and Mike must play amateur sleuths to protect their quietude. But policing local homicides is a terrible way to keep a low profile. It's giving Fyre Festival logistics. The conceptual tension relies on the exhausted juxtaposition of domestic mundanity and sudden violence. White picket fences sharp as knives. Ground-breaking prose. 

### Subverting the Cul-de-Sac Aesthetic

The thematic undercurrent here is painfully obvious. The suburban dream is a cage, darling. We get it. Every author from Shirley Jackson onward has beaten this dead horse until the glue factory calls. Yet, there is a certain guilty-pleasure architecture to these narratives. Hincenbergs leans heavily into the anxiety of assimilation. How does a woman accustomed to compartmentalizing bodies in trunks suddenly transition to neighborhood book clubs and artisanal sourdough starters? 

The psychological dichotomy is deliciously messy. Soph wants normalcy, yet her survival instincts are calibrated for collateral damage. One expects the pacing to move at breakneck speed, sacrificing psychological depth for high-concept momentum. The wealthy enclave serves as a pressure cooker, mirroring the toxic opulence of a season finale of *The Real Housewives of Salt Lake City*. 

### The Verdict on the Horizon

Will *Very Bad Neighbors* reinvent the domestic thriller? Absolutely not. It is engineered for airport lounges and sun-drenched pool decks where readers demand zero cognitive heavy lifting. It is fast-food fiction plated on bone china. Yet, there is a market for this specific brand of polished trash. 

Pour another glass of something expensive. Ignore the obvious narrative contrivances. Just pray the prose has more bite than the marketing copy suggests. 

I must go break up a wrestling match in the living room because the vintage Oushak rug is currently collateral damage.
