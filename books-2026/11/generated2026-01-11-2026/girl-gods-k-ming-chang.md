**Author:** K-Ming Chang  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

The archives of the House of Lancaster hold many curious ledgers, yet none quite so peculiar as the modern literary obsession with vanishing schoolgirls. K-Ming Chang presents us with *Girl Gods*, a forthcoming verse novel that arrives with the exact sort of dramatic pedigree one expects from the National Book Foundation's favored darlings. But let us look past the polished lineage. We are asked to ponder adolescent fury, secret blogs, and aquatic deities named Mazu rewriting the celestial register. One wonders if the author simply watched *The Bling Ring* while mainlining ancient mythology and decided to draft a manuscript on linen stationery. 

### The Aristocracy of Adolescence

Verse novels often suffer from a terrible affliction—they mistake line breaks for depth. And *Girl Gods* leans heavily into this breathless lyricism. Celeste stumbles upon a password. Stephanie vanishes. Stephanie returns, wearing an entirely different biological vessel courtesy of a Chinese sea goddess. One cannot help recalling the tragic architectural miscalculation of the Tacoma Narrows Bridge—all magnificent aesthetic motion, yet profoundly unstable at the core. Does Chang truly understand the weight of divine bargains, or is this merely another exercise in contemporary YA myth-making dressed up in poetic tulle? 

Yet, today, I find myself remarkably charitable. The aristocracy of grief usually bores me to tears. Decades of debutantes crying over crumbling family estates have made one rather immune to teenage angst. Yet there is something almost charmingly reckless about trading mortal flesh for marine divinity just to escape midterms. 

### Divine Bargains and Missing Ledgers

The synopsis leaves us stranded in the murky woods of narrative vagueness. We know only of a stolen password and a bodily swap. Chang asks what it means to become divine. But I ask: who is paying the upholstery bill for this metaphysical transformation? Bloodlines matter. Pedigrees matter. You cannot simply strike a bargain with a sea deity without auditing the metaphysical ledger. 

The verse format promises atmospheric devastation. Chang has a sharp ear, one must concede that much. If the syntax holds up against the sheer weight of celestial vanity, this might transcend the typical YA paperback shelf. One simply prays the poetry does not collapse under its own metaphorical weight before the final page turn.

I must now depart to select the appropriate seventeenth-century Flemish tapestries for the lobby of our new corporate headquarters.
