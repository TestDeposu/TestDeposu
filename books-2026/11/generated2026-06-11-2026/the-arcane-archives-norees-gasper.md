**Author:** Norees Gasper  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

The smell of cinnamon and melted brown sugar always makes my mind wander back to ink-stained pages and rainy afternoons spent squinting at dog-eared paperbacks while my youngest tracks flour across the linoleum, which brings me directly to Norees Gasper’s upcoming debut, *The Arcane Archives*. We are apparently wading knee-deep into another dark academia fantasy adventure—because Leigh Bardugo walked so an entire generation of supernatural archivists could aggressively sprint through gloomy libraries—and honestly, the premise rests entirely on the delightfully chaotic notion that every single person has a physical life book guarded by institutional red tape. Astra steals her missing twin’s book, finds a glaringly redacted memory involving a green-eyed stranger named Castor who conveniently lacks a life book altogether, and proceeds to drag a bizarre entourage consisting of a banshee, a demon cat, and an exhausted bartender all the way to the Isle of Skye. 

Cats. 

Astra’s quest operates on the tired engine of institutional secrets and missing sibling tropes, yet the inclusion of Scottish mythology and book-based magic gives the promotional material a cozy, rustic bite that reminds me of hunting through thrift store shelves for out-of-print ghost stories while the kids smear peanut butter on the pantry door and scream about a blue puppy on the television. 

Slow-burn romance is promised here, as if we haven't all read fifty variations of the brooding, memory-less man who claims he has never met the protagonist despite glaringly obvious cosmological evidence to the contrary. Gasper leans hard into the found family dynamic—a trope that went from a comforting narrative sanctuary to a mandated industrial checklist item around 2021—and I genuinely worry the plot will drown in its own atmospheric vibes, sacrificing sharp narrative momentum for the sake of lingering glances over dusty tomes and moody Scottish fog. 

Atmosphere. 

If Gasper manages to balance the sharp-tongued banter of a sarcastic bartender with the looming dread of blacked-out archival pages, this might actually survive the crushing weight of its own promotional blurbs. 

I have to go convince a toddler that taking a bath is not a punishment.
