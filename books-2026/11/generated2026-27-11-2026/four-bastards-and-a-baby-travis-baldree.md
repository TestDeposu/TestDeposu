The smell of browned butter and toasted pecans is currently drifting from the oven, heavy and warm enough to push back the early November chill pressing against the kitchen windowpanes, while the flour dust settles in a fine, quiet layer over the countertops where two little pairs of hands had just been happily rolling out pastry dough before running off to build some elaborate fortress in the living room. It is in this exact state of buttery, yeast-scented tranquility that I find myself looking ahead to Travis Baldree’s upcoming 2026 release, *Four Bastards and a Baby*, a book that apparently trades the gentle, cozy herbalism of *Legends & Lattes* for a subterranean death trap containing a cast of misfits, a mysterious egg, and an infant human who might just be the most terrifying thing in existence. 

And honestly? I am entirely here for it, because if anyone can take a premise that sounds like a chaotic Tuesday night at a local tavern and turn it into something genuinely charming, it is Baldree. 

Subterranean dungeons are generally exhausted territory by now—we have all read enough grimdark doorstoppers about endless labyrinthine floors to last several lifetimes—yet pivoting the usual grim survival trope into an accidental family comedy featuring a half-devil, a pacifist blacksmith, a genius tinker harboring dark secrets, and an angry sister trapped inside a sword feels like the exact antidote to the gloomy, rain-soaked November gloom outside. 

Perfection.

The thematic mechanics here rely heavily on the forced proximity of incompatible personalities, a classic found-family dynamic that practically screams comfort reading, even if the synopsis insists on wrapping it in lethal hazards and monstrous threats. 

Baking bread requires a precise balance of wild yeast, patient fermentation, and absolute trust in a process you cannot entirely control, which mirrors precisely what Baldree is attempting with this wildly disparate ensemble—throwing misfits into a shifting architecture and hoping the narrative rises without collapsing into a dense, unpalatable mess of recycled tropes. 

Will the execution match the cleverness of the setup? 

Probably. 

Or perhaps it will turn into an overly complicated disaster that leaves a bitter aftertaste. 

Either way, the addition of a human baby as the ultimate weapon of mass destruction is a stroke of sheer comedic audacity that redeems any lingering cynicism I might harbor toward endless dungeon crawls. 

Pass the cinnamon.

Sigh. I really need to get some sewing oil for the stuck ribbon on my vintage Underwood typewriter before the bread finishes baking.
