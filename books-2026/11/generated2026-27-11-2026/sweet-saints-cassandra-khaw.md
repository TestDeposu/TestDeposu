**Author:** Cassandra Khaw  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Give me a break. I am supposed to stretch this synopsis of *Sweet Saints* into an actual piece of writing just because someone upstairs needs another word-count triumph before the quarterly audit, while I am sitting here desperately trying to remember why I ever abandoned my drop-cloths for this literary servitude. But let us look at what Cassandra Khaw is cooking up for 2026. A cozy fantasy novella—with teeth. Groundbreaking. Truly, an epistemological coup for those of us who grew up choking on Terry Pratchett knockoffs and desperately needed our anthropomorphic folklore to draw actual blood. 

### The Aesthetics of Skinless Infants and Bureaucracy

Sister Hound sounds like the kind of cynical protagonist I try to sketch when I am feeling too lazy to invent a real human being. She rescues selkies and fox-brides from domestic incarceration. She hunts deadbeat patriarchs of the mythical variety. Yet she gets slapped with a doorstep infant—classic trope recycling, though executed with enough bite to make it mid-wit palatable. And honestly? I am so sick of deconstructing heroic journeys. Give me a bounty hunter with a mortgage and a squalling, occasionally skinless infant. I am tired. I just want to wrap myself in the worn, comfortable blanket of familiar folklore structures and pretend the gallery doesn't evict me next Tuesday. 

Khaw usually traffics in visceral dread, so applying that hyper-visceral sensibilities to the inherently fraudulent "cozy fantasy" market is a chef's kiss move. It is giving Shirley Jackson meets a checked-out gig-economy worker who accidentally adopted a maritime anomaly. 

### The Domesticity Trap

Look at the central conflict. To whom does a peculiar, occasionally skinless baby belong? This is the eternal bourgeois trap disguised as magic realism. Property rights versus maternal instinct. Selkie jurisprudence. And look, I am just typing words to hit the stupid quota so I can afford another tube of flake white oil paint, but this custody battle over a marine toddler has actual legs. Or fins. Whatever. 

The narrative leans hard into the friction between mythological autonomy and domestic domestication. We watch magical wives rescued only to face the bureaucratic nightmare of bureaucratic re-homing. It is basically the Department of Motor Vehicles, but the clerk is a vengeful seal-woman demanding her kin back. Absolutely zero surprises here, yet I will still preorder it because I have zero self-control and my aesthetic judgment is entirely compromised by chronic exhaustion. 

I need to run to a small-press poetry reading in Brooklyn before the rain starts.
