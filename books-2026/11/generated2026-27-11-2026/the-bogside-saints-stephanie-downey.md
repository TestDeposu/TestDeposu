**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

The rain against these floor-to-ceiling glass panes sounds remarkably like static this November afternoon, a dull percussion matching my internal weather. *The Bogside Saints* arrives on the horizon with all the subtlety of a falling chandelier. Stephanie Downey wants us to stare unflinchingly at Derry in 1972. Fine. I have spent millions collecting art born from agony, so perhaps I am uniquely qualified to critique the commercialization of human suffering. 

### The Optics of Misery

We are promised a sweeping debut where a shutter-bug heroine watches her childhood friends fracture along ideological lines. Liam chooses peace. Malachy chooses the Provisionals. Cara chooses a Leica and a very dangerous romance. It is the kind of setup that smells faintly of an algorithm trying to harvest a Booker Prize shortlist. Why must every historical drama pivot on a tragic catalyst forcing an innocent into the fray? It feels terribly pedestrian. Yet, the framing of photography as an isolating lens—a glass wall between the observer and the blood on the cobblestones—saves the premise from total banality. Or perhaps I am just projecting because I spent four hours this morning staring at a blank canvas wondering why anything matters. 

Let us be entirely honest for a fraction of a second. The synopsis reads like the pitch meeting that birthed *Titanic*—if Rose had a better zoom lens and a worse haircut. Downey is leaning hard into the classic structural beats of the political romance. Boys with different destinies, a girl caught in the crossfire, and the inevitable crunch of moral compromises breaking under pressure. It is derivative, yet I find myself morbidly invested in how badly these characters are going to ruin each other's lives. 

### The Cost of Devotion

The thematic spine here is the erosion of loyalty under the weight of sectarian violence. Love as collateral damage. It reminds one of the yacht party scene in *Succession* where everyone realizes they are already dead and just waiting for the paperwork to clear. Downey wants to show us that devotion cannot survive an urban warzone untouched. Groundbreaking stuff. Truly. 

And yet, I am strangely susceptible to the romantic fatalism of it all today. Give me star-crossed idiots in a burning city and I will happily write a blank check just to watch them crash. Or maybe I am simply exhausted by perfection. Give me mess. Give me bad choices made in damp back alleys. Just do not make it boring. 

Someone just emailed me to say the Monet replica I had my eyes on for the west gallery was purchased by that insufferable hedge fund manager from Zurich.
