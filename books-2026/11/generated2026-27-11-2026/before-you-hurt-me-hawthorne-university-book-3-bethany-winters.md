**Author:** Bethany Winters  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Staring at a blank page is equivalent to watching dust settle on a thousand-year-old amphora. Absolutely maddening. And yet, here we are, attempting to dissect Bethany Winters’ forthcoming *Before You Hurt Me*. The third installment in the *Hawthorne University* sequence arrives in 2026, promising a winter break holiday ruined by ancient relics and very deep pockets. 

Jack Hawthorne, professor of antiquities, wants nothing to do with heroic adventures. Naturally. Because no academic in modern fiction can simply grade papers and drink lukewarm Earl Grey without a billionaire kicking down their mahogany door. Enter Gordon Reese, sporting a blank check and a wild obsession with biblical skeletal remains. It is the classic Da Vinci Code gambit, dusted off for a modern campus. 

### The Dust of Old Men and Billionaires

Let us address the narrative machinery here. A skeptic hunting for miraculous bones that allegedly resurrect the dead? Groundbreaking. Utterly revolutionary. James Rollins and Steve Berry have literally built entire publishing empires on this exact brand of high-octane pseudo-archaeology. But wait, it gets better. Toss in a "fiery colleague" named Esperanza Habilla, and we have checked every single box on the academic-romance-thriller bingo card. Enemies to partners. Sarcasm hiding deep emotional trauma. Shadows and secrets protected at all costs. 

Speaking of old things that refuse to stay dead—why do animators think children enjoy forty-minute sequences of singing trash cans? My grandchildren are currently obsessed with some neon garbage fire of a streaming show where a sentient landfill sings about recycling. It makes about as much sense as a billionaire funding a clandestine dig for the prophet Elisha's femur based on a vague rumor. At least the trash can show has better lighting than a shadowy international syndicate chasing academics across the globe. 

### Shadows, Bones, and Academic Burnout

Winters relies heavily on the universal fatigue of the overworked professor. We are supposed to buy into the adrenaline. Yet, the synopsis plays out like a hyper-fixated freshman writing their first pulp fiction thesis at three in the morning. Jack needs money. Fine. Relatable. We all need money. But dragging a fiery colleague into a global manhunt over Old Testament relics feels like a severe overreaction to fiscal anxiety. 

The thematic core hinges on skepticism colliding with undeniable ancient power. Will Jack believe the bones work once someone starts shooting at them? Obviously. Characters in these books do not possess critical thinking skills; they possess plot armor. Expect mandatory narrow escapes in dusty catacombs, forced proximity in damp motels, and enough lingering gazes over stolen map fragments to power a small municipality. 

Predictions for this manuscript? Utter chaos. Formulaic, fast-paced, and utterly devoid of actual historical nuance. But readers will devour it anyway because we all secretly crave a billionaire handing us a blank check to escape our daily responsibilities. 

Now, if you will excuse me, I have to untangle three miles of blinking reindeer lights from the attic before my spouse completely loses patience and redecorates the lawn with my vintage rare-book collection.
