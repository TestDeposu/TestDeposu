The heater in the hallway has developed that exact metallic rattle that sounds like someone dropping loose change into an empty paint tin. November afternoons are already entirely too short for this nonsense, dragging long, bruised shadows across the hardwood before four o'clock even hits, leaving me sitting here wrapped in an oversized wool cardigan while waiting for Sher Lee to break my heart all over again in *Our Fractured Fates*. 

### Blood on the Plum Blossoms

Give me a good blood feud any day. Or better yet, give me the exquisite agony of star-crossed cultivators who simply refuse to stay dead. We are looking at a masterclass in emotional devastation here. The premise of Wen waking up in a completely different vessel after his entire clan gets thoroughly eradicated by his secret boyfriend’s father is the kind of deliciously unhinged melodrama I live for. Naturally, he infiltrates the elite cultivation academy. Naturally, the murderous patriarch's son—the very boy who once held his heart—gets assigned as his personal tutor without recognizing him. Oh, the sheer, crushing irony. Mo Xiang Tong Xiu walked so this specific brand of queer romantasy could sprint through a burning building. 

### The Art of the Grudge

People always complain about revenge plots being derivative. They whine about tropes like secret identities and forced proximity as if these aren't the foundational pillars of human literature since Sophocles. Sorry, lost my train of thought. But seriously, why would anyone want originality when you can have a brooding protagonist wearing a borrowed face, forced to sit politely through sword-fighting lectures given by the boy who essentially ruined his lineage? It is petty, it is dramatic, and I am entirely here for it. Lee understands the assignment completely. 

### Cultivation and Cold Coffee

My neighbor's terrible beagle has been barking at a plastic bag stuck in the hedge for forty-five minutes straight, which somehow feels entirely appropriate for a genre where people spend three hundred years brooding over a single missed glance on a moonlight bridge. Why do dogs insist on picking fights with inanimate debris? It reminds me of certain readers who demand realism from books about immortal swordsmen slicing mountains in half with a flick of their wrist. We do not read for realism. We read to watch two deeply traumatized young men stare across a classroom while internalizing decades of generational guilt and undeniable homoerotic tension. 

Trusting the enemy who happens to be your ex is a terrible life choice. Wen is going to make every single wrong decision possible, and I intend to cheer him on from the sidelines while my tea turns tepid. 

I need to unclog the shower drain before the entire second floor floods.
