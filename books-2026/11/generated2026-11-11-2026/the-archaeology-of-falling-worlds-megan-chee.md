**Author:** Megan Chee  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

The yeasty scent of cinnamon rolls is slowly fading into a quiet, flour-dusted stillness in my kitchen, leaving behind that particular brand of Sunday-afternoon peace where the children are finally building elaborate block towers instead of fighting over wooden spoons. Megan Chee’s upcoming *The Archaeology of Falling Worlds* arrives on the horizon with a peculiar kind of literary swagger, promising a collision of neon grit and pastoral wonder that sounds dangerously like marketing buzzwords taped to a spine. Yet, the premise of a garbage-picker in a gated metropolis finding a sapient, shape-shifting worm in the desert dust manages to evoke a strange sort of curiosity, especially if it leans closer to the heartbreaking ecological decay found in *Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind* rather than the exhausting cynicism of modern franchise cyberpunk. 

### Salvage Yards and Memory Holes

Resource scarcity always makes for sharp speculative fiction, provided the author avoids turning systemic cruelty into a playground aesthetic. Starga sounds like a logistical nightmare masqueraded as a sanctuary—a vertical metropolis overflowing with discarded debris from unreachable dimensions, which honestly reminds me of the absolute chaos left behind when my youngest decides to dump three hundred LEGO bricks onto the hardwood floor right before bedtime while insisting they are all vital pieces of a spaceship that cannot possibly be stepped on without causing an emergency room visit. 

And yet, this world-building relies heavily on the classic trope of the oppressed scavenger forced into one last heist. Groundbreaking? Hardly. But the inclusion of Alasar and the shape-shifting Silver hints at something richer beneath the sand dunes. 

### The Calculus of Survival

What saves a broken society from itself? The synopsis poses this lofty ethical dilemma with the subtlety of a falling anvil. Characters must choose between preserving a collapsing status quo or leaping blindly into an unwritten future. It is a tired binary. Real systemic change rarely happens because a street-smart gang member and a naive academic touch a magical worm; it happens through grueling, unglamorous collective exhaustion. Still, the emotional architecture holds promise. 

Baking bread requires a precise ratio of patience, heat, and structural integrity, much like weaving a sprawling multiverse tale without letting the seams tear apart from their own ambition. 

Fingers crossed.
