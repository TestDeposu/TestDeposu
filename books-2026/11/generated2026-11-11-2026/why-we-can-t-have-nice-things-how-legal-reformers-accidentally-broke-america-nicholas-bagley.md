Sit closer to the hearth, little ones, and let me tell you of the great curse that has fallen upon the kingdom. We used to carve monuments that pierced the clouds and forge iron roads across the burning deserts. Now? Now our scribes and high priests are utterly paralyzed by their own sacred scrolls. 

Nicholas Bagley has looked upon this modern labyrinth in *Why We Can't Have Nice Things: How Legal Reformers Accidentally Broke America* and decided to name the beast. Or at least, that is what the oracle's parchment promises us. I swear, trying to get a simple aqueduct built in this village nowadays requires appeasing seventy different bureaucratic water-sprites through endless petitions of parchment. It reminds me of watching that ridiculous animated show where the blue dog family spends twenty minutes arguing over a simple game of pretend while the parents slowly lose their minds on the rug—honestly, television peaked with *Gargoyles* in nineteen-ninety-four and we have been slouching toward Gomorrah ever since. Where was I? Ah, yes. The paralysis of our modern tribe.

### The Curse of the Golden Bureaucracy

The official scrolls tell us that back in the late sixties, well-meaning sorcerers of the law tried to protect the sacred groves from reckless developers. A noble quest, truly. Yet what did they forge? A monster made of red tape and infinite delays. 

Bagley argues that our obsession with the *procedure* has utterly devoured the *promise*. We now worship the ritual of the lawsuit rather than the harvest of results. 

### The Oracle's Prophecy

Will this text actually slay the dragon? Unlikely. Books written by professors from the University of Michigan rarely possess the sharp edge of a hero's spear. They prefer to stroke their beards by the fire and ponder the philosophy of the trap rather than kick the door down. We do not need another academic lamentation over why the stone bridge takes fifty years to permit. We need a hero willing to burn the courtrooms to ash. 

Still, if you enjoy watching wise men weep over the ruins they themselves engineered, this offering might soothe your winter nights. 

My publisher is breathing down my neck about deadlines again, but the third-grade papier-mâché volcano for the science fair takes absolute precedence over corporate demands.
