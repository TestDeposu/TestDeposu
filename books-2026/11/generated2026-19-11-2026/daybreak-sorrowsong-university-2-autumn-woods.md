The rain lashes against the Palladian panes like a frantic creditor at the gate. Everything is decaying. The velvet curtains smell faintly of damp coal, and my morning correspondence brings only tidings of aesthetic bankruptcy—specifically, the impending arrival of Autumn Woods’ *Daybreak*. 

### The Architecture of Petty Vengeance

We are expected to care about aristocratic youth behaving atrociously in the Scottish Highlands. Naturally. Because what else does a modern public crave if not the cinematic equivalent of the Titanic string quartet playing while the upholstery burns? The synopsis promises us a world ablaze, yet it reads less like Lord Byron and more like a poorly formatted thread on a gossip forum concerning Lord Lucan’s card debts. 

Alex Corbeau-Green and Ophelia Winters are locked in a perpetual dance of spite. Grumpy versus grumpy. How wonderfully exhausting. They came to Sorrowsong University to ruin each other—an ambition I can vaguely respect, provided it involves adequate prose and a generous supply of arsenic. But no. We are subjected to the pedestrian indignity of hidden evidence, campus murder mysteries, and CEOs acting like petulant toddlers. It is the literary equivalent of the Met Gala staircase collapse. 

### Sizzling Tension and Other Commercial Vulgarities

Ali Hazelwood calls it immensely fun. One shudders at the metric being utilized here. The publishers are leaning heavily on tropes like the groveling billionaire and the mandatory university setting—because nothing screams "dark academia" quite like corporate restructuring and tuition fees. 

The prose style, if the plot blurb is any true barometer, relies on the tired choreography of emotional blackmail. Ophelia is left alone and watched. Alex is in New York, desperate to forget. My God. The existential weight of a missed text message has rarely felt so profoundly dull. We are promised an explosive conclusion to the *Nightshade* duology, yet the mechanics of the synopsis smell suspiciously of focus groups and algorithmic keyword stuffing. Will they trust each other? Will love keep them alive? I would rather drink hemlock brewed from last Tuesday's Earl Grey. 

### The Autopsy of Romance

One detects the distinct odor of desperation in these marketing bullet points. The heavy-handed comparisons to *Maxton Hall* and RuNyx only accentuate the artistic void. Where is the lyricism? Where is the devastating ache of true modernist despair, rather than this recycled collegiate melodrama dressed up in a dark velvet blazer? They committed a murder on campus, apparently. Or perhaps they merely murdered the English language. The distinction grows blurrier by the page. 

And yet, here we sit. Waiting for July. Watching the clock tick closer to the inevitable descent. 

Good heavens, the phone is ringing. I must log off immediately and mentally prepare for my upcoming interview with *Vogue*.
