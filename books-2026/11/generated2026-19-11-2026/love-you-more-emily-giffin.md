**Author:** Emily Giffin  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-07-07 APL

Another flight delay. Three hours sitting in this leather armchair listening to someone loudly dispute a boarding pass while I wait to board a metal tube bound for a zip code where the local cuisine is deep-fried cheese and the Wi-Fi requires a landline. At least I have an advance brief to tear apart. Emily Giffin is back with *Love You More*, a title so aggressively generic it sounds like a rejected karaoke track from a nineties soft-rock station. 

### The Geography of Regret

Look at the premise. The synopsis practically leaks stale airport prose. We have Billie—obviously a doctor because writers refuse to give female protagonists hobbies that don’t require a white coat or an espresso machine—sitting pretty in Manhattan with a sensible, upscale fiance named Dean. Naturally, the past comes knocking via a rotary phone or a dramatic cell signal drop. Mick calls. Wisconsin beckons. 

Is this *Sweet Home Alabama* with a stethoscope? The New York Times Book Review blurb practically begs us to draw the parallel. But Reese Witherspoon had charm, whereas Billie sounds like she’s about to subject everyone in Madison to an exhaustive PowerPoint presentation on her mortgage portfolio. 

Giffin relies on the oldest crutch in commercial fiction: the geographical binary. New York equals ambition, cold salads, and emotional containment. The Midwest equals true love, high school nostalgia, and flannel shirts that smell faintly of woodsmoke. It is lazy world-building masquerading as emotional depth. Or perhaps I am being too harsh on the setting—actually, no, Wisconsin deserves every bit of literary condescension it gets when used as a shorthand for authentic virtue. 

### Pacing the Runway

The narrative engine here relies entirely on a withheld secret. Mick calls with "urgent news." What could it possibly be? He bought a failing dairy farm? He needs a kidney? Or—God forbid—he is still brooding by a lake, waiting for the girl who got away to abandon her thriving medical practice for a porch swing? 

The pacing on paper looks dangerously front-loaded. Giffin spends two paragraphs establishing Billie's perfect life only to detonate it in a single phone call before the coffee is cold. Yet I suspect the middle third will drag like an economy-class red-eye flight. We are guaranteed at least eighty pages of lingering glances across rustic tavern tables while Billie questions whether her engagement ring has enough ethically sourced carbon to match her soul. It’s Emily Henry territory without the wit, or a Nicholas Sparks adaptation minus the tragic canoe accidents. 

And yet. 

The market eats this up. My assistants rave about every suburban tearjerker that crosses the desk. Maybe the structural predictability is the entire point. People do not buy these books for narrative invention; they buy them for the comforting hum of a formula that never deviates from the flight plan. 

Boarding is finally called, which means I have to pack away my tablet and endure three hours of a middle seat next to someone who thinks cargo shorts are formal wear. At least my brother’s spare room won’t have Wi-Fi to remind me of the emails piling up in my absence. Which brings me to the absolute state of my apartment. I return to find my home office entirely overrun by a fleet of limited-edition plastic action figures my nephew insisted on shipping ahead of my arrival.
