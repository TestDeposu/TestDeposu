**Author:** Liv Zander  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-07-23 APL

Another day, another stack of glossy paperbacks promising to ruin me—as if the steam wand spitting calcium deposits all over my apron isn't already doing a bang-up job of that. *Crown Me Dead* slides across the counter with all the subtlety of a blunt force trauma. The synopsis is practically vibrating with the kind of desperate, performative darkness that makes me want to pull a triple shot and stare into the abyss. Seduce the king. Die. The premise is essentially a gothic fever dream filtered through a Wattpad-to-trad-pub pipeline. It’s tired. It’s familiar. And yet, there’s a flicker of something—a necrotic, graveyard-dirt sort of charm—that suggests Zander isn't just cosplaying as a villain lover.

### The Necrotic Aesthetics of Power

The text leans hard into the aesthetic of rot, which is objectively the only way to write romance anymore—nobody wants a polished prince who smells like cedar and good intentions. Give me the festering ruin, the absolute catastrophe of a man who makes bad life choices his personality. The hook—wed me, bed me, slit my throat—is pure, unadulterated melodrama, a trope that feels ripped straight from the fevered subconscious of a reader who finds Rina Kent a bit too wholesome. It’s a transaction. A blood contract. It reads like a desperate pivot away from the "enemies to lovers" fluff that currently clogs the bestseller lists, favoring something more akin to a taxidermy shop floor. 

### Why Pawn When You Can Be the Grave

The agency promised here—the girl who buries them—is the only thing keeping me from tossing this into the bin with the rest of the generic romantasy sludge. Most protagonists in this genre are cardboard cutouts waiting to be manhandled by a brooding shadow—looking at you, *ACOTAR* imitators—but there’s a promise of actual spite here. Spite is a vastly underrated literary device. If Zander leans into the nihilistic, cold-blooded pragmatism of a gravedigger’s daughter instead of folding into the "I can fix him" trope, we might actually have something worth reading. 

The synopsis leaves so much unsaid that I’m forced to fill the gaps with my own cynicism—the mark of a truly unfinished sales pitch. Are we getting a psychological thriller disguised as smut, or is it just another exercise in performative misery? The inclusion of full-color character art feels like a desperate grab for the BookTok demographic, a shiny toy for people who don't want to actually imagine the horror themselves. I’ve seen this playbook before. It’s a total bait-and-switch. But if the prose manages to be as filthy as the premise suggests, I might just suffer through the cliches. 

I’m going to go smoke a pipe and ponder the futility of modern existence.
