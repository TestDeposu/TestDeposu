**Author:** Rou Bao Bu Chi Rou  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-12-08 APL

The amber burn of this twenty-year-old single malt is currently the only thing keeping my nervous system from completely short-circuiting after reading the official brief for *Case File Compendium: Bing An Ben (Novel) Vol. 10*. Rou Bao Bu Chi Rou is about to put us through absolute emotional garbage, and I am seated here in a leather armchair that costs more than a used hatchback, questioning every single life choice that led me to this exact corporate retreat. 

### The Anatomy of Absolute Ruin

Two years of mourning. A dead protagonist. A ghost memoir. The synopsis reads like the author sat down, looked at our collective jugular veins, and decided to slice them open with a rusty razor just for the aesthetic. We are staring down the barrel of a concluding volume that promises grief so dense it feels physical, only to throw a curveball about a possible happy ending. Total tonal whiplash. One second we are drowning in Heideggerian despair, and the next we are promised a raunchy domestic epilogue that feels deeply unhinged. Honestly? It's giving supreme psychological damage. Peak psychological trauma dressed up in designer silks, and we are all going to eat it up like feral raccoons. 

### The Mechanics of Suffering

Or perhaps the grief angle is entirely a marketing fake-out designed to break our spirits before the final punchline. Wait. Scratch that. Rou Bao Bu Chi Rou never pulls punches; if Xie Qingcheng’s demise is just a narrative fake-out, I am throwing this glass straight through the panoramic window. The literary architecture here relies entirely on codependency so toxic it makes *Wuthering Heights* look like a wholesome Disney animation. Danmei readership loves a good emotional evisceration, but this tenth volume is pushing past mere angst into pure, unadulterated brain-rot territory. We crave the devastation. We need the catharsis. 

Yet, I am deeply skeptical of how they plan to jam a happily-ever-after and spicy extra chapters into the aftermath of absolute ruin without it feeling rushed. Give us the slow burn of psychological reconstruction, or give us nothing at all. 

I am going to close my laptop right now, pour another two fingers of this aggressively expensive scotch, and read a battered copy of Kerouac until the sun comes up.
