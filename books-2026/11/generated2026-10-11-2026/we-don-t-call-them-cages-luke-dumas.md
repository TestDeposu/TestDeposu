**Page Count:** Unknown

Darling, look at this atrocious marketing copy. Luke Dumas wants us to tremble before *We Don’t Call Them Cages* like peasants before a guillotine, but frankly, the prose smells intensely of middle-management panic. Let us dissect the wreckage. 

### The Menagerie of Mediocrity

A communications executive named Ashlee desperately spinning PR disasters while blood drips from the rafters. Groundbreaking. We are trapped in real time over a single agonizing day—a structural gimmick that usually masks an absolute dearth of actual ideas. And oh, the dialogue potential! We get bright-eyed Megan, who is apparently morally conflicted about visiting a glorified maximum-security habitat for majestic beasts. Then enters Brandon, a disgruntled zookeeper who has fully snapped. The man is literally channeling absolute unhinged goblin energy, absolutely feral because his wage is low and his soul is crushed. A total mood, honestly.

### Metaphors Placed in Cages

The thematic resonance here hits you over the cranium with the subtlety of a swinging iron bar. The title itself—*We Don’t Call Them Cages*—winks heavily at the prison of late-stage capitalism. We are all beasts pacing behind invisible bars of our own making, waiting for someone to unlatch the gate and let the chaos consume the corporate overlords. It's giving Michael Crichton meets *Falling* by T. J. Newman, minus the altitude. But let us be entirely real for a fleeting second. Can Dumas sustain a high-octane thriller purely on the frantic pacing of screaming tourists and loose apex predators, or will it devolve into a predictable slasher fest where every secondary character exists purely to be digested? 

### The Carnage Yet Unwritten

I predict an absolute disaster of narrative discipline. Dumas traded literary rigor for cheap, dopamine-drenched carnage, leaning into the same sensationalist gutter as Daniel Kraus’s *Whalefall*. Give me poetry. Give me cadence that doesn't read like an incident report from local law enforcement. Yet, the sheer audacity of unleashing lions upon unassuming suburbanites eating cotton candy is delightfully chaotic. Let the fauna feast upon the bourgeoisie, I say. 

Wait. The buzzer at the front iron gate just shrieked through the salon. The bespoke Italian leather chaise lounge from Milan has finally arrived, and heaven forbid the delivery couriers leave my ottoman out in the cruel, uncaring drizzle.
