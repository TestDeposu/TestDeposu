The cold rain is slamming hard against the double-paned glass right now, leaving these dull, gray streaks that make the November evening feel entirely too heavy. I am sitting at the dining table with a half-eaten sleeve of crackers, staring down at Taylor Adams's upcoming *Her Last Breath*, and I can already tell this friendship dynamic is going to ruin my week. 

Because we all have that one friend. You know the type. The one with the blinding, aggressive charisma who drags you out of your comfort zone because they think your quiet, predictable life is a tragedy waiting to be fixed. Tess just wanted to conquer a harmless bit of claustrophobia. And instead, her dazzling, take-no-shit best friend Allie had to pick a fight with a stranger at a cave entrance, completely unaware that people out in the world are actually unhinged. 

Speaking of unhinged, why does every single modern middle-grade television show insist on turning anthropomorphic rescue puppies into sarcastic mortgage brokers? My youngest has been watching this absolute garbage disposal of a cartoon for three straight weeks where the animated dog lectures the town mayor about zoning permits and municipal bonds. It makes me want to scream into a pillow. Give me the pure, unadulterated terror of being wedged into a limestone crawl space hundreds of feet underground instead. At least a subterranean murderer is honest about his intentions.

Adams is playing a dangerous game here, comparing a cramped cavern to *The Shining*. That is a massive literary hill to climb. Yet I am irrationally, stubbornly convinced this book is going to stick the landing purely because *No Exit* proved the man knows how to induce a panic attack in a locked room. 

### The Architecture of Betrayal

The real meat of this story isn't the guy with the grudge in the dark. It is the hospital bed betrayal. 

Tess makes it out alive. Barely. And then the detective walks in with a file full of secrets that completely rewrites who Allie actually was. I love this trope with my entire soul. Nothing beats the slow, agonizing realization that the person who shared your Cheerios and listened to your petty complaints for a decade was basically an undercover agent with a completely fabricated identity. Friendship is already an exercise in blind faith. Throw in a secret criminal history and a random subterranean ambush, and you have got a psychological goldmine. 

And I will defend Adams until my voice gives out. Some critics are going to whine that subterranean thrillers are becoming a bit too trendy after *The Descent* and every other claustrophobic survival novel clogging the bestseller lists. But they are entirely missing the point. The cave is just the pressure cooker. The real horror is finding out your best friend had a whole vault of skeletons you never knew about while you were busy picking out hiking boots together. 

I refuse to listen to anyone who says this sounds predictable. You cannot predict a betrayal when the person holding the knife was supposedly your emergency contact. 

I have to go look at a quadratic equation because my kid is standing behind me holding a pencil like it's a weapon.
