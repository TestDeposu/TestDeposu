There is a specific, hollow ache that settles behind your ribs when you watch someone you love drift away on an unpayable tide, a quiet sinking that no amount of folded laundry or midnight pacing can reverse, which perhaps explains why Susan Fox’s upcoming debut *Charlotte Cane* feels less like a detached literary exercise and more like a mirror held up to every agonizing choice we make to keep our footing in a world that demands we be smaller, or louder, or someone else entirely. 

Worth the hype. 

Look, we have all endured enough breathless comparisons to *I Have Some Concerns* or whatever dark academic thriller is currently sitting on every nightstand in Brooklyn, but Fox actually seems interested in the unglamorous rot of ambition rather than cheap campus parlor tricks. 

And why do we constantly demand that female artists apologize for the square footage they occupy? 

Every time I try to sit down with a manuscript after a long day of fielding phone calls from the middle school attendance office—where they treat a missing doctor's note like a federal indictment—I am forced to reckon with the sheer exhaustion of women who built their lives on compromises that eventually turned to ash. 

Remember that dreadful historical romance from last autumn where the heroine forgave a man who literally locked her in a Victorian conservatory because he brought her a rare orchid? 

My toddler son tried to eat a tube of industrial-grade zinc oxide ointment this morning while I was answering an urgent email about portfolio diversification, and yet somehow I am supposed to care deeply about the nuances of a longlist nomination when the world outside my window is literally melting into slate-gray slush. 

Still, Fox’s premise—a sixty-year-old woman whose belated literary triumph is weaponized by a ghost from her past—strikes a nerve precisely because it interrogates the terrifying speed at which our carefully curated reputations can unravel over a single hotel drink. 

Memory is a traitor. 

Guilt is worse. 

We sculpt our histories out of mud and expect them to hold up like granite when the weather turns bad, don't we? 

Stop pretending. 

My skin is currently shedding in pale, papery sheets from a botched TCA peel I underwent last Tuesday, and I have exactly twelve minutes to drive across town before the dermatology clinic locks its heavy glass doors for the weekend.
