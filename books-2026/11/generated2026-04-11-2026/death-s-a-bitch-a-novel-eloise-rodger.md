**Author:** Eloise Rodger  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Water is currently pouring directly through the shoulder seam of this flimsy plastic garment, soaking straight into my favorite sweater while I try to decipher what Eloise Rodger is actually attempting to pull off in *Death's a Bitch*. Against all odds, the publishing industry has decided that what we desperately need right now is another quirky supernatural bargain involving a psychiatric facility and a domestic Grim Reaper who apparently looks like an exhausted mother. Groundbreaking. Sally Rooney comparisons are tossed around these days with the reckless abandon of a toddler flinging handfuls of unbaked sourdough starter across a freshly wiped quartz countertop. 

### Sibling Bonds and Spectral Bargains

Eighteen-year-old Aggie is supposedly mourning her sister Marcie, who is locked away dealing with a serious eating disorder behind padded table corners and stick-on wall flowers. Enter Death. Not a terrifying skeletal figure reaping souls with a rusted scythe, but an elegantly dressed, perpetually irritated maternal archetype offering an exchange. Deliver on her unusual demands and Marcie comes home. Simple enough? Hardly. We are staring down a Faustian narrative dressed up in trendy YA contemporary clothing, and frankly, I am exhausted before I have even cracked the digital spine. 

Do you know what else is exhausting? Trying to bake three distinct varieties of nut-free muffins while a four-year-old screams about the existential injustice of blue plates versus red plates, only to watch every single crumb come back in their backpacks untouched at three o'clock because they spent lunchtime trading their dignity for a juice box. That is precisely how this premise feels—a chaotic recipe thrown together with expensive ingredients that might just turn into a soggy, unpalatable mess in the oven. 

Rodger wants us to believe this debut balances heartbreaking grief with razor-sharp wit in the vein of *My Sister, the Serial Killer*. Yet the synopsis relies entirely on vibes and high-concept absurdity rather than telling us how eighteen-year-old Aggie's internal voice actually functions beyond hating her college peers. 

### The Mortality Trap

We are promised an exploration of sickness and siblinghood. We get an impeccably styled death-dealer playing domestic tyrant. If this book does not ground its emotional stakes in the grim reality of psychiatric facilities rather than leaning entirely on the whimsical presence of an irritated maternal Grim Reaper, it will sink faster than a botched batch of choux pastry in a humid kitchen. 

I need to pack three lunches that will inevitably return uneaten.
