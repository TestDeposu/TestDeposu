I swear they multiply in the dark just to spite me while I am trying to butter four pieces of cinnamon toast at once and herd three children who have suddenly forgotten how to put their arms through coat sleeves into a minivan before the bell rings, which is precisely why reading Mike Bockoven’s upcoming *Your Best Chance of Survival* feels like peering into the exact kind of chaotic nightmare I am already living on a Tuesday morning—minus the billionaire luxury bunkers, though some days a subterranean retreat with fine dining and zero requests for juice boxes sounds rather appealing. 

### The Cost of Titanium Doors

The premise hinges on Guadiam, a high-end fallout shelter where the ungodly rich pay to ignore the burning world outside, which is a tired trope that feels suspiciously like a grocery store paperback version of *Station Eleven* mixed with *The White Lotus* on acid, except Stephen Graham Jones blurbed his last book so we are supposed to take this seriously as capital-L Literature rather than just another excuse to write about rich people tearing each other limb from limb for sport. 

Actually, wait. 

Maybe the cynicism is misplaced and Mike Bockoven is actually doing something genuinely subversive with the cannibalism and public executions rather than just throwing a lazy collection of splatterpunk tropes against a concrete wall to see what sticks. 

Cannibalism. 

### Snacks and Slaughter

The synopsis leans heavily into the absurdity of elite survivalism—complete with willing and unwilling dismemberments, because apparently when the climate collapses, the ultra-wealthy immediately revert to Regency-era dinner parties where the main course is your neighbor's accountant—and honestly, the tonal whiplash between luxury amenities and sheer feral madness is either going to be a brilliant satire of late-stage capitalism or an absolute mess of an editing room floor. 

Bleak.
