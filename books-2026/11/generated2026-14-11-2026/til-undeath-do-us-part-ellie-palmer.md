**Author:** Ellie Palmer  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Marriage is fundamentally about compromise, or so I am told by glossy magazines that cost fourteen dollars at the grocery store checkout while my seven-year-old smears yogurt into the upholstery of my station wagon, yet Ellie Palmer’s *’Til Undeath Do Us Part* suggests that the true secret to saving a rocky relationship isn't active listening or weekend getaways in Duluth, but rather contracting a blood-borne pathogen via an ancient mosquito that turns your mortician husband into your personal night-shift delivery boy for fresh plasma and awkward corpse disposal logistics. 

Groundbreaking. 

Because nothing says "we are rekindling our spark" quite like dodging a rival vampire syndicate in a tiny Minnesota town while simultaneously arguing over who forgot to empty the dishwasher before sunset. 

### Extinction-Level Communication Issues

One must admire the sheer audacity of leaning so hard into the paranormal rom-com industrial complex that we are now treating literal lycanthropy and vampirism as tired metaphors for suburban ennui, because apparently *The 5 Love Languages* just doesn't move units the way a sun-sensitive wife with a sudden penchant for homicide does. 

Do you know what else requires navigating a delicate balance of resentment, unexpressed emotional labor, and sudden bouts of inexplicable rage? Attending a toddler birthday party where the juice boxes are warm and the host expects you to clap while a child named Braxtyn plays a five-minute recorder solo of the *Paw Patrol* theme song. 

Yet people keep writing these books. 

And people keep reading them, nodding sagely at the snappy banter as if witty remarks about arterial spray are a sufficient substitute for actual narrative tension or character arcs that don't read like a discarded script for a straight-to-streaming Hulu special produced during the third trimester of a writers' room strike. 

### The Mortician's Burden

The synopsis gives away precisely nothing about the actual mechanics of this supposedly hilarious blood-bath, leaning entirely on the crutch of "corpse disposal" as a punchline rather than a logistical nightmare involving chlorine bleach and industrial-grade carpet cleaners—trust me on the bleach, you cannot simply scrub out certain stains after midnight—while relying on blurbs from authors who clearly owe the publisher a massive favor. 

One waits with bated breath for the inevitable sequel where they try couples counseling again, but this time they are ghosts, or accountants, or perhaps running a trendy brunch spot in Portland. 

Fascinating. 

I need to go break up a wrestling match in the living room before someone puts a permanent dent in the drywall.
