**Author:** Scott Moore  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Stare at a blank canvas long enough and the white starts to mock you. My eyes burn—dry, stinging remnants of a three-day binge reading *The Midnight Library* and every miserable existential paperback I could drag home from the estate sale. Yet here I am, staring at another marketing pitch instead of turps and oil. 

Scott Moore wants us to laugh. Naturally. Because the man who script-doctored *The Hangover* clearly believes grief is just a punchline waiting for a setup involving chardonnay and private investigators. 

### The Architecture of Suburban Deception

Kate, Molly, Jenn, Darlene. A quartet of mourning women traded in like used station wagons. And what do they do? They launch a "reverse murder mystery." God. The industry loves a clever hook. Give them widows, give them a coffee mug, give them a dead husband who isn't actually dead. It smells like a pitch meeting greenlit by executives who haven't set foot in a bookstore since 2014. 

Except—wait. Let me back up. Maybe I'm being too harsh on the premise. After all, subverting the tragic-widow trope with screwball capers could actually yield something biting about female solidarity in cul-de-sacs. 

Actually, no. I take it back. It sounds exhausting. 

### The Tropes We Deserve

We are drowning in the *Finlay Donovan* effect. Every thriller featuring a suburban woman with a latte in one hand and a corpse in the other has become a monolith. Elle Cosimano blurbed this one, naturally. It’s a closed ecosystem of back-cover praise where writers of breathless, witty domestic chaos endorse other writers of breathless, witty domestic chaos. 

And why am I even dissecting a synopsis for a book that won't drop until 2026? I am literally typing these words just to clear an invoice and pay for stretcher bars. 

The synopsis promises swanky bars and mansions. Suburbia versus the ultra-wealthy. A classic class-satire angle masked as a lark. If Moore actually leans into the biting absurdity of wealth disparity—using the grief group as a trojan horse for dismantling upper-class male cowardice—then *The Mad Widows* might survive the pile. If he leans too hard into the wacky bachelors and charming private investigators? Pass the chardonnay, because we are in for a long evening.

I need to approve the final guest list for my publisher's soirée.
