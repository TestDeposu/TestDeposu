**Author:** Johanna van Veen  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Velveted vellum uncut sheets from the eighteenth century do not smell like Dutch faelings, yet here we are with *The Literary Remains of Cornelia Feyen*. Johanna van Veen proposes a epistolary pastiche that promises the quaint domesticity of *The House in the Cerulean Sea* crossed with the archaeological whimsy of Heather Fawcett. But let us be entirely serious. We are looking at a literary trainwreck reminiscent of the Fyre Festival cheese sandwich, packaged neatly for people who buy their artisanal salt at Williams-Sonoma. 

### The Geopolitics of Gossip and Mud

Cornelia "Neeltje" Feyen—what a deeply unserious name for a protagonist—is allegedly behind on her manuscript because her scandalous affair with a married woman blew up faster than a British tabloid headline during a royal divorce. And so, she flees to the romantic isolation of Gravesmade. A half-human, half-faerie outcast seeking refuge from puritanical villagers. Groundbreaking. We are dealing with the exact brand of cozy municipal bureaucracy that makes contemporary fantasy feel like a mandatory HR seminar hosted by goblins. 

Yet, the epistolary framework demands scrutiny. Will van Veen weaponize the format for psychological claustrophobia, or simply use letters as cheap exposition dumps because writing actual dialogue is too much effort for modern hacks? The blurb relies heavily on the "I shouldn't touch her, but I must" sapphic governess trope. It's giving basic fanfiction on a rainy Tuesday.

### Folklore as Domestic Management

Dutch faerie folklore is deliciously strange. Water spirits, malicious changelings, curses that rot the turnips. But this synopsis reduces ancient, terrifying pagan mythos into a quaint rom-com obstacle course where supernatural creatures act like unruly toddlers at a Sunday brunch. Cornelia's ability to spot faerie curses is framed as community service. How utterly neoliberal. 

### The Aesthetic of the Messy Dyke

We must talk about the archetype. The chaotic, emotionally stunted writer fleeing scandal to a damp island is a tired trope that needs to be permanently retired alongside artisanal sourdough starters. Cornelia crashing into Josefien—the obligatory damsel in distress serving as a governess—is pure algorithmic pandering. It's high-key mid. But if van Veen leans into the gothic absurdity of half-faerie biology rather than leaning on Hallmark sentimentality, this text might actually possess some bite. If not, we are staring down the barrel of another bloated, terminally online paperback designed purely for BookTok girlies who think reading is a personality trait.
