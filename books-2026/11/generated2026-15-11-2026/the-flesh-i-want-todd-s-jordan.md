**Author:** Todd S. Jordan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Another mid-90s rural coming-of-age horror starring a lonely teenager with daddy issues and a penchant for roadkill. Groundbreaking. We are truly staring into the abyss of derivative indie acquisitions this quarter, and the abyss is yawning back with terminal boredom. Todd S. Jordan’s *The Flesh I Want* leans so heavily on the soggy-adolescent-male-gaze trope that I can smell the cheap cider and adolescent desperation through the pitch packet. 

### The Sussex Pastoral and Other Tired Tropes

Let us discuss the pacing—or rather, the inevitable narrative lag built into a premise where a kid feeds a feral well-creature until it mutates into his dream girl. It is giving me aggressive flashbacks to every mid-tier body horror manuscript that crossed my mahogany desk this fiscal year. But wait. Let us look at the structural mechanics here. Tim finds a blood-soaked lady in a hole, domesticates her with poultry scraps, and slowly crafts his ideal woman via targeted avian and insectoid nutrition. Bio-engineering by grocery delivery. Honestly? It’s giving unhinged simp behavior, but make it Cronenberg. 

The market is entirely oversaturated with this exact brand of literary visceral gross-out. We already survived the Caroline Kepnes copycats and the endless wave of feminist monster reboots. Now we are enduring the pathetic teenage boy's iteration of Pygmalion, featuring split spines and feathered necks. Groundbreaking stuff, Todd. Truly capturing the sophisticated nuance of male obsession by having the protagonist literalize his internal rot onto an unconsenting cryptid. 

### The Art of the Gimmick

And yet. The structural audacity of using a literal diet plan to sculpt a human prisoner into a customized fantasy object? That is some Grade-A psychological depravity. I will hand the author that. It pushes past mere shock value into a genuinely grotesque exploration of entitlement. When the power dynamic flips and the captive demands a human sacrifice whose identity she can consume—chef's kiss. That is the exact moment the narrative needs to stop dragging its feet through the Sussex mud and actually commit to the bit. If Jordan fumbles that third-act pivot and cops out with some tired metaphor about maternal abandonment and daddy's winery? I am going to throw my espresso cup through the floor-to-ceiling windows. 

Compare this to Monika Kim or Lucy Rose, and you instantly see the structural deficit. Those writers understand atmosphere as an intellectual weapon. Jordan seems content relying on shock-value biological body horror to mask what is essentially a very old, very tired story about horny teenagers making terrible lifestyle choices in the countryside. 

Drop the manuscript from the spring catalog immediately. Also, security needs to escort out the ghostwriter I hired last Tuesday because he completely misinterpreted my creative vision for the quarterly acquisitions memo and tried to pitch me a lifestyle blog about sourdough baking.
