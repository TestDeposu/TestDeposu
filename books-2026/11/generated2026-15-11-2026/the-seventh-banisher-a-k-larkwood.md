**Author:** A.K. Larkwood  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Flour is currently dusting every single vertical surface in my house because the seven-year-old insisted on cracking the eggs for the lemon blueberry muffins while standing on a wobbly barstool, and honestly, if I have to read one more cozy procedural where a plucky amateur baker solves a village murder with the help of a golden retriever, I am going to throw myself directly into the oven. Give me blood. Give me locked-room carnage on a damp crag. 

A.K. Larkwood is sliding into 2026 with *The Seventh Banisher*, and the publisher's little teaser is doing things to my adrenal glands that should probably require a medical license. 

### A Clockwork Body Count on a Cold Rock

Consider the setup: Great-Aunt Sophia, keeper of the Isles and warden against things that go bump in the dark, leaves behind a wicked little inheritance puzzle. Ten days. Ten nights. A remote island functioning like a meat grinder designed by Agatha Christie and populated entirely by scheming cousins who deserve what's coming to them. 

And almost everyone dies. 

That single word—*almost*—is doing the heavy lifting here. 

### The Aesthetics of Gothic Carnage

We are looking at a masterclass in atmospheric claustrophobia. Larkwood already proved in *The Unspoken Name* that she possesses an utterly ruthless imagination, refusing to hand-hold the reader through her world-building. Now, slap that sensibility into a Gothic mystery box where the ghosts are real and the puzzles are lethal. 

Tamsyn Muir compares it to Seishi Yokomizo. If you have not read the *The Honjin Murders*, go fix your life immediately. It is brutal, logical, and deeply weird. Marrying that strict, clockwork mechanism of classical whodunits to ghoul-haunted fantasy is a genius stroke. Yet the real psychological horror here stems from family dynamics. Because nothing brings out the absolute worst in people quite like a dead billionaire relative and a contested will. Add eldritch horrors to the mix? Chef's kiss. 

### Why the Wait Is Criminal

My coffee is stone cold. The kids are currently smearing blue-raspberry batter onto the rescue cat. But do I care? No. I care exclusively about who survives this wretched island and what that final witness has to say about the bodies piling up in the fog. 

Anyway, I have exactly four minutes to pack three school lunches that will inevitably return home untouched because the crusts were square instead of triangular.
