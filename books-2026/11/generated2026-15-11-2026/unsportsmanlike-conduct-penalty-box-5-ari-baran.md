**Author:** Ari Baran  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Tears are currently spotting my vintage linen blouse because the sheer emotional vulnerability promised by *Unsportsmanlike Conduct* is simply too much for my fragile heart to bear before noon. Rachel Reid already ruined my life in the best possible way with *Heated Rivalry*, and now Ari Baran dares to threaten my tear ducts with a grumpy-meets-sunshine—or rather, anxious-spreadsheets-meets-chaos-gremlin—dynamic that feels tailor-made to destroy me. 

Leo Cohen is an undersized defenseman carrying the crushing weight of a dirtbag reputation, which immediately triggers my protective instincts. I want to wrap him in a weighted blanket. And Devon Fraser? The pristine, tightly wound Canadian golden boy who is quietly unraveling under the immense pressure to be flawless? Stop it. Just stop it. Why must fictional men suffer so exquisitely for our collective entertainment? It hurts to watch people who are terrified of vulnerability stumble blindly toward one another. 

My cat just knocked over my artisanal oat milk latte onto a pristine stack of manuscripts, which is frankly a poetic metaphor for my entire emotional state right now. Honestly, publishing houses should pay for my dry cleaning every time they decide to drop an MM hockey romance featuring forced proximity and high-heat tension. Remember when reading used to be relaxing? Me neither. Back to Leo and Devon. 

### The Architecture of Desperation

Forced proximity in a shared living space is the oldest trick in the book, yet I fall for it every single time like a foolish romantic with zero self-preservation skills. Baran understands that real intimacy isn't found in grand gestures. It is found in the slow, agonizing erosion of walls built by years of professional pressure and public scrutiny. When animosity curdles into sudden, terrifying passion, the stakes stop being about winning a championship. The stakes become terrifyingly human. They have to risk losing their hearts, which is objectively much worse than a torn ACL. 

Sarina Bowen called this the best pairing Baran has ever written, and for once, I am inclined to trust an author blurb without getting cynical. The blueprint of dislike-to-lovers demands a delicate balance of snark and yearning. If Devon is hiding his frayed edges behind a polished corporate smile, and Leo is leaning entirely into his chaotic outsider persona just to survive, their eventual collision will be catastrophic. I need them to heal each other. I also need them to stop being stubborn and just communicate like adults, but nobody reads romance for healthy boundary-setting, do they? We read it for the sweet, agonizing ache of two broken people realizing they are safe.

Anyway, I have exactly four minutes to scrape together enough composure to pick up my niece from piano practice before her teacher starts sending passive-aggressive text messages.
