**Author:** Stacy Willingham  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Some sweating guy in a puffer vest just coughed directly onto my neck while the Q train screeched over the Manhattan bridge and I am genuinely questioning every life choice that led to me reading press releases on my phone instead of marrying rich. Stacey Willingham is back with *Keep Me Like a Secret* because apparently the publishing industrial complex decided we haven't suffered enough iterations of the traumatized Southern woman gaslighting herself in a sleepy hamlet where nothing ever happens except ritualistic kidnapping. God forbid an author set a thriller in Queens or industrial New Jersey where people actually know how to mind their business. Instead we are back in Fairhope, Alabama—because syllables like magnolia and humidity apparently do heavy lifting for lazy pacing. 

### The Nostalgia Industrial Complex

The premise leans hard into the 1999 millennial panic aesthetic because marketing departments realized they can milk Gen X childhood trauma until the cows come home. Four girls, one survivor, a wrongly convicted patsy—or maybe not a patsy because twenty-six years later the internet sleuths and legal technicalities demand a narrative pivot where the final girl is actually a bad person. Groundbreaking. Truly a hermeneutic paradigm shift in the tired genre of dead girl lit. Willingham is trading on the exact same suburban paranoia that made Gillian Flynn rich, except with less acid and more humidity. The thematic crux here relies entirely on epistemological decay—how memory curdles into fiction when the local sheriff's department shares a brain cell with the local bait shop. 

### Gaslighting as a Literary Device

We get the classic unreliable protagonist sliding into full psychological disintegration because another kid vanishes and suddenly she doubts her own decade-old testimony. It is giving massive structural exhaustion. We are expected to buy into this epistemic vertigo where the heroine questions whether she hallucinated her entire adolescent trauma. But honestly? The prose will probably read like a first draft churned out in a Starbucks by someone whose only exposure to the rural South is an episode of *True Detective* watched on mute. Expect endless paragraphs about cicadas buzzing and porch swings creaking—sensory filler designed to pad the word count until the inevitable mid-book twist where the killer turns out to be the local mayor's cousin who was mowing the lawn the whole time. 

Anyway the train is violently decelerating at Canal Street and some teenager is blasting drill rap out of a busted JBL speaker without a shred of self-awareness. I have to scrounge for quarters to do laundry down the street.
