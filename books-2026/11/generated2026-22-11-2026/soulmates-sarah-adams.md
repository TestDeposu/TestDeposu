**Author:** Sarah Adams  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

The synopsis for *Soulmates* is so slickly packaged it might as well be a gourmet meal served in a museum—elegant, but you’re already wondering if the ingredients are just reheated leftovers from *The Love Hypothesis*’s lab. Birdie Wilson’s journey from Seattle to San Francisco feels like a plot device plucked from a travelogue, not a character’s arc. The idea of fate dropping a ceramicist into the lap of a tech billionaire’s son is so *on-the-nose* it borders on the absurd—like if *The Hating Game*’s enemies-to-lovers had been replaced with a man who owns more yachts than a Kardashian. But here’s the thing: Sarah Adams has done this before, and we’re not here to question the magic, just the execution.

The synopsis leans *heavily* into the "serendipity as a plot crutch" trope, which is fine if you’re writing a rom-com where the stakes are as low as a bad Netflix date. Birdie’s "leave my number in a tip jar" gambit is the kind of move that makes you wonder if Adams is channeling *Bridgerton*’s scriptwriters on a caffeine bender—it’s cute, it’s clever, but it’s also the kind of thing that makes you suspect the author hasn’t actually had to *earn* a connection in real life. And then there’s the inheritance condition: forcing Birdie to reconcile with her stepsister for money? That’s not just a plot device, that’s a *plot crutch with a side of moral ambiguity*—unless, of course, you’re writing a book where the only thing more important than love is *ceramic studio funding*.

The real question is whether Adams can make this feel anything other than a rehash of *The Unhoneymooners*’ "fake dating" energy, but with more billionaires and less actual conflict. The synopsis doesn’t even *hint* at what makes Parker Voss more than a walking conflict-of-interest—is he charming? Ruthless? A secret poet? The way the synopsis describes him, he could be a mannequin in a Versace store, and we’d still be sold. And Danielle? She’s the kind of stepsister who exists solely to make Birdie’s life harder, like a plot device with a side of passive-aggressive energy. Where’s the depth? Where’s the *humanity*? This feels like a book that could’ve been titled *Fate’s Billionaire Bait* and still sold just as many copies.

But then again, maybe that’s the point. Maybe Adams is going for *The Notebook* meets *Silicon Valley*—a love story where the only thing more expensive than the ring is the therapy bill. The synopsis doesn’t give us enough to judge, but it does give us enough to *anticipate*—and anticipation, as we all know, is the spice of the romance genre. Still, I can’t help but think about how *Bridgerton*’s first season managed to make even its most ridiculous plot twists feel like they belonged in a Jane Austen novel. Can Adams do the same with a billionaire’s son and a ceramicist’s inheritance? Only time—and a very well-crafted manuscript—will tell.
