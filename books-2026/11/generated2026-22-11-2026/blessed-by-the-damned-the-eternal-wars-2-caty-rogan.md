**Author:** Caty Rogan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Another grey Tuesday afternoon dripping against the glass, and here I am, staring down the marketing copy for *Blessed by the Damned*—the sophomore effort by Caty Rogan—wondering why we keep handing the keys of our fictional institutions back to the very hierarchies that built their foundations on exploited labor and crushed bones. The synopsis practically vibrates with that exhausting YA exhaustion, shouting keywords like "feminine rage" and "spicy romance" as if those two things cancel out the fact that Leina, our beleaguered protagonist wielding her magical weapon and her winged war-horse, is still somehow expected to play corporate custodian for the ruling class that fractured her entire bloodline, which honestly feels a lot like watching local city council members try to slap a fresh coat of community-garden paint over a toxic waste dump and calling it grassroots activism, completely ignoring the structural rot underneath while expecting everyone to just clap and smile through the asbestos exposure. 

Exhausting.

And honestly, speaking of institutional decay, I spent three hours this morning arguing with the zoning board about why the community compost bin shouldn't be placed directly adjacent to the little league dugout—not that anyone cares about civic hygiene when they can just slap a trendy label on a disaster and sell it back to us at a markup, much like how Sarah J. Maas built an entire real estate empire out of fae courts that fundamentally operate like hyper-gentrified HOA boards with better swords. 

Yet we keep buying the hype. 

We watch these characters stumble into forbidden romance and "found family" tropes like corporate retreats disguised as rebellion, as if checking off boxes for "he falls first" and "she saves him" excuses a total lack of actual systemic critique. 

Do better. 

The structural mechanics hinted at in the teaser for *Blessed by the Damned*—this vague promise to "unmake it completely"—suggests a flicker of actual revolutionary grit beneath the tropes, even if I am deeply skeptical that a mass-market sequel published in 2026 will have the teeth to truly dismantle its own setting instead of just swapping out the tyrant at the top and calling it a day, leaving us right back where we started in the dirt. 

Whatever. 

I really need to wipe down the folding tables and find twelve juice boxes before the neighborhood playdate mob arrives in twenty minutes, and I am entirely unprepared.
