**Author:** Becky Mandelbaum  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

The rain is drumming against the glass again, a relentless, rhythmic tapping that sounds suspiciously like a creditor at the door. I suppose that is the perfect atmospheric backdrop for *Homewrecker*, a book that seems to thrive on the specific, suffocating variety of misery found in small-town Kansas and the performative perfection of the Pacific Northwest. Becky Mandelbaum is aiming for the throat with this one, dragging us through the wreckage of a life left behind to chase a ghost that probably never deserved the air it consumed. Why do we insist on returning to the scenes of our own undoing? It is the literary equivalent of touching the stove because you’ve forgotten what the burn felt like.

Piper, our protagonist—if we can call someone so clearly prone to self-sabotage that—is supposedly a budding artist, which is code for someone with enough talent to recognize her own failure but not enough grit to finish a children’s book. It is a tired trope, really, the "starving artist in a dead brother’s bedroom." The synopsis leans heavily into this, perhaps a bit too heavily. It suggests a haunting that acts as a metaphor for suppressed guilt, which is a neat little trick of genre-blending, though I suspect the real ghost here is the sheer, agonizing weight of comparison. Living in a house covered in someone else's murals while playing third wheel to a successful ex and his pristine fiancée? It sounds less like a ghost story and more like a slow-motion psychological demolition. I initially thought this might be a fresh take on the suburban gothic, something akin to the sharp, jagged edges of Ottessa Moshfegh or the suffocating domestic dread found in the works of Maria Semple, but the more I read this premise, the more I fear it might settle for being a quirky, middle-brow romp through middle-class ennui instead. Actually, looking closer at the promise of an "otherworldly plan," I suspect it might surprise me. It might be quite vicious.

Disaster.

The interplay of jealousy and ambition is where this will live or die. If Mandelbaum handles the envy properly—that specific, biting realization that your ex has moved into a better life while you have been busy collecting dust in Vinland—then we have a winner. If she softens the edges to make Piper "relatable," the entire thing collapses into a puddle of saccharine sentiment. We don't need more protagonists who make bad decisions for the sake of reader sympathy. We need people who burn their own houses down because they’re bored. The house itself, with its restless spirit and century-old bones, needs to be a character, not just a set piece. It needs to be as spiteful as the people living inside it.

The cost of orthodontia is ruinous, and the sheer price of these metal contraptions currently stuck in my children’s mouths is giving me a massive headache.
