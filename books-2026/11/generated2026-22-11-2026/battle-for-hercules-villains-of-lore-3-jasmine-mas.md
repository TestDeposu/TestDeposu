**Author:** Jasmine Mas  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Hercules is back, and she is spoiling for a fight. If the synopsis for *Battle for Hercules* is any indication, Jasmine Mas intends to lean even harder into the chaotic, high-stakes misery that made the previous installments absolute gold for anyone who likes their romance served with a side of psychological warfare. I’m already bracing myself for the exhaustion that comes with watching these characters refuse to communicate like functional adults. It’s glorious.

### The Audacity of Proximity

Forced proximity is a trope so tired it should have been put to sleep years ago, yet here I am, practically foaming at the mouth to see how Mas weaponizes it again. The synopsis screams that Medusa is being stalked by bodyguards she loathes—a dynamic that feels like a sharper, more jagged iteration of the tension found in books like K.A. Knight’s *Den of Vipers*. There is something profoundly irritating about the "my husbands are stalking me" line, though. It’s possessive, it’s arguably absurd, and it’s exactly why I’ll be pre-ordering this. You want the drama? You get the drama.

I spent the better part of this morning staring at a box of off-brand glitter pens that my neighbor insisted were "non-toxic" before they stained my mahogany table a violent shade of magenta. Honestly, it’s just like the publishing industry lately—everything is shiny, suspiciously cheap, and destined to leave a permanent mark on the furniture of my mind. Why do we insist on buying things that clearly promise a mess? Why does the blue ink not even work? It’s a tragedy, really, and it makes me appreciate that at least *Battle for Hercules* is upfront about the carnage it plans to inflict.

### The Olympian Cage

The academic setting feels like a fever dream. Olympians at university? It’s basically Greek mythology’s answer to the *Fourth Wing* frenzy, minus the dragons being the primary focus. Or maybe plus them—who can tell with this genre anymore? The synopsis mentions Titans multiplying and Zeus mobilizing, which implies the stakes are shifting from interpersonal bickering to actual structural collapse. Good. If Medusa is going to survive the psychological meat grinder of this series, she needs to stop worrying about her bodyguards and start worrying about the celestial debris hitting the fan.

I expect this book to be a total bloodbath of emotional unavailability and sharp, biting insults. Mas doesn’t write comfort reads; she writes the kind of books that make you question your own taste in men—or gods, in this case. If the “who did this to you” trope isn’t executed with the perfect amount of unhinged intensity, I will be writing a very pointed letter to the void. The bar is set to the floor, and I’m ready to watch them trip over it with style.

My daughter’s science project is currently on fire.
