The mist is still clinging to the lower pasture fence, dripping off old cedar posts while the red clover bends low beneath the dew, forcing me to pause between my morning chores and wonder how an entire literary season got so thoroughly hijacked by noise. Ellis Hunter’s upcoming 2026 release, *Soul Torn*, leans hard into the exhausted tropes of modern myth-making, weaponizing the American flag against itself in a war-torn theater of narrative stagnation. 

### Rotting Banners and Rust

The synopsis promises an abomination clad in stars and stripes—a tired allegory for institutional corruption that feels less like biting satire and more like a tired episode of prestige television stretched thin over three hundred pages of gray-scale carnage. And perhaps that is unfair. Wait, let me rephrase that entirely. Maybe the physical manifestation of a nation's moral decay wearing a patriotic standard is precisely the visceral shock the genre needs right now, cutting through the sanitized aesthetic of modern blockbuster fiction with the jagged edge of McCarthy’s *Blood Meridian* or the heavy, inescapable weight of Claire Vaye Watkins' ecological grief.

Or maybe it is just another loud exercise in brand maintenance disguised as literature.

We are promised ghosts from the past, shared shames, and a cast of costumed soldiers standing shoulder-to-shoulder against threats they cannot handle alone. Groundbreaking. Nothing screams ecological stewardship of the imagination quite like recycling the same corporate intellectual property until the soil is completely stripped of nutrients. Writers like Hunter should be cultivating new wild spaces in speculative fiction rather than replanting monoculture crops that choke out local flora. 

Monoculture.

The teenager just backed the John Deere into the mailbox and I need to go assess the structural integrity of the cedar post before the morning frost sets in.
