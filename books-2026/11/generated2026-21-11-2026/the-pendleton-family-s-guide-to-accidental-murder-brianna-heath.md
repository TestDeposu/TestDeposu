**Author:** Brianna Heath  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Another domestic farce masquerading as high-society intrigue lands upon my mahogany desk, reeking of desperate commercialism. Brianna Heath attempts a grand aristocratic dynasty in *The Pendleton Family's Guide to Accidental Murder*, yet she populates her fictional estate not with true blue-bloods, but with a thoroughly common assortment of thieves, grifters, and low-born charlatans. Imagine my sheer, unadulterated shock when yet another modern author tries to replicate the brisk, lethal parlor games of Benjamin Stevenson or *How to Solve Your Own Murder*, while bringing absolutely zero pedigree to the table. Utter trash, frankly. 

### The Pedigree Pretension

One must inspect the structural anatomy of this upcoming 2026 catastrophe. We are told—with breathless, marketing-department desperation—that this dysfunctional clan adheres to three rigid house rules. No secrets. No harming kin. No felonies upon ancestral soil. But what absolute hogwash. Any genuine patron of the arts knows that real generational wealth is built on plausible deniability and burying your dirty laundry beneath three feet of reinforced concrete, not writing up tedious spreadsheets for a nonagenarian's jubilee. Emmeline Pendleton fancies herself some sort of logistical mastermind because she owns a Microsoft Excel document. Please. The girl is giving total mid-tier corporate HR manager energy, attempting to coordinate a family of cutthroats with color-coded charts. 

### A Vulgar Surplus of Corpses

And then, naturally, a fresh corpse crashes the festivities like an uninvited hobo at Ascot. How dreadfully pedestrian. 

The synopsis leans entirely on the tired, overcooked trope of the locked-room whodunnit, devoid of any genuine psychological depth or stylistic sophistication. Everyone is a suspect. Everyone has a secret. Groundbreaking narrative architecture, truly. One can practically smell the cheap paper and the editorial compromises bleeding through the promotional copy. Heath promises a spectacular, fiery finale that will tear the clan apart, but I suspect it will read less like a Greek tragedy and more like a messy, unhinged TikTok feud set to instrumental strings. It is deeply offensive to those of us who appreciate the subtle art of quiet, devastating betrayal rather than lowbrow slapstick criminality. 

My personal chef had the audacity to use Chinese preserved truffles instead of fresh Périgord specimens in last night's risotto, and honestly, reading this promotional drivel elicited the exact same bitter disappointment.
