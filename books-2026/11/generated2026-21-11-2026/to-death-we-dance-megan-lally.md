**Author:** Megan Lally  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Stained lace and provincial morbidity—how terribly quaint. Megan Lally dares to tread the damp corridors of fallen aristocracy in *To Death, We Dance*, a forthcoming exercise in tropes that smells distinctly of stale violet water and cheap tallow candles. One imagines the manuscript smells the same. Society scandals and crumbling country manors? Truly, we are plumbed to the absolute depths of the architectural landfill. It reads like the plot outline fished straight from the wastepaper basket of the *Lord Lucan* disappearance estate files. 

### The Architecture of Decay

Maeva Whitney flees the metropolis because of a petty societal flutter. How fragile. She retreats to Rockwell Manor, a domicile that possesses eyes. Of course it does. Every mediocre Gothic revival since the sad demise of good taste requires ocular architecture. Lally relies heavily on the stale furniture of our literary past—drafty hallways, silent watchers, and the ominous proximity of neighborly sadism. The Holloways loom across town, hosting a bicentennial mourning festival called The Death Days. Eleven days of morbid pageantry. Because ten days simply lack the requisite dramatic inflation. 

And then, naturally, we acquire a brooding accomplice.

### The Anatomy of a Convenient Paramour

Beau. A name that tastes like powdered sugar and bitter disappointment. He lives under the very roof of the predatory Holloways, harboring secrets that are presumably as thin as cheap cambric. They share stolen glances. They whisper about bloodstained histories while the grandfather clock ticks with portentous melodrama. One waits for the inevitable swoon against a crumbling library shelf. Will Lally grant us a single line of genuinely intoxicating verse amidst this parade of predictable peril? Doubtful. The synopsis itself stutters with the breathless desperation of a debutante at her third dismal cotillion. 

### The Inevitable Sacrifice

Maeva must uncover the scheme before becoming the centerpiece of the local carnage. How taxing for her. The thematic weight here hinges entirely on survival against ancestral gluttony. Yet, the synopsis offers zero stylistic guarantees. No rhythmic cadence. No venomous wit. Just a flat recitation of peril wrapped in velvet ribbons and dark promises. We are told the dead are never truly gone. One wishes the originality of the plot would take a more permanent leave. 

I must terminate the employment of my current ghostwriter immediately; the wretched creature completely misunderstood my explicit creative vision for this season's salon critique.
