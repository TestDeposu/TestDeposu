**Author:** Carley Fortune  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Another algorithmic casualty hits the presses for 2026, and I am already exhausted. We are staring down the barrel of yet another commercial product disguised as fiction, courtesy of the assembly line that birthed *Happy Place*. Barry’s Bay awaits us once more in *Only in Summer*, and frankly, the predictability of it all makes me want to throw my espresso through the triple-paned glass of this ridiculously overpriced European import. 

### The Commodification of Nostalgia

The premise is aggressively basic. Eleanor Florek—what a painfully curated name for a local reporter—saves up for an A-frame shack just so she can escape her gossiping aunts. Groundbreaking existential dread. Enter Owen Kolstad. A "Viking in chinos." I read that phrase and literally gagged on my matcha. This man has no inner life. He is merely an empty vessel designed to trigger pheromones in bored suburbanites who bought second homes during the pandemic and now pretend to understand Thoreau while doom-scrolling on their iPads. 

### The Epistemology of the Flung Chalet

Carley Fortune relies entirely on the lazy architecture of forced proximity. The plot is so transparently algorithmic it gives me brain-rot. Two emotionally stunted capitalists retreat to nature—because nature is apparently just a backdrop for transactional intimacy—and refuse to fall in love, right up until they inevitably fall in love. It is pure capitalist realism dressed up in pastel paperback covers. Where is the friction? Where is the genuine ontological terror of mortality? Instead, we get a billionaire developer or whatever vague corporate sin Owen is hiding, paired with a woman whose deepest trauma is having too many aunts. 

We are recycling the same tired tropes ad nauseam because contemporary readership has the attention span of a goldfish on ketamine. The prose in these synopses reads like a marketing brief. "A connection like theirs is impossible to ignore." Please. Spare me the psycho-babble. 

Wait. Why is my back freezing? I just touched the dashboard control and realized the heated seats in this stupid European SUV are completely malfunctioning, and honestly, freezing my ass off in November while contemplating the industrialization of romance literature is an absolute tragedy.
