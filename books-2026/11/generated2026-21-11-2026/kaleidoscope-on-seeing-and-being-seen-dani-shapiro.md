It is midnight, my eyes burn with a quiet, bone-deep exhaustion, and yet here I am, staring at a publisher's pitch for Dani Shapiro's *Kaleidoscope: On Seeing and Being Seen*. 

### The Geography of Avoidance

Let us address the immediate absurdity of this premise. We are expected to believe that a private investigator named Jacob Decker—described with the breathless marketing cadence of a supermarket romance as a "tall, cool drink of perfection"—somehow ends up in Gnaw Bone, Colorado, wrestling with a dark past and an elusive brunette named Emme. Good grief. It is the kind of sanitized, manufactured wilderness that smells entirely of air freshener rather than wet topsoil, rotting pine needles, and the bitter copper tang of well water. 

And why must every emotional reckoning occur against a backdrop of manufactured isolation? Gnaw Bone sounds less like a real place on a map and more like a fever dream hatched in a Manhattan high-rise by someone who thinks dirt only exists in potted plants. Real soil gets under your fingernails and stays there for days. It stains your clothes and rots your fence posts if you do not stay vigilant.

Instead, we get Emme sabotaging her own happiness because of a vague, ominous "dark chapter."
