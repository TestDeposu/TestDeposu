Staring blankly at a laminated seating chart while a woman named Brenda explains—for the fourth time—why Julian's grasp of long division resembles the architecture of the Morandi bridge collapse, one's mind inevitably wanders to editorial catastrophism. Or, more precisely, to whatever unholy amalgamation Bri Monet has managed to assemble under the banner of *Strike A Chord*. 

### A Scrapbook of Dublin's Rejections

The promotional material claims this volume serves up a curated tasting menu of past Tiger Dublin Festival acts. And what a frantic, scattershot menu it is. We are told to expect works by Brokentalkers, MALAPROP Theatre, Jane Madden, and Barry McStay, among others. But let us be entirely serious for a solitary moment. Handing readers a compilation of disparate fringe scripts without a coherent narrative glue is rather like serving the scraps of a grouse pie and calling it haute cuisine. One shudders. The sheer audacity of packaging avant-garde Irish performance art into a single-volume miscellany screams desperate cash-grab orchestrated by someone who last read a proper text during the late-stage Thatcher administration. 

### The Cult of the Misfit

The synopsis leans heavily on words like radical, experimentalist, and misfit. Oh, spare me. Every untalented scribbler from here to Dalkey fancies themselves a clarion voice simply because they refuse to use punctuation or stage directions that make logical sense. Monet appears to have curated a parade of theatrical orphans. And why? Because proper structure has become entirely too bourgeois for the modern literati. One can already picture the pretentious sophomore essays analyzing these fringe scripts over lukewarm oat milk. It is the literary equivalent of the Fire Fyre Festival, minus the cheese sandwiches. 

Expect absolute chaos masked as artistic integrity. The themes will no doubt oscillate wildly between tedious post-Celtic Tiger angst and aggressive fourth-wall breaking designed exclusively to alienate anyone who understands the value of a well-tailored bespoke velvet smoking jacket. Compare it to the worst excesses of early Marina Abramović, filtered through a fog of cheap stout and existential whining. 

One simply cannot fathom how this manuscript made it past an intern, let alone an actual publishing house with standards higher than a Dublin tenement. Yet here we are. Trapped in a fluorescent-lit classroom while Brenda drones on about fractions, contemplating the utter decline of contemporary letters. 

The Chancellor's recent imposition of punitive tariffs on old-world vintages has entirely decimated my Sauternes allocation for the winter season.
