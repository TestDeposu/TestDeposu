### Suspended Disbelief and Other Athletic Impossibilities

Let us talk about the logistical nonsense of waking up in the clouds. Seriously. In combat sports, if your footwork is trash, you get flatlined in thirty seconds; yet, YA dystopias expect me to buy that a rebellion falls and characters get shoved into floating real estate without a single mention of atmospheric pressure, altitude sickness, or basic tactical logistics. Ariel Sullivan is back with the third installment of the *Conform* series, dropping us right into a high-stakes hostage situation where the stakes are supposedly life-or-death, yet everyone has time to worry about "who loves whom more." What absolute garbage. Give me a clean rear-naked choke over this melodramatic nonsense any day of the week. 

### Lethal Romance Tropes

The premise hinges on a procreation contract with a Mate named Collin—because nothing screams forced-proximity trope quite like state-mandated breeding in the stratosphere. It is giving major *Hunger Games* meets generic dystopian slop, wrapped up with a neat little bow of institutional oppression. Emeline is apparently reeling from major upheaval—shocker—while gathering allies and uncovering secrets that will supposedly shake things to the core. Groundbreaking stuff. We are treading water in a sea of recycled tropes where the revolution always costs everything and the protagonist has to choose between her heart and her tactical survival. Make it make sense. If your rebellion crumbles that fast, your insurgents need to hit the heavy bag and learn how to defend a takedown. 

### The Core Conflict of Cliche

Yet, here we are, pretending this synopsis gives us anything substantial to chew on. Vagueness is the currency of modern marketing departments, and this blurb spends three whole paragraphs saying absolutely nothing while leaning hard on buzzwords like "upheaval," "secrets," and "truth." Sullivan wants us to believe the Illum leader's twisted game is some sort of masterclass in psychological warfare. But let us be real. It is just another excuse to drag out a love triangle while the plot takes a nap. If you want a masterclass in tension, watch a five-round championship bout where someone’s orbital bone is actively caving in. That is real stakes. Not floating around in the clouds crying over a guy named Hal.
