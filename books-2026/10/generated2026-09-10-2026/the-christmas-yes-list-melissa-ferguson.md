**Author:** Melissa Ferguson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-06 APL

The synopsis of *The Christmas Yes List* is a glittering, if slightly *too* on-brand, invitation into the kind of rom-com that exists solely to make you question whether you’ve been duped by the algorithm’s endless scroll of "feel-good" promises. Chloe Hollis’s mission—collecting ten rejections before Christmas—is the kind of self-destructive, self-help-adjacent premise that would make a *Fleabag* character roll her eyes while sipping Earl Grey. But here’s the thing: the real villain isn’t the "ridiculous favors" she’s asking strangers for (which, let’s be honest, is the kind of plot device that would’ve been *brutal* in a *Bridgerton* adaptation). No, the villain is the way this story *could* be written—like a Hallmark movie that forgot to invite the edge.

James, the charming out-of-towner who says yes to everything, is the perfect foil for Chloe’s rigid "no" campaign. But his backstory—whatever it is—isn’t just a plot convenience; it’s a *temptation*. The synopsis hints at a collision of wills, a festive mosh pit of holiday chaos and undeniable chemistry, which is to say: this is *The Holiday* meets *Crazy Rich Asians* if *Crazy Rich Asians* had a side hustle in karaoke. The problem isn’t that the premise is lazy—it’s that it’s *too* lazy. Where’s the bite? Where’s the *subversion*? Because right now, this reads like a love letter to the kind of rom-com that exists to make you feel warm and fuzzy while also making you wonder if you’ve been gaslit into thinking "spontaneity" is a virtue.

And yet—here’s where the pivot happens. Maybe the genius of this is that it’s *supposed* to be this way. Maybe the point isn’t to subvert the tropes but to *lean into* them with such deliberate, almost *ironic* precision that it becomes a satire of its own genre. Imagine if the "ridiculous favors" weren’t just for laughs but a metaphor for the performative "yes-ness" of modern dating culture—where saying yes is the only way to be seen, and rejection is the ultimate act of rebellion. That’s the kind of depth that could make this more than just another *Book of Love* knockoff. But the synopsis doesn’t give us that. It gives us *The Love Hypothesis* meets *A Christmas Story* if *A Christmas Story* had a TikTok account.

The real question is: will this be the kind of book that makes you laugh, or the kind that makes you *uncomfortable*? Because right now, it’s just the kind that makes you *wait*. And that’s the most dangerous kind of anticipation—when you’re not sure if you’re being sold a story or a lifestyle.
