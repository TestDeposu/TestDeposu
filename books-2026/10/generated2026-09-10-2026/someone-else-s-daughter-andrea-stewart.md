**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Another glossy manuscript lands on the mahogany, and I can already smell the desperation lingering on the margins. Andrea Stewart wants us to tremble at the horrors of the digital Panopticon in *Someone Else's Daughter*. Yet, the synopsis reads like a panic-stricken PR brief drafted by a terrified junior publicist during a midday panic attack at SoHo House. How derivative. We are subjected to the tired trope of the estranged child crawling back to the family nest because of a missing sibling—oh, groundbreaking. This is the literary equivalent of the Fyre Festival aftermath played on an endless, whining loop.

### The Monetized Womb and Other Exhausting Clichés

The premise attempts to skewer the grotesque reality of modern influencer culture—selling your first period for brand sponsorships and algorithm optimization. But let us be entirely honest here. This ground has been plowed to dust by every millennial thriller writer with a laptop and a grudge against TikTok. The pacing promised by this blurb threatens to drag like the interminable second half of the Theranos trial. 

Ellie spots "disturbing details" in a video. Naturally. Because no thriller protagonist can simply call the police; they must independently investigate while brooding over childhood trauma involving a ring light. 

### The Illusion of Depth

The themes here are about as subtle as a brick thrown through a glass-fronted boutique in Mayfair. Commodification of youth. The decay of the domestic sphere under the harsh glare of the ring light. Yawning chasms of predictability. Stewart leans heavily on the coattails of *Listen for the Lie* and *The Last to Vanish*, hoping readers won't notice the recycled scaffolding holding this entire narrative edifice upright. And what of the missing family friend? A convenient narrative prop designed to accelerate tension where character development should rightfully live. 

Expect zero psychological nuance. Expect paper-thin villains who monetize their children between sips of sponsored green juice. Expect twists you can spot from a commercial flight over the Atlantic. 

My vintage champagne collection needs to be moved to the new cellar before the humidity ruins the 1996 Krug.
