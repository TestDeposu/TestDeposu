Concrete corridors echo with folding metal chairs, and somebody parked their cargo bike directly across the ramp, trapping the entire subcommittee inside the rec center until the owner returns with the key. We are supposed to be discussing neighborhood zoning variances, but my mind is hijacked by the corporate publishing machine yet again. 

Hannah Grace has decided that hockey rinks are simply too pedestrian for the modern romance demographic. *Champagne Problems* drags us out of the chilled arena and straight into the carbon-fiber exhaust of Formula 1. 

### The High-Octane Boredom of Elite Exhaustion

Glitz. Scandal. Fast cars. The holy trinity of contemporary marketing copy that actually means absolutely nothing. Ivy Miller has a rule—because every heroine in the twenty-first century is contractually obligated to possess a rigid personal mandate that crumbles the second a brooding athlete with expensive stubble enters a five-mile radius. Saint Thompson lost his championship to scandal. Oh, the humanity. The synopsis refuses to tell us what this scandalous event actually was, leaving us to assume he either committed securities fraud or failed to tip a barista. 

And why does every fictional racing driver possess the exact same hyper-fixated, alpha-male monomania? "Second place is never an option." Groundbreaking stuff. Truly revolutionary character architecture. I am practically vibrating with apathy. 

Speaking of aggressive pacing and unwarranted authority figures, my eight-year-old neighbor's obsession with *Paw Patrol* has reached a militant phase where Marshall the firefighting dalmatian is somehow dictating our municipal recycling schedules, and honestly, that animated puppy has a firmer grasp on logistical infrastructure than whoever wrote this publisher's blurb. 

Saint wants the date they never had. The tension is supposed to be exquisite. Instead, it reads like a carbon copy of every sports romance currently clogging up Target tables alongside Emily Henry knockoffs and dragons who hoard paperbacks. Casual sex never stays casual. What a shocking narrative trajectory. I predict a third-act misunderstanding involving a private jet and a hastily deleted Instagram post by page three hundred. 

The school run takes precedence over your editor's demands.
