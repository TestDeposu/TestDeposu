**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

The Scottish Highlands have always harbored a stubborn kind of romance, the sort that smells damply of peat and unrequited longing. *By Breath* leans entirely into this atmospheric weight, dragging us back to the mist-draped shores of Loch Chon with a premise that feels both comfortingly familiar and aggressively modern. P.C. Cast knows how to build a world where teenage alienation meets ancient, predatory myth. Yet, I am secretly exhausted by the mandatory inclusion of rival princes. Can a girl sketch landscapes and save a drowning water dragon without two royal men immediately turning her emotional real estate into a contested parliamentary seat? 

### The Currency of Ancient Debt

Desire in these stories always functions as a transaction. Maeve arrives seeking sanctuary in her great-aunt’s stables, armed with paintbrushes and an innocent love for trail horses, completely unaware that the local ecosystem operates on brutal fae politics. The synopsis promises a bond between human and royal kelpie that could shatter reality itself. But let us be entirely honest for a second. Actually, scrap that thought—KELPIES ARE TERRIFYING. Why are we romanticizing creatures that historically lure children into watery graves so they can devour their livers? Horse ears and silver-belled manes are a fantastic marketing strategy for the Unseelie court, but folklore used to have teeth. I sincerely hope Cast remembers the carnivorous nature of these beasts instead of giving us just another misunderstood, chiseled brooding stallion who enjoys long walks on the beach. 

Speaking of misunderstood brooding men, remember when fantasy literature allowed us to read a trilogy without a luxury resort developer showing up to ruin the natural habitat? I am having flashbacks to that wretched eco-thriller I forced myself to finish in July—the one with the sentient hotel pool and the eighty-page chapter on local zoning laws. At least Keir has the decency to be an enigmatic heir with secrets rather than a municipal tax dispute. 

### The Geometry of Blood and Water

What redeems the predictable shape of this love triangle is the quiet terror of accidental power. Maeve discovers magic running through her veins, which is the literary equivalent of finding out your vintage Honda Civic has a jet engine bolted under the hood. It makes no practical sense, yet we accept it because we desperately want to believe our ordinary lives conceal catastrophic significance. Holly Black handles this brand of razor-sharp fae cruelty with unmatched precision, and I pray Cast leans into that sharper edge rather than drowning the narrative in sweet, syrupy romance tropes. We need the cold water of the loch. We need the drowning weight of consequences. 

The sun is dipping below the fence line, and I just remembered I left my entire collection of seventies jazz LPs sitting directly beneath the chronic leak in the downstairs utility closet. If those Japanese pressings of Miles Davis warp because of a burst pipe while I was pondering Scottish water dragons, I am officially retiring from reading forever.
