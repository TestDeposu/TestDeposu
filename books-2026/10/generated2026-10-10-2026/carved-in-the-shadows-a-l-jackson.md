There is a very specific kind of silence that settles into a house after the tiny shoes are finally kicked off by the door, when the frantic demands for juice and lost plastic dinosaurs have melted into the heavy, thrumming dark, leaving you staring at the kitchen wall wondering how your entire identity got hollowed out and replaced by a vending machine for snacks and comfort. A.L. Jackson’s upcoming *Carved in the Shadows* somehow manages to tap directly into that quiet, suffocating terror of guarding the perimeter while your own internal foundation is crumbling away to dust. And honestly, looking at the premise, I am already exhausted. 

Mary patrolling her little Midwestern town while wrestling with recent tragedy feels uncomfortably close to the bone—not because of the supernatural window-knockers or vanishing livestock, but because every single mother reading this knows the exact weight of trying to keep the wolves from the door when you can barely keep your own eyelids open past nine o'clock. 

Speaking of animals disappearing and small-town dread, my toddler has been utterly obsessed with a cartoon about singing blue dogs for the past six months, and if I have to hear that relentlessly cheerful theme song one more time while folding tiny socks that will somehow lose their matches by tomorrow morning, I am going to march right outside and start carving strange symbols into the neighbor's prize-winning petunias myself just to feel something other than the soul-crushing monotony of domestic survival. 

Edna. 

We need to talk about Edna—the one-woman neighborhood watchman—because every community has one, and they are either your absolute salvation or the reason you drink boxed Pinot Grigio at four in the afternoon on a Tuesday. 

Jackson is stepping away from typical contemporary romance here and dipping toes into eerie, unexplainable phenomena, bringing to mind the creeping, atmospheric dread of a Stephen King or a sharper, more exhausted Shirley Jackson. 

Webb. 

Suspicious. 

The synopsis leans heavily on tropes we have seen a thousand times—the haunted law enforcement officer, the ominous stranger with a past, the mysterious carvings that mean trouble—and yet, the psychological undercurrent of maternal protection and lingering grief carries an undeniable pull for anyone whose brain is currently split between analyzing literary subtext and remembering to schedule the dentist appointment.

Will the town ever be the same? Probably not. Nothing ever is once the kids start waking up at five in the morning for no discernible reason. 

I am hiding in the pantry for five minutes of peace.
