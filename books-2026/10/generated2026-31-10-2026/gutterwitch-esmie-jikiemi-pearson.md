**Author:** Esmie Jikiemi-Pearson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Water is currently pooling in the pockets of this dreadful yellow plastic slicker, soaking straight through the cuffs of a sweater that was supposed to survive a simple dash across the school parking lot. The kids are still arguing in the back seat about who stole the last blueberry muffin while I stare down at the synopsis for *Gutterwitch*, wondering if Esmie Jikiemi-Pearson is going to give us another stale spin on the dark academia trope or actually dismantle the machinery. We have seen the impoverished scullery maid trope a thousand times before. Joan finds a fallen star, makes a wish, and suddenly she is trapped in a glittering cage of ancient academia and ruthless politics alongside a brooding faerie bastard named Caspar. It practically drips with the same moody aesthetic that Holly Black or Leigh Bardugo perfected years ago. Yet there is something undeniably delicious about a protagonist whose ambition is stated plainly as wanting to become the greatest witch who ever lived, rather than stumbling into power through reluctant heroism or sheer accident. Give me a girl who actively prays for dominance over circumstance instead of a meek bystander caught in the crossfire. Except the synopsis might be hiding a very predictable romantic triangle behind all those soaring promises of wild magic and impending war. Actually, scratch that entirely, because looking closer at the setup with Belinda the witch-mistress, the real friction here will likely bypass the boys completely and focus entirely on the brutal domestic servitude of magical classism. 

Dust. 

### The Chemistry of Rebellion

Elite universities in speculative fiction always rely on the exact same architecture of exclusion—halls built on snobbery, libraries overflowing with forbidden texts, and mentors who look like Silas, harboring secrets behind benevolent smiles while the world burns outside. And honestly, the October wind is howling against the car windows right now, smelling of wet asphalt and rotting sycamore leaves, which makes this whole setup feel terribly appropriate. Jikiemi-Pearson needs to do something genuinely subversive with the fae-witch dynamic if *Gutterwitch* is going to survive the weight of its own comparisons. We do not need another sanitized romance masquerading as institutional critique. We need blood under the fingernails. We need systemic rot rendered in prose that bites back. If Joan simply learns to weaponize her star-given magic to pass the midterms while stealing glances at Caspar across a gothic courtyard, I am throwing this book directly into the recycling bin. 

Must run now, the city council meeting about the demolition of the art deco theater starts in twenty minutes and I cannot miss it.
