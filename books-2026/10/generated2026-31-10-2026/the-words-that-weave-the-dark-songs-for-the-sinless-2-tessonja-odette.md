**Author:** Tessonja Odette  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

*The Lies That Summon the Night* left a certain ache in my chest that refuses to quiet down, so naturally, finding myself sighing over the promotional material for *The Words That Weave the Dark* feels almost inevitable. Tessonja Odette is back to torment us. And by us, I mean hopeless wrecks who demand that fictional characters communicate their feelings with the desperate clarity of a nineteenth-century love letter before anyone starts ripping each other's clothes off in a lunar-lit palace. 

### The Heavy Burden of Loving a Monster

Look at the premise. Inana is struggling with self-acceptance because she might accidentally murder her beloved. Truly, nothing kills the mood quite like spontaneous combustion of your entire social circle. Yet, Dominic hands her a sword and points her toward a king. Romance in contemporary fantasy has devolved into an endurance sport where emotional intimacy is traded for proximity to lethal danger. I want someone to write a book where the brooding shadow-wielder just buys his partner a nice cottage by the sea and makes her herbal tea. But no. We must have teahouse brothels and sin-drenched princes. 

Actually, speaking of domestic bliss, my local barista looked at me with genuine pity this morning when I ordered a quad-shot espresso just to get through the sheer volume of romantic angst filling my TBR pile. People always talk about finding their soulmate, but have you ever tried finding a decent bakery that doesn't use artificial vanilla? It is a tragedy of modern civilization. Give me a well-baked croissant over a brooding monarch any day of the week—though I suppose the prince in this sequel will probably look devastatingly bored while wearing crushed velvet, so I will inevitably preorder the special edition. 

### Shadows, Sin, and Syntax

The synopsis leans heavily into the delicious agony of restraint. Giving in to lust is lethal. Oh, please. We have heard this exact song before. It echoes Sarah J. Maas and stretches all the way back to *The Cruel Prince*, this relentless obsession with touch-starved protagonists whose skin literally hums with dark magic. But Odette writes with a lushness that usually elevates the cliché. 

The prose promises a sensory overload of lunar magic and secret brothels. My heart simply flutters at the thought of a good slow-burn betrayal. Just once, I want a heroine to prioritize her sleep schedule over saving a continent from total annihilation. Is that too much to ask? Apparently, yes. We must suffer through the shadows. 

Need to go scrub oatmeal off the antique desk.
