**Author:** Sharma Shields  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Steam is hissing from the group head because some teenager ordered a macchiato with oat milk at four in the afternoon like an absolute psychopath. Meanwhile, I am stuck staring at a marketing blurb for *Duckling*, wondering why every historical fiction author insists on exhuming dead writers just to slap a glossy coat of trauma porn on their biographies. 

Let’s be honest about what this synopsis is selling. We get the classic nineteenth-century misery pipeline: impoverished Danish child loses his dad, heads to the capital, and writes *The Little Mermaid* because some girl rejected him. Groundbreaking. Margaret Atwood calls the author "quirky, funny, dark," which usually translates to "this book contains whimsical depression and approximately zero genuine dread." I wanted Cronenbergian body horror—give me sailors mutating into crustaceans, or a swamp witch who actually flays people. Instead, we are apparently getting Victorian marital drama with Charles and Catherine Dickens. 

### The Aesthetics of Sadness

The synopsis promises that characters from Hans’s fairy tales will "come feverishly to life" as he wrestles with mortality. Okay, fine. That sounds vaguely unhinged, which is the bare minimum requirement for holding my attention while I wipe espresso grounds off the counter for minimum wage. But notice how vague the stakes are. The boundary between reality and fiction blurs. Wow. Riveting literary innovation. Groundbreaking stuff. Next thing you know, the ugly duckling is going to metaphorically represent his internal emotional landscape. Absolute brain-rot. 

And yet, Sharma Shields has a penchant for historical subversion. *The Cassandra* wasn't entirely toothless. If we are lucky, this will lean less into cozy historical fiction book-club bait and more into a genuinely psychotic fever dream where Andersen's creations torment him like bloodthirsty little ghouls. We need more literary fiction that actually rots from the inside out instead of wrapping itself in polite historical euphemisms. 

### Marketing Cliches and the Void

Why do I have to parse this PR copy right when the afternoon rush hits? I am literally just trying to hit a word count to appease the anonymous overlords keeping me tethered to this espresso machine, and now I have to pretend I care about Catherine Dickens navigating a humiliating betrayal. Vibes-wise, this feels like Guillermo del Toro’s *Pinocchio* mated with a *Masterpiece Theatre* divorce proceeding, which is either an artistic triumph or a total snooze-fest. No in-between. Let's hope the prose isn't total mid-wit trash. 

My Rolex needs servicing, which is a profound inconvenience, especially since it keeps getting milk foam sprayed directly into the bezel while I stand here writing literary critiques for free.
