**Author:** Kevin Jagernauth  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-12-01 APL

Another mid-century pastiche lands on the mahogany desk, smelling faintly of cheap pulp and desperate literary ambition. *The Longest Death* arrives via Kevin Jagernauth, promising a sun-baked sweatbox of forbidden desire and vault-cracking ineptitude in Riverton, USA. Yawn. One grows profoundly tired of authors attempting to exhume the rotting corpse of Dashiell Hammett just because they watched *Double Indemnity* once on a cracked iPad. But let us dissect the promotional parchment before us, considering I have roughly zero emotional investment left in this mortal coil. 

### The Aesthetics of Petty Grifts

The synopsis leans heavily on the tired trope of the terminally bored bureaucrat—Richard Cobson, whose absolute lack of internal friction makes him a thoroughly uncompelling vessel for tragedy. Yet the arrival of Marlon Montgomery—described with the compulsory smooth-talking swagger required by noir casting directors since 1952—instantly shifts the narrative gear into a queer Bonnie-and-Clyde dynamic that feels suspiciously designed for modern book club bingo cards. It is giving aggressively derivative fanfiction of Joseph Hansen, mixed with a dash of Donald E. Westlake's lighter capers, stripped of any genuine grit. 

### Complications and Collisions

Naturally, the scheme cannot remain a tidy two-man operation. Enter the obligatory supporting cast: Gloria Lamame wanting a cut because she is presumably just as bored as the protagonists, Frank Todd playing the disgraced badge looking for redemption, and Silas Dowd acting as the paranoid patriarch. It is an utterly predictable domino arrangement. The press materials scream about explosive and fatal collisions, but let us be entirely serious here—this is pure predictable slop for the chronically online lit-fic crowd. 

### The Final Verdict

Expect the prose to rely entirely on sensory shorthand—violet cigarettes and juke joints—to compensate for a structural plot that will likely unravel faster than a cheap suit in a monsoon. The atmospheric window-dressing does heavy lifting for a premise that feels violently middle-brow. Megan Abbott blurbs it, naturally, because the contemporary thriller ecosystem is an insular echo chamber of mutual admiration societies. 

I need to log off this terminal immediately and fire that wretched ghostwriter who somehow managed to completely misunderstand my creative vision for the peace treaty memoirs by confusing them with a regional cookbook.
