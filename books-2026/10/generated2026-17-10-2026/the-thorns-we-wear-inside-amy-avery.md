**Author:** Amy Avery  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-11-10 APL

October air tastes like dry champagne and old cedar, the kind of evening that demands a heavy velvet blazer and absolutely zero patience for mediocrity. Amy Avery arrives next November with *The Thorns We Wear Inside*, a sapphic offering that practically drips off the tongue with the same heavy, melancholic decadence as an exhausted socialite leaving a botched Met Gala afterparty. 

### The Architecture of Affliction

We are looking at a masterclass in gothic claustrophobia. Raye carries a curse that reduces living flora and fauna to dust—a decidedly dramatic handicap—while tethered to an internal parasite that behaves much like Camilla Parker-Bowles at a family dinner: omnipresent, uninvited, and utterly exhausting. But the brilliance of the premise lies in the parasitic symbiosis. Avery leans hard into the architecture of affliction, turning bodily decay into a masterclass of gothic ambiance. 

Mir occupies a decaying castle where time stands still. Think the Estoril Palace circa 1975, only wetter and considerably more cursed. And what a delightfully unhinged setup: two women, each saddled with an insatiable blood-seeking demon, trapped in an esoteric cage while monster hunters pace outside the courtyard like desperate paparazzi. 

### The Pathology of Devotion

The genre loves a good power dynamic, yet Avery twists the traditional romantasy architecture into something distinctly feral. We are promised a “will kill anyone but you” dynamic mixed with a literal love quadrangle involving the respective internal demons. Let us be entirely frank. Managing a relationship is difficult enough without your spleen actively conspiring with your girlfriend’s spleen to ruin date night. 

The inclusion of a poisonous touch that miraculously fails against Mir is peak gothic melodrama. It gives us that delicious, aching tension—the kind of exquisite physical agony that makes you want to throw your expensive stemware against the masonry just to feel something. It’s giving Elizabeth Taylor and Richard Burton screaming on a yacht in Portofino, minus the yacht, plus significantly more teeth. 

### Aesthetic Execution

One must wonder if the narrative architecture will hold up under the weight of such aggressively dark tropes. Romantasy trends currently lean heavily toward this brand of morally grey sadomasochism, often sacrificing narrative pacing for vibes. Yet, if the comparisons to *Nettle & Bone* hold any weight, we are in for a sharp, cynical treat that refuses to coddle its audience. The prose promises to be cinematic, seductive, and delightfully unhinged—qualities I demand in my fiction precisely because my real life is overwhelmingly orderly. 

The monsters are not merely lurking in the woods; they are quite literally under the skin. Avery invites us into a space where redemption is supposedly earned through devotion, though I suspect the bodies left in the wake of this twisted quadrangle will matter far more than any tidy moral resolution. 

The evening chill is settling nicely into the bone, and the garage is calling. I am taking the vintage Porsche out for a spin to clear my head of parasitic demons and gothic excess.
