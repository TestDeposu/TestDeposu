**Author:** Cho yeeun  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Another hyped-up premise hitting the canvas, and I am already squinting through the smoke. Cho Yeeun is dropping *A Mouth Full of Fins* in 2026, and the promotional machinery is trying to sell us a high-concept bout between toxic interpersonal relations and literal aquatic body-horror. Let us be real. If a guy kicks the bucket and his ribs are licked cleaner than a middleweight after a championship weigh-in, someone in the writer's room has been watching too many highlight reels of prime early-round submissions. 

### Striking the Wrong Chord in the Graveyard Shift

The setup is classic literary matchmaking — grieving band member gets stiffed with a defunct pet store and a basement full of carnivorous mythical fauna. But can we talk about the mechanics of this strike? Seonhyung loses her gig because of a backstabbing vocalist, inherits a spooky fish tank, and immediately pivots to nursing a mute, tongue-severed mermaid back to health like she is taping up a cutman's mess in a dingy locker room. The thematic obsession here is glaringly obvious. We are looking at a metaphorical pummeling on voice, silencing, and the brutal physics of eating versus being eaten. But I’m too sober for this chapter. The synopsis relies heavily on that classic horror trope where nobody checks the weird smell for days. Absolute amateur hour in the grappling department. You smell rotting meat downstairs, you do not slowly saunter down a trap door for a casual meet-and-cute with a bloodthirsty apex predator; you clear the corners with a flashlight and a heavy pipe. 

### The Ground Game of Desperation

Yet the underlying psychology hits with the heavy impact of a liver shot. The text promises a brutal deconstruction of what it means to crave someone's sound so badly you will feed them raw meat just to hear them hit the high notes again. It is giving me major shades of Mariana Enríquez meets vintage Stephen King, but filtered through a distinctly contemporary lens of absolute internet-brained aesthetic rot. The grit-to-glamour ratio here is entirely skewed. You cannot just shove a mutilated mermaid into a damp basement and expect the literary judges to score it a clean ten-point must without some serious stylistic footwork. Cho has a habit of subverting expectations in Korean horror, but this synopsis reads like a fighter telegraphing a spinning back-fist from three miles away. 

### The Final Bell

Will the prose actually land the knockout punch, or will it gas out by the third act after blowing all its energy on shock-value imagery? I have my doubts. The synopsis leans so hard into the ethereal-meets-visceral aesthetic that it risks pulling a muscle before the bell even rings. Still, if the pacing holds up and the psychological claustrophobia hits with proper leverage, this might just avoid getting choked out by its own convoluted metaphors. I have to go argue with my landlord about the persistent leak in the ceiling.
