**Author:** Tzeyi Koay  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

My leather-bound octavos are currently rotting on the mahogany shelves because I am staring at a calendar that simply refuses to turn fast enough for *A Curse Carved in Ink*. Tzeyi Koay has not even delivered the bound proofs yet, and honestly? The literary world is an absolute dumpster fire of delayed galleys while I wait to see if this Jiangshi nonsense actually delivers on its high-concept promises, or if it is just another overpriced paperweight suffering from the Fyre Festival of YA crossovers. 

### The Pathology of Antique Bookstores and Dead Best Friends

We are expected to believe that a dusty antiquarian shop holds generational trauma wrapped in a blood-borne curse called the Jiangshi’s Claw. Groundbreaking. Every cynical Manhattan archivist knows that old paper smells like vanillin and lignin degradation, not ancient demonic possession—though, granted, the latter would at least justify the extortionate prices at Sotheby's. Koay leans heavily into the claustrophobia of hereditary obligation here. Zhenya wants out of the family business, but fate said, "hold my chardonnay" and forced her to murder her best friend, Shuhua. Slaps. Literally slaps. 

### Necromancy, Bureaucracy, and Ten Circles of Hell

Death is apparently just a minor administrative hurdle when you have a queer love triangle to resolve in the Chinese underworld. Diyu features ten brutal layers, which sounds suspiciously like the seating chart at a Met Gala disaster. Zhenya has to descend this bureaucratic nightmare alongside two immortals—a brooding judge of hell and a dangerously charming goddess of death. Peak bi panic. If you are not playing *Hades* while highlighting passages about divine sadism and underworld jurisdiction, you are doing this aesthetic completely wrong. 

### Romance, Folklore, and the 2026 Drought

Shelley Parker-Chan and Joan He walked so Koay could aggressively sprint through folklore-steeped carnage. The publisher’s blurb throws around comparisons to *She Who Became the Sun* and *Daughter of the Moon Goddess* as if we haven't read twenty derivative myth-retellings this fiscal quarter alone. Yet—and this is a massive yet—the promise of ink-carved curses and queer underworld negotiation hits different. It drips with potential. Or it reads like fanfiction written by an exhausted grad student on Adderall. There is zero middle ground with these standalone romantic fantasies. 

### Why Am I Suffering Like This?

The audacity to schedule a 2026 release date when my patience expired circa 2024 should be classified as a Geneva Convention violation. My publicist is demanding an immediate statement for the press, but I am too busy drafting angry emails to my independent bookseller in Mayfair.
