Aunt Linda insists on burning cinnamon candles this early in October, transforming the screen porch into something resembling a Yankee Candle outlet fire. Between the cloying haze and the distant sound of lawnmowers, I am forced to look at *Dead Man's Journey*. Preston and Child are returning to the well. Again. 

### The Spanish Inquisition Had Better Stay Buried

Upside-down skeletons in Santa Fe. How original. Because nothing screams prestige summer reading quite like a Hollywood actress throwing a tantrum over historical bones at an exclusive resort. The synopsis for this latest Nora Kelly outing reads like a discarded pitch meeting for a straight-to-streaming adaptation starring Katherine Heigl circa 2012. Actually, scratch that—it has all the narrative nuance of the Fyre Festival documentary. 

Or perhaps it doesn't. My god, who am I kidding? The plotting here might actually be tighter than I initially assumed, provided they lean hard into the archival procedural elements that made the early Pendergast novels feel genuinely claustrophobic instead of mere formulaic churn.

### Relics, Retaliation, and Ridiculousness

Corrie Swanson tagging in to handle a copycat killer with an "ancient power" is a tired trope straight out of Dan Brown's less coherent filing cabinets. We are trading archaeological rigor for cinematic cheap thrills. Yet, the juxtaposition of seventeenth-century Spanish zealotry with modern real estate development offers a delicious structural tension. The Inquisition in New Mexico is a fascinating, brutally under-utilized historical vein. If they avoid turning the witch-trial angle into a supernatural cartoon, there is bone-dry salvation here. But let us be entirely honest with ourselves. This whole enterprise smells faintly of contractual obligation. I'd wait for the paperback.

My screen is dying, and Aunt Linda is currently knocking on the screen door to ask if I want a slice of zucchini bread that looks like a cinderblock. I have to go bake three dozen cupcakes by tomorrow morning because apparently volunteering for the church bake sale while intoxicated on corporate zoom calls is a binding verbal contract in this zip code.
