**Author:** Chris Cleave  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

One simply despairs at the current literary obsession with the common folk. Every second prospectus landing on the mahogany desk in the East Wing reeks of the same banal hand-wringing. Now the darling of the middle-brow paperback crowd, Dr. Chris Cleave—yes, the very same man who wrung our tear ducts dry with *Little Bee*—has decided to hang up his novelist's quill and don a cardigan. A pivot to non-fiction. How dreadfully predictable. Yet, judging strictly by the publisher's glossy promises for *Human Again: The Lost Art of Being Alive*, the aristocracy of nerves is apparently in shambles. We are all quite cooked, darling. Absolutely cooked. 

### The Pedigree of Exhaustion

The premise rests on a rather tedious democratization of misery. Apparently, the hoi polloi and the blue-bloods alike are collapsing under the weight of existence. Cleave—operating from his dual throne of clinical psychotherapist and bestseller royalty—wants us to believe that anxiety is merely an "intelligent adaptation." Oh, please. Spare me the psychoanalytical sugarcoating. One suspects this entire volume is designed to give the metropolitan middle-classes a sophisticated excuse for their afternoon prosecco binges. And yet, there is a biting utility here. If the plebs stop crying on the Central Line, perhaps my morning commute will finally resume some semblance of civilized silence.

### The Pathology of the Modern Epoch

Consider the aesthetic lineage. We are meant to swallow a cocktail of neuroscience, philosophy, and good old-fashioned bedside manner. It gives off heavy *The School of Life* vibes, blended with the relentless self-actualization drivel peddled by Alain de Botton. Yet Cleave writes with a certain ruthless literary cadence that usually saves his fiction from the recycle bin. He insists we are not broken. Just human. Groundbreaking. Truly, stop the presses. But notice the rhetorical sleight-of-hand. By pathologizing the entire century rather than the individual, he absolves the reader of all personal culpability. Pure genius for sales, truly. Why fix your wretched constitution when you can simply blame late-stage capitalism and family trauma? 

### The Verdict from the Balcony

Will this tome rescue the fractured psyche of the nation? Unlikely. It will likely grace the bedside tables of country-house weekend guests who genuinely believe they are victims of ancestral burdens because their Wi-Fi dropped during Ascot. Still, as cultural artifacts go, it promises to be a deliciously earnest piece of middle-class hand-wringing. One almost has to respect the hustle. Turning modern whining into hardback gold requires a specific type of upper-middle-class alchemy. 

Must dash now. I have an exclusive vintage wine tasting event at the country club in an hour, and the chauffeur is already honking the Bentley.
