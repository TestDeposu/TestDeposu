The calendar sits on my mahogany desk, mocking me with its slow crawl toward November 2026. Why must we wait an eternity for S. Isabelle to hand us the sequel? *The Revolution of Olivia Witherson* promises to drag us back into the cobblestone alleys of 1860s Paris, and frankly, my tea is growing cold while I tap my fountain pen against the blotter. Give it to us now. Or do the publishers expect us to simply sit about embroidering cushions until late autumn?

### The Architecture of Confinement

Paris in the nineteenth century was never merely a backdrop. It was a pressure cooker of silk gowns, police surveillance, and industrial soot. Yet here we find Miss Olivia Witherson, the epitome of high-society docility, strapped into the narrative trajectory of a Titanic deckchair arrangement. She expects a wealthy fiancé, a townhouse, and a lifetime of politely suffocating dinners with Samuel Desir. 

Then comes the kidnapping. 

How delightfully pedestrian, yet utterly necessary to shatter the gilded cage. S. Isabelle is clearly channeling Edith Wharton by way of the July Revolution, dragging a pristine upper-class protagonist out of her drawing room and dropping her squarely into the muck. One can already picture the ruined hems of those imported skirts. 

### The Aesthetics of Insurgency

Enter the Saints Tristes. A gang of ruffians operating out of the outskirts, distributing medicine and organizing the very factory hands who sweat for Samuel Desir. It is the Marie Antoinette affair played out with trade unions and stolen bread. And naturally, Olivia must contend with Jac Santil—the infuriating rogue who presumably possesses a heart of gold beneath his scruffy waistcoat. 

Call it the *Phantom Thread* dynamic meets the barricades of *Les Misérables*, minus the barricades and plus a great deal of unresolved romantic tension in a damp hideout. 

Yet, I harbor a distinct, aristocratic dread about the political awakening trope. Will it be sharp? Will it slice through the bourgeois hypocrisy with the precision of a guillotine blade? Or will it devolve into a predictable sermon about the evils of capitalism disguised as a bodice-ripper for the TikTok generation? One shudders at the thought. Give us ruthless geopolitical maneuvering, not a sanitized afternoon tea with the proletariat. 

### The Verdict on the Wait

Comparisons to *Bridgerton* and *The Davenports* are thrown about like confetti at a state funeral, but I expect more teeth from this author. Isabelle established a rather sharp baseline with *The Great Misfortune of Stella Sedgwick*, and I refuse to tolerate a softening of the edges simply because a romance label is slapped across the spine. The aristocracy never yields its privilege without a fight, and neither should the prose. 

My tailor is downstairs, tapping his foot and waiting to fit me for the upcoming awards ceremony while I waste my afternoon glowering at press releases.
