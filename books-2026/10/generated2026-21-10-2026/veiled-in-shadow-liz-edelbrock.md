**Author:** Liz Edelbrock  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-12-01 APL

The synopsis of *Veiled in Shadow* reads like a fever dream stitched together from the discarded drafts of a *Twilight* fanfic that somehow survived the Great Romance Purge of 2012. The idea of a princess trading her kingdom for a stranger’s bed—because, of course, nothing says "political alliance" like a train full of courtiers and a bodyguard who may or may not be the love interest—is so tired it should be buried with the rest of the "enemies-to-lovers" trope’s unclaimed bodies.

And yet—here’s the real crime—it’s not even *original* in the way it’s trying to be. The synopsis leans so hard into the "forbidden love" narrative that it forgets to ask why anyone would find it remotely compelling. Delana’s grief over her father’s fading magic is a cliché so old it’s been exhumed from *The Princess Bride*’s crypt. The murder on the train? A plot device so predictable it’s like watching a bad *Titanic* remake where the iceberg is just a metaphor for "oh no, the love story is doomed." The killer’s identity is already a given—it’s the prince, isn’t it?—because why would it be anyone else? The real question is whether Edelbrock has the chops to make this feel like anything more than a *Bridgerton* episode where the stakes are slightly higher but the emotional payoff is about as substantial as a gilded teacup.

The bodyguard, Bastian, is the only character with any potential, but only if Edelbrock can resist the urge to turn him into a brooding, silent type who exists solely to make Delana’s internal monologue about "how he smells like rain and betrayal." That’s not tension—that’s a *Game of Thrones* fanfiction outline. And the train? An enchanted train is the literary equivalent of a "mysterious stranger" in a romance novel. It’s a gimmick, not a world. The real magic would be if Edelbrock could make the setting feel like more than just a prop for the love story, but given the synopsis, I suspect we’re in for a lot of "the train’s windows weep silver" nonsense, which is just *The Night Circus* without the charm.

The thematic core—what will Delana risk for Bastian, and what might it cost the kingdom—is so broad it could apply to any soap opera where a woman makes a deal with the devil for love. The tension between duty and desire is a well-worn path, but the execution here feels like it’s being driven by a GPS that’s only ever given directions to the nearest "enemies-to-lovers" pit stop. The real question is whether Edelbrock can make this feel like anything more than a rehash of *The Cruel Prince*’s emotional whiplash, where the stakes are personal but the prose is lazy.

And then there’s the killer—because of course there’s a killer. The synopsis doesn’t even bother to hint at who it might be, just that Delana is the target. That’s not suspense; that’s a *Clue* board where every character is a suspect and the only real mystery is whether the prince will finally get his happy ending. The fact that the synopsis doesn’t even attempt to make the threat feel urgent is telling. It’s like watching a *Mission: Impossible* movie where the bomb countdown is just there to fill time.

The only thing that might save this from being a complete snoozefest is if Edelbrock can make the magic system feel like more than just a backdrop. Memory magic fading? That’s a trope so old it’s been in every YA fantasy since *The Giver*. The fact that the synopsis doesn’t even attempt to explain how it works—just that it’s fading—is a red flag. It’s like writing a romance novel where the love interest’s job is "mysterious" but never actually describing what they do. The reader is supposed to just *trust* that the magic will be interesting, which is the literary equivalent of trusting a chef to make a five-course meal from a single potato.

The real crime here is the synopsis’s refusal to take any risks. It’s a love story first, a mystery second, and a political thriller third—all mashed together like a smoothie with no discernible flavor. The fact that the killer’s identity is already a given (it’s the prince, isn’t it?) and the love story is the real focus is a betrayal of the reader’s time. It’s like showing up to a *Godfather* marathon and being handed a *Bridgerton* fanfic instead.

But perhaps that’s the point. Perhaps Edelbrock is banking on the fact that readers will forgive her for the lack of originality because the love story is "forbidden" and the train is "enchanted." It’s the literary equivalent of a fast-food restaurant that promises "fresh ingredients" but only serves the same three items on repeat. The only thing that might salvage this is if the prose is so lush it can make up for the plot’s laziness, but given the synopsis, I suspect we’re in for a lot of "the air smelled like lavender and lies" nonsense, which is just *The Night Circus* without the charm.
