Another grey morning presses against the windowpanes like a damp wool coat, matching the exact temperament of my soul while the aristocracy bickers over seating charts. Sue Lynn Tan prepares to inflict the second installment of her current trilogy upon us with *For Ever More*. And frankly, the prospect fills me with a quiet dread. 

The initial volume, *Never Ever After*, received the usual breathless hyperbole from people who clearly missed the golden age of high fantasy. Now we are promised a fugitive princess, an usurped throne, and the inevitable emotional turmoil of a young girl forced to decide whom to trust. Groundbreaking concepts, truly. One might wonder if anyone in the modern publishing apparatus remembers the concept of genuine originality, or if we are all doomed to recycle ancestral home tropes until the paper mills run dry. 

### The Burden of Stolen Thrones

Yining is fleeing the Iron Mountains. With unlikely allies, naturally. Because what is a fantasy sequel without a ragtag band of misfits sharing dry rations and profound glances around a campfire? The synopsis leans heavily on the tired mechanics of power dynamics and stolen crowns. Yet, we are told she must protect her heart from harm. This altered my brain chemistry. Yes, nothing quite says epic stakes like ensuring a teenage girl's affections remain uninjured while a kingdom burns to ash. 

Or perhaps the politics will actually matter. Wait. Let me retract that momentary lapse in judgment. Who am I kidding? The politics in these modern rehashes are merely decorative wallpaper designed to distract from endless, repetitive internal monologues about duty and destiny. 

Renée Ahdieh and Roshani Chokshi slapped their names across the cover with effusive praise, comparing the prose to jewels and prisms. One suspects the endorsement checks cleared nicely before the ink dried. Tan certainly knows how to spin a lush sentence, much like Elizabeth Lim in *Six Crimson Cranes*. But aesthetic gloss cannot salvage a plot that reads like a publisher's focus group checklist. 

### The Perils of the Special Edition

And do not get me started on the commercial desperation of the marketing campaign. We are badgered into ordering the special first edition immediately to secure designed case covers, illustrated endpapers, and shiny foil. Because the prose itself is clearly insufficient to compel a purchase. They must wrap the mediocrity in embossed cardboard to satisfy the Instagram curio collectors. 

The Three Kingdoms are ruled by a traitorous queen. Nowhere is safe. Yining must find the truth. We have heard this exact song before, played on a cracked lute by an uninspired bard. 

My tailor has been lingering in the hallway for twenty minutes, tapping his silver pocket watch with escalating annoyance because I still need to be fitted for the velvet trousers for Friday's charity gala, and this dreary weather refuses to lift.
