Stanza and meter should govern mortality, yet here we are, bracing ourselves for Maxie Dara’s forthcoming *A Grim Reaper's Guide to Living Twice*. 

The premise limps in like a tired alexandrine. A dead thief commandeers the mortal shell of an afterlife functionary. One expects the rhythmic precision of Terry Pratchett or the dark, velvet cadence of Neil Gaiman. Instead, the publisher’s pitch offers up "bunioned feet" and geriatric punchlines. 

### The Metrics of the Absurd

And poetry demands dignity. Yet Dara hurls her protagonist into the indignity of corporate bureaucracy paired with identity theft. 

Wait. Let me reconsider my stance entirely. Perhaps the vulgarity of bunions against the infinite abyss of the cosmos is precisely the jarring juxtaposition modern letters require. Or perhaps it is merely lazy prose masking itself as whimsical high-concept fiction. 

Because the synopsis insists upon a "priceless list of souls." How utterly pedestrian. A MacGuffin wrapped in celestial parchment. We are meant to tremble at the prospect of a bureaucratic heist involving geriatric reapers and rogue misfits. Yet the execution smells entirely of algorithmic plotting—a desperate attempt to bottle the sparkling dry wit of Christopher Moore and dilute it for the masses who consume paperbacks by the airport kilo. 

### The Burden of Form

Language ought to sing. It should lacerate the soul and mend it with silk thread. Instead, we are promised a protagonist who cannot keep his foot out of line without ruining an afterlife. The stakes remain profoundly inert. If a soul collector loses a list, does the universe shudder, or do we simply yawn? 

I demand art that bleeds. This synopsis merely perspires. 

My assistant has somehow managed to misplace my passport precisely twenty-four hours before my flight to Paris, leaving me trapped here with this dreadful publicity copy.
