**Author:** Elliott Rose  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-27 APL

Winter is nothing more than a slow-acting poison, and Elliott Rose has decided to spike the mulled wine with *Saddles & Snowfall*. Two novellas, zero subtlety, and the kind of narrative structural integrity one expects from the Fyre Festival aftermath. Yet, I stare at the synopsis of this upcoming collection like it is a pristine Caravaggio found moldering in a damp cellar. Betrayal has a distinct metallic taste. It clings to the palate long after the cheating exes have packed their polyester bags and left the snow-locked ranch. 

### The Architecture of Despair

And what a magnificent trainwreck of hormones we are promised. A slip of the thumb brings a lifelong cowboy crush—specifically, the best friend’s father—into the aperture of domestic ruin. Freud would weep into his schnapps. This is the literary equivalent of the Titanic rearranging deck chairs while playing a Charli XCX bass boost. Rose leans into the crimson-tinted snow of Crimson Ridge with the unhinged precision of a surgeon operating blindfolded after three martinis. Forbidden dynamics wrapped in ugly Christmas sweaters. We are expected to digest this emotional charcuterie board without so much as a napkin. 

### The Mechanics of Bad Decisions

Tequila as a prophylactic against existential dread? Relatable, frankly. The second novella swaps paternal yearning for sibling-adjacent chaos, because nothing screams healing quite like a drunken three-way with an ex-girlfriend's brother. Peak mess. Absolute gutter-tier serotonin mining, and I respect the commitment to the bit. Rose constructs an aesthetic universe where flannel meets fatalism. The prose will undoubtedly swim in the same shallow, glistening waters as a Colleen Hoover fever dream orchestrated by Baz Luhrmann. 

We buy the art because the brushstrokes of betrayal look entirely too much like our own reflection. My phone buzzes on the mahogany desk, reminding me that some wretched app-notification is waiting, but I ignore it. The snow piles higher outside the leaded glass. Let the blizzards rage. Let the bad text messages fly. 

Now, if you will excuse me, I must go appraise some heirloom jewelry my aunt bequeathed to me before she fled to Zurich with a younger tennis instructor.
