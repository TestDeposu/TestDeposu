The boarding gate agent’s voice drones through the terminal with the exact cadence of a death knell. Another forty-minute delay on the London leg. Perfect. Gives me ample time to contemplate the literary abyss represented by Kevin Moore’s upcoming novelette, *Waking Sleeping Beauty*. 

### Real Estate and Reproductive Stakes

Look at the logistical nightmare of this premise. A duplex investment strategy transformed into a domestic panopticon. And for what? A localized breeding tournament between a groggy husband and a next-door boyfriend. It reads less like transgressive fiction and more like the architectural blueprints of the Edgartown ferry disaster. 

### Domestic Architecture as Farce

Bourgeois architecture was never meant to accommodate this level of banal hedonism. Moore presents a suburban arrangement where property lines blur into shared bodily fluids. Yet the execution smells faintly of late-night Cinemax and hastily drafted Craigslist personals. Call it Philip Roth by way of a plumbing manual. But without the prose style. Or the wit. 

Characters trip over patio stones in the dark, flashing wedding bands like amateur detectives in a bad noir. Justin wants stories. Dylan wants overnight stays. Amber wants a genetic tiebreaker. 

### The Aesthetics of Excess

Ten thousand words. That is the length of an extended memo from an agitated partner at a failing hedge fund. Moore stuffs every possible demographic checkbox into this tiny word count—group sex, gay sex, voyeurism—as if terrified the reader might look away for a single second. Desperation has a specific literary register. It is loud. It lacks negative space. 

### The Prognosis

This book will undoubtedly find an audience among people who buy their wine in plastic boxes at airport kiosks. It attempts to subvert traditional matrimony by turning a fertility contest into a neighborly sporting event. Instead, it merely confirms that the middle-class imagination has officially flatlined. 

My offshore accountant is demanding a secure phone call.
