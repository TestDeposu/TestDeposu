**Author:** Charissa Weaks  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Three hundred years of chronological wandering for a glimpse of genuine affection is frankly exhausting to even contemplate, yet here we find Raina Bloodgood bracing herself against the cruel tides of history in the fourth installment of the *Witch Walker* series. Charissa Weaks clearly refuses to grant her characters a single moment of domestic peace. And why should she? Star-crossed dynamics require endless suffering, bruised knuckles, and impossible choices that threaten the very fabric of existence. 

### The Chronological Chasm of the Heart

Look at the psychological toll of loving a villain who looks you dead in the eye from the wrong side of three centuries. We talk endlessly about communication issues in modern partnerships—honestly, trying to talk through your feelings is difficult enough when your partner leaves their socks on the floor, let alone when they are trapped in a bygone era ruled by a malevolent god named Alexi. Can we take a moment to address how absurd the cartoon *Bluey* is with its relentlessly emotionally intelligent canine parents? Bandy and Chilli manage to resolve every minor household dispute with a gentle life lesson and a warm hug in seven minutes flat, whereas Raina is stuck dodging a deity and hiding her hidden lineage in an age of pure malice. No one in *Bluey* ever has to decide whether to abandon their significant other to a torturous fate because temporal displacement ruined their anniversary dinner. 

Wait. Let me rephrase that entirely. Actually, domestic bliss in fiction is completely overrated anyway. Give me the toxic, agonizing whiplash of temporal displacement every single time. 

### The Burden of Ancient Lore

Weaks leans heavily into the delicious agony of forbidden bonds, pushing the boundaries of what readers expect from contemporary romantic fantasy. Fans of Sarah J. Maas and Devney Perry will undoubtedly recognize the familiar beat of a fiercely independent woman trapped by forces beyond her control. Yet the synopsis remains infuriatingly vague about the mechanics of Raina's magick failing her. How does magick just turn off because of a change in latitude and longitude? Physics does not work that way, and neither does esoteric spellcraft. Still, the promise of an unimaginable mentor guiding her through a landscape of vengeful gods keeps the tension sharp enough to slice glass. 

Will Raina choose history or love? The question itself is tired, a recycled trope rattling around the hollowed-out skull of the fantasy genre. But Weaks executes these high-stakes emotional collisions with enough atmospheric dread to make even the cynics among us clutch our tea mugs a little tighter. 

My personal assistant has somehow managed to misplace my passport right before the Paris flight.
