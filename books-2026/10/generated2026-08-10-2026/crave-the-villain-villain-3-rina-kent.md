**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Let’s address the elephant in the room. Why do we keep trusting men who moonlight as homicidal maniacs after spending their mornings instructing mortals on how to throw a proper left hook? *Crave the Villain* drops us squarely into the chaotic universe of Rina Kent, where personal boundaries are nonexistent and every single professional athlete is secretly packing lethal weapons. We are looking at a narrative framework that leans heavily into the tried-and-true juxtaposition of sunshine and shadow—because apparently, a woman cannot simply enjoy her morning coffee without a brooding hitman scaling her apartment walls like an overgrown, murderous gecko. 

The synopsis itself reads like a mad-libs template designed specifically to hyper-fixate BookTok into an early grave, throwing around words like "special forces" and "fairy tale" with reckless abandon while conveniently omitting any actual stakes beyond whether Maddie can break her tragic streak of dating absolute losers. And honestly? I am exhausted. I spend my entire Tuesday afternoons breaking up turf wars over juice boxes in the driveway, only to come inside and read about a trained killer who thinks his primary moral redemption arc is calling a grown woman "sunshine" right before he goes out to eradicate a syndicate. 

Wait. Let me rephrase that completely. 

Actually, the psychological architecture here is fascinatingly robust. Beneath the neon glare of Manhattan, the archetype of the protective monster functions as a literal manifestation of modern romantic anxiety—the terrifying realization that the people we let into our homes carry enough baggage to derail a freight train. 

Obsession.

Look, if Grayson so much as breathes near Maddie without offering a comprehensive background check and a verified bank statement, I am personally driving down to New York to rescue her myself, because these fictional heroines deserve better than a guy whose idea of romance is locking them in a penthouse suite for their own protection. Fans of J.T. Geissinger and HD Carlton know the drill by heart, but I swear to heavens, if these two spend three hundred pages staring intensely at each other across a boxing ring while avoiding actual communication, I am going to lose my absolute mind. 

I am entirely unprepared to host the neighborhood playdate.
