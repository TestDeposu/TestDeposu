**Author:** Audrey Niffenegger  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-06 APL

Alba DeTamble. The name alone drags a heavy chain of literary baggage behind it, doesn't it? We spent years watching her father flicker in and out of domesticity like a faulty fluorescent bulb, and now we are expected to believe the daughter is somehow going to ground this franchise in a dystopian Chicago? Please. The synopsis promises a "kaleidoscope of perspectives," which is usually publisher-speak for "I couldn't decide whose head to stay in for more than five pages." I worry for Alba. She is trapped in a genetic condition that makes the concept of a "growth arc" essentially impossible, and yet we are supposed to cheer for her as she builds a community in a world that is clearly falling apart. If Niffenegger tries to shoehorn a romance with this "safe, supportive anchor" named Zach into a timeline that literally refuses to exist in a linear fashion, I am going to lose my mind. It reeks of the same desperate, sentimental urge to fix what isn't broken—a problem I encountered recently while trudging through a vapid YA novel where the protagonist spent four hundred pages mourning a hamster, while the rest of the school was busy dealing with a standardized testing scandal that was far more gripping, yet the author kept pivoting back to the rodent's tiny, tragic funeral rites as if that were supposed to hold the weight of an entire cultural critique on the failure of our educational institutions to actually foster critical thinking, but instead, we were just left with damp fur and bad prose—and it is precisely this brand of distracting, emotional padding that I fear will dilute the potential grit of *Life Out of Order*.

Predictable.

Isadora, this mysterious stepsister, sounds like the only character with any real teeth—a "digital museum" curator? That is a premise worth exploring, assuming the author doesn't bury it under a pile of metaphors about how "art saves us." It is all very *Station Eleven* meets a glitchy TikTok feed. Niffenegger has a habit of making her characters suffer just enough to look interesting on a back cover, but rarely enough to actually change them. My prediction? This will be the literary equivalent of a high-end, overpriced candle—smells expensive, burns out far too quickly, and leaves you wondering why you bought it in the first place. You can almost feel the marketing machine grinding gears to push this into the "genre-defining" category. Spare me. Let the girl exist in the mess without trying to turn her trauma into a glossy digital artifact. I’m tired of reading about "future-witnessing" protagonists who never bother to change anything because, apparently, free will is just a suggestion. We deserve a character who actually breaks the machine instead of just curating it.
