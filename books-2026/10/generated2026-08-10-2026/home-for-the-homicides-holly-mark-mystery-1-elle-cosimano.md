**Author:** Elle Cosimano  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-13 APL

Stabbing someone with an icicle ornament feels like a desperate reach for metaphor in a landscape that frankly deserves more nuance, though perhaps I am just projecting my own irritation at the prospect of another "cozy" crime procedural that hinges entirely on the forced proximity of two bickering, professionally compromised agents. *Home for the Homicides* leans heavily into the irony of a town stuck in permanent festive purgatory—a setting that feels less like a narrative choice and more like a claustrophobic cage where the lack of crime is treated as a plot hole rather than a reflection of human nature—and honestly, the thought of Holly and Mark dancing around their "ghosts of Christmas past" while trying to secure a promotion to Major Crimes is exhausting because it suggests the stakes are entirely centered on corporate advancement and sexual tension rather than the moral weight of the death itself. Where is the soul in a murder investigation that treats a dead tourist as a stepping stone for an HR review? Do authors truly believe we need more of these "sexy, light-hearted romps" when the world is currently fraying at the edges, or is this just another byproduct of the algorithmic obsession with marrying the Hallmark aesthetic to the police procedural, a subgenre that rarely manages to be either truly funny or remotely threatening? It feels like we are being sold a version of justice that comes pre-wrapped in tinsel, and I am not entirely sure I have the stomach to watch these two play house while they hunt a killer.

Trivial.

Honestly, it reminds me of that dreadful sequel to *The Night Circus* everyone kept forcing on me last winter—the one where the imagery was so thick you could choke on the prose, yet the characters had the emotional depth of a puddle in a drought. Why does contemporary fiction insist on making every romantic protagonist a hot mess with a "screwed up" career path? It’s as tiresome as watching my neighbor’s toddler try to eat a crayon for the fifth time this week while the parents just stare blankly at their phones. My own life is currently a series of unreturned emails and the lingering, bitter taste of a coffee that went cold three hours ago, and I find myself wondering if these characters—Frost and Shepherd—are just hollow vessels designed to keep us from noticing how thin the plot actually is. I would take a gritty, messy disaster over this polished, festive facade any day of the week, but then again, I suppose that’s the point, isn't it? 

I suspect this will read exactly like a mashup of Janet Evanovich’s chaotic, paycheck-to-paycheck charm and the sharper, moodier edges of a Tana French novel—if you sanded off all the grit and replaced it with peppermint-scented air freshener. It’s a bold marketing play to lean into the "will they/won't they" dynamic in a world where the stakes should, by all rights, be about blood, not promotions. My money says the mystery is entirely secondary to the bedroom antics.

Need to check the tire pressure on my wife’s car before her road trip.
