**Author:** Katee Robert  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-20 APL

The espresso machine is wheezing its death rattle right alongside my faith in contemporary fantasy. October air smells like damp pavement and dead ambition—fitting, considering we are about to plunge headfirst into another iteration of the YA-to-adult pipeline where nobody can just sit down and have a cup of coffee without someone threatening to execute them for treason against the Minor Court. Katee Robert is trading the neon-drenched marble of her *Dark Olympus* books for *The Tarot Trials*, a high-octane exercise in intellectual property recycling that promises shadow, illusion, and a whole lot of aggressively derivative world-building. 

### The Aristocratic Bloodsport Industrial Complex

We get it. The ruling elite are corrupt. The heroine is a scrappy underdog with a dead mom and a massive chip on her shoulder. Kestrel is stepping into the arena because apparently, statutory murder is just the local government’s preferred method of retirement planning. Yet another tournament narrative where betrayal is a question of *when*, not *if*—groundbreaking stuff, truly. I am clutching my chipped ceramic mug in sheer, unadulterated awe of this unprecedented literary architecture. The synopsis leans hard on the kind of vague, high-stakes gesturing that makes you wonder if an actual human wrote the plot or if a marketing algorithm just threw a dart at a board of tropes. Master her new powers of shadow and illusion? Oh, absolutely slay, queen, go ahead and overthrow the oppressive patriarchy of the major arcana while I wipe down this sticky counter for the fourth time today.

### The Aesthetic of Manufactured Dread

There is zero genuine terror here, just industrial-grade competence designed to keep suburban readers awake past midnight. Carissa Broadbent and V.E. Schwab have a lot to answer for, mostly because their stylistic progeny keep spawning these glossy, humorless power fantasies where every protagonist has a tragic backstory and a lethal aesthetic. Kestrel wants revenge on the fellow Champion who killed her mother, which sounds suspiciously like every single revenge arc published since the invention of the printing press. Give me some visceral, messy psychological horror where people actually lose their minds instead of gracefully navigating a poisonous game of alliances while maintaining their winged eyeliner. 

The prose will inevitably rely on tired signifiers of edginess—sharp cheekbones, brooding gazes, and magic systems that function entirely on vibes rather than internal logic. But hey, at least the book is coming out without DRM, so you can illegally download your corporate-manufactured escapism with zero friction. I am going to go sit on a park bench in the freezing drizzle and aggressively people-watch until someone makes eye contact with me for too long.
