**Author:** Emily Heil  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-06 APL

Some manuscripts arrive holding the exact weight of a quiet Tuesday night when the floorboards creak and you find yourself staring at the ceiling, wondering if the foundations are rotting away while the kids finally sleep upstairs. Emily Heil’s upcoming *A Pinch of Magic* promises precisely that brand of domestic escapism. A ghostwriter hiding in plain sight. A storm-wrecked grandmotherly estate. A spectral visitor demanding justice for a century-old slight. 

### The Recipe for Unfinished Business

Cam Parker is invisible by design. But invisibility is just fear wearing a tailored coat. The synopsis leans hard into the cozy paranormal playbook—think *Legends & Lattes* crossed with a Nora Ephron script, though let us be entirely honest: half these contemporary cozy entries feel manufactured in a sterile board meeting where an algorithm cross-referenced baking shows with local ghost tours. Yet, the premise hints at something slightly heavier beneath the sugar dusting. Grief, perhaps. Or the terrifying mandate of having to fix what broke while you were looking away. 

Actually, scratch that entire observation about algorithms. The culinary backdrop might just be an excuse for lazy prose, a crutch for authors who think mentioning an underbaked soufflé passes for emotional depth. 

Speaking of culinary disasters, why is it that every single animated cooking show my eight-year-old watches features a frantic raccoon dropping salt into the batter while screaming about time management? It makes absolutely zero sense. You have talking woodland creatures operating commercial-grade deep fryers without basic health inspections, and I am supposed to sit there nodding along pretending the physics hold up while my coffee goes stone cold and the drywall dust from the hallway patch job settles permanently into my lungs. 

Where was I? Right. Silver Cove. 

### Small-Town Scaffolding

The coastal-Virginia setting implies a specific kind of claustrophobia where everyone knows your middle name and your old debts. Enter Ian McCabe, the requisite carpenter with charm and complication written into his DNA. He is a walking red flag wrapped in flannel. Or maybe he is just a guy holding a hammer. The synopsis does not grant him enough dimension to tell the difference. 

The thematic core here revolves around control. Cam’s predictable world collapses because she lets in variables—ghosts, carpenters, hurricanes. Life refuses to stay inside the lines of a recipe card. We build these elaborate routines to keep the chaos at bay, pretending that if we just measure the flour right, the ceiling will not cave in and the kids will stay safe in their beds. Then a storm hits Brooklyn, or a Tuesday hits you sideways, and you are scrambling for a ghost to tell you what to do next. 

This book will likely land on bestseller lists this October. Readers crave the illusion of safety wrapped in supernatural whimsy. Whether Heil delivers actual emotional resonance or just recycled tropes remains to be seen from a three-paragraph blurb. 

I really need to get the plastic skeletons up on the front porch before my spouse loses patience and hires a neighborhood teenager to do it out of sheer pity.
