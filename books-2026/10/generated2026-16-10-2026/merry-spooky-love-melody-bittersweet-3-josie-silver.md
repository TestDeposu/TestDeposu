**Page Count:** Unknown

Bilateral negotiations in the Balkans taught me one immutable truth: stubborn bureaucrats will always choose utter ruin over admitting a spectral ledger sheet is balancing itself. Yet Josie Silver expects us to buy that a deceased retail matriarch named Lucy Libertine can haunt her own heirs out of a department store purely out of spite. 

### The Geopolitics of Retail Spite

Melody Bittersweet returns in *Merry Spooky Love* to arbitrate what looks like a hostile corporate takeover executed entirely by ectoplasm. And honestly? I respect the hustle. Real estate disputes usually involve tedious zoning commissions and miserable lawyers in charcoal suits. But throwing a dead aunt into the mix who refuses to let the capitalist machinery grind for sixty days? Utterly diabolical statecraft. 

Wait. Let me retract that entirely. The real estate clause is a bureaucratic nightmare, not statecraft. It is merely municipal probate law wrapped in cheap cotton shrouds. 

Silver attempts to fuse the saccharine architecture of holiday romance with the kind of logistical ghost-busting absurdity normally reserved for late-night cable syndication. It gives major Sophie Kinsella energy, if Kinsella suddenly decided to drop serious epistemic nihilism on her readership while keeping the prose aggressively mid. 

### The Skepticism Accord

The central diplomatic crisis here is not the ghost. It is Fletcher Gunn. The man refuses to acknowledge the paranormal despite dating an actual practitioner of the ethereal arts. A classic diplomatic impasse. How does one build a coalition with a partner who gaslights your very profession? Their chemistry is allegedly deep, yet the man calls in a rival ghost-hunter just to spite the domestic budget. Red flag? Perhaps. Or perhaps it is simply elite mid-tier relationship gaslighting for clout. 

The synopsis leans heavily on the ticking clock. Sixty days. Christmas Eve. A department store hanging by a metaphysical thread. It is high-stakes melodrama rendered in pastel shades. 

My vintage champagne collection needs to be moved to the new cellar.
