The coffee is cold again. Staring at the glowing screen while the laundry sits damp in the machine, I find myself looking at Ken Ballen’s upcoming *The Better Animal* and wondering if we really need another speculative warning about the collapse of democracy in the year 2040. Because nothing screams subtle political commentary quite like an authoritarian regime brought down by an election candidate who is literally a dog. Yes, you read that correctly. A canine politician, brought to us via artificial intelligence, leading the bruised souls of a fractured nation toward redemption. It sounds like a satirical column from *The Onion* that accidentally got stretched out into a full-length novel, yet here we are, pretending this is high-concept literature for fans of political fiction and LGBTQ stories. 

### Barking Up the Wrong Ballot Box

The premise hinges on a future America caught in the ruthless grip of corruption, where two men—Wing, a brilliant young scientist, and Jake, a hardened former presidential advisor—find themselves orbiting Kit the dog's revolutionary campaign while nursing their own profound personal trauma. Actually, let me rephrase that entirely. Perhaps reducing a sprawling political allegory to a gimmick about a presidential pooch is missing the point. Perhaps the talking point about a canine candidate is genius. Or maybe it is just utterly absurd. Who knows. Not my cup of tea.

The synopsis leans heavily on buzzwords like "redemption," "love," and "truth," which usually means the prose will either soar with poetic grace or drown in unearned sentimentality. We are promised a world where a non-human voice cuts through the noise of prejudice and hatred, offering a language humanity has never heard before. That is a heavy lift for a book that has yet to hit the shelves. Ballen wants to channel the biting dystopian bite of Margaret Atwood or perhaps the tender speculative humanism of Becky Chambers, yet the tightrope walk between political satire and earnest emotional healing looks precarious at best. 

### Waiting for the Howl

Can a story about a dog running for president truly capture the crushing weight of grief and political despair, or will it collapse under the sheer weight of its own conceit? The emotional architecture relies on Wing and Jake overcoming their respective pasts alongside their furry leader, which feels like a narrative choice designed to bypass human complexity through the emotional shortcut of an animal. We crave stories that help us make sense of the heavy, suffocating silence left behind by loss, and I am deeply skeptical that a canine AI savior is going to fill that specific void in my chest. 

Literally.

Hard pass.

The shouting just escalated past the hallway landing, so I have to go pry two small bodies apart before someone loses an eye over who gets to play with the plastic dinosaur.
