**Author:** Christina Henry  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-11-17 APL

Come closer to the hearth, little ones, and let your old grandfather tell you a tale about the terrifying things that happen when the old gods leave their feathers behind in the mortal cradles of modern suburbia, because Christina Henry is about to unleash *Pinfeathers* upon us in late 2026, and I can already smell the metaphorical ozone of ancient myths bleeding into sterile nursery drywall. We have heard these old whispers before—think of Angela Carter waking up from a fever dream or Shirley Jackson peering behind the chintz wallpaper of domestic bliss—where the miracle of childbirth mutates instantly into a chilling, avian metamorphosis that turns a mother's sweet lullaby into a desperate chant against the dark. And yet, looking strictly at the meager breadcrumbs of the official synopsis, I find myself violently skeptical of whether this modern literary bird can actually fly, or if it will simply crash-land into the tired, predictable tropes of internet-era paranoia, YouTube conspiracy rabbit holes, and the cinematic cliché of the hysterical maternal instinct pitted against the cold, logical gaslighting of doctors and grandmothers who clearly spend too much time doomscrolling through digital madness. 

The ancient Greeks knew a thing or two about winged monsters descending from the heavens to snatch away our peace, turning innocent babes into changelings or Harpies while the parents wept into their porridge, but I swear on my graying beard that if I have to read one more thriller where the central conflict hinges on whether Grandma is a QAnon lunatic or a prophetess of doom, I am going to feed this manuscript to the hearth fire. 

Silence.

### The Hatching of Dread

Nora Pacetti sits at the center of this domestic storm, allegedly protecting her little Olivia from sinister forces while sporting that classic, unshakeable stubbornness that we are supposed to applaud as sacred intuition even when every single rational adult in the room is begging for a pediatrician's referral. Henry has always excelled at dark folklore adaptations—look at how she twisted Peter Pan and Alice in Wonderland into jagged little teeth—so I harbor a begrudging, irrational hope that this upcoming horror will actually commit to the weirdness of avian biology instead of copping out with a boring psychological metaphor about postpartum anxiety or sleep deprivation-induced hallucinations. Give us the feathers, Christina. Give us the beak, the hollow bones, the terrifying urge to migrate away from the mortgage payments and the suburban lawnmowers. Do not dare waste our time with three hundred pages of bureaucratic medical gaslighting only to reveal it was all just a bad dream brought on by too much late-night screen time, because my old bones have survived enough literary bait-and-switches to know when an author is plucking our feathers just to watch us squawk. 

Bah.

I have to go scrounge together some cash for rent.
