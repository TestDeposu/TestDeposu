**Author:** Lene D. Buttner  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Thirty-six milliseconds. That is precisely how long my attention span survives before an author tries to repackage the exact same quirky-monster trope currently bankrupting mid-list publishers. *When Life Gives You Corpses* arrives courtesy of Lene D. Buttner in 2026, dragging its heavily marketed, cannibalistic little claws across the YA landscape like a bloated corpse drifting down the Mississippi after the 1927 flood. Everyone is suddenly obsessed with cozy body horror. Give me strength. 

### Cannibalism for Cowards

Let us examine the foundational architecture here. A cursed man-eating mantis working a day job in a magical sinkhole amusement park. Fascinating. Groundbreaking. Truly, Kafka wept. But wait—he only eats fingers. How delightfully sanitized for the TikTok generation. We refuse to commit to actual, visceral monstrosity anymore. Instead, we dilute our apex predators into misunderstood barista-types who occasionally snack on a cuticle. 

And then the missing wizard appears. Nim. Armed with optimism and an enchanted backpack. I can already hear the aggressive flipping of manuscript pages in suburban coffee shops. This is the literary equivalent of the Exxon Valdez disaster: slow, blindingly predictable, and drowning in corporate synergy designed to hit every single algorithmic requirement for a queer fantasy hit. 

### The Commodification of Snark

Notice how the synopsis leans entirely on blurbs rather than actual stakes. Sarah Beth Durst calls it charming. C. S. Pacat calls it a must-read. Naturally. Because calling a book about a human-eating insect dating a magical tourist anything less than a triumph of modern letters would get one blacklisted from the next seasonal catalog circuit. 

We are expected to believe that a subterranean death-trap doubling as an amusement park offers genuine narrative tension. Yet, the synopsis immediately defangs its own danger. Man-eating fish? Walls of tentacles? Pshaw. These are just neon-lit set pieces for what is ultimately a forced road trip between two characters who possess the emotional depth of a puddle in a Soho parking lot. They are stronger together than apart. Riveting. Pass the smelling salts. 

### The Predictable Trajectory

One can map the structural beats of this manuscript with a blindfold on and a martini in hand. First act: sarcastic misanthrope begrudgingly drags an overly optimistic twink through a dangerous theme park. Second act: forced proximity leads to lingering glances over a campfire of charred appendages. Third act: corporate conspiracy unraveled just in time for a wholesome, hand-holding finale that completely ignores the initial premise of, you know, eating people. 

If I wanted to read about a manipulative boss hiding devastating secrets while low-level employees suffer, I would simply review my quarterly P&L statements. At least those feature actual bloodshed. Buttner is undoubtedly talented at engineering marketable vibes for the TJ Klune demographic—bless their fragile little hearts—but manufactured coziness applied to body horror is like serving fois gras at a vegan daycare. 

My publicist is currently screaming outside my glass door about the morning press cycle.
