**Author:** Abigail Avis  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

London, 1969. The wallpaper is peeling and the air tastes faintly of coal dust and impending collapse. Abigail Avis brings us *Wet Ink*, a book about the quiet violence of suburban compliance. Mitzy Barlow sells airtight plastic containers to women whose lives are just as hermetically sealed. And honestly? I understand the existential panic of a Tupperware party. It feels remarkably similar to watching the neighborhood recycling bins overflow into the street while municipal funding for basic infrastructure quietly evaporates into nothingness. We are drowning in petroleum byproducts. At least Mitzy decides to use hers for subversion. 

### The Subversive Seal of Approval

The premise is brilliantly absurd. Slip dirty stories into the salad bowls. Give the housewives of the late sixties something to clutch other than their pearls. Comparisons to *Lessons in Chemistry* are inevitable, though I suspect this will be sharper, or at least smell more intensely of melting polymer. But why do we always demand that fiction about female liberation wrap itself in quirky humor? Can a housewife just be furious without being charming? Apparently not in publishing. They must have wit. They must have warmth. God forbid a domestic rebellion smells like sweat and genuine rage. 

### The Pseudonym Paradox

Queen B. It sounds like a mid-tier pop star or an invasive insect species currently chewing through local utility poles. The synopsis relies heavily on the threat of scandal—indecent conduct in a suburban cul-de-sac. Groundbreaking stakes. The Cold War is happening outside, labor strikes are looming, yet we are supposed to tremble at the thought of a bored mother writing fanfiction about *Jane Eyre* getting handled properly. Yet, I will read it. I will read it because the alternative is staring at the ceiling tiles counting the water stains until the grid finally fails completely. 

The washing machine just flooded the laundry room.
