**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-13 APL

Let us be entirely honest about what happens when a hype-machine tries to manufacture a bloodbath. Rachel Kitch drops *The Forest Bleeds* in October 2026, and already the marketing copy is throwing around comp titles like a promoter trying to sell a pay-per-view main event between two featherweights who haven't thrown a real punch since the weigh-in. *The Atlas Six* meets *Mexican Gothic*? Please. That is the literary equivalent of matching a champion against a journeyman on three days' notice just to fill out the undercard. 

### Footwork, Flinches, and Forced Confinements

Saige Chambers is supposedly this chronically over-prepared blood witch, which sounds suspiciously like a fighter who spends twelve weeks shadowboxing in a hoodie only to gas out the second someone steps inside her guard and starts winging heavy hooks. Yet the premise relies on a classic trapped-in-the-gym trope—bus turns off the highway, gates slam shut, and suddenly everybody is playing defense. Where is the counter-punch? When a guy with a rotting leg tells you to fix his marrow or take a dirt nap, you do not stand around debating group dynamics. You check your footing, you control the center of the ring, and you exploit the opponent's glaring mobility issues. 

### The Grapple Game and Psychosexual Clinches

The synopsis leans hard into the psychological clinch—long-term crushes, reluctant attractions to rotting landlords, and colleagues vanishing like prelim fighters whose contracts just quietly expired. But here is where the matchup falls apart structurally. If you are locked in a cage with a man whose flesh is literally melting off the bone and emitting foul magic, romance should be the absolute last thing on your radar unless you enjoy getting choked out from full guard. The power dynamics here smell less like a high-level grappling exchange and more like sloppy choreography where nobody knows who is calling the shots. Vaishnavi Sri is over there as a poison witch, and Saige is busy developing ocular lock-on for the creepy boss. Classic mistake. You never take your eyes off the striking coach. 

### The Championship Rounds Prediction

Expect this book to drag through the middle frames like a heavyweight title fight that went ten rounds too long. The synopsis hides behind vague atmospheric threats—far worse fates lurking in the woods—because it lacks the knockout power to show us what those fates actually are. It is all build-up, zero impact, like throwing feints for three straight rounds and wondering why the crowd is booing. Kitch wants a submission win, but she is telegraphing every single movement from across the canvas. 

I am going to stare at a blank canvas until inspiration strikes.
