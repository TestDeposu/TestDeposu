**Author:** Gerardo Sámano Córdova  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Hold onto the brass pole because the subway is slamming into the 14th Street station and my coffee is about to stain my jeans. Yet my eyes are locked onto this screen. Gerardo Sámano Córdova is following up his absolute beast of a debut *Monstrilio* with *Imps!* and I am already sweating through my trench coat. Look. The synopsis gives us almost nothing about the actual plot—just a guy knocking on a door, an estranged twin brother named Pablo, and twenty years of baggage packed into Mexico City. Classic teaser bullshit. But do I care? Absolutely not. 

### The Architecture of a Freakout

Córdova specializes in gorgeous pathologies. Alejandro Varela called him that, and Varela doesn't miss. What we are looking at here is a pressure cooker. FRANCISCA FINDS JOY. And then a weird guy shows up. Boom. Thirty-five years of sibling resentment detonates right on cue. It is claustrophobic. It is feverish. Actually, scratch that—it sounds less like a claustrophobic trap and more like a hallucinogenic bender that will probably make me cancel my weekend plans and stare at my own walls. 

The synopsis screams trickery and broken bones. I need blood. Or at least some serious psychological carnage. *Monstrilio* gave us raw, bleeding grief wrapped in body horror, so expecting a quiet family drama here is like expecting a quiet ride on the L train at rush hour. It is going to derail. It has to. Córdova deals in the grotesque and the tender, smashing them together until you cannot tell if you want to throw up or cry. 

### Twins, Trickery, and Total Collapse

And let us talk about the twins trope. Usually, it is cheap soap opera garbage. Think late-night cable junk or cheap thrillers meant for airports. But with Córdova? Expect something closer to Mariana Enríquez or Agustina Bazterrica crashing headfirst into Shirley Jackson. The twenty-year gap in the sibling dynamic means whatever broke them apart when they were teenagers is finally coming home to collect interest. And interest rates are sky-high. 

The text promises monsters, mischief, and mayhem. Give me all three. Give me the weird stranger standing on the terrace while the sparrows squabble. Let the Mexico City heat bake these broken adults until they crack open. I am betting right now this book will be banned in three southern states by autumn 2026. Put money on it. Or do not. I am too busy gripping the handrail as the train violently jerks to a halt and some tourist steps directly onto my boot. 

The anticipation is legitimately exhausting. I cannot read another bland corporate thriller about a missing wife or a dead body in a suburban yard. We need weird. We need fiction that tastes like copper and cheap tequila. Córdova is carving out his own bizarre corner of contemporary horror, and *Imps!* looks like the sledgehammer he is using to expand it. 

I am going to go drink absinthe and pretend I am in 1920s Paris.
