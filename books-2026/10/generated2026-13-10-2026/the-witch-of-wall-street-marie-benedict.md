Another historical fiction packet lands on the counter, smelling faintly of burnt oat milk and corporate desperation. Marie Benedict is back at it, digging up another dusty Victorian skeleton and slapping a feminist gloss on the spine. Because heaven forbid we write a horror novel about actual blood-drinking ghouls when we have the robber barons of the Gilded Age. The marketing copy for *The Witch of Wall Street* promises us Hetty Green—a woman so ruthless she out-fucking-maneuvered Carnegie and Morgan. 

### The Gilded Age Ledger of Doom

Look, the synopsis hits every single formulaic beat of the genre. Rebellious woman? Check. Patriarchy-defying business acumen? Check. A "shocking betrayal" that hardens her soul into a cold, diamond-cut shell? Double check. It reads like a checklist manufactured in a fluorescent-lit conference room by people who think a good scare is a slightly lower quarterly dividend. And yet. There is something deliciously bleak about the nickname "The Witch of Wall Street." They called her a witch simply because she beat men at their own rigged game of capitalistic vampirism. 

Wait. Let me backtrack on calling it entirely formulaic. Honestly, a cold-hearted miser who hoards wealth while society shrieks at her autonomy sounds infinitely more terrifying than half the ghosts haunting contemporary horror shelves anyway. 

God give me strength. I am staring at these three paragraphs of publisher-mandated hype while the espresso machine hisses like a dying lung, and I am supposed to stretch this thin soup into an analytical masterpiece just to pad my word count. Why do I even agree to write these previews? Nobody reads past the second sentence. They just want to know if Hetty saves New York from financial ruin or if she turns into a literal monster. 

### The Economics of Isolation

The thematic spine here rests entirely on the horror of absolute independence. Benedict clearly wants us to see Hetty's isolation as a triumph of grit. But read between the marketing lines. A woman surrounded by titanic monsters of industry, choosing to trust no one after a betrayal, building a fortress of gold coins just to keep the cold world out? That is pure Gothic isolationism. It is Edgar Allan Poe in a corset and a bustle. Forget the sanitized biography angle. Give me the psychological rot of a person who traded human warmth for a ticker tape. 

I predict the narrative will soften her too much. Historical fiction lovers demand a redemption arc, a tender moment where the fierce mother redeems the ruthless titan. They refuse to let a woman simply be a monster of her own making. They always have to explain her away with a tragic backstory. 

The rain is starting to smear the chalk menu outside the window, and I have fifty more words to grind out before I can close this cursed laptop. I need to get out of here. I must ride my bicycle through the rain to clear my writer's block.
