**Author:** Kevin Maurer  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Another biography about a man who refuses to die, just what my mahogany-paneled library was lacking. Kevin Maurer is rolling the dice on *Code Name Dagger*, a forthcoming 2026 slice of historical non-fiction centering on Major Jerry Sage. A Canadian-born operative who collected fifteen prison escape attempts like merit badges, earning the absurd moniker "Cooler King." Naturally, Hollywood got its grubby paws on his legacy first, morphing him into the blueprint for Steve McQueen's cinematic sprint toward freedom. 

### The Aesthetics of Stubbornness

Or perhaps I mean his sheer, unadulterated annoyance to the Third Reich. The synopsis tells us he harassed the Nazis until the war's final curtain dropped, which sounds exhaustingly petty. I can respect that level of spite. It reminds me of the time the board tried to oust me from the shipping conglomerate and I simply bought out their mistresses' art galleries. Power is about outlasting your captors, after all. Maurer is promising us fresh intel on Sage's stint in a German POW camp and his tangled web within William "Wild Bill" Donovan's precursor to the OSS. 

Yet, let us be entirely honest here. I care very little about the OSS. In fact, intelligence agencies are terribly pedestrian organizations staffed by men who peaked during college rowing season. 

Actually, wait. Scratch that. Donovan’s early outfit had a chaotic, amateur-hour charm that corporate America lost somewhere around the seventies. It reads less like an elite espionage ring and more like the infamous Fyre Festival logistics meeting—a complete disaster executed with absolute, terrifying confidence. 

### Shadows and Solitary Confinement

What interests me is the claustrophobia. Fifteen sentences in solitary confinement. That is psychological torture dialed to an exquisite, suffocating pitch. The synopsis mentions he slipped through the cracks of history—a legend swallowed by time. Good. Most legends deserve to molder. They are too clean, too scrubbed of the gray moral rot that makes human suffering actually readable. When I want grit, I reach for Joe Abercrombie or Richard Morgan, where characters bleed real bile instead of patriotic slogans. Maurer is tackling a true story, meaning he is shackled to the facts. No severed heads, no nihilistic gods, just a bloke crawling through barbed wire because he hates taking orders. 

One wonders if Sage's incessant escape artistry was a symptom of a deeper malady, or merely an extreme manifestation of boredom. Billionaires understand boredom intimately. It is a quiet, rotting sickness that makes you fund deep-sea submersibles or write check-lists for your enemies. Sage just chose a more kinetic outlet. 

Pre-orders are already open for those who need a surrogate personality. I will leave it on the yacht for the deckhands to skim while I stare blankly at the horizon, waiting to feel something. 

Excuse me, I have to fire a ghostwriter who severely misunderstood my creative vision regarding my memoirs.
