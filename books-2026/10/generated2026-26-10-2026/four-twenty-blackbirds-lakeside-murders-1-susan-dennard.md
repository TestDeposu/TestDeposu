Nursery rhymes have long served as the macabre lullabies of choice for anyone wishing to curdle the blood before bedtime. Susan Dennard leans into this bruised-fruit aesthetic with *Four & Twenty Blackbirds*, a forthcoming exercise in small-town malice that promises the atmospheric dread of a rainy autumn evening without requiring actual frost on the ground just yet. 

### The Calculus of Cliché
Wren Peters is constructed from the spare parts of every thriller heroine to ever walk a paperback page. A dancer and a math nerd? Truly, the author decided to empty the entire occupational drawer into one unfortunate teenager. We are expected to swallow the tired cadence of generational feuds, blue-eyed adversaries, and visions that plague the protagonist at the exact worst narrative juncture. Rival families locked in ancient hatreds while their progeny trade smoldering glances across frozen lakes. How original. One can practically hear the editorial boardroom clapping themselves on the back for matching *Twin Peaks* kitsch with the neon angst of *Riverdale*. 

Wait. Let me retract my disdain for the lineage trope. Perhaps the archaic feud holds a certain savage poetry after all, provided Dennard avoids the soggy middle that ruined half of Marisha Pessl's earlier output. 

### The Frozen Rhetoric of Suspicion
The prose relies heavily on nursery rhyme dread—that childish sing-song cadence meant to evoke the uncanny. Yet the synopsis reads less like chilling literature and more like a checklist manufactured by a Focus Group for Young Adult Despair. The missing siblings, the cryptic dreams bleeding into waking hours, the undeniable friction between characters whose surnames carry decades of bad blood. It is formulaic. It is derivative. It wears its influences like cheap velvet at a damp October garden party. 

### The Atmospheric Pretension
Give me genuine poetic terror or give me nothing at all. Give me the linguistic precision of Shirley Jackson instead of this glossy, highly marketable approximation of darkness. But I must confess, despite my better judgment, the promise of a blustery island setting hits precisely right as the October wind rattles the French doors downstairs. I despise myself for wanting to see how the pie metaphor holds up. 

I have to log off now and mentally prepare for my upcoming interview with *Vogue*.
