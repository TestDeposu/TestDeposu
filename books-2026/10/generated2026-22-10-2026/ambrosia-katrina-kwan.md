**Author:** Katrina Kwan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

There is something deeply exhausting about the modern literary obsession with deception as a substitute for emotional intelligence, yet *Ambrosia* by Katrina Kwan lands on the upcoming 2026 horizon promising a dark academia murder mystery that forces a woman to disguise herself as a man just to uncover who killed her husband, Zhen, after finding gold in his veins at the University of Quartë. 

And yet, watching a protagonist rely on elaborate subterfuge rather than direct, honest communication makes my teeth ache with the memory of past heartaches where people simply refused to talk to each other like functioning adults who respect mutual vulnerability. 

Because when partnerships are built on buried secrets—as the synopsis heavily hints Zhen kept from Li before his unfortunate demise in a frozen river—everything inevitably collapses into toxic resentment, cold academic ambition, and literal murder. 

Why must fictional spouses always hide their dangerous alchemic extracurriculars in the basement instead of sitting down over a cup of tea to discuss their boundary issues? 

It gives off major Rebecca Kuang's *The Poppy War* meets Donna Tartt vibes, wrapped in the tired, patriarchal framing of historical gatekeeping where women are forbidden from studying higher sciences unless they glue on a fake mustache. 

Honestly, if a man cannot even be honest about his extracurricular gold-veined murder plots, how can anyone ever trust a tenured position in the Order of Alchemic Science? 

Communication is the bedrock of any lasting commitment, not cross-dressing espionage.

Tired.

My youngest just painted the family cat blue.
