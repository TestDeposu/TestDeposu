**Author:** Christopher Goffard  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-12-08 APL

October brings a damp, biting chill that seeps right through the windowpanes, making you want to wrap a heavy quilt around every fragile fictional soul in Southern California before some literary sadist manages to break them. Christopher Goffard is apparently preparing to do just that with *The Windowman*, a forthcoming narrative about occult slayings, a bungled prosecution, and the kind of nationwide manhunt that makes you want to reach into the pages, grab the characters by their shoulders, and scream at them to lock their doors and throw away the keys. We are promised a trio of focal points—a devoted Orange County schoolteacher, a tormented prosecutor, and a hapless method actor desperate for a breakout role—all orbiting a vanished killer named Damon Midnight like moths flying straight into a blowtorch. 

Poor Annabelle Picolini. She loves a man accused of occult slayings, which is a staggering display of poor romantic judgment that instantly triggers every protective instinct I possess, because we all know the literary universe has zero mercy for people who see the good in monsters. And poor Blaire Belmont, the pathetic actor trying to inhabit the psyche of a predator just for a movie part, walking around wearing a murderer's skin like an ill-fitted thrift store jacket. 

Idiocy.

Or maybe pure genius. On second thought, let us backtrack entirely on calling Blaire an idiot, because the synopsis explicitly frames this as a wry, gallows-humor-infused satire on our collective, nauseating obsession with true crime and fame. Goffard is channeling the asphalt grit of James Ellroy and the sardonic rhythm of Elmore Leonard, trading cozy comfort for sweat-drenched asphalt and media circuses. But I swear, if Annabelle gets hurt because she brought a casserole to the wrong suburban patio, I am personally driving down to Orange County to dismantle the entire publishing industry piece by piece. 

These characters deserve better. They deserve quiet afternoons drinking lukewarm tea under a sun that doesn't beat down on a backdrop of grisly headlines. Instead, they are trapped in a twisty psychological crime novel where the clues to the killer's fate are scattered across public faces and private lies like broken glass. The synopsis leaves the exact page count a mystery, which only heightens the paranoid dread of watching innocent bystanders orbit a void. We are looking at a brutal dissection of celebrity worship wrapped in procedural suspense, and while the literary world will undoubtedly lap up the dark comedy, my heart remains firmly lodged in my throat for the fictional fools who think they can play with fire without getting burned to ash. 

Ouch.
