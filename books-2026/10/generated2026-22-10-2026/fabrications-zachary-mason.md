**Author:** Zachary Mason  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-12-01 APL

Outside, the drizzle refuses to quit, hammering against the windowpane with the steady, dull persistence of a migraine. Zach Mason’s *Fabrications* lands on the desk like yet another monument to masculine anxiety over the future, because heaven forbid men imagine the apocalypse without centering their own cleverness. 

### The Silicon Patriarchs

We are promised a Borges-esque labyrinth of artificial minds, but let us be entirely serious for a single second. The synopsis leans hard on the tired trope of the benevolent machine, framing these entities as detached gods watching over us messy mortals. And what do these omnipotent intelligences do with their boundless epochs? Well, naturally, they manage hedge funds, dole out medical nostalgia to addled patriarchs, and draft legal loopholes for mother nature. Groundbreaking. Nothing screams peak male philosophy quite like reducing the cosmos to a Silicon Valley venture capitalist’s wet dream—giving nature personhood so we can probably sue a hurricane for breach of contract, lmao. 

Yet the pedigree is undeniably heavy. We get the obligatory nods to Calvino and Ishiguro, dropping literary names like designer labels on a thrift-store coat to bully the reader into reverence. But wait—let me back up. Perhaps I am being too vicious before the ink has even dried. Mason is a computer scientist turned novelist, which usually translates to prose that smells aggressively of cold server rooms and unwashed hoodies. Still, the premise of an implanted medical program keeping a woman with dementia tethered to her fading self holds a quiet, devastating gravity. That is the one vignette holding up this entire structural house of cards. 

### The Gendered Machine

Who gets to be the ghost in the machine? Always the caretaker. Always the maternal proxy or the cold corporate ledger. The synopsis drops names like Ted Chiang and Karen Russell as if invoking speculative royalty will magically blind us to the stale power dynamics at play. A hedge fund manager clones himself across the centuries because his ego is apparently too immense to dissolve into the heat death of the universe. Honestly? Tragic. Or perhaps just desperately cliché. The text promises a nuanced interrogation of alien intellects, but my money is on yet another narrative where women exist primarily as memory fragments for men to salvage or mourn. 

The prose style will undoubtedly coast on clever conceptual framing while starving us of actual visceral grit. Give me messy, bleeding human agency over sterile computational philosophy any day of the week. 

I am going to spend the evening reading Sylvia Plath by candlelight.
