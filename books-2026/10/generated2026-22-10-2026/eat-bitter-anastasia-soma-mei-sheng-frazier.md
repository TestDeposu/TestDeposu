**Author:** Soma Mei Sheng Frazier  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Twenty-two years old and staring down the barrel of a modern art museum assistantship while your own creative ambitions rot in a desk drawer? Groundbreaking. Soma Mei Sheng Frazier is dropping *Eat Bitter, Anastasia* in 2026, and I am already exhausted by the sheer volume of quarter-life exhaustion radiating from the publisher's press release. 

### The Architecture of Stuckness

We get it. Oakland is vibrant. Gentrification hurts. Victorian houses in Chinatown have black mold and sentimental baggage. Yet the synopsis leans heavily on the kind of manic-pixie-caretaker tropes that usually populate late-night cable dramas rather than substantial contemporary fiction. Anastasia Ān Xiá Huang inherits a fixer-upper with her wisecracking best friend—because of course the best friend has to be a wisecracker, otherwise how would we know to laugh through the tears?—and suddenly we are knee-deep in childhood trauma and absentee fathers. 

Wait. Let me retract that severity for a second. Actually, the art school angle might save it. Or ruin it entirely. 

### Palette Cleansers and Pedagogy

No, scratch that optimism. I refuse to believe a book featuring an "irrepressible elderly neighbor" and a "resurfaced childhood crush" can avoid the gravity well of every coming-of-age cliché written since the Clinton administration. It’s giving me heavy flashbacks to the stack of YA paperbacks left behind in the back row by kids who are supposed to be reading *The Great Gatsby* instead of whispering about their weekend drama. If I have to read one more sentence about a protagonist "caught between loving and leaving" their hometown, I am going to throw a dry-erase marker across the room. 

Frazier is channeling the spirit of Celeste Ng crossed with a slightly more caffeinated Kevin Kwan, minus the wealth porn. But is there actual literary bite here? Or just another soft-focus Instagram grid of grief and gentrification wrapped in pastel cover art? The synopsis hides behind buzzwords like *bracing revelations* and *colorful ode*. Give me specifics. Give me jagged edges. Do not smooth over the trauma with a fresh coat of low-VOC paint and a quirky old person next door. 

Art school applications as a ticking clock are a weak inciting incident. Real twenty-somethings do not map out their existential crises this neatly. They scroll through apartment listings until their eyes bleed and eat cereal for dinner. Still, I will be buying it the day it drops, mostly because I need to know if the mysterious street artist is just a metaphor for her estranged father. It always is. 

I need to go grade a stack of quizzes and pretend I understand a calculus assignment involving limits that look like ancient runes.
