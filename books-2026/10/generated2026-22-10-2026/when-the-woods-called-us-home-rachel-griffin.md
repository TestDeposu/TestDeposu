**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Another autumn, another dreary Pacific Northwest export about women with mysterious forest-borne curses and missing relatives who clearly just wanted to escape the local artisanal coffee prices. Rachel Griffin is back at it with *When the Woods Called Us Home*, a text designed to be read exclusively while wearing an oversized flannel sweater you bought ironically at a vintage boutique in Silver Lake. 

### Arborial Melodrama and Other Forestry Hazards

The premise rests entirely on Emma Pierce, a woman who spends her working hours hanging out one hundred feet up in wind-battered trees because apparently standard terrestrial real estate lacks sufficient existential dread. Emma climbs to escape a family curse that claims the lives of Pierce women. Naturally. Because if your lineage comes with a lethal expiration date, the logical response is to dangle yourself from a piece of nylon rope. 

The synopsis breathlessly informs us that a local disappearance rattles the small town of Bone Creek. A boy named Parker vanishes. Eli’s brother, to be exact. And suddenly everyone—including Emma’s sister and boyfriend—is under suspicion. But wait. Let me backtrack for a second. Actually, no, the boyfriend’s brother vanishing is the least interesting thing here. The real victim is the English language, battered to a pulp by marketing copywriters who desperately need to invent new ways to describe damp foliage and generations-old grudges.

I’m probably breaking an embargo by saying this, but half of these rural gothic thrillers are written using a mad-libs generator that pulls random nouns from a hat labeled "Moody Nature Tropes."

### Unresolved Baggage and Forgotten Memories

We are promised a deep dive into forgotten childhood memories from the night Emma’s mother died. Because no contemporary thriller can exist without the mandatory repressed memory trope acting as the narrative crutch. It is the literary equivalent of leaving your dirty dishes in the sink for three weeks and hoping they evolve into a functional ecosystem. 

The comparison to *Once There Were Wolves* is doing heavy lifting here. It’s doing Olympic-level gymnastics. Every publisher in Manhattan apparently believes that adding pine trees and a family feud automatically elevates a manuscript to prestige status. Yet we are left with the same tired beats: a brooding protagonist, a town harboring dark secrets, and a climax that will inevitably involve someone running through the dark woods while a twig snaps menacingly in the background. Riveting stuff for people who find suburban lawncare too thrilling.

Honestly, why am I even analyzing the marketing boilerplate for a book that won't hit shelves until 2026? I am literally just typing words into a glowing rectangle to satisfy an arbitrary invoice quota while my oat milk latte goes lukewarm on a desk stacked too high with rejection slips and past-due utility bills. 

I have to go deal with my landlord now. The ceiling leak in the bathroom is currently mimicking a picturesque Washington rainstorm, minus the moody atmosphere.
