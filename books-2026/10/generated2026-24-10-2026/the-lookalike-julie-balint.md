**Author:** Julie Balint  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Thirty-three hormonal adolescents spent the last six hours trying to convince me that the Pythagorean theorem is merely a social construct. Because of this, my soul has permanently detached from my body. So when I look at Julie Balint’s upcoming psychological thriller, *The Lookalike*, I am searching for sheer, unadulterated escapism. Give me an absurd premise or give me death. 

### The Grand Rebranding of Random Nobodies

The premise hinges on twenty-five-year-old Kristen Wells—a broke, profoundly bored nobody from Louisiana—looking at a missing person's broadcast, spotting her near-perfect double, Nova Kennedy, and deciding to casually swap lives like changing a pair of socks. Because nothing screams foolproof logic quite like impersonating a wealthy missing socialite from a prominent Cape Cod family. And why not? People do it on television all the time. Actually, let me correct that; nobody in their right mind does this unless they have a severe concussion or a death wish. But let us suspend our collective disbelief for the sake of the genre. 

Balint leans heavily into the classic imposter syndrome trope, weaponizing the irresistible fantasy of shedding an unremarkable existence to step into a designer wardrobe. Or perhaps she doesn't. The synopsis relies entirely on the kind of vague, punchy marketing-speak that leaves us guessing whether this will read like Patricia Highsmith’s *The Talented Mr. Ripley* or a very confused episode of *Gossip Girl*. 

### Identity Theft as Extreme Self-Care

Identity replacement remains the ultimate literary indulgence. We all secretly wonder who we would be if we just got on a plane, dyed our hair, and started over with a fake backstory. Yet, Kristen takes this impulse to a genuinely unhinged extreme by walking right into the icy dysfunction of the victim's actual family. 

The psychological weight here should theoretically explore class resentment. Yet I predict it will devolve into a messy, melodramatic soap opera where nobody notices that their beloved daughter suddenly has a completely different vocal cadence and an accent from the bayou. People are remarkably unobservant when they are blinded by inherited wealth and grief. 

### The Inevitable Threat of Anonymous Post-its

Naturally, anonymous threatening notes appear immediately because no thriller can function without someone sliding ominous parchment under a mahogany door. Someone knows Kristen’s secret. They always do. It's terribly cliché, but honestly, at this point, I welcome the lazy writing. Give me blackmail. Give me red herrings. Just keep it moving before the bell rings and I have to confiscate three more vapes. 

This book is going to sell a million copies in airport terminals next summer, regardless of whether the plot holds together under basic scrutiny. Readers love a messy protagonist making aggressively terrible decisions. I am going to buy a hardcover copy the second it drops. No, wait, I take that back; I am going to ignore it completely out of spite because the synopsis refuses to explain how Kristen magically alters her facial bone structure to match a missing heiress. 

My grading red pen is currently missing, buried somewhere beneath a pile of confiscated cell phones. I am going to walk outside, drink a very cold beer on the porch, and stare blankly at the lawn until my brain cells regenerate.
