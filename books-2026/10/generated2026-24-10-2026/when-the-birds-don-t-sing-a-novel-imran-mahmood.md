**Author:** Imran Mahmood  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-12-01 APL

Another thriller about a middle school fire and a misplaced lighter. Groundbreaking. We are truly living through the cinematic universe of the Costa Concordia captain, just handing over our safety gear to the nearest bully because God forbid we cause a scene. Imran Mahmood is back with *When the Birds Don't Sing*, and the synopsis reads like a deposition from a very tedious corporate compliance seminar wrapped in the damp wool of a coming-of-age trauma. 

### The Mycology of Regret

Caleb Silver is a London mycology professor now. His wife is dead. Mushrooms grow in decay, which is about as subtle as a sledgehammer to the glass conservatory. Mahmood wants us to care about a decades-old arson investigation tied to a middle school lighter. But let us be honest for a fraction of a second. This whole premise is nothing to write home about. Actually, wait. I take that back completely. The mycology angle is genuinely inspired—there is something deliciously bleak about a man spending his adult life studying subterranean rot because he handed over a plastic flame in the seventh grade. 

### Justice Served Lukewarm

Revenge is a dish best served cold, or in this case, cultivated in a damp subterranean container alongside oyster mushrooms. The dual-timeline gimmick is exhausted. Every author with a word processor thinks jumping between a sweaty gymnasium and a quiet British study constitutes literary architecture. Yet the synopsis promises accountability and the cost of silence. Spare me the moral posturing. I read grimdark when I want characters who peel each other's skin off for sport, not middle-aged academics agonizing over playground cowardice. Give me Joe Abercrombie or give me nothing. 

The net tightens. Secrets unearth. The usual marketing filler. If Honey Childs—what a wretched name, by the way—actually knew who started the fire all those years ago, why wait until her former classmate's wife dies to make a fuss? The logistics are absurd. But plot holes are standard issue in these domestic suspense packages. 

My driver is currently making a complete mess of the approach to the Hamptons, crawling through this dreadful standstill on the Long Island Expressway while I stare at my phone reading about middle school arsonists.
