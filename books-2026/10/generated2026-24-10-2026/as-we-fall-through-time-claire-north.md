The kids just dumped half a bag of unwashed chocolate chips directly onto the floured counter, and honestly, the sheer audacity matches the premise of Claire North’s upcoming novel, *As We Fall Through Time*. 

### Playing God with the French Revolution

Chronological tampering always brings out the absolute worst in protagonists. Cal thinks he can simply nudge history like a stray rolling pin off the edge of a kitchen table to save a doomed revolutionary lover. But meddling with centuries never goes well. It is messy. It is exhausting. Frankly, if I had to deal with historical butterfly effects every single time I tried to bake a decent sourdough brioche, I would simply give up and buy sliced white bread from the corner store. 

Speaking of grocery store disappointments, why is every modern television adaptation of cozy mysteries turning into a bleak, grey, Nordic-noir bloodbath where nobody even owns a whisk? My six-year-old asked me yesterday why Miss Marple never uses an electric stand mixer, and I had to explain the historical implications of Victorian kitchen appliances for twenty agonizing minutes while my batch of snickerdoodles burned to a crisp in the oven. 

North ignores all kitchen logic entirely. Instead, she throws us into a dizzying temporal loop where Fadimatu discovers her own mummified corpse in a museum in 2018. 

### The Ultimate Paradox of Memory

Death before its time? A murder that hasn't happened yet? It gives off massive *The First Fifteen Lives of Harry August* energy, which is precisely why pre-orders are currently sitting at the top of my mental priority list, right above buying vanilla bean paste. The synopsis leans heavily on the agonizing weight of predetermined ends. Can affection survive structural ruptures in the timeline? Probably not. But we are here for the emotional wreckage anyway. 

And yet, the promotional blurbs comparing this to Adrian Tchaikovsky and Kate Quinn feel slightly aggressive. Do we really need twenty blurbs screaming that a book is an absolute masterpiece before the ink is even dry on the manuscript? Let the prose breathe, people. Let the temporal paradoxes speak for themselves without shouting from the dust jacket. 

My noise-canceling headphones are failing completely because the subway rumble beneath the floorboards is vibrating straight up through my chair legs today.
