**Author:** Andi Epimoni  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-12-01 APL

The wind’s got a way of carrying the stench of last night’s takeout straight into the living room—someone’s left a half-eaten burrito in the corner like it’s a trophy. I’m here, pretending to listen to my cousin’s third marriage proposal (this one involving a "soul contract" with a tarot reader), while my phone buzzes with a synopsis that’s so slick it might as well be a love arrow itself. *Lovestruck* isn’t just a book—it’s a middle finger to the idea that romance novels have to be either saccharine or soul-crushing. Andi Epimoni’s got the audacity to make us care about a breaker and an archer, two people whose jobs are literally to ruin and create love, respectively. The synopsis doesn’t tell us much—just enough to make you lean in, like a bad habit you can’t quit.

The premise is a delightful inversion of the "enemies to lovers" trope, but with a cosmic twist. Demi, the breaker, is the human equivalent of a relationship therapist with a gun—her job is to end love stories that would otherwise spiral into disaster. Gray, the archer, is the golden boy of fate, the guy who shoots love arrows and watches couples fall in perfect harmony. But when Gray misfires—because apparently, even gods of love can have a bad aim day—the whole system’s about to collapse. Now Demi, who’s spent her life dismantling love, has to team up with her ex (the one who broke her heart, because of course) to fix Gray’s mistake. And here’s the kicker: fixing someone else’s love story forces Demi to confront her own.

The synopsis doesn’t give us the emotional core, but it’s implied—Demi’s got a heart that’s been broken before, and now she’s being forced to face the truth about why. It’s the kind of setup that makes you think of *The Love Hypothesis* meets *The Break-Up*, but with the existential dread of *Normal People* if it were written by a person who’s seen too many bad reality TV shows. The tension isn’t just about saving the world (or whatever this otherworldly consequence is)—it’s about whether Demi can separate her professional detachment from her personal pain. And that’s the real hook: a romance where the love story isn’t just about the couple in the spotlight, but about the people who’ve spent their lives trying to control it.

The problem with the synopsis? It’s too vague. We don’t know what the "consequences of otherworldly proportions" are—are we talking apocalyptic love overload, or just a really bad breakup? Is Demi’s ex still a threat, or is this a redemption arc? The lack of detail makes it feel like a placeholder for something bigger, which is either a sign of ambition or a sign that Epimoni’s saving the juicy bits for the book. Either way, it’s frustrating because the potential is so high. Imagine if this were written by Emily Henry—same sharp wit, same ability to make you root for the messy parts—but with the mythic scale of *The Song of Achilles*. Instead, we’ve got a synopsis that’s more tease than feast.

And then there’s the meta joke: a breaker and an archer, two people whose jobs are literally to ruin and create love, forced to work together. It’s the kind of setup that makes you wonder if Epimoni’s poking fun at the very idea of fate and free will. Is love something you can control, or is it just chaos with a bow on it? The synopsis doesn’t answer that, but it’s the kind of question that makes you want to keep reading.

I’m supposed to be writing this review, but honestly, I’d rather be anywhere else. The cousin’s still talking about "energy frequencies," and the burrito’s starting to smell like regret. Maybe *Lovestruck* will be the escape I need—if it delivers on the promise of being "imaginative, fun, and the perfect escape from reality," then it’s got a shot. But if it’s just another love story dressed up in cosmic fantasy, well, then I’ve got a pantry to hide in for five minutes of peace.
