**Author:** Chinaza Bado  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-11-24 APL

Nobody organizes a tenant union on a Tuesday evening without expecting the city council to suddenly rewrite the zoning laws just to spite us, which brings us squarely to the structural violence inherent in *The Call of Crowns*. Chinaza Bado drops us right back into Ahkelbulin—presumably a geographic nightmare of corrugated iron estates and absentee landlords living in high-security gated compounds while the rest of the neighborhood shares a single functioning water pump—where Khalil and Zikora are once again stuck under the heavy thumb of a dynastic ruling class that treats human lives like disposable equity. And look, I have read this exact architectural critique of unchecked monarchical power a hundred times before, usually dressed up in the same tired velvet curtains and poisoned wine goblets that George R.R. Martin popularized before he decided to spend the rest of his natural life refusing to finish a manuscript, or perhaps more accurately, like a bloated fantasy adaptation of *Succession* where everyone wears too much leather and talks endlessly about blood oaths instead of corporate hostile takeovers. 

Palace intrigue. 

The synopsis tells us absolutely nothing concrete, relying instead on a relentless barrage of marketing buzzwords—viper’s nest, high-stakes game, warring factions, ancient powers—that could literally describe any mid-tier epic fantasy released by a desperate mid-sized imprint trying to capture the elusive crossover market currently dominated by Rebecca Yarros and her endless supply of winged reptiles. Zikora is "far from home and concealing a powerful secret," which is authorial shorthand for a protagonist who will inevitably spend three hundred pages staring blankly out of stained-glass windows while hiding a glowing birthmark or a forbidden magic spark, while poor Khalil is trapped in the middle trying to balance his ethics against a paycheck, a dilemma I witness every single time the municipal sanitation department tries to quietly defund our local community center while building another luxury parking garage for out-of-town commuters who leave their hybrid SUVs blocking the crosswalks. 

Bribes. 

Will the ancient powers awaken? 

Probably! 

Everything is transactional in these fictional kingdoms, just like the local co-op board elections where Mrs. Henderson tries to rig the proxy votes for the community garden so she can pave over the heirloom tomatoes to install an industrial dumpster. Yet we lap it up because the alternative is looking too closely at our own crumbling municipal infrastructure and realizing that the tyrants wearing crowns in our world don't even bother with magical blood oaths—they just hire private security firms and call it public safety. 

Watch the tempo. 

I really must dash downstairs before the neighbor's kid hits the wrong octave on that cursed upright piano again and shatters the remaining neighborhood goodwill we managed to salvage after the zoning board meeting.
