**Author:** Daisy Johnson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-11-24 APL

Hold onto the sticky metal pole because someone just elbowed my ribs while trying to squeeze past the subway doors, and honestly, forcing me to sit here banging out an anticipatory preview for *Long Wave* instead of actually reading the damn thing is a psyop. Word counts are capitalism’s cruelest trap, but here we are. 

Daisy Johnson is back. And thank god, because modern literary fiction has been drowning in hyper-stylized autofiction about people eating toast in London apartments. 

### The Lighthouse Ghost-Train

We are getting three generations of women trapped on a desolate coastal English landscape—classic gothic atmospheric creepiness that feels less like a cozy weekend getaway and more like a psychological trapdoor dropping straight into generational trauma. Jeff VanderMeer compared it to his own stuff, which tracks; Johnson clearly loves an uncanny ecology where the environment mirrors the narrator's brain-rot. 

### Motherhood as a Horror Movie

Forget sunshine and pastel nurseries. The synopsis leans hard into the feral, unhinged edges of postpartum reality—the sleep deprivation dissolving the barrier between past and present, a toddler abandoned on a remote island, and an epistemic dread that your DNA is a loaded gun pointed at your kid. It’s giving *We Need to Talk About Kevin* meets Shirley Jackson, but filtered through Johnson’s notoriously hypnotic, weird-fiction prose. 

### The Anticipation Mechanics

Look, the synopsis is deliberately cagey. We get atmospheric breadcrumbs—a beaconless lighthouse, storm-lashed shores, maternal paranoia—without a single concrete plot point about how these timelines actually collide. Will it be a literal time-slip ghost story, or just a psychological unravelling smeared across the salt-spray? I don't care, I just need the prose to hit like a double espresso. Johnson doesn't do boring, and her linguistic gymnastics are legendary. 

Expect this book to completely wreck the algorithm when it drops next November. It’s going to be aggressively discussed in every pretentious book club from Brooklyn to Berlin. 

Now I have to close my laptop and cycle through freezing rain like an absolute idiot just to cure this ungodly writer's block.
