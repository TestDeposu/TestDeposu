**Author:** Jessa Maxwell  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

The radiator is knocking again—that metallic death rattle that reminds me we are living inside a rusted tin can while October wind hurls freezing rain against the double-paned glass. Leave me alone about the draft. I am staring at the digital press release for *The Last Match* by Jessa Maxwell, and I am already aggressively ready to throw hands on behalf of these fictional souls. God forbid people just want to heal their pathetic little artistic careers in peace without some trust-fund hotel owner biting the dust at the base of a cliff. 

This is what happens when you corral fifty influencers and z-list celebrities into an historic Newport estate and expect zero bloodshed. 

Look at them in the synopsis—struggling through sunlit yoga and champagne like they aren't all one bad TikTok algorithm away from total psychological annihilation. Tegan Reyes is a once-famous artist, which means she is probably broke, eating stale cereal in a studio apartment just like mine, and desperate enough to barter her remaining dignity for a vibe shift. Ruby Valenti wants redemption. Omar Abebe is carrying the heavy, ghost-ridden baggage of a trivia champion haunted by loss. And Lacy Bryant is hunting a secret treasure in the hotel basement because regular old capitalism is simply too boring. 

### The Architecture of Desperation

Maxwell is returning to the exact locked-room aesthetic of *The Golden Spoon*, which gave us rich people behaving badly in a sequestered ecosystem. But let us be real for a second—this is giving Agatha Christie meets *The White Lotus* after doing way too many lines of espresso at three in the morning. And honestly? I am here for the mess. Give me the bourgeoisie melodrama. Give me the weaponized brunch. 

Yet, I harbor a fierce, borderline violent protectiveness over characters like Tegan. Do not let these algorithm-chasing hacks chew her up. We live in an era where everyone is trying to monetize their own nervous breakdown, and *The Last Match* seems poised to skewer that exact cultural sickness with a sharp little stiletto. 

### Pairing Death With Dessert

The synopsis tells us a storm closes in, alliances fracture, and the legendary matchmaker Magnus McBride is pairing people up for what will inevitably be a bloodbath. Because nothing says romance quite like a locked-room murder mystery where the appetizers are canapés and the main course is existential dread. Will it rely on cheap tropes? Probably. Do I care? Absolutely not, because my brain is fried from reading three hundred pages of structuralist theory by candlelight. 

If this book does not feature at least one unhinged monologue delivered over a charcuterie board while lightning strikes the Atlantic, I am going to riot in the hallway. Maxwell writes with that whip-smart pacing Hank Philippi Ryan loves—fast enough that you forget how absurd it is that fifty influencers are trapped in a Newport mansion while a corpse cools on the rocks below. 

Someone please tell the landlord the heat is insufficient. I am late for a heated philosophical debate about semiotics at a smoky underground jazz club where the espresso machine is broken and everyone smells like damp wool.
