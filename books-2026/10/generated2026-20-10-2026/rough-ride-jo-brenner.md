Dusting the flour off my hands while listening to the mixer hum against the counter, I stared at the press release for Jo Brenner’s upcoming 2026 release, *Rough Ride*, wondering how we devolved from Agatha Christie’s polite arsenic poisonings into this modern literary landscape of aggressively chained cowboys. 

And look, I am entirely exhausted by the current romance-thriller crossover trend. Give me a locked-room country estate murder with a vicar, a spinster, and a slightly poisoned scone over a wealthy ranching dynasty hero who apparently solves his trust issues by locking documentary filmmakers in his bedroom. 

### Dusting Flour off the Pages

The premise relies heavily on the oldest trick in the pulp fiction playbook—enemies to lovers wrapped in a pseudo-investigative revenge plot. Sara Feldman wants vengeance for her aunt, while Carter Brose wants to keep his secrets buried deeper than a forgotten pan of sourdough starter left too long in the back of the pantry. Yet the synopsis remains infuriatingly vague about the actual mechanics of this murder mystery. Is it a whodunit? Or is it merely an excuse to spend three hundred pages watching a bronc-riding champion negotiate a hostage-adjacent contract involving the phrase "he’ll be his in every way"? I suspect the actual detecting takes a backseat to the dramatic glare across a dusty arena. 

Speaking of domestic chaos, my youngest just managed to dump an entire bag of organic brown sugar onto the family dog, turning a peaceful Tuesday afternoon into a frantic rescue mission involving warm washcloths, howling beagles, and sticky paw prints tracked across every single square inch of newly mopped hardwood flooring, which somehow still feels more structurally sound than the flimsy power dynamics in this book's central relationship. 

Brenner clearly aims for the same dark, morally gray emotional landscape populated by authors like Penelope Douglas or Sierra Simone. But I worry the mystery element will get entirely lost beneath the sheer weight of the steam. A good thriller requires a fair play puzzle. This synopsis promises a dark chemistry spiraling into depravity, which usually translates to zero actual clues and entirely too many lingering descriptions of Stetson hats and brooding jawlines. 

Call me old-fashioned. I prefer my amateur sleuths solving crimes while drinking Earl Grey, not while being bound to a headboard by a legendary playboy who clearly skipped anger management. 

Ouch. I just stepped squarely on a rogue plastic building block left directly in front of the pantry and I genuinely need to sit down on the floor and breathe through the pain for a minute.
