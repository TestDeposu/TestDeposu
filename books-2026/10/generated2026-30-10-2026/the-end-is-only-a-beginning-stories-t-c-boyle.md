**Author:** T. C. Boyle  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Another twelve pieces of short fiction arriving from T.C. Boyle and everyone expects us to clap like seals at a circus. Look. I am staring at this synopsis while a teenager at the counter asks me if oat milk is dairy-free and I swear my soul is slowly leaking out of my left ear. My eyes rolled so hard they hurt. Because apparently, we are supposed to be utterly shook by a greatest-hits compilation of boomer anxieties disguised as literature. Let us break down this mess.

### The Panic Room of the Boomer Brain

The marketing copy wants us to believe these twelve stories burn like a fever in the blood. Please. A stoned girl breaking into a house? A screenwriter confusing his own make-believe with reality? Groundbreaking. Truly revolutionary stuff. It reads like a C-plus writing workshop final project that somehow got high-fived into print by editors who have never actually spoken to a human under thirty. And yet, the sheer thematic desperation here is fascinating—in a grim, rubbernecking-at-a-multi-car-pileup sort of way. 

### Dystopian Bingo and Other Exhaustions

We have got the mandatory futuristic panic check-list: a 3D-printed gun, a wasp sting—because why not—and Greenland swallowed whole by Trump’s America in 2036. Wow. So edgy. So deeply corrosive to the status quo. It is basically Raymond Carver for people who still subscribe to The New Yorker purely to look smart on commuter trains. Boyle apparently frames individuals as the root of all evil—according to the critics, anyway—which is just a fancy way of saying he refuses to interrogate systemic rot and would rather poke fun at weirdos in the suburbs. Groundbreaking philosophical depth. Total brain-rot. Give me Shirley Jackson or give me actual death, because this middle-brow suburban existentialism makes me want to scream into an industrial blender. 

Read the blurbs if you enjoy name-dropping contests. Apparently, Boyle is the bastard child of Flannery O'Connor and Monty Python, which sounds like an absolute nightmare of try-hard whimsy. But will it deliver actual dread? Actual, bone-chilling, cold-sweat terror? Absolutely not. It is going to be quirky. It is going to be zany. It is going to feature a man-eating tiger as a heavy-handed metaphor for climate change or middle-age or some other banal garbage. Total mid. I am sick of reading about upper-middle-class neuroses when the world outside this window is literally melting and nobody can afford rent. But sure, let us read another paragraph about a screenwriter having an existential crisis in a pool. Absolute clown behavior. 

I am going to close this tab now, walk outside, and aggressively people-watch until someone does something profoundly stupid enough to make me forget I ever read the word virtuoso.
