**Author:** Mark D. Freeman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-12-29 APL

The crisp autumn air here smells faintly of expensive pipe tobacco and decaying prestige, yet my mind is entirely assaulted by the sheer, unmitigated banality of humanity’s latest literary panic. Mark D. Freeman expects us to tremble over *Ravenous*. A Canadian eco-thriller about late-forming ice and very angry polar bears. Groundbreaking. 

One strolls past centuries of architectural vanity, watching undergraduates scurry like frantic little ants beneath ivy that refuses to die on schedule, only to be handed a synopsis that reads like a rejected pitch meeting from the Titanic's final hours. We have a brave manager, ignorant capitalists who love money more than survival, and a rogue beast that decides human flesh is preferable to waiting for a seasonal freeze. It is the Andrea Doria playing out on a frozen tundra, except everyone involved possesses the survival instincts of a golden retriever locked in a hot sedan. 

### The Economics of Apex Predators

The conceit relies entirely on the capitalist hubris trope. Business owners ignoring ecological warning signs for profit. How desperately quaint. One almost expects a boardroom scene featuring cigars and evil laughter just to round out the cartoonishness. Freeman clearly thinks he is drafting a biting critique of our modern environmental apocalypse. But subtlety died somewhere around the third quarter of the twentieth century, and this text is aggressively digging up the corpse. 

Nature taking its ultimate revenge. Oh, spare me. 

### The Desolation of Cliché

We are promised a storm that traps the town with the planet’s largest land carnivores. And yet, the synopsis reads like a paint-by-numbers exercise executed by someone who has only ever observed the wild through an IMAX lens. Where is the suffocating, nihilistic dread that makes a true grimdark venture worth the leather-bound binding? Instead, we get bureaucratic wildlife management mixed with tooth-and-claw action sequences. It’s Michael Crichton written by an intern at a regional tourism board. 

Give me Cormac McCarthy’s stark, beautifully rendered despair any day over this middle-brow disaster tourism. At least *Blood Meridian* understood that the landscape itself is indifferent to our petty little economic squabbles. Here, the bears are practically wearing little sandwich boards reading *Capitalism Failed Us*. 

The prose will no doubt lean heavily on adjectives like "chilling" and "relentless." Every single plot beat can be mapped out from fifty yards away like a limestone facade on a neoclassical library. One shudders to think how many trees were slaughtered to print a manuscript this fundamentally uninspired. 

God forbid the ice ever actually forms, because then these poor tourists might have to go home and read something with actual teeth. 

Now, if you will excuse me, my driver has just texted. I am forced to endure a excruciatingly tedious board meeting for my pet architectural preservation charity, surrounded by bores who think restoring a gable is a personality trait.
