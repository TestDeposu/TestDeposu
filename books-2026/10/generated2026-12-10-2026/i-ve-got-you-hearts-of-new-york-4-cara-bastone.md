**Author:** Cara Bastone  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Another manuscript featuring domestic proximity and forced interiority lands on my mahogany desk, smelling faintly of publisher desperation and cheap vanilla. October rain lashes against the reinforced glass of the west wing, matching my visceral disgust for contemporary romance. Cara Bastone clearly believes that trapping two reluctant adults and a stray teenager under one roof constitutes high-stakes literature. Because nothing screams psychological thriller quite like a flooded rental unit and shared laundry facilities. 

### The Architecture of Manufactured Coziness

One expects literary friction. Instead, we are subjected to the literary equivalent of a corporate retreat in the Poconos. The synopsis reads like an architectural blueprint for the Hindenburg. Soli is "free," which is industry code for emotionally stunted, while Ethan bears the standard-issue tragic backstory—a scar, a business, a child, and an inconveniently noble disposition. It is the Titanic hitting the iceberg of mandatory cohabitation, choreographed with all the subtlety of the Exxon Valdez oil spill. 

Yet people will pre-order this by the tens of thousands. They crave the comforting lie that a man with a damaged face and a plumbing emergency can fix an independent woman’s aversion to commitment. Give me a blood-soaked siege in a nameless imperial courtyard any day. Give me characters who flay each other with actual swords rather than passive-aggressive kitchen scheduling. 

### The Cult of Domesticity

The central thesis of *I've Got You* appears to be that a teenager showing up unannounced can cure an urban professional's wanderlust. Groundbreaking. We are meant to swoon over the forced proximity trope as if it hasn't been recycled since the Bronte sisters stopped writing. And the landlord angle? Ethan is desperately guarding his tenant list while nursing his emotional wounds. How terrifying. One shudders at the sheer brutality of sharing a breakfast nook. 

Bastone is trading in the soft, palatable brand of comfort reading that feels deeply offensive when the world outside is freezing over into a bleak, grey autumn. Give me a grimdark warlord betraying his bloodline over a slice of manufactured rom-com domesticity any afternoon of the week. 

My wealth manager is calling on the private line to discuss the acquisition of that modest sixteenth-century estate in the Loire Valley, and frankly, dealing with land titles sounds infinitely more thrilling than reading another chapter about a single dad renovating a basement apartment.
