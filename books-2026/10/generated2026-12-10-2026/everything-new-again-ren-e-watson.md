**Author:** Renée Watson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-10-13 APL

Carrots are supposed to be uniform, yet these ones are mocking me from the cutting board with their jagged, triangular rebellion. I am supposed to be focusing on chopped root vegetables and upcoming YA releases, but my mind remains entirely hijacked by that absolute betrayal in chapter twelve of the folklore anthology I finished reading an hour ago. How does a sworn deity of the harvest just vanish into a well without warning, abandoning a blood pact that took three centuries to establish? It makes my current meal prep feel profoundly meaningless. Still, publishers keep sending synopsys packets for *Everything New Again* by Renée Watson, demanding an analytical lens while my kitchen smells like boiling onions and bitter disappointment. 

At least this book avoids the ancient trap of gods behaving badly. Or does it? The premise drops us into a small-town landscape where a teenager named Bliss works in a family funeral home and asks big, heavy questions about legacy while people dismiss her as the weird kid. And because this is contemporary YA romance, enter Solomon—visiting from Portland, nursing abandonment issues from a mother who left him, and working at a flower shop. Naturally. Because what screams teenage romance more than the juxtaposition of mortuary science and floral arrangements? It is an aesthetic choice that practically writes itself, teetering dangerously close to a metaphor about the circle of life that Zeus himself would find a bit too on the nose. 

If I have to read one more story where a moody city boy travels to a sleepy hamlet just to collide with an existential girl who smells like embalming fluid or old books, I might throw these carrots directly against the drywall. 

Predictable. 

Yet, Watson usually brings an emotional gravity to her characters that saves them from sliding into flat archetypes. Fans of *The Sun Is Also a Star* or Jandy Nelson's chaotic dreamscapes will likely devour this setup like hungry wolves. But I am exhausted by the formula of trauma bonding over flower deliveries and grief aesthetics. We are dealing with standard-issue teenage philosophy cloaked in floral petals. 

Whatever. 

I am going to drink a cold beer on the porch and stare at the lawn.
