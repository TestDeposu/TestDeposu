**Author:** Colleen Coble  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-11-10 APL

Look, I have grading stacked three feet high on a wobbly pine desk while twelve-year-olds repeatedly drop heavy textbooks on the floor directly above my ceiling tiles, so the absolute last thing I need right now is another breathless thriller built entirely out of recycled espionage tropes and daddy-issue clichés. Yet here we are staring down the upcoming release of *She Will End This* by Colleen Coble and Rick Acker, a novel that apparently thinks readers will simply forget we already watched this exact psychological gymnastics routine in *The Last Thing He Told Me* and every third paperback thriller cluttering airport kiosks since the Cold War ended. The plot relies on the ultimate exhausted gimmick of a protagonist taking a simple DNA test—because apparently nobody in modern fiction can just go to family therapy—only to discover that her sweet, ordinary life is a massive lie and her actual father is a disgraced Mossad assassin named Abaddon, which sounds less like a believable international operative and more like a rejected villain from a Saturday morning cartoon. Naturally, a five-year-old presidential granddaughter gets kidnapped immediately because stakes apparently cannot exist in this genre without dragging toddlers into the crossfire, forcing our newly minted assassin-spawn protagonist to team up with an FBI agent whose father was allegedly killed by said assassin, creating a dynamic where professional law enforcement officers apparently have zero vetting protocols and will just casually partner up with a civilian whose bloodline belongs to an international hit squad while running through the Pacific Northwest wilderness because apparently every single thriller writer in America signed a mandatory contract stating characters must flee through damp pine trees at least once per manuscript. 

No.

Actually, scratch that completely, because looking at the publisher's comparison to *Big Little Lies* makes me realize I might be misjudging the sheer, unapologetic camp value of an intelligence operative named Abaddon hunting down a preschooler. 

We are expected to chew on profound philosophical questions about whether our protagonist is defined by the blood in her veins or the choices she makes—a theme so painfully shopworn that I am fairly certain it was carved into a Babylonian clay tablet somewhere around 2000 BCE—while wading through faith-based romantic suspense tropes that feel painfully out of place next to black-ops Mossad vendettas. Coble usually traffics in gentle, faith-filled romantic mysteries, so injecting an international cartel of shadowy intelligence agents into her usual wheelhouse feels like forcing a Sunday school teacher to direct *John Wick 4* with a budget funded entirely by church bake sales. The synopsis leans heavily on buzzwords like pulse-pounding and web of secrets without offering a single concrete detail about what these characters actually do besides run, trust the wrong people, and grapple with their messy family trees. If I have to read one more sentence about a desperate race against time involving an abducted child and a legacy of betrayal, I am going to confiscate every YA dystopian novel in the building and feed them to the shredder. 

Exhausting.

I have to go outside right now and physically haul the neighbor's fat orange tabby cat down from the very top of the massive old oak tree before animal control gets called for the third time this week.
