Cousin Gary is currently explaining his third-favorite cryptocurrency while lukewarm ham sweats under patio plastic, and God help me, I am staring down a blank page trying to unpack Erin Hànyù Lynch’s upcoming *Fleet of Wonders* just to hit a word count. 

Spoiler alert: my cheap plastic raincoat has sprung a leak right over my left shoulder, perfectly dampening the cardigan I stole from my roommate’s laundry basket. 

### The Aesthetics of Con-Artistry and Corporate Brutalism

We are apparently getting another YA fantasy duology about thieves masquerading as academics to invent weapons for an imperial war machine—because nothing screams anti-capitalist critique quite like a high-stakes science fair where the grand prize is a lifetime of trauma and a stipend. The synopsis drops names like *Babel* and *Powerless* as touchstones, which is industry-speak for "we took Leigh Bardugo’s dark academia aesthetic and mashed it into a blender with red-hot romantasy trash-gremlin brain-rot." 

And honestly? I am morbidly fascinated by the structural gymnastics required to make mixed-race slums and naval engineering competitions vibe with messy hormonal tension. 

### The Romance Industrial Complex

Let us look at the lineup. A darkly alluring factory heir. An imperious earl’s son. A sweet, bookish medical student. A lethally magnetic aristocrat. It reads less like an ensemble cast and more like a curated dating app for people with severe daddy issues and maritime fetishes. 

Yet the emotional crux of the synopsis hinges on a classic anti-imperialist pivot—the sudden, tragic realization that helping the conquerors means backstabbing the homeland. Groundbreaking. Revolutionary. Truly a historic paradigm shift in the history of fictional rebellion. But if Lynch leans into the moral rot of institutional complicity rather than just giving us two hundred pages of moody brooding on a mahogany-paneled ship deck, this might actually transcend the usual YA sludge. 

Or it will just be another five-hundred-page manuscript where the plot screeches to a halt every ten chapters so the characters can engage in heavy-breathing eye contact across a plotting table. I predict a mid-book betrayal involving stolen blueprints, a thunderstorm kiss, and at least one monologue about how love is a liability in a ruthless empire. My brain is turning to absolute mush out here in the drizzle. 

The toddler has hidden your favorite fountain pen again.
