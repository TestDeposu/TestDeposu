**Author:** Sara Raasch  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Sweaters are piling up by the front door and the maple leaves outside are turning the exact shade of bruised copper, which means my tolerance for fictional couples struggling with basic communication is officially at zero. Sara Raasch is bringing us *Joyfully Ever After*, and if Iris and her stubborn little friends-with-benefits situation with the St. Patrick’s Day Princess does not resolve into proper domestic bliss immediately, I am going to throw a very adult tantrum involving hot cider and slammed cupboard doors. We have been waiting through spring flings and summer reconciliations for this grand finale of the Royals and Romance series, and honestly, the sheer chaos of merging holidays sounds less like a clever premise and more like the seating chart for my family Thanksgiving—which is to say, pure psychological warfare. Hex has to choose between his future and his Holiday during the crisp chill of October celebrations, and if he fumbles this bag because of some nebulous existential crisis, I will never forgive the genre. Give Emily Henry and Casey McQuiston a run for their money with this anthropomorphized calendar nonsense, sure, but make sure the chemistry reads like actual people who enjoy sharing a Sunday morning coffee instead of cardboard cutouts reciting tropes at each other. 

Brutal.

The thematic architecture here leans heavily on institutional pressure versus personal desire, wrapped in the glossy sheen of holiday monarchy. Because nothing says modern romance quite like geopolitical stakes tied directly to Easter baskets and Halloween candy distribution. Yet I demand emotional authenticity underneath the glitter. Loch and the soft-hearted Prince of Christmas are sealing their bond through an ancient Irish festival, and frankly, I care about this more than my own retirement fund. We need these pairings to feel lived-in. We need the kitchen table arguments about who forgot to buy the festive napkins. Sara Raasch better deliver on the cozy banter, or I am sending the manuscript straight to the recycling bin while the wind howls against the windowpanes.
