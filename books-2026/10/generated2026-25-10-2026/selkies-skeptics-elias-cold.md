**Author:** Elias Cold  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-11-17 APL

The synopsis of *Selkies & Skeptics* reads like a fever dream stitched together by a committee of Victorian parlor-game enthusiasts who’d also written a *Tinder* bio for a werewolf. There’s a lighthouse—obligatory, like a haunted house in a horror movie—but where’s the *actual* tension? Where’s the *psychological* wreckage? Instead, we get a girl who lies her way into a faerie society, which sounds less like *The Secret History* and more like a *Shark Tank* pitch for a "magical girl club" where the only real magic is the kind that comes from a well-timed "I’m not a liar, I’m a *visionary*."

The premise hinges on the idea that Luella’s deception will unravel, but—*and this is the real crime*—there’s no sense of what that unraveling *means*. Is this a story about the cost of belonging? The ethics of performative activism? Or is it just a *very* elaborate game of telephone where the final message is "I’m a selkie, you’re a selkie, let’s all hold hands"? The Society for the Protection of Faeries sounds like it was invented by someone who’d read *The Secret Garden* and then Googled "how to make my YA novel sound pretentious." It’s the kind of organization that would host a "Faerie Etiquette 101" seminar where the only rule is "don’t ask too many questions," because the real magic is in the *illusion* of magic.

And then there’s the imposter. Oh, the *imposter*. This is where the synopsis starts to feel like it was cobbled together from a *Sherlock Holmes* fanfic and a *Stranger Things* episode guide. The imposter knows Luella’s lying, but—*here’s the kicker*—we don’t know *why* they care. Is this a story about betrayal? About the fragility of trust? Or is it just a *very* convoluted way of saying "someone’s a fraud, and now we have to figure out who"? The synopsis doesn’t even *try* to sell the stakes beyond "someone’s lying, and someone else is lying about the lying." It’s the literary equivalent of a *Choose Your Own Adventure* book where every choice leads to the same page: "You’re still lying. Try again."

The real sin here is the *vagueness*. The synopsis doesn’t just *hint* at themes—it *avoids* them entirely. Is this a story about the performative nature of identity? About the ways we construct ourselves to fit into groups? Or is it just a *very* elaborate metaphor for the fact that everyone’s full of shit? The lack of specificity is almost *deliberate*, like the author is playing a game of "How Little Can I Say Before This Feels Like a *Twilight* Fanfiction?" The result is a narrative that feels like it’s been written by someone who’s read *The Secret Garden* and *The Hunger Games* and then tried to mash them together in a blender, only to realize they’d forgotten to add the salt.

The only thing worse than a lie, the synopsis claims, is the truth with teeth. But the truth here is just a *very* sharp stick poking at the reader’s ribs, because the synopsis doesn’t even *attempt* to make the stakes feel real. It’s the literary equivalent of a *Tinder* profile that says "I’m mysterious" and then immediately follows up with "I also really like pineapple on pizza." The result is a story that feels like it’s been written by someone who’s never actually *lived* in the world they’re describing, who’s just regurgitating tropes like they’re *actual* insights.

And yet—*and this is the real tragedy*—there’s a kernel of something here. The idea of a girl who lies her way into a world she doesn’t belong in, only to realize that the world she *does* belong in is just as broken, is *almost* compelling. But the synopsis doesn’t *lean* into it. It doesn’t *explore* it. It just *drops* it like a bad joke and moves on to the next thing, like a *reality TV* host who’s run out of ideas.

The only thing left to do is consult with my wealth manager about whether I should invest in the rights to this book, because at this point, it’s the kind of thing that would make a *very* good *Netflix* series—if the writers had the guts to actually *write* it. But as it stands? It’s just another entry in the *very* crowded field of "I wrote a book about a girl who lies her way into a magical society, and now we’re all supposed to care." And honestly? I’d rather watch *The Real Housewives of New York* than read this.
