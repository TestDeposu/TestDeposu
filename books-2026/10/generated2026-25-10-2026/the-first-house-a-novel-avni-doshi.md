**Author:** Avni Doshi  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-11-10 APL

Look, I have zero desire to sit here typing another five hundred words about a domestic fallout just to pad some publisher's marketing budget before I check the replay on the middleweight championship fight. But here we are. Avni Doshi is following up *Burnt Sugar* with *The First House*, and apparently, marriages are shattering left and right while cicadas swarm and random classical statues appear in front yards like bad CGI in a round-one knockout. 

### The Footwork of a Broken Home

The premise drops a heavy right cross right out of the gate. A husband announces he is done after thirteen years, and the protagonist feels her head detach. Pure theater. Yet, I am waiting for the actual strike. Synopses love to lean heavily on surrealist set-dressing—headless metaphors, looming insect plagues, domestic estrangement from the "perfect" sister still living under the parental roof. But will the prose actually dodge, weave, and counterpunch, or will it just lean against the ropes for three hundred pages whining about the bourgeoisie? Doshi has the literary footwork, certainly. Douglas Stuart and Rumaan Alam are already throwing glowing pull-quotes at the cover like excited cornermen tossing towels into a ring. Still, I need kinetic energy. I need blood on the canvas, not just moody domestic introspection while staring at a lawn ornament.

### Glass Jaws and Drowning Omens

Then there is the family backstory—an old astrological warning that everyone will die in water, leading to a lifelong avoidance of pools, oceans, and basic aquatic survival. Cinematic? Absolutely. A heavy-handed metaphor for the suffocating nature of family expectation? You bet. If a writer is going to telegraph a drowning metaphor from page one, the payoff better land cleaner than a spinning backfist. Too many contemporary novels set up these grand, atmospheric omens only to fizzle out in the final rounds with a disappointing draw. I want to see this woman swim to the other side without the author holding her hand or offering a TED Talk on closure. 

### The Referee’s Decision

If you enjoyed the claustrophobic family dynamics of Ottessa Moshfegh or the sharp, bruising marital wreckage found in Lauren Groff’s *Fates and Furies*, you will probably pre-order this the second the link drops. But I am keeping my guard up. I have seen too many flashy contenders gas out before the championship rounds. Let us see if Doshi can actually sustain the tension, or if this is just another fancy exhibition match that leaves the audience staring at an empty ring. 

Anyway, I need to go hand-bind a chapbook for a friend's upcoming release before my coffee goes cold.
