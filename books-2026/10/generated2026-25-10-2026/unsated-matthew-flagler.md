**Author:** Matthew Flagler  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-12-01 APL

Staring at a blank canvas for three hours while my mind drifts into a strange, frictionless void of total acceptance, I find myself contemplating the upcoming release of *Unsated*. Matthew Flagler is leaning hard into the tired horror trope of the haunted mother-child woods retreat—ground already heavily tread by Shirley Jackson and occasionally dragged through the mud by cheap paperback ghost stories. Yet, the synopsis promises a psychological unravelling disguised as monster fiction. 

### The Wilderness as a Rorschach Test

And honestly? The whole trope of dragging emotional baggage into the woods is deeply overdone. We get it. Trees equal generational trauma. Melody Brooks escapes an abusive relationship only to have her daughter Sara vanish into an ancient, hungry forest. Groundbreaking. But the real meat here isn't the monster behind the tree—it’s the insidious idea that parental damage is epigenetic garbage passed down like bad furniture. Flagler is attempting to literalize psychological rot. Will it slap? Probably, if the pacing holds up. Or it will completely crash and burn into a derivative mess of metaphorical heavy-handedness. 

The text relies heavily on capitalized dread—DARKNESS CLOSES IN—which usually signals a lack of subtle prose. But let us be real for a second. Sometimes readers do not want a quiet, slow-burn literary ghost story about grief. Sometimes we want a feral, gnarly creature feature that uses domestic abuse as a springboard for pure, unadulterated body horror. The synopsis hits every beat of the contemporary elevated-horror checklist with cynical precision.

### Appetites and Ancestral Garbage

Yet, there is an interesting juxtaposition between physical starvation and emotional consumption buried in this promotional blurb. Some hungers are passed down. That phrasing hits different when you are staring at a half-finished canvas wondering why you even bother trying to subvert the male gaze. Flagler is signaling a descent into madness where the domestic terror of an ex-partner bleeds seamlessly into an ancient, insatiable woodland entity. It is messy. It is loud. It is vibe-based storytelling at its finest. 

Expect this to read like a cross between *The Ritual* and standard domestic suspense, though hopefully with less predictable plot mechanics. If Flagler actually commits to the bit—meaning the monster is literally just the generational trauma made manifest and he doesn't cop out with a mundane human villain twist—it could actually be sick. If it plays it safe, it's just another Wednesday afternoon read destined for the bargain bin. 

The bassist in the hallway just hit a brown note that is currently vibrating straight through my molar and I seriously need to pack up my easel and escape this apartment before the existential dread matches the synopsis.
