**Author:** Shailee Thompson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

The synopsis for *While You Were Screaming* reads like a corporate press release for a horror rom-com that’s been *so* carefully crafted it might as well be a TikTok algorithm’s wet dream—equal parts schmaltzy and self-aware, but with the emotional depth of a Hallmark card left in the rain. The problem? It’s *too* polished. The vagueness isn’t just a stylistic choice; it’s a cop-out. You could swap in any number of "couples retreat gone wrong" tropes—think *The Guest* meets *The Love Hypothesis* if *The Love Hypothesis* had been directed by Ari Aster—and the synopsis wouldn’t blink. It’s the literary equivalent of a fast-food menu where every item says "surprise ingredient."

The fake-dating trope is so overworked it’s practically a genre unto itself—*The Hating Game*, *Book Lovers*, *The Kiss Quotient*—but here it’s not just a setup, it’s a *ticking time bomb*. The idea of infiltrating a couples retreat as a "perfect match" is such a tired conceit that it’s almost funny if it weren’t so *predictable*. And yet, the synopsis leans into the absurdity with a wink, as if to say, "Yeah, we know it’s dumb, but watch us make it *funny*." But here’s the thing: dark humor and genuine tension don’t mix like oil and water. The synopsis hints at escalating stakes—"increasingly dangerous tests of devotion"—but it never clarifies *what* those tests are. Are we talking psychological manipulation? Physical endurance? A literal game of "truth or dare" with a chainsaw? The ambiguity is either genius or laziness, and at this point, it’s hard to tell which.

The thematic core seems to revolve around the idea of love as both salvation and damnation—classic horror-rom-com territory, sure, but the execution risks feeling like it’s borrowing from *The Shining*’s "family is a trap" energy while trying to sell itself as a rom-com. The synopsis drops the line *"deciding who deserves it"* like it’s some deep, existential revelation, but in reality, it’s just the kind of vague, cinematic phrasing that makes you wonder if the author’s ever actually read a horror novel. Compare that to *Mexican Gothic*’s razor-sharp critique of toxic relationships or *The Only Good Indians*’ unflinching look at grief—here, the emotional weight feels like it’s been slapped on after the fact, like a last-minute CGI enhancement.

And let’s talk about the characters. Laurie Hamilton, the "filmmaker with red flags for days," is the kind of protagonist who exists solely to be the audience’s proxy for skepticism. Connor Kincaid, the "dangerously handsome podcaster," is the kind of love interest who’d make a *Bachelor* contestant blush. The dynamic between them is set up as a slow-burn, but the synopsis doesn’t even *attempt* to sell it—it just says, *"she’s secretly falling for him."* Falling for him? Really? After what, three paragraphs of exposition? The chemistry here is being treated like a plot device, not a character arc. It’s the literary equivalent of a Netflix show where the romance is just there to distract you from the fact that the writing is *actually* terrible.

But here’s the kicker: the synopsis *knows* it’s playing fast and loose with expectations. It’s not trying to be *serious*; it’s trying to be *fun*. And in a market where horror-rom-coms are either *The Last of Us* meets *The Notebook* or *Euphoria* meets *The Ring*, *While You Were Screaming* is positioning itself as the latter—sleazy, self-aware, and just desperate enough to work. The name-dropping of Brynne Weaver and Grady Hendrix is a smart move, because those authors straddle the line between genre and literary fiction with a precision that *this* synopsis lacks. Weaver’s *The Last Time I Lied* has the same kind of dark humor and psychological tension, but where Weaver’s work feels *earned*, this synopsis feels like it’s just trying to *imitate*.

I’m not even sure what to make of it. Is this a book that’s going to deliver the kind of subversive, genre-bending fun of *The Only Good Indians*? Or is it going to be the kind of rom-com that makes you want to unsubscribe from the author’s newsletter? The synopsis doesn’t tell you. It just leaves you with the nagging suspicion that you’re about to read a book that’s *so* confident in its own cleverness that it’s forgotten how to actually *write*.
