**Author:** J.D. Robb  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Thirty-two dollars for a textbook that was last updated when George Bush was in office. That is the actual financial ruin funding my coffee black enough to melt concrete while I dissect *Perfect in Death*. Because J.D. Robb—or Nora Roberts wearing her futuristic precinct badge—is back at it again with Lieutenant Eve Dallas. Sixty-four books. Read that number out loud and try not to experience immediate somatic dread. 

### Scalpels, Cobblestones, and God Complexes

The synopsis gives us a headless corpse on a dark cobblestoned street. Classic noir beats, sure, but let us talk about the actual biological plausibility here. The blurb tosses around phrases like "illegal organ harvesting" and "medical knowledge" right before dragging us into the hallowed halls of academia. If you are going to write a forensic thriller involving surgical precision, for the love of ATP, explain the cellular preservation window. You cannot just casually misappropriate transplant logistics for dramatic effect without accounting for ischemic times. Actually, wait. The medical angle is completely secondary to the institutional rot anyway. Never mind the cellular necrosis; what we are really looking at is yet another tired allegory about elite hubris. 

### The Pursuit of Absolute Perfection

Or maybe not. Let me walk that back because the pursuit of perfection is literally the engine running this entire sprawling dystopia. Manicured campus lawns masking feral ambition. Manhattan doctors playing God because their prefrontal cortexes are fried on ambition and designer stimulants. Eve Dallas walks into these ivory towers like a human garbage disposal for dirty secrets. She hates elitism almost as much as I hate my landlord charging twenty-five dollars a month for a basement storage locker that leaks sewage. 

And Peabody will be there, rolling her eyes and eating some synthetic pastry while Eve threatens a neurosurgeon with a badge and a glare. The thematic weight here rests on the friction between raw, bloody biology and the sterile aesthetic of high-society achievement. But let us be real. This reads like Michael Connelly crossed with a cyberpunk soap opera. We all know the drill by now. The victim seemed too good to be true. The family is hiding something mundane yet tawdry. Some arrogant academic is going to monologues about transhumanism or cellular immortality right before the cuffs click. 

Yet I will still pre-order it on my phone while sitting in the back of a three-hundred-person lecture hall listening to an adjunct drone on about molecular pathways. Because sometimes you just need a caffeine-addled homicide detective to remind you that rich people are garbage. 

The bodega on the corner just turned into a cold-pressed juice boutique charging eight dollars for celery water, and honestly, losing the last decent dive bar in this ZIP code hurts worse than failing organic chemistry.
