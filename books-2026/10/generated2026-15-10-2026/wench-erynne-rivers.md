**Author:** Erynne Rivers  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-07-16 APL

Raking these maples is an exercise in futility. The wind simply refuses to cooperate, scattering gold-brown debris across the sodgers just as I finally get a decent pile going. But looking at the promotional material for Erynne Rivers’ *Wench*, at least someone is managing to completely flip a tired narrative on its head. 

We are drowning in endless, lifeless retellings right now. Every publisher in New York seems convinced that teens and young adults will only read a book if it features a brooding monarch in a dark castle who stares intensely at a wall for three hundred pages. Give me a break. Compare this refreshing premise to the absolute dreck that flooded young adult fiction last semester—books with titles like *The Dark Shadow of Whispering Shadows*, where the main character learns absolutely nothing and just groans about her destiny for twelve chapters while a CGI wolf stares blankly into the middle distance. Respectfully, what? It is pure literary garbage that treats adolescents like they’ve never read a sentence with a semicolon in it. 

Rivers takes the classic Robin Hood myth—a story usually dominated by flannel-clad men shouting about redistribution of wealth—and centers the entire conflict on Maid Marian getting bundled off to marry a literal monster before getting carjacked by the very fae her parents warned her about. 

### Unlearning the Propagandized Forest

The thematic core here relies heavily on dismantling institutional indoctrination. Marian grows up believing Sherwood is a literal hellscape populated by monsters. Then she actually meets the outcasts. Shocking concept. People in power lie to maintain control. Teens deal with this exact brand of systemic gaslighting every single day in homeroom when administrators pretend dress codes solve bullying. Rivers understands that the most potent rebellion isn't just swinging a sword at a corrupt sheriff; it is unlearning the garbage your elders drilled into your skull. 

### The Romance We Do Not Deserve

And the romantic tension sounds delightfully predictable in the best possible way. Captor-to-confidant is a classic dynamic because it forces proximity. Marian has to choose between the predictable misery of human patriarchy or a chaotic, magical future with a man named Robin who apparently looks like he stepped out of a high-end fantasy art print. Critics calling it "cozy" and "lyrical" clearly haven't looked at the psychological implications of stockholm adjacent yearning, but frankly, I do not care. Give me the emotional whiplash. 

This debut is going to eclipse everything else on the shelves this July. It has that effortless charm that modern romantasy desperately needs right now. I just hope the pacing holds up once the Sheriff's men actually show up. 

Now I need to go inside and ice my lower back before grading forty essays on comma splices, provided I don't cripple myself first because I just stepped on a remarkably sharp plastic toy in the hallway.
