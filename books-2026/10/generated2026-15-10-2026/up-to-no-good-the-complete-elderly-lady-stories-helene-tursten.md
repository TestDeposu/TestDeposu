One must marvel at the modern penchant for dressing up petty criminality as geriatric whimsy, as if a rusted cleaver becomes charming simply because it is held by someone with advanced cataracts. We are expected to swoon over a collection titled *Up to No Good: The Complete Elderly Lady Stories* by Helene Tursten, a compilation of murderous exploits involving an octogenarian squatter in Gothenburg who apparently handles lease disputes with a shovel. How delightfully quaint. One can practically smell the decomposing bodies beneath the begonias, perfectly paired with the bonus cross-stitch patterns. One wonders if Marie Antoinette would have kept her head had she simply embroidered a scenic cottage while ridding Versailles of her creditors. 

### The Aristocratic Right of Eminent Domain

Maud lives rent-free in a sizable apartment because of a seventy-year-old contract clause. Let us pause and reflect on the absolute vulgarity of squatting, regardless of whether the perpetrator uses a walker. Property rights are the bedrock of civilization, inherited through bloodlines of proper breeding—not secured through illicit graves and clandestine landscaping. Yet, the contemporary reading public eats this up, preferring their matriarchs unhinged and homicidal rather than dignified and heavily in debt. It is the literary equivalent of the House of Windsor letting Prince Andrew choose the interior decor for Balmoral. Simply indefensible.

Detectives are circling. Suspense is meant to be generated. But the synopsis admits right away that she has evaded detection thus far, rendering the entire endeavor as toothless as the protagonist claims *not* to be. 

### Culinary Crimes and Domestic Treason

Recipes included in a volume of murder mysteries. What a tired, pedestrian gimmick. Agatha Christie never felt the need to include a scone recipe after poisoning Lord Peter's aunt, because true art requires no domestic bribe to hold the reader's attention. But here we are, catering to the same demographic that treats the Harrods food hall like a contact sport. We are sliding backward into barbarism, trading proper character development for folksy Scandinavian noir that reads like a cookbook edited by Jeffrey Dahmer. 

### The Inevitable Cinematic Degradation

Naturally, Hollywood must sink its manicured claws into this nonsense. An upcoming series starring Glenn Close. Perfect. Because if there is one thing missing from our cultural landscape, it is another vehicle for an esteemed actress to ham her way through a caricature of domestic psychopathy while wearing oversized knitwear. It gives off strong *Sunset Boulevard* meets the Ikea catalog energy, and frankly, Norma Desmond had better posture. 

My corporate lawyer is calling me on the secure line about a rather messy litigation issue involving a disputed land grant in the south of France, so I must cut this short before my blood pressure reaches the levels of a taxed commoner.
