**Author:** Caroline Wray  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

October winds are rattling the loose windowpanes of this cursed rental again, blowing a steady chill across the floorboards while I stare at yet another pile of unsolicited galleys that cost seventy cents more to ship than they are worth. Caroline Wray is attempting something genuinely unhinged with *Henry*, and honestly? I am so seated for the carnage. 

### The Geometry of Oblivion

A blazing contemporary reimagining of the Tudor dynasty's greatest marital sport, relocated across a century and a half of American and English anxieties. Because why write historical fiction when you can just drop Tudor trauma into 1966 New Orleans and let the humidity rot the velvet? Susan Choi calls Wray an unsparing observer of the human heart — a blurb that usually means everyone in this book is going to behave like an absolute feral rat for the sake of validation. And frankly? We love to see it. We are talking about six distinct archetypes orbiting a singular, gravitational black hole of male ego. Lina, Anne, Hannah, Katy, Jane, Parker. A whole constellation of women trying to figure out how to be the main character of a narrative where they were merely cast as the side-piece prop. 

### The Architecture of Desperation

The premise hinges on a terrifyingly simple, devastatingly relatable question: how much dignity are you willing to trade on the open market just to feel perceived by someone who fundamentally views you as disposable real estate? Wray tracks this pathetic descent from a reluctant Mardi Gras queen in the sixties to a headspring ballerina in Manhattan, sliding right down into a crumbling English castle where a reality TV producer is orchestrating synthetic affection. It gives major *The Girlfriend Experience* meets Hilary Mantel on an bender. Yet the synopsis remains delightfully vague about the exact mechanics of how these women intersect — do they share the same monster, or is Henry just a systemic virus mutating through different decades? The structural ambition alone is giving me vertigo, mostly because I am currently dizzy from living off stale black coffee and cheap cigarettes while watching the autumn light fade before four in the afternoon. 

And if Wray actually pulls off this multi-temporal, multi-class vivisection of patriarchal collateral damage without devolving into pretentious structural bloat, she might just out-do every other cynical domestic thriller currently clogging up the bestseller lists. Or it will completely implode under the weight of its own cleverness. Either way, the vibes are immaculate. 

I have to bolt right now or I am going to miss the start of the midnight philosophical showdown down at the smoky basement jazz joint where nobody asks about your overdue rent.
