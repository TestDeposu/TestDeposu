**Author:** Anushka Joshi  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Silks rustle in the dark. We are expected to swoon over 1929 Bombay magazines and celluloid dreams as if the Mitford sisters were running casting calls in Lahore. Anushka Joshi brings us *Starlet*, a forthcoming monument to artifice and the cruel weight of a made-up surname. 

### The Grand Mirage of Velvet and Deception

Let us examine the architecture of this lie. Estelle Thompson sheds her skin to become Merle Oberon, trading the quiet ache of colonial alienation for blinding studio lights and red-carpet waltzes. But what is a starlet if not a glorified porcelain doll painted by men who consume light for a living? Not that anyone asked me. The marketing machine leans hard on the obligatory *The Seven Husbands of Evelyn Hugo* comparisons—because apparently, modern readers possess the critical palate of a golden retriever chasing laser pointers—yet I suspect Joshi’s prose carries a sharper, more melancholic resonance. We are promised an Oscar nomination born of sheer fraud. Imagine the sheer, unadulterated Fyre Fest energy of lying to the Academy while your mother mops up the dressing room. 

### The Cartography of Loneliness Under Studio Bulbs

The thematic spine here is not merely glamour; it is the exquisite, suffocating terror of erasure. How much of a soul remains when the director yells cut and the gorgeous gowns are locked in a vault? Estelle lives a bifurcated existence—vampy screen siren by daylight, ghost by midnight. Joshi’s synopsis hints at a psychological claustrophobia that reminds me terribly of Virginia Woolf’s more frantic dinner parties crossed with the tragic, unhinged social climbing of *The Talented Mr. Ripley*. Yet I worry the narrative might buckle under the weight of its own high-concept premise. Will the prose sing with the lyrical precision of true poetry, or will it devolve into a Wikipedia page decorated with adjectives? 

### The Price of a Manufactured Smile

Celebrity is a jealous god demanding human sacrifice. The inclusion of the mother disguised as a maid provides an electric current of domestic espionage—a silent, grinding sacrifice happening just off-camera while the flashbulbs pop. One hopes Joshi resists the urge to tidy up the moral ambiguities with a neat little bow. Let the deceit rot the teeth. Let the glamour taste like ash and bad gin. 

My chauffeur is blaring the horn in the courtyard. I must log off and mentally prepare for my interview with *Vogue*.
