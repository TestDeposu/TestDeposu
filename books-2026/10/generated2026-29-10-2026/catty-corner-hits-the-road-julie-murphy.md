**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026 APL

Julie Murphy is attempting to bottle that hyper-specific, sticky kind of manufactured warmth in *Catty Corner Hits the Road*, a forthcoming 2026 chapter book about a half-girl, half-cat named Catty whose grand RV excursion with Granny Tabby and friends gets predictably complicated by looming interpersonal secrets and boundary violations disguised as embarrassing family anecdotes. Naturally. Because nothing says emotional safety quite like a trusted elder publicizing your most vulnerable childhood mortifications to an audience of your peers while hurtling down an interstate at seventy miles per hour in a Winnebago. 

There is an exhausting literary fixation lately on packing children's media with these neat little conflict-resolution vectors—as though every developmental fissure caused by close quarters, repressed peer anxiety, and forced familial intimacy can be smoothed over within the span of a hundred illustrated pages by visiting a roadside tourist trap like The World's Largest Ball of Yarn. 

Actually, let us reexamine that entire thesis for a moment. Perhaps the yarn ball isn't just a quirky backdrop for juvenile antics, but rather a remarkably heavy-handed metaphor for the tangled knots of unspoken shame that children are forced to carry when adults weaponize nostalgia against them under the guise of harmless affection. 

Whichever way the narrative swings, the tension hinges entirely on Bebe keeping a "big secret," which is industry shorthand for a very manufactured, low-stakes plot device designed to teach eight-year-olds about radical honesty without ever actually touching the raw, messy nerves of actual human alienation. 

Hardly groundbreaking.

Boring.
