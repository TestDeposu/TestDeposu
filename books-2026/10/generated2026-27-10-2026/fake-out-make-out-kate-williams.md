**Author:** Kate Williams  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-06-04 APL

Look at the way modern storytellers stitch together their little myths—taking the grand, agonizing trials of ancient mortals and dragging them down into the neon-lit trenches of corporate espionage and forced proximity tropes. Kate Williams has apparently decided that running tracks and chronic illness aren't quite enough suffering for her protagonist, Charlie Ross, so she tosses her headfirst into an international ring of stolen arms and super yachts alongside a perpetually grumpy espionage director named Declan Davidson, because apparently nobody is allowed to simply heal in peace anymore without a handsome spymaster yelling at them about national security while jets whisk them across romantic European cities. 

A rare win.

Or at least, it pretends to be one, though I suspect the actual manuscript will read like every single billionaire-adjacent paperback currently clogging the airport shelves—glossy, predictable, and sweating aggressively from trying to outrun its own genre conventions. 

Yet the mythology here is fascinatingly modern. We have the classic hero's descent, replaced entirely by a medical layoff and an inflated corporate budget. Charlie trusts everyone except her broken body, while Declan trusts absolutely no one—a dynamic that feels less like genuine psychological friction and more like a recycled screenplay by someone who skimmed Homer on a transatlantic flight and thought, "Yes, but what if Odysseus hated human resources?" 

Actually, the spies are entirely irrelevant. Let us be entirely serious. This is just Greek tragedy wearing a tactical vest.

The author wants us to care about a cache of stolen arms at the World Games, but let us not pretend anyone is buying this book for the geopolitics. They are buying it for the exact same reason my grandmother used to spin tales of gods turning into swans to harass unsuspecting mortals—the sheer, unadulterated joy of watching two stubborn archetypes collide until sparks fly off their titanium armor. 

Will the corporate bureaucracy of FIRE logistics ruin the romantic tension? Probably. Will the European super yacht scenes feel like a stock photography folder came to life? Assuredly. 

Still. I suppose I can forgive a multitude of narrative sins when the marketing promises high-stakes cardio and emotional damage wrapped up in a shiny New Adult debut. 

Must go stoke the coals now before the neighborhood barbecue descends into absolute anarchy.
