**Author:** Daisy Garrison  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-12-01 APL

Steam is hissing from the espresso machine like a dying lung while some trust-fund sociopath orders an iced matcha with oat milk and zero joy. I am staring at a synopsis for a manuscript called *Tommy from the Moon* by Daisy Garrison because apparently, society has decided we need yet another theatrical romance where everyone is painfully gorgeous and emotionally stunted. God forbid someone writes a book about a guy who gets dragged into an industrial meat grinder or a basement full of skinless ghouls. No. We get Broadway. We get *Romeo and Juliet*. Because nothing screams genuine dread quite like theater kids quoting Shakespeare while weaponizing their daddy issues under the harsh lights of 2026.

### The Audition Trap and Other Slasher Tropes

Grace Franklin shows up to dance and somehow bags Juliet because the director is having a psychotic break with big-name casting. Classic trope-rot. Garrison is selling us the ultimate parasocial wet dream—the unknown chorus girl catching the eye of her childhood celebrity crush, Miles Drake. It is basically the plot of every fanfiction ever hammered out on a sticky keyboard at 3:00 AM by someone mainlining Red Bull. The synopsis tries to pitch this as an ode to New York City youth, but let us be real. It smells intensely of gentrified overpriced apartments, damp rehearsal studios, and the creeping, nameless horror of realizing you are never going to be able to afford a root canal. 

And Miles? The poor little rich boy. His fame weighs on him in ways Grace cannot possibly comprehend because she is too busy hiding her sketchy backstory about his first big break. Oh, the suspense is straight-up excruciating. I am shaking in my scuffed boots. 

### The Inevitable Bloodbath of Acting Choices

The synopsis leans hard into the meta-theatrical cheese. Everyone knows how the play ends, yadda yadda yadda. Will they write their own ending? Or are they doomed to crash and burn? It is giving catastrophic trainwreck energy. This thing is going to read like Emily Henry tried to direct a gritty A24 psychological thriller and then gave up halfway through to write five chapters of longing glances behind a prop table. The prose will undoubtedly be dripping with the kind of manic twee joy that makes you want to throw your ceramic mugs directly against the brick wall. Give me a protagonist getting hunted by a chainsaw-wielding maniac in the dark woods any day over two privileged narcissists confusing rehearsal heat with an actual personality. 

### Final Verdict

Expect this thing to chart immediately because the masses love a good parasocial hallucination wrapped in velvet and stage lights. It will probably be adapted into a sanitized streaming series by next spring. Meanwhile, the rent on my apartment just went up another three hundred dollars, and the last decent dive bar on this block was unceremoniously bulldozed to make room for a boutique artisanal candle collective.
