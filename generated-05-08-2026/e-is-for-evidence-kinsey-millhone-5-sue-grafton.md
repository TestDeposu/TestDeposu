# E is for Evidence (Kinsey Millhone, #5)

**Author:** Sue Grafton  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1988

**E Is for Evidence: Sue Grafton’s Sharp-Witted Masterpiece of Suspense and Self-Reliance**

Sue Grafton’s *E Is for Evidence*—the fifth installment in her groundbreaking Kinsey Millhone alphabet series—is a razor-sharp thriller that blends clever whodunit intrigue with the unflinching grit of a woman who refuses to be anyone’s victim. Published in 1988, the novel stands as a testament to Grafton’s ability to craft a protagonist who is as compelling as the mysteries she solves. Kinsey Millhone, a no-nonsense private investigator with a sharp tongue and sharper instincts, finds herself at the center of a frame-up that threatens her career, her reputation, and—most dangerously—her life. What follows is a cat-and-mouse game of deception, where the real enemy isn’t just the person trying to destroy her, but the system that would rather believe the worst of her than the truth.

At its core, *E Is for Evidence* is a story about survival—both professional and personal. Kinsey, a twice-divorced, fiercely independent woman who has built her life on her own terms, is suddenly forced to confront the fragility of that autonomy. When an anonymous $5,000 deposit lands in her bank account—only to be tied to a suspicious industrial arson case—she becomes the prime suspect in a crime she didn’t commit. The frame-up isn’t just a professional setback; it’s a personal assault on her integrity, forcing her to dig deeper than ever before to uncover the truth. Grafton’s genius lies in her ability to make Kinsey’s vulnerability feel authentic without sacrificing her strength. The novel doesn’t glorify her resilience; it treats it as a hard-won necessity, a daily battle against skepticism and prejudice.

The framing device of the alphabet series—each book named after a letter of the alphabet—often gets overshadowed by the sheer quality of Grafton’s storytelling, but in *E Is for Evidence*, it serves a purpose. The "E" isn’t just a gimmick; it’s a thematic anchor. Evidence, in all its forms—physical, circumstantial, and emotional—drives the plot forward. Kinsey’s investigation becomes a meticulous dissection of clues, each one leading her closer to the truth while also revealing the cracks in the facade of those around her. Grafton’s writing is precise, almost surgical, with a dry wit that keeps the tension from feeling oppressive. Her dialogue crackles with the kind of sharp repartee that makes Kinsey’s interactions with clients, suspects, and even her own demons feel vividly real.

What elevates *E Is for Evidence* beyond a typical mystery novel is its exploration of the cost of independence. Kinsey’s loner status isn’t just a quirk of her personality; it’s a shield against the world’s expectations. Yet, when that shield is stripped away—when she’s accused of corruption and forced to defend herself—she must confront the isolation that comes with being a woman in a male-dominated field. Grafton doesn’t shy away from the sexism Kinsey faces, but she doesn’t let it define her either. Instead, she uses it as fuel, turning every obstacle into a challenge to be outsmarted. The novel’s strength lies in its balance: Kinsey is flawed, but her flaws make her relatable; she’s tough, but her toughness is earned through struggle.

The pacing of *E Is for Evidence* is another standout feature. Grafton moves the story forward with a steady hand, never rushing the suspense but also never dragging. The frame-up itself is a masterclass in misdirection, with red herrings and false leads that keep the reader guessing until the very end. The supporting cast—from Kinsey’s ex-husband, who reenters her life with complications, to the eccentric characters she encounters in her investigations—adds layers of depth to the narrative. Even the smaller details, like Kinsey’s meticulous case notes or her dry observations about human nature, reinforce her as a protagonist who is as fascinating as the mysteries she solves.

Grafton’s prose is lean and efficient, with a rhythm that mirrors Kinsey’s own sharp, no-nonsense approach to life. There’s a certain economy to her writing that makes every word count, whether she’s describing a crime scene, a tense confrontation, or the quiet moments of Kinsey’s introspection. The novel’s humor—often dark but always witty—cuts through the tension, reminding the reader that even in the darkest of situations, Kinsey has a way of finding the absurd in the ordinary.

Ultimately, *E Is for Evidence* is more than just a mystery; it’s a celebration of self-reliance and the power of persistence. Kinsey Millhone is a heroine who doesn’t fit neatly into any mold, and that’s what makes her so compelling. She’s not a damsel in distress, nor is she an infallible detective. She’s a woman who has built her life on her own terms and refuses to let someone else’s lies destroy it. Grafton’s novel is a testament to the idea that truth, like justice, is something you have to fight for—and that the fight itself is often half the battle.

For fans of classic mysteries, Grafton’s work remains a benchmark, blending the best of traditional whodunit elements with a modern, feminist edge. *E Is for Evidence* is a standout in the series, proving that even in a world where evidence can be manipulated and reputations ruined, the right kind of detective—and the right kind of story—can always find a way to prevail.
