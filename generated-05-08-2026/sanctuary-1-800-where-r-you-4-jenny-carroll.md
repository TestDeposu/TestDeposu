# Sanctuary (1-800-Where-R-You, #4)

**Author:** Jenny Carroll  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Jenny Carroll, better known to the literary world as the blockbuster powerhouse Meg Cabot, delivers another fast-paced, high-stakes triumph in Sanctuary, the fourth installment of the gripping 1-800-Where-R-You series. Published in 2009, this novel once again proves Carroll’s unmatched ability to blend the gritty reality of teenage life with the electrifying thrill of the supernatural. At its core, Sanctuary is a masterclass in tension, exploring the precarious balance between forced compliance and moral duty, all while navigating the treacherous waters of adolescent identity.

The central theme of Sanctuary revolves around unlikely alliances and the complex morality of power. Sixteen-year-old Jessica Mastriani—gifted with the uncanny ability to locate missing persons—has long fought to keep her psychic gifts under wraps, desperate to avoid becoming a government pawn. However, the plot thickens when her personal mission to find a missing local boy intersects directly with the shadowy agenda of Dr. Cyrus Krantz, the very U.S. government agent sent to co-opt her. Carroll brilliantly uses this dynamic to deconstruct the classic "enemy mine" trope. Jess is forced to look past the bureaucratic suits and cold demeanor of her captors to confront a far more tangible, localized evil: a heavily armed backwoods militia driven by blind hate and fear. 

Through this collision of federal surveillance and extremist paranoia, Carroll examines how fear breeds fanaticism. The secluded, suspicious community where the story unfolds acts almost as a character itself—a pressure cooker of hostility. Jess’s psychic visions do not merely serve as a convenient plot device; they are a heavy burden, forcing her to look into the darkest corners of human nature. The author uses this supernatural lens to comment on real-world prejudices, making the stakes feel painfully authentic despite the paranormal premise. 

Stylistically, Carroll’s voice remains one of her greatest assets. Written with her signature razor-sharp wit and snappy, authentic dialogue, Sanctuary avoids the trap of taking itself too grimly seriously, even when dealing with life-or-death situations. Jess Mastriani is a refreshingly flawed protagonist. She is stubborn, fiercely independent, and armed with a sarcastic inner monologue that keeps the narrative grounded. This distinct comedic timing prevents the darker themes of militia extremism and government manipulation from overwhelming the reader. At the same time, the romantic tension—particularly the complicated triangle involving Dr. Krantz and Jess’s devoted would-be boyfriend, Rob—adds a layer of emotional vulnerability that softens the harder edges of the suspense plot. 

The pacing is relentless. Carroll structures the narrative like a ticking clock, propelling Jess from one dangerous confrontation to the next. The atmosphere of paranoia is palpable; every shadow in the backwoods feels threatening, and every character hides a motive. It is this immersive world-building that makes the novel so striking. Readers are not just observing a mystery unfold; they are trapped in the suffocating tension alongside the characters, desperate for a breakthrough.

Sanctuary is a shining example of YA suspense done right. It successfully elevates a standard missing-person procedural into a deeper exploration of trust, sacrifice, and what it truly means to have a sanctuary in a world full of danger. Carroll weaves humor, heart, and high-octane thrills into a seamless narrative that leaves readers breathless and eager for more. Whether you are a longtime fan of the series or a newcomer drawn to supernatural mysteries, this installment delivers on every front, cementing its place as a standout in the genre.
