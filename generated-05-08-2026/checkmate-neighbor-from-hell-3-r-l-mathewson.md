# Checkmate (Neighbor from Hell, #3)

**Author:** R.L. Mathewson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

**Checkmate (Neighbor from Hell #3) by R.L. Mathewson: A Masterclass in Dark Comedy and Forbidden Desire**

R.L. Mathewson’s *Checkmate*—the third installment in the *Neighbor from Hell* series—is a masterful blend of razor-sharp wit, morally ambiguous romance, and the kind of dark humor that lingers like a bad joke you can’t stop laughing at. If the first two books in this series established Mathewson’s knack for crafting enemies-to-lovers dynamics with a twist of psychological tension, *Checkmate* takes that formula to a whole new level, weaving in themes of obsession, deception, and the fine line between manipulation and passion.

At its core, *Checkmate* is a story about power—who holds it, how it’s wielded, and the cost of playing the game. Connor O’Neil, the series’ quintessential villain-turned-antagonist, is a man who believes the ends justify the means. His obsession with securing a dream—one that Rory James, the series’ reluctant heroine, stands in the way of—drives the narrative forward with a relentless, almost predatory intensity. But here’s the twist: Connor isn’t just a villain. He’s a man who’s been hurt, who’s been wronged, and who’s willing to do whatever it takes to protect himself. That ambiguity is what makes him so compelling. He’s not a one-dimensional bad guy; he’s a man who’s been played by life, and now he’s playing back.

Rory, on the other hand, is the series’ everyman—frustrated, flawed, and stubbornly human. She’s the kind of protagonist who makes you root for her even when she’s being unreasonable, because she’s relatable. Her journey in *Checkmate* is one of realization: she’s spent the series believing she knows Connor, only to discover that she’s been blind to the depths of his motives. The revelation that “she was wrong” isn’t just a plot twist—it’s a gut-punch, a moment where the reader is forced to question their own assumptions about the characters. Mathewson excels at this kind of narrative subversion, making the reader feel like they’ve been played, just like Rory.

The series’ signature dark humor is on full display here, but with a sharper edge. The banter between Connor and Rory is electric, charged with tension that’s equal parts sexual and psychological. There’s a moment in *Checkmate* where Connor steals Rory’s hot cocoa—a seemingly petty act—only to reveal its deeper significance later. It’s a small detail, but it’s the kind of thing that makes the reader sit up and pay attention. The humor isn’t just for laughs; it’s a tool, a way to mask the underlying tension until it explodes into something far more dangerous.

Mathewson’s writing style is a delightful contradiction. His prose is clean and efficient, never bogged down by unnecessary flourishes, but his dialogue crackles with life. He has a knack for creating characters who feel real, who have quirks and flaws that make them endearing even when they’re infuriating. The chemistry between Connor and Rory is palpable, and the way Mathewson balances their dynamic—equal parts attraction and repulsion—keeps the reader hooked.

Thematically, *Checkmate* delves into the idea of truth and perception. Connor’s entire plan hinges on Rory never finding out the truth, but the question is: what is the truth? Is it the version Connor presents, or is it something else entirely? The answer lies in the gray areas, in the moments where neither character is entirely right or wrong. This moral ambiguity is what makes the series so engaging. It’s not just about who gets the girl; it’s about who gets to define reality.

One of the most striking aspects of *Checkmate* is its pacing. Mathewson knows how to build tension, how to drip-feed information so that the reader is constantly guessing, constantly second-guessing. The blackmail, the shared dream project, the stolen hot cocoa—each of these elements is a piece of a larger puzzle, and the way they come together is a testament to Mathewson’s skill as a storyteller.

In the end, *Checkmate* is more than just a romantic comedy. It’s a story about power, about the games people play, and about the cost of winning. It’s a book that will leave you laughing one minute and questioning everything the next. And that’s exactly why it’s so good.
