# Carnage (L.O.R.D.S., #5)

**Author:** Shantel Tessier  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Unknown

**Carnage: A Brutal, Unflinching Descent into the Heart of Chaos**

Shantel Tessier’s *Carnage*—the fifth installment in the *L.O.R.D.S.* series—is not a book for the faint of heart. It is a visceral, relentless exploration of power, betrayal, and the unraveling of morality, wrapped in a narrative so gripping it feels less like fiction and more like a cautionary tale carved from real-world brutality. Tessier, a master of psychological tension and morally ambiguous storytelling, delivers a novel that thrives on its characters’ descent into madness, where loyalty is a currency and survival is a privilege reserved for the ruthless.

At its core, *Carnage* is a story about the cost of ambition and the fragility of alliances. The novel follows a cast of characters already hardened by the events of previous books, but here, Tessier strips them down to their most primal instincts. The power dynamics are razor-sharp, with every interaction laced with the potential for violence—whether overt or psychological. The author’s writing is stark, unapologetic, and dripping with a dark humor that underscores the absurdity of the situations her characters find themselves in. Tessier doesn’t shy away from graphic depictions of brutality, but it’s not gratuitous; it’s necessary, a reflection of the world she’s built, where mercy is a luxury and trust is a death sentence.

What makes *Carnage* particularly compelling is Tessier’s ability to make even the most despicable characters sympathetic. The line between hero and villain blurs so thoroughly that the reader is left questioning who the true monsters are—and who is merely surviving by playing the game. The prose is tight, efficient, and relentless, with a rhythm that mirrors the chaos unfolding on the page. There’s a sense of inevitability here, as if the characters are being pulled toward their fates by an unseen force, their choices no longer their own but dictated by the weight of their pasts.

The novel’s strength lies in its unflinching portrayal of power. Tessier doesn’t romanticize it; she exposes its rotten core, showing how easily it corrupts, how quickly it turns friends into enemies, and how utterly disposable the people caught in its grip become. The dialogue is sharp, the betrayals are swift, and the consequences are brutal. There’s a sense of creeping dread, as if the reader is watching a train wreck in slow motion, unable to look away.

Yet, for all its darkness, *Carnage* is not without moments of unexpected depth. Tessier’s characters are flawed, complex, and often contradictory, which makes their struggles feel authentic. The novel doesn’t offer easy answers or neat resolutions; instead, it leaves the reader with the unsettling realization that in this world, there are no true winners, only those who manage to outlast everyone else.

If you’ve followed the *L.O.R.D.S.* series, you’ll recognize the familiar themes of survival and betrayal, but Tessier elevates them here, pushing the boundaries of what the series has become. The pacing is relentless, the tension never-ending, and the payoff is as satisfying as it is brutal. This is not a book for those seeking redemption or happy endings; it’s for those who want to witness the unraveling of the human spirit in all its glory—and horror.

*Carnage* is a masterclass in dark storytelling, a novel that lingers long after the last page, leaving the reader with the lingering question: *How far would you go to survive?* Tessier’s answer is chilling, and it’s one you won’t soon forget.
