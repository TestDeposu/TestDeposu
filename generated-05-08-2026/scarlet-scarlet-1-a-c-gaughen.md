# Scarlet (Scarlet, #1)

**Author:** A.C. Gaughen  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

**A Fresh Arrow in the Quiver: *Scarlet* Rewrites Robin Hood for the Modern Reader**

A.C. Gaughen’s *Scarlet* isn’t just another retelling of the Robin Hood legend—it’s a bold, subversive reinvention that strips away the myth and replaces it with something far more compelling: a story of survival, identity, and the razor’s edge between loyalty and betrayal. By centering the narrative on Will Scarlet—a character often overshadowed in traditional tales—Gaughen crafts a tale that is as sharp as its eponymous protagonist’s knives, blending historical romance with the grit of outlaw life.

At its core, *Scarlet* is a story about the power of secrets. The novel’s unnamed protagonist, known to Nottingham as Will Scarlet, is a master of deception, hiding her true past beneath layers of disguise and deception. Her relationship with Robin Hood, though central to the legend, is rendered with a tension that feels fresh and electric. Unlike the chivalric ideal of the original tales, Gaughen’s Robin is a man of contradictions—charismatic, flawed, and undeniably magnetic. His interactions with Scarlet are laced with an unspoken chemistry that lingers like a half-drawn bowstring, always on the verge of snapping. The author’s skill lies in making the reader question: *Is she drawn to him because of his strength, or because he’s the only one who sees past her lies?*

Gaughen’s prose is lean, efficient, and dripping with atmosphere. The world of Nottingham is vividly rendered, from the mist-laden forests where outlaws hide to the oppressive weight of the Sheriff of Nottingham’s rule. The author’s dialogue crackles with wit and menace, particularly when Scarlet’s sharp tongue cuts through the chatter of the Merry Men. There’s a rhythmic quality to the writing—each sentence feels deliberate, like a well-aimed arrow. Yet, despite the historical setting, the emotional core of the story resonates with modern sensibilities. Scarlet’s struggle to reconcile her past with her present mirrors the internal conflicts of many contemporary protagonists, making her a relatable figure even in a medieval world.

What sets *Scarlet* apart is its refusal to romanticize the outlaw life. Gaughen doesn’t shy away from the brutality of survival—betrayal, violence, and moral ambiguity are woven into the fabric of the story. The arrival of a figure from Scarlet’s past isn’t just a plot twist; it’s a catalyst that forces her to confront the choices that shaped her. The tension between her loyalty to Robin and her own survival instincts creates a narrative that is as gripping as it is emotionally charged.

For fans of historical romance with a dark edge, *Scarlet* is a standout. It’s a tale of rebellion, not just against tyranny, but against the very idea of who we’re expected to be. Gaughen’s writing is deceptively simple, yet it carries the weight of a story that refuses to be forgotten. If you’ve ever wanted a Robin Hood tale that feels as fresh as it does timeless, this is it—a book that proves even legends can be rewritten with new fire.
