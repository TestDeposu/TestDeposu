# The Feather Thief: Beauty, Obsession, and the Natural History Heist of the Century

**Author:** Kirk Wallace Johnson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

In "The Feather Thief: Beauty, Obsession, and the Natural History Heist of the Century," Kirk Wallace Johnson masterfully weaves a captivating narrative that delves into the intriguing world of fly-tying and the obsessive individuals who inhabit it. With a writing style that is both engaging and informative, Johnson recounts the true story of Edwin Rist, a talented young flautist who orchestrated a daring heist at the British Museum of Natural History, stealing hundreds of rare bird skins worth staggering amounts of money.

Johnson's fascination with the case began when his fly-fishing guide shared the story of the feather thief, and from that moment on, he was consumed by a desire to understand the motivations behind Rist's actions. As he embarked on a years-long investigation, Johnson found himself entangled in a complex web of characters, each driven by their own unique brand of obsession. From the fly-tiers who would stop at nothing to acquire the rarest feathers to the collectors who would pay top dollar for them, Johnson skillfully exposes the darker side of human nature.

One of the most striking aspects of "The Feather Thief" is Johnson's ability to balance the thrilling narrative with a thought-provoking exploration of the human condition. As he delves deeper into the world of fly-tying, he raises important questions about the ethics of collecting and the destructive nature of human desire. The Victorian art of salmon fly-tying, which once seemed like a quaint and harmless hobby, is revealed to be a complex and often cutthroat world, where the pursuit of beauty can lead individuals to commit unspeakable crimes.

Johnson's writing style is equally impressive, as he seamlessly transitions between the world of fly-tying and the realm of true crime. His prose is engaging and accessible, making the book an enjoyable read for both enthusiasts of the sport and those who are simply drawn to a good story. The author's use of vivid imagery and descriptive language brings the world of fly-tying to life, immersing the reader in a world that is both fascinating and unsettling.

Throughout the book, Johnson also explores the theme of obsession, highlighting the ways in which it can both inspire and destroy. Edwin Rist's actions, while shocking and misguided, are also a testament to the power of passion and dedication. As Johnson notes, Rist's obsession with fly-tying was not just about the act of tying flies, but about the sense of connection it gave him to the natural world. This nuanced exploration of obsession adds depth and complexity to the narrative, making "The Feather Thief" more than just a thrilling true-crime story.

In conclusion, "The Feather Thief" is a gripping and thought-provoking book that will appeal to fans of true crime, nature writing, and adventure stories. Johnson's engaging writing style and meticulous research make the book a compelling read, while the themes of obsession and the destructive nature of human desire add depth and complexity to the narrative. As a testament to the power of storytelling, "The Feather Thief" is a must-read for anyone looking for a book that will captivate and inspire.
