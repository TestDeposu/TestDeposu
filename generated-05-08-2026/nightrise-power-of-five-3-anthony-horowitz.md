# Nightrise (Power of Five, #3)

**Author:** Anthony Horowitz  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

Anthony Horowitz is no stranger to the art of suspense, but with Nightrise, the third installment in his critically acclaimed Power of Five series, the master of YA thriller-fantasy elevates his craft to a terrifying new echelon. Originally published in 2012, this pulse-pounding narrative dives headfirst into a world on the brink of apocalyptic ruin, where the boundaries between ancient malevolence and modern reality blur with devastating consequences. 

At the core of Nightrise lies a profound and chilling theme: the exploitation of the innocent by a corrupt, predatory global elite. Unlike its predecessors, which focused on individual awakenings, this novel expands the scope of the battle, framing the conflict not just as a supernatural war, but as a systemic fight against modern indifference and corporate greed. Horowitz uses the bleak backdrop of a maximum-security juvenile detention facility in the Arizona desert as a microcosm for a society that preys upon its most vulnerable. The antagonists are not merely monsters of myth; they wear suits, wield corporate influence, and manipulate governments. This grounding of cosmic horror in the gritty, stark realities of institutional cruelty gives the narrative an urgent, contemporary resonance that elevates it far beyond standard genre fare.

The beating heart of this installment, however, is the introduction of the third and fourth of The Five: twin brothers Scott and Jamie Tyler. Possessing a deep, almost uncanny psychic bond, the twins are separated early in the narrative, forcing them to navigate parallel nightmares. Horowitz explores the theme of brotherhood and connection as the ultimate antidote to isolation and despair. The psychic link between the twins serves as both their greatest strength and their most agonizing vulnerability, beautifully illustrating how human bonds can transcend physical distance and psychological torture. Through their eyes, the author examines the resilience of the human spirit when pushed to absolute breaking point.

Stylistically, Horowitz’s prose is razor-sharp, cinematic, and relentlessly fast-paced. He understands the mechanics of tension better than almost anyone working in contemporary fiction. Short, punchy chapters leave readers dangling over precipitous cliffhangers, while his visceral descriptive language makes the suffocating heat of the Nevada desert and the oppressive sterility of the Nightrise facility palpable. There is a cinematic quality to his scene-setting; you can practically hear the ambient hum of high-tech security systems and feel the prickle of supernatural dread in the air. 

Yet, what truly sets Horowitz apart in Nightrise is his tonal balancing act. He writes for a younger demographic without ever talking down to his audience. The violence is stark, the stakes are absolute, and the moral ambiguity faced by the young protagonists reflects the complexities of the real world. The Old Ones may be ancient, primordial threats, but the human collaborators who serve them are painted in chillingly realistic shades of grey and black.

Nightrise is a breathless, deeply atmospheric triumph of dark fantasy. It deepens the mythology of the Gatekeepers series while raising the emotional and physical stakes to unbearable heights. Horowitz delivers a masterclass in pacing, atmosphere, and thematic resonance, cementing his status as a titan of speculative fiction. For anyone willing to step through the gate, this is an unforgettable, white-knuckle descent into darkness.
