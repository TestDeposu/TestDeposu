# Left Neglected

**Author:** Lisa Genova  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

In Left Neglected, bestselling author and neuroscientist Lisa Genova once again proves her unmatched ability to translate complex medical conditions into deeply human, emotionally resonant fiction. Following her acclaimed debut Still Alice, which explored early-onset Alzheimer’s disease, Genova turns her sharp diagnostic eye and compassionate pen toward a lesser-known neurological phenomenon: left neglect. The result is a compelling, fast-paced, and striking novel about the fragility of modern life and the unexpected grace found in forced deceleration.

The story centers on Sarah Nickerson, a quintessential modern superwoman. Juggling a high-powered corporate job, three demanding children, a supportive husband, and an endless mental checklist of societal expectations, Sarah is the epitome of the contemporary working mother trying to have, do, and be it all. Genova captures this hyper-accelerated lifestyle with acute accuracy. Sarah’s existence is a blur of Bluetooth calls, rushed breakfasts, and tightly calibrated schedules. But Genova reminds the reader that it only takes a single, distracted second for the scaffolding of a meticulously managed life to collapse. 

When a momentary glance at a cell phone leads to a devastating car crash, Sarah survives, but her brain does not emerge unscathed. She wakes up with left neglect, a rare and fascinating traumatic brain disorder resulting from damage to the right hemisphere of the parietal lobe. Sarah’s brain simply ceases to acknowledge the existence of the left side of her world. She shaves only the right side of her face, eats only the food on the right half of her plate, reads only the right sides of sentences, and eventually, fails to recognize her own left arm as part of her body. 

Genova’s background as a neuroscientist shines brilliantly here, lending immense authenticity to Sarah’s terrifying medical reality. Yet, what elevates Left Neglected from a mere medical drama into a work of literature is its powerful thematic core. The physical condition of left neglect serves as a brilliant, poignant metaphor for Sarah’s life before the accident. Even when she had full neurological function, Sarah was suffering from a metaphorical form of neglect. She was blind to the left side of her life—the quiet moments, the emotional needs of her children, the true intimacy with her husband, and her own profound exhaustion. She was so hyper-focused on professional achievement and forward momentum that she was missing the entirety of her actual existence. 

Genova’s writing style is accessible, empathetic, and immersive. She refuses to rely on cheap melodrama, choosing instead to ground the narrative in realistic rehabilitation processes and genuine emotional evolution. Through Sarah’s grueling occupational and physical therapy, the reader experiences the frustration of relearning how to dress, walk, and interact with space. More importantly, we witness the agonizing, beautiful process of Sarah learning to slow down. 

As Sarah retrains her brain to consciously scan the left side of her visual field, she simultaneously learns to pay attention to the overlooked people and priorities in her heart. The pacing mirrors this journey: it begins with a breathless, chaotic rush before abruptly halting into the stillness of a hospital room, eventually finding a new, more balanced rhythm. 

Left Neglected is ultimately a story of resilience and redemption. It challenges the modern obsession with perfectionism and busyness, forcing us to ask uncomfortable questions about our own daily distractions. Genova crafts a narrative that is both an urgent cautionary tale and an inspiring celebration of the human spirit's capacity to adapt. It is a striking reminder that sometimes, it takes a catastrophic loss of perception to finally see what truly matters.
