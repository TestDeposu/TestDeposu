# The Fairy Godmother (Five Hundred Kingdoms, #1)

**Author:** Mercedes Lackey  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

**A Delightful Romp Through the Five Hundred Kingdoms: A Review of "The Fairy Godmother"**

Mercedes Lackey's latest foray into the world of fantasy romance, "The Fairy Godmother," is a charming and engaging tale that breathes new life into the classic Cinderella narrative. As the first installment in the Five Hundred Kingdoms series, this book sets the stage for a fantastical journey that will transport readers to a realm where legends come alive and fairy tales are lived.

The story follows Elena Klovis, a young woman whose destiny was once tied to the Cinderella legend. However, fate had other plans, and Elena must now navigate a new reality where breaking free from the "The Tradition" that governs the Five Hundred Kingdoms comes with a steep price. Enter her fairy godmother, who offers Elena a most unexpected job: to become her apprentice and learn the ways of magic. As Elena embarks on this new path, she finds herself guiding others along their own fated paths while dealing with arrogant princes and the complexities of her own relationships.

At its core, "The Fairy Godmother" is a thought-provoking exploration of the nature of fate, free will, and the responsibilities that come with one's destiny. Lackey skillfully weaves together elements of fantasy, romance, and character-driven drama to create a narrative that will resonate with readers who appreciate tales of self-discovery and empowerment.

One of the most striking aspects of this book is Lackey's masterful use of world-building. The Five Hundred Kingdoms is a richly detailed and immersive realm, where myth and legend collide with the mundane. Her descriptions of the magical landscapes, creatures, and characters are vivid and captivating, drawing the reader into the world with ease. Moreover, Lackey's take on the classic fairy tale archetypes is refreshingly nuanced, subverting expectations and adding depth to what could have been simplistic characters.

Lackey's writing style is another highlight of "The Fairy Godmother." Her prose is engaging, witty, and accessible, making this book an excellent choice for readers who appreciate a fast-paced and entertaining read. Her characters are well-developed, with flaws and strengths that make them relatable and human. Elena's journey, in particular, is a compelling exploration of self-discovery, as she navigates the complexities of her new role and confronts the challenges of her own desires.

In terms of themes, "The Fairy Godmother" explores a range of thought-provoking ideas, including the tension between fate and free will, the challenges of self-discovery, and the importance of embracing one's true nature. Lackey's narrative is replete with moments of insight and humor, making this book an enjoyable and satisfying read.

In conclusion, "The Fairy Godmother" is a delightful romp through the Five Hundred Kingdoms, a fantasy realm that will captivate readers with its rich world-building, engaging characters, and nuanced exploration of themes. Mercedes Lackey's writing style is sure to please fans of fantasy romance, and this book is an excellent choice for anyone looking for a compelling and entertaining read. So, if you're ready to embark on a journey through the Five Hundred Kingdoms, then look no further than "The Fairy Godmother."
