# Fate (My Blood Approves, #2)

**Author:** Amanda Hocking  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

**A Dark Romance Reimagined: Amanda Hocking’s *Fate* and the Enduring Allure of *My Blood Approves***

Amanda Hocking’s *My Blood Approves* saga has long been a cornerstone of modern dark romance, a series that blends gothic atmosphere, morally ambiguous love, and the intoxicating pull of forbidden passion. *Fate*—the second installment in this reimagined, expanded bundle—carries forward the saga’s signature themes with a fresh urgency, proving that Hocking’s knack for emotional intensity and atmospheric storytelling remains as sharp as ever. This isn’t just a sequel; it’s an evolution, a deepening of the world Alice Bonham inhabits, where the line between survival and surrender blurs with every heartbeat.

At its core, *Fate* is a story about the cost of love—and the lengths one will go to protect it. Alice, now entangled in a world of vampires, faces choices that test her humanity, her loyalty, and her will to survive. The romance here is electric, but it’s never sanitized. Hocking doesn’t shy away from the brutality beneath the allure, nor does she romanticize the danger. Instead, she frames it as a dance between desire and destruction, where every kiss could be a kiss of death, and every promise might come with a price. The tension isn’t just sexual; it’s existential. Alice’s journey is one of reckoning, as she grapples with the fact that some loves are worth dying for, while others might just be worth surviving.

What makes *Fate* stand out is Hocking’s ability to weave emotional depth into her prose without sacrificing pacing. Her writing is lean, visceral, and unapologetically dramatic—perfect for readers who crave both escapism and substance. The dialogue crackles with tension, the world-building is immersive, and the characters, particularly Alice, are painfully relatable. She’s flawed, vulnerable, and fiercely resilient, a protagonist who feels like a breath of fresh air in a genre often dominated by more polished, less messy heroines. Her struggles—with trust, with power, with her own mortality—resonate because they’re universal, even as the stakes feel impossibly high.

The expanded bundle is a masterstroke, offering not just the five core novels but also a prequel novella (*Letters to Elise*), a holiday short story (*Little Tree*), and bonus lore (*World of My Blood Approves* and *Brief Histories*). These additions deepen the world, giving readers more context, more backstory, and more reasons to lose themselves in this gothic, blood-soaked universe. The prequel, in particular, is a delightful tease, offering a glimpse into the origins of the saga’s most compelling characters. Meanwhile, *Little Tree* serves as a charming, standalone interlude—proof that Hocking’s storytelling versatility knows no bounds.

Yet, *Fate* isn’t just about escapism. It’s a meditation on the nature of fate itself—whether it’s something we’re destined for, or something we choose. Alice’s arc is one of defiance, as she refuses to be a passive victim, even when the world conspires against her. There’s a quiet strength in her choices, a refusal to let fear dictate her path. This is a story about empowerment, about reclaiming agency in a world that wants to control you. It’s dark, it’s dangerous, and it’s utterly compelling.

For fans of dark romance, *Fate* delivers the kind of emotional punch and atmospheric richness that Hocking is renowned for. It’s a story that lingers, that haunts you long after the last page, because it’s not just about vampires and love—it’s about the human cost of both. The expanded bundle is a must-read for anyone who wants to dive deeper into this world, to explore its lore, its characters, and its enduring appeal. Hocking’s writing is as sharp and seductive as ever, a reminder that some stories are worth getting lost in—even if the world you lose yourself in is one of blood and shadows.

If you’ve ever wondered what it would be like to fall for something—or someone—dangerous, *Fate* is your answer. Just be prepared to get lost in the darkness.
