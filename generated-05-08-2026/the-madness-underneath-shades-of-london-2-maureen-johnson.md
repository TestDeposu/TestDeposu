# The Madness Underneath (Shades of London, #2)

**Author:** Maureen Johnson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

London is a city built on bones, a sprawling labyrinth where the weight of history presses hard against the modern pavement. In The Madness Underneath, the second installment of Maureen Johnson’s acclaimed Shades of London series, this atmospheric truth becomes the literal battleground for a teenage girl trying to navigate the terrifying landscape of the supernatural. Following a harrowing brush with a Jack the Ripper copycat, protagonist Rory Deveaux finds herself fundamentally altered. No longer just a newcomer attempting to fit into a prestigious boarding school, Rory possesses a newly intensified paranormal ability that bridges the gap between the living world and the restless dead. When a baffling new string of deaths plagues the city, Rory realizes these tragedies are far from accidental. Blending sharp British wit with genuine, bone-chilling suspense, Johnson crafts a sequel that dives deeper into the dark, hidden corners of the English capital and the human psyche.

At the heart of the novel lies the compelling theme of invisible burdens and the desperate need to be heard. Rory is an outsider twice over: an American teenager displaced in a historic London boarding school, and a reluctant seer burdened with the knowledge of things the rest of the world desperately chooses to ignore. The supernatural elements in Johnson’s universe do not exist merely for cheap scares; they serve as a brilliant metaphor for trauma, isolation, and the weight of secrets. Just as London conceals its ancient horrors beneath layers of concrete and commercialism, Rory must mask her profound psychological scars behind a veneer of teenage normalcy. The thematic core of the book questions what happens when a young person is forced to carry the weight of public safety on shoulders already buckling under the pressure of fitting in. When the secret ghost-policing squad dismisses her warnings, the narrative sharply critiques institutional apathy and the dangerous tendency of authority figures to silence those who speak uncomfortable truths.

Johnson’s authorial style is the true anchor of the narrative, elevating the book above standard young adult paranormal fare. Her prose is deceptively breezy, anchored by a biting, self-aware sense of humor that prevents the darker material from becoming overly oppressive. Rory’s internal monologue is pitch-perfect—acerbic, observant, and authentically adolescent without ever dipping into grating territory. Yet, this conversational tone acts as a clever Trojan horse for genuinely unsettling horror. Johnson excels at pacing; she lulls the reader into a false sense of security with witty banter about school life, British colloquialisms, and interpersonal drama, only to suddenly pull back the curtain on a scene of stark, atmospheric terror. The depiction of London itself is written with such vivid, sensory detail that the city practically breathes alongside the characters. The damp air, the looming Gothic architecture, and the chilling presence of the unseen combine to create a deeply immersive environment. Furthermore, Johnson’s plotting is tight and layered, seamlessly blending procedural mystery elements with supernatural lore. She maintains an intoxicating sense of ambiguity throughout, ensuring the reader is constantly second-guessing who—or what—is truly responsible for the escalating terror.

The Madness Underneath succeeds brilliantly because it refuses to rest on the laurels of its predecessor. Maureen Johnson deepens her world-building, expands her characters, and ramps up the stakes with surgical precision. It is a smart, chilling, and compulsively readable thriller that proves the scariest monsters are often the ones hiding in plain sight. For readers who crave a story where sharp intellect meets spine-tingling suspense, this novel is an absolute triumph of contemporary supernatural fiction.
