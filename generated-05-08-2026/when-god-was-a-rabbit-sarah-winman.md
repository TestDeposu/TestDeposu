# When God Was a Rabbit

**Author:** Sarah Winman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2026

In Sarah Winman's breathtaking debut novel, "When God Was a Rabbit," the author skillfully weaves together a poignant and captivating tale that delves into the complexities of love, friendship, and the human experience. The narrative follows Eleanor Maud, a young girl growing up in 1970s England, as she navigates the ups and downs of life, constantly seeking validation and acceptance from the world around her.

One of the standout features of this novel is its unique narrative voice. Winman's prose is a masterful blend of wit, humor, and pathos, making it impossible not to become completely invested in Elly's journey. The author's use of lyrical language and evocative descriptions transports the reader to the vibrant landscapes of England and New York City, immersing them in the world of the characters.

At its core, "When God Was a Rabbit" is a story about the search for meaning and connection in a chaotic world. Elly, the protagonist, grapples with the notion of a higher power and the concept of God as a benevolent force in her life. This theme serves as a poignant metaphor for the universal human quest for love, acceptance, and belonging. Through Elly's relationships with her brother Joe, her best friend Jenny Penny, and others, Winman masterfully illustrates the various forms love can take, from familial bonds to platonic connections.

The author's exploration of the complexities of female friendship is particularly noteworthy. The relationship between Elly and Jenny Penny is multifaceted and nuanced, with all its attendant joys, rivalries, and heartbreaks. This portrayal feels refreshingly authentic and rings true to the reader, capturing the messy, unpredictable nature of female friendships.

Throughout the book, Winman's writing is characterized by its tenderness, humor, and unflinching honesty. The author tackles difficult subjects, such as grief, trauma, and identity, with sensitivity and insight, never shying away from the pain and vulnerability that come with real emotional experience. At the same time, the novel also celebrates the resilience of the human spirit, highlighting the remarkable capacity for love and connection that exists within us all.

One of the most striking aspects of "When God Was a Rabbit" is its sweeping historical context. The book seamlessly spans multiple decades and geographical locations, from post-war England to pre-9/11 New York. Winman's meticulous research and attention to historical detail add depth and richness to the narrative, infusing the story with a sense of timelessness and universality.

In conclusion, "When God Was a Rabbit" is a triumph of contemporary literature. Winman's mesmerizing debut novel is a must-read for anyone who has ever felt the sting of rejection, the balm of love, or the thrill of connection. Through its powerful exploration of the human experience, this book reminds us that, despite our differences, we are all connected in ways both profound and unseen.
