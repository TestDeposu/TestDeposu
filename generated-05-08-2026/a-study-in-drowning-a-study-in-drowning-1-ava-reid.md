# A Study in Drowning (A Study in Drowning, #1)

**Author:** Ava Reid  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

A Study in Drowning pulls the reader into a mist‑laden world where the line between myth and memory is as thin as the veil that separates Hiraeth Manor’s crumbling halls from the restless spirits that linger within. Ava Reid’s debut YA novel feels less like a conventional fantasy and more like a spell cast in prose—each sentence shimmering with the same uncanny beauty that haunts Effy Sayre’s tattered copy of *Angharad*. The novel’s central theme is the perilous allure of storytelling itself: how the tales we cling to can both sustain us and lead us to our undoing. Effy’s lifelong devotion to the Fairy King myth is not merely a whimsical escapism; it is a lifeline forged in the trauma of loss and the yearning for meaning in a world that refuses to give her answers. When she is thrust into the contest to redesign Myrddin’s estate, the fairy tale she has idolized becomes a laboratory where truth, ambition, and desire are tested against the stone‑cold reality of legacy and deceit.

Reid handles this theme with a style that is simultaneously lush and incisive. Her prose reads like a gothic ballad—rich in sensory detail, yet precise enough to cut through the fog of superstition. Descriptions of Hiraeth Manor breathe: the scent of damp stone, the whisper of wind through broken stained‑glass, the way moonlight pools on cracked marble like spilled ink. These atmospheric touches are never decorative for their own sake; they mirror Effy’s internal landscape, amplifying her visions and doubts. At the same time, Reid’s dialogue crackles with intellectual rivalry. Preston Héloury, the stoic literature scholar determined to debunk Myrddin’s genius, offers a counterpoint to Effy’s romantic idealism. Their exchanges are sharp, layered with academic wit and a slow‑burning tension that evolves from antagonism to something far more complicated—a rivals‑to‑lovers dynamic that feels earned rather than forced.

What sets Reid apart is her ability to weave multiple genres into a cohesive whole without letting any one element overwhelm the others. The novel sits comfortably at the intersection of historical fantasy, gothic mystery, and dark academia, yet it never feels like a checklist of tropes. Instead, each genre serves the central inquiry into how narratives shape identity. The myth of Angharad functions as both a mirror and a mask: it reflects Effy’s deepest desires while also obscuring the more painful truths about her family’s past and the manor’s haunted history. Reid’s treatment of the supernatural is similarly nuanced; magic is never a convenient deus ex machina but a palpable force that interacts with the characters’ psychology, often manifesting as obsession, guilt, or the seductive promise of redemption.

Critically, the novel’s pacing mirrors the ebb and flow of a tide—slow, deliberate buildup gives way to sudden, shocking revelations that leave the reader breathless. Reid trusts her audience to sit with ambiguity, allowing the gothic atmosphere to linger long after the final page. This restraint is a strength; rather than over‑explaining the folklore, she lets the mythic whispers haunt the margins, inviting readers to fill the silences with their own imaginations. The result is a reading experience that feels both intimate and expansive, as if one is wandering the echoing corridors of Hiraeth Manor alongside Effy, hearing the distant lilt of a fairy‑song that may be real, may be imagined, and may be the very thing that saves—or destroys—her.

In sum, *A Study in Drowning* is a hauntingly beautiful debut that showcases Ava Reid’s talent for blending lyrical prose with psychological depth. Her exploration of storytelling as both salvation and peril resonates powerfully in a genre that often leans on escapism alone. Readers who crave atmospheric dark academia, gothic romance, and a mythic mystery that refuses to yield easy answers will find themselves irresistibly drawn into this tale—just as Effy is drawn to the Fairy King’s shadow.
