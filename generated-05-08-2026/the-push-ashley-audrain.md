# The Push

**Author:** Ashley Audrain  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

What makes a mother? Is it an instinctive, all-consuming warmth, or is it a social contract we are forced to sign in the dark, terrified we might not be strong enough to keep it? In her chilling and relentlessly gripping 2021 debut, The Push, author Ashley Audrain strips away the cultural varnish of glossy parenting magazines to expose the jagged, bleeding underbelly of motherhood. This is not a gentle story of lullabies and first steps; it is a tense, claustrophobic psychological drama that asks the ultimate taboo question: What happens when you look at your own child and suspect she is a monster?

The novel follows Blythe Connor, a woman desperate to break the generational curse of emotional detachment handed down by her own cold mother. When she gives birth to her daughter, Violet, Blythe expects the transformative, sunlit joy she was promised. Instead, she finds herself drowning in the exhausting, monotonous trenches of early childcare, plagued by a mounting, icy conviction that Violet is fundamentally different—and deeply unsettling. Violet does not gaze into her mother’s eyes with adoration; she watches, calculates, and resists connection. 

Yet, the true horror of The Push does not stem solely from the chilling possibility that Blythe’s child might be inherently malicious. The real terror lies in the gaslighting. Blythe’s husband, Fox, dismisses her gut instincts as postpartum paranoia, anxiety, and overactive imagination. As Fox minimizes her fears, Audrain expertly steers the narrative into dangerous, unreliable waters, forcing the reader to constantly teeter on the edge of a knife: Is Blythe a visionary mother seeing the truth of her child, or is she losing her grip on reality? 

Audrain’s writing style is both poetic and propulsive. Her prose is razor-sharp, cutting straight to the bone of domestic anxiety with sentences that hum with suppressed tension. She structures the novel as an extended, urgent confession—Blythe is speaking directly to Fox, detailing the anatomy of their broken marriage and the making of their family’s destruction. This direct-address format creates an intoxicating intimacy. You cannot look away; you are trapped inside Blythe’s racing mind, forced to feel every sting of disbelief and every breath of suffocating dread.

When the couple has a second child, a boy named Sam, the narrative shifts brilliantly. With Sam, Blythe finally experiences the blissful, effortless maternal bond she had always dreamed of. Sam is affectionate and warm, and even Violet seems to dote on her baby brother. For a fleeting moment, Blythe believes her nightmare is over, that she simply misunderstood her daughter all along. But Audrain is a master of suspense, and the devastating, heart-stopping tragedy that follows changes everything in an instant, leading to a climax that will leave readers breathless.

The Push is a tour de force that challenges everything society assumes about female intuition, maternal instinct, and the terrifying weight of what we owe our children. It tackles the intersection of nature and nurture with unflinching brutality, proving that some pushes in life are so violent they shatter a person beyond repair. Immersive, harrowing, and impossible to put down, Audrain’s debut is destined to spark fierce book club debates. It is a stark reminder that sometimes the scariest thing in the world isn’t what’s hiding in the dark—it’s the stranger sitting right across from you at the breakfast table, sharing your blood.
