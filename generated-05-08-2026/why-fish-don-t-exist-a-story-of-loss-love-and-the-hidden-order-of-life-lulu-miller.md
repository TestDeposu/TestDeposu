# Why Fish Don’t Exist: A Story of Loss, Love, and the Hidden Order of Life

**Author:** Lulu Miller  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

Lulu Miller’s debut book, *Why Fish Don’t Exist: A Story of Loss, Love, and the Hidden Order of Life*, is a literary anomaly. It defies neat categorization just as effectively as its subject matter defies biological definition. Part biography, part deeply vulnerable memoir, and part philosophical adventure, Miller’s book takes the reader on a dizzying expedition through the history of science, only to land somewhere entirely unexpected: an interrogation of how we assign meaning to human existence. 

At the heart of the narrative is David Starr Jordan, a towering figure in late-19th and early-20th-century science. As a relentless taxonomist, Jordan was obsessed with categorizing the natural world, ultimately discovering nearly a fifth of the fish known to humans at the time. Miller frames Jordan not merely as a historical footnote, but as a staggering symbol of human hubris and resilience. When disaster struck—most notably the 1906 San Francisco earthquake, which smashed his life's work of preserved specimen collections to the floor—Jordan did not despair. He immediately began sewing tags back onto the surviving fish amidst the wreckage. 

Initially, Miller viewed Jordan with skepticism, interpreting his relentless drive as a foolish, stubborn denial of reality. However, as she found herself navigating her own profound personal and professional unravelling, her perspective shifted. She began to see Jordan not as a cautionary tale, but as a potential blueprint for endurance in a fundamentally chaotic universe. 

Yet, as Miller peels back the layers of Jordan’s life, the book takes a breathtaking, dark turn. The quest for absolute order in nature reveals a sinister underside. Jordan’s unwavering belief in a rigid hierarchy of life—where humans sat firmly at the top, with some humans unfortunately deemed closer to the bottom—lured him into the dark waters of the American eugenics movement. The man who dedicated his life to naming and organizing creation was also complicit in the forced sterilization of marginalized populations, blinding himself to the complex, messy reality of human life in the name of evolutionary progress.

This is where Miller’s writing style shines with brilliance. She weaves her personal heartbreak, historical investigation, and philosophy together with the propulsive energy of a masterclass thriller. Her voice is intimate, irreverent, deeply empathetic, and entirely devoid of academic dryness. She writes with a conversational vulnerability that makes heavy existential concepts feel intensely personal. 

The ultimate thesis of the book is captured brilliantly in its provocative title. Through modern genetic science, Miller dismantles the very concept of the "fish." Scientifically speaking, a lungfish is more closely related to a cow than to a salmon; "fish" is a human-invented category that ignores evolutionary truth in favor of our desire to pigeonhole the world. By realizing that fish do not actually exist, Miller forces us to confront a larger, liberating truth: the rigid hierarchies and ladders of importance we impose on life are entirely of our own making. 

*Why Fish Don’t Exist* is a magnificent, subversive triumph. It is a book that begins as a study of a stubborn scientist and evolves into a profound manifesto on how to find wonder, love, and meaning in a universe that is indifferent to us. Miller does not offer easy answers for how to survive loss or chaos; instead, she offers something better—a radical reimagining of how we see the world beneath our feet, and the courage to love it even when its order falls apart.
