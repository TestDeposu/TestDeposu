# How to Love

**Author:** Katie Cotugno  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

"How to Love" by Katie Cotugno is a poignant and evocative debut novel that delves into the complexities of first love, heartbreak, and second chances. Through the eyes of Reena Montero, we experience a love story that spans years, weaving a narrative of messy, complicated relationships and the bittersweet path to forgiveness.

The novel's dual timeline, alternating between "Before" and "After" chapters, masterfully unravels the mystery of Sawyer LeGrande's disappearance and his subsequent return. This narrative structure allows Cotugno to explore the nuances of Reena and Sawyer's relationship, revealing the depth of their emotions and the intensity of their love.

One of the most striking aspects of Cotugno's writing is her ability to capture the intensity and all-consuming nature of first love. Reena's feelings for Sawyer are palpable, and Cotugno's prose conveys the desperation, longing, and uncertainty that often accompany this stage of life. As Reena navigates her emotions, Cotugno skillfully conveys the turmoil and vulnerability that come with loving someone who may not love you back.

However, it's not just the romance that makes "How to Love" compelling; it's also the exploration of Reena's growth and development as a character. After Sawyer's disappearance, Reena is left to pick up the pieces and rebuild her life. Her journey is marked by resilience, determination, and a fierce devotion to her daughter, Hannah. Cotugno's portrayal of Reena's transformation from a lovesick teenager to a strong, independent mother is both authentic and inspiring.

The supporting characters in the novel are equally well-crafted, particularly Reena's family and friends. Cotugno's depiction of the relationships between Reena and her loved ones adds depth and complexity to the narrative, highlighting the ways in which our connections with others shape us and influence our choices.

Cotugno's writing style is lyrical and evocative, with a keen attention to detail that brings the characters and their world to life. Her prose is both poignant and humorous, capturing the ups and downs of adolescence and the challenges of young adulthood.

If there's one criticism to be made, it's that the novel's pacing can be somewhat uneven. At times, the transitions between the "Before" and "After" chapters feel a bit disjointed, disrupting the flow of the narrative. However, this is a minor quibble, and the novel's strengths far outweigh its weaknesses.

Overall, "How to Love" is a captivating and emotional read that will resonate with fans of Sarah Dessen and John Green. Cotugno's debut novel is a powerful exploration of love, heartbreak, and the complexities of human relationships. With its well-developed characters, nuanced themes, and engaging narrative, "How to Love" is a must-read for anyone looking for a compelling and thought-provoking story.
