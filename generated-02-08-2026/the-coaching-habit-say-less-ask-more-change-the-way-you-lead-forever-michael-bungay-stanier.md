# The Coaching Habit: Say Less, Ask More & Change the Way You Lead Forever

**Author:** Michael Bungay Stanier  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

In "The Coaching Habit: Say Less, Ask More & Change the Way You Lead Forever", Michael Bungay Stanier revolutionizes the way managers and leaders approach coaching, transforming it from a formal, infrequent event to an informal, everyday habit. Drawing on his extensive experience training over 10,000 managers worldwide, Stanier offers a refreshing and innovative approach to coaching that yields impressive results.

At the heart of the book lies the idea that coaching is not about providing answers or advice, but about empowering others to find their own solutions. Stanier argues that this requires courage, vulnerability, and a willingness to ask questions rather than offer solutions. He provides seven essential coaching questions that can be used in various situations, from kick-starting conversations to staying focused and ensuring that others find coaching beneficial.

What sets "The Coaching Habit" apart from traditional how-to manuals is its engaging and conversational tone. Stanier's writing is witty, accessible, and infused with a deep understanding of human behavior and neuroscience. He combines insider information with research-based insights, making the book an invaluable resource for managers and leaders seeking to develop practical coaching skills.

One of the book's greatest strengths is its focus on turning practical advice into practiced habits. Stanier recognizes that coaching is an art that requires practice and dedication to master. He provides interactive training tools and exercises to help readers integrate the seven coaching questions into their daily work, making it easier to develop a coaching habit that yields tangible results.

Brené Brown, author of "Rising Strong" and "Daring Greatly", aptly describes the book as "practical and inspiring", highlighting the transformative impact of Stanier's coaching questions on leadership and support. Indeed, "The Coaching Habit" has the potential to change the way managers and leaders work, enabling them to work less hard and have more impact.

In a world where leadership and management are often associated with providing answers and solutions, Stanier's book offers a refreshing alternative. By emphasizing the importance of asking questions and empowering others, he provides a powerful framework for developing coaching skills that can be applied in various contexts.

In conclusion, "The Coaching Habit" is a must-read for managers, leaders, and anyone seeking to develop practical coaching skills. Stanier's engaging writing style, combined with his expertise and research-based insights, make the book an invaluable resource for anyone looking to improve their leadership and management skills.
