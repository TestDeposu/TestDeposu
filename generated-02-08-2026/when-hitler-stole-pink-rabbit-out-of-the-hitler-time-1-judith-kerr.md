# When Hitler Stole Pink Rabbit (Out of the Hitler Time, #1)

**Author:** Judith Kerr  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

"When Hitler Stole Pink Rabbit" by Judith Kerr is a poignant and enduring tale of a young girl's experience with the turmoil of World War II. This award-winning novel is the first installment in a beloved trilogy, drawing heavily from Kerr's own life as a Jewish family fled Nazi Germany in the early 1930s. With her unique blend of wit, warmth, and historical insight, Kerr guides readers through the bewildering and often frightening journey of her protagonist, Anna.

The novel masterfully conveys the disorienting nature of living in a country that has been drastically changed overnight. As 9-year-old Anna navigates her way through her increasingly uncertain world, her once-familiar surroundings are replete with menacing symbols of the growing threat of Nazi Germany. The introduction of Adolf Hitler's sinister face on posters sets the tone for an escalating series of events that will propel Anna's family, along with millions of others, into an exile from their homeland.

Throughout the narrative, Kerr skillfully captures the essence of Anna's emotions, skillfully conveying the complexity of living through historical events from the perspective of a child. The author's use of humor and warmth never undermines the gravity of the situation, making the story both haunting and ultimately hopeful. Anna's experiences are often juxtaposed with her own curiosity and wonder, creating an emotionally charged account of her family's odyssey.

One of Kerr's greatest strengths lies in her ability to balance the darker elements of the narrative with the inherent innocence of her protagonist. Her portrayal of Anna's relationship with her family, particularly her brother Max and mother, is tender and authentic, adding depth to the story. Furthermore, Kerr's masterful use of language ensures that the historical context is not lost amidst the personal struggles of the characters.

The novel's portrayal of displacement and the struggle to adapt to a new environment resonates profoundly. Kerr's vivid descriptions of the family's journey from Germany to England, and their eventual settling in, poignantly evoke the sense of dislocation that many people have faced throughout history. Her own experiences as a Jewish refugee inform the narrative, lending an authenticity and weight to the story.

Ultimately, "When Hitler Stole Pink Rabbit" is a captivating and poignant portrayal of one family's courage in the face of adversity. Judith Kerr's masterful storytelling, coupled with her insightful and engaging style, makes this novel an unforgettable reading experience. This is a must-read for anyone interested in the personal stories behind one of the most pivotal episodes in modern history.
