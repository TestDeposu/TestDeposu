# Blood for Blood (Ziba MacKenzie, #1)

**Author:** Victoria Selman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

Dark, visceral, and relentlessly atmospheric, Victoria Selman’s Blood for Blood introduces readers to a chilling new voice in psychological crime fiction. Right from its haunting opening pages, the novel establishes an environment of suffocating tension, transforming the picturesque London neighborhood of Primrose Hill into a claustrophobic hunting ground. Within the span of a single week, two young women are found brutally murdered, stabbed to death in a manner that suggests the calculated precision of a seasoned predator. Scotland Yard immediately suspects a serial killer is loose on the capital's streets, prompting them to bring in criminal profiler Ziba MacKenzie. But Selman quickly pivots the narrative from a standard police procedural into something far more intimate, dangerous, and psychologically complex. 

The core theme of Blood for Blood explores the terrifying vulnerability of identity and the inescapable ghosts of the past. When Ziba first examines the crime scenes, the investigation ceases to be a professional detachment and transforms into a waking nightmare. The murdered women bear a startling, undeniable resemblance to Ziba herself. This doppelgänger motif is handled with masterclass suspense, elevating the stakes from a race to catch a killer to a desperate struggle for self-preservation. Selman uses this physical mirroring to explore deeper psychological fragmentation. Ziba is not merely hunting a monster; she is forced to confront the chilling possibility that she is the intended final piece of a morbid puzzle. The narrative skillfully blurs the line between the hunter and the hunted, examining how past traumas can manifest into physical threats that eventually come back to claim us. 

Selman’s writing style is taut, sharp, and fiercely urgent. She eschews bloated exposition in favor of brisk pacing and razor-sharp dialogue that keeps the reader perpetually off-balance. The prose is lean yet evocative, painting London not as a postcard city, but as a labyrinth of shadows where danger lurks behind every affluent Victorian facade. Her background in the psychological nuances of criminal behavior shines through in the meticulous crafting of Ziba MacKenzie. Ziba is far from the infallible, emotionless detective cliché often found in thriller fiction; she is brilliantly sharp, yet visibly scarred and profoundly human. Her internal monologue reveals a woman walking a tightrope between professional duty and overwhelming dread, making her triumphs hard-won and her moments of terror genuinely palpable. 

Furthermore, Selman excels at misdirection. As Ziba digs deeper into the lives of the victims, trying to unearth the invisible thread connecting them to each other—and ultimately to her—the plot unfurls like a tightly wound spring. Every potential suspect feels both plausible and deeply unsettling, keeping the reader guessing until the narrative's breath-stealing climax. The atmosphere grows progressively heavier, matching Ziba’s realization that this case might be her last, both professionally and biologically. 

Blood for Blood is a triumphant debut for the Ziba MacKenzie series, offering a masterclass in modern psychological suspense. Victoria Selman has crafted a taut, adrenaline-fueled page-turner that lingers in the mind long after the final page is turned. For fans of dark, character-driven crime fiction who appreciate a protagonist with grit and a mystery that genuinely subverts expectations, this novel is an absolute must-read.
