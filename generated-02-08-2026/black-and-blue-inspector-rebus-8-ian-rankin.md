# Black and Blue (Inspector Rebus, #8)

**Author:** Ian Rankin  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

In the gritty world of Scottish crime fiction, Ian Rankin stands tall, and his eighth Inspector Rebus novel, Black and Blue, is a testament to his mastery of the genre. This gripping thriller is a complex web of intrigue, deception, and murder, set against the backdrop of Edinburgh's toughest suburbs and the oil rigs of Aberdeen.

At the heart of the story is the enigmatic Inspector John Rebus, a man with a penchant for getting entangled in the most complex and sinister cases. This time, he's tasked with investigating the murder of an off-duty oilman, which leads him to cross paths with a copycat killer, dubbed "Bible Johnny" by the media. As Rebus navigates the treacherous landscape of police politics and media scrutiny, he must confront his own demons and make difficult choices to stay one step ahead of the killer.

Rankin's writing style is as flinty as the Scottish landscape he so vividly describes. His prose is sharp, witty, and laced with a dark humor that adds depth to the narrative. The characters are multidimensional and richly drawn, with Rebus being the epitome of a flawed yet fascinating protagonist. The author's ability to balance multiple plot threads and create a sense of tension that permeates every page is a hallmark of his skill as a storyteller.

One of the main themes that emerges from the novel is the exploration of identity and how it can be manipulated and distorted. The killer, Bible Johnny, is a master of reinvention, using the legend of the original Bible John to further his own twisted agenda. Rebus, too, is forced to confront his own identity and the persona he's created as a detective. This theme is woven throughout the narrative, adding a layer of complexity to the story that elevates it beyond a simple crime thriller.

Rankin's use of setting is also noteworthy. The bleak, rugged landscape of Scotland provides a stark contrast to the darkness that lurks within the human heart. The oil rigs of Aberdeen, with their harsh conditions and isolated workers, serve as a potent symbol of the isolation and disconnection that can lead individuals down a path of destruction.

The supporting cast is equally well-drawn, from the enigmatic and alluring Rhona to the complex and troubled Lenny Spaven. Each character adds depth and nuance to the narrative, making it impossible to guess who will be the next victim or who will ultimately be revealed as the killer.

In conclusion, Black and Blue is a masterclass in crime fiction, with Ian Rankin at the helm, expertly guiding the reader through a complex and twisted narrative. With its intricate plotting, vivid characters, and exploration of themes that linger long after the final page is turned, this novel is a must-read for fans of the genre. Rebus, with all his flaws and contradictions, is a character that will stay with you long after the book is finished, and his story is one that will haunt you for a long time to come.
