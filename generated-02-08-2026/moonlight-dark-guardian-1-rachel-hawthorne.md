# Moonlight (Dark Guardian, #1)

**Author:** Rachel Hawthorne  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Rachel Hawthorne’s 2009 young adult paranormal romance, Moonlight, plunges readers headfirst into a world where the rugged wilderness meets the breathless, high-stakes intensity of first love. As the inaugural installment in the Dark Guardian series, this novel establishes a vibrant playground for supernatural secrets, blending the timeless allure of adolescent self-discovery with the feral pulse of werewolf mythology. At its core, Moonlight explores the intoxicating and inescapable theme of fated mates, anchoring a fast-paced narrative in the magnetic pull between destiny and personal awakening.

The thematic heart of the novel beats around identity and belonging, brilliantly mirrored in the duality of its setting. Set against the majestic, sprawling backdrop of a national park, Hawthorne uses the great outdoors not merely as a scenic backdrop, but as a living, breathing entity that reflects the wild, untamed nature of her characters. Rheena—an adopted seventeen-year-old girl who finds peace in camping and the open air—embodies the universal adolescent quest to understand who she is and where she fits in. Little does she know, her deeply ingrained love for nature is a biological foreshadowing of a heritage she has yet to claim. When the veil of her ordinary human existence is torn away, revealing a dormant wolf gene and a secret pack known as the Dark Guardians, Hawthorne crafts a compelling metaphor for the terrifying yet exhilarating transition into adulthood.

Intertwined with this coming-of-age arc is the potent trope of the protector romance. Lucas, the brooding, powerful pack leader, serves as both an anchor and a catalyst. His agonizing restraint—watching Rheena from afar, waiting for her to awaken to her true nature while shielding her from unseen perils—infuses the narrative with a palpable tension. Hawthorne excels at capturing the delicious agony of a one-sided attraction that is secretly mutual, building the romantic momentum alongside the external threats of a sinister bio-tech company and a dangerous rogue shifter. 

Hawthorne’s writing style in Moonlight is brisk, accessible, and unapologetically atmospheric. She writes with a cinematic clarity that appeals directly to the young adult demographic, prioritizing emotional immediacy and sensory details. The rustle of pine needles, the snap of a campfire, and the electric thrill of a lingering glance are rendered with a vividness that pulls the reader straight into the forest alongside the characters. While the prose doesn’t bog itself down in overly complex world-building, it delivers precisely what the genre demands: breathless pacing, high emotional stakes, and a heavy dose of supernatural mystery. 

Ultimately, Moonlight succeeds because it captures the raw vulnerability of first love and marries it seamlessly with the primal energy of pack dynamics. It is an entertaining, deeply engaging summer romance adventure that sets a high bar for its series, leaving readers eager to dive deeper into the shadows of the Dark Guardians' forest.
