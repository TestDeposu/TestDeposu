# Small Favor (The Dresden Files, #10)

**Author:** Jim Butcher  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

**Small Favor: Where Urban Fantasy Meets High-Stakes Mystery**

Jim Butcher’s *Small Favor*—the tenth installment in *The Dresden Files*—is a masterclass in blending urban fantasy with the sharp, suspenseful edge of a classic mystery. At its core, this book is about trust, betrayal, and the fine line between justice and vengeance, all wrapped in Butcher’s signature blend of wit, action, and dark humor. If you’ve ever wondered what happens when a professional wizard gets caught in a web of political intrigue, supernatural threats, and personal demons, *Small Favor* delivers with relentless energy.

The story follows Harry Dresden, Chicago’s only licensed wizard, as he’s pulled into a case that threatens to unravel the city’s fragile balance between the mundane and the magical. The premise is simple: someone is manipulating events from the shadows, and Harry—alongside his ragtag team of allies and enemies—must uncover the truth before the city burns. But Butcher never lets the plot rest on its laurels. Instead, he weaves in layers of political maneuvering, ancient curses, and personal stakes that keep the reader hooked from the first page.

What makes *Small Favor* stand out is Butcher’s ability to balance high-stakes fantasy with the gritty realism of a noir-style mystery. Harry isn’t just a hero; he’s a flawed, often cynical man who carries the weight of his past. His relationships—with his love interest, his friends, and even his enemies—are as compelling as the supernatural threats he faces. The book thrives on tension, whether it’s the slow unraveling of a conspiracy or the explosive confrontations that follow. Butcher’s writing is sharp, his dialogue crackles with personality, and his world-building remains immersive, even as the series grows more complex.

The real strength of *Small Favor* lies in its themes. Butcher explores the idea of justice—what it means, who gets to define it, and how far one might go to protect what they believe in. Harry’s moral compass is rarely absolute, and his choices often force him into uncomfortable positions. This ambiguity makes him more relatable and the story more gripping. There’s also a strong undercurrent of loyalty and sacrifice, as allies are tested and old wounds resurface.

Butcher’s style is unmistakable. His prose is lean, his pacing relentless, and his humor—often dry or dark—keeps the mood from becoming too heavy. The book is packed with action, from magical duels to high-speed chases, but it never feels rushed. Instead, each scene serves a purpose, whether it’s advancing the plot, deepening character dynamics, or setting up the next twist.

For fans of urban fantasy, *Small Favor* is a must-read. It’s a perfect example of how the genre can thrive when it’s grounded in strong storytelling, memorable characters, and a world that feels both familiar and fantastical. If you’ve been waiting for the next chapter in Harry Dresden’s adventures, this book delivers in spades—packed with intrigue, heart, and the kind of satisfying payoff that keeps readers coming back for more.
