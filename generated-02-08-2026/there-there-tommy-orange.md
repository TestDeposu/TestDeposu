# There There

**Author:** Tommy Orange  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

There There, the debut novel by Tommy Orange, is a powerful and poignant portrayal of the urban Native American experience. This Pulitzer Prize finalist and National Bestseller tells the story of twelve characters from Native communities, all connected to one another in ways they may not yet realize, as they converge on the Big Oakland Powwow. Through their stories, Orange sheds light on the complex and painful history of Native Americans, while also celebrating their beauty, spirituality, and resilience.

One of the most striking aspects of Orange's writing style is his ability to balance multiple narrative voices and storylines. The novel jumps between characters, each with their own unique voice and perspective, yet somehow, Orange manages to weave their stories together seamlessly. This chorus of voices creates a sense of urgency and energy, propelling the reader forward and making the book impossible to put down.

At its core, There There is a novel about identity, community, and the struggle to find one's place in the world. The characters are all grappling with their own sense of self, trying to reconcile their Native heritage with the harsh realities of modern life. Through their stories, Orange explores the complexities of Native American identity, delving into themes of historical trauma, cultural erasure, and the ongoing struggle for recognition and respect.

Orange's writing is both poetic and unflinching, capable of conveying the deepest emotions and most painful truths with a precision and nuance that is nothing short of remarkable. His prose is infused with a sense of rage and urgency, yet it is also tempered with a deep sense of compassion and empathy. This is a writer who is unafraid to confront the darkest aspects of human experience, yet is also capable of finding beauty and hope in the most unexpected places.

One of the most impressive aspects of There There is its ability to balance the personal and the historical. Orange's characters are not just individual people, but also representatives of a larger community, one that has been shaped by centuries of trauma, displacement, and marginalization. Through their stories, Orange sheds light on the ongoing legacies of colonialism and historical trauma, yet he also celebrates the resilience and creativity of Native American communities.

In short, There There is a masterpiece of contemporary American literature. It is a novel that will make you laugh, cry, and think, often all at the same time. It is a novel that will challenge your assumptions and broaden your understanding of the world. And it is a novel that will stay with you long after you finish reading it, its characters and stories lingering in your mind like a ghostly presence.

If you haven't already, do yourself a favor and read There There. It is a book that will change the way you think about identity, community, and the complexities of human experience. And it is a book that will leave you eagerly anticipating Orange's next project, Wandering Stars.
