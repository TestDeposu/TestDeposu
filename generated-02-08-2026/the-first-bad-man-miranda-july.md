# The First Bad Man

**Author:** Miranda July  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

The First Bad Man, Miranda July's debut novel, is a mesmerizing and unapologetic exploration of the human experience, delving into the complexities of loneliness, desire, and the search for connection. July's writing is a masterclass in subtlety, weaving together a narrative that is both tender and unflinching, often in the same breath.

At the center of the novel is Cheryl Glickman, a quirky and endearing protagonist whose life is turned upside down by the arrival of Clee, her bosses' twenty-year-old daughter. This unlikely pairing sets off a chain reaction of events that forces Cheryl to confront her deepest desires and insecurities. Through their complicated and often fraught relationship, July expertly exposes the intricacies of female relationships, laying bare the tensions and contradictions that can both unite and divide women.

July's prose is a joy to behold, characterized by a sly humor and a deep empathy for her characters. Her writing is infused with a sense of wonder and curiosity, as if she is constantly seeking to understand the mysteries of the human heart. This is particularly evident in her portrayal of Cheryl's inner world, where July skillfully captures the turbulence of her protagonist's thoughts and emotions.

One of the novel's greatest strengths is its ability to balance the absurd with the profound. July's characters are often ridiculous and self-absorbed, but they are also deeply relatable, and their struggles and desires are universally recognizable. This is a novel that is unafraid to tackle the big questions – What does it mean to love and be loved? How do we navigate the complexities of relationships? – but it does so with a lightness of touch that is both refreshing and disarming.

Throughout the novel, July's unique voice and perspective shine through, imbuing the narrative with a sense of freshness and originality. Her writing is a testament to her skill as a storyteller, and her ability to craft a narrative that is both engaging and thought-provoking.

In short, The First Bad Man is a triumph, a novel that will resonate with readers long after they finish reading it. It is a powerful exploration of the human experience, and a testament to July's skill as a writer. If you're looking for a novel that will challenge and delight you, then this is the book for you.
