# Undone

**Author:** Cat Clarke  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

Grief is rarely a straight line. More often, it is a jagged, violent fracture that leaves us searching for something—anything—to hold onto when the world as we know it collapses. In her 2014 young adult novel Undone, author Cat Clarke plunges headfirst into the darkest corners of adolescence, exploring the devastating ripple effects of cyberbullying, unrequited love, and the unbearable weight of unanswered questions. 

The story centers on Jem Halliday, a teenager whose universe is anchored by her deep, unrequited love for her best friend, Kai. Even though Kai is gay and will never look at her romantically, his presence is the defining axis of her life. That is, until a malicious act of online outing shatters everything. Publicly exposed and unable to bear the crushing cruelty of his peers, Kai commits suicide. In an instant, Jem is left in a void of incomprehensible grief. 

What follows is not merely a tale of mourning, but a gripping descent into vengeance and discovery. Before his death, Kai leaves Jem twelve letters—one for each month of the year. In them, he begs her to leave the past alone and not to investigate his death. But Clarke understands human nature too well to let her protagonist walk away. Jem cannot let it go. Driven by a volatile mix of heartbreak and white-hot fury, she launches her own unofficial investigation to unmask the person responsible for destroying Kai’s life. 

Clarke’s writing style is unapologetically raw and emotionally unflinching. She captures the suffocating claustrophobia of high school social hierarchies and the agonizing helplessness of being a bystander to tragedy. Through Jem’s first-person narrative, the reader is strapped into a rollercoaster of guilt, anger, and desperation. Clarke does not offer easy comfort or a neatly wrapped resolution; instead, she leans into the messiness of trauma. The twelve letters serve as both a haunting tether to the boy Jem lost and the ticking clock of her own unraveling. 

Undone stands out in the young adult thriller and contemporary landscape because it refuses to romanticize grief or gloss over the very real, fatal consequences of digital cruelty. It asks a haunting question: How far would you go to make someone pay for a stolen life? Fans of emotional, high-stakes dramas like Gayle Forman’s If I Stay will find a kindred spirit in this book—a story that hurts to read, yet is impossible to put down. Cat Clarke has crafted a gripping, poignant thriller about loyalty and retribution that lingers long after the final page has been turned.
