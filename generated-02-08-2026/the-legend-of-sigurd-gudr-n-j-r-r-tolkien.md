# The Legend of Sigurd & Gudrún

**Author:** J.R.R. Tolkien  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

**The Legend of Sigurd & Gudrún: Tolkien’s Forgotten Norse Tragedy**

J.R.R. Tolkien was many things—a scholar, a mythmaker, a linguist—but above all, he was a storyteller who understood the raw, primal power of epic tragedy. With *The Legend of Sigurd & Gudrún*, his long-lost retelling of the Völsunga Saga, Tolkien delivers a work as haunting as it is meticulously crafted, a tale of doomed love, vengeance, and the inescapable weight of fate. Published posthumously in 2009, this dual-poem narrative—*The Lay of the Völsungs* and *The Lay of Gudrún*—is a masterclass in how to weave ancient myth into a modern, breathless tragedy, all while staying true to the bone-chilling rhythms of Norse legend.

At its heart, *The Legend of Sigurd & Gudrún* is a story of betrayal and ruin. Sigurd, the dragon-slayer, is a hero of mythic stature, but his greatest triumphs are overshadowed by the curses that follow him. His awakening of Brynhild, the Valkyrie encased in fire, is a moment of poetic grandeur, but their love is doomed from the start—bound by oaths, deception, and the cruel machinations of the Niflung court. Tolkien’s prose is unflinching in its portrayal of human folly: Gunnar, Sigurd’s blood-brother, is tricked into wearing the enchanted ring that binds him to Brynhild’s will, while Sigurd himself is murdered by those he trusted most. The tragedy spirals into madness—Brynhild’s suicide, Gudrún’s vengeful descent into monstrosity—each act a ripple of a single, fatal choice.

What makes this work so compelling is Tolkien’s unerring command of style. He doesn’t just translate the Norse sagas; he *reimagines* them in a verse form that crackles with the same alliterative fury as the *Poetic Edda*. The stanzas are tight, rhythmic, almost incantatory, each line carrying the weight of a curse or a prophecy. There’s a primal energy here, a sense that these are stories meant to be told around a fire, where the words themselves feel like spells. Tolkien’s deep scholarship shines through—his familiarity with the *Völsunga Saga* and the Eddic poems ensures that every detail, from the dragon’s hoard to the shape-shifting sorceress, feels earned. Yet he never lets academic rigor stifle the story’s emotional punch. The betrayals sting, the vengeance burns, and the final reckoning is as inevitable as it is devastating.

The second poem, *The Lay of Gudrún*, is where Tolkien’s genius truly shines. Gudrún, once a noblewoman, becomes a vengeful queen, her grief twisting her into something darker than her brothers. Her marriage to Atli (the historical Attila) is a grotesque farce, a descent into madness where she orchestrates the slaughter of her kin before exacting her own terrible justice. Tolkien doesn’t glorify her; he shows her as a woman broken by loss, yet unrecognizable in her rage. It’s a chilling portrayal of how love and vengeance can consume a soul.

For fans of Tolkien’s work, this is a revelation. While *The Lord of the Rings* and *The Hobbit* are his most famous creations, *The Legend of Sigurd & Gudrún* proves that he was equally at home in the darker, more mythic realms of Norse legend. It’s a work that rewards close reading, where every line feels like a piece of a larger, tragic tapestry. If you’ve ever wondered what Tolkien’s take on the *Völsunga Saga* would be, this is the answer—and it’s every bit as powerful as you’d hope.

A masterpiece of retelling, *The Legend of Sigurd & Gudrún* is essential reading for anyone who loves epic poetry, tragic heroes, or the kind of storytelling that lingers long after the last page.
