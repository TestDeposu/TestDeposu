# Losing My Breath

**Author:** J. Rose Black  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

Gilded Age New York was a paradox of dizzying opulence and crushing despair, a metropolis where sprawling Fifth Avenue mansions cast long shadows over the squalid tenements of the Lower East Side. In her captivating historical novel Losing My Breath, author J. Rose Black paints this fractured world with a vibrant, intoxicating brush. Known for crafting richly atmospheric tales, Black delivers a story that functions simultaneously as a high-stakes journalistic thriller, a passionate romance, and a sharp critique of the patriarchal forces that sought to silence turn-of-the-century women.

At the heart of the novel is Vera Garland, a fiercely ambitious reporter determined to break free from the superficial confines of the fashion and lifestyle pages to which female journalists were routinely relegated. Vera is a protagonist defined by her tenacity and her hunger for truth, qualities that make her immediately compelling. When the legendary Hope Diamond is acquired by jeweler Pierre Cartier, Vera smells a story. Beneath the glitz of high society, she suspects a calculated scheme to manipulate the gem's value and exploit its infamous, blood-soaked mythology. But what begins as a professional coup quickly spirals into a deeply personal crusade when her investigation intersects with the corrupt magazine publisher responsible for her father's tragic death. 

Black’s prose shines brightest when contrasting the glittering exterior of New York’s elite with the gritty, dangerous realities lurking beneath. The author employs a lush, sensory style that immerses the reader entirely in the era—from the rustle of silk gowns at extravagant balls to the suffocating tension of underground deals. New York itself becomes a living, breathing character in the narrative, pulsing with the frantic energy of the suffrage movement and the ruthless machinations of powerful men who view human lives as mere collateral in their pursuit of wealth.

Central to the novel's exploration of ambition and betrayal is its thematic core: the cost of independence. Black expertly dissects the systemic barriers faced by women striving to make their mark in a man’s world. Vera’s struggle is not merely about exposing Cartier or avenging her father; it is a battle for autonomy. Every step she takes toward professional vindication threatens to be undermined by the very society she is trying to conquer. 

The introduction of a young Russian jeweler adds a layer of dangerous romantic tension to the plot. As Vera enlists his help, professional lines blur into personal vulnerability. Black handles this romance with nuance, avoiding the trap of reducing her heroine to a damsel in distress. Instead, Vera’s growing affection for the jeweler acts as a double-edged sword, heightening the stakes as she finds herself ensnared in a web of his own deceptions. This romance grounds the sweeping historical backdrop in raw, relatable human emotion, emphasizing the universal need for love and connection amidst chaos.

Losing My Breath is a triumph of historical fiction, marrying meticulous period detail with a breathless pacing that keeps the pages turning. J. Rose Black has crafted a mesmerizing tale of retribution, ambition, and the dangerous pursuit of destiny. For readers who revel in atmospheric historical mysteries, complex heroines, and the glittering grit of the early twentieth century, this novel is an absolute must-read that will leave you utterly spellbound.
