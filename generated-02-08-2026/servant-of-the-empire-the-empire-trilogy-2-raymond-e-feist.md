# Servant of the Empire (The Empire Trilogy, #2)

**Author:** Raymond E. Feist  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1997

In the realm of epic fantasy, few authors have managed to captivate audiences quite like Raymond E. Feist. With "Servant of the Empire," the second installment in The Empire Trilogy, Feist once again proves his mastery of the genre, weaving a rich tapestry of love, hate, and sacrifice that is as alien as it is familiar.

Set against the backdrop of an ancient, sprawling empire, the story is a sweeping drama that follows the lives of its characters as they navigate the treacherous waters of politics, magic, and war. With a keen eye for detail and a deep understanding of the complexities of human nature, Feist crafts a world that is at once fantastical and yet, somehow, relatable.

One of the most striking aspects of Feist's writing style is his ability to balance action and introspection. The book is filled with moments of high-stakes drama, from battles and assassinations to court intrigue and magical duels. Yet, amidst all the excitement, Feist never forgets to explore the inner lives of his characters, delving into their motivations, desires, and fears with a level of psychological nuance that is all too rare in the fantasy genre.

At the heart of the story is the complex, often fraught relationship between Mara, a young noblewoman, and Kevin, a barbarian warrior from a distant land. Their love is forbidden, yet they are drawn to each other with a force that cannot be denied. As they navigate the treacherous landscape of their world, they must confront their own demons and make impossible choices that will have far-reaching consequences.

Feist's world-building is, as always, meticulous and detailed. The empire he creates is a vast, sprawling entity, full of different cultures, customs, and magical traditions. From the towering cities of the imperial heartlands to the distant, mist-shrouded mountains of the barbarian north, every location is vividly realized, with its own unique history and mythology.

If there is a flaw to the book, it is that the pacing can sometimes feel a bit slow. Feist is a master of the long game, and he is willing to take his time to develop his characters and plot. While this pays off in the end, with a truly epic conclusion that will leave readers breathless, it can make for a somewhat uneven reading experience.

Overall, however, "Servant of the Empire" is a triumph, a fantasy epic that will appeal to fans of Guy Gavriel Kay, Robin Hobb, and other masters of the genre. With its rich world-building, complex characters, and sweeping drama, it is a must-read for anyone who loves fantasy, adventure, and romance.
