# Valor (The Faithful and the Fallen, #2)

**Author:** John Gwynne  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

John Gwynne’s *Valor*, the second installment in *The Faithful and the Fallen* series, arrives with the crushing weight of shattered peace and escalating ruin. Published in 2014, this epic fantasy refuses to merely coast on the momentum of its predecessor; instead, it plunges the Banished Lands deeper into a merciless crucible of war, fractured loyalties, and mythological dread. Gwynne has crafted a narrative where the lines between heroism and villainy blur, and where every step forward is paid for in blood and heartbreak.

At the heart of *Valor* lies the overarching theme of choice and consequence. The novel operates on the grim premise that oaths are both sacred tethers and deadly chains. Every character is trapped in the momentum of their own decisions or the heavy expectations of destiny. Corban, once a simple boy dreaming of hound and hawk, is forced to mature in the crucible of survival, fleeing westward only to find that the war has already poisoned the horizon. Nathair, driven by an unyielding zeal for a mythic destiny, marches deeper into a campaign that threatens to consume his humanity. Through these parallel journeys, Gwynne explores the corrosive nature of power and the terrifying ease with which righteous causes are corrupted into instruments of tyranny.

Gwynne’s stylistic triumph lies in his unapologetic embrace of classic, high-stakes epic fantasy infused with a visceral, modern grit. He writes with a cinematic clarity that makes battlefields roar to life. The action is relentless, brutal, and intimate—you feel the mud, the exhaustion, and the panic of the soldiers on the line. Yet, the true strength of his prose is not just in the clash of steel, but in the quiet, agonizing moments between the violence. 

The multi-POV structure is handled with remarkable dexterity. Whether following Veradis wrestling with a splintering conscience in the royal court, or Maquin enduring the brutal indignities of pirate slavers and pit-fighting in a relentless pursuit of vengeance, Gwynne keeps the pacing taut. No single storyline feels like filler; every thread weaves into a larger, terrifying tapestry where the apocalypse is not a distant threat, but a daily reality. 

*Valor* is a masterclass in middle-book storytelling. It refuses easy resolutions, opting instead to test its characters to their absolute breaking points. It is dark, deeply emotional, and relentlessly gripping. For readers looking for a sprawling world steeped in Norse-inspired myth, towering giants, fierce animal companions, and characters you will root for with your whole heart, John Gwynne delivers a triumph of modern fantasy.
