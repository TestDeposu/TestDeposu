# Release

**Author:** Patrick Ness  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Patrick Ness has long been celebrated for his ability to capture the visceral, often messy intensity of youth, but in his 2017 novel *Release*, he distills this talent into something truly luminous. Inspired by the structural elasticity of Virginia Woolf’s *Mrs. Dalloway* and the raw, earnest emotional candor of Judy Blume’s *Forever*, Ness crafts a modern masterpiece that unfolds over the course of a single, transformative Saturday. It is a book that examines the profound friction between who we are forced to be and who we desperately long to become.

At the heart of the novel is Adam Thorn, a teenager whose life is fracturing along multiple fault lines. Between a suffocatingly religious family, a predatory and cruel ultimatum from his boss, and the lingering, ghost-like ache of unrequited love for his sort-of ex, Enzo, Adam’s world is on the brink of collapse. Yet, amidst the chaos, there are anchors: his fiercely loyal best friend, Angela, and his sweet, devoted new boyfriend, Linus. As Adam navigates this twenty-four-hour crucible, old memories collide with new heartaches, forcing him to confront the heavy armor he has built around himself. 

The central theme of *Release* is precisely what the title promises: the terrifying, yet ultimately liberating act of letting go. Ness explores the suffocating weight of expectation—familial, religious, and societal—and contrasts it with the dizzying freedom of self-acceptance. Adam’s journey is not a neat, sanitized coming-of-age arc, but a messy, authentic battle for autonomy. He must learn to untie the bindings of his past, step out of the shadows of his unrequited desires, and summon the profound courage required to live his truth in the open. 

Ness’s authorial style here is nothing short of mesmerizing. He blends stark realism with a touch of the ethereal, allowing Adam’s internal landscape to bleed seamlessly into the reality of his day. The prose is simultaneously raw and darkly funny, perfectly mirroring the chaotic oscillation of teenage emotion where profound existential dread and mundane adolescent anxieties exist side by side. Furthermore, Ness weaves in a secondary, mythic narrative thread involving a young woman awakening in the woods, adding a haunting, allegorical layer that echoes Adam’s own spiritual and physical liberation. 

*Release* is a triumph of empathy and storytelling. It honors the pain of heartbreak without romanticizing it, and it champions the healing power of found family. In Adam Thorn, Patrick Ness has created a character who feels entirely real—flawed, overwhelmed, and fiercely resilient. This novel is a vital, unforgettable reminder that sometimes the only way to survive is to dismantle the walls we have built, and that in the terrifying process of letting go, we finally find our freedom.
