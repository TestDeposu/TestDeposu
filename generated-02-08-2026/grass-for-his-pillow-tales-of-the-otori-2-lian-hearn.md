# Grass for His Pillow (Tales of the Otori, #2)

**Author:** Lian Hearn  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2003

In "Grass for His Pillow," Lian Hearn masterfully weaves a tale of love, deception, and survival in medieval Japan, a world of breathtaking beauty and treacherous politics. This second installment of the "Tales of the Otori" series is a sweeping epic that delves deeper into the complexities of a society governed by strict social hierarchies and the delicate balance of power.

At the heart of the story lies the star-crossed love between Takeo, a young assassin, and Shirakawa Kaede, the determined heir to the Murayama domain. Their love is forbidden, and they must navigate the treacherous landscape of their world to be together. Takeo, an orphan forced into a life of stealth and murder, struggles to reconcile his past and his present, while Kaede must assert her authority in a male-dominated society and protect her domain from those who seek to exploit her vulnerability.

Hearn's writing is as evocative as it is precise, transporting readers to a world of cherry blossom trees, snow-capped mountains, and ancient temples. Her prose is rich in sensory detail, conjuring the sounds, smells, and textures of medieval Japan. The characters are multidimensional and complex, with rich inner lives and motivations that drive the plot forward.

One of the most striking aspects of "Grass for His Pillow" is its exploration of the tension between duty and desire. Takeo and Kaede are both bound by their obligations to their families, their clans, and their society, yet they are drawn to each other with a passion that threatens to upend their lives. This conflict is echoed in the larger struggle for power and control that shapes the world of the Otori.

Hearn's style is characterized by a subtle blend of historical detail and mythological resonance. The world she creates is meticulously researched, yet it is also imbued with a sense of wonder and magic. The result is a narrative that is both grounded in reality and elevated by a sense of enchantment.

In "Grass for His Pillow," Lian Hearn has crafted a novel that is both a thrilling adventure and a nuanced exploration of the human condition. The book is a testament to her skill as a storyteller and her ability to transport readers to a world that is both familiar and unknown. If you are looking for a compelling and immersive read, "Grass for His Pillow" is an excellent choice.
