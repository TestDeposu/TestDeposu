# Ensaio Sobre a Cegueira

**Author:** José Saramago  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

In the hauntingly poignant "Ensaio Sobre a Cegueira" (Blindness), José Saramago weaves a mesmerizing narrative that delves into the very fabric of human existence. This masterpiece, first published in 1995, has been re-released in a special edition to commemorate the author's centennial birthday, complete with a foreword by Julián Fuks and a critical fortune that solidifies its position as a modern classic of Portuguese literature.

Saramago's prose is akin to a gentle breeze on a summer's day, soothing and unassuming, yet carrying with it the weight of a maelstrom. His writing style is characterized by long, winding sentences that meander through the narrative like a lazy river, slowly revealing the depths of human emotion. It is a unique and captivating approach, one that draws the reader inexorably into the world he has created.

In "Blindness," that world is one of unmitigated chaos. A mysterious "white blindness" has descended upon society, rendering everyone sightless. The government's response is to quarantine the afflicted, and it is here that the story truly begins. A small group of individuals, including a woman who feigns blindness to accompany her husband, find themselves at the epicenter of this maelstrom. As they navigate their new reality, they are forced to confront the very essence of humanity.

Saramago's exploration of this theme is both poignant and profound. Through the characters' struggles, he lays bare the inherent contradictions of human nature. We see the best and worst of humanity, often simultaneously, as individuals grapple with their newfound disability. The author's use of blindness as a metaphor for the human condition is nothing short of genius, allowing him to probe the depths of our existence with unflinching honesty.

One of the most striking aspects of "Blindness" is its ability to evoke strong emotions in the reader. Julián Fuks, in his foreword, describes the book as "one that makes you cry, one that moves you, and strangely, perhaps it is through tears that it saves us." This is no exaggeration. Saramago's prose has a way of seeping into the reader's psyche, of touching the raw nerve of human emotion. It is a testament to his skill as a writer that he can evoke such a powerful response.

The special edition of "Blindness" is a treasure trove for fans of Saramago's work. The inclusion of excerpts from his "Cadernos de Lanzarote," in which he discusses the writing process, offers a fascinating glimpse into the mind of the author. The critical fortune, featuring essays by Leyla Perrone-Moisés, Marco Lucchesi, and Maria Alzira Seixo, provides a nuanced understanding of the book's themes and motifs.

In conclusion, "Ensaio Sobre a Cegueira" is a masterpiece of modern literature. Saramago's unique writing style, combined with his profound exploration of the human condition, makes for a reading experience that is both haunting and uplifting. If you haven't already, do yourself a favor and immerse yourself in this world of "white blindness." You won't regret it.
