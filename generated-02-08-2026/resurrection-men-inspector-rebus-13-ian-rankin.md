# Resurrection Men (Inspector Rebus, #13)

**Author:** Ian Rankin  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2003

In the thirteenth installment of the Inspector Rebus series, Ian Rankin masterfully weaves a complex web of intrigue and deception that will keep readers on the edge of their seats. "Resurrection Men" is a gripping tale of redemption, corruption, and the blurred lines between right and wrong.

At the heart of the novel is Inspector John Rebus, a man whose rough exterior and unorthodox methods have landed him in hot water once again. Sent to a rehabilitation center for troubled cops, Rebus is forced to confront his own demons and team up with a group of misfits known as the "resurrection men." As they embark on a covert mission to take down a group of ruthless drug dealers, Rebus begins to suspect that his new colleagues may be more involved in the crime than they're letting on.

Meanwhile, Detective Sergeant Siobhan Clarke is investigating the murder of an art dealer, which leads her to a shocking connection with Rebus's case. As the two join forces, they unravel a sinister plot that reaches the highest echelons of Scottish society. With its intricate plot twists and turns, "Resurrection Men" is a true page-turner that will keep readers guessing until the very end.

Rankin's writing style is, as always, masterful. His vivid descriptions of Edinburgh's dark alleys and gritty streets transport the reader to the heart of Scotland's capital city. His characters are richly drawn and complex, with Rebus being a particular standout. The inspector's rough-around-the-edges demeanor and sharp wit make him a compelling and relatable protagonist.

One of the main themes of "Resurrection Men" is the idea of redemption and second chances. Rebus, a man who has made his fair share of mistakes, is given the opportunity to make amends and prove himself once again. Through his character, Rankin explores the complexities of human nature and the difficulties of making amends for past wrongs.

The author's use of the "resurrection men" as a metaphor for redemption is particularly striking. These men, all of whom have been given a second chance, are tasked with taking down a group of ruthless criminals. As they navigate the treacherous underworld of Scottish crime, they must confront their own personal demons and decide whether to take the path of redemption or succumb to their old ways.

Overall, "Resurrection Men" is a gripping and thought-provoking novel that will appeal to fans of crime fiction and mystery. With its intricate plot, complex characters, and vivid descriptions of Edinburgh's streets, it is a true masterpiece of the genre.
