# The Last Cuentista

**Author:** Donna Barba Higuera  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

**The Last Cuentista: A Cosmic Tale of Memory, Resistance, and the Power of Story**

Donna Barba Higuera’s *The Last Cuentista* is more than a science fiction adventure—it is a luminous meditation on what it means to be human, a defiant celebration of memory, and a haunting exploration of how stories bind us together. At its core, this Newbery-winning novel is a *cuento*, a tale that lingers like a whispered secret, blending the mythic with the urgent. Petra Peña, a girl who dreams of becoming a storyteller like her abuelita, becomes the last keeper of Earth’s history after a comet wipes out humanity. When she awakens on a new planet, she discovers she is the sole survivor of a collective purge—one where the Collective, a tyrannical regime, has erased the past to control the future. Petra’s mission, then, is not just survival but *resurrection*: bringing back the voices of those lost, the languages forgotten, and the humanity nearly extinguished.

Higuera’s prose is as rich and textured as the stories she weaves. The novel opens with the Spanish phrase *"Había una vez,"* a classic fairy-tale incipit that immediately signals this is a tale rooted in tradition, in the oral histories passed down through generations. Petra’s voice is sharp, resilient, and deeply human—she is a girl who loves *El Rey León* and *Star Wars*, who dreams of telling stories under the stars, and who, when faced with annihilation, refuses to let her world die with her. Her journey is one of quiet rebellion, where storytelling becomes an act of defiance against erasure. The Collective’s obsession with purging the past mirrors real-world fears of historical revisionism, making Petra’s struggle feel both timeless and urgently relevant.

What makes *The Last Cuentista* so powerful is its ability to balance high-stakes sci-fi with intimate, emotional depth. The ship’s journey to a new planet becomes a metaphor for the fragility of culture—how easily it can be lost, and how desperately it must be preserved. Higuera’s world-building is immersive, from the eerie silence of the purged passengers to the oppressive control of the Collective, whose leader, the enigmatic and chilling Director, embodies the dangers of a society that fears its own history. Yet, the novel never loses its warmth. Petra’s relationships—with her family, her friends, and even the ship’s AI—are tender and real, reminding us that even in the face of extinction, humanity’s capacity for love and connection endures.

Themes of memory and identity are woven throughout, reinforcing the idea that stories are the scaffolding of our existence. When Petra realizes she is the only one who remembers Earth, she grapples with the weight of that knowledge—how to share it without overwhelming others, how to ensure the past isn’t just remembered but *honored*. The novel’s climax is a triumph of creativity over oppression, where Petra’s storytelling becomes a weapon against tyranny, proving that language, culture, and memory are the ultimate acts of resistance.

Higuera’s style is both lyrical and precise, blending the poetic with the scientific. The descriptions of the comet’s destruction are visceral, the ship’s interior a mix of sterile efficiency and hidden rebellion. The novel’s structure—jumping between Petra’s past on Earth and her present on the ship—creates a sense of urgency, as if the reader is racing alongside her to uncover the truth before it’s too late. The ending is bittersweet, a testament to the power of hope even in the face of loss, leaving readers with the haunting beauty of a story well-told.

*The Last Cuentista* is a book for anyone who has ever feared losing their roots, their language, or their history. It is a call to remember, to preserve, and to fight for the stories that make us who we are. In an era where misinformation and erasure are rampant, Higuera’s novel is a vital reminder: stories are not just entertainment—they are the soul of humanity.
