# Hood Feminism: Notes from the Women That a Movement Forgot

**Author:** Mikki Kendall  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

Mainstream feminism has long had a blind spot, and Mikki Kendall’s Hood Feminism: Notes from the Women That a Movement Forgot serves as the uncompromising, urgent wake-up call the movement desperately needs. In this piercing collection of essays, Kendall exposes how conventional feminist discourse has historically centered the white, middle-class experience, while systematically ignoring the survival needs of women of color, low-income women, and those residing in marginalized communities. This is not merely a critique; it is a profound reimagining of what true gender equality must encompass.

At the heart of Kendall’s work is a powerful central theme: feminism cannot exclusively focus on glass ceilings when so many women are trapped beneath the floorboards. Through eighteen incisive chapters, Kendall redefines the scope of feminist issues, arguing that systemic struggles like poverty, food insecurity, inadequate housing, gun violence, lack of quality education, and restricted access to healthcare are fundamentally feminist issues. Mainstream feminism often treats these survival-level struggles as secondary or unrelated concerns, focusing instead on corporate representation or interpersonal microaggressions. Kendall forcefully dismantles this hierarchy of oppression, demonstrating that a woman cannot genuinely be liberated if she is worrying about her next meal or dodging bullets in her neighborhood. By bridging the gap between social justice and mainstream gender politics, Kendall insists that the fight for women's rights is hollow if it leaves the most vulnerable behind.

Kendall’s authorial style is striking, razor-sharp, and deeply pragmatic. She writes not from an ivory tower of academic theory, but from the frontline of lived experience and cultural critique. Her prose is unflinching, direct, and accessible, cutting through academic jargon to speak a resonant truth. Instead of offering gentle suggestions, Kendall confronts the reader with uncomfortable realities, calling out hypocrisy within activist spaces with unmatched clarity. Yet, despite the heaviness of the topics—ranging from state-sanctioned violence to reproductive justice—her writing is imbued with a fierce hope. She is not trying to tear down feminism; rather, she is trying to save it from itself by forcing it to be more inclusive, accountable, and intersectional. 

For readers looking to engage deeply with Kendall’s revolutionary ideas, modern reading circles and activist groups will find immense value in companion guides designed for this book. Equipped with thought-provoking discussion prompts, creative exercises, and contextual frameworks, these resources help readers unpack Kendall's challenging arguments and apply them to contemporary social landscapes. Whether used in a classroom setting or a community book club, Hood Feminism demands active participation, reflection, and ultimately, action.

Ultimately, Hood Feminism is an indispensable text for anyone who claims to champion equality. It challenges us to expand our empathy, check our privileges, and redefine solidarity. Mikki Kendall does not just invite us to the table; she demands that we rebuild the house entirely. Essential, bracing, and long overdue, this book is a modern feminist classic that will leave you forever changed.
