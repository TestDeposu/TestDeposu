# Two Truths and a Lie (The Lying Game, #3)

**Author:** Sara Shepard  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

Sara Shepard has carved out a distinct literary niche as the undisputed queen of adolescent suspense, and with Two Truths and a Lie, the third installment in The Lying Game series, she proves that her addictive formula is far from exhausted. Known for her penchant for glamorous settings, morally ambiguous teenagers, and labyrinthine plots—hallmarks that made Pretty Little Liars a cultural phenomenon—Shepard once again delivers a breathless, high-stakes mystery that thrives on the dark underbelly of teenage privilege. 

At the thematic core of the novel is the insidious nature of identity and the dangerous weight of deception. The narrative continues to follow Emma Paxton, a hardened foster child forced to impersonate her murdered twin sister, Sutton. This central premise is more than just a convenient plot device; it serves as a fascinating exploration of belonging, alienation, and the masks people wear to survive. Emma is not merely solving a murder; she is constantly performing, trying to inhabit a life that was stolen from her while hiding the terrifying truth of her own vulnerability. The luxury of Sutton’s world—gleaming mansions, exclusive country clubs, and designer clothes—contrasts sharply with the emotional isolation Emma feels, creating a tense atmosphere where every social interaction is a potential minefield. 

Shepard’s authorial style is tailor-made for compulsive page-turning. She employs a fast-paced, episodic narrative structure that bombards the reader with cliffhangers at the end of nearly every chapter. Her prose is accessible, punchy, and heavily dialogue-driven, capturing the snappy, often venomous cadence of affluent high schoolers. Yet, what elevates Shepard above standard YA thriller fare is her mastery of misdirection. Sutton Mercer was no innocent victim in life; her legacy is one of calculated cruelty, elaborate pranks, and shattered confidences. By making the victim deeply unlikable, Shepard complicates the moral landscape of the novel. Every friend, rival, and ex-boyfriend has a motive, transforming Tucson’s elite social circle into a nest of vipers where empathy is a liability. 

In Two Truths and a Lie, this atmosphere of paranoia is amplified by the return of Thayer Vega, a brooding, enigmatic figure from Sutton’s past whose sudden reappearance upends Emma’s fragile equilibrium. Shepard excels at crafting suspects who oscillate seamlessly between terrifying and sympathetic. Thayer’s presence forces Emma to reckon with the darker aspects of the sister she is desperately trying to mourn and understand. Is he a vengeful outcast returning to settle an old score, or is he the only genuine ally Emma has in a town built on falsehoods? Shepard keeps the reader guessing through a carefully orchestrated chess game of red herrings and withheld information. 

While the novel relies heavily on the tropes of contemporary YA melodrama—secret parties, complicated romances, and whispered hallway gossip—Shepard anchors the chaos with emotional resonance. Emma’s internal journey from survivor to detective provides a grounded emotional core amidst the sensationalistic plot twists. She is a protagonist you root for not because she is perfect, but because her resilience in the face of insurmountable deception is genuinely inspiring. 

Ultimately, Two Truths and a Lie is a sparkling, suspenseful triumph that rewards loyal fans and new readers alike. Sara Shepard demonstrates an uncanny ability to tap into the anxieties of youth while delivering a chilling whodunit. It is a stylish, swift read that reminds us just how lethal high school secrets can be, proving once again that when it comes to crafting twisted teen mysteries, Shepard plays the game better than anyone else.
