# The Brightest Night (Wings of Fire, #5)

**Author:** Tui T. Sutherland  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

With The Brightest Night, the fifth installment of the immensely popular Wings of Fire series, author Tui T. Sutherland delivers a thrilling and emotionally resonant climax to the original story arc. Published in 2014, this volume shifts the narrative spotlight onto Sunny, the perpetually underestimated SandWing dragonet, challenging the very foundation upon which the entire series was built. 

Sutherland’s greatest strength as a storyteller has always been her ability to treat younger readers with profound respect, infusing high-stakes fantasy with deeply relatable emotional struggles. In this novel, the overarching theme pivots from the inevitability of destiny to the radical power of choice. Throughout the first four books, the dragonets of destiny—Clay, Tsunami, Glory, Starflight, and Sunny—have marched to the rhythm of a prophecy written in blood and shadow, believing their only purpose was to stop a devastating generational war among the dragon tribes. But as the foundations of that prophecy crumble under the weight of deceit and dark revelations, Sunny is forced to ask the most terrifying question of all: What happens when the path chosen for you is a lie, and you have to forge your own?

Sutherland captures Sunny’s internal transformation with remarkable nuance. While Sunny is often dismissed by her peers as too small, too naive, or too idealistic to matter in a brutal world of political assassination and tribal warfare, the author uses this perspective as the moral compass of the book. Sunny’s unwavering optimism is not portrayed as foolishness, but as a revolutionary form of courage. In a continent torn apart by greed and vengeance, her refusal to succumb to cynicism becomes the ultimate weapon. 

Stylistically, Sutherland maintains the fast-paced, action-oriented momentum that has captivated millions of fans worldwide. Her prose is accessible yet vivid, painting the scorching, unforgiving landscape of the SandWing kingdom with stark clarity. The dialogue snaps with youthful energy, balancing moments of genuine levity with the heavy, impending dread of a continent on the brink of collapse. Furthermore, Sutherland expands the lore of Pyrrhia in fascinating ways, introducing buried secrets, deadly surprises, and a startling new perspective on scavengers—the human creatures long viewed by dragons as mere pests. This clever narrative pivot forces the dragons to reexamine their place at the top of the food chain, adding a layer of philosophical depth to the adventure.

The Brightest Night successfully deconstructs the classic chosen-one trope, subverting expectations right up to the final pages. It teaches its audience that history is not a rigid script to be blindly followed, but a blank page waiting to be written by those brave enough to demand peace. For long-time fans of the series and newcomers alike, Sutherland’s fifth book is a triumphant, heart-pounding journey that proves the most powerful magic in the world is free will.
