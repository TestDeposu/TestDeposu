# Rules of Ascension (Winds of the Forelands, #1)

**Author:** David B. Coe  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

**A Galloping Masterpiece: Why *Rules of Ascension* (Winds of the Forelands #1) is a Fantasy Triumph**

David B. Coe’s *Rules of Ascension* isn’t just the first book in the *Winds of the Forelands* series—it’s a bold, heart-pounding reinvention of fantasy itself. At its core, this novel is a story about transformation, identity, and the unbreakable bond between rider and mount, but it’s so much more than that. Coe weaves a tale that’s equal parts high-stakes adventure, emotional depth, and imaginative world-building, all while delivering a protagonist who defies easy categorization: Dun Lady’s Jess.

Jess isn’t just a horse. She isn’t just a woman. She is both—and neither—simultaneously, a creature of duality trapped in a world that doesn’t quite understand her. The magic that binds her to her rider, Dun Lady, is external, a spell that grants her human intelligence while preserving her equine instincts. This isn’t a were-horse or a shapeshifter; it’s a being caught between two worlds, struggling to find her place in a human society that sees her as either monstrous or pitiful. Coe’s genius lies in making Jess’s internal conflict palpable—her eagerness to embrace humanity, her frustration with its limitations, and her relentless devotion to her rider, who remains her anchor in a chaotic world.

The novel’s strength lies in its exploration of identity. Jess isn’t just adapting to her new form; she’s learning to navigate a world that doesn’t accommodate her. Her reactions—her sudden bursts of speed when startled, her instinctive understanding of terrain, her struggle with human social cues—are rendered with such authenticity that readers can’t help but root for her. Coe, a lifelong horse enthusiast, infuses every scene with equestrian realism, from Jess’s movements to her bond with her rider. It’s a detail that elevates the story beyond typical fantasy tropes, making Jess’s journey feel fresh and deeply human.

But *Rules of Ascension* isn’t just about Jess. It’s a sprawling, character-driven epic where every figure—from the noble but flawed Dun Lady to the morally ambiguous allies and enemies—feels real. Coe’s writing is sharp, immersive, and effortlessly engaging, blending political intrigue, personal stakes, and high-octane action. The pacing is relentless, pulling readers along as Jess races through forests, battles rival factions, and uncovers the secrets of her past. Yet, despite the urgency, there’s never a moment where the emotional core of the story is lost. The bond between Jess and her rider is the emotional backbone of the novel, a testament to Coe’s ability to craft relationships that resonate long after the last page.

What sets *Rules of Ascension* apart is its willingness to challenge expectations. Fantasy often relies on familiar tropes—chosen ones, magic systems, and grand prophecies—but Coe subverts them with a protagonist who isn’t a hero in the traditional sense. Jess is flawed, impulsive, and sometimes reckless, yet her loyalty and resilience make her unforgettable. The world she inhabits is rich with lore, from the remnants of a fallen civilization to the political maneuvering of warring factions, but it’s never overstuffed. Instead, Coe grounds his world-building in Jess’s perspective, making it feel tangible and immediate.

The novel also benefits from its connections to *Dun Lady’s Jess*, the standalone novella that introduced the character. While *Rules of Ascension* stands on its own, it deepens the lore and expands the stakes, offering a more complex and layered narrative. The interplay between the two works adds another layer of richness, hinting at a larger mythos that Coe is carefully unfolding.

In the end, *Rules of Ascension* is a fantasy novel that refuses to be pigeonholed. It’s an adventure, yes, but one with heart. It’s a story about magic, but one rooted in the real-world bond between humans and animals. It’s a tale of political intrigue, but driven by the personal struggles of its protagonist. Coe’s writing is accessible yet profound, his world-building expansive yet intimate, and his characters unforgettable. Whether you’re a horse lover, a fantasy enthusiast, or simply someone who enjoys a well-crafted story, this book will captivate you from the first page to the last.

If you’ve ever wanted a fantasy novel that feels fresh, emotionally resonant, and brimming with originality, *Rules of Ascension* is the perfect place to start. It’s a triumph of world-building, character development, and narrative pacing—a book that doesn’t just tell a story but makes you feel every gallop, every leap, and every moment of triumph alongside its extraordinary protagonist.
