# King's Dragon (Crown of Stars, #1)

**Author:** Kate Elliott  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1997

In Kate Elliott's majestic epic fantasy, King's Dragon (Crown of Stars, #1), a world on the brink of collapse sets the stage for an unforgettable tale of struggle, self-discovery, and transformation. Set in an alternate Europe, the narrative masterfully weaves historical parallels with a rich tapestry of mythology and magic, plunging readers into the midst of civil war, interlopers, and supernatural portents.

Elliott's writing is a treasure trove of imaginative detail, plunging readers into a world where even the most reluctant characters are transformed by the forces that shape their destinies. The novel begins with a fragile balance of power, as King Henry's hold on Wendar is contested by his sister Sabella, fueled by those discontent with the current state of their kingdom. Meanwhile, the Wendar is beset by northern raids from the Eika, an inhuman force that seems resistant to traditional battles, and an eerie aura of restored ruins inhabited by long-lost beings.

It's in this backdrop that two central characters emerge: Liath, a young woman with an unseen but extraordinary talent for sorcery and Alain, whose quest to unravel the enigma of his parentage becomes a journey of self-discovery. These protagonists, each navigating their internal turmoil, are inexorably drawn into the epic conflict ahead. Liath is compelled to master her gift and seize what is rightfully hers, while Alain must confront the truth about his lineage and accept the destiny for which he was born.

Throughout the novel, Elliott skillfully crafts a rich and layered story with well-developed characters, drawing parallels between history, myth, and the struggles facing these fictional civilizations. By merging the historical and fantastical elements of her narrative, Elliott creates a world that not only feels real but also eerily echoes our world's own historical conflicts.

The narrative itself is a compelling and dynamic dance of politics, warfare, and myth. Elliott handles these disparate elements with a deft hand, imbuing even the most complex battle scenes and sorcerous confrontations with an otherworldly charm. Her characters emerge from this chaos as multifaceted protagonists who are equally human and symbolic, each carrying the weight of their personal struggle as they fight for survival and the future of their world.

As the stakes grow and the forces of war begin to shift, Elliott crafts an ending that propels this first installment into a world of great possibilities for the story that unfolds. By weaving an intricate web of character development, magical world-building, and an epic conflict that spans continents, Elliott creates an irresistible epic fantasy that stands out even today in a crowded field.
