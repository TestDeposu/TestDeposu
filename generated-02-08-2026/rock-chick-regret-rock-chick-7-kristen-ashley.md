# Rock Chick Regret (Rock Chick, #7)

**Author:** Kristen Ashley  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

Kristen Ashley has carved out a distinct and fiercely loyal corner of the romance genre, and with Rock Chick Regret, the seventh installment in her beloved series, she delivers precisely what her fans crave: high-stakes alpha-male romance, relentless loyalty, and a world where the line between family and the motorcycle club blurs entirely. Centered on the intense, rugged members of the Chaos Motorcycle Club, this novel shifts its focus to Lanie Heron and Hop Kincaid, blending the grit of club life with an emotionally charged, contemporary love story that leaves a lasting impression.

The central theme of Rock Chick Regret is the arduous journey of healing from profound trauma and learning to trust again. Lanie Heron is a woman who has walked through the fire. Having survived a previous relationship that brought her to the brink of death, her heart is firmly locked away behind walls of self-preservation. She isn't looking for a white knight or happily-ever-after; she is simply looking to reclaim her autonomy and scratch an itch with a one-night stand. Enter Hop Kincaid, a heavily tattooed, fiercely protective biker who has long viewed Lanie as untouchable—a polished, classy woman who seemingly doesn't belong in his rough-and-tumble world. 

When Lanie boldly propositions Hop, it sparks a collision of two vastly different worlds. Ashley uses this premise to explore the theme of defying preconceived notions. Hop expects Lanie to be out of his depth, yet he quickly discovers that she is not only resilient enough to handle his chaotic life, but she is also the missing piece to his existence. The ensuing narrative is less about the physical spark—which is undeniably scorching—and more about the psychological hurdle of convincing a traumatized woman that she is safe in the arms of a dangerous man. Hop must prove that his brand of protection isn't a cage, but a sanctuary.

Ashley’s signature writing style is immediately recognizable on every page. Known for her maximalist approach to storytelling, she does not rush her narratives. She immerses the reader deeply into the domestic minutiae, the vibrant community dynamics, and the endless banter of the extended "Rock Chick" universe. Her prose is unapologetically conversational, dripping with attitude, emotional raw-nerve exposure, and hyper-masculine posturing balanced by fiercely independent heroines. Some critics might find her styling dense or repetitive, but for her target audience, this richness creates a living, breathing world that feels like returning home to a group of eccentric, fiercely loyal friends. 

Hop is the quintessential Kristen Ashley hero—possessive, gruff, fiercely devoted, and vocal about his feelings to a degree that borders on the poetic despite his rough exterior. Lanie matches him stride for stride, shedding her vulnerability layer by layer to reveal a core of steel. Their dynamic sizzles with tension, anchored by the high-stakes backdrop of the Chaos MC, where danger is always lurking just around the corner, waiting to test the strength of their newfound bond.

Ultimately, Rock Chick Regret succeeds because it delivers an emotional knockout punch wrapped in leather and chrome. It is a story about shedding the ghosts of the past, embracing the messy reality of love, and realizing that sometimes, the very thing you are running from is the exact shelter you need. Ashley writes romance with the volume turned all the way up, and for readers looking for intense passion, high drama, and a guaranteed happily-ever-after with a dangerous edge, this book hits every single note.
