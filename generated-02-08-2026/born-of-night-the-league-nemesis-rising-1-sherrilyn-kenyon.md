# Born of Night (The League: Nemesis Rising, #1)

**Author:** Sherrilyn Kenyon  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Born of Night, the inaugural installment of Sherrilyn Kenyon's The League: Nemesis Rising series, is a gripping and unapologetic foray into a realm of ruthless assassins, corrupt governments, and the unyielding struggle for power. Published in 2009, this novel has undoubtedly left an indelible mark on the science fiction and fantasy genres.

At the heart of the narrative lies the intricate dance between Nykyrian Quikiades, a Command Assassin with a troubled past, and Kiara Zamir, the unwitting target of a deadly conspiracy. As their worlds collide, they must navigate a complex web of trust, loyalty, and deception. Kenyon masterfully crafts their characters, imbuing them with a depth and nuance that makes their struggles feel palpably real.

One of the most striking aspects of Born of Night is its unflinching portrayal of a corrupt and unforgiving universe. The League, a seemingly omnipotent force, is revealed to be as vulnerable to corruption as the very governments it serves. This moral ambiguity lends a layer of complexity to the narrative, forcing the reader to confront the gray areas that exist between right and wrong.

Kenyon's writing style is as captivating as it is unapologetic. Her prose is visceral, with a keen focus on the brutal realities of a world where life is cheap and allegiances are fleeting. The action sequences are expertly choreographed, with a keen emphasis on the physical and emotional toll of combat. The romantic elements, though subtle, add a welcome layer of depth to the narrative, underscoring the human need for connection in a world devoid of empathy.

Throughout the novel, Kenyon's world-building is meticulous and immersive. The Ichidian Universe is a rich tapestry of cultures, histories, and mythologies, each woven together to create a breathtakingly detailed landscape. The characters that inhabit this world are equally well-realized, with their own distinct voices, motivations, and backstories.

If there is a criticism to be made, it is that the supporting cast can feel somewhat one-dimensional at times. Certain characters, though intriguing, are relegated to the periphery of the narrative, their potential left untapped. However, this is a minor quibble in an otherwise engaging and thought-provoking novel.

In conclusion, Born of Night is a gripping and unapologetic thrill ride that will appeal to fans of science fiction, fantasy, and action-packed adventure. Sherrilyn Kenyon's masterful world-building, coupled with her visceral writing style, makes for a compelling narrative that will leave readers eager for more.
