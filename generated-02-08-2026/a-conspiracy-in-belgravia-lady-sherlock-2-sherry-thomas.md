# A Conspiracy in Belgravia (Lady Sherlock, #2)

**Author:** Sherry Thomas  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

A Conspiracy in Belgravia, the second installment in Sherry Thomas's Lady Sherlock series, is a masterful blend of wit, intelligence, and intrigue, set against the rich backdrop of Victorian London. This gripping mystery follows Charlotte Holmes, a brilliant and unorthodox detective, as she navigates a complex web of loyalty, deception, and family secrets.

Thomas's writing is a true delight, with a keen attention to historical detail and a deep understanding of the societal norms that governed women's lives during this period. Her prose is witty and engaging, with a keen sense of humor that adds a delightful lightheartedness to the narrative. The character of Charlotte Holmes is a particular standout, with her razor-sharp mind and unconventional approach to detective work making her a truly compelling protagonist.

At the heart of the novel is the mystery of Myron Finch, Charlotte's illegitimate half-brother, who has gone missing. Lady Ingram, the wife of Charlotte's benefactor, approaches Sherlock Holmes (aka Charlotte) with a plea for help in finding her former lover. As Charlotte delves deeper into the case, she must navigate a complex landscape of family loyalties, hidden agendas, and unexpected alliances.

One of the most striking aspects of Thomas's writing is her ability to balance the demands of a complex mystery with the need to develop her characters. Charlotte's relationships with those around her – including her sister Livia, Mrs. Watson, and the enigmatic Lord Ingram – are multifaceted and nuanced, adding depth and richness to the narrative.

Throughout the novel, Thomas also explores themes of identity, class, and social status, raising important questions about the constraints placed on women during this period. Charlotte's own position as a woman operating in a male-dominated field is a particular point of interest, and Thomas handles this aspect of the narrative with sensitivity and intelligence.

If there is a criticism to be made, it is that the pacing of the novel can be somewhat uneven at times. Some plot threads feel slightly underdeveloped, and the resolution of the mystery, while satisfying, feels a tad rushed. However, these are minor quibbles in what is otherwise a truly engaging and enjoyable read.

In short, A Conspiracy in Belgravia is a delightful and engaging mystery that will appeal to fans of Sherlock Holmes, historical fiction, and strong female protagonists. With its witty prose, clever plot twists, and nuanced character development, this novel is a true standout in the genre.
