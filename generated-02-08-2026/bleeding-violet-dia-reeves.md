# Bleeding Violet

**Author:** Dia Reeves  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

Bleeding Violet, the 2010 novel by Dia Reeves, is a haunting and deeply unsettling tale that delves into the complexities of mental illness, love, and the supernatural. On the surface, the story appears to be a dark fantasy, replete with ghostly possessions, demon hunters, and interdimensional portals. However, as the narrative unfolds, it becomes clear that Reeves is tackling far more profound themes, using the tropes of the genre to explore the inner turmoil of her protagonist, Hanna.

Hanna is a protagonist like no other – fiercely independent, unapologetically herself, and struggling to cope with the harsh realities of her own mental health. Her inner world is a maelstrom of emotions, thoughts, and experiences that are both deeply relatable and utterly unique. Through Hanna's voice, Reeves masterfully conveys the sense of disorientation and disconnection that often accompanies mental illness, creating a sense of intimacy and immediacy that draws the reader inexorably into her world.

As Hanna navigates the treacherous landscape of Portero, Texas, she finds herself torn between her growing feelings for Wyatt, a young Mortmaine with a troubled past, and her desperate desire to save her mother from the clutches of a malevolent spirit. This central conflict serves as a catalyst for Hanna's journey, forcing her to confront the darkest aspects of her own psyche and the true nature of her relationships.

Reeves' writing style is a perfect match for the subject matter, blending elements of Southern Gothic, dark fantasy, and psychological thriller to create a narrative that is both propulsive and deeply unsettling. Her prose is economical and direct, yet possessed of a poetic intensity that adds depth and complexity to the story. The world-building is similarly impressive, with the supernatural elements woven seamlessly into the fabric of the narrative.

One of the most striking aspects of Bleeding Violet is its refusal to offer easy answers or comforting resolutions. Hanna's journey is a difficult and often painful one, marked by setbacks, betrayals, and moments of stark terror. And yet, even in the midst of such darkness, Reeves finds a way to convey a sense of hope and resilience, a testament to the enduring power of the human spirit.

In short, Bleeding Violet is a novel that will appeal to fans of dark fantasy, Southern Gothic, and psychological thrillers. It is a story that will haunt you long after you finish reading it, a testament to the enduring power of Dia Reeves' vision and craftsmanship.
