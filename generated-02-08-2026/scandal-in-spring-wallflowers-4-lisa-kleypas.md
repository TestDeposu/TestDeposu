# Scandal in Spring (Wallflowers, #4)

**Author:** Lisa Kleypas  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Lisa Kleypas is a grandmistress of historical romance, and with Scandal in Spring, the fourth and final installment in her beloved Wallflowers series, she delivers a sparkling, emotionally resonant triumph. Published in 2009, this novel completes the journey of the quartet of societal outcasts who bound themselves together to secure marriage matches. While the previous books explored quiet wallflowers blooming in unexpected ways, this volume tackles a theme as timeless as it is intoxicating: the fine line between passionate loathing and undeniable love, wrapped in a narrative of personal growth and familial expectation.

At the heart of the novel is Daisy Bowman, a delightfully quirky, free-spirited American heiress who would rather read adventure novels than conform to the rigid decorum of the London season. Having successfully helped her three closest friends find their happily-ever-afters, Daisy now faces the ticking clock of her final season. Her exasperated, pragmatic father issues a stern ultimatum: secure a husband immediately, or return to America to marry Matthew Swift, a ruthless, ambitious entrepreneur whom Daisy utterly despises. 

What follows is a masterclass in enemies-to-lovers tension, a trope Kleypas executes with supreme confidence. Daisy’s desperate quest to find *anyone* else to marry leads to comedic misadventures and a delightful showcase of her irrepressible personality. But Kleypas subverts expectations by introducing Matthew Swift not as a mustache-twirling villain, but as a complex, deeply loyal man whose outward ruthlessness masks a vulnerable, long-held devotion to Daisy herself. As the forced proximity melts their mutual animosity, a blazing sensuality takes over, shifting the dynamic from a battle of wills to a collision of hearts.

Kleypas’s signature style shines brilliantly here. Her prose is effortlessly fluid, balancing witty, sharp dialogue with lush, evocative descriptions of Victorian-era London and the English countryside. She excels at internal pacing, allowing the emotional stakes to rise organically alongside the physical attraction. Daisy is not just a quirky heroine for comedic effect; her journey is one of self-discovery. She learns to harness her unconventional strengths rather than hiding them, proving that her vibrant nature is her greatest asset. Similarly, Matthew’s transformation from cold-hearted rival to a deeply tender protector highlights Kleypas’s ability to craft multi-dimensional male protagonists who are both fiercely powerful and hopelessly romantic.

The introduction of a late-stage scandalous secret adds a layer of dramatic urgency, testing the foundations of Matthew and Daisy’s newfound bond. Yet, Kleypas ensures that the conflict never feels contrived; instead, it serves as the crucible that proves the resilience of their love. The resolution is deeply satisfying, offering a brilliant crescendo not only for Daisy and Matthew, but for the entire Wallflower sisterhood that fans have grown to adore over four books.

Scandal in Spring is a captivating, laugh-out-loud, and deeply sensual conclusion to a landmark historical romance series. Kleypas proves once again why she remains a gold standard in the genre, delivering a book that is as heartwarming as it is steamy. It is a triumphant celebration of love that defies expectations, blooms in the most unlikely places, and proves that sometimes, the person you think you hate is the exact one your soul has been waiting for.
