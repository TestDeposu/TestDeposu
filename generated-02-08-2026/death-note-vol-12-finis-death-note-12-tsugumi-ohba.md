# Death Note, Vol. 12: Finis (Death Note, #12)

**Author:** Tsugumi Ohba  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

The final chess move has been played, the pieces have fallen, and the grand game of godhood and justice reaches its haunting conclusion in Death Note, Vol. 12: Finis. Written by Tsugumi Ohba and illustrated with Takeshi Obata’s razor-sharp aesthetic, this final volume delivers a masterclass in psychological tension, bringing one of manga's most defining rivalries to a breathtaking, inevitable end. 

At its core, Ohba’s narrative has never merely been a supernatural thriller about a boy with a notebook that kills; it is a profound philosophical exploration of absolute power and the corruption of idealism. Light Yagami began his journey as a bored prodigy seeking to purge the world of evil, gradually morphing into the very tyrant he sought to destroy, adopting the messianic persona of "Kira." In Finis, Ohba confronts this descent head-on. The central theme crystallizes around the question of human hubris: Can mortal man ever truly wield divine judgment without losing his humanity? Light’s ultimate undoing is not brought about by a sudden stroke of bad luck, but by the accumulated weight of his own arrogance—the fatal belief that he alone is smart enough to outwit both human justice and cosmic fate.

Ohba’s writing style in this concluding volume is brilliantly calculated, functioning much like a high-stakes legal drama fused with a psychological thriller. The pacing is relentless. Rather than relying on mindless action, Ohba weaponizes exposition, turning internal monologues, statistical probabilities, and meticulously planned contingencies into edge-of-your-seat drama. Every panel feels heavy with consequence, and the dialogue crackles with intellectual warfare. Even as the net closes around Light, Ohba maintains the intellectual dignity of his characters, ensuring that the final confrontation is a battle of wits to the very last breath. 

What makes Finis particularly striking is its refusal to offer a comforting or moralistic Hollywood ending. Instead, Ohba provides a chillingly pragmatic resolution that honors the dark, cynical tone established in the opening chapters. Light Yagami’s final moments are stripped of all divine grandeur, exposing the terrified, desperate boy hiding beneath the veneer of a self-appointed god. It is a deeply human ending to a story driven by inhuman concepts.

Death Note, Vol. 12: Finis stands as a monument to serialized storytelling done right. Tsugumi Ohba crafted a narrative that challenged readers to question the boundaries of morality, justice, and righteousness. This volume does not just close a book; it seals a legendary chapter in manga history, leaving behind a lingering question that echoes long after the final page is turned: If given the power to judge the world, who would judge you?
