# Unhumans: The Secret History of Communist Revolutions

**Author:** Jack Posobiec  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2024

In "Unhumans: The Secret History of Communist Revolutions," Jack Posobiec delivers a searing indictment of the tactics employed by far-left movements throughout history, and a stark warning about the dangers of their resurgence in the present day. This book is not a dry, academic treatise on ideology or politics, but a visceral, real-world exploration of the human cost of radical revolutions.

Posobiec's central argument is that communism, socialism, Marxism, and other radical-isms are not philosophies, but tactics designed to unleash terror on ordinary people and revoke their fundamental human rights. He contends that these movements follow a predictable pattern, from the initial call for change to the eventual declaration of a new order, and that this pattern is repeating itself in our time.

Through a series of shocking, and often disturbing, historical examples, Posobiec lays bare the tactics employed by radical movements to achieve their goals. He exposes the ways in which they manipulate public opinion, demonize their opponents, and use violence and intimidation to silence dissent. He also reveals the devastating consequences of these tactics for ordinary people, who are often caught in the crossfire of radical activism.

One of the strengths of "Unhumans" is its accessibility. Posobiec writes in a clear, concise style that makes the book easy to follow, even for readers who may not be familiar with the intricacies of radical politics. He also uses real-world examples to illustrate his points, making the book feel more like a narrative than a dry, academic treatise.

At the same time, however, Posobiec's approach can be somewhat polemical. He is clearly passionate about his subject, and his outrage at the tactics employed by radical movements is palpable. While this passion can be engaging, it also sometimes tips over into hyperbole, and Posobiec's language can be overly dramatic.

Despite this, "Unhumans" is an essential read for anyone concerned about the rise of radical activism in our time. Posobiec's analysis of the tactics employed by these movements is insightful and thought-provoking, and his warnings about the dangers of their resurgence are timely and urgent.

Ultimately, "Unhumans" is a call to action, urging readers to wake up to the threat posed by radical movements and to take steps to defend their human rights. It is a book that will make you angry, but also empower you to make a difference. As Posobiec himself puts it, "We must stop what is coming." This book is a crucial step in that process.
