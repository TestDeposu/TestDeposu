# Dark Slayer (Dark, #17)

**Author:** Christine Feehan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

In Dark Slayer, the seventeenth installment of her enduringly popular Carpathian series, #1 New York Times bestselling author Christine Feehan dives once more into the shadowy, high-stakes realm of immortal predators, ancient bloodlines, and all-consuming passion. Published in 2009, this entry showcases Feehan at the height of her atmospheric prowess, blending gothic romance with visceral fantasy to explore the ultimate literary theme of the series: redemption through fated love against a backdrop of insurmountable isolation.

At the heart of the narrative is Ivory Malinov, a woman appropriately dubbed the Dark Slayer. Having endured centuries of abandonment by her family and ostracization by her own kind, Ivory’s existence has been reduced to a solitary crusade of survival and vengeance. Her only true companions are the wolves, and her sanity has been precariously maintained by a single-minded purpose: to destroy her greatest enemy. Feehan excels at portraying the psychological toll of such ancient trauma. Ivory is not just a warrior; she is a haunted soul on the brink of complete emotional desolation, making her eventual awakening all the more potent.

Enter Razvan, a character carrying an equally staggering burden. Branded a criminal and a dragonseeker, Razvan’s lineage is both a blessing and a curse. Raised as a captive enemy by his own people, he has endured unimaginable torment. When the novel opens, he is not seeking a hero’s journey, but the finality of the dawn—an end to his agonizing existence. Feehan brilliantly sets up a high-velocity collision of two doomed souls. When Ivory catches the scent of Razvan, the instinct for survival sparks something entirely unexpected: a recognition of a kindred spirit, a lifemate, and a mutual salvation.

Feehan’s signature writing style is on full display here, characterized by lush, sensory descriptions, intense emotional stakes, and a relentless pacing that keeps the pages turning. She treats the Carpathian world with an established, immersive gravity. The prose oscillates between the brutal realities of war and the tender, fierce vulnerability of two people who have never known safety. The central theme of this novel explores how love acts as the ultimate catalyst for healing. Ivory and Razvan are both outcasts, marked by the cruelty of others, yet their connection proves that even the most scarred and cursed individuals can find purpose in the arms of a destined partner. 

The dynamic between the two protagonists is charged with both physical chemistry and deep emotional resonance. Feehan expertly balances the elements of danger and desire. Every interaction is layered with the weight of their respective pasts, elevating the romance beyond mere physical attraction into a battle for their very souls. In spirit, flesh, blood, love, and war, Ivory and Razvan are forged as mirrors to one another, making their alliance both terrifying and exhilarating to witness.

Dark Slayer delivers everything longtime fans of Christine Feehan crave while offering enough mythos and emotional depth to captivate newcomers to the Carpathian saga. It is a dark, seductive fairy tale for adults, proving that even in the deepest shadows of despair, the primal pull of a lifemate can reignite the will to live and fight another day.
