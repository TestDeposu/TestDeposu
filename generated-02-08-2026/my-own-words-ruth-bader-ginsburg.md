# My Own Words

**Author:** Ruth Bader Ginsburg  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

To read Ruth Bader Ginsburg is to witness the architecture of a remarkably disciplined mind at work. In her 2016 collection, My Own Words, the late Supreme Court Justice offers readers a rare and intimate vantage point into the intellectual engine that drove decades of legal and cultural transformation. Co-curated with her authorized biographers, Mary Hartnett and Wendy W. Williams, this volume serves as much more than a biographical retrospective. It is a masterclass in civic engagement, legal philosophy, and the quiet power of persistence, delivered in the distinctive, unflappable voice of an American icon.

The central theme running through the collection is the relentless, methodical pursuit of equality and justice, anchored by an unwavering faith in the United States Constitution. Yet, what makes My Own Words so compelling is its surprising thematic breadth. Ginsburg refuses to be pigeonholed solely as a legal titan of equal protection. Instead, the book showcases her astonishing intellectual range, moving seamlessly from the intricate, often opaque machinery of the Supreme Court to deeply personal reflections on her Jewish heritage. In one of the book’s most charming and unexpected diversions, she explores her lifelong passion for law and lawyers in opera, revealing a playful, artistic soul behind the formidable judicial robes. Furthermore, Ginsburg offers a compelling argument for the value of comparative law, advocating for the wisdom of looking beyond U.S. shores to understand how other democracies grapple with universal human rights.

Ginsburg’s authorial and rhetorical style is the true star of the collection. Devoid of bombast or theatricality, her prose is characterized by precision, clarity, and an understated wit. Whether she is drafting a biting dissent or delivering a commencement address, Ginsburg writes with a surgical economy of language. Every word is placed with intent. Her voice is unfailingly serious yet delightfully wry, capable of disarming opponents not with volume, but with indisputable logic and graceful phrasing. The biographical introductions provided by Hartnett and Williams frame these writings with warmth and necessary context, drawing on hundreds of interviews to illuminate the human being behind the historic accomplishments. 

In today’s fractured political landscape, My Own Words acts as both a soothing balm and a vital call to action. It reminds us that progress is not achieved through fleeting outrage, but through rigorous preparation, civil discourse, and an enduring commitment to democratic institutions. Ginsburg’s writings demonstrate that conviction, paired with intellectual rigor, can fundamentally reshape a nation.

For anyone seeking to understand the mind of one of our generation's most influential figures, this book is indispensable. It is a testament to a life spent in service of the law, narrated by a woman who wrote her own history on her own terms.
