# I is for Innocent (Kinsey Millhone, #9)

**Author:** Sue Grafton  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1992

Sue Grafton's 'I is for Innocent' is a gripping and cleverly crafted mystery that will keep you on the edge of your seat. The ninth installment in the Kinsey Millhone series, this novel is a testament to Grafton's mastery of the genre. With its intricate plot, complex characters, and sharp wit, 'I is for Innocent' is a must-read for fans of crime fiction.

At the heart of the story is the case of David Barney, a man acquitted of murdering his wealthy wife, Isabelle. Five years later, Isabelle's ex-husband, Ken Voigt, is suing Barney for her estate, claiming that the jury made a fatal mistake. Enter Kinsey Millhone, a fiercely independent and sharp-witted private investigator who takes on the case. As Kinsey delves deeper into the mystery, she uncovers a web of secrets, lies, and deceit that lead her to question everything.

Grafton's writing style is a major part of the book's appeal. Her prose is crisp, concise, and engaging, making it easy to become fully immersed in the world of the story. Her characters are well-developed and complex, with Kinsey being a standout. Her tough, no-nonsense exterior hides a sharp mind and a deep sense of empathy, making her the perfect protagonist for this type of story.

One of the main themes of 'I is for Innocent' is the concept of innocence. Is David Barney truly innocent, or is he a master manipulator who has managed to fool everyone? As Kinsey digs deeper, she begins to question everything she thought she knew about the case. The novel raises important questions about the nature of truth and how it can be distorted or manipulated.

Grafton's use of language is also noteworthy. Her dialogue is natural and engaging, and her descriptions of the California landscape are vivid and evocative. The pacing of the novel is well-balanced, with a steady stream of twists and turns that keep the reader guessing until the very end.

In short, 'I is for Innocent' is a compelling and engaging mystery that will appeal to fans of crime fiction. With its intricate plot, complex characters, and sharp wit, it's a must-read for anyone who enjoys a good whodunit. Sue Grafton's writing style is a major part of the book's appeal, and her use of language is both natural and engaging.
