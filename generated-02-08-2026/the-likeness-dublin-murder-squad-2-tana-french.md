# The Likeness (Dublin Murder Squad, #2)

**Author:** Tana French  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2008

The Likeness, Tana French's sophomore novel, is a masterfully woven tale that delves deep into the complexities of identity and belonging. Following the success of her debut, In the Woods, French once again proves herself to be a force to be reckoned with in the realm of crime fiction. This gripping narrative is a testament to French's ingenious plotting and her ability to craft characters that are as multifaceted as they are relatable.

At the heart of The Likeness is Cassie Maddox, a former member of the Dublin Murder Squad who has transferred out of the unit. However, an urgent phone call brings her back to a crime scene that is as eerie as it is intriguing. The victim bears an uncanny resemblance to Cassie, and her ID identifies her as Alexandra Madison, an alias Cassie once used as an undercover cop. This coincidence sets off a chain of events that sees Cassie going undercover once again, this time to unravel the mystery surrounding the victim's identity and her untimely demise.

As Cassie becomes increasingly entrenched in the victim's life, she finds herself navigating a complex web of relationships and alliances. The lines between her own identity and that of the victim begin to blur, and Cassie is forced to confront the very nature of her own sense of self. This exploration of identity is a recurring theme throughout the novel, and French handles it with a level of nuance and sensitivity that is nothing short of remarkable.

One of the standout features of The Likeness is French's writing style, which is as evocative as it is atmospheric. Her prose is infused with a sense of tension and foreboding, which perfectly complements the novel's suspenseful plot. French's use of language is deliberate and measured, and she has a keen eye for detail that adds depth and texture to the narrative.

The characters in The Likeness are equally well-crafted, with Cassie being a particularly notable example. Her struggles to reconcile her past and present selves are deeply relatable, and her inner turmoil is palpable. The supporting cast is equally well-developed, with each character bringing their own unique perspective to the narrative.

Overall, The Likeness is a gripping and thought-provoking novel that is sure to appeal to fans of crime fiction and suspense. French's masterful plotting and nuanced characterization make for a compelling read, and her exploration of identity and belonging is both timely and timeless. If you're looking for a novel that will keep you on the edge of your seat, look no further than The Likeness.
