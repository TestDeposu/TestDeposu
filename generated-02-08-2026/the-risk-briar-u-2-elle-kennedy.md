# The Risk (Briar U, #2)

**Author:** Elle Kennedy  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

**The Risk: A Spark of Defiance in Elle Kennedy’s Boldest Romance Yet**

Elle Kennedy’s *The Risk*—the second standalone in her *Briar U* series—is a sizzling, morally ambiguous romance that dares to blur the lines between rebellion and ruin. At its core, this book is a love letter to the kind of defiance that comes with growing up: the kind that makes you question everything you’ve been taught, the kind that turns a "no" into a challenge. Here, the stakes aren’t just about hearts—they’re about reputations, legacies, and the terrifying freedom of choosing yourself, even when the world tells you not to.

The story follows **Avery**, a sharp-witted, fiercely independent woman whose life is already a minefield of expectations. As the daughter of Briar University’s hockey coach, she’s expected to toe the line—no scandals, no controversies, no *risks*. But Avery isn’t interested in playing by anyone’s rules. She’s ambitious, pragmatic, and, most importantly, *done* with people telling her what she can and can’t do. When she needs a favor from **Jake Connelly**—Harvard’s cocky, infuriatingly charming star player—a fake relationship becomes the only way to secure her dream internship. The problem? Jake isn’t interested in playing pretend. He wants the real thing. And Avery? She’s not sure she can afford to lose control.

What makes *The Risk* so compelling isn’t just the slow-burn tension between Avery and Jake—though their chemistry is electric, a mix of friction and undeniable attraction that feels like a physical force. It’s the way Kennedy explores the *cost* of defiance. Avery isn’t just fighting for her own desires; she’s fighting against the weight of her father’s legacy, the judgment of her peers, and the fear that one wrong move could derail everything she’s worked for. Jake, for his part, is a master of manipulation—charismatic, ruthless, and utterly convinced that he knows what’s best for her. Their dynamic isn’t just a battle of wills; it’s a dance of power, where every step could either liberate or destroy her.

Kennedy’s writing is as sharp as it is seductive. She has a knack for crafting dialogue that crackles with wit and venom, making even the most heated arguments feel like a performance. The banter between Avery and Jake is a highlight—full of sarcasm, double entendres, and the kind of playful insults that make you want to cheer for both of them, even when they’re at each other’s throats. But where Kennedy truly shines is in her portrayal of Avery’s internal struggle. She doesn’t romanticize rebellion; she lays bare its consequences. Avery’s fear isn’t just of losing Jake—it’s of losing herself, of becoming the very thing she’s fighting against: a girl who bends to pressure, who cares too much about what others think.

The book’s setting—Briar University, a world of elite athletes, cutthroat ambition, and old-money politics—serves as both a stage and a character. The tension between the hockey team and the rival school’s players isn’t just a plot device; it’s a metaphor for the larger conflict Avery faces: the expectation that she should conform, that she should play by the rules of a system that was never designed for her. Jake, as a player from the rival team, becomes the ultimate symbol of that rebellion—someone she’s supposed to avoid, but who also represents the one person who might understand the cost of breaking the rules.

Of course, no romance is complete without its fair share of drama, and *The Risk* delivers in spades. There are secrets, betrayals, and moments where Avery’s carefully constructed facade threatens to crumble. But what makes these moments so gripping is how Kennedy balances them with Avery’s growth. She’s not a damsel in distress; she’s a woman who learns to trust her instincts, even when they lead her into uncharted territory. And Jake? He’s not just a love interest—he’s a mirror, reflecting back to Avery the parts of herself she’s been too afraid to embrace.

The Risk isn’t just a romance; it’s a story about the courage to take chances, even when the odds are stacked against you. It’s about the messy, exhilarating freedom of choosing yourself over safety, of realizing that sometimes, the only way to survive is to stop playing by someone else’s rules. Kennedy’s writing is as intoxicating as it is thought-provoking, a perfect blend of steamy tension and emotional depth that keeps you hooked from the first page to the last.

If you loved *The Hating Game* for its sharp banter and *Beach Read* for its emotional complexity, *The Risk* is the book for you—a bold, defiant romance that refuses to let you look away.
