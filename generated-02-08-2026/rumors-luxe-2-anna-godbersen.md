# Rumors (Luxe, #2)

**Author:** Anna Godbersen  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

In "Rumors," the second installment of Anna Godbersen's Luxe series, the opulent world of Manhattan's elite in 1899 is once again laid bare, revealing the intricate web of relationships, scandals, and secrets that bind these dazzling socialites together. This delicious sequel to "The Luxe" is a masterful blend of romance, jealousy, and betrayal, set against the backdrop of a bygone era.

Godbersen's writing is as sumptuous as the world she describes, with meticulous attention to historical detail that transports readers to the gaslit streets of Gilded Age New York. Her characters are multifaceted and complex, with the enigmatic Diana Holland taking center stage as she navigates the treacherous landscape of high society. The mysterious circumstances surrounding her sister Elizabeth's demise have left a power vacuum, and those closest to the deceased are now embroiled in a ruthless game of cat and mouse.

At the heart of "Rumors" is the theme of reputation and the devastating consequences of gossip. In this rarefied world, one's standing is everything, and the whispers of the past can be just as deadly as any scandal. Godbersen expertly weaves together the threads of her characters' lives, exposing the dark underbelly of their privileged existence. As alliances are forged and broken, and secrets are revealed, the stakes grow higher, threatening to destroy the very foundations of their social hierarchy.

One of the most striking aspects of Godbersen's writing is her ability to craft characters that are at once relatable and flawed. Diana, with her sharp wit and determination, is a compelling protagonist, while the seductive Penelope Hayes is a deliciously malicious presence, always lurking in the shadows, waiting to strike. Even the minor characters are fully realized, with their own motivations and desires that add depth and complexity to the narrative.

Throughout the book, Godbersen's style is as engaging as it is evocative. Her prose is witty and engaging, with a keen eye for the absurdities of high society. The dialogue is sharp and natural, capturing the nuances of her characters' relationships with ease. Whether she's describing the opulent ballrooms of Manhattan's elite or the cramped, dingy tenements of the lower classes, Godbersen's writing is always vivid and immersive.

In short, "Rumors" is a captivating and deliciously scandalous novel that will appeal to fans of "Gossip Girl" and "The Great Gatsby" alike. With its intricate plot, complex characters, and sumptuous setting, this book is a must-read for anyone who loves historical fiction, romance, or just a good dose of scandal. So, if you're ready to enter the rarefied world of Manhattan's elite in 1899, then "Rumors" is the perfect book for you.
