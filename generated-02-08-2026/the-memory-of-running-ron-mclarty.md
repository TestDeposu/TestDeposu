# The Memory of Running

**Author:** Ron McLarty  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2004

The Memory of Running by Ron McLarty is a mesmerizing tale of self-discovery, love, loss, and redemption that will captivate readers from the very first page. McLarty masterfully weaves a narrative that is both poignant and humorous, as the protagonist, Smithson "Smithy" Ide, embarks on an extraordinary journey that will leave readers cheering for this lovable, yet flawed, hero.

On the surface, Smithy appears to be a loser – an overweight, friendless, chain-smoking, and drunk forty-three-year-old man who has given up on life. However, as the story unfolds, McLarty skillfully reveals the complexities of Smithy's character, exposing a deep sense of vulnerability, sensitivity, and longing. The loss of his parents and sister within a span of one week sets Smithy on a downward spiral, but it also serves as a catalyst for his transformation.

McLarty's writing style is a perfect blend of wit, humor, and pathos, making it impossible not to become invested in Smithy's journey. The author's use of vivid imagery and descriptive language brings the protagonist's world to life, from the nostalgic charm of his childhood home in Rhode Island to the open roads and highways that he travels on his trusty Raleigh bicycle.

As Smithy pedals across the country, he encounters a cast of eccentric characters who add to the richness and diversity of the narrative. From the kind-hearted strangers who offer him shelter and food to the fellow travelers who share their stories and wisdom, each person plays a significant role in shaping Smithy's perspective on life.

Through Smithy's journey, McLarty explores themes of grief, guilt, and redemption, raising questions about the human condition and our capacity for forgiveness and self-compassion. The author shows how even in the darkest moments, there is always hope for transformation and renewal, and that it's never too late to change the course of our lives.

The Memory of Running is a testament to the power of storytelling, with McLarty's masterful prose and engaging narrative making it a compelling read from start to finish. This novel is a must-read for anyone who has ever felt lost or alone, and for those who believe in the human spirit's capacity for resilience and redemption.
