# The Ladies of Mandrigyn (Sun Wolf and Starhawk #1)

**Author:** Barbara Hambly  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

**The Ladies of Mandrigyn: A Swashbuckling Masterpiece of Ambition, Betrayal, and Unforgettable Women**

Barbara Hambly’s *The Ladies of Mandrigyn*—the first installment in the *Sun Wolf and Starhawk* series—is a dazzling, darkly comic, and morally ambiguous fantasy that redefines the mercenary genre. Here, the sword doesn’t just cut flesh; it carves through the illusions of honor, loyalty, and even survival. At its heart, this is a story about power, the women who wield it, and the men who either bow to it or burn under its weight. Hambly, a *New York Times* bestseller with a razor-sharp wit and a penchant for subverting expectations, crafts a world where every deal is a gamble, every alliance a knife’s edge away from betrayal.

Sun Wolf, the series’ titular mercenary, is a force of nature—brutal, cunning, and utterly without remorse. He’s the kind of leader who commands fear not out of cruelty, but because he’s already calculated the cost of defiance. His first lieutenant, Starhawk, is his equal in every way: a woman so lethal she makes the most hardened soldiers hesitate. Their dynamic is electric, a dance of mutual respect and simmering tension, where trust is a luxury, not a given. Together, they’ve carved a name for themselves in a world drowning in war, but their reputation is built on more than just skill—it’s built on the bodies of those who underestimated them.

Then comes Mandrigyn, a port city reduced to rubble by a mad wizard of terrifying power. The city’s emissary arrives with gold, promises, and a proposition: Sun Wolf’s army for a price. The mercenary, ever the pragmatist, refuses. But in this world, refusal is just another kind of answer. The women of Mandrigyn—led by the enigmatic and formidable Lady Isolde—are not the kind to take no for an answer. They’re the kind to make you regret it.

What follows is a masterclass in political maneuvering, where every conversation is a chess match and every alliance a temporary truce. Hambly’s prose is lean, precise, and dripping with irony. She doesn’t shy away from the grimy underbelly of war or the cutthroat nature of power struggles, but she balances it with sharp humor and a keen eye for human folly. The dialogue crackles with wit, the betrayals are inevitable but never predictable, and the stakes are always high—whether it’s a matter of life, treasure, or the fate of a kingdom.

What makes *The Ladies of Mandrigyn* truly stand out is its unflinching portrayal of women in power. Starhawk is not a damsel in distress; she’s a force of nature, every bit as ruthless as Sun Wolf. The women of Mandrigyn, from the cunning Lady Isolde to the fiery and resourceful locals, are not passive victims but active agents in their own salvation. Hambly doesn’t romanticize their struggles; she presents them as they are—fierce, calculating, and willing to do whatever it takes to survive. In a genre often dominated by brooding heroes and one-dimensional women, this is a refreshing change.

The book’s strength lies in its ambiguity. There are no clear heroes, no easy victories, and no neat resolutions. Sun Wolf and Starhawk are not good men; they’re survivors. Their morality is flexible, their loyalty conditional, and their actions often leave more blood in their wake than glory. This isn’t a story about redemption; it’s about power, and the cost of wielding it. Hambly doesn’t offer easy answers, and that’s part of its genius.

Visually, the world is richly imagined—lush, dangerous, and steeped in the kind of political intrigue that makes every decision a high-stakes gamble. The magic, when it appears, is not some whimsical fairy-tale spellcasting but a force of nature, as terrifying as it is unpredictable. The city of Mandrigyn itself is a character, its beauty and decay painting a vivid backdrop for the drama unfolding within its walls.

For fans of dark fantasy, political intrigue, and morally complex protagonists, *The Ladies of Mandrigyn* is a must-read. It’s a book that rewards close reading, where every line carries weight and every character is layered with depth. Hambly’s writing is effortless yet masterful, blending action, humor, and sharp social commentary into a narrative that’s as satisfying as it is thought-provoking.

If you’ve ever wanted a fantasy where the women aren’t just along for the ride but the ones driving the story, where the mercenaries are as morally gray as the world they inhabit, and where every deal comes with a price—then this is your book. *The Ladies of Mandrigyn* is not just the start of a series; it’s a statement. A reminder that in a world of war and magic, the most dangerous weapons are often the ones wielded by those who refuse to be victims.
