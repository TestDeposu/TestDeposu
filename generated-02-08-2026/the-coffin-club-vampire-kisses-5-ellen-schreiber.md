# The Coffin Club (Vampire Kisses, #5)

**Author:** Ellen Schreiber  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Ellen Schreiber’s fifth installment in the Vampire Kisses saga, *The Coffin Club*, thrusts Raven back into the moon‑lit streets of Dullsville with a restless urgency that feels both familiar and freshly perilous. The novel’s beating heart is the tension between love’s illuminate and the shadows that threaten to swallow it whole—a classic push‑pull that Schreiber sharpens by forcing Raven to chase Alexander’s disappearance while simultaneously being lured into the clandestine depths of the Coffin Club. Here, the titular club is more than a gothic hangout; it becomes a metaphorical labyrinth where desire, danger, and self‑discovery intersect. As Raven slips through a hidden door into a catacomb where the house drink is literally type A or B blood, she confronts not only external enemies but also the internal question of how far she will go to protect the boy she loves when his own secrets begin to surface.

Schreiber’s style remains unmistakably sizzling: short, punchy sentences drive the narrative forward with a heartbeat‑like rhythm, while her lush, sensory descriptions paint the underground world in velvety blacks and crimson glints. She balances teen‑voice authenticity with a wistful, almost poetic darkness, allowing readers to feel Raven’s pulse quicken with every whispered clue and every forbidden glance. The pacing never falters—each revelation is a beat that propels the next, making the mystery of Alexander’s absence as compelling as the allure of the underworld itself. By the time Raven stands at the threshold of the secret hangout, the reader is already half‑drawn into the shadows, eager to see whether love can illuminate the darkness or be consumed by it.

*The Coffin Club* proves that Schreiber can deepen her series without losing the breezy, addictive charm that made the early books cult favorites. It’s a tale where romance and horror entwine, reminding us that the most dangerous secrets are often the ones we keep from ourselves.
