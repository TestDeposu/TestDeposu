# The Singing (The Books of Pellinor, #4)

**Author:** Alison Croggon  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

The Singing, the final installment of Alison Croggon's epic quartet, The Books of Pellinor, is a mesmerizing tale of magic, adventure, and the unbreakable bond between siblings. The story unfolds in a world where the forces of darkness are rapidly gaining ground, and the fate of the Seven Kingdoms hangs precariously in the balance. Against this backdrop of impending doom, Croggon masterfully weaves a narrative that is both a thrilling page-turner and a poignant exploration of the human condition.

At the heart of the story are Maerad and Hem, two siblings who possess a unique gift – the ability to harness the power of the Singing, a mystical music that has the potential to vanquish the Nameless One, the supreme enemy of the realm. As they embark on separate journeys, desperately searching for each other amidst the chaos and destruction, Croggon skillfully ratchets up the tension, creating a sense of urgency that propels the reader forward.

One of the most striking aspects of Croggon's writing style is her ability to craft a world that is both richly detailed and deeply immersive. The landscapes of Edil-Amarandh are vividly rendered, from the lush forests to the snow-capped mountains, and the characters that inhabit this world are equally well-drawn. Maerad and Hem, in particular, are complex and multidimensional, their motivations and emotions expertly nuanced to create a sense of depth and authenticity.

The theme of the Singing is central to the story, and Croggon explores it with a level of sophistication and subtlety that is rare in fantasy literature. The Singing is more than just a metaphor for the power of music; it is a symbol of hope, of resilience, and of the human spirit's capacity to overcome even the most daunting challenges. As Maerad and Hem draw closer to their goal, the Singing becomes a powerful force that transcends the boundaries of reality, speaking to something deep within the reader.

Croggon's prose is lyrical and evocative, with a musical quality that is perfectly suited to the story's themes. Her writing is never overly elaborate or flowery, but it has a simplicity and elegance that belies its depth and complexity. The pacing is expertly managed, with a narrative that flows effortlessly from one scene to the next, building towards a climax that is both thrilling and emotionally resonant.

In short, The Singing is a triumph, a fitting conclusion to a quartet that has captivated readers with its unique blend of magic, adventure, and epic storytelling. Croggon's writing is superb, her world-building meticulous, and her characters richly drawn. This is a book that will appeal to fans of fantasy and adventure, but also to anyone who loves a good story, well-told. With its themes of hope, resilience, and the power of the human spirit, The Singing is a must-read for anyone looking for a story that will linger long after the final page has been turned.
