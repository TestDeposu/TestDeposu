# The Long Patrol (Redwall, #10)

**Author:** Brian Jacques  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1998

The Long Patrol, the tenth installment in Brian Jacques' beloved Redwall series, is a captivating tale of courage, honor, and the unyielding spirit of youth. Set against the picturesque backdrop of Salamandastron Mountain and the idyllic Redwall Abbey, this story follows the journey of Tamello De Fformelo Tussock, a young and ambitious hare determined to join the prestigious Long Patrol.

Through Tammo's eyes, Jacques masterfully explores the themes of growing up, perseverance, and the importance of learning from one's mistakes. As Tammo navigates the challenges of becoming a warrior, he is guided by the wise and enigmatic Russa, a nomad squirrel who teaches him the value of patience, discipline, and loyalty. As the story unfolds, Tammo finds himself at the forefront of a brewing battle between the Long Patrol and the villainous rat Rapscallions, who threaten to destroy the peaceful Redwall Abbey.

Jacques' writing style is, as always, a true delight. His vivid descriptions of the natural world, coupled with his unique blend of anthropomorphic characters and poetic language, transport readers to a world of wonder and magic. The author's use of alliteration, as seen in the book's opening phrase, "Barradum! Barradum! Barrabubbitybubbityboom!", adds a playful touch to the narrative, making the story feel both timeless and ageless.

One of the most striking aspects of The Long Patrol is its ability to balance lighthearted moments with more serious themes. Jacques tackles complex issues such as mortality, sacrifice, and the consequences of war, yet does so in a way that is accessible and engaging for readers of all ages. The characters, too, are well-developed and relatable, with each one bringing their unique personality and quirks to the story.

As the battle between good and evil reaches its climax, Jacques' pacing becomes increasingly suspenseful, leaving readers on the edge of their seats as they cheer on Tammo and the Long Patrol. The story's conclusion is both satisfying and emotional, as the young heroes of Redwall emerge victorious, but not without scars.

In conclusion, The Long Patrol is a must-read for fans of the Redwall series and anyone looking for a story that will captivate and inspire. With its richly detailed world, memorable characters, and timeless themes, this book is sure to delight readers of all ages. As with all of Jacques' works, The Long Patrol is a testament to the power of imagination and the importance of holding onto one's sense of wonder.
