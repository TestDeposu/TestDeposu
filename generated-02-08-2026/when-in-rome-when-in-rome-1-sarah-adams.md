# When in Rome (When in Rome, #1)

**Author:** Sarah Adams  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

When in Rome offers a breezy, heart‑warming escape that feels like a sun‑drenched slice of pie served fresh from Noah Walker’s oven. Sarah Adams takes the timeless premise of Audrey Hepburn’s Roman Holiday—royalty slipping away for a taste of normal life—and transplants it to the sleepy streets of Rome, Kentucky. The result is a delightful clash of glitter‑filled pop stardom and flour‑dusted small‑town charm, where opposites don’t just attract; they spark a slow‑burn romance that feels both familiar and refreshingly sincere.

At the story’s core is Amelia Rose, the burned‑out “princess of pop” who trades stadium lights for a busted‑down car on a stranger’s front lawn. Adams paints her with a deft hand: Amelia’s glamorous façade cracks to reveal a kind‑hearted, slightly goofy woman yearning for authenticity. Across the porch, Noah Walker is the gruff, pragmatic baker whose life revolves around his grandmother’s pie shop and the well‑meaning meddling of his neighbours. Their initial friction—his irritation at her celebrity baggage versus her awe at his unpretentious world—sets up a classic opposites‑attract dynamic, but Adams avoids caricature. Instead, she lets their personalities unfold through witty banter, tender moments of vulnerability, and shared experiences that range from midnight pie‑tasting tours to impromptu karaoke in the town square.

Adams’ style shines in its light, conversational rhythm, reminiscent of a friend recounting a favorite summer romance over coffee. She peppers the narrative with pop‑culture nods—references to Roman Holiday, fleeting glimpses of Amelia’s chart‑topping hits, and the occasional meme‑worthy quip—that keep the tone buoyant without sacrificing emotional depth. The prose is clean and accessible, yet she manages to slip in poignant observations about loneliness in the spotlight and the quiet courage required to let someone see the real you. The pacing mirrors a leisurely road trip: scenes linger just long enough to savor the setting, then accelerate as the protagonists’ feelings deepen, culminating in a satisfying, hopeful resolution that leaves room for imagination.

What makes When in Rome stand out is its ability to balance frothy fun with genuine sentiment. Adams never lets the premise become a gimmick; instead, she uses the small‑town backdrop as a mirror for Amelia’s internal journey, allowing the heroine to rediscover joy in simple pleasures—a fresh‑baked croissant, a neighbor’s wave, the feeling of being known for who she is rather than what she sings. Noah, in turn, learns to soften his edges, realizing that love isn’t about fixing someone else but sharing life’s ordinary magic.

In short, When in Rome is a feel‑good rom‑com that reads like a warm hug: witty, tender, and undeniably charming. It’s a perfect pick for anyone craving a love story that celebrates both the sparkle of fame and the comfort of home—proof that sometimes the best adventures happen when you take a wrong turn and end up exactly where you were meant to be.
