# Valiant (Modern Faerie Tales, #2)

**Author:** Holly Black  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2006

In Holly Black's captivating sequel to "The Spiderwick Chronicles" (although officially a part of the Modern Faerie Tales trilogy), we delve into the gritty and enchanting world of faeries in the midst of New York City's underbelly. With "Valiant", the second book in her critically acclaimed trilogy, Black masterfully weaves a dark and intricate narrative that explores the complexities of love, loyalty, and the blurred lines between right and wrong.

One of the most striking aspects of "Valiant" is its protagonist, Valerie, a vulnerable yet fiercely independent teenager who, in a bid to escape the traumas of her past, takes refuge in the unlikeliest of places: a gang of squatters dwelling within the labyrinthine network of New York City's subway tunnels. As Valerie navigates this treacherous new world, she finds herself increasingly entangled in the lives of faeries – mischievous, capricious, and often deadly creatures that defy the conventions of the human world above.

Through Valerie's story, Black skillfully subverts the notion of faeries as benevolent, gentle creatures, instead crafting a narrative where the stakes are high and the perils are very real. Her faeries are a product of the city, reflecting its grittiness, corruption, and decay – a reflection of the darker aspects of human nature that we dare not confront.

At its core, "Valiant" is a beautifully written exploration of identity, morality, and the fragility of the human heart. Through Valerie's relationships with the faeries – particularly the enigmatic and endearing Sean – Black poses thought-provoking questions about the nature of loyalty, duty, and the lengths to which we will go to protect those we care about.

If I were to identify a weakness in Black's writing, it would be her tendency to sacrifice subtlety for the sake of plot progression. Occasionally, the narrative feels overhyped, and the pacing can be frenetic. However, these issues are more than compensated by Black's skillful world-building and her unwavering commitment to creating complex, multidimensional characters.

As with Black's preceding works, "Valiant" boasts an unflinching, evocative prose that plunges readers headfirst into the darker recesses of the faerie realm. Her world-building is meticulous, drawing on the rich cultural heritage of mythology and folklore to create an atmosphere that is both eerily familiar and deeply unsettling.

"Valiant" is, without a doubt, a compelling sequel to the Modern Faerie Tales trilogy. If you're looking for a story that explores the intricacies of love, loyalty, and the human condition, then "Valiant" is a must-read. Be prepared for a narrative that will take you on a rollercoaster ride of emotions, with no clear answers and few easy resolutions. For Black's "Valiant" is a testament to the transformative power of storytelling, a reminder that even in the bleakest of worlds, there lies the potential for redemption, hope, and the possibility of a new beginning.
