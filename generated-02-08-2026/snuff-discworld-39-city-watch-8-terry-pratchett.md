# Snuff (Discworld, #39; City Watch, #8)

**Author:** Terry Pratchett  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

Terry Pratchett's 39th Discworld novel, Snuff, is a masterful blend of satire, mystery, and social commentary, cementing his status as one of the most beloved and respected authors of his generation. In this eighth installment of the City Watch series, Commander Sam Vimes is forced to trade in the familiar streets of Ankh-Morpork for the countryside, where he embarks on a journey that is both a fish-out-of-water story and a scathing critique of class and prejudice.

As Vimes navigates the complexities of rural life, Pratchett's signature wit and humor are on full display. The author's keen eye for detail and his ability to find the absurd in even the most mundane situations make for a hilarious and entertaining read. However, beneath the humor lies a sharp commentary on the social ills of Pratchett's world, and our own. The novel tackles issues of class, privilege, and the often-blurred lines between justice and morality.

One of the most striking aspects of Snuff is its thoughtful exploration of the tensions between urban and rural life. Vimes, a product of the city, finds himself at odds with the customs and traditions of the countryside, and Pratchett uses this culture clash to highlight the often-insurmountable barriers between different social classes. Through Vimes's struggles to adapt, Pratchett shows us that even the most well-intentioned individuals can be products of their environment, and that true understanding and empathy require a willingness to listen and learn.

Pratchett's writing style is, as always, a joy to behold. His prose is effortless, with a narrative voice that is both engaging and authoritative. The characters, too, are well-drawn and complex, with Vimes being a particular standout. His gruff, no-nonsense demeanor is perfectly captured, and his interactions with the rural community are both poignant and humorous.

If there is a criticism to be made, it is that the novel's pacing can be somewhat uneven at times. The mystery at the heart of the story is engaging, but it takes a backseat to the social commentary and satire. While this is not necessarily a bad thing, it does mean that the plot can feel somewhat meandering at times.

Overall, however, Snuff is a triumphant addition to the Discworld series, and a testament to Pratchett's skill as a writer and social commentator. It is a novel that will appeal to both longtime fans of the series and newcomers alike, and its themes of class, prejudice, and social justice are just as relevant today as they were when the book was first published.
