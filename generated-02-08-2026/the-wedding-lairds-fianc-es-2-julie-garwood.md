# The Wedding (Lairds' Fiancées, #2)

**Author:** Julie Garwood  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

**A Spark That Ignites: Julie Garwood’s *The Wedding*—Where Passion Meets Secrets in a Dazzling Romance**

Julie Garwood’s *The Wedding*, the second installment in the *Crown’s Spies* series, is a masterclass in high-stakes romance—where fire meets ice, and a single stolen kiss could unravel everything. At its heart, this is a story of defiance, desire, and the dangerous allure of secrets, wrapped in Garwood’s signature blend of wit, tension, and emotional depth. If you adore the slow-burn intensity of Jude Deveraux or the sharp banter of Julia Quinn’s Regency romances, *The Wedding* delivers with a vengeance.

Christina Bennett is London’s most captivating enigma—a woman of razor-sharp intellect and untamed spirit, whose beauty masks a past she guards with fierce loyalty. She has spent her life navigating the treacherous waters of high society, where every glance, every whispered rumor, could expose the truth she’s sworn to protect. Then comes Lyon, the arrogant but undeniably magnetic Marquis of Lyonwood, whose boldness knows no bounds. When he claims a kiss that tastes of rebellion, he doesn’t just want Christina—he wants the woman beneath the mask, the one who refuses to be tamed. But Christina isn’t a prize to be won; she’s a fortress, and Lyon is the storm that will either break her down or force her to stand her ground.

Garwood’s strength lies in her ability to craft characters who are as compelling as they are flawed. Lyon is a man of privilege and passion, his arrogance a shield against vulnerability, but beneath the surface, he’s haunted by his own demons—demons that make him all the more desperate to claim what he can’t resist. Christina, meanwhile, is a force of nature: fiercely independent, fiercely protective, and fiercely determined to keep her secrets. Their dynamic is electric, a dance of push and pull where every encounter feels like a battle of wills. Garwood doesn’t shy away from the tension—there’s no false romance here, no easy victories. Instead, she builds a relationship that feels raw, real, and utterly addictive.

What sets *The Wedding* apart is its masterful balance of intrigue and intimacy. The novel is steeped in the lush, decadent world of Regency-era London, where whispers in drawing rooms and coded messages in letters add layers of suspense. But Garwood never lets the plot overshadow the emotional core. The chemistry between Lyon and Christina is palpable, their words sharp as daggers yet laced with an undeniable attraction. There’s no slow burn here—it’s a controlled inferno, where every glance, every touch, feels like a step closer to the explosion.

Garwood’s prose is clean, efficient, and utterly immersive. She doesn’t waste words on unnecessary flourishes; instead, she focuses on the tension, the dialogue, and the psychological cat-and-mouse game between her leads. The pacing is brisk, the stakes high, and the payoff—when it comes—is worth every moment of anticipation. By the time the final pages unfold, you won’t just be rooting for Lyon and Christina; you’ll be breathless, desperate to know if their defiance will lead to triumph or ruin.

At its core, *The Wedding* is a story about the power of secrets—and the cost of revealing them. Christina’s past isn’t just a plot device; it’s the thing that could destroy her, and Lyon’s obsession with uncovering it isn’t just about possession. It’s about understanding the woman he’s fallen for, even if she’d rather die than let him see her true self. Garwood explores themes of trust, sacrifice, and the lengths we’ll go to protect what we love, all while keeping the romance sizzling.

For fans of historical romance, *The Wedding* is a standout—one that blends the political intrigue of a spy thriller with the passion of a love story. It’s a book that doesn’t just entertain; it challenges, it provokes, and it leaves you craving more. And if you thought you knew what a Regency romance could be, Garwood’s work will remind you that the best stories are the ones that dare to be dangerous.
