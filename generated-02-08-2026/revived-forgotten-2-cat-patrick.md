# Revived (Forgotten, #2)

**Author:** Cat Patrick  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

What does it mean to truly live when your entire existence is measured not by milestones, but by resurrections? In her compelling 2012 novel Revived, author Cat Patrick takes this haunting premise and turns it into a masterclass in young adult speculative fiction. Following her intriguing debut Forgotten, Patrick returns with a high-concept thriller that smartly balances sci-fi intrigue with the deeply relatable, often painful angst of teenage identity.

The narrative centers on Daisy Appleby, a fifteen-year-old girl who is no stranger to the afterlife. Following a fatal bus crash in her early childhood, Daisy became a prime subject in a secretive, covert government program utilizing an experimental drug known simply as Revive. The catch? Every time Daisy dies, she must be rebooted—meaning a new name, a new city, and a starkly wiped slate. By the time readers meet her, she has endured five deaths and five forced reinventions. 

Patrick’s prose shines brightest when exploring the psychological toll of this transient lifestyle. The central theme of Revived is not merely the science-fiction wonder of defeating death, but the profound emotional cost of a life without roots. How do you form attachments when your entire world is designed to be temporary? How do you build a sense of self when your history is classified, redacted, and routinely erased? Daisy’s internal monologue captures this exquisite agony. She is a girl constantly mourning a version of herself that no one else remembers, forced to wear new skin like an ill-fitting coat in every new town. 

The author’s style is brisk, accessible, and cinematic. Patrick wastes no time plunging the reader into the disorienting reality of Daisy's existence, beginning with a striking, high-impact first chapter that immediately establishes the high stakes. Yet, for all its thrilling elements involving clandestine government oversight and corporate-style experimentation, the heart of the book remains grounded in human connection. The dynamic shifts when Daisy crosses paths with Matt and Audrey McKean, charismatic siblings who inadvertently pierce her carefully constructed armor and offer her the elusive gift of true friendship. Through these relationships, Patrick contrasts the cold, sterile reality of the Revive program with the messy, vibrant warmth of authentic teenage connection.

As the plot thickens, the scope expands beyond a simple teenage drama into a genuinely riveting conspiracy thriller. Daisy realizes that the experiment she is trapped in is far larger, and considerably more sinister, than she was ever led to believe. Patrick expertly ratchets up the tension, transforming a character study into a breathless race for autonomy and survival. 

Ultimately, Revived is much more than a fast-paced speculative thriller. It is an emotionally charged exploration of what makes us human. Cat Patrick asks us to consider whether life is merely a biological pulse or the accumulation of memories, scars, and loved ones. By the time the novel reaches its powerful, resonant conclusion, readers will find themselves thoroughly captivated by a protagonist who fights desperately for the right to finally stay in one place, and to finally live a life that is truly her own.
