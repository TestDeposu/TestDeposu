# Abuse (Abuse, #1)

**Author:** Nikki Sex  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Music, Manipulation, and Millions: A Critique of Nikki Sex’s Abuse

In the sprawling, often formulaic landscape of modern billionaire romance, few authors attempt to blend the glitz of extreme wealth with the heavy, psychological chains of domestic abuse. In Abuse (Book 1 of the series), author Nikki Sex steps into this fraught territory, delivering a short yet intensely provocative novella. Though brief, the novel packs a visceral punch, attempting to harmonize the dark realities of a toxic relationship with the intoxicating fantasy of a savior-billionaire and a Broadway soundtrack.

At its core, the novel tackles a deeply resonant and painful theme: the insidious nature of psychological self-worthlessness. Protagonist Rachel embodies the tragic mindset of someone trapped not merely by an abuser, but by her own deeply ingrained conviction that she deserves nothing better. When her abusive boyfriend exits the stage, albeit temporarily, Rachel is forced into a confrontation with her own agency. Sex does not shy away from portraying the paralyzing grip of emotional abuse, capturing the agonizing internal dialogue of a woman conditioned to believe her own value is zero. 

Enter the classic trope of the genre—the brooding, impossibly wealthy English billionaire—who serves as both a catalyst for passion and a mirror reflecting Rachel’s hidden potential. Yet, what sets Sex’s style apart in this introductory installment is the integration of music. Woven intricately into the narrative, classic Broadway tunes and the act of singing become emotional outlets and narrative signposts. Music acts as Rachel’s true inner voice, singing the desires and hurts she is otherwise too muted to express. 

Sex’s authorial style is unapologetically direct, steamy, and fast-paced. Catering strictly to an adult audience, the prose moves swiftly from the suffocating dread of a failing relationship to the searing heat of forbidden passion. There is an urgent, almost breathless quality to the storytelling, mirroring the high stakes of Rachel's emotional awakening. While the brevity of the work leaves little room for slow-burn character development, Sex maximizes every page, leaning heavily on sensory details—the swell of a Broadway melody, the tension of a toxic ultimatum, and the electric touch of a stranger who sees worth where others see only flaws.

Abuse is not a gentle read; it is a melodramatic, high-tempo plunge into the dichotomy of despair and desire. It asks readers to root for a protagonist fighting not just for a new man, but for her very right to exist without fear. For fans of sizzling contemporary romance who enjoy a heavy dose of musical theater flair alongside their billionaires, Nikki Sex delivers a compelling, provocative overture to a four-part saga.
