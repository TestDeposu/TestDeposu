# The Outstretched Shadow (Obsidian Mountain, #1)

**Author:** Mercedes Lackey  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

A Masterclass in Magic, Dogma, and Rebellion: A Review of The Outstretched Shadow

In the grand tradition of epic fantasy, few tropes are as enduring—or as frequently subverted—as the rigid academy where young protagonists learn the ways of power. In The Outstretched Shadow (2007), co-authors Mercedes Lackey and James Mallory breathe vibrant, pulsating life into this familiar framework. The result is a striking opening salvo to the Obsidian Trilogy, a novel that uses its 2007 publication context to deliver a timeless commentary on institutional dogma, patriarchal control, and the liberating terror of unlearning.

At its core, the novel’s central theme revolves around the eternal struggle between orthodoxy and intuition, personified here as High Magic versus Wild Magic. When we first meet Kellen Tavadon, he is a product of his environment: the pampered, indoctrinated son of the Arch-Mage in the pristine Golden City of the Bells. Lackey and Mallory construct this society with chilling efficiency. It is a world where magic is strictly regulated, codified, and—most revealingly—restricted exclusively to men under the patriarchal thumb of the Council of Mages. Women are deemed fundamentally unfit, and any magic that defies the rigid, bureaucratic rules is branded as dangerous, heretical, and inherently corrupting. 

Kellen’s awakening begins not with ambition, but with curiosity, when he stumbles upon the forbidden Books of Wild Magic. The narrative shift at this juncture is where the authors’ stylistic prowess truly shines. As Kellen transitions from the sterile, rule-bound academicism of High Magic to the organic, heartbeat-rhythm of Wild Magic, the prose itself seems to expand. High Magic is depicted as a cage of geometry and memorization; Wild Magic is rendered as a living, breathing force that requires emotional resonance and symbiosis with the natural world. 

What makes The Outstretched Shadow a compelling read is that Kellen’s rebellion is not treated as an easy power fantasy. When he is inevitably banished and forced to run for his life with the Outlaw Hunt snapping at his heels, his newfound freedom is terrifying. Lackey and Mallory do a commendable job of showcasing Kellen’s internal friction. He is a boy forced to reconcile a lifetime of systemic lies with the sudden, undeniable reality of centaurs, dryads, Elven royalty, and—most disruptively—the brilliant female Wild Mages his society taught him to look down upon. 

The pacing is relentless, balancing breathless escape sequences with rich world-building that pulls from classic high fantasy while subverting its tropes. The looming threat of Demons lurking deep within Obsidian Mountain provides the requisite apocalyptic stakes, yet the true tension of the novel remains ideological. The Demons do not merely want to conquer the world through brute force; they are weaponizing human prejudice, capitalizing on the deep-seated distrust between the followers of High Magic and Wild Magic to ensure that their enemies remain divided. 

Stylistically, Lackey’s signature emotional depth blends seamlessly with Mallory’s knack for grand-scale world-building. They refuse to paint the conflict in simple strokes of black and white. While the Council of Mages is repressive, the looming war requires a fragile reconciliation of these diametrically opposed magical forces. Kellen’s journey asks a vital question that resonates far beyond the page: What happens when the stories you were told about the world turn out to be a carefully constructed cage?

The Outstretched Shadow is a triumph of modern epic fantasy, offering readers a richly textured universe, profound thematic weight, and a protagonist worth rooting for. It reminds us that true strength is found not in blind obedience to ancient rules, but in the courage to question them.
