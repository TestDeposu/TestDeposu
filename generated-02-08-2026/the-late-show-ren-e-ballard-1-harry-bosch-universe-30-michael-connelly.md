# The Late Show (Renée Ballard, #1; Harry Bosch Universe, #30)

**Author:** Michael Connelly  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

In Michael Connelly's latest thriller, The Late Show, the acclaimed author delves into the uncharted territory of his female protagonists, Renée Ballard, the protagonist and a complex, driven young detective. In an impressive departure from his beloved character, Harry Bosch, Connelly introduces a gritty and poignant narrative that unravels the dark underbelly of Los Angeles' most infamous nightlife.

The Late Show is a gripping tale that skillfully navigates between multiple threads of investigation, weaving a complex web of intrigue and suspense as Ballard navigates her midnight shift in Hollywood. A once rising star in the LAPD, Ballard's career stagnates as retaliation against filing a sexual harassment complaint against her supervisor. This sets the stage for Ballard's unyielding spirit and defiance as she takes on two cases that she desperately wants to solve before handing over the reins to the daytime units.

One of the standout features of Connelly's craft in this novel is his masterful manipulation of atmosphere and setting. From the sun-kissed Hollywood streets to the claustrophobic alleyways and cramped nightclubs, Connelly skillfully paints an immersive world where the lines between right and wrong blur. The contrast between these stark, yet vibrant environments serves as a perfect backdrop for Ballard's inner turmoil, highlighting the complexities of her character.

Ballard herself is an electrifying protagonist, crafted with remarkable depth and nuance. It's not simply her unwavering dedication to justice that makes her stand out, but also her personal demons, shaped by years of service in a male-dominated, often corrupt, institution. Her relationships with colleagues, including a particularly poignant dynamic with her partner, are multifaceted and authentic. Connelly's exploration of the human side of these characters adds a layer of emotional resonance to the narrative, setting The Late Show apart from other crime thrillers.

The plot, while convoluted at times, is an exemplary display of Connelly's mastery over pacing and tension. With each twist and turn, the stakes grow higher, drawing readers inexorably into the labyrinth of Ballard's investigation. The connections between the cases start to unfurl, exposing a web of deceit and malice that is impossible to look away from. Each new revelation raises the ante, propelling the story forward with breathless intensity.

As in many of Connelly's previous novels, The Late Show also touches upon the broader social commentary, albeit with less explicitness in this instance. It's the hidden corners of Los Angeles, the seedy underbelly that Ballard navigates with a mix of anger and empathy, that serves as a commentary on the city's dark side and the consequences of corruption and apathy. The result is a thought-provoking narrative that lingers long after the final pages are turned.

Ultimately, The Late Show is a testament to Connelly's versatility as an author and the depth of his storytelling prowess. By introducing a remarkable new heroine in Ballard and delving into a lesser-explored side of Los Angeles, Connelly once again elevates the crime fiction genre. His propulsive writing and intricate plotting guarantee an immersive reading experience that readers won't soon forget.
