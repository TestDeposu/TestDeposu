# Through the Woods

**Author:** E.M. Carroll  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

In the shadowy, rain-drenched expanse of Grayton, E.M. Carroll crafts a chilling yet tender atmospheric journey in the 2014 novel Through the Woods. Blending the visceral terror of classic monster lore with the delicate ache of an emotional awakening, Carroll delivers a mesmerizing exploration of isolation, forced proximity, and redemption. At its core, the novel examines the central theme of how true intimacy often requires the stripping away of civilized artifice, forcing two profoundly lonely souls to confront their vulnerabilities in the darkest of places.

The narrative centers on an arranged marriage between Bernard and Charlotte Preston, a union built on financial security and social standing rather than affection. Charlotte yearns for genuine devotion, while Bernard—an alchemist consumed by his desperate quest to cure a werewolf curse—harbors a quiet, distant adoration for his wife. Carroll’s stylistic brilliance shines in these early chapters, utilizing sparse, evocative prose to capture the cold emotional distance between the couple. The relentless rain of Grayton serves as a physical manifestation of their stagnant, loveless lives, setting a melancholic tone that prepares the reader for the storm to come.

When catastrophe strikes on the night of the Spring Ball, and both Bernard and Charlotte are infected by the werewolf curse, the novel pivots from a tale of domestic alienation into a harrowing survival story. Forced to flee merciless wolf hunters and plunge deep into the unforgiving wilderness, the Prestons must rely entirely on one another. Carroll’s writing becomes increasingly sensory and urgent during these sequences. The oppressive canopy of the woods, the biting chill, and the primal hunger of their new reality mirror the internal shifts happening within the characters. 

What makes Through the Woods particularly striking is its restraint. Marketed as a sweet romance with a low heat level, Carroll resists the urge to rely on explicit passion, choosing instead to fan the flames of emotional connection. As Bernard and Charlotte shed their societal masks and fight for survival as beasts, the barriers of their loveless marriage crumble. Their blossoming affection feels hard-won, forged in the crucible of shared terror and mutual reliance. Yet, the wilderness holds its own malicious intent; unseen evil forces loom just beyond the treeline, ensuring that their newfound bond is tested to its absolute limits.

Carroll’s pacing is expertly measured, balancing internal psychological depth with external peril. The atmospheric world-building lingers long after the final page, leaving the reader with a haunting impression of nature, both red in tooth and claw and strangely restorative. Through the Woods is a captivating read that proves love can sometimes be found in the most monstrous of circumstances.
