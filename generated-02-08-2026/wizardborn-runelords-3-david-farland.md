# Wizardborn (Runelords, #3)

**Author:** David Farland  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

Wizardborn, the third installment in David Farland's epic fantasy series, The Runelords, is a mesmerizing tale of magic, politics, and self-discovery. Set in a world where magic is based on the transfer of attributes, or "endowments," from one person to another, the story follows Gaborn, the Earth King, as he navigates the complexities of ruling a kingdom while facing the threat of the monstrous Reavers.

Farland's writing style is reminiscent of the great fantasy authors of our time, such as Stephen R. Donaldson and Robert Jordan. His world-building is meticulous and detailed, with a complex magic system that underpins the entire narrative. The characters are multidimensional and relatable, with Gaborn's struggles to come to terms with his lost powers and his destiny as the Earth King being particularly compelling.

One of the standout features of Wizardborn is its exploration of the human condition. Farland delves deep into the psychological and emotional struggles of his characters, creating a sense of empathy and understanding in the reader. The character of Averan, a young girl who has eaten a Reaver and absorbed some of its memories, is a prime example of this. Her journey is both haunting and fascinating, as she struggles to reconcile her own identity with the alien memories that now reside within her.

The plot is fast-paced and engaging, with multiple storylines that intersect and converge in unexpected ways. The threat of the Reavers looms large over the entire narrative, creating a sense of tension and urgency that propels the reader forward. The action scenes are vivid and intense, with Farland's descriptive prose bringing the battles to life in vivid detail.

Throughout the book, Farland explores themes of power, identity, and the nature of evil. The Reavers, with their insectoid appearance and hive-like society, serve as a foil to the human characters, highlighting the complexities and nuances of human nature. The character of Raj Ahten, the great warlord, is a particularly interesting study in contrasts, as he struggles to maintain his own sense of identity and purpose in the face of overwhelming odds.

Overall, Wizardborn is a gripping and immersive read that will appeal to fans of epic fantasy. Farland's writing is engaging and evocative, with a keen eye for detail and a deep understanding of the human condition. The book is a testament to the author's skill and craftsmanship, and is sure to leave readers eagerly anticipating the next installment in the series.

In conclusion, Wizardborn is a must-read for anyone who loves fantasy, magic, and adventure. With its richly detailed world, complex characters, and gripping plot, it is a book that will keep you on the edge of your seat from start to finish. So if you're looking for a new series to get lost in, look no further than The Runelords.
