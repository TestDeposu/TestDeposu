# Cemetery Boys (Cemetery Boys, #1)

**Author:** Aiden Thomas  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

Image a world where tradition and identity violently collide, where the veil between the living and the dead is as thin as whispered family expectations. In his groundbreaking 2020 paranormal young adult debut, Cemetery Boys, Aiden Thomas crafts a vibrant, emotionally resonant universe that breathes fresh life into the urban fantasy genre. The novel follows Yadriel, a trans teenage boy belonging to a traditional Latinx community of brujos and brujas. Desperate to prove his rightful place within his family and claim his gender identity in a culture deeply rooted in binary gender roles, Yadriel takes matters into his own hands. He performs a secret ritual to become a full-fledged brujo—only to accidentally summon the wrong spirit. 

Enter Julian Diaz, the school’s notorious resident bad boy, who is newly and violently dead, utterly confused, and entirely refusing to transition quietly into the afterlife. What follows is a high-stakes, supernatural murder mystery blended with a slow-burn romance that anchors the novel’s heavier emotional beats. Thomas explores profound themes of acceptance, grief, and the struggle for self-definition against the backdrop of a richly rendered Latinx magical realism. Yadriel’s journey is not merely about proving his supernatural capabilities to a hesitant family; it is a poignant metaphor for the universal need to be truly seen for who we are. 

Thomas’s writing style is effortlessly engaging, striking a rare balance between snappy, contemporary teenage dialogue and deeply atmospheric world-building. The depiction of the cemetery, the intricate rules of spirit magic, and the warmth of a bustling, imperfect Latinx family are painted with vivid strokes. Julian’s chaotic, magnetic energy provides the perfect comedic and emotional foil to Yadriel’s introverted, determined nature. Their evolving dynamic forms the beating heart of the book, moving gracefully from reluctant alliance to a tender, deeply affecting romance. 

Cemetery Boys is much more than an entertaining paranormal romp; it is a necessary, heart-rending, and ultimately joyous milestone for LGBTQ+ representation in young adult literature. Thomas treats heavy themes—transphobia, community exclusion, and sudden loss—with profound empathy and care, ensuring the narrative never loses its sense of wonder or humor. By the time the final pages arrive, readers will find themselves thoroughly spellbound by Thomas’s imaginative prowess and emotional honesty. This is an essential, unforgettable read that marks the arrival of a brilliant new voice in fantasy.
