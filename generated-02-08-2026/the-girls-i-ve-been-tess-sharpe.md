# The Girls I've Been

**Author:** Tess Sharpe  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

In "The Girls I've Been" by Tess Sharpe, the boundaries of identity, love, and deception are pushed to the limit. This gripping thriller follows Nora O'Malley, the daughter of a con artist, as she navigates a life of fabricated personas and calculated lies. With a unique blend of suspense, action, and romance, Sharpe masterfully crafts a narrative that not only keeps you on the edge of your seat but also explores the complexities of self-discovery and the power of female relationships.

At its core, "The Girls I've Been" is a story about the fluidity of identity and the masks we wear to survive. Nora, having grown up as her mother's protégé in the art of deception, has perfected the art of reinvention. However, when her past and present collide in a dramatic bank heist, she must confront the true depths of her own identity. Sharpe's portrayal of Nora's journey is both authentic and relatable, as she grapples with the tension between her fabricated personas and her true self.

One of the standout aspects of Sharpe's writing is her ability to balance action-packed suspense with nuanced character development. The pacing of the novel is expertly crafted, with each twist and turn keeping the reader invested in Nora's fate. The supporting cast, including Nora's girlfriend Iris and her ex Wes, add depth and complexity to the narrative, exploring themes of love, loyalty, and friendship.

Sharpe's prose is witty, engaging, and unapologetic, making her a compelling voice in the world of young adult fiction. Her writing is both accessible and sophisticated, tackling complex themes with ease. The dialogue is snappy and realistic, bringing the characters to life in a way that feels both authentic and relatable.

What sets "The Girls I've Been" apart from other thrillers is its unapologetic exploration of queer relationships and feminist themes. Sharpe's portrayal of Nora's relationships is raw, honest, and unromanticized, offering a refreshing take on the often-stereotyped world of young adult romance. The novel's exploration of female empowerment and the power of women supporting women is both timely and necessary.

In conclusion, "The Girls I've Been" is a gripping, thought-provoking thriller that will keep you captivated until the very end. With its unique blend of suspense, action, and romance, Sharpe's novel is a must-read for fans of young adult fiction. The author's masterful exploration of identity, love, and deception makes for a compelling narrative that will leave you questioning the very nature of truth and identity.
