# A Song of Legends Lost (Invoker Trilogy, #1)

**Author:** M.H. Ayinde  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

In the realm of epic fantasy, few debuts have been as highly anticipated and widely praised as M.H. Ayinde's A Song of Legends Lost, the first installment of the Invoker Trilogy. This mesmerizing tale of revenge, rebellion, and war has captured the attention of renowned authors, including Tasha Suri, Andrea Stewart, Lavie Tidhar, and James Islington, who have all sung its praises.

At its core, A Song of Legends Lost is a story about the cyclical nature of violence and the devastating consequences of a war that has ravaged the Nine Lands for thousands of years. The people of this realm are bound by their history, which is marked by the brutal suppression of the Scathed people by their greyblood servants, and the subsequent rise of the invoker clans, who wield the power to summon their ancestors in battle. However, as the war drags on, it becomes increasingly clear that the invoker clans' strategy is no longer effective, and a new approach is needed to bring an end to the conflict.

Enter Temi, a commoner who inadvertently invokes a powerful spirit that could potentially tip the balance in favor of the Nine Lands. However, as Temi soon discovers, not all spirits are benevolent ancestors, and some are more malevolent than anyone could have imagined. This sets off a chain of events that challenges the very fabric of the Nine Lands' society and forces its people to confront the darkness within themselves.

Ayinde's writing style is nothing short of masterful. With a keen eye for detail and a deep understanding of the complexities of human nature, the author weaves a rich tapestry of characters, each with their own motivations and conflicts. The world-building is meticulous and immersive, drawing the reader into a realm that is both familiar and yet, utterly unique.

One of the standout features of A Song of Legends Lost is its thought-provoking exploration of the consequences of violence and the nature of power. Ayinde raises important questions about the morality of war and the impact it has on individuals and society as a whole. The author's use of the invoker clans and their ancestral magic serves as a powerful metaphor for the ways in which our past experiences shape our present and inform our future.

Throughout the book, Ayinde's prose is evocative and engaging, with a keen sense of pacing that propels the reader forward. The action scenes are intense and well-choreographed, while the quieter moments are filled with emotional depth and resonance. The characters are multidimensional and relatable, with Temi being a particularly compelling protagonist.

In short, A Song of Legends Lost is a stunning debut that marks the arrival of a major new talent in the world of epic fantasy. With its rich world-building, complex characters, and thought-provoking themes, this book is a must-read for fans of the genre. If you're looking for a story that will keep you on the edge of your seat and leave you eager for more, then look no further than A Song of Legends Lost.
