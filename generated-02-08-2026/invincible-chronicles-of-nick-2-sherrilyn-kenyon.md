# Invincible (Chronicles of Nick, #2)

**Author:** Sherrilyn Kenyon  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

Sherrilyn Kenyon returns to the chaotic, razor-sharp world of her teenage protagonist in *Invincible*, the second installment of the *Chronicles of Nick* series. Released in 2011, this young adult urban fantasy accelerates the high-stakes balancing act of adolescent angst and supernatural horror, proving that surviving a zombie attack is merely the entry fee into a much darker, more complicated existence. 

At its core, *Invincible* explores the universal theme of identity and the terrifying process of choosing one’s path when every external force seems determined to make that choice for you. Nick Gautier is no longer just a kid trying to survive a rough neighborhood; he is trapped in a cosmic tug-of-war, enslaved to a hidden society of shapeshifters and demons who have laid claim to his soul. Kenyon uses this supernatural mythology as a brilliant, heightened metaphor for the teenage experience. The anxieties of high school—harsh principals, confusing romantic entanglements, and the pressure to conform—are brilliantly mirrored by the literal existential dread of being groomed by dark powers. Nick is forced to ask the ultimate question: When your destiny is seemingly written by monsters, how do you reclaim your own free will?

Kenyon’s signature style is immediately recognizable on every page. She weaves a breathless narrative pace with her trademark snappy dialogue, pop-culture references, and gritty urban atmosphere. Even in the face of apocalyptic stakes—such as learning to raise the dead by the end of the week or facing eternal damnation—Nick’s voice remains authentically teenage. He copes with trauma, fear, and profound betrayal through humor and sarcasm. This emotional shielding makes him an incredibly endearing and relatable protagonist. Kenyon doesn't just write action sequences; she invests deeply in the internal emotional landscape of her characters, ensuring that the banter is always backed by genuine vulnerability and high emotional stakes.

*Invincible* successfully expands the vast lore of Kenyon’s established universe while keeping the narrative tightly focused on Nick’s personal evolution. It is a thrilling, fast-paced read that captures the excruciating awkwardness of youth and blends it seamlessly with pulse-pounding supernatural danger. For fans of urban fantasy who appreciate witty protagonists, intricate world-building, and stories where the line between hero and monster is razor-thin, Kenyon’s latest chapter is an absolute triumph.
