# Rush (Breathless, #1)

**Author:** Maya Banks  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

Maya Banks' "Rush" is a tantalizing tale of forbidden love and unrelenting desire that will leave readers breathless. This first installment in the Breathless trilogy sets the stage for a scorching hot romance between Gabe Hamilton, a successful businessman, and Mia Crestwell, his best friend's little sister. The fourteen-year age gap and prior familial connection only serve to heighten the sense of taboo, making their clandestine affair all the more thrilling.

Banks masterfully weaves a narrative that is both seductive and emotionally charged. Her writing style is characterized by vivid descriptions of the characters' innermost thoughts and feelings, drawing the reader into the depths of their passion and vulnerability. As Gabe and Mia navigate their whirlwind romance, the author skillfully exposes the complexities of their relationship, laying bare the fears, doubts, and insecurities that threaten to tear them apart.

One of the most striking aspects of "Rush" is its exploration of the power dynamics at play in Gabe and Mia's relationship. As a dominant and experienced lover, Gabe is accustomed to being in control, while Mia is drawn to his commanding presence and unapologetic sensuality. Banks handles this delicate balance with care, ensuring that Mia's agency and autonomy are never compromised, even as she succumbs to Gabe's charms.

The author's portrayal of Gabe's character is particularly noteworthy, as he is both captivating and flawed. His possessiveness and jealousy are tempered by a deep-seated need for connection and intimacy, making him a compelling and relatable protagonist. Mia, too, is a well-crafted character, her growth from a wide-eyed ingénue to a confident and self-assured woman a joy to behold.

Throughout the novel, Banks maintains a breakneck pace, hurtling the reader toward a dramatic and suspenseful climax. The supporting cast is equally well-developed, with Gabe's best friend and business partner, Jace, playing a pivotal role in the unfolding drama.

If there is one criticism to be levied against "Rush," it is that the novel's themes of obsession and possession may not sit comfortably with all readers. However, Banks handles these complex issues with sensitivity and nuance, never shying away from the darker aspects of Gabe and Mia's relationship.

Ultimately, "Rush" is a captivating and addictive read that will appeal to fans of erotic romance and contemporary fiction alike. Maya Banks' masterful storytelling and vivid characterization make this novel a must-read for anyone seeking a passionate and emotionally charged tale of love and desire.
