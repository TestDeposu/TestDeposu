# Mordant's Need (Mordant's Need, #1-2)

**Author:** Stephen R. Donaldson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1986

Mordant's Need, a two-volume series by Stephen R. Donaldson, is a thought-provoking and mesmerizing tale that delves into the complexities of identity, power, and the human condition. The story follows Terisa Morgan, a young woman who feels disconnected from her life in New York City, as she is transported to the kingdom of Mordant through a mysterious mirror. This unexpected journey sets her on a path of self-discovery and transformation, as she navigates a world of magic, politics, and intrigue.

One of the most striking aspects of Donaldson's writing is his ability to craft complex, multidimensional characters. Terisa, the protagonist, is a fascinating example of this. Initially, she is portrayed as a passive and uncertain individual, struggling to find her place in the world. However, as she navigates the challenges of Mordant, she undergoes a remarkable transformation, revealing a strength and resilience that is both inspiring and empowering.

The world of Mordant itself is a masterful creation, full of intricate details and clever plot twists. Donaldson's use of magic and illusion adds a layer of depth and complexity to the story, as characters must navigate a reality that is constantly shifting and uncertain. The mirrors that play such a central role in the story serve as a powerful metaphor for the fragility and multiplicity of human identity, reflecting and refracting the characters' perceptions of themselves and the world around them.

Throughout the book, Donaldson explores themes of power, oppression, and resistance. The kingdom of Mordant is a patriarchal society, where women are marginalized and exploited, and Terisa's presence serves as a catalyst for change. As she navigates the treacherous landscape of Mordant's court politics, she must confront the darker aspects of human nature and the ways in which power is exercised and maintained.

Donaldson's writing style is characterized by his unique blend of philosophical and psychological insights, combined with a deep understanding of the fantasy genre. His prose is dense and allusive, rewarding close reading and reflection. At times, the pacing can be slow, as the author takes the time to develop his ideas and characters, but the payoff is well worth the effort.

Overall, Mordant's Need is a rich and compelling novel that will appeal to fans of fantasy, science fiction, and philosophical fiction. Donaldson's masterful storytelling and complex characters make for a deeply engaging and thought-provoking read. As Terisa navigates the challenges of Mordant, she must confront the ultimate question: what does it mean to be a person, to have a sense of self and purpose in a world that is constantly shifting and uncertain?

Mordant's Need is a must-read for anyone interested in exploring the deeper themes and complexities of the human condition. With its intricate world-building, complex characters, and thought-provoking ideas, this book is sure to leave a lasting impression on readers.
