# The Coming of Conan the Cimmerian (Conan the Cimmerian, #1)

**Author:** Robert E. Howard  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2003

Between the sinking of Atlantis and the rise of our known civilizations lies a time undreamed of—a brutal, vibrant epoch where shining kingdoms stretch beneath the stars and iron rules supreme. Into this savage Hyborian Age strides Conan the Cimmerian, black-haired, sullen-eyed, and sword in hand, destined to tread the jeweled thrones of the earth under his sandalled feet. 

In The Coming of Conan the Cimmerian, Del Rey has given readers the definitive, uncompromised gateway to Robert E. Howard’s immortal creation. Spanning the author’s first thirteen Conan tales presented in their original, unedited glory and chronological writing order, this volume is not merely a collection of fantasy stories; it is the foundational bedrock of an entire literary genre. Howard didn't just contribute to fantasy; in a meteoric, tragic career cut short by his suicide, he single-handedly invented sword-and-sorcery. 

The central theme pulsating through every page of this collection is the eternal clash between decaying, decadent civilization and the raw, unbridled vitality of the natural world. Howard’s Conan is the ultimate embodiment of the Nietzschean ubermensch filtered through the lens of pulp adventure. He is neither a polished knight errant nor a selfless savior; he is a force of nature, a pragmatist driven by survival, ambition, and an insatiable appetite for life. Whether he is a desperate youth picking locks in shadowy alleys, a swashbuckling pirate commanding the loyalty of cutthroats, or a seasoned commander leading armies, Conan remains relentlessly authentic. He strips away the hypocrisies of walled cities, exposing the rot festering beneath silken robes and marble pillars. 

Howard’s style is nothing short of revolutionary for its time, and it remains remarkably potent today. Eschewing the leisurely, meandering pace of high fantasy pioneers like J.R.R. Tolkien, Howard writes with the breathless urgency and kinetic kineticism of a tightening bowstring. His prose is muscular, visceral, and dripping with atmosphere. He paints his worlds with broad, blood-red strokes of dark fantasy, blending the weird menace of H.P. Lovecraft’s cosmic horror with high-octane swashbuckling adventure. When Conan faces giant spiders, sinister sorcerers whispering forbidden incantations, or shambling ghouls in forgotten tombs, the danger feels immediate and lethal. There is no safe middle ground in Howard’s universe; triumph is bought with sweat and crimson, and defeat means a gruesome demise.

This volume is elevated further by invaluable scholarly material, including Howard’s original drafts, revealing synopses, and a hand-drawn map of the Hyborian world that grounds the mythos in geographical reality. Coupled with Mark Schultz’s striking illustrations, the package honors the pulpy roots of the character while treating the material with the archival respect it has long deserved.

For modern readers accustomed to sanitized, over-explained magic systems, diving into The Coming of Conan the Cimmerian is like taking a bracing gulp of untamed wine. It is fast, furious, and intoxicatingly feral. Howard captured a primal human yearning for adventure, autonomy, and triumph against insurmountable odds. These timeless tales prove that while dynasties crumble and oceans reclaim empires, the raw, defiant spirit of the Cimmerian endures, forever marching toward his destiny across a world undreamed of.
