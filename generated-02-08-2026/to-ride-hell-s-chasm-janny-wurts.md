# To Ride Hell’s Chasm

**Author:** Janny Wurts  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

In Janny Wurts's mesmerizing standalone novel, To Ride Hell's Chasm, the tranquility of the kingdom of Sessalie is shattered when Princess Anja vanishes on the eve of her betrothal banquet. This seemingly simplistic premise gives way to a labyrinthine tale of intrigue, where nothing is as it appears, and the truth is shrouded in an aura of mystery.

Taskin, the esteemed Commander of the Royal Guard, and Mykkael, the rough-hewn newcomer and captain of the garrison, are entrusted with recovering the princess. Their divergent backgrounds and approaches – Taskin's icy competence versus Mykkael's battle-hardened instincts – set the stage for a clash of wills and a test of their mettle. As the investigation unfolds, the duo's contrasting perspectives lead to a radical explanation that challenges the very fabric of the kingdom's understanding of reality.

Mykkael's theory, dismissed as fantastical by the court, sets him at odds with those who would rather cling to the familiar and the comfortable. His conviction, forged in the fire of his experience, drives him to confront the unthinkable: that Princess Anja may not have been taken by force, but fled the palace to escape a malevolent evil that threatens to consume them all.

Janny Wurts's masterful handling of the narrative reveals a complexity that belies the seemingly straightforward premise. Her world-building is a masterclass in subtlety, where myth, legend, and history mingle in a rich tapestry of wonder. The author's prose is a testament to her skill as a wordsmith, spinning a narrative that is at once both intimate and vast, personal and epic.

One of the standout features of To Ride Hell's Chasm is its characters, each one richly textured and multidimensional. Taskin's icy facade hides a depth of emotion, while Mykkael's rough exterior conceals a compassionate heart. The supporting cast adds depth and nuance to the tale, drawing the reader into their world with ease.

The pace of the novel is relentless, hurtling the reader through a maelstrom of twists and turns that keeps them guessing until the very end. Wurts's ability to balance action, suspense, and character development is a testament to her mastery of the craft.

Raymond E. Feist's praise for Wurts as a "gifted creator of wonder" is well-deserved, and it is clear that Stephen Donaldson is equally impressed with her talent. If the quality of To Ride Hell's Chasm is any indication, it is little wonder that her works have earned her a devoted following.

In To Ride Hell's Chasm, Janny Wurts delivers a work of fantasy that is at once both sweeping and intimate, a true epic that will resonate with readers long after they finish reading the final page.
