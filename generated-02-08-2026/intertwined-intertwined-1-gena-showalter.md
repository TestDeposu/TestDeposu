# Intertwined (Intertwined, #1)

**Author:** Gena Showalter  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

**A Dark, Dazzling Odyssey: Gena Showalter’s *Intertwined* and the Chaos of a Boy with Too Many Souls**

Gena Showalter’s *Intertwined*—the prequel to her *White Rabbit Chronicles*—is a wild, intoxicating ride through a world where the supernatural isn’t just real; it’s *personal*. At its heart, this series is a story about Aden Stone, a sixteen-year-old boy who isn’t just one person but a vessel for four distinct souls, each with their own voices, memories, and desires. The result? A mind that’s a battleground, a body that’s a playground for forces beyond human control, and a life where every decision could unravel into something mythic—or catastrophic.

Showalter’s genius lies in her ability to weave psychological depth with gothic spectacle. Aden isn’t just a boy with powers; he’s a boy *haunted* by them. The souls trapped inside him—each with their own agendas, their own traumas—create a fractured, almost Shakespearean internal monologue. One moment, Aden is a reckless teen trying to fit in at a halfway house; the next, he’s a time-traveling seer, a necromancer, or a body-snatcher, his actions spiraling out of his control. The tension between his human fragility and the monstrous potential within him is the book’s driving force. It’s a theme Showalter explores with brutal honesty: what does it mean to be *you* when you’re constantly being rewritten?

But *Intertwined* isn’t just a story about a boy with too many souls—it’s a story about the creatures that *want* those souls. Vampires, werewolves, and other mythical beings sense Aden’s power and see him as either prey or a weapon. The danger isn’t just external; it’s embedded in the very fabric of his existence. Showalter’s world-building is rich with lore, blending classic monsters with fresh, unsettling twists. The vampires here aren’t just blood-drinking aristocrats; they’re political players, manipulators, and sometimes, tragically, the only ones who might understand Aden’s torment. The werewolves are no less complex, their pack dynamics as fraught with betrayal as human society’s.

What makes *Intertwined* truly gripping is its balance of romance and ruin. Aden’s relationships—especially with the enigmatic vampiress who claims to want to help him—are charged with a dangerous intimacy. There’s a romantic tension here that’s as intoxicating as it is perilous, a reminder that in Showalter’s world, love and danger are often two sides of the same coin. The book doesn’t shy away from the brutal consequences of Aden’s choices, whether they’re personal or supernatural. There are moments of dark humor, sure, but the tone is consistently moody, atmospheric, and relentlessly gripping.

Showalter’s writing style is a perfect match for the story. Her prose is sharp and evocative, with a knack for vivid descriptions that make the supernatural feel visceral. She doesn’t over-explain; instead, she lets the mystery of Aden’s powers and the world around him breathe. The dialogue crackles with wit and tension, and the pacing is relentless, keeping readers hooked as Aden’s fate unravels in increasingly shocking ways.

At its core, *Intertwined* is a story about identity, control, and the cost of power. Aden is a boy trying to navigate a world that’s already decided he’s broken—but the real question is whether he can ever be whole. Showalter doesn’t offer easy answers. Instead, she delivers a dark, dazzling adventure where the line between hero and monster is as blurred as Aden’s own fractured psyche. If you love stories where the supernatural feels personal, where romance is as dangerous as it is beautiful, and where every page turns into a new kind of revelation, then *Intertwined* is a must-read.
