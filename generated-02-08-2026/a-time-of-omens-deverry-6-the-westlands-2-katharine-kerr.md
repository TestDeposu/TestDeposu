# A Time of Omens (Deverry, #6; The Westlands, #2)

**Author:** Katharine Kerr  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1992

In A Time of Omens, the sixth installment of her sprawling Deverry cycle and the second volume in The Westlands sub-series, Katharine Kerr continues to solidify her reputation as one of the premier architects of Celtic-inspired high fantasy. Published in 1992, this novel showcases an author fully in command of her craft, weaving a complex tapestry of reincarnation, political upheaval, and looming supernatural dread. Kerr does not merely write fantasy; she breathes life into an intricate, living history where the past continuously bleeds into the present.

The central theme of A Time of Omens revolves around destiny, identity, and the heavy burden of consequence. Kerr’s universe is defined by the rigid spiritual law of the dweomer and the unending cycle of rebirth, which dictates that actions in past lives invariably demand resolution in the current one. This overarching motif is brilliantly mirrored in the dual journeys of the novel's primary figures. On one hand, a young, rightful king must travel in disguise to claim his throne, navigating the treacherous waters of prophecy and political machination. On the other, the exiled lord Rhodry Maelwaedd returns to his roots as a mercenary silver dagger, wandering from the ethereal grasslands of the elves back to the volatile hills of Deverry. Both characters are running toward—and ultimately confronting—fates that are far greater than themselves. 

Yet, what elevates the narrative is the atmospheric dread introduced by the shadowy Guardians, whose cryptic omens hint at an impending cataclysmic war. Kerr uses these omens not as cheap narrative tropes, but as psychological weights that press down on every character, forcing them to question their choices and loyalties. The sense of an approaching storm is palpable on every page, creating an underlying tension that drives the plot forward with relentless momentum.

Stylistically, Kerr is a master of immersive world-building and character-driven plotting. Her prose is fluent, graceful, and deeply evocative, effortlessly limning a Celtic-based medieval world that feels simultaneously ancient and immediate. She eschews generic fantasy tropes in favor of cultural depth, paying meticulous attention to the societal structures, folklore, and magic systems of both humans and elves. Her characters—men, women, and elven folk alike—are remarkably human in their flaws, desires, and fears. They are colorful, resilient, and morally complex, refusing to fit neatly into boxes of pure good or absolute evil. 

A Time of Omens is a triumph of epic fantasy storytelling. It demands patience and rewards attention, offering a deeply layered narrative that resonates long after the final page. For readers who appreciate intricate plotting, rich historical texture, and a profound exploration of fate versus free will, Kerr’s latest chapter in the saga remains an essential and captivating read.
