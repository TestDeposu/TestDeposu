# In the Hand of the Goddess (Song of the Lioness, #2)

**Author:** Tamora Pierce  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

In the second installment of Tamora Pierce’s Song of the Lioness series, Alanna of Trebond continues to walk the razor‑thin line between the world she was born into and the one she has forged for herself. Disguised as a boy, she serves as squire to Prince Jonathan, her closest confidant and the heir to the throne of Tortall. When a malevolent sorcerer—driven by ancient grudges and a hunger for power—sets his sights on the prince’s life, Alanna must summon every ounce of her swordsmanship, burgeoning magic, and unshakable loyalty to shield the future king. The stakes are not merely political; they are deeply personal, forcing Alanna to confront the cost of keeping her secret and the possibility that protecting those she loves may require her to relinquish the very dreams that have driven her since she first donned a page’s boy’s garb.

At its core, *In the Hand of the Goddess* explores the theme of identity forged in fire. Pierce deftly interrogates the tension between societal expectation and inner truth, using Alanna’s dual life as a mirror for readers grappling with their own sense of self. The novel asks: What does it mean to be authentic when the world demands a mask? How does one honor friendship, duty, and personal ambition when they pull in opposite directions? Through Alanna’s internal struggle, Pierce celebrates courage not as the absence of fear but as the resolve to act despite it—a lesson that resonates far beyond the fantasy realm.

Pierce’s style remains both accessible and lyrical, a hallmark that has endeared her to generations of young adult readers. She blends brisk, kinetic action sequences with moments of quiet introspection, allowing the narrative to breathe between clashes of steel and spells. Her dialogue crackles with authenticity; Jonathan’s earnest optimism, Alanna’s sharp wit, and the sorcerer’s chilling menace each feel distinct, enriching the tapestry of Tortall’s court. Moreover, Pierce weaves magic into the fabric of everyday life without over‑exposition—spells are felt as extensions of character rather than flashy set‑pieces, reinforcing the novel’s emphasis on inner strength over external spectacle.

The pacing is deliberate yet relentless. Early chapters establish the fragile peace of the palace and the growing intimacy between Alanna and Jonathan, while the middle section ratchets up tension as the sorcerer’s machinations unfold. Pierce skillfully balances world‑building with character development, ensuring that each revelation about Tortall’s history or Alanna’s burgeoning powers feels earned rather than expository. The climax delivers a satisfying blend of swordplay and sorcery, culminating in a choice that forces Alanna to confront the ultimate sacrifice: revealing her true self or preserving the life of the friend she would die for.

Overall, *In the Hand of the Goddess* is a compelling continuation of a series that redefines what heroic fantasy can be for young adults. Pierce’s nuanced exploration of gender, loyalty, and personal growth, paired with her vivid, engaging prose, makes this novel both an exciting adventure and a thoughtful meditation on the cost of authenticity. Readers who cherish strong, complex heroines and richly imagined worlds will find themselves eagerly turning pages, eager to see how Alanna’s journey unfolds in the chapters to come.
