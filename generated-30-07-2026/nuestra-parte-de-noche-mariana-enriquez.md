# Nuestra parte de noche

**Author:** Mariana Enriquez  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

Mariana Enriquez does not merely write horror; she summons it, bleeds it onto the page, and forces the reader to confront it in the broad daylight of history. In her monumental, hypnotic novel Nuestra parte de noche, published in 2021, the Argentine author delivers a masterclass in dark fiction that transcends the boundaries of genre, cementing her status as one of the most vital and fearless voices in contemporary literature. 

At its core, the novel is a harrowing road trip across a fractured landscape. A father, Juan, and his young son, Gaspar, drive from the stifling tension of Buenos Aires toward the cascading waters of Iguazú, fleeing a shadowy destiny. It is the era of Argentina’s brutal military junta—a time when the real-world horrors of state-sponsored terror, disappearances, and military checkpoints bleed seamlessly into something far more ancient and otherworldly. Gaspar’s mother has died under deeply suspicious circumstances, and Juan is desperate to shield his son from inheriting her tragic burden: to serve as a medium for "the Darkness," an entity worshipped by a secretive, predatory elite known as the Order. 

Enriquez’s thematic genius lies in how she intertwines the supernatural with the deeply political. The grotesque rituals of the Order—obsessed with eternal life, human sacrifice, and perverse fleshly liturgies—are mirrored by the atrocities of the dictatorship. In Enriquez’s universe, political tyranny and cosmic horror are cut from the exact same cloth. The monsters in the dark are terrifying, yes, but they are chillingly analogous to the monsters wearing military uniforms in the light. Through this chilling parallel, the novel explores the crushing weight of inheritance, the agonizing dynamics of fatherhood, and the desperate, futile desire to outrun one's fate.

Stylistically, Nuestra parte de noche is a triumph of atmosphere and precision. Enriquez writes with the icy elegance of a surgeon, handling viscera, blood, and psychological trauma with a chilling, effortless naturalism. Her prose is hypnotic, casting a dark spell that draws the reader through shifting timelines—from the psychedelic haze of 1960s London to the claustrophobic, mutating rooms of the secret society. Comparisons to Stephen King, Shirley Jackson, and even Mary Shelley’s Frankenstein feel entirely justified, yet Enriquez retains a distinct, unmistakably Latin American gothic sensibility. She refuses to offer cheap scares or comforting resolutions; instead, every drop of blood and every shadow serves a precise narrative and political purpose.

This is a sprawling, uncompromising epic that demands total surrender from its audience. Enriquez commands the page with the force of a freight train, delivering an unputdownable experience that lingers long after the final page is turned. Nuestra parte de noche is not just the defining horror novel of our time; it is a dark, dazzling masterpiece that illuminates the deepest, most terrifying recesses of human cruelty and desire.
