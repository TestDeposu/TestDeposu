# Dance of Death (Pendergast, #6; Diogenes, #2)

**Author:** Douglas Preston  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

**Dance of Death: A Chilling Tale of Pendergast's Early Days**

In "Dance of Death," Douglas Preston takes us back to the formative years of Aloysius Pendergast, the enigmatic FBI Special Agent who has captivated readers in the authors' collaborative Pendergast series. This time, however, Preston stands alone, weaving a spellbinding short story that delves into the darker recesses of New Orleans' folklore and Pendergast's own past.

The story unfolds in the French Quarter, where the Tooth Fairy is no benign figure, but a sinister legend that demands appeasement. When young Diogenes Pendergast loses a tooth, his older brother Aloysius, ever the skeptic, decides to test the legend's veracity. The consequences, as one might expect from a Pendergast tale, are dire and chilling.

Preston's prose is as evocative as ever, painting a vivid picture of New Orleans' sultry, decaying beauty and the eerie, otherworldly atmosphere that permeates the city's oldest neighborhoods. He masterfully builds tension, drawing readers in with his intricate plotting and expert pacing. The narrative is peppered with Preston's signature historical and cultural details, adding depth and authenticity to the story.

At the heart of "Dance of Death" lies the complex dynamic between the Pendergast brothers. Aloysius, the elder, is a man of logic and reason, yet he harbors a deep-seated fear of the supernatural, a fear that Preston explores with subtle psychological insight. Diogenes, on the other hand, is a child of imagination and faith, his innocence serving as a stark contrast to his brother's cynicism. Their relationship is a central theme, one that Preston develops with nuance and sensitivity.

The story also touches on the theme of belief versus disbelief, a common thread in the Pendergast series. Preston explores this theme through the lens of the Tooth Fairy legend, using it to challenge Aloysius' rational worldview and forcing him to confront his own fears.

"Dance of Death" is a gripping, atmospheric tale that serves as both a compelling standalone and an intriguing addition to the Pendergast series. Preston's masterful storytelling, combined with his ability to create complex, engaging characters, makes this short story a must-read for fans of the series and newcomers alike.
