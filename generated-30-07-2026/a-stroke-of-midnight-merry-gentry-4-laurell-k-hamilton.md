# A Stroke of Midnight (Merry Gentry, #4)

**Author:** Laurell K. Hamilton  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2005

A Stroke of Midnight, the fourth installment in Laurell K. Hamilton's Merry Gentry series, is a gripping and dark tale of power, deception, and the eternal struggle between good and evil. Hamilton masterfully weaves together the intricate world of faerie and the mundane reality of Los Angeles, creating a rich and immersive narrative that will leave readers breathless.

At the heart of the story is Merry Gentry, a private investigator with a secret: she's the heir to the dark throne of faerie. As Princess Meredith, she's caught between two worlds, navigating the treacherous landscape of faerie politics and the sinister forces that seek to destroy her. With a baffling double homicide to solve, Merry must confront the darker aspects of her own nature and the true extent of her powers.

Hamilton's writing style is, as always, a major draw. Her prose is lush and evocative, conjuring vivid images of the faerie realm and its inhabitants. Her characters are multidimensional and complex, with Merry being a particularly compelling protagonist. Her struggles to balance her human and faerie selves are deeply relatable, and her growth throughout the story is both believable and satisfying.

One of the standout features of A Stroke of Midnight is its exploration of the faerie world. Hamilton delves deep into the mythology and politics of this realm, creating a richly detailed and immersive environment that's both fascinating and terrifying. The faerie courts, with their intricate hierarchies and deadly power struggles, are a marvel of world-building, and the characters that inhabit them are equally well-crafted.

The romance in A Stroke of Midnight is also noteworthy. Merry's relationship with Mistral, Master of Storms, is a slow-burning fire that simmers throughout the story, adding an extra layer of tension to the already fraught narrative. Their passion is a catalyst for Merry's growth, awakening powers she never knew she possessed and drawing her deeper into the world of faerie.

If there's one criticism to be made, it's that the story can be overwhelming at times. The sheer number of characters and plot threads can be difficult to keep track of, and some readers may find themselves struggling to keep up. However, this is a minor quibble in an otherwise excellent book.

In conclusion, A Stroke of Midnight is a gripping and immersive tale that will appeal to fans of urban fantasy and romance alike. Hamilton's writing is, as always, top-notch, and her world-building is second to none. If you're looking for a compelling and dark fantasy series, the Merry Gentry books are an excellent choice.
