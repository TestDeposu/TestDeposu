# Five Children and It (The Psammead Trilogy, #1)

**Author:** E. Nesbit  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

In "Five Children and It", E. Nesbit masterfully weaves a timeless tale of adventure, curiosity, and the consequences of one's desires. This enchanting first installment of the Psammead trilogy follows the lives of five siblings who stumble upon a mystical sand-fairy while excavating a gravel pit on a scorching summer day. With a dash of whimsy and a pinch of magic, Nesbit effortlessly transports readers to a world where the boundaries between reality and fantasy blur, and the possibilities are endless.

As the children, Robert, Anthea, Cyril, Jane, and the baby, Baby Scrope, learn to navigate the complexities of this newfound world, they discover the true essence of the Psammead - an ancient and powerful creature capable of granting one wish per day. While the prospect of a single wish might seem deceptively simple, Nesbit astutely illustrates the perils of unchecked desires, revealing how even the most well-intentioned wishes can have unforeseen and often disastrous consequences. This masterful handling of the 'be careful what you wish for' theme not only captivates readers but also offers a poignant commentary on the human condition.

Through her signature blend of wit, insight, and poetic prose, Nesbit paints a rich tapestry of childhood experiences - from the carefree joys of exploration and imagination to the trials and tribulations of growing up. With an uncanny ability to tap into the complexities of childhood, Nesbit skillfully balances the idyllic with the mundane, creating a narrative that feels both nostalgic and timeless.

One of the most striking aspects of Nesbit's writing is her capacity to balance the surreal with the mundane. The children's wishes, ranging from the extravagant to the mundane, not only drive the plot forward but also offer a profound exploration of the human psyche. In a manner both poignant and humorous, Nesbit lays bare the insecurities, flaws, and contradictions that make us human, demonstrating that, even in the face of overwhelming magic, our deepest desires and fears remain our most potent adversaries.

The illustrations by H. R. Millar, newly scanned from the original edition, add an extra layer of charm to this beautifully crafted collection. The beautiful, delicate, yet whimsical illustrations complement Nesbit's writing in perfect harmony, transporting readers to a world of fantasy and wonder.

In conclusion, "Five Children and It" is a delightful and thought-provoking addition to the world of children's literature. E. Nesbit's masterful storytelling, combined with her ability to capture the essence of childhood, makes this book a timeless classic that will captivate readers of all ages. As Neil Gaiman, Jacqueline Wilson, and Kate Saunders attest, Nesbit's work continues to inspire and delight, making "Five Children and It" an essential read for anyone who has ever dreamed of a world where the boundaries between reality and fantasy blur.
