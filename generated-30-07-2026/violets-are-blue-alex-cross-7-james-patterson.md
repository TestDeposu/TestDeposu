# Violets Are Blue (Alex Cross, #7)

**Author:** James Patterson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2001

James Patterson’s 2001 thriller *Violets Are Blue* finds Washington, D.C. homicide detective and psychologist Alex Cross thrust into a nightmarish underworld where the line between macabre fantasy and horrifying reality blurs completely. As the seventh installment in Patterson’s powerhouse series, this novel leans heavily into the dark, sensationalist territory that has made the author a household name, delivering a fast-paced narrative that plays upon our deepest, most primal fears of the unknown and the depraved.

The central theme of *Violets Are Blue* revolves around the chilling allure of obsession and the monstrous lengths to which individuals will go when they abandon their humanity. The story kicks off with the gruesome murders of two joggers in San Francisco's Golden Gate Park. The crime scene bears all the grotesque hallmarks of ritualistic vampire killings—a hypothesis hastily dismissed by local authorities and the FBI as mere urban legend or copycat theatrics. However, Alex Cross recognizes the unmistakable scent of true evil and takes the case, a decision that drags him across the country from the foggy parks of California to the sultry streets of New Orleans, Savannah, Las Vegas, and Los Angeles. 

Patterson constructs a shadowy netherworld populated by secret clubs, dangerous role-players, and twisted posers who dabble in the occult and the macabre. The thematic core questions how easily ordinary people can slip across the threshold from harmless playacting into genuine, blood-soaked sadism. This exploration of subcultures obsessed with death and immortality serves as a striking backdrop for a much more personal battle. Cross is not merely hunting a disturbed serial killer; he is being stalked by his most terrifying nemesis yet—the elusive, merciless criminal known as the Mastermind. This psychological chess match forces Cross to confront his own deepest fears, shifting the narrative from a standard police procedural to an intense, visceral battle for survival that threatens everything and everyone he holds dear.

Patterson’s signature literary style is on full display here, engineered specifically to create an addictive, cinematic reading experience. Eschewing long, meandering descriptive passages, Patterson employs his trademark ultra-short chapters that act like rapid-fire pulse checks. This breathless pacing gives the book an undeniable momentum, propelling the reader forward with relentless cliffhangers at the end of nearly every page. It is a style designed for maximum engagement, turning what could be a dense, heavy psychological thriller into a high-octane page-turner that practically reads itself. 

While purists of literary suspense might occasionally crave deeper character introspection and more elaborate prose, Patterson compensates with sheer narrative velocity and atmospheric grit. He paints the various American cities Cross visits with broad, moody strokes, creating a kaleidoscopic tour of America's underbelly. The dialogue is punchy, the stakes are staggeringly high, and the psychological tension between Cross and the Mastermind provides a magnetic focal point that anchors the globe-trotting chaos.

Ultimately, *Violets Are Blue* succeeds by leaning into its own lurid premise. It is a blood-soaked, delightfully spooky, and relentlessly entertaining rollercoaster that showcases James Patterson at the height of his commercial thriller powers. For fans of the Alex Cross series and readers who relish dark, cat-and-mouse suspense grounded in the eerie fringes of human behavior, this 2001 entry remains a memorably chilling ride.
