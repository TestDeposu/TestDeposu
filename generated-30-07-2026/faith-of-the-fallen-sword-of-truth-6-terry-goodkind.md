# Faith of the Fallen (Sword of Truth, #6)

**Author:** Terry Goodkind  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

**Faith of the Fallen: A Masterclass in Epic Fantasy**

In the sprawling, intricate world of Terry Goodkind's Sword of Truth series, 'Faith of the Fallen' stands as a testament to the author's prowess in weaving complex narratives that keep readers on the edge of their seats. The sixth installment in the series, this book is a tour de force of epic fantasy, brimming with political intrigue, high-stakes action, and a profound exploration of faith and power.

Goodkind, known for his intricate world-building and morally complex characters, delivers yet again in 'Faith of the Fallen'. The story picks up where 'The Pillars of Creation' left off, with Emperor Jagang rising once again in the Old World, threatening to plunge the world into chaos. Richard Rahl, the First Wizard, and Kahlan Amnell, the Mother Confessor, must once again take up arms to protect their world, but this time, their paths diverge.

Richard, accompanied by the formidable Mord-Sith, Cara, ventures into the heart of the Old World to confront Jagang. Meanwhile, Kahlan, unwilling to heed an ancient prophecy, raises an army to quash a rebellion in the Midlands. This separation of the series' power couple allows Goodkind to explore their individual strengths and weaknesses, as well as the complex dynamics of their relationship.

Goodkind's style is as distinctive as ever, with his signature long, descriptive passages that paint vivid pictures of the world and its inhabitants. His characters are complex, flawed, and relatable, even as they navigate extraordinary circumstances. The dialogue is sharp and engaging, driving the plot forward with a sense of urgency that keeps the pages turning.

'Faith of the Fallen' also delves into themes of faith, prophecy, and the consequences of power. Goodkind explores the idea of faith as a force that can move mountains, but also as a double-edged sword that can lead to blind obedience or dangerous fanaticism. He challenges readers to question their own beliefs and the extent to which they would go to protect them.

The action sequences are as thrilling as ever, with Goodkind's signature blend of magic and martial arts. The battles are visceral and brutal, yet never gratuitous, serving to advance the plot and reveal character. The magic system, too, is intricate and well-thought-out, with a clear logic that grounds the fantastical elements in reality.

In 'Faith of the Fallen', Goodkind has crafted a story that is both a gripping adventure and a thought-provoking exploration of faith and power. It is a testament to his skill as a storyteller that, even after six books, he can still surprise and engage his readers. Fans of the series will not be disappointed, and new readers will find a rich, complex world to lose themselves in.
