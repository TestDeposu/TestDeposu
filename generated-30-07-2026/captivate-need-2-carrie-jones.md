# Captivate (Need, #2)

**Author:** Carrie Jones  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

**A Hero Emerges in the Chaos of 'Captivate'**

Carrie Jones weaves a poignant and captivating tale in her sophomore novel, 'Captivate', the sequel to the widely acclaimed 'Need'. In this powerful coming-of-age story, Jones expertly captures the tumultuous world of 14-year-old Liliana Faltin, as she navigates the intricate web of family secrets, toxic relationships, and her own struggles to find her place in the world.

Set against a backdrop of small-town America, where everyone knows each other's business, Faltin's life is a minefield of chaos. Her unreliable father, her backstabbing best friend, and her troubled older sister all contribute to a sense of disorder that Liliana struggles to cope with. But amidst the mess, a glimmer of hope emerges in the form of Paolo, a charming and genuine newcomer who ignites a spark in Liliana that she thought was long extinguished.

With a unique narrative voice that echoes the raw emotion and vulnerability of its protagonist, 'Captivate' delves into the complexities of adolescence with unflinching honesty. Jones masterfully explores themes of family dynamics, peer pressure, and the struggles of self-discovery, crafting a narrative that is both deeply relatable and deeply moving.

One of the standout features of 'Captivate' is Jones' lyrical prose, which brings to life the characters and their world with a poetic intensity that is nothing short of breathtaking. Her writing has been likened to that of Alice Sebold and Jodi Picoult, and it's easy to see why: Jones' sentences soar with a musicality that is simply captivating (pun intended).

But beneath the literary flourishes, 'Captivate' is a book that wears its heart on its sleeve. It's a story about the power of resilience, the importance of speaking truth to power, and the unwavering resolve of the human spirit. In a world where so many young people are struggling to find their voice, Jones offers a beacon of hope in the form of Faltin's determination to find her place in the world.

'Captivate' is not just a book for young adults; it's a book for anyone who has ever struggled to find their way in the world. It's a testament to the enduring power of love, family, and friendship, and a reminder that even in the darkest of times, there is always a glimmer of hope.

**Ultimately, 'Captivate' is a triumph of storytelling, a nuanced and deeply affecting exploration of the complexities of adolescence. If you're looking for a book that will leave you thinking long after the last page has been turned, then look no further. 'Captivate' is a must-read.**
