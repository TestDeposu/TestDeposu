# Fool's Assassin (The Fitz and the Fool, #1)

**Author:** Robin Hobb  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

Fool's Assassin, the latest installment in Robin Hobb's beloved Farseer saga, is a masterful tale of love, loss, and the unyielding power of the human spirit. This novel, the first in The Fitz and the Fool trilogy, marks a triumphant return to the world of the Six Duchies, where Hobb's imagination has soared for decades.

At the heart of the story is FitzChivalry Farseer, the enigmatic royal bastard and former king's assassin, who has left his life of intrigue behind. Masquerading as Tom Badgerlock, Fitz is now married to his childhood sweetheart, Molly, and leading the quiet life of a country squire. However, the appearance of menacing, pale-skinned strangers casts a sinister shadow over Fitz's past and his future, forcing him to once again take up his old life as an assassin.

Hobb's writing style is as captivating as ever, weaving a complex narrative that explores the intricacies of the human heart. Her prose is evocative and immersive, drawing the reader into the world of the Six Duchies with ease. The characters are richly drawn, with Fitz being one of the most compelling and nuanced protagonists in fantasy literature.

Throughout the novel, Hobb explores themes of identity, loyalty, and the power of relationships to shape us. Fitz's bond with the Fool, who has been missing for years, is a particularly poignant aspect of the story, and Hobb handles it with her characteristic sensitivity and depth.

One of the most striking aspects of Fool's Assassin is its thoughtful pacing. Hobb takes her time to develop the story, allowing the reader to become fully invested in the characters and their world. The result is a novel that feels both epic in scope and intimate in its characterizations.

The world-building in Fool's Assassin is also noteworthy, with Hobb expanding on the mythology and history of the Six Duchies. The novel is full of subtle details and nods to previous books in the series, making it a delight for fans of the Farseer saga.

In short, Fool's Assassin is a triumph, a novel that showcases Hobb's mastery of the fantasy genre. It is a story that will appeal to fans of character-driven fiction, complex world-building, and epic storytelling. If you're new to the Farseer saga, this is an excellent place to start, and if you're a returning reader, you'll find that Fool's Assassin is a worthy addition to the series.
