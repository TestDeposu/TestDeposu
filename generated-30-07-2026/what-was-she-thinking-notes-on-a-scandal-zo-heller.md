# What Was She Thinking? [Notes on a Scandal]

**Author:** Zoë Heller  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2006

In "Notes on a Scandal," Zoë Heller weaves a darkly comedic tale of obsession, morality, and the blurred lines between truth and lies. The novel's central theme explores the complexities of human relationships and the destructive power of unchecked emotions. At the heart of this gripping narrative is the complicated bond between Barbara Covett, a repressed schoolteacher, and Sheba Hart, a charismatic art teacher embroiled in a scandalous affair with an underage student.

Heller's masterful storytelling plunges readers into a world where nothing is as it seems. Barbara, with her meticulous diary entries, presents herself as an objective observer, but her own secrets and motivations are slowly unraveled as the story unfolds. Her fascination with Sheba's tumultuous life serves as a mirror, reflecting her own unfulfilled desires and the loneliness that has haunted her for years.

Through Barbara's eyes, Heller skewers the hypocrisy and superficiality of the upper-middle-class world she inhabits. The school's pretentious parents, the teachers' cliquey dynamics, and the suffocating social rules that govern their lives all come under withering inspection. Meanwhile, Sheba's infatuation with the student, Steve, is portrayed with disturbing relish, underscoring the dangers of unchecked passion and the ways in which relationships can deteriorate into toxic control.

What's striking about "Notes on a Scandal" is Heller's ability to balance satire with nuance. Her writing is razor-sharp, but not without a deep understanding of the characters' flaws and vulnerabilities. Barbara, in particular, is a riveting creation – a deeply flawed, yet relatable, protagonist whose inner turmoil is both heartbreaking and comical.

One of Heller's most impressive feats is her ability to keep the reader guessing. As the narrative hurtles towards its shocking climax, the lines between truth and fabrication become increasingly blurred. Barbara's diary entries, which initially seem like a reliable narrative device, begin to reveal their own biases and contradictions. The reader is left questioning everything, even the most seemingly objective accounts.

The novel's dark humor, too, is a testament to Heller's skill. Her wit is cutting, but never cruel, and she uses humor to deftly expose the absurdities of her characters' world. The result is a novel that's both a scathing critique of societal norms and a poignant exploration of the human condition.

Ultimately, "Notes on a Scandal" is a thought-provoking novel that will linger long after you finish reading it. Heller's exploration of the complexities of human relationships, the dangers of unchecked emotions, and the blurred lines between truth and lies will haunt you. With its razor-sharp writing, complex characters, and dark humor, this novel is a triumph of modern literature.
