# The Persian Boy (Alexander the Great, #2)

**Author:** Mary Renault  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

**Embracing the Passionate Soul of a Conqueror: A Review of "The Persian Boy" by Mary Renault**

Mary Renault's "The Persian Boy" is a mesmerizing historical novel that delves into the most tumultuous years of Alexander the Great's life through the eyes of his devoted lover, Bagoas. This captivating story not only provides an intimate glimpse into the Macedonian king's personal life but also masterfully weaves historical events and intricate relationships, leaving the reader enchanted and invested in the outcome.

As the second installment of the Novels of Alexander the Great trilogy, "The Persian Boy" is a sequel to "Fire from Heaven" and precedes "Funeral Games". The narrative begins with the arrival of Bagoas in Alexander's court, given to the king as a gift after his conquest of Persia. The young and vulnerable Bagoas captures Alexander's heart, and their relationship blossoms amidst the chaos of wars, wives, and treacherous plots against the king's life. 

Mary Renault's prose is breathtakingly rich, transporting readers to the ancient world with an eerie precision. Her ability to immerse readers in the culture, customs, and emotions of the past is unmatched. As reviewer Hilary Mantel aptly puts it, "Mary Renault is a shining light to both historical novelists and their readers." Through Renault's masterful storytelling, we enter the intricate world of the Macedonians, witnessing the complexities of royal politics, friendships, and romance, all the while being constantly reminded of the anachronisms that made ancient civilizations so fascinating.

One of the novel's greatest strengths is its nuanced portrayal of Alexander the Great, a man of contradictions - an unstoppable conqueror driven by ambition, yet haunted by his own vulnerability. Through Bagoas' eyes, we witness the king's intense passion, boundless energy, and fierce intelligence. Renault skillfully highlights the emotional undertones in the characters' relationships, crafting a world where politics, love, and war intertwine in a thrilling and captivating dance.

Moreover, "The Persian Boy" sheds light on a hitherto neglected aspect of history - the complexities of the relationships between kings and their courtiers, concubines, or lovers, particularly in the ancient world. By giving voice to Bagoas' experiences, Mary Renault skillfully humanizes these often- overlooked figures, allowing us to appreciate their agency, desires, and emotions. 

This masterful novel raises profound questions about leadership, loyalty, and love, all while maintaining the tension of Alexander's precarious reign. Ultimately, "The Persian Boy" unfolds as a riveting and poignant account of triumph and heartbreak that is both deeply engaging and unsettling in its depth.
