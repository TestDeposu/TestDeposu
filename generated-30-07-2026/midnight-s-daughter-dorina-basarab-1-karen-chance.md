# Midnight's Daughter (Dorina Basarab, #1)

**Author:** Karen Chance  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2008

Urban fantasy has long thrived on the edgy, the dangerous, and the supernatural, but few authors dive into the deep end of paranormal mayhem quite like Karen Chance. In her 2008 launchpad novel, Midnight’s Daughter, Chance introduces readers to a world where ancient mythologies collide with modern grit, establishing a high-octane aesthetic that would go on to define a generation of supernatural storytelling. This first installment in the Dorina Basarab series wastes no time pulling punches, offering a breathless, action-packed ride that explores the heavy burdens of bloodlines, sanity, and familial dysfunction on a legendary scale.

At the thematic heart of Midnight’s Daughter is the exploration of identity and heritage, channeled brilliantly through its protagonist, Dorina Basarab. As a dhampir—half-human and half-vampire—Dory exists in the agonizing in-between. Dhampirs in this universe are notoriously prone to losing their minds, descending into homicidal madness driven by their dual natures. Yet, Dory has fiercely clung to her sanity, a testament to her sheer willpower and internal fortitude. Chance uses this biological and psychological tightrope to examine a compelling central question: How much of who we are is dictated by our blood, and how much by our choices? Dory’s struggle against her innate predatory instincts mirrors the broader theme of breaking free from toxic generational curses. The fact that her family tree includes not just any vampires, but her enigmatic father and her psychotic Uncle Dracula himself, turns a personal identity crisis into a sprawling, terrifying saga of familial obligation. 

Chance’s writing style is nothing short of relentless. From the opening pages, the author establishes a breakneck pace that rarely lets up, prioritizing adrenaline, witty banter, and visceral combat. The prose is punchy, heavily stylized, and deeply entrenched in Dory’s cynical yet fiercely protective first-person perspective. Chance excels at world-building through chaos, dropping the reader into a society where supernatural politics are as deadly as physical combat. There is a distinct cinematic quality to her action sequences; they are choreographed with a frantic energy that keeps the pages turning well into the night. 

Moreover, Chance has a gift for revitalizing tired tropes. Taking figures of absolute horror, such as the historic Dracula, and weaving them into a complex contemporary narrative requires a deft hand. She strips away the romanticized veneer often found in urban fantasy of this era, leaning instead into the grotesque, the ruthless, and the terrifying aspects of immortality. Yet, she balances this darkness with a sharp, sarcastic humor that acts as a vital pressure valve against the sheer tension of the plot. 

The introduction of Louis-Cesare, a gorgeous and lethal master vampire forced into an uneasy alliance with Dory, allows Chance to play with romantic tension without sacrificing the plot’s primary momentum. Their dynamic crackles with friction, showcasing the author’s ability to craft compelling interpersonal relationships amidst the flying bullets and ancient curses. 

Midnight’s Daughter is a triumphant opening salvo for the Dorina Basarab series. Karen Chance delivers a masterclass in urban fantasy pacing, blending high-stakes supernatural horror with a fiercely independent, deeply flawed protagonist you cannot help but root for. For readers craving a paranormal adventure with serious bite, a razor-sharp wit, and a mythology turned delightfully on its head, Dory’s chaotic world is an absolute must-read.
