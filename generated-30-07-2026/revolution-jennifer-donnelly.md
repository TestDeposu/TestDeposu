# Revolution

**Author:** Jennifer Donnelly  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

Grief is a merciless architect. It strips away the familiar, dismantles the future, and leaves the survivor standing among the ruins of what used to be. In her critically acclaimed 2010 novel Revolution, award-winning author Jennifer Donnelly examines this devastating reality with unflinching honesty, weaving a masterclass in emotional resonance that bridges two centuries of human suffering and survival. 

The contemporary narrative anchors us in Brooklyn, where Andi Alpers is teetering on the absolute edge of self-destruction. Consumed by fury at her absent father, suffocated by her mother’s paralysis in the face of tragedy, and utterly shattered by the death of her younger brother Truman, Andi is a ticking time bomb. Expulsion from her elite private school looms, prompting an intervention that forces her out of her comfort zone and into the frozen streets of Paris for winter break. 

It is within the shadowed, melancholic beauty of the French capital that Donnelly’s narrative truly transcends the standard boundaries of young adult fiction. Wandering through the city’s labyrinthine history, Andi unearths the diary of Alexandrine Paradis, a young actress who lived more than two hundred years ago during the violent upheavals of the French Revolution. Alexandrine’s dreams of the Paris stage were brutally hijacked by a fateful, inescapable encounter with a doomed prince, plunging her into a tragic historical reality. 

As Andi immerses herself in these antique pages, the diary transforms from a distraction into a dangerous obsession. Donnelly brilliantly uses this dual-timeline structure not merely as a narrative device, but as a profound thematic mirror. Two girls, separated by centuries yet bound by an identical agony of loss, begin to converse across the barrier of time. When Andi embarks on a chilling midnight journey through the legendary catacombs of Paris, the past ceases to be ink on paper and erupts into a terrifying, breathing presence. 

Donnelly’s authorial style is nothing short of sumptuous. Her prose is rich in mood, character, and unflinching emotion, treating the reader to a meticulously constructed multi-layered narrative. She avoids the pitfalls of superficial melodrama, grounding both the modern Brooklyn high-stress environment and the bloody, chaotic backdrop of the French Revolution with astonishing historical detail and psychological weight. Every description feels deliberate; every emotional beat resonates with the raw, jagged edges of trauma and healing. 

What makes Revolution a triumph is its refusal to offer easy answers. It acknowledges that the struggles of the human heart are eternal—that the terror of losing someone we love, the blinding heat of rage, and the desperate search for agency are battles fought in every era. Donnelly crafts a haunting, beautifully told story that is as wise as it is cunningly worked. 

Laureled with multiple starred reviews and prestigious accolades—including being named an ABA Indies Choice Young Adult Book of the Year and an ALA-YALSA Top Ten Best Book—Revolution is an absorbing, unforgettable literary experience. It is a testament to the fact that while history may change its costume, the human heart remains forever unchanged.
