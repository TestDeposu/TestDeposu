# Written in Red (The Others, #1)

**Author:** Anne Bishop  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

Written in Red, the first installment in Anne Bishop's The Others series, is a captivating and immersive tale that delves into a world where supernatural creatures, including vampires and shape-shifters, reign supreme, preying on humans who are forced to live in fear. The story follows Meg Corbyn, a young and gifted blood prophet, known as a cassandra sangue, who possesses the ability to see the future when her skin is cut. This unique gift, however, comes with a steep price, as Meg is enslaved by her Controller, who exploits her visions for his own gain.

When Meg manages to escape, she finds solace in the Lakeside Courtyard, a business district operated by the Others, where she encounters shape-shifter Simon Wolfgard. Despite initial reservations, Simon hires Meg as the Human Liaison, and as their relationship deepens, he learns the truth about her past and the danger that follows her. As the story unfolds, Bishop masterfully weaves a complex web of intrigue, politics, and social hierarchy, raising essential questions about power, oppression, and what it means to be human.

One of the most striking aspects of Written in Red is Bishop's unique world-building, which is both fascinating and unsettling. The Others, with their distinct culture and rules, are multidimensional and relatable, making it easy to become invested in their lives and struggles. The author's vivid descriptions and meticulous attention to detail bring the world to life, immersing the reader in a reality that is both familiar and strange.

Bishop's writing style is characterized by her ability to craft compelling characters, each with their own distinct voice and personality. Meg, with her innocence and determination, is a relatable and endearing protagonist, while Simon, with his dry wit and hidden vulnerabilities, is a captivating and complex character. The supporting cast, including the enigmatic and deadly vampires, the mischievous and cunning shape-shifters, and the ruthless and cunning humans, adds depth and richness to the narrative.

The main theme of Written in Red is the exploration of power dynamics and the consequences of oppression. Bishop masterfully examines the relationships between humans and the Others, highlighting the tensions and conflicts that arise from their coexistence. Through Meg's experiences, the author sheds light on the darker aspects of human nature, including exploitation, violence, and prejudice. However, the novel also offers a message of hope and resilience, as Meg and the Others work together to build a more inclusive and equitable society.

Throughout the book, Bishop's prose is engaging, and her pacing is well-balanced, moving seamlessly between action, suspense, and quieter moments of character development. The author's use of language is evocative and immersive, making it easy to become fully engaged in the world and its inhabitants.

In conclusion, Written in Red is a gripping and thought-provoking novel that will appeal to fans of urban fantasy, paranormal romance, and science fiction. With its unique world-building, compelling characters, and timely themes, this book is a must-read for anyone looking for a captivating and immersive story. Anne Bishop's writing style is a perfect blend of action, suspense, and emotional depth, making Written in Red a memorable and haunting read.
