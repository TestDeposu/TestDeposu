# The Art of Avoiding Your Alpha (Wildwood, #2)

**Author:** Lola Glass  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Unknown

Lola Glass returns to the enchanting, slightly chaotic world of her supernatural suburbia with The Art of Avoiding Your Alpha, the second installment in the Wildwood series. While paranormal romance often relies on instant, soul-deep connections that defy logic, Glass takes a delightfully refreshing detour. Instead of leaning into the traditional tropes of breathless submission to fate, this novel centers on the art—and sheer exhaustion—of running away from the one person the universe insists is meant for you. 

At its core, the main theme of the book is the tension between predestined destiny and personal agency. In a genre saturated with alpha-male dominance and immediate surrender, Glass explores what happens when the heroine actively chooses rebellion. The narrative thrives on the delicious friction of avoidance. Rather than swooning at the first growl or submitting to biological imperatives, the protagonist treats her fated bond like an inconvenient tax audit or a persistent telemarketer. This dynamic injects much-needed humor and modern agency into a supernatural framework that can sometimes take itself too seriously. 

Glass’s authorial style is brisk, snappy, and unapologetically entertaining. She understands that readers of contemporary paranormal romance crave witty banter and palpable sexual tension, and she delivers both in spades. Her prose does not strive for high literary pretension; instead, it prioritizes pacing, emotional resonance, and laugh-out-loud situational comedy. The dialogue crackles with energy, turning every reluctant encounter between the main characters into a game of high-stakes verbal chess. Glass balances the lighter, comedic elements with genuine warmth, ensuring that the humor never undermines the underlying romance. 

What makes The Art of Avoiding Your Alpha stand out in the crowded Wildwood universe is its commitment to character growth through stubbornness. The protagonist is endurably flawed, wonderfully stubborn, and fiercely protective of her independence. When the alpha in question finally corners her, the payoff feels earned precisely because she made him work for every single inch of ground. 

Ultimately, Lola Glass has crafted a breezy, addictive, and deeply satisfying read that proves sometimes the best part of finding your true mate is watching how long you can successfully pretend you didn't notice them. Perfect for fans of lighthearted shifter romance and enemies-to-lovers dynamics, this book is a testament to the fact that love is great, but running away with your dignity intact is an art form.
