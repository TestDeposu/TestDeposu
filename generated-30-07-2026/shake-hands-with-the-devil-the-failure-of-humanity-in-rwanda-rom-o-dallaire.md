# Shake Hands with the Devil: The Failure of Humanity in Rwanda

**Author:** Roméo Dallaire  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Unknown

In "Shake Hands with the Devil: The Failure of Humanity in Rwanda," Roméo Dallaire presents a deeply personal and haunting account of the 1994 Rwandan genocide. As the Force Commander of the United Nations Assistance Mission for Rwanda (UNAMIR) during the pivotal year, Dallaire offers an unflinching examination of the international community's failure to intervene in the face of unimaginable atrocities.

Dallaire's narrative is both visceral and meticulously detailed, weaving together a tapestry of individual stories, bureaucratic inefficiencies, and the sheer scope of the tragedy. With a rare blend of military pragmatism and introspective sensitivity, the author confronts the darkest corners of human nature, forcing readers to confront their own capacity for compassion and action in the face of overwhelming suffering.

Throughout the book, Dallaire grapples with the existential questions that haunted him during those fateful months. How could the international community, having witnessed the Rwandan crisis unfold over months, fail to prevent or even mitigate the genocide? What does it say about our collective humanity when leaders, policymakers, and ordinary citizens alike seem willing to turn a blind eye to the suffering of others, especially in the absence of overt economic or strategic interests?

Dallaire's own testimony serves as a powerful counterpoint to the abstract theorizing and bureaucratic obfuscations that often accompany discussions of international crises. His experiences as a military commander, a humanitarian, and a witness to unspeakable horrors lends the narrative an authenticity and emotional resonance that will leave readers shaken.

Through "Shake Hands with the Devil," Dallaire issues a cri de coeur for greater accountability and awareness in the face of humanitarian crises worldwide. By refusing to shy away from the most difficult questions, the author shines a light on the darkest recesses of the human condition, illuminating the moral ambiguities that govern our collective responses to tragedy.

The book's climax, where Dallaire confronts the Rwandan government's refusal to accept UN forces beyond their initial deployment, is particularly searing, laying bare the complexities and frailties of international diplomacy. It is an unvarnished assessment of the international system's capacity to prevent and respond to catastrophes like the Rwandan genocide, stripped of the euphemisms and diplomatic niceties that often shroud such discussions.

Dallaire's prose, while sometimes clinical in its military descriptions, is always marked by a clear-eyed commitment to truth and a sense of moral outrage that propels the narrative forward. Whether describing the eerie stillness of a post-genocide landscape or the raw emotions of the survivors, "Shake Hands with the Devil" is a deeply moving and ultimately unsparing examination of humanity's capacity for destruction and its limits.

As we reflect on the lessons of Rwanda and confront the ongoing crises that plague our world, "Shake Hands with the Devil" stands as a compelling and essential read, one that will challenge readers to confront their own role in shaping a world where such atrocities can no longer occur.
