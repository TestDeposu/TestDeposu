# A Lick of Frost (Merry Gentry, #6)

**Author:** Laurell K. Hamilton  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

In A Lick of Frost, the sixth installment of Laurell K. Hamilton’s bestselling Merry Gentry series, the author once again plunges readers into a shimmering, visceral underworld where ancient mythologies collide with modern noir. Published in 2007, this chapter of Meredith Gentry’s saga wastes no time reminding its audience why Hamilton remains a titan of urban fantasy. The book weaves a heady tapestry of political backstabbing, erotic empowerment, and high-stakes survival, cementing the series’ distinct literary footprint.

At the heart of the novel lies the eternal struggle for autonomy within a patriarchal and deeply brutal society, establishing bodily autonomy and political sovereignty as the narrative's twin main themes. Meredith, juggling her roles as a former Los Angeles private investigator, a targeted faerie princess, and a reluctant heir, finds her womb treated as a geopolitical battleground. To secure the throne and save her own life from her sadistic aunt, Queen Andais, Meredith must conceive a child. Yet, Hamilton elevates this central premise beyond mere pulp convention. The fertility quest becomes a profound exploration of reclaiming female power, agency, and pleasure in the face of systemic oppression. Meredith’s body is heavily policed by both Unseelie royalty and Seelie enemies, yet she consistently refuses to be a passive vessel. Through her deep, protective bonds with her elite royal guards—her lovers and fierce defenders—she builds a sanctuary of mutual consent, fierce loyalty, and emotional intimacy that stands in stark contrast to the transactional cruelty of the faerie courts.

Hamilton’s stylistic signature is on full display here: a lush, unapologetically sensory prose style that marries gritty urban realism with baroque faerie opulence. Whether describing the neon-lit grit of Los Angeles or the suffocating, glittering malice of the sidhe courts, Hamilton employs a deeply atmospheric first-person perspective. Merry’s narrative voice is world-weary yet resilient, blending hard-boiled detective cynicism with an aching vulnerability. The action sequences are visceral and sharp, contrasting sharply with the lingering, almost hypnotic pacing of the romantic and political encounters. 

Furthermore, Hamilton excels at expanding her universe's mythos. The introduction of new legal and existential threats—such as the manipulative machinations of King Taranis using mortal laws to entrap Meredith’s guard—adds a thrilling, almost legal-thriller-esque tension to the supernatural proceedings. It grounds the fantastical politics in terrifyingly relatable power dynamics, proving that monsters in suits or crowns are far more dangerous than beasts in the shadows.

A Lick of Frost is a dazzling, darkly intoxicating read that caters perfectly to fans of mature urban fantasy. Hamilton does not shy away from the messy, complicated intersections of sex, power, and politics. Instead, she leans into them, delivering a striking critique of authority while serving up an endlessly entertaining supernatural drama. For those willing to brave the frosty depths of the faerie courts, Merry Gentry’s latest trial is an absolute must-read.
