# The Twelfth Card (Lincoln Rhyme, #6)

**Author:** Jeffery Deaver  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2005

"The Twelfth Card" by Jeffery Deaver is a gripping thriller that weaves a complex web of intrigue, seamlessly merging the past with the present. The sixth installment in the Lincoln Rhyme series, this novel delves into the dark underbelly of American history, masterfully exploring the theme of unresolved injustices and their far-reaching consequences.

At the heart of the narrative lies a young high school girl from Harlem, Geneva Settle, who unwittingly becomes the target of a ruthless assassin. As Geneva digs deeper into the life of her ancestor, a former slave, she unwittingly unearths secrets that have lain dormant for over 140 years. It is up to Lincoln Rhyme, a quadriplegic forensic expert, and his protégée, Amelia Sachs, to unravel the tangled threads of the past and prevent further bloodshed.

Deaver's writing is akin to a masterfully crafted puzzle, with each piece meticulously crafted to fit into a larger narrative. His labyrinthine plot twists and turns, expertly keeping the reader on the edge of their seat. The author's meticulous attention to historical detail adds depth and authenticity to the narrative, making it all the more believable and immersive.

One of the standout features of Deaver's writing is his ability to balance action and suspense with a richly nuanced exploration of character. Lincoln Rhyme, with his sharp wit and intellect, is a compelling protagonist, while Amelia Sachs provides a perfect foil to his analytical mind. The supporting cast is equally well-developed, with each character bringing their own unique perspective to the narrative.

The theme of unresolved injustices is a powerful one, and Deaver handles it with sensitivity and skill. Through the lens of Geneva's ancestor's story, the novel sheds light on the brutal realities of slavery and the long-lasting impact it has had on American society. The author's exploration of these themes is both thought-provoking and timely, adding a layer of depth to the narrative that elevates it beyond a simple thriller.

In conclusion, "The Twelfth Card" is a masterful thriller that will keep you on the edge of your seat. With its intricate plot, richly nuanced characters, and thought-provoking themes, it is a must-read for fans of the genre. Deaver's writing is akin to a symphony, with each element working in harmony to create a narrative that is both suspenseful and satisfying.
