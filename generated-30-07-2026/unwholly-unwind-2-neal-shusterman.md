# UnWholly (Unwind, #2)

**Author:** Neal Shusterman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

When Neal Shusterman published *UnWind* in 2007, he introduced readers to a chillingly plausible dystopian future where unwanted teenagers could be legally dismantled for organ harvesting. It was a concept so visceral and morally complex that it redefined modern young adult speculative fiction. Yet, in the 2012 sequel, *UnWholly*, Shusterman manages to achieve the near-impossible: he expands the scope of his universe without losing its intimate, psychological core. He shifts the narrative from a desperate fight for survival to a sprawling, ideological war, proving that the true horror of unwinding is not just the act itself, but the institutionalized apathy that allows it to thrive.

The central theme of *UnWholly* dives deeper into the commodification of human life. In the wake of the Happy Jack Harvest Camp revolt led by Connor, Risa, and Lev, society can no longer pretend it does not know what happens to its troubled youth. But instead of abolishing the practice, the corporate and political machinery behind it adapts. Unwinding is no longer just for inconvenient teenagers; it is poised for expansion, eyeing prisoners and the impoverished. Shusterman brilliantly mirrors our own world's darkest economic realities, illustrating how capitalism can industrialize empathy and ethics, turning human flesh into a traded commodity. 

Amidst this socio-political chess match, Shusterman introduces one of his most compelling characters: Cam. Composed entirely of harvested parts from different unwinds, Cam is a futuristic Frankenstein navigating the ultimate identity crisis. He is a boy who is technically everyone and no one, forced to grapple with the heavy, philosophical burden of whether a constructed being can possess a soul. Through Cam, the author explores the novel's secondary, yet equally profound theme: the definition of humanity. If your heartbeat belongs to a boy who loved music, your eyes to a girl who loved art, and your hands to an athlete, who are you truly? 

Shusterman’s writing style remains a masterclass in pacing and perspective. By utilizing multiple third-person point-of-view chapters, he grants the reader a panoramic view of a fractured world. We see the crumbling morality from the perspective of fugitive heroes fighting for reform, ruthless bounty hunters driven by profit, and indoctrinated youths drinking the Kool-Aid of the Pro-Choice (pro-unwind) movement. The prose is sharp, unflinching, and designed to provoke discomfort. Shusterman does not hand-feed his audience moral absolutes; instead, he drags them into the grey areas, forcing them to confront the terrifying logic of a society that values utility over sanctity.

*UnWholly* is not merely a companion piece or a bridge to a trilogy finale; it is a vital, high-stakes expansion of a world built on flesh and bone. It challenges our assumptions about where life begins and ends, questioning the price of progress and the elasticity of the human soul. Gripping, deeply unsettling, and fiercely intelligent, Shusterman’s sequel solidifies the UnWind dystopia as one of the defining speculative universes of the twenty-first century.
