# Please Look After Mom

**Author:** Kyung-Sook Shin  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

In "Please Look After Mom," Kyung-Sook Shin weaves a poignant and suspenseful tale that delves into the intricacies of family dynamics, the complexities of motherhood, and the fragility of human relationships. This National Bestseller and winner of the Man Asian Literary Prize is a masterful exploration of the secrets and sorrows that often remain unspoken within families, only to surface in times of crisis.

The story begins with the disappearance of sixty-nine-year-old So-nyo in a crowded Seoul subway station, an event that sets off a desperate search by her family. As the narrative unfolds, the voices of So-nyo's daughter, son, husband, and her own inner monologue offer a nuanced and multifaceted portrayal of a woman who, despite her unassuming presence, has lived a life marked by quiet struggles, sacrifices, and unfulfilled desires.

Shin's writing is characterized by a lyrical simplicity, a directness that belies the depth and richness of the emotions she conveys. Her prose is a gentle yet unflinching probing of the human heart, laying bare the contradictions, the unspoken resentments, and the unconditional love that define family relationships. Through So-nyo's story, Shin shines a light on the often-overlooked lives of mothers, those pillars of strength and selflessness who, in their quiet dedication, frequently sacrifice their own aspirations and desires.

The novel is also a powerful exploration of the complexities of motherhood, a role that is both deeply personal and universally relatable. So-nyo's character embodies the paradoxes of motherhood – the fierce love, the suffocating sacrifices, the quiet desperation, and the unspoken regrets. As the narrative peels back the layers of So-nyo's life, it reveals a woman who, despite her own flaws and weaknesses, has been the bedrock of her family, a testament to the enduring power of maternal love.

One of the most striking aspects of Shin's writing is her ability to balance the specificities of Korean culture and society with the universal themes that transcend geographical boundaries. The novel offers a fascinating glimpse into contemporary Korean life, but its exploration of family dynamics, the struggles of motherhood, and the fragility of human relationships is instantly recognizable and relatable.

In "Please Look After Mom," Shin has crafted a novel that is both a tribute to the mysteries of motherhood and a powerful exploration of the human condition. This is a book that will linger in the reader's mind long after the final page has been turned, its themes and characters haunting the imagination like a whispered secret. As Abraham Verghese so aptly puts it, this is a "terrific novel that stayed with me long after I'd finished its final, haunting pages."
