# Collide (Collide, #1)

**Author:** Gail McHugh  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

Torn Between the Safe Haven and the Dangerous Unknown: A Review of Gail McHugh’s Collide

In the crowded landscape of contemporary romance, few tropes endure quite like the classic love triangle. Yet, it takes a deft hand to elevate familiar narrative territory into something visceral and consuming. In her 2013 bestselling novel Collide, author Gail McHugh plunges readers headfirst into the tumultuous emotional landscape of grief, desire, and impossible choice. McHugh crafts a high-stakes romance that hinges not just on physical attraction, but on the terrifying vulnerability of a young woman trying to anchor herself in a world that has violently slipped out of her control.

At the thematic core of Collide is the agonizing friction between security and passion. Following the sudden death of her mother and her recent college graduation, protagonist Emily Cooper relocates to New York City to build a future with her longtime boyfriend, Dillon Parker. Dillon represents everything a grieving young woman should logically want: he is steadfast, comforting, predictable, and deeply familiar. Yet, McHugh uses Emily’s internal monologue to brilliant effect, illustrating how emotional trauma leaves a person simultaneously craving safety and desperately needing to feel alive again. 

Enter Gavin Blake. If Dillon is the calm harbor, Gavin is the tempest. Wealthy, darkly alluring, and unapologetically intense, Gavin crashes into Emily’s life with a magnetic force that defies rational explanation. McHugh’s primary thematic exploration takes shape in the battlefield of Emily’s psyche: a war between what her mind dictates as safe and what her body and instincts recognize as an inescapable pull. As the narrative unfolds, McHugh peels back the glossy exterior of both men. Dillon’s seemingly pristine facade begins to crack, revealing unsettling truths, while Gavin’s brooding exterior conceals a profound, pain-filled past that demands empathy rather than judgment. The central question of the novel quickly evolves from a superficial choice between two handsome suitors into a deeper psychological journey: How can a woman trust her own heart when the ground beneath her is constantly shifting?

McHugh’s authorial style is defined by its unrelenting pacing and sensory-rich prose. From the opening chapters, she establishes an atmosphere of palpable urgency. Her writing style leans heavily into the visceral experiences of her characters, utilizing short, punchy interactions and lingering descriptions of physical chemistry that amplify the tension. McHugh does not merely tell the reader that Emily is attracted to Gavin; she immerses the audience in the breathless, dizzying aftermath of their encounters, making the forbidden nature of their connection feel intensely real. 

Furthermore, the author excels at manufacturing suspense within the romance genre. Rather than relying solely on external obstacles, McHugh uses psychological tension to drive the plot forward. The pacing mirrors Emily’s internal chaos—frantic, emotional, and unpredictable. The New York City setting itself acts almost as a tertiary character, vibrant and overwhelming, mirroring the chaotic whirlwind of Emily’s new reality. McHugh’s dialogue is sharp and emotionally charged, driving the conflict relentlessly toward a cliffhanger that virtually demands readers immediately open the sequel.

Collide is unapologetically dramatic, leaning into the heightened emotions characteristic of New York Times bestselling new adult fiction. While purists of traditional romance might find the emotional turbulence exhausting, that very intensity is the book’s greatest strength. McHugh captures the dizzying disorientation of early adulthood, where grief, ambition, and lust collide to alter the trajectory of a life forever. 

For readers who revel in angsty love triangles, sizzling chemistry, and heroines pushed to their absolute emotional limits, Gail McHugh’s Collide is a compulsive, breathless read. It is a story that forces us to ask uncomfortable questions about the nature of love, loyalty, and the dangerous cost of following one's undeniable desires.
