# Revelation (Matthew Shardlake, #4)

**Author:** C.J. Sansom  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

**Revelation: A Masterclass in Tudor Intrigue**

C.J. Sansom's "Revelation" is not just a sequel, but a triumphant return to the world of Matthew Shardlake, the hunchbacked lawyer navigating the treacherous waters of Tudor England. The fourth installment in the series is a testament to Sansom's prowess as a historical novelist, weaving a complex tapestry of political intrigue, religious fervor, and personal drama that keeps readers on the edge of their seats.

At the heart of "Revelation" lies the theme of faith and its consequences, explored through the lens of the Book of Revelation. As Henry VIII's court buzzes with the king's pursuit of Catherine Parr, Shardlake finds himself entangled in a web of religious fanaticism and apocalyptic prophecies. The boy he defends, Nicholas Hobbey, is a religious zealot convinced that the end times are nigh, his delusions fueled by the enigmatic Book of Revelation. Sansom masterfully uses this religious fervor to create a sense of impending doom, mirroring the political and social upheaval of the Tudor era.

Sansom's style is as compelling as ever, his prose rich with historical detail yet accessible to modern readers. He has a knack for bringing the past to life, from the grimy streets of London to the opulent halls of the Tudor court. His characters are equally vivid, each with their own unique voice and motivation. Shardlake, now in his late forties, is a more world-weary figure than in previous novels, his physical ailments and moral dilemmas adding depth to his character.

The plot is a labyrinthine puzzle, with Shardlake's investigation into his friend's murder intersecting with his defense of Hobbey and his growing involvement in the court's intrigues. Sansom expertly juggles these threads, keeping the reader guessing until the very end. The pacing is excellent, building to a thrilling climax that resolves the various plotlines in a satisfying yet unexpected manner.

"Revelation" is not just a mystery novel; it's a historical drama, a character study, and a meditation on faith and its consequences. Sansom's ability to blend these elements into a compelling narrative is what makes him one of the finest historical novelists of our time. If you're a fan of Hilary Mantel, Margaret George, or Philippa Gregory, "Revelation" is a must-read.
