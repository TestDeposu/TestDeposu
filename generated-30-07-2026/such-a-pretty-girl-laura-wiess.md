# Such a Pretty Girl

**Author:** Laura Wiess  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

Laura Wiess’s 2007 young adult novel, Such a Pretty Girl, is not an easy book to read, but it is an unforgettable one. At its core, the novel tackles the darkest, most agonizing corners of domestic abuse and incest with an unblinking, raw intensity. Wiess does not exploit trauma for cheap emotional impact; instead, she examines the psychological aftermath of abuse with surgical precision. The narrative centers on fifteen-year-old Meredith, whose life is upended when her abusive father is released from prison years ahead of schedule. Promised nine years of safety, Meredith got only three, and the sudden shattering of that fragile illusion forms the ticking clock of the novel. 

The primary theme of Such a Pretty Girl is survival—not just physical survival, but the reclamation of a stolen identity. Wiess explores how abuse fractures a young mind, forcing a child to build intricate internal fortresses just to endure the unendurable. Meredith’s world is one of terrifying isolation, and the author brilliantly captures the claustrophobia of fear that leaves victims feeling entirely alone even in a crowded world. Yet, beneath the terror lies a fierce, desperate resilience. Meredith is not merely a passive victim waiting for rescue; she is a young woman fighting for her own agency, struggling to find her voice in a society that too often fails to listen to the children it is meant to protect. 

Wiess’s writing style is lyrical yet unflinching. She employs a haunting, poetic prose that elevates the subject matter, contrasting the grim reality of Meredith’s daily existence with the fierce beauty of her inner spirit. The pacing is relentless, mirroring the anxiety that grips the protagonist from the moment she learns of her father’s impending release. Wiess avoids melodrama, choosing instead to lean into the quiet, devastating truths of domestic trauma. The dialogue is sparse and authentic, and the emotional resonance is achieved through evocative imagery and deep psychological insight. Comparisons to literary titans like Harper Lee and J.D. Salinger, while lofty, are justified in the way Wiess crafts a young female protagonist who feels vividly, painfully real. 

Such a Pretty Girl is a heavy, emotionally taxing masterpiece that demands much of its reader. It requires empathy, patience, and the stomach to confront the ugliest aspects of human behavior. However, the reward for this emotional labor is a profound story of courage and redemption. Laura Wiess has crafted a novel that lingers in the mind long after the final page is turned, serving as a vital reminder of the strength it takes to face one's deepest demons and reclaim a stolen future.
