# Enchantment

**Author:** Orson Scott Card  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1999

In the realm of fantasy, few authors have mastered the art of reimagining timeless tales as skillfully as Orson Scott Card. His 1999 novel, Enchantment, is a testament to this prowess, as he weaves a captivating narrative that reinterprets the classic story of Sleeping Beauty. This thought-provoking tale delves into the complexities of love, destiny, and the human experience, making it an absolute must-read for fans of the genre.

At its core, Enchantment is a story about the transformative power of love and its ability to transcend the boundaries of time and culture. Card's protagonist, Ivan, is a complex and deeply human character, driven by a mix of curiosity, courage, and vulnerability. His journey from a frightened ten-year-old boy to a determined young adult is both poignant and inspiring, as he navigates the challenges of his past and the uncertainties of his future.

One of the most striking aspects of Enchantment is Card's masterful use of language and his ability to craft a rich, immersive world that draws the reader in from the very first page. His descriptions of the Carpathian forest and the ancient world that Ivan discovers are nothing short of enchanting, evoking a sense of wonder and magic that is all too rare in modern fantasy.

Card's writing style is characterized by a unique blend of lyricism and accessibility, making Enchantment a pleasure to read for audiences of all ages and backgrounds. His use of symbolism and metaphor adds depth and complexity to the narrative, inviting readers to ponder the deeper themes and meanings that underlie the story.

Throughout the book, Card explores a range of thought-provoking ideas, from the nature of love and destiny to the tensions between tradition and modernity. His portrayal of the clash between ancient cultures and the contemporary world is both nuanced and insightful, highlighting the challenges and opportunities that arise when different worlds collide.

If there is a weakness to Enchantment, it is the somewhat predictable nature of the plot. While Card's reimagining of Sleeping Beauty is undeniably original and engaging, the overall trajectory of the story is not particularly surprising. However, this is a minor quibble in an otherwise outstanding novel, and one that is more than offset by the richness and complexity of the characters, world-building, and themes.

In conclusion, Enchantment is a captivating and deeply satisfying novel that showcases Orson Scott Card's mastery of the fantasy genre. With its richly imagined world, compelling characters, and thought-provoking themes, this book is sure to delight readers of all ages. Whether you are a fan of classic fairy tales, epic fantasy, or simply great storytelling, Enchantment is an absolute must-read.
