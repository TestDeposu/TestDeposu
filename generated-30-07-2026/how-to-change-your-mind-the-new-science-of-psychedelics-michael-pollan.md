# How to Change Your Mind: The New Science of Psychedelics

**Author:** Michael Pollan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

**Title: A Mind-Bending Journey: Michael Pollan's "How to Change Your Mind"**

In "How to Change Your Mind," Michael Pollan embarks on a fascinating exploration of the burgeoning field of psychedelic therapy, delving into the science, history, and personal experiences that surround these controversial substances. With his signature clear-eyed and assured prose, Pollan navigates the complex landscape of the mind, offering readers a compelling blend of journalism, memoir, and scientific inquiry.

At the heart of Pollan's investigation lies a profound question: Can psychedelics help us understand and transform our consciousness, and in doing so, improve our lives? To answer this, he immerses himself in the latest research, speaking with scientists, therapists, and patients who have experienced the transformative power of psychedelics firsthand. From the hallowed halls of prestigious universities to the clandestine corners of the underground psychedelic community, Pollan's journey is as captivating as it is enlightening.

One of the book's most striking aspects is Pollan's willingness to participate in his own experiment. His personal experiences with LSD, psilocybin, and the little-known toad venom 5-MeO-DMT are recounted with candor and curiosity, offering readers a front-row seat to the often-mystical experiences these substances can evoke. These chapters are not only engaging but also deeply introspective, as Pollan grapples with the implications of his experiences on his understanding of self and reality.

Pollan's narrative is further enriched by his meticulous historical research. He deftly navigates the complex politics and cultural shifts that have shaped our relationship with psychedelics, from the counterculture revolution of the 1960s to the current resurgence of interest in their therapeutic potential. By contextualizing the present within the past, Pollan helps readers appreciate the magnitude of the shift occurring in our collective consciousness.

However, "How to Change Your Mind" is not without its criticisms. Some readers may find Pollan's personal experiences overshadow the scientific and historical aspects of the book. Moreover, the lack of a clear argument or thesis may leave some readers craving a more definitive stance on the subject. Nevertheless, these are minor quibbles in an otherwise outstanding work of participatory journalism.

In essence, "How to Change Your Mind" is a thought-provoking, engaging, and often profound exploration of the mind's vast potential. It is a testament to Pollan's skill as a writer and journalist, his ability to weave together complex ideas into a compelling narrative, and his courage to explore the unknown. This book is not just about psychedelics; it's about consciousness, connection, and the human search for meaning. It's about how we change our minds, both literally and metaphorically.
