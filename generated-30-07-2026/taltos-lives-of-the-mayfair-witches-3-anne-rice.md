# Taltos (Lives of the Mayfair Witches, #3)

**Author:** Anne Rice  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1994

**A Mesmerizing Journey into the Heart of the Mayfairs**

In the world of Gothic fiction, few authors weave as intricate and enchanting a tale as Anne Rice. Her Vampire Chronicles are a testament to her mastery of the genre, but it's in her Mayfair Witches series that she truly shines. With **Taltos**, the third installment of the series, Rice invites us on a wondrous journey into the heart of a New Orleans dynasty of witches, forever besieged by ghosts, spirits, and their own dizzying powers.

**Taltos** is a book that defies easy categorization. It's a curious blend of gothic, glamour fiction, alternate history, and high soap opera, as noted by **The Washington Post Book World**. Rice's writing is, as always, lyrical and evocative, transporting us back through the centuries to a civilization half-human, of wholly mysterious origin. This is a world at odds with mortality and immortality, justice and guilt, where the boundaries between good and evil are constantly blurred.

At the heart of the story is Ashlar, a Taltos who finds himself propelled into the midst of the Mayfair family's tangled web of secrets and power struggles. As he delves deeper into this mysterious world, he becomes increasingly entwined with the Mayfairs, whose heritage is inexorably linked to their own destiny as witches. Rice's skillful weaving of historical and supernatural elements creates a rich tapestry that's as fascinating as it is unsettling.

One of the hallmarks of Rice's writing is her ability to craft characters that are both complex and relatable. The Mayfairs, in particular, are a fascinating cast of characters, each with their own motivations and flaws. From the enigmatic Aurora to the tempestuous Deirdre, each character is carefully drawn to illustrate the intricate dynamics at play within this family.

Rice's style is, as always, sumptuous and engaging, conjuring up a sense of atmosphere that's as vivid as it is immersive. Her use of language is masterful, with a focus on descriptive passages that paint a picture of New Orleans as a city steeped in history, mystery, and ancient magic. This is a world where the past and present converge, where the boundaries between reality and fantasy are constantly blurred.

Throughout **Taltos**, Rice explores themes of power, identity, and the consequences of playing with forces beyond human control. The Mayfairs are a family torn apart by their own ambitions and desires, each one struggling to assert their dominance over the others. This is a story about the darker aspects of human nature, about the corrupting influence of power and the devastating consequences of unleashed passions.

In the end, **Taltos** is a book that will captivate fans of Rice's earlier work, while also attracting new readers who are drawn to the world of Gothic fiction. It's a testament to her skill as a storyteller that she can create such a rich and immersive world, one that continues to captivate us long after we finish reading.
