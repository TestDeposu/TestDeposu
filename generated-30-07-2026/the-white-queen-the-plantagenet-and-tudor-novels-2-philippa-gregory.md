# The White Queen (The Plantagenet and Tudor Novels, #2)

**Author:** Philippa Gregory  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

**A Crown of Ambition and Intrigue: Philippa Gregory's "The White Queen"**

Philippa Gregory, the master of Tudor-era fiction, weaves another spellbinding tale in "The White Queen," the second installment of her Plantagenet and Tudor Novels series. This time, she brings to life the captivating story of Elizabeth Woodville, a commoner who ascends to the throne of England and becomes a pivotal figure in the tumultuous Wars of the Roses.

At the heart of "The White Queen" lies the theme of ambition, a force that propels Elizabeth and her mother, Jacquetta, through the treacherous landscape of medieval politics. Elizabeth's beauty and cunning capture the heart of King Edward IV, and she seizes the opportunity to elevate her family's status, marrying him in secret and later becoming his queen. Jacquetta, with her mystical reputation and unwavering determination, serves as a driving force behind Elizabeth's rise, using her influence and alleged powers to secure their family's dominance.

Gregory's storytelling prowess shines in her ability to create complex, relatable characters amidst historical figures. Elizabeth is no passive pawn but a strong-willed woman navigating a man's world, making tough decisions to protect her family and maintain her power. Her relationship with her mother is a compelling dynamic, showcasing the intricate bonds between women navigating a patriarchal society.

Gregory's signature style is evident in her richly detailed historical backdrop and her knack for presenting historical events from a fresh perspective. She masterfully weaves fact and fiction, creating a narrative that feels both authentic and engaging. The Wars of the Roses, a complex series of conflicts, is made accessible through the personal stories of Elizabeth and her family, making history feel immediate and compelling.

However, "The White Queen" is not without its flaws. The pacing can feel uneven at times, with some sections bogged down by excessive detail, while others rush through significant events. Yet, these are minor quibbles in an otherwise engrossing read.

In conclusion, "The White Queen" is a captivating tale of ambition, power, and the lengths a mother and daughter will go to protect their family. Gregory's signature style and compelling characters make this a must-read for fans of historical fiction. It's a fascinating exploration of the lesser-known story of Elizabeth Woodville, a woman who dared to challenge the norms of her time and left an indelible mark on history.
