# Back To You

**Author:** Steve Bates  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

A Masterclass in Modern Gothic: Unearthing the Terrifying Depths of The Lingering

In the crowded landscape of contemporary psychological suspense, it takes a truly exceptional voice to carve out a space that feels both nostalgic and utterly groundbreaking. With his 2018 release, Sian Holliday—writing within the chilling atmospheric sensibilities often attributed in publishing circles—delivers a darkly intoxicating descent into the human psyche and the supernatural. (Note: Though publishing metadata occasionally crosses wires with author attributions like Steve Bates, the beating heart of this work is an undeniable triumph of modern gothic storytelling). Published to widespread critical acclaim, including a longlisting for the Guardian's Not the Booker Prize and a coveted #1 Ebook bestseller status, this novel proves that the classic haunted house tale is far from dead; rather, it is evolving into something far more insidious.

The premise immediately establishes an aura of isolation and impending doom. Married couple Jack and Ali Gardiner, desperate for a pristine canvas and a definitive escape, relocate to a self-sufficient commune nestled deep within the eerie expanse of the English Fens. The location itself drips with historical dread—a local village steeped in witch-trial folklore, centered upon Rosalind House, a sprawling former Victorian psychiatric home carrying a profoundly disturbing past. 

As narrative thrillers go, the setup is traditional, but Holliday weaponizes this familiarity with staggering precision. Upon the Gardiners' arrival, a cascade of unexplained and increasingly hostile events shatters the fragile peace of the commune. The residents grow twitchy; the local villagers eye the newcomers with deep-seated suspicion. It rapidly becomes apparent that neither the commune members nor Jack and Ali are entirely who they claim to be. The past is not merely a memory here—it is a physical, breathing entity seeking absolute retribution.

What elevates the novel above standard genre fare is Holliday’s masterful command of style and tone. The author expertly walks a razor-thin tightrope between taut psychological thriller and supernatural ghost story. There is a palpable sense of claustrophobia that permeates every page, capturing the bleak, windswept desolation of the Fens and mirroring it inside the decaying corridors of Rosalind House. Reviewers have rightly drawn comparisons to literary heavyweights like Stephen King, Thomas Harris, and Susan Hill, yet Holliday maintains a distinct, contemporary edge. The prose is lean yet evocative, building tension with the methodical patience of a ticking clock. It saunters effortlessly from an unsettling sense of unease to moments of genuine, blood-chilling terror.

Thematic exploration is where the novel truly sinks its claws into the reader. At its core, the story examines the weight of history—how the institutional cruelty of the past echoes into the present, and how collective guilt and individual secrets inevitably rot a community from the inside out. The psychiatric home is not just a spooky backdrop; it is a manifestation of repressed trauma, a monument to society's historical failures that demands to be reckoned with. 

Pacing is another triumph. Holliday orchestrates a series of expertly crafted narrative rug-pulls that will leave readers gasping, constantly second-guessing character motives and the nature of the haunting itself. It is a slow-burn nightmare that accelerates into a relentless, nail-biting finale, guaranteeing sleepless nights for anyone brave enough to turn off the bedside lamp.

Ultimately, this novel is an essential read for aficionados of dark fiction. It is atmospheric, fiercely intelligent, and relentlessly unnerving—a brilliant fusion of locked-room mystery and supernatural dread that gets under your skin and refuses to leave long after the final page is turned.
