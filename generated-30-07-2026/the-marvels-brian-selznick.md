# The Marvels

**Author:** Brian Selznick  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

In the realm of children's literature, few authors have demonstrated the mastery of storytelling and artistic innovation as Brian Selznick does in "The Marvels." This 2015 release is part of a thematic trilogy that includes "The Invention of Hugo Cabret" and "Wonderstruck," solidifying Selznick's reputation as a Caldecott Medalist and a creative force to be reckoned with.

The Marvels is a sweeping tale of two interconnected stories that span over a century, defying conventional narrative structures. The first part unfolds in nearly 400 pages of continuous pictures, transporting readers to the high seas of 1766, where young Billy Marvel survives a shipwreck and finds employment in a London theatre. His family's journey as brilliant actors is expertly interwoven, forming a foundation for the generations to come.

The story then leaps forward in time to 1900, when the talented and proud Leontes Marvel is cast out of the theatrical world. His expulsion sets the stage for a century-long journey of discovery and self-discovery. Meanwhile, in the second narrative thread, runaway teenager Joseph Jervis finds refuge with an enigmatic uncle in London. The mysterious Albert Nightingale's mansion, with its otherworldly portraits and ghostly presences, bewitches Joseph, prompting him to unravel the secrets of his family's past.

Selznick's storytelling prowess lies in his ability to craft two standalone narratives that blend and converge in a way that is both surprising and, upon reflection, inevitable. This masterful manipulation of time and space invites readers to piece together the enigmatic connections between the two stories, much like a puzzle. Through a rich tapestry of words and captivating images, Selznick leads the reader on a thrilling adventure that defies the boundaries of traditional storytelling.

One of the most striking aspects of "The Marvels" is Selznick's innovative use of art and text. The book's design is nothing short of breathtaking, with beautifully rendered illustrations that transport the reader to another era. The way in which the author seamlessly integrates text and image will leave readers awestruck, particularly in the first narrative thread, which is presented in a single, continuous sequence of images.

Throughout "The Marvels," Selznick celebrates the transformative power of storytelling. His love for the art form shines through in every page, every illustration, and every word. He masterfully weaves a tapestry of themes that explore the complexities of identity, family, and the human experience.

In "The Marvels," Selznick has created a true masterpiece of children's literature that will leave readers of all ages in awe. This is a story that will be remembered for generations to come – a testament to the enduring power of imagination and the magic of storytelling.
