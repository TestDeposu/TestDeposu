# The Darkness That Comes Before (The Prince of Nothing, #1)

**Author:** R. Scott Bakker  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2008

In the realm of epic fantasy, few books dare to tread the uncharted territories of the human condition with the same audacity as R. Scott Bakker's "The Darkness That Comes Before". The first installment in the Prince of Nothing series, this dense and thought-provoking novel defies the conventions of the genre, presenting a rich tapestry of complex characters, intricate world-building, and philosophical themes that will leave readers questioning the very fabric of reality.

Set against the backdrop of a catastrophic event known as the "Apocalypse," the story unfolds in the world of Eärwa, a realm where the veil between the sacred and the profane is thin. The narrative follows the lives of two men and two women as they become embroiled in a complex web of intrigue when they cross paths with Anasûrimbor Kellhus, a charismatic enigma with seemingly supernatural abilities. As Kellhus navigates the treacherous landscape of politics and war, the reader is drawn into a world where the lines between truth, power, and redemption are constantly shifting.

One of the most striking aspects of "The Darkness That Comes Before" is Bakker's unique writing style, which combines elements of classical philosophy with a visceral, modernist sensibility. His prose is a joy to behold, with sentences that meander like a labyrinthine narrative, only to reveal a deeper truth on the next page. The author's mastery of language is matched only by his willingness to challenge the reader, pushing the boundaries of convention and subverting expectations at every turn.

Bakker's Eärwa is a world in the throes of transformation, scarred by a history of catastrophic events that have left its inhabitants with a profound sense of trauma and desperation. As the narrative hurtles towards its conclusion, the stakes are raised, and the reader is forced to confront the darkest aspects of human nature. In this sense, "The Darkness That Comes Before" is a work of profound psychological complexity, a slow-burning fire that creeps beneath the skin, probing the depths of the human condition with surgical precision.

If Bakker's intentions are to challenge the reader, he succeeds in spades. The narrative is a dizzying whirlwind of ideas, concepts, and philosophical allusions that will leave even the most intrepid scholar of fantasy in its wake. From the echoes of Plato's cave to the existential despair of Camus' "Absurd," Bakker draws upon a vast reservoir of knowledge, weaving a tapestry that is as breathtaking as it is disorienting.

And yet, despite its formidable intellectual ambition, "The Darkness That Comes Before" remains a novel that is as accessible as it is challenging. The characters are multidimensional and relatable, their struggles and motivations a testament to the enduring power of human emotion. Kellhus, in particular, is a fascinating figure, a creature of contradictions and paradox who embodies the enigmatic presence of the unknown.

In the end, "The Darkness That Comes Before" is a work of stunning audacity, a fantasy epic that defies genre conventions and pushes the boundaries of what we expect from a story. R. Scott Bakker's unique vision, coupled with his mastery of language and his willingness to confront the reader with the darkest aspects of human nature, make this novel a triumph of modern fantasy literature. Readers who are willing to embark on this journey will be rewarded with a world that is both familiar and strange, a universe of breathtaking beauty and terrible consequences.
