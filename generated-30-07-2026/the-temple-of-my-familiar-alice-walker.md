# The Temple of My Familiar

**Author:** Alice Walker  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

The Temple of My Familiar, written by the illustrious Alice Walker, is a poignant and evocative exploration of the African experience in America. This novel, though published in 1990 and re-released in 2011, is a timeless masterpiece that masterfully interweaves the lives of dozens of characters, each grappling with the brutal legacy of their ancestors. Walker's writing is akin to a rich tapestry, intricately woven from the threads of old fables, history, and mythology, creating a profoundly spiritual explanation for centuries of shared African American experience.

At its core, The Temple of My Familiar is a powerful exploration of identity, community, and the unbreakable bonds that tie us to our heritage. Walker's characters, though diverse in their experiences and backgrounds, are all connected by the shared thread of their African ancestry. From the recent African immigrants struggling to find their place in a new world, to the woman who grew up in the mixed-race rainforest communities of South America, each character must come to terms with the troubled lives of their ancestors in order to find peace and understanding in their own.

One of the most striking aspects of Walker's writing is her ability to craft a sense of continuity and connection between her characters. Celie and Shug, the beloved protagonists of The Color Purple, subtly shadow the lives of the characters in The Temple of My Familiar, serving as a reminder of the enduring power of love, family, and community. Walker's use of magical realism adds a layer of depth and complexity to the narrative, blurring the lines between past and present, myth and reality.

Throughout the novel, Walker's prose is as vivid and evocative as it is precise and economical. Her writing is infused with a sense of wonder, curiosity, and deep empathy for her characters, making it impossible not to become fully invested in their lives. The Temple of My Familiar is a novel that will appeal to readers who appreciate lyrical prose, complex characters, and a narrative that is both deeply personal and universally relatable.

The inclusion of a new letter written by the author adds a touching and intimate element to the novel, providing readers with a glimpse into Walker's own thoughts and reflections on the writing process. The illustrated biography of Alice Walker, featuring rare photos from the author's personal collection, is a lovely bonus that adds to the overall sense of depth and context.

In short, The Temple of My Familiar is a masterpiece of contemporary literature, a novel that will resonate with readers long after the final page has been turned. It is a testament to the power of storytelling, the importance of understanding and honoring our heritage, and the boundless potential of the human spirit.
