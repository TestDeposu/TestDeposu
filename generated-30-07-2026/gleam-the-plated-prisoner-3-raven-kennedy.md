# Gleam (The Plated Prisoner, #3)

**Author:** Raven Kennedy  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2026

**Gleam: A Mesmerizing Dance with Darkness**

In the vein of her previous smash hit, "The Plated Prisoner," Raven Kennedy returns with "Gleam," the first installment in a captivating romantasy duology that weaves together the legend of the Pied Piper with a unique, dark fantasy twist. Kennedy's signature style—richly immersive world-building, intricate plot twists, and a tantalizing slow-burn romance—once again takes center stage, drawing readers into a mesmerizing dance with the unknown.

At the heart of "Gleam" lies a compelling exploration of identity, power, and the consequences of our actions. Our protagonist, a half-Bane, half-fae outcast, grapples with her dual nature in a world that shuns her, forcing her to navigate a treacherous landscape filled with both physical and emotional perils. Kennedy masterfully crafts a protagonist who is equal parts vulnerable and resilient, making her an instantly relatable heroine despite her extraordinary circumstances.

The arrival of the enigmatic Pied Piper, a figure shrouded in mystery and danger, adds an electrifying layer to the narrative. His motives are unclear, his methods questionable, yet his magnetism undeniable. Kennedy's exploration of the captor-captive dynamic is as thought-provoking as it is enthralling, challenging readers to question their own perceptions of power and control.

Kennedy's prose is as lush and evocative as ever, painting vivid images of the post-apocalyptic world she's created. The fae realm, with its otherworldly beauty and sinister undertones, is a character unto itself, providing a stark contrast to the grimy, plague-ridden human cities. Yet, it's Kennedy's ability to infuse her world with a sense of history and mythology that truly sets "Gleam" apart. The legend of the Pied Piper is seamlessly woven into the fabric of the story, adding depth and resonance to the plot.

The romance, as promised, is slow-burning and intense, a dance of wills between two damaged souls. Kennedy excels at creating chemistry that sizzles off the page, and the banter between the protagonist and the Piper is as sharp and witty as it is charged with sexual tension. Yet, it's the emotional connection that Kennedy forges between them that truly resonates, making their journey one that readers will be eager to follow.

"Gleam" is not without its challenges. The world-building can be dense at times, and the pacing may feel slow to those unaccustomed to Kennedy's style. However, for readers who appreciate intricate plotting and richly drawn characters, "Gleam" is a feast for the senses.

In conclusion, "Gleam" is a captivating addition to Raven Kennedy's oeuvre, a dark fantasy that explores the complexities of identity, power, and love. With its richly drawn world, intricate plotting, and compelling characters, "Gleam" is a must-read for fans of Kennedy's previous works and a promising start to a new series.
