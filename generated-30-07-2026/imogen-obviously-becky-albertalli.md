# Imogen, Obviously

**Author:** Becky Albertalli  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

In Imogen, Obviously, bestselling author Becky Albertalli returns to the sunlit, emotionally articulate landscape of modern adolescence, delivering a novel that is as timely as it is tender. Known for her signature warmth, humor, and deep empathy for the queer teenage experience—cemented by her beloved debut, Simon vs. the Homo Sapiens Agenda—Albertalli once again proves why she is a master of the contemporary YA genre. Published in 2023, this Stonewall Honor Book is a bighearted, deeply vulnerable dive into self-discovery that tackles the often messy, beautiful gray areas of sexuality and identity.

At the center of the story is Imogen Scott, a self-proclaimed "hopelessly heterosexual" ally who wears her status like a badge of honor. She is fluent in queer media discourse, fiercely supportive, and practically a fixture in her high school’s Pride Alliance. Armed with two queer best friends—the hyper-vigilant Gretchen and college-freshman Lili—Imogen fancies herself the ultimate champion of the community. But allyship takes a complicated turn when Imogen visits Lili on campus and discovers a harmless little white lie has snowballed: Lili has told her new college friends that she and Imogen used to date. 

To protect Lili’s social standing and avoid an awkward explanation, Imogen plays along, leaning into a borrowed queer history. But as she integrates herself into Lili’s vibrant friend group, she is drawn into the orbit of Tessa—a chaotic, freckle-faced, and irresistibly magnetic college student. Suddenly, the lines between performance and reality begin to blur. As Imogen spends more time with Tessa, she is forced to confront a dizzying realization: what if her fiercely defended heterosexuality was never quite as straight as she thought?

Albertalli’s greatest strength as an author lies in her distinct style, which balances breezy, banter-heavy dialogue with profound emotional intelligence. She captures the authentic cadence of Gen-Z speech without ever sounding dated or out-of-touch. The prose crackles with pop culture references, witty text exchanges, and high school anxieties, yet it never loses sight of the emotional gravity beneath the surface. Albertalli treats her characters with immense tenderness, ensuring they feel like real teenagers—flawed, well-meaning, and desperately trying to figure out who they are in a world that constantly demands labels.

The main theme of Imogen, Obviously is the deconstruction of the rigid boxes we build for ourselves and the grace required to step outside of them. Albertalli brilliantly explores the nuances of the queer spectrum, focusing heavily on comp-het (compulsory heterosexuality) and the unique, often invisible pressure of the "straight ally" identity. Imogen’s journey highlights how terrifying it can be to unlearn what you thought you knew about yourself, especially when you have spent years publicly advocating for a community you secretly felt you didn't have the right to belong to. The book brilliantly dismantles the myth that self-discovery has a strict timeline or that questioning your identity later in life invalidates your past. 

Ultimately, Imogen, Obviously is a joyous, comforting, and deeply reassuring read. It is a love letter to the awkwardness of growing up, the beauty of fluid identities, and the transformative power of allowing yourself to tell the truth—even when it scares you. Albertalli has crafted a contemporary classic that will resonate deeply not just with teenagers navigating their own awakenings, but with anyone who has ever had to rewrite their own story.
