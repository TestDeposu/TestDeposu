# The Power Broker: Robert Moses and the Fall of New York

**Author:** Robert A. Caro  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1974

**The Power Broker: A Colossal Portrait of Unchecked Power**

Robert A. Caro's "The Power Broker" is not just a biography; it's a seismic exploration of power, ambition, and the shaping of a city. Published in 1974, this Pulitzer Prize-winning tome is a modern American classic that reveals the astonishing story of Robert Moses, the man who, for over four decades, held New York in the palm of his hand.

Caro, with his meticulous research and masterful storytelling, unravels the enigma of Moses, a man who, denied power through democratic means, created his own empire outside the political framework. We watch as Moses, an idealistic young heir, transforms into a power-hungry titan, his vision for New York evolving from a "miraculous flowering" of parks and parkways to the smog-choked sprawl we know today.

At the heart of "The Power Broker" lies Caro's unparalleled access to Moses' personal and professional records, which he uses to paint a multidimensional portrait of a man who was both a visionary and a tyrant. We see Moses' brilliance in creating public authorities that operated beyond public scrutiny, his ruthlessness in using dossiers to silence opponents, and his insatiable appetite for power, which ultimately led to his downfall.

Caro's style is immersive and engaging, despite the book's length. He weaves political intrigue, personal drama, and architectural history into a narrative that reads like a political thriller. He masterfully connects Moses' story to national figures like Franklin D. Roosevelt and Nelson Rockefeller, providing a fascinating lens through which to view twentieth-century American politics.

"The Power Broker" is not just a book about Robert Moses; it's a cautionary tale about the dangers of unchecked power. It's a testament to Caro's skill that he can make a 1,200-page book about a man who held power for decades feel like a gripping page-turner.
