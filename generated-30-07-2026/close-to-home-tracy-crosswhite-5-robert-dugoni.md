# Close to Home (Tracy Crosswhite, #5)

**Author:** Robert Dugoni  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

In "Close to Home" by Robert Dugoni, a gripping and intense legal thriller, attorney David Sloane is drawn into a complex web of intrigue and deception when a widow, Beverly Ford, asks for his help in suing the United States government and military for the mysterious death of her husband, James, a national guardsman killed in Iraq. As Sloane delves deeper into the case, he finds himself entangled in a decades-old military doctrine that seems impossible to overcome.

Dugoni masterfully weaves a narrative that not only explores the intricacies of the military justice system but also delves into the personal costs of seeking truth and justice. Sloane, a former soldier himself, is driven by a strong sense of duty and loyalty, which makes him an endearing and relatable protagonist. His willingness to take on the government and military, despite the risks, is a testament to his unwavering commitment to justice.

The author's style is characterized by his ability to craft a story that is both suspenseful and thought-provoking. The pacing is well-balanced, with a mix of action, drama, and intrigue that keeps the reader engaged. Dugoni's writing is concise and evocative, making it easy to visualize the characters and settings. The dialogue is natural and believable, adding to the overall sense of realism.

One of the strengths of the book is its ability to tackle complex themes and issues in a way that is both informative and entertaining. Dugoni sheds light on the often-murky world of military justice, highlighting the challenges faced by those seeking truth and accountability. The book also explores the personal costs of war, not just for the soldiers but also for their families and loved ones.

The supporting cast of characters is equally well-developed, with each one bringing their own unique perspective to the story. Charles Jenkins, a reclusive former CIA agent turned private investigator, is a particularly intriguing character, whose skills and expertise prove invaluable to Sloane's case. The antagonist, while not explicitly stated, is a formidable presence throughout the book, adding an air of tension and suspense to the narrative.

As the story unfolds, Sloane's family becomes increasingly entangled in the case, putting them in grave danger. This adds an element of personal stakes to the narrative, making Sloane's quest for justice even more urgent and compelling.

Overall, "Close to Home" is a gripping and thought-provoking thriller that will keep readers on the edge of their seats. Dugoni's masterful storytelling and well-developed characters make for a compelling narrative that is both informative and entertaining. If you're a fan of legal thrillers, military fiction, or just great storytelling in general, this book is a must-read.
