# Heartless Hunter (The Crimson Moth, #1)

**Author:** Kristen Ciccarelli  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2024

Dangerous Games and Deadly Stakes: A Review of Kristen Ciccarelli’s Heartless Hunter

In the landscape of modern romantic fantasy, few tropes endure quite like the enemies-to-lovers dynamic. Yet, executing this well-worn path with genuine tension, high-stakes plotting, and magnetic chemistry is a rare feat. Enter Kristen Ciccarelli’s Heartless Hunter, the intoxicating first installment in The Crimson Moth duology. Ciccarelli takes the classic cat-and-mouse game between hunter and hunted and breathes vibrant, bloody life into it, delivering a gripping tale of deception, revolution, and the perilous cost of desire.

At the heart of the novel is Rune Winters, a character navigating a masterclass in dual identities. By day, she glides through the glittering, superficial salons of the republic as a vapid socialite, a persona designed to deflect suspicion. By night, she transforms into the Crimson Moth, a daring vigilante dedicated to rescuing her fellow witches from execution in the wake of a brutal revolution. Rune’s internal conflict—torn between the safety of obscurity and the burning obligation to save her kind—drives the narrative's propulsive momentum. 

Enter Gideon Sharpe, a notorious, unforgiving witch hunter whose loyalty to the new regime is matched only by his formidable intellect. Gideon despises the decadence Rune represents, yet when he discovers her merchant ships are being used to smuggle renegade witches, he initiates a counter-plot. He begins courting her to extract information, setting the stage for a deliciously complex romance built entirely on mutual deception. Both protagonists are playing roles, yet the brilliance of Ciccarelli’s style lies in how the layers of armor begin to strip away, revealing genuine compatibility and fierce mutual respect beneath the lies.

Ciccarelli’s writing style is both sumptuous and sharp. She paints the world with a vivid, atmospheric brush, contrasting the opulent, powder-dusted decadence of high society with the gritty, desperate underworld of hunted outcasts. The pacing is relentless. The author masterfully maintains a tight, breathless rhythm, ensuring that every stolen glance, sharp piece of dialogue, and near-miss heightens the romantic and physical danger. The tension does not merely simmer; it boils, anchored by witty banter and the constant, terrifying threat of exposure.

The central theme of Heartless Hunter explores the duality of human (and magical) nature. It questions what we must hide to survive versus what we dare to reveal for love. Rune and Gideon are reflections of one another on opposite sides of a bloody ideological divide. Ciccarelli uses their forced proximity to interrogate justice, prejudice, and the collateral damage of revolution. Gideon is not a mustache-twirling villain, nor is Rune a flawless hero; both are deeply flawed, shaped by trauma and duty, which makes their inevitable collision all the more compelling.

As the first book in a duology, Heartless Hunter strikes the difficult balance of delivering a satisfying narrative arc while leaving the reader desperate for what comes next. It is a story that feels both comfortingly familiar to fans of romantic fantasy and thrillingly fresh in its execution. Ciccarelli has crafted a world where a ballroom dance can be as lethal as a drawn blade, and where falling in love is the most dangerous act of rebellion of all. For readers seeking high-stakes magic, simmering chemistry, and a heroine who is as tender as she is fierce, this novel is an absolute must-read.
