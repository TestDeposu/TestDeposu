# Airman

**Author:** Eoin Colfer  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Eoin Colfer is universally celebrated for his ability to inject breakneck momentum and irrepressible wit into speculative fiction, most notably through his blockbuster Artemis Fowl series. Yet, with his 2009 standalone novel Airman, Colfer pivots from whimsical fantasy to a grittier, historically grounded realm of invention and survival. The result is a breathless, deeply affecting triumph that captures the intoxicating romance of early aviation while anchoring its narrative in the brutal realities of late-nineteenth-century politics.

Set in the 1890s on the fictional, sovereign Saltee Islands off the rugged Irish coast, the novel introduces us to Conor Broekhart. Conor is a boy quite literally born to fly, his arrival into the world coinciding with a turbulent hot-air balloon flight. As he grows, his existence is an idyllic blend of scientific inquiry—mastering the physics of lift and drag with his eccentric tutor—and youthful camaraderie with Princess Isabella, the daughter of the islands' benevolent king. Colfer establishes this utopian setting with lush, sensory prose, making the salty sea air and the dizzying heights of the castle towers palpably real to the reader.

However, Colfer is too astute a storyteller to let paradise endure. The novel’s central theme quickly emerges: the perilous intersection of human ambition and corrupt power. When Conor stumbles upon a nefarious conspiracy orchestrated by the cold-blooded Marshal Vikare to murder the king and seize control of the Saltees, his world shatters. Falsely accused of the regicide, Conor is cast into the horrific, subterranean prison of Little Saltee. 

It is within the crushing despair of this rock-hewn dungeon that Airman truly takes flight. Colfer shifts the narrative tone into a gripping tale of resilience. Stripped of his freedom, Conor spends years enduring psychological and physical torture, yet he refuses to let his spirit—or his obsession with flight—be extinguished. The author crafts a compelling meditation on the tenacity of the human intellect. In the darkest of places, Conor uses science, observation, and sheer willpower to engineer his survival and, ultimately, his escape. 

Colfer’s writing style in Airman represents a maturation of his craft. While his signature pacing remains brisk and cinematic, the prose here carries a gravity and emotional resonance that elevates the material beyond typical young adult adventure. The action sequences are meticulously choreographed, marrying Victorian-era steampunk aesthetics with genuine historical plausibility. When Conor finally takes to the air in a makeshift flying machine of his own design, the payoff is nothing short of transcendent. It is a thrilling, cinematic climax that rewards the reader's patience with pure adrenaline and emotional catharsis.

Airman is a masterclass in pacing, character development, and thematic depth. It is a story about a boy who loses everything, only to literally rise above his oppressors on wings of his own making. Eoin Colfer has crafted an unforgettable ode to the indomitable nature of human ingenuity and a soaring adventure that deserves a permanent place on every reader’s bookshelf.
