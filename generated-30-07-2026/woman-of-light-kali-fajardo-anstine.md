# Woman of Light

**Author:** Kali Fajardo-Anstine  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

In Woman of Light, Kali Fajardo-Anstine does not merely write historical fiction; she excavates a buried American West, sweeping away the dust of erasure to reveal a landscape rich with resilience, magic, and enduring Indigenous Chicano bloodlines. Set against the simmering, racially volatile backdrop of 1930s Denver, this national bestseller is an ambitious, multigenerational epic that anchors its grandeur in the intimate, quiet pulse of everyday survival. 

At the center of this sprawling tapestry is Luz “Little Light” Lopez, a tea leaf reader and laundress whose life is upended when her brother, Diego, is driven out of town by a violent white mob. Left to navigate a world increasingly hostile to her existence, Luz is thrust into a journey of self-preservation that extends far beyond the cobblestone streets of Depression-era Colorado. As she struggles to keep her footing, Luz begins to experience vivid, transporting visions. These aren't mere hallucinations; they are ancestral echoes bridging her to her people’s Indigenous homeland in the Lost Territory. Through Luz, Fajardo-Anstine establishes the novel's central, beating theme: the sacred, perilous duty of storytelling as a form of resistance. There is one seer in every generation, we are told, and Luz must shoulder the immense weight of keeping her family's history alive before it is swallowed by the aggressive tide of white colonization and erasure.

Fajardo-Anstine’s literary style is nothing short of transfixing. Described by Roxane Gay as “dazzling, cinematic, intimate, and lyrical,” the prose moves with the fluid grace of oral storytelling. The author possesses a rare gift for sensory world-building. She renders 1930s Denver with gritty authenticity—the scent of steam from the laundry, the clink of teacups, the menacing hum of rising fascism, and the suffocating dust of the plains. Yet, she pivots effortlessly into the ethereal, sweeping the reader backward through decades of love, betrayal, and displacement. The historical and the mystical do not clash in these pages; rather, they bleed into one another naturally, mirroring the complex heritage of the Lopez family. 

What makes Woman of Light truly unforgettable, however, is its gallery of complicated, flawed, and fiercely resilient characters. Fajardo-Anstine rejects flat archetypes, instead crafting women who endure systemic violence and daily indignities with fierce grace. Luz is a remarkable heroine—vulnerable yet unyielding, burdened by gifts she never asked for yet vitalized by the ancestral strength humming in her veins. 

Ultimately, Woman of Light is a triumph of reclamation. Fajardo-Anstine has gifted contemporary literature with a western saga that challenges the traditional, whitewashed myths of the American frontier. It is a haunting, luminous reminder that history is not just a record of the victors, but a living, breathing song carried forward by those who refuse to let their stories fade into oblivion.
