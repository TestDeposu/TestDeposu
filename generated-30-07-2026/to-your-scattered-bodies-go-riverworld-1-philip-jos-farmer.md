# To Your Scattered Bodies Go (Riverworld, #1)

**Author:** Philip José Farmer  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2024

Imagine drawing a final breath on Earth, expecting the quiet void of oblivion or the pearly gates of theological promise, only to blink open your eyes naked on the grassy banks of a winding, seemingly infinite river under an alien sky. This is the audacious premise of Philip José Farmer’s Hugo Award-winning masterpiece, To Your Scattered Bodies Go, the magnificent opening salvo of the legendary Riverworld series. Farmer takes the ultimate human mystery—death—and gleefully turns it into a grand, planetary laboratory for the resurrected history of our species.

Led by the indomitable Victorian adventurer and writer Sir Richard Francis Burton, the novel immediately hooks the reader by populating its bizarre afterlife not with angels and demons, but with every single human being who ever lived. From Neanderthals to 20th-century philosophers, billions of souls are simultaneously reborn along the shores of this colossal waterway, all roughly restored to their physical prime. Adding a layer of brilliant, darkly humorous logistical irony to this existential puzzle, every resurrected human is equipped with a metallic cylinder known as a "grail"—a mysterious device that dispenses daily necessities, from food and tobacco to alcohol and lipstick, courtesy of the enigmatic "Ethicals" who engineered this colossal resurrection.

Farmer’s authorial style is a breathless fusion of classic pulp adventure, historical fiction, and high-concept philosophical science fiction. He writes with a muscular, propulsive energy that mirrors his protagonist. Burton is the quintessential Farmer hero: fiercely independent, intellectually insatiable, and deeply flawed. Through Burton's perspective, the novel explores its central, haunting theme: the democratization of eternity and the stubborn persistence of human nature. 

In Riverworld, death has been conquered, but human conflict has not. Farmer uses this vast sociological canvas to ask profound questions about purpose, morality, and identity. Without the finality of death, what drives human ambition? Are we doomed to repeat the tribalism, violence, and hierarchical struggles of our mortal lives on a cosmic playground? The juxtaposition of historical figures—such as Hermann Göring rubbing shoulders with medieval knights and ancient queens—serves as a biting commentary on the absurdity of human history, while simultaneously fueling a relentless, fast-paced narrative.

To Your Scattered Bodies Go is striking because it refuses to treat the afterlife as a place of passive rest. Instead, it frames eternity as an urgent call to rebellion and exploration. Farmer crafts a world that feels simultaneously vast and claustrophobic, beautiful and terrifying. For readers who crave imaginative world-building combined with deep philosophical inquiry, this book remains an unmissable classic. It is a dazzling, provocative reminder that even across the threshold of death, the human story is only just beginning.
