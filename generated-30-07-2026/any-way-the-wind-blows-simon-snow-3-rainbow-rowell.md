# Any Way the Wind Blows (Simon Snow, #3)

**Author:** Rainbow Rowell  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

In the highly anticipated finale to the Simon Snow trilogy, Rainbow Rowell masterfully weaves a poignant and thought-provoking narrative that delves into the complexities of endings, closure, and the aftermath of trauma. Any Way the Wind Blows is a triumphant conclusion to the series, one that will leave readers both heartbroken and hopeful.

Rowell's writing style, as always, is a character in its own right – warm, witty, and disarmingly honest. Her characters, now familiar friends, are multidimensional and relatable, their struggles and triumphs deeply human. The story is a rich tapestry of emotions, relationships, and personal growth, as Simon, Baz, Penelope, and Agatha navigate the uncharted territories of their lives.

At its core, Any Way the Wind Blows is a book about endings – about the messy, often painful process of closing chapters and moving forward. Rowell explores the bittersweet nature of closure, the ways in which it can be both liberating and suffocating. Her characters must confront the traumas of their past, the secrets they've kept, and the lies they've told themselves. It's a journey that's both deeply personal and universally relatable.

One of the most striking aspects of the book is its thoughtful exploration of the Chosen One narrative. Rowell cleverly subverts the tropes of the genre, revealing the flaws and vulnerabilities of her characters. Simon, in particular, is a fascinating study in the deconstruction of the Chosen One myth. His struggles with identity, purpose, and belonging are deeply resonant, and his ultimate decision is both surprising and satisfying.

The supporting cast is equally well-developed, with each character bringing their unique voice and perspective to the narrative. Baz, with his trademark wit and sarcasm, is a standout, as is Penelope, whose quiet strength and determination are a testament to her growth throughout the series. Agatha, too, is a revelation, her character undergoing a transformation that's both heartbreaking and empowering.

The world-building, as always, is meticulous and immersive. Rowell's magical system is complex and nuanced, with its own history, mythology, and rules. The return to Watford and the World of Mages is a welcome one, and the author's affection for the world she's created is evident on every page.

If there's a criticism to be made, it's that the pacing can be uneven at times. The book is a slow burn, with a meandering narrative that's more focused on character development than plot progression. However, this is a minor quibble, and the payoff is well worth the investment.

In conclusion, Any Way the Wind Blows is a triumphant finale to the Simon Snow trilogy. It's a book about endings, closure, and the power of stories to shape our understanding of ourselves and the world around us. Rainbow Rowell's writing is, as always, a joy to read – warm, witty, and disarmingly honest. This is a book that will stay with readers long after the final page is turned, a testament to the enduring power of storytelling.
