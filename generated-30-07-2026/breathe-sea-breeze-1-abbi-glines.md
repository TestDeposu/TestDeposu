# Breathe (Sea Breeze, #1)

**Author:** Abbi Glines  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

Abbi Glines has carved out a distinct niche in the contemporary New Adult romance genre, and her 2013 novel, Breathe, stands as a quintessential entry point into her sun-drenched, emotionally charged literary universe. Set against the sultry backdrop of the shore town Sea Breeze, the novel delivers a fast-paced, addictive narrative that blends the classic Cinderella trope with the glittering, high-stakes world of rock stardom. At its core, Breathe explores the transformative power of genuine affection when it collides with profound responsibility, examining how two vastly different worlds can briefly align under the weight of undeniable chemistry.

The central theme of the novel pivots on the juxtaposition between forced maturity and carefree youth. Seventeen-year-old Sadie White is the antithesis of the stereotypical carefree teenager; she is a young woman hardened by a life of domestic servitude and the exhausting burden of raising her own neglectful mother. When Sadie takes on a summer housekeeping job on a nearby affluent island, her life intersects with Jax Stone, a globally renowned teen rock star. While the premise threatens to lean heavily into familiar fan-fiction territory, Glines anchors the narrative in Sadie’s emotional exhaustion. Sadie is not just unimpressed by Jax’s fame; she is fundamentally too tired to care. Her emotional armor—forged out of sheer necessity—serves as the primary catalyst for the romance. Jax, accustomed to sycophants and fair-weather crowds, finds himself utterly disarmed by her indifference. The core theme thus becomes one of mutual salvation: Jax offers Sadie a rare chance to be cared for rather than doing the caring, while Sadie grounds Jax in a reality he has long since lost touch with.

Glines’s authorial style is characterized by its economy of language, rapid pacing, and heavy reliance on intense emotional immediacy. She writes with a cinematic clarity that prioritizes dialogue and internal yearning over dense descriptive prose. The narrative voice is unabashedly romantic, leaning into melodrama and high-stakes emotional beats that will resonate deeply with fans of the genre. Glines does not bog her readers down with flowery metaphors; instead, she captures the breathless, consuming nature of first love through short, punchy chapters and alternating perspectives that build palpable sexual and emotional tension. The prose is designed to be consumed in a single sitting, mirroring the fleeting, intense nature of a memorable summer romance. 

While the plot moves swiftly, the strength of the book lies in its atmospheric setting. Sea Breeze feels tangible, painted with the heat of summer, the allure of the ocean, and the stark contrast between the wealthy elite and the struggling locals. Glines uses this environment to amplify the emotional stakes, making the island feel like an isolated incubator where Sadie and Jax can temporarily escape the pressures of their respective realities. 

Ultimately, Breathe is a satisfying, emotionally gratifying read that delivers precisely what its target audience desires. It is a story about shedding the heavy armor of responsibility and allowing oneself to feel, even when the risks are insurmountably high. Abbi Glines successfully captures the heady, intoxicating essence of a summer that changes everything, proving that sometimes, the only thing you need to survive is a single breath of fresh air.
