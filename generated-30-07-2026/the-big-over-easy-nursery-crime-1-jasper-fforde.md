# The Big Over Easy (Nursery Crime, #1)

**Author:** Jasper Fforde  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2005

**Title: "The Big Over Easy" by Jasper Fforde: A Hilarious, Twisted Take on Nursery Rhymes**

In "The Big Over Easy," Jasper Fforde, the master of literary mischief, turns his pen to the world of nursery rhymes, spinning a yarn that's as delightfully absurd as it is cleverly plotted. This is no ordinary crime novel; it's a wild, whimsical ride through the seedy underbelly of a world where nursery characters live and breathe, and murder is a very real possibility.

At the helm of this peculiar investigation is Inspector Jack Spratt, a family man and head of Reading's Nursery Crime division. When Humpty Dumpty, an ovoid D-class celebrity with a penchant for women and a past as shady as his shell, meets his untimely end beneath a wall, Spratt is on the case. Joined by his new partner, Sergeant Mary Mary, they delve into Humpty's sordid past, uncovering a web of secrets that reaches into the highest echelons of Reading society.

Fforde's style is nothing short of brilliant. He seamlessly weaves together the familiar and the fantastical, creating a world that's both instantly recognizable and utterly alien. The humor is sharp and witty, with laugh-out-loud moments that sit side by side with more subtle, literary jokes. It's a wonderfully readable riot, cleverly plotted and magically overstuffed yet amazingly digestible, making it a perfect read for anyone who wants the thrill of a good crime novel larded with highly literate humor.

Fforde's characters are as delightful as they are eccentric. Jack Spratt is a hero for our times, a man trying to do his best in a world that's gone mad. Mary Mary, his partner, is a refreshing addition, a character who's as capable as she is charming. And then there's Humpty Dumpty himself, a character so richly drawn that you can't help but feel a pang of sympathy for the big egg, even as you're laughing at his antics.

"The Big Over Easy" is more than just a crime novel. It's a love letter to the power of storytelling, a testament to the enduring appeal of nursery rhymes, and a sharp critique of the society we live in. It's a book that will make you laugh, make you think, and leave you eager for more of Fforde's unique brand of literary mayhem.
