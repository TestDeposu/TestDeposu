# Living Dead Girl

**Author:** Elizabeth Scott  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

**Living Dead Girl: A Chilling Tale of Captivity and Resilience**

Elizabeth Scott's "Living Dead Girl" is a haunting exploration of the human spirit's capacity to endure and resist, even in the face of unimaginable horror. This young adult novel, reminiscent of works like "What Girls Are Made Of" and "The Lovely Bones," delves into the psyche of Alice, a girl abducted at the age of ten and held captive for five years.

Scott's narrative style is stark and unflinching, painting a vivid picture of Alice's grim reality without resorting to gratuitous violence. The story is told in the first person, allowing readers to experience Alice's terror, despair, and, ultimately, her quiet determination to survive. The author's use of short, sharp sentences mirrors the abrupt, brutal nature of Alice's life, creating a sense of urgency and tension that keeps readers on the edge of their seats.

The main theme of "Living Dead Girl" is resilience in the face of adversity. Alice's captor, Ray, has systematically broken down her sense of self, her hope, and her will to resist. Yet, despite the relentless cruelty she endures, Alice clings to life, her spirit unbroken. Scott explores the complex dynamics of captivity, illustrating how Alice's survival is not just a physical feat but a mental and emotional one as well.

Scott also challenges the reader's perceptions of power and agency. Alice, despite being the victim, is not a passive character. She actively seeks ways to assert her will, to maintain a sense of self, and to resist Ray's control. This portrayal of Alice as a survivor rather than just a victim is one of the most powerful aspects of the novel.

However, "Living Dead Girl" is not without its flaws. The pacing can feel uneven at times, with some sections moving too quickly while others linger too long on Alice's internal monologue. Additionally, the ending, while satisfying in many ways, feels a bit too neat and tidy, given the raw, brutal reality of the rest of the novel.

Despite these minor issues, "Living Dead Girl" is a powerful, thought-provoking read that will stay with you long after you've turned the last page. It's a stark reminder of the strength of the human spirit and a testament to the power of storytelling to give voice to the voiceless.
