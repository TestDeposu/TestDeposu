# The Young Elites (The Young Elites, #1)

**Author:** Marie Lu  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

**The Young Elites: A Dark, Enthralling Debut in Marie Lu's New Series**

In her latest offering, Marie Lu, the acclaimed author of the Legend series, presents a captivating tale of power, revenge, and the complexities of identity in "The Young Elites," the first installment of a new trilogy. Lu's signature style—fast-paced plot, intricate world-building, and morally grey characters—is on full display, making this novel an enthralling read that will leave fans eager for more.

At the heart of "The Young Elites" lies the theme of otherness and the struggle for acceptance. Adelina Amouteru, our protagonist, is a "malfetto," a survivor of the blood fever that has left her scarred and ostracized. Her father's revulsion and society's fear force Adelina to grapple with her identity and the extent of her powers, which she discovers are far beyond those of her peers. Lu masterfully explores Adelina's journey from victim to vengeful heroine, delving into the dark corners of her psyche as she embraces her abilities and seeks retribution.

Lu's world-building is as impressive as her character development. The kingdom of Kenettra, with its intricate politics, rich history, and diverse cast of characters, feels lived-in and authentic. The Young Elites, those who possess mysterious powers, add an intriguing layer to the story, their abilities ranging from the mundane to the extraordinary. Lu's exploration of power dynamics, both political and magical, is nuanced and thought-provoking, keeping readers on the edge of their seats.

One of the standout aspects of "The Young Elites" is Lu's ability to create compelling, morally ambiguous characters. Teren Santoro, the leader of the Inquisition Axis tasked with hunting down the Young Elites, is a fascinating foil to Adelina. His motivations are complex, and his actions, while often brutal, are driven by a desire to protect his nation. Enzo Valenciano, a member of the Dagger Society, a secret organization of Young Elites, is another intriguing character, his enigmatic past and true intentions remaining shrouded in mystery.

Lu's prose is as engaging as ever, her writing style concise yet evocative, painting vivid images with a deft hand. The pacing is swift, with each chapter ending on a cliffhanger that propels the reader forward. The narrative is told from multiple perspectives, allowing readers to gain insight into the minds of various characters and adding depth to the story.

However, "The Young Elites" is not without its flaws. Some plot points feel predictable, and certain characters' motivations could use more development. But these are minor quibbles in an otherwise impressive novel.

In conclusion, "The Young Elites" is a dark, captivating tale that will appeal to fans of Lu's previous work and new readers alike. With its complex characters, intricate world-building, and thought-provoking themes, it is a strong start to a promising new series.
