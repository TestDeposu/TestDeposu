# From Here to Eternity: Traveling the World to Find the Good Death

**Author:** Caitlin Doughty  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

In "From Here to Eternity: Traveling the World to Find the Good Death," mortician Caitlin Doughty masterfully navigates the uncharted territories of death and mourning in our increasingly sanitized and commercialized funeral industry. This critically acclaimed bestseller is an odyssey that transcends cultural boundaries, inviting readers to reexamine the rituals and taboos surrounding death.

With unwavering curiosity and courage, Doughty sets out to understand how other cultures honor the dead, immersing herself in customs that defy American norms. Her quest takes her to the breathtaking yet unsettling landscapes of Zoroastrian sky burials, where deceased loved ones are fed to vultures and left to the mercy of the wind. In Bolivia, she encounters the fascinating tradition of skull cleaning, where women meticulously preserve and honor the skulls of their ancestors to channel the power of the dead.

Through Doughty's remarkable journeys, the reader is introduced to an array of death cultures and traditions that not only challenge our own but also raise profound questions about the nature of dignity, respect, and connection with the deceased. By juxtaposing these customs with the often-reviled practices of Western funeral industries, Doughty skillfully highlights the emotional disconnection that can arise from a commercialized and heavily regulated approach to death.

As an author, Doughty's narrative voice is both a strength and a hallmark of her work. Her writing is infused with an irrepressible curiosity, a gentle yet unflinching respect for the deceased, and a willingness to confront her own fears of mortality. Her tone is neither prurient nor somber; instead, it is imbued with a sense of wonder, awe, and compassion that draws the reader in and refuses to let go.

Throughout the book, Doughty engages with other cultures and traditions not as a detached observer but as a participant, listener, and even fellow seeker. By sharing her own experiences and vulnerabilities, she crafts a narrative that is as intimate as it is expansive, inviting readers to reflect not just on death and its various manifestations but on the human experiences that surround and define it.

Doughty's work is complemented by the poignant and expressive illustrations of Landis Blair, which add a visual dimension to her already vivid descriptions. These illustrations serve not merely as a complement but as a necessary extension of Doughty's narrative voice, illuminating the often-overlooked beauty and significance of death cultures.

Ultimately, "From Here to Eternity" is a powerful testament to the resilience and diversity of human experiences, inviting readers to reimagine their relationships with death and the dead. Through her remarkable travels and unflinching explorations, Doughty reminds us that the most meaningful and dignified approaches to death are those that honor our connections with the deceased, rather than the commercial interests of the funeral industry.

In an era where death is too often sanitized, suppressed, or avoided, Caitlin Doughty's masterpiece stands as a timely and necessary corrective, urging us to reclaim our agency and respect in the face of mortality.
