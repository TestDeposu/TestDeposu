# The Good, the Bad, and the Undead (The Hollows, #2)

**Author:** Kim Harrison  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Kim Harrison returns with The Good, the Bad, and the Undead, the deliciously gritty second installment in her acclaimed Hollows series. Building upon the urban fantasy foundation laid in Dead Witch Walking, Harrison elevates her world-building, blending hard-boiled supernatural noir with razor-sharp wit and a pulse-pounding narrative that refuses to let go.

At the heart of Harrison’s style is a relentless fusion of genres. She takes the classic tropes of detective fiction—rain-slicked streets, moral ambiguity, and a hard-edged protagonist down on her luck—and drops them headfirst into a vibrant, alternate Cincinnati where witches, vampires, pixies, and pixilated sprites live openly among humans. The thematic core of this novel explores the complexities of survival, identity, and the heavy toll of blurred moral boundaries. Rachel Morgan, a former runner for the paranormal Inden Security, is no pristine superhero. She is fiercely independent, deeply flawed, and constantly teetering on the edge of disaster. Harrison uses Rachel’s precarious situation—fleeing the deadly underworld while trying to carve out a legitimate living as an independent witch-for-hire—to examine how far one will go to protect chosen family.

The primary theme of the book centers on the tension between nature and nurture, monster and humanity. Through Rachel’s unlikely and tense domestic arrangement with her living vampire roommate, Jenks the fiercely loyal pixie, and Ivy Tamwood, a vampire trying desperately to hold onto her soul, Harrison deconstructs the traditional monster archetype. The narrative brilliantly challenges the notion of absolute good and evil. The human authorities are frequently just as corrupt and terrifying as the supernatural predators lurking in the shadows, forcing Rachel to navigate a treacherous labyrinth of shifting alliances. 

Harrison’s prose is breathless, snappy, and effortlessly immersive. She has a distinct talent for action choreography, balancing high-stakes magical showdowns with snappy, organic dialogue that grounds the fantastical elements in raw realism. The pacing is relentless, moving from one harrowing scrape to the next, yet Harrison never sacrifices character depth for spectacle. Rachel’s internal monologue is laced with biting sarcasm and vulnerability, making her an intensely relatable protagonist who stumbles, bleeds, and learns the hard way.

In The Good, the Bad, and the Undead, Harrison proves that the sophomore slump is a myth. She deepens the mythology of the Hollows while raising the emotional stakes, delivering a captivating thrill ride that cements her status as a premier voice in urban fantasy. It is dark, funny, sensual, and utterly addictive—a must-read for anyone who likes their magic with a heavy dose of grit and attitude.
