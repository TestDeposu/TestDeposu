# Behemoth (Leviathan, #2)

**Author:** Scott Westerfeld  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

In the world of Scott Westerfeld's Leviathan series, science and fantasy blend together in a captivating tapestry of adventure, politics, and self-discovery. In Behemoth, the second installment of this beloved series, Westerfeld masterfully weaves a narrative that will keep you on the edge of your seat, questioning the boundaries between fact and fiction, and the true meaning of identity.

Deryn Sharp, a determined and resourceful young woman, poses as a boy in the British Air Service, a remarkable feat in a time where women's roles are strictly defined. Alek Petrovich, the charismatic heir to the Clankers' empire, navigates the complexities of war and deception, all while hiding his true identity from those around him. When these two unlikely allies meet on the airship Leviathan, they set in motion a chain of events that will change their lives forever.

Behemoth is a story about the blurred lines between reality and perception. In a world where science and wonder coexist, the distinction between what is real and what is imagined becomes increasingly fluid. Westerfeld's skillful storytelling draws us into a universe where airships and automatons are as much a part of everyday life as they are a product of fantasy. It's a world where a creature of unimaginable power, the behemoth, can wreak havoc on an enemy ship with a single bite.

Through Deryn and Alek's experiences, Westerfeld explores the complexities of identity, as both characters navigate the intricacies of deception and disguise. Deryn's pose as a boy forces her to confront the societal expectations placed upon women, while Alek's true identity as the heir to the Clanker empire drives him to make choices that put his family and legacy at risk. In this way, Westerfeld raises thought-provoking questions about the role of authenticity in a world where social norms can be suffocating.

Westerfeld's writing style is characteristically engaging, with a narrative voice that is both accessible and imaginative. He seamlessly blends high-stakes action with personal relationships and character growth, all within a meticulously crafted world that will transport you to an alternate 1910s. The pace is relentless, propelling the reader forward even as the stakes grow increasingly high. Through his masterful storytelling, Westerfeld has created a rich and immersive world that will leave you eager for more.

In Behemoth, Westerfeld has woven a tale that resonates long after the final page is turned. This book is not just an entertaining adventure or an imaginative exercise in world-building; it's a nuanced exploration of identity, deception, and the consequences of our choices. It's a powerful reminder that our perceptions shape our understanding of reality, and that sometimes, the greatest wonders of all lie just beyond the boundaries of what we consider to be real.
