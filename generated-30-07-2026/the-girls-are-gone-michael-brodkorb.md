# The Girls Are Gone

**Author:** Michael Brodkorb  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

The Girls Are Gone, written by Michael Brodkorb, is a gripping and thought-provoking true crime story that delves into the mysterious disappearance of two teenage sisters, Samantha and Gianna Rucki. On the surface, the book appears to be a heartbreaking account of two young lives lost, but as the narrative unfolds, it reveals a complex web of deceit, manipulation, and conspiracy that will leave readers questioning the very fabric of the justice system.

Brodkorb's masterful storytelling weaves together a intricate tapestry of events, shedding light on the tumultuous marriage of David Rucki and Sandra Grazzini-Rucki, and the subsequent divorce that sets the stage for the girls' disappearance. The author's meticulous research and attention to detail are evident throughout the book, as he painstakingly reconstructs the events leading up to and following the disappearance of the Rucki sisters.

One of the most striking aspects of the book is the stark contrast between the actions of the two parents. David Rucki's tireless efforts to find his daughters, despite the overwhelming odds against him, are a testament to the enduring power of paternal love. In contrast, Sandra Grazzini-Rucki's abandonment of her children and her subsequent disappearance raise more questions than answers, leaving the reader to ponder the motivations behind her actions.

Brodkorb's writing style is clear and concise, making the book an easy read despite the complexity of the subject matter. He skillfully navigates the treacherous waters of family dynamics, divorce, and custody battles, never shying away from the harsh realities of the situation. The author's commitment to telling the truth, no matter how difficult, is admirable, and his dedication to the Rucki family's story is evident on every page.

Throughout the book, Brodkorb raises important questions about the failures of the justice system and the ways in which it can be manipulated by those with the means and the motive. The Girls Are Gone is not just a story about two missing sisters; it is a scathing indictment of the systemic failures that allowed their disappearance to occur.

Ultimately, The Girls Are Gone is a must-read for anyone interested in true crime stories, family dynamics, and the complexities of the human condition. Brodkorb's masterful storytelling and meticulous research make for a compelling narrative that will keep readers on the edge of their seats until the very end.
