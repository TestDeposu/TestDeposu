Brigid Kemmerer’s debut, **Defy the Night**, is a glittering splash of high fantasy romance that feels at once treacherously original and irresistibly familiar. **The core theme—inequality versus compassion—moves with a clear, narrative pulse throughout the book**. Kemmerer lays her plot on a fragile axis: a sovereign’s despotic power, a famine that devours the poor, and one woman’s refusal to accept a system that favors the wealthy. The morbid scarcity of Moonflower Elixir becomes a metaphor for a kingdom that worships itself onto a throne of privilege and inherited privilege. Tessa’s clandestine “moonlit” missions are acts of insurrection that transcend the daring mechanics of a heist; they are a quiet defiance of a system that refuses to coddle its most vulnerable. As the night grows louder and the kingdom grows tighter, her actions illustrate the power of agency over intention: she is not simply stealing for the thrill, she is responding to an imperative that higher places have smashed. The narrative’s tension is amplified by the understated danger of being caught—a reminder that even sympathies can be fatal if they are not tucked into the shadows.
