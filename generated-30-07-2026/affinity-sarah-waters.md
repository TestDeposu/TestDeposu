# Affinity

**Author:** Sarah Waters  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2002

In Affinity, Sarah Waters achieves something rare in historical fiction: she does not merely reconstruct the Victorian era; she allows its claustrophobia, its societal hypocrisy, and its suppressed desires to seep directly into the reader's bones. Published in 1999 and cementing Waters' reputation as a master of atmospheric suspense following her dazzling debut Tipping the Velvet, this novel trades the flamboyant music halls of her previous work for the damp, oppressive stones of Millbank Prison and the dim, curtained parlors of spiritualist seances. The result is a haunting, psychologically astute masterpiece that explores the dark intersections of confinement, obsession, and the desperate human need for connection.

Set against the brooding backdrop of late nineteenth-century London, the novel unfolds primarily through the dueling diary entries of two women whose lives become dangerously intertwined. Margaret Prior is an upper-class woman delicately recovering from a personal tragedy and a suicide attempt. Seeking a sense of purpose and a path toward rehabilitation, she begins visiting the inmates at Millbank, a monolithic fortress of despair where murderers and thieves rot in silence. Amidst this bleak human warehouse, Margaret encounters Selina Dawes, an enigmatic spiritualist imprisoned after a tragic séance left one death and a trail of psychological devastation in its wake. 

What begins as charitable curiosity quickly metastasizes into an intoxicating fixation. Waters brilliantly orchestrates the power dynamic between Margaret, the privileged visitor wielding the keys, and Selina, the captive medium who holds the mysterious power of the unseen. As Margaret is drawn deeper into Selina’s twilight world of spirits, messages from the beyond, and veiled passions, the boundaries between sanity and madness, truth and illusion, begin to dissolve. Waters uses the spiritualist craze of the era not as mere gothic window-dressing, but as a brilliant metaphor for the Victorian condition itself—a society obsessed with peering behind veils, desperate to communicate with what has been forcefully silenced or locked away.

Waters’ stylistic prowess lies in her sensory world-building. Victorian London in her hands is a living, breathing entity of fog, sulfur, damp wool, and institutional starch. The descriptions of Millbank Prison are so tactile one can almost feel the chill of the damp brick and hear the heavy echoing of iron doors. Yet, for all its external gloom, the true brilliance of Affinity rests in its internal landscape. Waters maps the topography of female isolation with devastating precision. Both Margaret and Selina are trapped—one by the suffocating expectations of polite society and familial duty, the other by stone walls and the stigma of the occult. Their burgeoning attraction offers a dangerous form of liberation, a forbidden affinity that transcends the rigid social strata of their world.

The novel is a masterclass in slow-burn tension. Waters parcels out revelations with exquisite control, leading the reader toward a finale that is as shocking as it is emotionally devastating. The epistolary structure, alternating between Margaret’s yearning intellect and Selina’s enigmatic perspective, creates an unreliable, deeply intimate narrative tension. You question everything you read, suspecting every shadow, yet you remain entirely helpless against the pull of the narrative current. 

Ultimately, Affinity is more than a gothic thriller or a historical romance; it is a profound exploration of redemption through the eyes of the marginalized. Sarah Waters crafts a world where the supernatural and the psychological mirror one another, proving that the most terrifying ghosts are the ones we harbor within ourselves. For anyone drawn to atmospheric storytelling, complex female protagonists, and narratives that linger long after the final page is turned, this novel is an absolute triumph of literary suspense.
