# Exit Music (Inspector Rebus, #17)

**Author:** Ian Rankin  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2008

**Exit Music: A Symphony of Suspense and Nostalgia**

Ian Rankin's **Exit Music** is the seventeenth installment in the Inspector Rebus series, a bittersweet swansong that echoes the impending retirement of its titular character. Rankin masterfully weaves a tapestry of intrigue, nostalgia, and moral ambiguity, painting a vivid portrait of Edinburgh as the city's dark underbelly is laid bare.

The story unfolds against the backdrop of a chilly Edinburgh autumn, mirroring Rebus's own waning career. Rankin's prose is as crisp and evocative as ever, each sentence a sharp, precise instrument in his symphony of suspense. He deftly balances the tension of Rebus's investigation into the murder of a Russian poet with the personal turmoil of a man grappling with the end of his professional life.

At the heart of **Exit Music** lies the theme of legacy—what we leave behind and the ghosts that haunt us. Rebus, a flawed but formidable detective, is a man defined by his past. His investigation into the poet's murder forces him to confront not only the city's criminal underworld but also his own demons. Rankin expertly explores the moral grey areas that Rebus inhabits, making him a compelling, if not always likable, protagonist.

Rankin's style is as distinctive as Rebus's gruff, world-weary voice. He employs a deft use of dialogue to reveal character and advance the plot, while his narrative is punctuated by vivid, often unsettling, images that linger in the mind. The pacing is measured, building to a crescendo that is as satisfying as it is unexpected.

**Exit Music** is more than just a crime novel; it's a love letter to Edinburgh, a eulogy for a retiring detective, and a meditation on the nature of legacy. Rankin's prose is as sharp as ever, his plotting intricate and satisfying. Fans of the series will find much to cherish here, while newcomers will be drawn into Rebus's world, eager to explore the labyrinthine streets of Edinburgh and the even darker paths of the human psyche.
