# Murder Most Unladylike (Murder Most Unladylike, #1)

**Author:** Robin Stevens  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

**Murder Most Unladylike: A Thrilling Ride on the Orient Express**

In the vein of Agatha Christie's classic "Murder on the Orient Express," Robin Stevens' "Murder Most Unladylike" whisks us aboard the luxurious Orient Express, where a murder and a missing ruby necklace set the stage for a gripping mystery. This third installment in the Wells & Wong series is a delightful blend of wit, suspense, and vintage charm.

Stevens masterfully weaves a tapestry of intrigue, with each passenger harboring secrets and potential motives. The dynamic duo of Hazel Wong and Daisy Wells, founders of the Wells & Wong Detective Society, are on the case, but they're not alone. This time, they've got competition, adding an extra layer of tension and suspense.

Stevens' strength lies in her ability to create authentic, engaging characters. Hazel and Daisy are a delightful pair, their banter and camaraderie reminiscent of classic detective duos. The supporting cast is equally well-drawn, each character adding depth and complexity to the plot.

The author's style is reminiscent of the Golden Age of detective fiction, with a modern twist. The story is told in the first person by Hazel, whose unique perspective and voice bring a freshness to the genre. Stevens' use of language is evocative and engaging, painting vivid pictures of the opulent train and its inhabitants.

The plot is a masterclass in misdirection, with red herrings and twists keeping the reader guessing until the very end. The solution to the mystery is satisfyingly clever, a testament to Stevens' skill as a plotter.

"Murder Most Unladylike" is a thrilling ride on the Orient Express, filled with suspense, humor, and clever plotting. It's a must-read for fans of classic detective fiction and a testament to Stevens' skill as a writer. Highly recommended.
