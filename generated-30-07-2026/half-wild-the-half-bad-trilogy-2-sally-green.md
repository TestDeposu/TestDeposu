# Half Wild (The Half Bad Trilogy, #2)

**Author:** Sally Green  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

In Sally Green's gripping sequel to Half Bad, the second installment of the Half Bad Trilogy, we're plunged headfirst into the dark world of witches and their ruthless regime. Half Wild picks up where its predecessor left off, with Nathan, the protagonist and a Half-Blood Witch, on the run from the Council of Fairborn Witches. Trained to kill his father, the most feared Blood Witch, Nathan has evolved into a formidable force, driven by his desire for freedom and love.

Green's vivid world-building is a highlight of the book, masterfully weaving together modern-day Europe with a sinister realm of magic. The Council's sinister grip on society is oppressive, and the way Green conveys the exploitation of witches as tools for murder and destruction is chilling. Her exploration of the complexities of good and evil is thought-provoking, leaving the reader questioning the nature of these concepts and where the lines blur.

Green's writing style is immersive and evocative, making it easy to become fully invested in Nathan's perilous journey. Her prose is lyrical, weaving together action, suspense, and romance in a narrative that's both visceral and heart-wrenching. Her characters are multidimensional and complex, with flaws and strengths that make them relatable and human.

One of the most striking aspects of Half Wild is its emphasis on survival and the moral compromises that come with it. Nathan, driven by his love for Annalise and his desire to escape the Council's clutches, is forced to form uneasy alliances and make difficult choices. Green expertly conveys the fragility of life and the risks we take for love. As Nathan navigates this treacherous world, he must confront the darkness within himself and confront the evil that threatens to consume him.

Throughout the book, Green raises important questions about the nature of morality, power, and the consequences of our choices. Her world is dark and unforgiving, but it's also a testament to the human spirit's capacity for resilience and resistance. As Nathan struggles to come to terms with his true nature and his place in the world, readers are forced to confront their own understanding of good and evil.

In conclusion, Half Wild is a gripping and thought-provoking sequel that will appeal to fans of fantasy, romance, and drama. Sally Green's writing is evocative, immersive, and unflinching, plunging readers into a world of dark magic and heart-pounding action. The book's exploration of good and evil, survival, and the human condition will leave readers questioning their own assumptions and morals. With its complex characters, intricate world-building, and moral complexity, Half Wild is a must-read for anyone looking for a thrilling and emotionally resonant story.
