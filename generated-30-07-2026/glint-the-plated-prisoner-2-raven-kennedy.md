# Glint (The Plated Prisoner, #2)

**Author:** Raven Kennedy  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2026

In "Glint," the second installment of The Plated Prisoner series, Raven Kennedy masterfully weaves a captivating tale of dark fantasy, romance, and secrets, drawing inspiration from the legend of the Pied Piper. This romantasy duology is set in a world where fae and dark magic reign, and the lines between good and evil are blurred.

The story follows a half-Bane, half-fae protagonist who is hated and feared by society due to their mixed heritage. Their life is a constant struggle for survival, with vicious glares and cursed outbreaks being a norm. However, everything changes when a Plague descends upon their city, threatening certain death. Enter the Pied Piper, a mysterious figure who rids the city of the Plague but at a steep cost: 130 people, including the protagonist, are taken as payment.

Kennedy's writing style is engaging, with a perfect blend of dark fantasy and romance. The world-building is intricate, with a complex magic system and a rich history that adds depth to the story. The characters are multi-dimensional, with the protagonist being a strong and determined individual who is not afraid to stand up for themselves. The Pied Piper, on the other hand, is an enigmatic figure with a dark past, and their motivations are slowly revealed throughout the story.

The romance is a slow burn, with a palpable tension between the protagonist and the Pied Piper. Their relationship is complex, with a mix of forced proximity, captivity, and a deep emotional connection. The banter between them is witty and engaging, adding a layer of humor to the story.

One of the main themes of the book is the struggle for power and control. The protagonist is caught between their desire for freedom and the Piper's need for control. This dynamic is expertly woven throughout the story, creating a sense of tension that keeps the reader engaged.

Kennedy's use of tropes is also noteworthy. The "who hurt you?" trope is expertly handled, with the Piper's dark past being slowly revealed. The forced proximity and captive romance tropes are also well-executed, adding to the tension and romance in the story.

Overall, "Glint" is a captivating read that will appeal to fans of dark fantasy and romance. Kennedy's writing style is engaging, and the world-building is intricate and complex. The characters are multi-dimensional, and the romance is a slow burn that will keep readers invested in the story.
