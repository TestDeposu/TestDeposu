# Hell Bent (Ninth House Trilogy, #2)

**Author:** Leigh Bardugo  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

Leigh Bardugo returns with Hell Bent, the razor-sharp and blood-soaked second installment in her acclaimed Ninth House series, plunging readers back into the gothic underbelly of Yale University. While the first novel introduced us to the clandestine world of ancient secret societies and institutionalized dark magic, Hell Bent expands its supernatural borders to tackle something far more personal and desperate: damnation, redemption, and the relentless pursuit of the impossible.

At the narrative’s core is Galaxy “Alex” Stern, a protagonist who fundamentally shatters the archetype of the Ivy League scholar. A survivor of trauma and violence, Alex is bound neither by the sanitized traditions of academia nor the moral platitudes of polite society. In Hell Bent, Bardugo centers the main theme of the novel on institutional accountability and the heavy toll of survival. Yale’s secret societies do not merely dabble in the occult; they commodify human suffering, leveraging ancient magic for modern privilege. Alex’s mission shifts from simple surveillance to a daring, unauthorized rescue mission: she intends to break into Hell itself to free Darlington, her missing mentor and the former Gamma of Lethe. 

Bardugo’s authorial style shines through its seamless blend of gritty urban realism and high-stakes dark fantasy. She writes with a visceral, unapologetic punch that elevates supernatural horror into a biting social critique. New Haven is rendered not merely as a picturesque college town, but as a liminal purgatory where ghosts of the past and living monsters walk side by side. The prose is atmospheric, laced with cynicism yet anchored by a fierce emotional core. Bardugo refuses to offer easy resolutions, instead leaning into the moral ambiguity of her characters. Alex Stern is not a hero in the traditional sense; she is scarred, stubborn, and profoundly human, making her desperate crusade all the more compelling.

Hell Bent is a masterclass in pacing, atmosphere, and character-driven fantasy. Bardugo expertly weaves mythology, academic elitism, and visceral horror into a breathless narrative that refuses to let go. For fans of dark academia and complex urban fantasy, this sequel delivers a hypnotic, thrilling, and ultimately devastating journey into the dark heart of power.
