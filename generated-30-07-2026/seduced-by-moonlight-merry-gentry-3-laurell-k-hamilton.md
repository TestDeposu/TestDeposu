# Seduced by Moonlight (Merry Gentry, #3)

**Author:** Laurell K. Hamilton  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2004

Seduced by Moonlight, the third installment in Laurell K. Hamilton's Merry Gentry series, is a spellbinding tale of magic, power, and seduction. The story follows Merry, a half-mortal faerie princess, as she navigates the treacherous world of the Unseelie Court, where her aunt, the Queen of Air and Darkness, reigns supreme. With her unique blend of sensuality and fantasy, Hamilton masterfully weaves a narrative that is both captivating and unsettling.

One of the primary themes of the book is the exploration of power and its corrupting influence. Merry's discovery of a long-lost chalice awakens the dormant magic within her guards, restoring their powers and sparking a chain reaction of events that threatens to upend the delicate balance of the faerie court. As Merry's powers surge, she becomes the focal point of attention for both her allies and enemies, who seek to exploit her abilities for their own gain. Hamilton skillfully portrays the complexities of power, revealing how it can be both a blessing and a curse, and how those who wield it must constantly navigate the dangers of corruption and manipulation.

Hamilton's writing style is characterized by her vivid descriptions of the faerie world, which are both beautiful and terrifying. Her use of language is lyrical and evocative, conjuring up images of lush landscapes, shimmering magic, and ancient rituals. The author's ability to create a sense of atmosphere and mood is unparalleled, drawing the reader into the world of the Unseelie Court and refusing to let go. The characters, too, are multidimensional and complex, with Merry being a particularly well-crafted protagonist. Her struggles to balance her mortal and faerie heritage, combined with her growing powers and the dangers that come with them, make her a relatable and sympathetic character.

The author's exploration of sensuality and eroticism is also a notable aspect of the book. The relationships between Merry and her guards are intense and passionate, and Hamilton does not shy away from depicting the explicit nature of their encounters. However, these scenes are not gratuitous or exploitative, but rather serve to illustrate the deep emotional bonds between the characters and the ways in which their relationships are intertwined with their magical abilities.

Throughout the book, Hamilton's world-building is meticulous and detailed, revealing a richly imagined universe of faerie politics, magic, and mythology. The Unseelie Court is a dark and foreboding place, filled with treacherous alliances, ancient grudges, and hidden dangers. The author's use of faerie lore and mythology adds depth and complexity to the narrative, creating a sense of history and tradition that underpins the story.

In conclusion, Seduced by Moonlight is a captivating and unsettling tale of magic, power, and seduction. Hamilton's writing style is characterized by her vivid descriptions, complex characters, and meticulous world-building, making this book a must-read for fans of fantasy and urban fantasy. With its themes of power, corruption, and sensuality, this novel is sure to appeal to readers who enjoy a good story with complex characters and morally ambiguous themes.
