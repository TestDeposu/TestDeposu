# Seduce Me at Sunrise (The Hathaways, #2)

**Author:** Lisa Kleypas  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2008

Band of gypsies, scarred pasts, and the dizzying, high-stakes architecture of Victorian-era yearning take center stage in Lisa Kleypas’s 2008 triumph, Seduce Me at Sunrise. As the second installment in the beloved Hathaways series, this novel captures Kleypas at the absolute zenith of her powers, delivering a masterclass in historical romance where forbidden desire is not merely a plot device, but a living, breathing entity. 

At the heart of the narrative is a devastatingly effective push-and-pull dynamic between Kev Merripen and Winnifred Hathaway. Kleypas wastes no time thrusting the reader into the delicious agony of unrequited—yet fiercely reciprocated—passion. Kev, a man of mysterious Gypsy origins rescued by the Hathaways in his youth, operates under the crushing burden of self-preservation and unworthiness. He believes his inner darkness will consume delicate, luminous Win. This creates the novel’s central thematic exploration: the tension between protection and possession, and the painful nobility of self-denial. 

Kleypas excels at crafting protagonists who feel simultaneously larger-than-life and deeply, achingly human. Win, initially portrayed in the series opener as fragile, evolves in this volume into a woman of quiet, formidable resilience. Her physical vulnerability contrasts brilliantly with her emotional fortitude, making her far more than a passive heroine waiting to be rescued. When she returns to England after a devastating separation, transformed and determined to take control of her destiny, the power dynamic shifts. Kev, who has hardened into an emotional fortress to keep her safe, suddenly finds himself outmatched by a woman who no longer needs his permission to love him. 

The author’s style is nothing short of transportive. Kleypas possesses a rare synesthetic ability to write prose that feels tactile, lush, and deeply sensory. Whether she is describing the sprawling, eccentric chaos of the Hathaway estate, the atmospheric gloom of Victorian London, or the simmering physical tension between the leads, her language hums with a quiet electricity. The pacing is pitch-perfect, balancing witty repartee, family warmth, and genuine suspense with emotionally heavy sequences that leave the reader breathless. Furthermore, the inclusion of a charming, seductive rival vying for Win’s attention adds just the right amount of combustible jealousy to force Kev out of his self-imposed exile.

What ultimately elevates Seduce Me at Sunrise above standard genre fare is its profound exploration of healing. Kleypas treats trauma and physical frailty with immense grace, allowing her characters to find strength not by eradicating their scars, but by learning to share them. The romance burns slow and hot, fueled by years of repressed longing, resulting in payoffs that are as emotionally resonant as they are deeply passionate.

For fans of historical romance, Lisa Kleypas offers an intoxicating blend of intelligence, wit, and unbridled sensuality. Seduce Me at Sunrise is a dazzling achievement that lingers in the mind long after the final page is turned, cementing Kleypas’s reputation as a true artisan of the genre.
