# Jesus the Christ: A Study of the Messiah and His Mission According to Holy Scriptures, Both Ancient and Modern

**Author:** James E. Talmage  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

Commissioned at the height of the early twentieth century and endorsed by the highest governing bodies of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, James E. Talmage’s *Jesus the Christ* remains a monumental pillar of Restoration literature. Originally published in 1915, this enduring classic transcends a standard theological treatise, offering readers a sweeping, deeply reverent biographical study of the Savior's mortal ministry, premortal existence, and eternal mission. Talmage approaches his subject not merely as a historian or a passive observer, but as a devoted apostle articulating the profoundest convictions of his soul.

At the very heart of the book lies its central theme: the absolute continuity of divine revelation concerning the Messiah across all dispensations. Talmage weaves together the sacred records of antiquity—the Old and New Testaments—with the distinct voice of modern scripture, including the Book of Mormon, Doctrine and Covenants, and the Pearl of Great Price. By harmonizing these diverse scriptural witnesses, the author constructs a seamless narrative that portrays Jesus Christ as the Creator, Jehovah of the Old Testament, the incarnate Babe of Bethlehem, the crucified Redeemer, and the resurrected Lord. This holistic approach reinforces the Latter-day Saint doctrine that the Gospel of Jesus Christ has been proclaimed in every age to draw humanity back into the presence of God.

Talmage’s literary style is a masterclass in nineteenth- and early twentieth-century religious prose: elevated, eloquent, meticulously researched, and unyielding in its devotion. He writes with the analytical precision of a trained scientist—Talmage was, notably, a geologist and academic—combined with the burning zeal of a prophet. Every chapter is fortified by extensive scriptural footnotes and rigorous historical context, ensuring that the work appeals as much to the inquiring intellect as it does to the pious heart. Yet, despite its scholarly rigor, the prose never loses its devotional warmth. When Talmage describes the agony in Gethsemane or the triumph of the resurrection, his language swells with a sacred cadence that invites deep contemplation and emotional resonance.

For the modern reader, *Jesus the Christ* is more than a historical artifact or a relic of early 20th-century publishing. It serves as an anchor for faith, providing a structured, comprehensive framework for understanding the identity and mission of Jesus of Nazareth through a unique Restoration lens. Whether read as a daily devotional companion, an intensive study guide, or an introduction to Latter-day Saint Christology, Talmage’s masterpiece continues to stand tall, challenging believers to draw closer to the Master through a deeper comprehension of His infinite atonement and boundless love.
