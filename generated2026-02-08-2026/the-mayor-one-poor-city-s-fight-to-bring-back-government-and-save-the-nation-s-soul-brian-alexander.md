# The Mayor: One Poor City’s Fight to Bring Back Government and Save the Nation’s Soul

**Author:** Brian Alexander  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-12-01

What to Expect from *The Mayor: One Poor City’s Fight to Bring Back Government and Save the Nation’s Soul*  

Brian Alexander, the author who garnered widespread acclaim for his immersive narrative *The Hospital*, returns with a new work that promises to be as timely as it is thought‑provoking. Set in Chester, Pennsylvania—a majority Black city just south of Philadelphia grappling with gun violence, crumbling infrastructure, a hospital on the brink of closure, and a reputation that has eroded public trust—*The Mayor* follows an unlikely coalition as they attempt to revive faith in government from the ground up.

At the heart of the story is Stefan Roots, a wastewater‑plant operator who decides to run for mayor. His campaign team is anything but conventional: a former kid drug dealer, a one‑time hip‑hop impresario, a white evangelical graduate student, and a wealthy white businessperson. Together, these political novices confront a century‑long legacy of mismanagement, exploring whether fresh faces and unconventional alliances can break the cycle of disinvestment and cynicism.

Alexander does not limit his gaze to Chester alone. He weaves in parallel portraits of activists and officeholders elsewhere—a quixotic former state legislator in Maine, West Virginia’s only elected transgender office holder, and a young Black Democratic Socialist in a predominantly white Philadelphia suburb—each striving, sometimes succeeding, to push back against the forces that have hollowed out communities nationwide. Through these intersecting narratives, the book examines how the deification of market capitalism and prolonged political malfeasance have fueled inequality, privatization, and profit‑seeking, ultimately shaping the national political landscape that gave rise to figures like Donald Trump.

Readers familiar with Alexander’s previous work can expect his signature blend of deep reporting, vivid character sketches, and a narrative that moves between the tragic and the hopeful. *The Mayor* promises to be an extraordinary lens through which to view America’s current crises, offering both a stark diagnosis and a glimpse of possible renewal.

If you admired *The Hospital* for its compassionate yet incisive look at institutional failure, you will likely find *The Mayor* an equally compelling read—one that challenges us to imagine how a single city’s struggle might illuminate a path forward for the entire nation.
