# Chosen Son (Fallen City Duology, #2)

**Author:** Adrienne Young  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

**Chosen Son: A Love Story That Will Shatter the Walls of Isara**

Adrienne Young’s *Chosen Son*—the long-awaited sequel to *Fallen City Duology* (2024)—promises to be the explosive, morally complex finale to a saga that has already captivated readers with its blend of political intrigue, forbidden romance, and divine stakes. Set in the walled city of Isara, where power is measured in bloodlines and rebellion simmers beneath the surface, this second installment delivers a narrative as sharp as the blades of the Philosopher’s Legion and as dangerous as the love between its two central figures.

At the heart of *Chosen Son* is Luca Matius, a legionnaire bound by duty to uphold his family’s legacy in the Forum. But when his noviceship with the city’s Philosopher thrusts him into a crisis that could rewrite Isara’s fate, he finds himself entangled in a web of secrets—and a woman whose defiance could either save or doom them all. Maris Casperia, raised in the shadow of the Citadel’s corrupt elite, has spent her life preparing for a life of service. Yet her years as a novice to the last Priestess of the Old War’s stolen magic have awakened in her a hunger for something more: a future where Isara is not ruled by fear, but by choice. Their meeting is not merely fateful—it is *divinely* ordained, setting in motion a chain of events that will force them to navigate a rebellion, a holy war, and the wrath of gods who have long played puppet masters over their lives.

What makes *Chosen Son* so gripping is its refusal to offer easy answers. Luca and Maris are not just lovers; they are pawns, strategists, and eventually, unwilling symbols of a conflict they did not create. Their alliance, born of necessity, will fracture under the weight of Isara’s collapsing order. As Luca is thrust into the role of rebellion’s face, Maris is pulled into the Citadel’s inner workings, where loyalty is a currency and betrayal is a survival tactic. The question isn’t just whether they can reunite—it’s whether they can do so without leaving the city in ruins.

Young’s writing has already proven she can weave romance with razor-sharp political tension (*Fallen City* was praised for its immersive world-building and morally ambiguous characters). *Chosen Son* takes that skill to new heights, delivering a story where love and power are both weapons and liabilities. The stakes are existential: a secret that could ignite a holy war, a rebellion that may or may not succeed, and a pair of protagonists who must decide how much they’re willing to sacrifice—for each other, for their city, or for the gods who have long dictated their destinies.

If you loved *Fallen City* for its lush, oppressive world or its morally complex heroes, *Chosen Son* will not disappoint. This is a story where every choice has consequences, where love is both a rebellion and a betrayal, and where the gods themselves seem to be pulling the strings. Prepare for a finale that will leave you breathless—because in Isara, the only thing more dangerous than the walls keeping people in is the truth that could tear them down.
