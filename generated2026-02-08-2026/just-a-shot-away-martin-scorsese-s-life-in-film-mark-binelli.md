# Just a Shot Away: Martin Scorsese's Life in Film

**Author:** Mark Binelli  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Expected publication 2026-11-03

**Just a Shot Away: The Unseen Life of Martin Scorsese Awaits—Mark Binelli’s Bold New Biography Arrives in 2026**

Martin Scorsese’s name is synonymous with cinema itself. A titan of the New Hollywood, a visionary who redefined the art of filmmaking with works like *Taxi Driver*, *Raging Bull*, and *The Departed*, the director’s career spans seven decades, leaving an indelible mark on every generation of filmmakers. But how much do we *really* know about the man behind the masterpieces? How did the asthmatic boy from Little Italy become the most influential filmmaker of his time? And what secrets lurk in the shadow of his legendary career?

On November 3, 2026, *Just a Shot Away: Martin Scorsese’s Life in Film*—the first major biography of the director in decades—will finally pull back the curtain. Written by Mark Binelli, a sharp-eyed chronicler of Hollywood’s most fascinating figures (*The New York Times* bestseller *The New Kings of New York*), this book promises to be nothing short of revelatory. Drawing on previously unseen sources, original research, and exclusive interviews with Scorsese himself, his family, and collaborators like Robbie Robertson, Robert De Niro, and Isabella Rossellini, Binelli crafts a portrait as dynamic and unpredictable as Scorsese’s best films.

### **From Little Italy to the Big Screen: The Making of a Legend**
The book begins where many of Scorsese’s films do—in the gritty, violent streets of mid-20th-century Little Italy, where a young Martin, battling asthma and a restless spirit, found his first escape in the movies. Binelli doesn’t just retell the familiar story of a boy obsessed with film; he immerses readers in the raw, unfiltered world of Scorsese’s childhood, where wiseguys, gangsters, and the flickering glow of drive-in theaters shaped his imagination. This isn’t just a biography—it’s a recreation of the sensory, chaotic environment that birthed a genius.

The transition from aspiring filmmaker to Hollywood powerhouse is explored with equal depth. Binelli traces Scorsese’s early struggles—his halting path to recognition, the personal demons that threatened to derail his career, and the wild, cocaine-fueled excesses of his early years. There are accounts of festival-hopping escapades, reclusive nights in the Mulholland Drive bachelor pad (shared with Robertson), and the behind-the-scenes chaos that fueled classics like *Alice Doesn’t Live Here Anymore* and *After Hours*—films that, while beloved, have often been overshadowed by Scorsese’s more famous works.

### **The Genius and the Grit: A Career That Defined an Era**
What makes *Just a Shot Away* so compelling isn’t just the personal revelations—it’s the way Binelli contextualizes Scorsese’s career within the broader history of cinema. From the tectonic shifts of New Hollywood to his later collaborations with studios and international co-productions, the book charts how Scorsese’s filmmaking evolved alongside the industry itself. Whether dissecting the psychological intensity of *Taxi Driver*, the brutal realism of *Raging Bull*, or the cinematic spectacle of *The Last Waltz*, Binelli doesn’t just analyze the films—he places them within the cultural moment that made them possible.

But this isn’t a hagiography. Binelli’s approach is unflinching. He doesn’t shy away from the controversies, the setbacks, or the moments when Scorsese’s personal life threatened to overshadow his art. Instead, he weaves these elements into a narrative that feels as alive and unpredictable as Scorsese’s best work.

### **Why This Biography Feels Like a Scorsese Film Itself**
If you’ve ever wondered what it was like to be in the room when *Raging Bull* was being shot—or to witness the creative friction between Scorsese and De Niro—this book delivers. Binelli’s prose is as sharp and immersive as Scorsese’s direction, blending archival research with firsthand accounts to create a portrait that feels both intimate and epic. It’s a book that doesn’t just inform—it *entertains*, much like the films it chronicles.

For fans of Scorsese, this is a must-read. For those new to his work, it’s an invitation into a world where cinema, life, and art collide in the most electrifying ways. And for anyone who loves a great story—especially one with the kind of drama, genius, and reinvention that only Scorsese could deliver—*Just a Shot Away* is already shaping up to be the definitive account of one of the greatest filmmakers of all time.

Mark Binelli has done it again. After capturing the rise and fall of the New York music scene in *The New Kings of New York* and chronicling the life of a legendary actor in *The Last Romantics*, he’s turned his keen eye toward the man who helped define modern cinema. The result? A book as bold, as layered, and as unforgettable as the films it celebrates.
