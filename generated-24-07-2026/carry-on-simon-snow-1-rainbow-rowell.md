# Carry On (Simon Snow, #1)

**Author:** Rainbow Rowell  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

**Carry On: A Love Story for the Chosen One Who Couldn’t Choose Himself**

Rainbow Rowell’s *Carry On* is a book that sneaks up on you—like a spell cast in the dark, its magic unfurling slowly until you realize you’re already under its spell. It’s a story about destiny, but not the kind that feels predetermined. It’s about the messy, contradictory, human heart of it all, wrapped in the trappings of a magical battle between good and evil, where the real war is fought in the quiet spaces between "I love you" and "I hate you."

At its core, *Carry On* is a love story—or rather, three love stories. There’s the love between Simon Snow and his roommate, Baz Pitch, a vampire with a sharp tongue and a sharper glare. Their relationship is the gravitational center of the book, a push-and-pull of animosity and longing that feels as old as magic itself. Then there’s Simon’s fraught, almost desperate love for his girlfriend, Agatha, a girl who can’t decide if she wants to be the heroine of his story or her own. And finally, there’s the love Simon has for himself—or rather, the love he’s still learning to give himself, despite the world (and his own self-doubt) telling him he’s not enough.

Simon Snow is the Chosen One who was chosen wrong. He’s the worst hero Watford School of Magicks has ever seen—his wand barely works, his magic is a disaster, and half the time, he’s more likely to set something on fire than save the day. But here’s the thing: Simon’s not a failure because he’s weak. He’s a failure because he’s been told his whole life that he’s supposed to be something he’s not. The prophecy, the destiny, the weight of expectation—it’s all a cage, and Simon is the one who finally realizes he doesn’t have to live inside it.

Baz, on the other hand, is everything Simon isn’t: confident, ruthless, effortlessly powerful. He’s also a vampire, which in this world means he’s not just a monster but a social pariah, a creature of night and hunger. Their dynamic is electric—part enemies, part frenemies, part something far more dangerous. Rowell writes their chemistry with a precision that borders on cruel. Every insult feels like a caress, every glare like a touch. Their love isn’t soft or easy; it’s hard-won, messy, and sometimes downright ugly. But it’s real. And in a book where so much feels like performance—Simon playing the hero, Baz playing the villain, the world playing along with the idea of destiny—Baz and Simon’s love is the one thing that doesn’t feel like an act.

Rowell’s style is deceptive in its simplicity. She writes with a conversational ease, as if she’s leaning over your shoulder, whispering secrets. But beneath that casual tone lies a razor-sharp wit and an unflinching emotional honesty. She doesn’t shy away from the ugly parts of love—jealousy, resentment, the way two people can love each other and still wound each other deeply. The dialogue crackles with energy, full of wit and subtext, and the pacing is relentless, pulling you through the story like a current.

What makes *Carry On* so special, though, is how it subverts expectations. It’s a fantasy novel, yes, but it’s also a ghost story, a mystery, and a coming-of-age tale all at once. The magic system is delightfully vague, the monsters are terrifying in their own right, and the stakes feel personal rather than world-ending. Rowell isn’t interested in grand battles or epic showdowns; she’s interested in the people caught in the middle of them. Simon’s struggle isn’t just against the Humdrum, the magic-eating monster wearing his face. It’s against the idea that he has to be someone he’s not to matter.

And that’s the heart of the book—the idea that we don’t have to carry the weight of destiny alone. Simon’s journey is one of self-acceptance, of learning that being the Chosen One doesn’t mean being perfect. It means being human. It means making mistakes, falling in love with the wrong people, and still finding a way to stand up when it matters most.

*Carry On* is a book for anyone who’s ever felt like an impostor, like they’re not enough, like they’re too much. It’s a story about the love that saves us—not because it’s perfect, but because it’s real. And in a world full of prophecies and chosen ones, that might just be the most magical thing of all.
