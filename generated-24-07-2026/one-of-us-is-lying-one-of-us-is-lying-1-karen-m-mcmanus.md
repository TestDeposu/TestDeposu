# One of Us Is Lying (One of Us is Lying, #1)

**Author:** Karen M. McManus  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Karen M. McManus’s *One of Us Is Lying* is a masterclass in modern YA mystery, blending the tension of a locked-room whodunit with the raw, messy dynamics of high school life. Published in 2017, this #1 *New York Times* bestseller doesn’t just ride the wave of its *Pretty Little Liars* meets *The Breakfast Club* premise—it elevates it, crafting a story that’s as much about secrets and survival as it is about the fragile facades we all wear.

At its core, *One of Us Is Lying* is a thriller about perception and deception. The premise is deceptively simple: five students enter detention, but only four leave alive. Simon, the school’s notorious gossip and creator of the app *About That*, collapses and dies during detention, his face turning an unnatural shade of purple. The official ruling? Anaphylaxis. But the students know better. Simon was about to expose each of them—Bronwyn, the overachiever with a Harvard-bound future; Addy, the golden girl with a hidden rebellious streak; Nate, the troubled bad boy on probation; and Cooper, the all-American athlete hiding a secret that could ruin him. When the police declare Simon’s death a murder, the four become prime suspects, each with motive, means, and a past they’d kill to keep buried.

McManus’s genius lies in her structure. The novel unfolds through alternating perspectives—Bronwyn, Addy, Nate, and Cooper—each narrating their own version of events while Simon’s posthumous posts, dripping with blackmail-worthy truths, lurk in the background like a ticking time bomb. This format isn’t just a gimmick; it’s a narrative device that forces the reader to question everything. Who is lying? Who is telling the partial truth? And who, in their desperation to protect their reputation, might have crossed a line from which there’s no return?

The book’s themes resonate deeply because they’re universal: the pressure to be perfect, the fear of exposure, and the lengths people go to when backed into a corner. Bronwyn’s meticulous planning for her future is undone by one impulsive decision; Addy’s blind trust in her boyfriend shatters when she discovers his double life; Nate’s criminal past isn’t as black-and-white as it seems; and Cooper’s identity crisis threatens to unravel his entire world. McManus doesn’t just write about secrets—she dissects the psychology behind them, making each character’s choices feel painfully real.

Her writing style is crisp and engaging, with a knack for dialogue that crackles with tension. The prose is clean but never simplistic, balancing the urgency of the mystery with the emotional weight of the characters’ struggles. There’s a reason this book has been adapted into a Peacock series—it’s cinematic in its pacing, with cliffhangers that demand you keep turning the pages. Yet, for all its thriller elements, *One of Us Is Lying* never loses sight of its characters. The mystery isn’t just about solving a crime; it’s about how these four disparate individuals, thrown together by circumstance, begin to see each other—and themselves—in a new light.

What makes this book stand out is its refusal to offer easy answers. The Bayview Four aren’t saints, nor are they villains—they’re flawed, relatable teenagers making terrible decisions under immense pressure. The twist isn’t just a reveal; it’s a gut-punch that reframes everything you thought you knew. And in a genre often criticized for relying on tropes, McManus delivers something fresh: a mystery where the real crime isn’t murder, but the way society polices young people’s lives, their mistakes, and their growth.

*One of Us Is Lying* is more than a page-turner—it’s a sharp, witty, and deeply human exploration of how far we’ll go to protect our secrets, and what happens when those secrets threaten to destroy us. It’s the kind of book that lingers long after the last page, leaving you to question not just who killed Simon, but how well you really know the people around you.
