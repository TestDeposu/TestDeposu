# The Infinite State (The Decurion Saga, #1)

**Author:** Richard Swan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2026

With The Infinite State, the explosive opening salvo of The Decurion Saga, international bestselling author Richard Swan pivots from the blood-soaked grimdark fantasy of his acclaimed Empire of the Wolf trilogy and sets his sights firmly upon the stars. Publishing in 2026, this blistering science fiction epic marks a bold new frontier for a writer already celebrated for his unflinching examinations of power, corruption, and the machinery of state control. Swan has crafted a gripping, high-stakes narrative that functions simultaneously as a pulse-pounding survival thriller and an eviscerating, timely critique of totalitarianism. 

At the pulsating heart of the novel is a chillingly oppressive interstellar hegemony ruled by the omnipresent governing party, Pater Aeternus. Citizens of the galaxy-spanning Decurion Empire are conditioned from birth to chant a liturgy of absolute submission. The ideological conditioning asks a rhetorical question that brooks no dissent: Who gives you life? The answer is hammered into the populace with terrifying regularity: Pater Aeternus. Who gives you purpose? Pater Aeternus. Who gives you joy? Pater Aeternus. Life under this regime is rigidly stratified, suffocatingly surveilled, and meticulously controlled. Existence here is something to be endured rather than lived, a bleak reality where individuality is systematically erased in favor of eternal servitude to the State.

Into this machinery of oppression steps Katherine Fuller, a woman standing at a catastrophic flashpoint in her life. Following the death of her husband—an esteemed, high-ranking member of the ruling party—Katherine inherits an estate of unfathomable wealth. In a lesser novel, this would simply be a story of grief and privilege. But in Swan’s capable hands, this fortune becomes the catalyst for a dangerous, galaxy-altering conspiracy. Armed with vast resources, Katherine conceives a vision so radical, so profoundly heretical to the Decurion Empire, that the mere whisper of it is punishable by death: she intends to purchase a virgin world and forge a democracy. 

To achieve the impossible, Katherine must navigate a treacherous web of espionage and alliance-building, enlisting a disparate trio of souls similarly bruised by the regime. Alongside a widowed party member, the narrative weaves the harrowing journeys of a disgraced investigator and a reckless hypersled pilot. Each brings their own desperate motivations and fatal flaws to the enterprise, transforming a simple act of defiance into a tense, multi-perspective powder keg. As this fragile coalition plots their escape from the suffocating authority of the fascist state, Swan masterfully ramps up the tension, ensuring that every secret whispered and every alliance forged feels perpetually on the brink of catastrophic exposure.

True to form, Swan’s literary style is razor-sharp, relentless, and unflinching. He does not merely build a world; he constructs an entire sociopolitical panopticon and invites the reader to suffocate within its well-oiled gears. The prose is lean and muscular, driving the plot forward with an unrelenting momentum that mirrors the frantic desperation of its protagonists. Yet, beneath the breakneck pacing and adrenaline-fueled set pieces—particularly those involving the dizzying mechanics of hypersled piloting—lies a profound intellectual rigor. Swan uses the vastness of space to expose the claustrophobia of absolute power, dissecting how authoritarian regimes co-opt language, memory, and human emotion to maintain dominance. 

The conflict in The Infinite State transcends the physical battle for survival. It is an ideological war waged between the monolithic, crushing weight of eternal autocracy and the fragile, glowing ember of human self-determination. The Empire, naturally, cannot allow its wayward daughter to succeed. As Katherine’s clandestine project takes shape, she is forced to discover exactly how far Pater Aeternus is willing to go to protect its monopoly on existence. The answer is terrifying, serving as a dark mirror to our own historical and contemporary anxieties surrounding surveillance, freedom, and the fragility of democratic institutions.

With The Infinite State, Richard Swan proves that his transition to science fiction is nothing short of triumphant. He delivers a masterclass in speculative world-building and character-driven suspense, anchored by a central premise that is as inspiring as it is perilous. For readers hungry for intelligent, hard-hitting sci-fi that refuses to shy away from the darker corners of human nature and political governance, this is an unmissable opening chapter to a saga that is destined to dominate the genre.
