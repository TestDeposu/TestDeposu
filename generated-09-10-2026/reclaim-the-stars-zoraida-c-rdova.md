# Reclaim the Stars

**Author:** Zoraida Córdova  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

To look at the stars is to map our own desires, fears, and histories onto the infinite dark. In the dazzling speculative anthology *Reclaim the Stars*, curated and edited by acclaimed author Zoraida Córdova and published in 2022, seventeen visionary talents do precisely this—and so much more. This collection takes the sprawling richness of the Latin American diaspora and launches it light-years ahead, sinks it into haunted Argentine soil, and dives it deep into Caribbean coastal waters. It is an extraordinary testament to the fact that speculative fiction is not a monolith, but a boundless cosmos waiting to be fully inhabited.

At the heart of this collection lies a unifying and urgent thematic current: reclamation. For too long, mainstream sci-fi and fantasy have relegated marginalized voices to the periphery, treating them as token additions rather than architects of new worlds. Córdova and her stellar lineup of contributors dismantle these borders with unapologetic joy, rage, and wonder. The stories confront themes of displacement, identity, and cultural heritage, but they do so through the lens of the impossible. Here, princesses wage interstellar warfare without losing the warmth of their roots, swamps whisper ancient secrets in cadence with contemporary heartbreaks, and mermaids guard histories that colonial powers tried to erase. The overarching message is clear: our folklore, our languages, and our people do not merely belong in the future and in fantasy—they define them.

Zoraida Córdova’s editorial vision anchors the anthology, ensuring that while the settings range from dystopian futures to magical realism, the stylistic heartbeat remains remarkably cohesive. The authors featured—a powerhouse roster including Vita Ayala, David Bowles, J.C. Cervantes, Sara Faring, Romina Garber, Isabel Ibañez, Anna-Marie McLemore, Yamile Saied Méndez, Nina Moreno, Circe Moskowitz, Maya Motayne, Linda Raquel Nieves Pérez, Daniel José Older, Claribel A. Ortega, Mark Oshiro, and Lilliam Rivera—bring distinct narrative textures to the table. Some stories lean into high-octane space opera with sharp, cinematic pacing, while others unfold with the lyrical, haunting cadence of oral folklore passed down through generations. 

What makes the style of *Reclaim the Stars* so striking is its refusal to pressurize its cultural elements into easily digestible boxes for the uninitiated. Spanish phrases drift naturally through dialogue, cultural touchstones are deployed without heavy-handed exposition, and mythology is woven into the narrative DNA with casual reverence. The prose across the board is vivid and visceral. Whether describing the neon-lit corridors of a distant spaceship or the humid, heavy air of a Latin American folklore-infused swamp, the writing pulls you entirely into its sensory landscape. 

The pacing of the anthology is expertly balanced. It moves fluidly between the pulse-pounding urgency of rebellion and the quiet, introspective grief of ghosts holding onto unfinished business. Readers will find themselves traversing time and space, moving seamlessly from speculative retellings to wholly original myths crafted by some of the most exciting storytellers working today. It honors the diversity of the diaspora not by painting it with a single brush, but by celebrating its infinite, colorful fractures and intersections.

Ultimately, *Reclaim the Stars* is more than just a brilliant assembly of short fiction; it is a cultural landmark for young adult and speculative literature. It proves that when we hand the pen to those who have historically been written out of the narrative, the resulting stories are not just entertaining—they are revolutionary. For anyone yearning for fiction that stretches beyond the tired tropes of traditional fantasy and science fiction, this anthology is an essential journey. Open these pages, look up at the constellations of new possibilities, and prepare to have your universe permanently expanded.
