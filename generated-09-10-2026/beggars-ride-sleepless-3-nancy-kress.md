# Beggars Ride (Sleepless, #3)

**Author:** Nancy Kress  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

**Beggars Ride: A Dystopian Masterpiece Where Sleep Is the Ultimate Privilege**

Nancy Kress’s *Beggars Ride*—the third installment in her *Sleepless* trilogy—is a razor-sharp exploration of what it means to be human in a world that has traded comfort for control. Leisha Camden, a genetically enhanced "Sleepless," is more than just a superhuman; she is a symbol of humanity’s desperate gambles with evolution. Her ability to function without sleep, paired with an elevated intellect and potential immortality, makes her both a marvel and a target. But *Beggars Ride* isn’t just about the science—it’s a searing critique of power, identity, and the cost of progress.

Kress, a master of speculative fiction, weaves a narrative that feels urgent and inevitable. The Sleepless are not just a biological experiment; they are a societal experiment, one that forces the reader to confront uncomfortable questions: *Who gets to decide what humanity should become?* The world of *Beggars Ride* is one where sleep is a luxury, a privilege reserved for the "normal" population, while the Sleepless—brilliant, relentless, and increasingly rebellious—are either revered or reviled. The tension between these factions is electric, driven by Leisha’s defiance and the looming threat of persecution.

What makes *Beggars Ride* stand out is Kress’s unflinching realism. She doesn’t shy away from the ethical dilemmas of genetic modification, the psychological toll of immortality, or the ways power corrupts even the most well-intentioned systems. The Sleepless are not just superhumans; they are flawed, complex individuals grappling with their own humanity. Leisha’s journey is one of resistance, not just against oppression, but against the dehumanizing forces of a world that sees her as either a god or a monster.

Kress’s prose is lean, precise, and full of tension. She excels at building suspense, whether through political intrigue, scientific discovery, or personal drama. The world-building is immersive, with a richly detailed society where sleep deprivation is both a weapon and a weaponized privilege. The dialogue crackles with intelligence and urgency, making the stakes feel immediate.

At its core, *Beggars Ride* is a story about rebellion. The Sleepless are not just fighting for survival; they are fighting for the right to exist as they are, unaltered by the world’s fear. Leisha’s defiance is contagious, and her determination to challenge the status quo makes her one of the most compelling protagonists in near-future sci-fi.

For fans of thought-provoking dystopian fiction, *Beggars Ride* is a must-read. It’s a book that lingers long after the last page, forcing the reader to question their own assumptions about progress, power, and what it means to be human. Nancy Kress has once again delivered a story that is as intellectually stimulating as it is emotionally gripping—a testament to her ability to blend hard science with deep human themes.
