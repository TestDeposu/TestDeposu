# Dragon and Slave (Dragonback, #3)

**Author:** Timothy Zahn  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

Timothy Zahn has long established himself as a master of meticulous plotting and high-stakes science fiction, notably through his legendary contributions to the Star Wars expanded universe. In Dragon and Slave, the third installment of his inventive Dragonback series, Zahn continues to refine the winning formula that blends buddy-cop camaraderie, corporate espionage, and classic space opera adventure. This volume sees our dynamic duo—fugitive teenager Jack Morgan and Draycos, a symbiote K’da warrior who lives on Jack's back as a biomorphic tattoo—plunge even deeper into the galaxy-spanning conspiracy threatening the alien’s entire race. 

At the heart of the novel lies a compelling exploration of symbiosis, trust, and resilience. The central theme of the book examines what it truly means to be bound to another living soul. Jack and Draycos are literally inseparable, sharing a physical and mental connection that forces them to rely on each other for survival. Zahn uses this forced partnership to explore the evolution of their relationship from cautious acquaintances to brothers-in-arms. As Jack finds himself voluntarily sold into slavery on a heavily guarded Brummga estate to extract vital intelligence, the theme of sacrifice takes center stage. Jack's willingness to endure brutality for a greater cause highlights the moral maturity underpinning Zahn's juvenile and young adult science fiction, ensuring the narrative resonates with older readers as well.

Zahn’s authorial style remains one of his greatest assets. He is a writer who respects his readers' intelligence, favoring intricate problem-solving over mindless action. Even when the stakes are at their highest, Zahn slows down the pace to let the characters strategize, analyze environments, and exploit logical loopholes. In Dragon and Slave, this manifests as a tense, high-tech infiltration wrapped inside a grueling survival scenario. The introduction of the Brummga—brutish, physically formidable aliens traditionally underestimated by the galaxy—allows Zahn to play with expectations. Instead of relying solely on brute strength, Jack must navigate a society governed by strict hierarchies and formidable computer systems, turning the narrative into a clever sci-fi heist. 

The pacing is brisk, peppered with Zahn’s trademark snappy dialogue and sharp banter between Jack and the culturally rigid Draycos. The internal dynamic between the human fugitive, trying to clear his own name of a framed crime, and the alien warrior provides both high dramatic tension and moments of genuine levity. Zahn excels at world-building through action; rather than dumping exposition, he reveals the lore of the K’da and the mechanics of this universe organically as Jack and Draycos encounter new obstacles.

Dragon and Slave is a thoroughly entertaining chapter in an underrated YA sci-fi series. It delivers clever plotting, high-octane suspense, and a heartwarming core relationship that anchors the cosmic scale of the conflict. For fans of smart, tactical science fiction and classic space adventures where brains triumph over brawn, Timothy Zahn proves once again why he is a master of the craft.
