# Cold the Night, Fast the Wolves (Edge Worlds, #1)

**Author:** Meg Long  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

Meg Long’s debut novel, Cold the Night, Fast the Wolves, hurls readers headfirst into a merciless, frozen wasteland where survival is not a given, but a daily, bloody negotiation. Set on a treacherous ice-bound planet, this gripping YA science fiction adventure blends the high-octane tension of a classic endurance race with the raw, emotional resonance of a found-family story. Long does not ease her audience into this world; instead, she drops us directly into the sub-zero wind chill, forcing us to feel the bite of the frost right alongside her characters.

At the narrative’s frozen heart is seventeen-year-old Sena Korhosen, a survivor hardened by grief and isolation. Five years after losing her mothers to the planet's brutal sled race, Sena wants nothing to do with the icy tracks. Yet, desperation makes strange bedfellows of bad choices. After double-crossing a local gangster, Sena finds herself on the run, clutching her only ticket to freedom: a fierce, feral fighting wolf named Iska. What follows is a high-stakes migration across a landscape engineered to kill them. To secure passage off-world with a team of opportunistic scientists, Sena must weaponize her inherited survival skills and guide them across the finish line. 

The book’s central theme is inextricably bound to its harsh setting: trust as a vital survival currency. In a world where the environment—populated by ice goblins and giant bears—wants you dead, and where human predators lurk around every snowdrift, isolation is a death sentence. Long brilliantly mirrors Sena's internal healing through her relationship with Iska. The bond that forges between the human girl and the abused, lethal wolf is the emotional anchor of the novel. It is not an instant, magical taming, but a slow, cautious dance of mutual respect and shared desperation. Through Iska, Sena learns to let down her armor, discovering that vulnerability is not weakness, but the ultimate catalyst for endurance. 

Long’s writing style is kinetic, breathless, and intensely atmospheric. She employs stark, sensory prose that makes the cold practically tangible. You can hear the howling winds, smell the ozone and copper of the frozen tundra, and feel the burning ache of exhaustion in your own muscles as the sleds race forward. The pacing is relentless, echoing the ticking clock of the race itself. Action sequences are choreographed with precision, balancing the kinetic thrill of the chase with the claustrophobic dread of the wilderness. 

While the narrative hits familiar beats of the dystopian survival genre—the corrupt authority figures, the ticking--clock escape, the ragtag group of allies—Long elevates these tropes through sheer atmospheric immersion and the dynamic between Sena and Iska. The world-building is gritty and lived-in, avoiding over-exposition in favor of letting the brutal environment speak for itself. 

Cold the Night, Fast the Wolves is a striking, teeth-gritting debut that proves Meg Long is a formidable new voice in speculative fiction. It is a story about outrunning your past, confronting the bitterest elements of nature and human nature alike, and realizing that sometimes, the only way through the dark is together. For fans of high-stakes survival stories and ferocious animal companions, this icy thrill-ride is an absolute must-read.
