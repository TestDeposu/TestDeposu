# The Stowaway (Roswell High, #6)

**Author:** Melinda Metz  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2000

**The Stowaway: A Haunting Love Story of Trust, Secrets, and the Cost of Survival**

Melinda Metz’s *The Stowaway*—the sixth installment in the *Roswell High* series—is a gripping, emotionally charged tale that lingers long after the final page. Set against the backdrop of Roswell’s supernatural intrigue, this novel dives deep into the psychological and moral complexities of trust, betrayal, and the fragile nature of love in the face of danger. While the series is often celebrated for its blend of romance and the paranormal, *The Stowaway* stands out as a more introspective, character-driven story, where the stakes are personal rather than just fantastical.

At its core, the novel follows Michael, a young man haunted by his escape from a sinister underground compound. His mind is a battleground of flashbacks, each one a reminder of the horrors he endured—and the secrets he’s sworn to keep. Then there’s Cameron, the enigmatic girl he met behind bars. She’s the only one who seems to understand him, who feels the weight of his silence as if it were her own. But Cameron’s past is far more complicated than it appears. She fell for Michael while spying on him for Sheriff Valenti, a man whose methods were as ruthless as they were effective. Now, torn between loyalty to her former handlers and the love she’s begun to feel for Michael, she grapples with a guilt that feels inescapable. The question isn’t just whether she can tell Michael the truth—it’s whether she can survive the consequences of doing so.

Metz’s writing here is a masterclass in tension. She doesn’t rely on jump scares or over-the-top supernatural spectacle to keep readers on edge; instead, she crafts a slow-burning dread that thrives on psychological realism. The compound’s horrors are implied rather than explicitly detailed, leaving their impact to seep into the reader’s imagination. Michael’s trauma is rendered with raw honesty, his internal monologues a testament to the way fear and memory can distort reality. Meanwhile, Cameron’s internal conflict is rendered with a nuance that makes her one of the most compelling characters in the series. She’s not a villain, nor is she a saint—she’s a girl caught between duty and desire, her choices forcing her to confront the cost of her actions.

What makes *The Stowaway* particularly striking is how it explores the theme of trust as both a weapon and a vulnerability. Michael trusts Cameron with his secrets, but can he trust her intentions? Cameron trusts her own judgment, but can she trust the system that once controlled her? The novel doesn’t offer easy answers. Instead, it forces readers to sit with the ambiguity of moral gray areas, where love and loyalty collide, and where the line between hero and traitor is blurred. This isn’t just a story about escaping a compound—it’s about the aftermath, the lingering scars, and the impossible choices that define us long after the danger has passed.

Metz’s prose is clean but evocative, her dialogue natural and sharp. She has a knack for creating characters who feel real, flawed, and deeply relatable. The romance between Michael and Cameron is tender but not saccharine; it’s a love that grows from understanding, not just attraction. And while the supernatural elements of *Roswell High* are ever-present, *The Stowaway* grounds them in the human experience, making the stakes feel personal rather than just fantastical.

For fans of the series, this book is a welcome return to the emotional depth that made *Roswell High* so beloved. It’s a story about survival, not just of the body, but of the soul—and the painful, necessary process of rebuilding trust in a world that has already betrayed you. If you’ve ever wondered what happens after the escape, after the secrets are uncovered, after the love is tested, *The Stowaway* delivers a haunting, thought-provoking answer.

In a series often criticized for its reliance on formula, *The Stowaway* proves that Melinda Metz still has the ability to craft a story that lingers in the mind long after the last page. It’s a testament to the power of character-driven drama, where the real magic isn’t in the monsters beneath the surface, but in the people struggling to find their way back to the light.
