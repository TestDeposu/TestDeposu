# The Dragon Conspiracy (SPI Files, #2)

**Author:** Lisa Shearin  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

Urban fantasy thrives on high stakes, snappy dialogue, and a protagonist who can juggle saving the world while rolling her eyes at the absurdity of it all. In The Dragon Conspiracy, the second installment of Lisa Shearin’s acclaimed SPI Files series, the author doubles down on the winning formula established in The Grendel Affair, delivering a breathless, high-octane romp through a hidden, magical Manhattan. 

The narrative kicks off with a brilliantly chaotic sequence: a high-society gala at the Metropolitan Museum of Art interrupted not by jewel thieves in tuxedos, but by a trio of shrieking, flesh-ripping harpies. They aren't after ordinary bling; they steal the Dragon Eggs, a cluster of seven cursed diamonds possessing apocalyptic potential. If utilized by the wrong mage, these gems could effectively wipe out the entire supernatural population of the tristate area. Enter Supernatural Protection and Investigations (SPI), the covert agency tasked with keeping the peace between humanity and the things that go bump in the night. 

At the heart of the novel is Makenna Fraser, a seer whose gift for glimpsing the threads of probability makes her an invaluable asset, even if it leaves her perpetually exhausted and nursing a fresh set of bruises. Shearin’s choice of a first-person perspective grounds the chaotic magical world in Makenna’s dry wit and pragmatic resilience. Alongside her partner, Ian—a centuries-old rakshasa with a penchant for impeccably tailored suits and lethal violence—Makenna embarks on a frantic, twenty-four-hour race against the clock to recover the stones.

The central theme of The Dragon Conspiracy is the fragile coexistence of hidden societies and the weaponization of identity. The threat posed by the Dragon Eggs is not merely physical; it is existential. It targets the very essence of what makes vampires, werewolves, and other supernaturals who they are, reframing a classic heist plot into a deeply personal fight for survival. Shearin uses this premise to explore loyalty, corporate espionage within the supernatural underworld, and the heavy burden of responsibility carried by those on the front lines of a secret war.

Shearin’s writing style is brisk, cinematic, and relentlessly entertaining. She has a remarkable talent for world-building that feels lived-in rather than info-dumped. New York City is transformed into a sprawling, multi-layered urban jungle where fae, mages, and monsters rub shoulders with unsuspecting tourists. The action scenes are choreographed with precision, moving at a cinematic pace that matches the ticking clock of Makenna’s twenty-four-hour deadline. Yet, it is the character chemistry that truly elevates the book. The banter between Makenna and Ian crackles with sharp humor and underlying tension, providing a steady emotional anchor amidst the magical mayhem.

While the novel leans heavily into familiar urban fantasy tropes—the ticking-clock narrative, the cynical yet moral protagonist, the wisecracking supernatural sidekick—Shearin executes them with such craft and enthusiasm that the journey feels entirely fresh. The pacing rarely lets up, offering readers barely enough time to catch their breath before plunging Makenna into yet another perilous encounter.

The Dragon Conspiracy is a stellar sophomore outing that proves Shearin’s universe has long-term staying power. It delivers everything fans of contemporary fantasy could hope for: high-stakes thrills, razor-sharp wit, and a heroine you cannot help but root for. If you like your magic fast-paced, dangerous, and served with a heavy side of sarcasm, Shearin's latest file is an absolute must-read.
