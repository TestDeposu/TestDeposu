# The Bloody Crown of Conan (Conan the Cimmerian, #2)

**Author:** Robert E. Howard  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2004

To read Robert E. Howard is to step into a primal theater of blood, sweat, and ancient sorcery where civilization is always just one heartbeat away from a dark and howling abyss. With *The Bloody Crown of Conan*, published posthumously in 2004, readers are treated to a magnificent volume that captures the Cimmerian at the absolute height of his literary evolution. This collection does not merely offer tales of wandering thievery; it showcases the brooding, complex transition of a scarred barbarian into a pragmatic, battle-hardened monarch. 

At the core of this collection lies a compelling and pervasive theme: the eternal, brutal friction between untamed nature and decadent civilization. Howard was never merely an escapist; he possessed a visceral, almost anthropological fascination with the rise and fall of empires. In stories like *The People of the Black Circle*, *The Hour of the Dragon*, and *A Witch Shall Be Born*, Conan is pitted against the insidious rot of ancient kingdoms, treacherous viziers, immortal sorcerers, and manipulative queens. Yet, time and again, it is the barbarian’s unyielding will, raw physical prowess, and unapologetic vitality that outlasts the fragile, decaying monuments of civilized man. Conan does not conquer out of a desire for bureaucratic governance, but because his sheer presence demands dominance over a hostile universe.

Howard’s literary style is the pulsating heartbeat of sword and sorcery, a genre he single-handedly forged and defined. As Stephen King aptly noted, Howard’s prose glows with a fierce, eldritch light born of a frenzied, breathless intensity. There is a palpable rhythm to his sentences—a relentless, galloping cadence that mirrors the thundering hooves of Cimmerian warhorses. His descriptions are lush, visceral, and dripping with atmospheric dread. When Howard describes a shadowy citadel, a rotting corpse brought to unnatural life, or the blistering sands of a desert death-march, the reader does not simply visualize the scene; they feel the grit of the dust, smell the ozone of dark magic, and hear the clashing of heavy steel. 

What makes *The Bloody Crown of Conan* an essential artifact for both longtime devotees and curious newcomers is its editorial integrity. By presenting two of these masterworks directly from Howard’s original typescripts, alongside a treasure trove of unpublished outlines, notes, and drafts, the volume acts as an archaeological dig into the author's fevered imagination. We get to witness the raw ore of Howard’s creative process before it was smoothed by later hands. 

This collection proves that Conan is much more than a pop-culture cliché of a muscle-bound brute. He is a brooding philosopher of the battlefield, a force of nature personified, and the ultimate literary embodiment of survival against impossible odds. Howard’s prose remains as intoxicating and lethal as a poisoned blade, cutting straight through the pretension of modern fantasy to touch something ancient, wild, and deeply human within us all.
