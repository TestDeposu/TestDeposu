# Hold Me Closer, Necromancer (Necromancer, #1)

**Author:** Lish McBride  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

Lish McBride’s 2010 debut novel, Hold Me Closer, Necromancer, storms into the urban fantasy genre with a refreshingly irreverent voice, turning the typical dark and brooding tropes of magic on their head. At its core, the novel explores the theme of identity and inheritance, asking a compelling question: What happens when the life you thought you were building is suddenly upended by a bloodline you never knew you had? 

The protagonist, Sam, begins his journey as an utterly ordinary slacker flipping burgers at a Seattle fast-food joint. His world is mundane, grounded in the struggles of minimum-wage work and everyday boredom. However, McBride uses this grounded beginning to sharply contrast the bizarre, high-stakes paranormal reality that crashes into Sam's life when he crosses paths with Douglas, a sadistic necromancer. This inciting incident sets off a frantic, week-long race for survival that tests Sam’s latent abilities and forces him to reconcile his newfound paranormal heritage with his human sensibilities. 

McBride’s greatest stylistic triumph lies in her dialogue and narrative tone. Eschewing the melodrama often found in supernatural YA fiction, she infuses Sam’s perspective with biting sarcasm, pop-culture-infused wit, and a distinctly cynical charm. Even in moments of extreme peril—when raising the dead for cash or dodging homicidal magic users—the prose maintains a breezy, conversational momentum. Seattle itself acts as a vital character in the book; McBride paints the Pacific Northwest not as a gothic backdrop, but as a bustling, coffee-fueled ecosystem where werewolves, witches, and necromancers mingle just beneath the surface of everyday society. 

The novel thrives on its dynamic character relationships. Sam is not a chosen one who welcomes his destiny with open arms; he is reluctant, overwhelmed, and remarkably human, which makes his growth all the more rewarding. The antagonist, Douglas, provides a chilling counterbalance to Sam’s laid-back demeanor, offering a genuine sense of threat that keeps the pacing tight and the tension high. 

Hold Me Closer, Necromancer succeeds because it balances genuine danger with laugh-out-loud humor. It is a story about claiming your own agency in a world that tries to write your script for you, wrapped in a fast-paced supernatural adventure. For readers looking for a clever, bite-sized urban fantasy that doesn't take itself too seriously while still delivering high stakes, McBride’s debut remains an absolute must-read.
