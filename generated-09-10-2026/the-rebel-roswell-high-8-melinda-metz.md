# The Rebel (Roswell High, #8)

**Author:** Melinda Metz  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2002

Trust, betrayal, and the agonizing ache of belonging anchor The Rebel, the eighth installment in Melinda Metz’s iconic Roswell High series. Published in 2002, this taut young adult novel captures the high-stakes emotional turbulence that defined the turn-of-the-millennium sci-fi phenomenon. Metz wastes no time thrusting her characters into moral quicksand, scaling down epic alien mythology into deeply intimate questions of loyalty and identity. 

The narrative’s central thematic engine revolves around the fragile nature of family and the lengths to which an outcast will go to secure it. For seven books, Michael Guerin has played the role of the hardened rebel, a solitary figure armored by cynicism and defensive fury. Yet, beneath the leather jacket lies a deep, aching void—a desperate hunger for blood kin. When that dream finally materializes with the arrival of his brother, Trevor, Metz masterfully exposes Michael’s greatest vulnerability. The tough-talking alien is suddenly reduced to a hopeful kid desperate for approval, blinded by the very affection he has craved for years. 

Metz’s writing style thrives on this friction between external supernatural peril and internal psychological pressure. Her prose is brisk, conversational, and emotionally urgent, perfectly calibrated for a readership navigating the treacherous waters of adolescence. The author does not grant her characters easy grace. Instead, she forces Michael into a crushing dilemma: choose the brother who validates his existence, or protect the human friends who form his true, chosen family. This tension is brilliantly amplified by a parallel subplot involving Maria, whose little brother is kidnapped. Maria’s spiraling guilt and desperate reliance on a distracted Michael inject a gritty, noir-like realism into the sci-fi framework, proving that the mundane heartbreaks of life can be just as devastating as interstellar warfare.

What makes The Rebel stand out in the Roswell High canon is its refusal to rely on black-and-white morality. Trevor is not a mustache-twirling villain, nor is he a savior; he is painted in shades of gray, leaving both Michael and the reader guessing his true motivations until the very edge of the precipice. Metz keeps the pacing relentless, utilizing cliffhangers and shifting loyalties to create an atmosphere of paranoia where no one is entirely safe, least of all the protagonist. 

Ultimately, Melinda Metz delivers a poignant critique of how easily our deepest desires can be weaponized against us. The Rebel is a fast-paced, emotionally resonant thriller that reminds fans why Roswell High remains a beloved touchstone of YA fiction. It asks a timeless, haunting question: when you finally find where you come from, what do you do when it asks you to destroy who you’ve become?
