# Last Call (Fault Lines, #1)

**Author:** Tim Powers  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

**Last Call: Tim Powers Weaves History, Queer Courage, and Gothic Atmosphere into a Masterful Tale of Resistance**

Tim Powers, the master of blending historical intrigue with supernatural dread, delivers another electrifying work with *Last Call*—the first installment in the *Fault Lines* series. Set against the smoldering backdrop of 1984’s miners’ strike in South Wales, this novel is not merely a historical drama but a searing exploration of solidarity, identity, and the quiet revolutions that shape history. Powers, known for his lush prose and ability to fuse fact with fiction, crafts a story that is as politically charged as it is hauntingly atmospheric.

At its core, *Last Call* is a story of unlikely alliances. In a Britain ravaged by Margaret Thatcher’s austerity measures and the devastating rise of AIDS, a group of London’s defiant queer community—artists, activists, and outsiders—ventures into the coal-mining valleys of South Wales. There, they find not just a cause to support but a community fighting for its very survival. The miners, embattled by strikebreakers, police brutality, and economic collapse, are initially wary of the city-dwelling "perverts" who, in the eyes of the press and government, are already scapegoats for a plague. Yet, as the novel unfolds, a fragile but profound friendship emerges. The queer activists, armed with more than just solidarity, help sustain the valley through food drives, medical aid, and sheer moral support—while the miners, in turn, offer a radical acceptance that challenges the very foundations of homophobia and class division.

Powers’ strength lies in his ability to make history feel visceral. The 1984 miners’ strike was a pivotal moment in British labor history, a brutal clash between the state and the working class that echoed globally. But Powers doesn’t just recount it—he immerses the reader in the gritty reality of picket lines, blacked-out villages, and the psychological toll of deprivation. The novel’s setting is as much a character as any of its people: the damp, coal-stained valleys, the flickering glow of miners’ lamps, the suffocating weight of Thatcher’s iron grip. Yet, beneath the political tension, Powers weaves in elements of the supernatural, a signature of his work. Whispers of the old Welsh folklore linger, and the specter of AIDS looms like a curse, blurring the line between historical tragedy and something far more eerie.

The queer characters in *Last Call* are not mere footnotes to history—they are fully realized, flawed, and resilient. Powers portrays them with a mix of tenderness and grit, capturing the fear, the defiance, and the quiet joy of a community under siege. Their journey from outsiders to allies is both inspiring and heartbreaking, a testament to the power of collective action. The novel doesn’t shy away from the complexities of queer life in the 1980s—the stigma, the internalized shame, the fragile hope—but it also celebrates the bonds that form in the face of adversity.

Powers’ prose is rich and immersive, his descriptions painting a vivid picture of a Britain on the brink. His dialogue crackles with authenticity, whether it’s the raw passion of a miner’s speech or the sharp wit of a queer activist. The novel’s pacing is masterful, balancing the political drama with moments of quiet intimacy, ensuring that the reader is never just a spectator but an active participant in the unfolding story.

What makes *Last Call* particularly compelling is its theme of resistance. It’s a story about two marginalized groups—miners and queers—who, despite everything, choose to stand together. In doing so, they challenge not just the government’s policies but the very idea of who is "worthy" of dignity. The novel’s subtext is clear: solidarity is not just a moral choice but a revolutionary act. It’s a reminder that history is not just written by the powerful but by those who refuse to be erased.

Tim Powers has always been a writer who understands the intersection of the personal and the political. *Last Call* is no exception. It’s a novel that demands to be read—not just for its historical significance, but for its emotional resonance. It’s a story of courage, of the small acts of defiance that can change the world, and of the people who refuse to be broken.

If you’ve ever wondered how history is made by ordinary people in extraordinary times, *Last Call* is the book to read. It’s a gripping, atmospheric, and deeply moving tale that proves Powers remains one of the most compelling voices in contemporary historical fiction.
