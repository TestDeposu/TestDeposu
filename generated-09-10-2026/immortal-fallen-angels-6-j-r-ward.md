# Immortal (Fallen Angels, #6)

**Author:** J.R. Ward  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

Immortal, the sixth installment of J.R. Ward’s Fallen Angels saga, thrusts readers back into a cosmic chess match where the Creator’s game hinges on seven souls standing at seven crossroads. Jim Heron—half‑damned, half‑human, haunted by the losses he’s already incurred—finds himself once more teetering on the edge of annihilation. His resolve to shield Sissy, the innocent he wrested from Hell, becomes both his greatest strength and the exploitable crack that the demon Devina is eager to widen. Ward’s narrative pulses with a relentless urgency; every scene feels like a blow‑by‑blow account of a battle that could tip the balance between salvation and oblivion.

The novel’s core theme is the paradox of redemption through vulnerability. Jim’s journey forces him to confront the hollowness inside him—a heart he believes he no longer possesses—and to discover whether genuine love can resurrect something he has long deemed dead. Ward treats this inner conflict not as a mere subplot but as the engine driving the external stakes. As Jim wrestles with the choice between a suicidal plunge into Purgatory’s shadows and the safer, more selfish path of self‑preservation, the reader is invited to ponder what it truly means to save humanity when one’s own soul feels beyond repair.

Stylistically, Ward remains a master of gritty, cinematic prose. Her sentences are sharp and tactile, favoring concrete images over flowery abstraction: the scent of brimstone in a back‑alley, the echo of a blade sliding through flesh, the tremor in a lover’s whisper. Dialogue crackles with the same intensity that defines her Black Dagger Brotherhood series, yet here it is sharpened by a mythic undertone that gives the Fallen Angels universe its distinctive flavor. World‑building is economical but effective; Ward drops readers directly into the fray, trusting them to pick up the rules of the Creator’s game through action rather than exposition. This approach keeps the pacing brisk, ensuring that the novel’s 400‑plus pages never feel bogged down by lore dumps.

What sets Immortal apart from its predecessors is the heightened emotional resonance that accompanies the series’ trademark action. While earlier books delighted in the spectacle of angelic warfare and vampiric intrigue, this entry leans into the quiet, aching moments between battles—Jim’s hesitant touches, Sissy’s wary trust, the flicker of hope that surfaces when darkness seems absolute. Ward balances these tender interludes with the brutal, unflinching violence that fans expect, creating a rhythm that makes the eventual climax feel both inevitable and hard‑won.

In sum, Immortal delivers a potent mix of high‑stakes supernatural conflict and intimate, soul‑searching drama. Ward’s signature blend of ruthless pacing, visceral detail, and heartfelt character work shines through, offering both longtime followers of the Fallen Angels series and newcomers a compelling reason to stay up late turning pages. The novel asks a haunting question: can a creature bereft of heart and soul be saved by something he refuses to believe in? The answer, as Ward shows, lies not in divine intervention but in the courage to love despite the odds.
