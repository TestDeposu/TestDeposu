# Patrimony (Pip & Flinx, #13)

**Author:** Alan Dean Foster  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

Title: The Quest for Identity Amidst Galactic Crisis: A Review of Alan Dean Foster’s Patrimony

In the vast and sprawling tapestry of modern science fiction, few partnerships possess the enduring charm of Philip X-9 and his venomous, highly affectionate minidrag, Pip. In Patrimony, the thirteenth installment of Alan Dean Foster’s beloved Pip & Flinx series, the veteran author returns to form with a narrative that deftly balances high-stakes cosmic peril with deeply personal, intimate introspection. Published in 2007, this novel tackles the ultimate question that has hovered over the red-headed protagonist for decades: Who is his father?

Foster’s greatest strength as a storyteller has always been his ability to render exotic universes accessible and wondrous. In Patrimony, he channels this signature style into a narrative driven by a classic literary dichotomy. While the rest of the galaxy faces an impending, apocalyptic doom in the form of an approaching exterminating scourge—requiring the location of an ancient, sentient weapons platform—Flinx makes a predictably human, utterly selfish choice. He turns his back on galactic salvation to chase a dying man's whisper about a remote world called Gestalt. 

This thematic focus on patrimony over politics is what elevates the novel. Foster strips away the grand opera of space warfare to examine the psychological weight of abandonment and the innate human need for origin. Flinx’s journey is not merely about finding a man; it is about confronting the source of his agonizing, god-like powers and his own identity. By pitting a universe-shattering threat against a young man's desperate need for familial closure, Foster creates a compelling narrative tension. Flinx’s quest is flawed, impulsive, and entirely relatable, grounding the cosmic absurdity in raw emotional truth.

True to the series' roots, Foster does not let his protagonist walk this path easily. Gestalt proves to be as inhospitable as it is obscure. True to his signature action-adventure pacing, Foster wastes little time thrusting Flinx into the meat grinder of the planet's wilderness. When a ruthless bounty hunter targets Flinx for a hefty reward, sending our hero plummeting into a raging river surrounded by ravenous, carnivorous beasts, the novel shifts into high gear. 

Yet, it is here that Foster’s style shines brightest. Rather than descending into grim-dark despair, the narrative retains its characteristic light touch, buoyed by the telepathic bond between Flinx and Pip. The danger is palpable, but the tone remains delightfully adventurous, relying on Flinx’s hard-earned experience and resourcefulness to navigate impossible odds. Foster writes with a conversational ease, painting vivid, alien landscapes without bogging the reader down in exhaustive technobabble. 

Patrimony may not reinvent the wheel of space opera, but it is a masterclass in episodic character development. For long-time fans, it delivers the long-awaited emotional payoff they have craved, answering lingering questions while providing all the humor, peril, and loyal companionship that defines the series. For newcomers, it serves as a testament to Alan Dean Foster’s enduring knack for making the infinite universe feel intensely personal.
