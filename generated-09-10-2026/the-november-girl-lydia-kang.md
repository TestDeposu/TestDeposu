# The November Girl

**Author:** Lydia Kang  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Lydia Kang’s 2017 novel, The November Girl, invites readers into a chilling, atmospheric corner of the American Midwest, transforming the folklore of the Great Lakes into a compelling narrative of survival, isolation, and identity. Set against the forbidding, beautiful backdrop of Isle Royale, Kang crafts a haunting world where the boundary between nature and myth blurs, delivering a speculative young adult romance that feels both ancient and refreshingly original. 

At the heart of the novel lies the evocative theme of humanity versus merciless nature. The protagonist is inextricably bound to the brutal gales of Lake Superior, a supernatural entity tethered to the infamous storms that terrorize the northern waters. Kang brilliantly uses this premise to explore what it truly means to be human. For most of the year, the protagonist exists in a fragile, partially human state, but as autumn yields to the howling winds of November, she must surrender to the lake's dark dominion. Her strength is measured in tragedy—every ship that shudders and sinks, every life claimed by the icy depths, feeds her power. This macabre symbiosis forms the emotional core of the book: can a creature born of destruction and governed by seasonal instinct learn to harbor compassion?

The arrival of a runaway boy seeking refuge from his own earthly pain disrupts this solitary, cyclical existence. Kang handles this classic reluctant-romance trope with a deft touch. The boy’s presence acts as a catalyst, testing the icy borders of a faulty heart that has forgotten how to care. Through their developing bond, Kang explores the transformative power of warmth and vulnerability against a setting defined by sub-zero temperatures and unforgiving elements. Yet, this is no ordinary escapist romance. The looming threat is literal and inescapable; nature cannot be reasoned with, and the approaching storms care nothing for budding human attachments. 

Kang’s authorial style is striking for its sensory immersion and lyrical pacing. She writes with a keen eye for the stark, desolate beauty of Isle Royale. The prose mirrors the weather it describes—choppy and fierce during moments of conflict, yet deeply reflective and cold during the quiet lulls between gales. Kang personifies Lake Superior not merely as a backdrop, but as a living, breathing antagonist that exerts a gravitational pull over the narrative. The pacing builds steadily, echoing the meteorological phenomenon at the story's center, culminating in a breathless confrontation with the witch of November.

The November Girl stands out in the crowded speculative fiction landscape by trading generic dystopian tropes for regional folklore and atmospheric dread. It is a tale that examines the burdens we carry, the sacrifices required to survive, and the desperate, beautiful urge to hold onto our humanity even when the elements conspire to freeze us from the inside out. Kang delivers a poignant, shivering romance wrapped in a ghost story of the lake, leaving an impression as lingering as the memory of a autumn gale.
