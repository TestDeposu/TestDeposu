# Dragons of the Highlord Skies (Dragonlance: The Lost Chronicles, #2)

**Author:** Margaret Weis  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Stepping back into the sprawling, high-fantasy expanse of Krynn is always a homecoming of sorts, but Margaret Weis ensures that the journey is as chilling as the frozen wastes themselves in Dragons of the Highlord Skies. As the second installment in the Dragonlance: The Lost Chronicles series, this 2009 release brilliantly weaves through the beloved chronology of Dragons of Winter Night, illuminating the shadowy spaces between monumental historical events. Weis, a foundational architect of modern fantasy, proves once again why she remains a master of her craft, delivering a narrative that balances sweeping epic warfare with intimate, character-driven emotional stakes.

At the heart of the novel lies a compelling thematic exploration of destiny versus agency, and how the choices made in the darkest of hours define the soul. Weis uses the stark, unforgiving backdrop of the southern continent of Icereach not merely as a set piece, but as a physical manifestation of the emotional isolation experienced by her characters. The narrative splits its focus brilliantly between two formidable women—the fierce, unyielding Dragon Highlord Kitiara Uth Matar, and her idealistic rival, the elven noble Laurana. This dual-track structure allows Weis to dissect the corrupting allure of power juxtaposed against the enduring strength of sacrifice. 

Kitiara’s arc anchors the novel’s most striking and atmospheric moments. Her forced pilgrimage to the dread fortress of Dargaard Keep to face the terrifying Lord Soth is a masterclass in gothic tension. Weis captures the suffocating dread of a character walking willingly into the jaws of damnation, balancing Kitiara’s undeniable ambition with a desperate survival instinct. It is a testament to Weis's stylistic prowess that even as Kitiara marches alongside the darkness, the reader remains utterly transfixed by her agency and unyielding grit.

Conversely, the journey of Laurana, alongside stalwarts like Sturm, Flint, and Tasslehoff, embodies the classic, beating heart of heroic fantasy. Weis writes these iconic companions with a profound, lived-in tenderness. Their dialogue snaps with familiar banter, yet it is underscored by the sobering reality of a continent at war. Laurana’s evolution from a sheltered elven princess into a tactical commander of men and knights is charted with nuance and earned hardship. The juxtaposition of her path toward leadership with Kitiara’s descent into the machinations of the Dark Queen creates a brilliant narrative tension that drives the plot forward with relentless momentum.

Stylistically, Weis relies on the strengths that made the original Dragonlance chronicles global phenomena: brisk pacing, vivid world-building, and an earnest emotional core that avoids the cynicism often found in contemporary grimdark fantasy. She writes with a cinematic clarity, making the howling blizzards of Icereach and the brooding, gothic halls of Dargaard Keep palpable. Even readers who have not visited Krynn in decades will find themselves instantly re-enveloped by the rich lore, the clash of steel, and the desperate hope that defines the struggle against the Dragon Highlords.

Dragons of the Highlord Skies is far more than a nostalgic cash-in or a mere footnote to a larger story. It is a vital, thrilling expansion that deepens our understanding of Krynn's most complex figures. Margaret Weis reminds us why she helped shape generations of fantasy enthusiasts, offering a tale that is as emotionally resonant as it is epic in scale. For fans of high fantasy, epic quests, and morally grey heroines, this frozen expedition is an absolute triumph.
