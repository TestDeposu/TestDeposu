# The Dark One (Roswell High, #9)

**Author:** Melinda Metz  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2001

The Dark One, the ninth installment in Melinda Metz’s Roswell High series, thrusts readers straight back into the alien‑tinged drama of Roswell, New Mexico, where love, loss, and destiny collide with the relentless ticking of a clock that refuses to stop. The novel’s central promise—“True love never dies”—is not merely a sentimental slogan; it becomes the narrative engine that drives Isabel’s desperate flight, Max’s frantic rescue, and Alex’s unexpected redemption arc. Metz weaves this theme through a tight, pulse‑quickening plot that never loses sight of the emotional stakes at its heart.

Isabel Evans, once the quintessential girl‑next‑door with a secret extraterrestrial heritage, has fled her home after a traumatic revelation that leaves her physically and emotionally fragile. Her isolation is palpable; Metz renders Isabel’s inner turmoil with concise, visceral prose that lets readers feel every shudder of fear and every flicker of hope. Max Evans, Isabel’s protective brother and the series’ perennial hero, is forced to confront the limits of his own power. His determination is portrayed not as grandiose superheroics but as a gritty, almost weary resolve—Metz’s style shines here in its ability to convey heroism through restraint rather than bombast. Max’s urgency is matched by a ticking clock motif that Metz repeats in short, staccato sentences, reinforcing the sense that every second counts.

Enter Alex Whitman, the former love interest who has returned to Earth transformed—both physically and emotionally. Metz’s depiction of Alex’s metamorphosis is subtle yet striking; she avoids melodramatic exposition, instead showing his changed demeanor through small, telling details: the way he carries himself, the quiet confidence in his gaze, the restrained smile that hints at a newfound self‑acceptance. Alex’s internal conflict—pride versus compassion—forms the novel’s moral core. Metz handles this dilemma with a deft balance of introspection and action, allowing Alex’s growth to feel earned rather than contrived. When Alex finally decides to set aside his ego to aid Isabel, the scene resonates because Metz has spent the preceding chapters laying the groundwork for his transformation through nuanced character beats rather than overt exposition.

The author’s style throughout The Dark One is marked by its accessibility and emotional immediacy. Metz employs a straightforward, present‑tense narrative that propels the reader forward, mirroring the novel’s own sense of urgency. Dialogue is crisp and true to each character’s voice, allowing the teenage protagonists to sound authentic without slipping into cliché. Descriptive passages are economical yet evocative; Metz prefers to hint at the alien landscape of Roswell—its desert heat, the strange glow of the night sky—rather than overload the reader with minutiae. This restraint serves the story well, keeping the focus on interpersonal relationships and the overarching theme of enduring love.

What elevates The Dark One beyond a typical teen romance is Metz’s willingness to intertwine the supernatural with very human vulnerabilities. Isabel’s ailment is not just a plot device; it becomes a metaphor for the ways love can both wound and heal. Max’s relentless pursuit underscores the protective instincts that often accompany deep affection, while Alex’s journey illustrates that true love sometimes demands sacrifice and humility. Metz never lets the fantastical elements overshadow these emotional truths; instead, she uses the alien backdrop to amplify the universality of the characters’ feelings.

In sum, The Dark One delivers a compact, emotionally charged experience that stays true to the Roswell High legacy while standing strong on its own. Melinda Metz’s clear, heartfelt prose and her keen understanding of teenage yearning make the novel’s central message— that love persists despite time, distance, and even otherworldly peril—feel both timeless and timely. Fans of the series will find satisfaction in the continuation of beloved arcs, and newcomers will be drawn in by the novel’s brisk pacing and sincere exploration of what it means to love someone enough to set aside one’s own pride.
