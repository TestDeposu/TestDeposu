# Abducted (Alien Mate Index, #1)

**Author:** Evangeline Anderson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

Evangeline Anderson’s 2018 novel, Abducted, leaps headfirst into the delightfully unhinged waters of sci-fi romance, blending high-concept cosmic kidnapping with a deeply relatable, modern body-positive narrative. As the inaugural entry in the Alien Mate Index series, this book wastes no time establishing its unique brand of interplanetary escapism. Anderson crafts a vibrant, fast-paced romp that refuses to take itself too seriously, yet successfully anchors its absurdity in genuine emotional stakes and an unapologetically fierce protagonist.

At the heart of Abducted is the theme of bodily autonomy and self-acceptance in the face of universally toxic beauty standards—even if those standards are enforced by an extraterrestrial smart house. Meet Chloe Deems: a plus-sized woman with pink hair, a razor-sharp attitude, and a profound love for her own curves. When Chloe is snatched from Earth and subjected to an alien weight-loss regimen overseen by a sarcastic talking scale and a cruel diet of endless lettuce and celery, Anderson uses sci-fi satire to skewer diet culture. The unbreakable glass cupboard containing tantalizing Thin Mints serves as a brilliant metaphor for the frustrations of earthly deprivation, writ large across the galaxy. Chloe’s journey is not about changing her body to fit a mold, but about fighting for her right to exist on her own terms, making her an instantly endearing and refreshing heroine for the genre.

Enter Tark, a Beast Kindred whose struggles with a debilitating stutter have marginalized him in his own society. Tark is a refreshing departure from the hyper-articulate alpha heroes typical of sci-fi romance. His vulnerability immediately humanizes him, creating a poignant thematic parallel between Chloe’s societal rejection based on appearance and Tark’s isolation due to speech. When their paths cross in the prison house, the dynamic shifts from a standard rescue mission to a mutual quest for liberation and self-worth. 

Anderson’s writing style is punchy, humorous, and delightfully campy. She balances high-stakes peril—such as a terrifying abduction and a harrowing journey to the Resort of Resonant Oneness—with laugh-out-loud comedy and sizzling chemistry. The transition from the high-tech prison to a multi-dimensional resort allows Anderson to lean into the genre's erotic tropes with maximum comedic and romantic effect. The cringe-inducing, hyper-intimate exercises required at the resort push both characters out of their comfort zones, generating genuine tension and blushing moments that showcase Anderson's knack for blending awkwardness with undeniable heat.

While the plot moves at a breakneck speed—capping off with a cliffhanger that funnels directly into the sequel, Finding the Jewel—Anderson never loses sight of her characters' emotional growth. The central conflict forces Tark to weigh the magical cure for his lifelong silence against the burgeoning love he feels for Chloe, elevating the stakes from a mere physical escape to a profound emotional breakthrough.

Abducted is a stellar example of sci-fi romance done right: it is brightly colored, wildly imaginative, and anchored by a heroine who refuses to be shrunk, literally or figuratively. Evangeline Anderson delivers a breezy, highly entertaining read that satisfies the sweet tooth of romance fans while serving up a hearty dose of empowerment.
