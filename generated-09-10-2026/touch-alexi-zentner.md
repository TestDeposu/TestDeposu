# Touch

**Author:** Alexi Zentner  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

In the tradition of great magical realism, Alexi Zentner’s debut novel, Touch, carves out a frozen sanctuary where the boundaries between life and death dissolve into the bitter winter air. Set in the fictional, dying north woods boomtown of Sawgamet, the novel is less a traditional narrative than it is a sprawling, atmospheric tapestry of generational grief, resilience, and wonder. Zentner crafts a world where the supernatural is as palpable as the biting cold, introducing a landscape where monsters and witches share the unforgiving wilderness with singing dogs and golden caribou. 

At its core, Touch is an exploration of memory and the haunting weight of legacy. The novel centers on Stephen, a local pastor sitting vigil on the eve of his mother’s funeral. Through Stephen, Zentner anchors the narrative in a profound generational lineage, echoing the mythic summer thirty years prior when Stephen’s grandfather returned to Sawgamet in a desperate, obsessive search for his long-dead wife. This mirroring of quests forms the thematic backbone of the book: the human inability to let go of what we have lost, and the ways in which our ancestors' ghosts continue to shape our present. 

Zentner’s style is arguably the true protagonist of the novel. Blending the cadence of oral folklore with the stark realism of a frontier logging town, his prose is both lyrical and brutal. He captures the crippling beauty of winter and the surreal, hazy light of northern summers with painterly precision. The environment in Touch is not merely a backdrop; it is an active, breathing entity that dictates the rhythms of survival and mourning. Zentner employs a mythic storytelling voice that elevates ordinary tragedies into epic proportions, making the miraculous feel entirely earned within the harsh logic of Sawgamet.

While some contemporary fiction relies on cynicism, Touch leans unashamedly into wonder and emotional gravity. It is a ripping yarn that simultaneously functions as a sublime meditation on mortality. Zentner reminds us that grief is a landscape we all must navigate, one where the dead are never truly gone, but merely waiting just out of sight behind the frost-covered windowpanes. A mesmerizing, chilling, and ultimately heartwarming achievement, Touch proves that Zentner is a voice of rare and striking imagination.
