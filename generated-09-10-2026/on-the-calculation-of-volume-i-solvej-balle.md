# On the Calculation of Volume I

**Author:** Solvej Balle  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2024

**Solvej Balle’s *On the Calculation of Volume II*: A Cosmic Unraveling of Time, Self, and the Senses**

Solvej Balle’s *On the Calculation of Volume II* is not merely a sequel—it is an expansion of a universe, a fractal deepening of a mystery that feels both intimate and vast. Where the first volume introduced us to Tara Selter’s disorienting descent into the "eighteenths of November," a temporal labyrinth where time fractures like glass, this second installment plunges us deeper into the paradox of existence itself. Balle, a master of lyrical ambiguity, weaves a narrative that is at once a philosophical inquiry, a sensory odyssey, and a heartbreaking meditation on the erosion of self.

At its core, *Volume II* is a story about the unraveling of Tara’s identity as the world around her begins to *unfold*—not just in the literal sense of time stretching and warping, but in the way perception itself becomes fluid. The novel’s most striking innovation is its exploration of the "tactility of things," a world where objects, sounds, and even smells seem to vibrate with newfound life, as if the universe has suddenly decided to speak in a language beyond human comprehension. Balle’s prose is a symphony of sensory overload: "the world had begun whispering to us in a new language." This isn’t just atmospheric—it’s existential. The novel suggests that reality is not a fixed entity but a dynamic, breathing thing, one that Tara, and by extension the reader, must grapple with as it resists being pinned down.

Yet for all its grandeur, the emotional core remains deeply personal. Tara’s crisis is one of disintegration. She describes herself as a "relic of the past," a "little Roman coin" carried without purpose, a fragment of a self that has been "poured out, carried off down the stream of eighteenths." This is a devastating metaphor for the way time, in Balle’s hands, is not just a line but a sieve, sifting away pieces of us until we are left with nothing but echoes. The novel’s genius lies in its refusal to offer easy answers. Instead, it invites the reader to sit with the discomfort of uncertainty, to wonder alongside Tara whether any part of her—any part of *us*—remains intact when the world itself is in flux.

The journey north and south, through Europe’s shifting seasons and languages, is more than a travelogue—it’s a desperate attempt to reclaim time. Tara’s oscillations between winter and spring mirror her own fractured sense of duration, as if she believes that by chasing the seasons, she might somehow anchor herself in something stable. But Balle, ever the subverter of expectations, complicates this. There is no resolution here, no triumphant return to wholeness. Instead, the novel embraces the messiness of existence, the way we cling to fragments of ourselves like a drowning person to driftwood. The result is both exhilarating and heartbreaking, a story that feels like being pulled into a black hole of introspection while still being dazzled by the beauty of the fall.

Balle’s style is a marvel—dense with metaphor, poetic yet precise, and always alive with a sense of wonder. Her sentences unfurl like constellations, each word carefully placed to create a sense of depth and movement. The novel’s structure, too, is a work of art. It doesn’t just tell a story; it *is* the story, a living, breathing entity that refuses to be contained. The result is a reading experience that is as immersive as it is unsettling, a book that lingers long after the last page because it forces you to question not just what you’ve read, but what it means to exist in a world that is constantly rewriting itself.

*On the Calculation of Volume II* is not for the faint of heart. It demands engagement, patience, and a willingness to be unsettled. But for those who dare to enter its world, it offers something rare: a novel that feels like a revelation, a book that makes you see time, space, and self in entirely new ways. Balle doesn’t just write about the unraveling of Tara’s identity—she writes about the unraveling of *ours*, and in doing so, she crafts something truly extraordinary.
