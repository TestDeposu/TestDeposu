# A Time of Changes

**Author:** Robert Silverberg  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Imagine a society where uttering the words I or me is considered the ultimate obscenity, a depravity so grotesque that it threatens the very fabric of civilization. This is the chilling premise of Robert Silverbergs Nebula Award-winning masterpiece, A Time of Changes. Long out of print and fiercely relevant, this dystopian classic invites readers into the tightly controlled world of Borthan, a distant planet bound by the austere tenets of the Covenant. On Borthan, individuality is a sin, privacy is mandated by law, and the inner life of the soul must remain perpetually hidden. To reveal ones true thoughts, emotions, or desires is the heinous crime of self-baring, punishable by exile or death. 

Silverberg constructs a profound exploration of human psychology and totalitarian control, focusing on the harrowing journey of Kinnall Darival, a prince of Salla. Through Kinnall, Silverberg investigates the suffocating weight of a culture built on the systematic erasure of the self. When an off-world visitor from Earth shatters Kinnall's worldview, the protagonist is thrust into a dangerous awakening. He realizes that a society terrified of intimacy and emotional honesty is fundamentally broken. Driven by a desperate need for authentic human connection, Kinnall embarks on a perilous crusade to tear down the walls of isolation, risking his throne, his sanity, and his life to teach his people the terrifying, beautiful reality of what it means to be truly human.

What makes A Time of Changes a towering achievement in speculative fiction is Silverbergs masterful handling of its central theme: the tension between societal conformity and the fierce, unyielding drive for self-expression. In an era where modern communication often feels performative and shallow, Silverbergs Borthan serves as a dark mirror to our own anxieties surrounding vulnerability and exposure. The author brilliantly juxtaposes the claustrophobia of emotional suppression against the liberating, albeit terrifying, prospect of radical honesty. Silverberg does not merely present a dystopian setting; he immerses the reader in the psychological torment of a mind waking up in a world designed to keep everyone asleep.

Stylistically, Silverberg writes with a lyrical intensity and psychological depth that elevates the narrative far beyond traditional science fiction. The prose is rich, introspective, and deliberately paced, mirroring the slow, agonizing unravelling of Kinnall’s conditioned mind. Because Borthan culture forbids the open discussion of personal feelings, Silverberg adopts a confessional, memoir-like tone for his protagonist. This narrative choice creates an intimate bond between the reader and Kinnall, making his rebellion feel deeply personal and urgent. Every page pulses with the psychological friction of a man learning a forbidden vocabulary, translating abstract concepts of empathy and love into actionable, revolutionary truths.

A Time of Changes is a triumph of imaginative world-building and philosophical inquiry. Silverberg challenges us to consider the price of our own privacy and the value of genuine connection in a world that often encourages emotional detachment. It is a timeless reminder that the most revolutionary act one can commit is to truly know oneself and to share that knowledge with another. For fans of deep, character-driven dystopian fiction and thought-provoking speculative literature, this newly resurrected classic is an absolute must-read that lingers long after the final page is turned.
