# Tricks of the Trade (Paranormal Scene Investigations, #3)

**Author:** Laura Anne Gilman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

**A Spellbinding Blend of Mystery and Magic: *Tricks of the Trade* by Laura Anne Gilman**

Laura Anne Gilman’s *Tricks of the Trade*—the third installment in the *Paranormal Scene Investigations* series—is a masterclass in blending urban fantasy with hard-boiled detective work, where the supernatural isn’t just a backdrop but the very engine of the story. At its core, this book is about the thin line between justice and chaos when magic goes rogue, and the brave souls who dare to walk it. Bonnie Torres, the series’ sharp-witted and no-nonsense paranormal investigator, is back, and this time, the stakes couldn’t be higher—or weirder.

Gilman’s strength lies in her ability to make the occult feel visceral and immediate. The *Cosa Nostradamus*, the secretive cabal of magical beings, looms over the narrative like a shadowy corporate boardroom, but with more curses and less spreadsheets. Bonnie and her team—likely including the ever-reliable (and probably sarcastic) partner from previous books—are caught between a high-profile murder case involving witchcraft and a break-in at a magical hotspot, the Electric Apple. What starts as a routine investigation quickly spirals into something far more dangerous: a hunt by an unseen, powerful entity that seems determined to silence them. Gilman doesn’t just hint at the threat; she ratchets up the tension with a slow-burn dread that lingers like the hum of a spell gone wrong.

The prose is lean, efficient, and dripping with Gilman’s signature wit. Her dialogue crackles with the kind of snappy repartee that makes the characters feel like old friends—Bonnie’s dry humor and quick thinking shine through, even as the supernatural elements demand her full attention. The world-building is deft, too. The Electric Apple isn’t just a bar; it’s a nexus of magic, gossip, and danger, where every patron could be hiding something. Gilman’s ability to weave in lore about magical factions, curses, and the politics of the occult without overwhelming the plot is a testament to her skill as a storyteller. The book feels like a love letter to classic noir, but with a supernatural twist—think *The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo* meets *Buffy the Vampire Slayer*, if the vampire slayer was a cynical, coffee-chugging investigator with a knack for getting into trouble.

What makes *Tricks of the Trade* particularly compelling is its exploration of the cost of truth. Bonnie and her team aren’t just solving crimes; they’re unraveling threads that could destabilize entire magical communities. The NYPD’s involvement adds another layer of tension, as human law enforcement grapples with phenomena they don’t understand. Gilman doesn’t shy away from the moral gray areas—who gets to decide what’s a crime when magic is involved? Whose justice matters most? These questions hang in the air like a poorly cast spell, unresolved but undeniable.

The pacing is relentless, with Gilman balancing action, intrigue, and character moments seamlessly. The mystery unfolds in a way that keeps readers guessing, but never at the expense of the characters’ depth. Bonnie’s growth—her increasing confidence, her willingness to take risks—feels earned, and her relationships with her team (and perhaps a new ally or two) add emotional weight to the proceedings.

For fans of urban fantasy, this book is a must-read. Gilman’s world is rich enough to feel expansive, but tight enough to keep the focus on the characters and the immediate threat. The humor, the heart, and the high-stakes supernatural intrigue all come together to create a story that’s as satisfying as it is suspenseful. If you’ve ever wondered what it would be like to be the person called when magic goes wrong, *Tricks of the Trade* delivers that thrill—and then some.

In the end, Gilman’s series proves that the best paranormal investigations aren’t just about solving crimes; they’re about navigating the chaos, the consequences, and the cost of knowing too much. And in a world where magic is as much a weapon as it is a tool, that’s a lesson worth learning.
