# The Valkyrie's Daughter (Sigrid and the Valkyries #1)

**Author:** Tiana Warner  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

In The Valkyrie’s Daughter, Tiana Warner breathes fresh, frost-kissed life into Norse mythology, crafting a YA fantasy debut that soars as high as any winged mare. Published in 2022 and rightly earning a coveted spot on Paste Magazine’s pick for Best New Fantasy Books, Warner’s novel takes well-worn tropes of chosen ones and celestial realms and grounds them in a deeply human, remarkably tender narrative about identity, longing, and the courage to rewrite one's destiny.

At the heart of the novel is Sigrid, an underdog whose ordinariness immediately endears her to the reader. In a world where status is dictated by your mount—and Sigrid is stuck with a plain, wingless horse rather than a majestic valkyrie steed—her dreams of sky-bound glory seem utterly out of reach. Warner uses this premise to explore the central theme of inherent worth versus societal expectation. Sigrid’s journey is not merely about gaining magical power or fighting monsters; it is about unlearning the internal narrative that mediocrity equates to uselessness. When Vanaheim is ambushed and a prophetic vision shatters her quiet existence, Sigrid’s ascent from stable hand to reluctant hero feels earned, raw, and profoundly satisfying.

What truly elevates Warner’s style, however, is the intricate emotional scaffolding she builds around her action-heavy plot. The introduction of Mariam—a fallen valkyrie who walks a dangerous line by allying with the enemy—injects the narrative with a crackling, enemies-to-lovers tension. The dynamic between Sigrid and Mariam is handled with a delicate yet compelling touch. Warner allows their relationship to breathe, balancing the high-stakes peril of a quest that leads straight to the gates of Hel with the slow-burn, magnetic pull of awakened feelings. Mariam is no simple archetype; she is beautifully complex, dangerous, and alluring, complicating Sigrid's mission and forcing her to question the rigid morality of the world she has always known.

Warner’s prose is brisk, evocative, and cinematic. She paints Vanaheim and the perils of the nine worlds with vivid strokes, immersing the reader in the chill of the North and the thunder of hooves. Yet, for all its grand world-building, the novel never loses sight of its emotional core. The juxtaposition of peaceful, mundane stable life against the harrowing, fatalistic glory of the valkyries creates a poignant internal conflict for Sigrid. As the paradise she left behind begins to fade into a distant memory, the true cost of heroism becomes painfully clear.

The Valkyrie’s Daughter is a triumphant opening salvo for the Sigrid and the Valkyries series. Tiana Warner has delivered a striking adventure that combines pulse-pounding mythology, high-stakes romance, and an unforgettable protagonist. It is a compelling testament to the fact that the most powerful weapon a hero can wield is not a sword, but the audacity to forge their own path—even if it leads straight to the underworld.
