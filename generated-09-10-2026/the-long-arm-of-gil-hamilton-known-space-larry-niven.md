# The Long Arm of Gil Hamilton (Known Space)

**Author:** Larry Niven  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

Larry Niven has long been celebrated as one of the true titans of modern hard science fiction, a writer uniquely gifted at juggling immense concepts of time, space, and societal evolution while keeping his narratives ruthlessly grounded. In The Long Arm of Gil Hamilton, Niven returns to his celebrated Known Space universe to deliver a masterclass in speculative fiction that marries the grit of a hard-boiled detective noir with the boundless horizons of interplanetary expansion.

Set in a fractured future where humanity has splintered into three distinct cultures—the asteroid-dwelling Belters, the lunar "lunies," and the gravity-bound "flatlanders" of Earth—the novel immediately establishes a rich, lived-in sociological framework. At the center of this world is Gil Hamilton, an operative for the United Nations police force known as the ARM. Armed with an uncanny, invisible psychokinetic third arm, Hamilton is a compelling protagonist who navigates the cultural fault lines of human civilization with quiet determination and sharp intellect.

The narrative kicks into high gear when Hamilton travels to the Moon for a political convocation, only to find a dear friend and Earth native framed for attempted murder. Against the chilling backdrop of lunar jurisprudence—where capital punishment is swiftly enacted alongside the compulsory harvesting of organs—Hamilton finds himself in a high-stakes race against time. Niven expertly uses this central murder mystery not just as a vehicle for suspense, but as a lens to explore deeper thematic currents, contrasting the stark, unforgiving realities of frontier life with the bureaucratic politics of the inner solar system.

True to Niven’s signature style, the brilliance of the book lies in its seamless integration of hard scientific extrapolation with pulse-pounding narrative drive. The world-building is effortless; Niven does not merely tell the reader about the physiological and psychological divides between flatlanders, lunies, and Belters—he makes them palpable through the characters' biases, movements, and survival strategies. The procedural elements of Hamilton’s investigations are bolstered by inventive futuristic tech and clever deductions, satisfying both mystery enthusiasts and die-hard science fiction fans alike. 

Furthermore, Niven's characteristic wit and crisp prose keep the pages turning. As Tom Clancy once noted, the vast scope of Niven's imagination is so immense that it takes a writer of supreme talent to make such complex world-building feel entirely natural and accessible. The inclusion of a new authorial introduction in this edition adds a welcome layer of intimacy, offering readers a glimpse into the mind of a Grand Master who has spent decades shaping the landscape of speculative fiction.

The Long Arm of Gil Hamilton is a taut, atmospheric, and intellectually stimulating journey. It proves that even when operating on a tighter, more intimate mystery scale, Niven loses none of the grand vision that defines his best work. For veterans of Known Space and newcomers alike, this gripping tale of justice, culture clash, and biological dread is an absolute must-read that lingers long after the final page is turned.
