# Protected (Alien Mate Index, #2)

**Author:** Evangeline Anderson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2026

Protected (Alien Mate Index, #2) thrusts readers into a gritty, pulse‑pounding romp where middle‑aged resilience collides with interstellar intrigue. Cassie, a curvy, perimenopausal human thrust onto an alien world, finds herself abandoned by a cheating lizard‑man husband just as a savage Hunger Virus turns the populace into ravenous Infected. Her survival hinges on two unlikely saviors: Ravik, a half‑feral Beast Kindred whose infection wavers at the whisper of her scent, and Severin, a stoic Blood Kindred scientist who discovers that Cassie’s shifting hormones may be the cure they desperately need.

Evangeline Anderson’s signature style shines through in this installment—she blends hard‑edge science‑fiction worldbuilding with steamy, emotionally charged romance and a dash of dark humor. The alien society feels lived‑in; its rigid caste‑based mating rules clash beautifully with Cassie’s defiant, flesh‑and‑blood humanity. Anderson’s prose is vivid yet accessible, moving swiftly from claustrophobic bunker corridors to the blood‑smeared streets outside, while never losing sight of Cassie’s internal landscape. Her perimenopause isn’t a gag; it’s a plot catalyst, reframing “the change” as a source of unexpected power and agency in a universe that often reduces women to mere breeding stock.

The novel’s central theme—how biology, desire, and destiny intertwine when societal taboos are tested—resonates deeply. Cassie becomes the fulcrum on which two warring Kindred factions pivot, challenging the Beast Kindred’s “no‑share” creed and the Blood Kindred’s devotion to pure logic. Anderson navigates the tension between instinct and intellect with a deft hand, letting the romance sizzle without sacrificing the stakes of the plague‑ridden world. The forbidden bond that forms is both tantalizing and perilous, promising either salvation or a catastrophic rupture of Kindred law.

What elevates Protected beyond a typical alien‑mate romp is Anderson’s willingness to let her heroine’s middle‑aged body be a source of strength rather than a punchline. Cassie’s hot flashes, mood swings, and evolving pheromones are rendered with scientific curiosity and erotic frankness, turning what could be a niche trope into a fresh, empowering narrative device. The action sequences are tight and visceral, the dialogue crackles with wit, and the emotional beats land with genuine weight.

In short, Protected delivers a satisfying blend of high‑octane survival horror, inventive alien romance, and a fiercely relatable protagonist whose “change” becomes the galaxy’s best hope. Fans of Anderson’s Kindred series will find familiar hallmarks—rich world‑building, steamy encounters, and a heroine who refuses to be sidelined—while newcomers will be drawn in by the novel’s bold premise and heart‑pounding momentum.
