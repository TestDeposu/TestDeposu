# The Vanished (Roswell High, #7)

**Author:** Melinda Metz  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2000

Melinda Metz’s The Vanished, the seventh installment in the gripping Roswell High series published in 2000, plunges readers back into a high-stakes world where adolescent angst collides with extraterrestrial danger. As the penultimate novel of the original run, this book serves as a critical bridge, testing the resilience of its characters against mounting cosmic and emotional odds. Metz’s signature style—fast-paced, intensely character-driven, and emotionally raw—shines brightly here, proving why this YA sci-fi series maintains a devoted cult following.

At the heart of The Vanished is the compelling theme of sacrifice versus self-preservation. Max Evans is driven by an unyielding guilt and loyalty, embarking on a desperate mission to rescue his fallen friend Alex, who remains stranded on the harsh alien home planet. Max’s quest requires one of the elusive Stones of Midnight, setting off a frantic search that highlights the heavy burdens these young people carry. They are teenagers forced to make life-or-death decisions that adults cannot even fathom, a motif Metz handles with surprising gravity.

Meanwhile, Liz Parker is forced to confront the limits of her devotion. While Max is consumed by his extraterrestrial crusade, Liz seeks a semblance of normalcy, finding solace and unexpected excitement in the company of a charming newcomer named Adam. Metz uses this subplot to explore the theme of emotional drift and the painful friction between destiny and everyday desire. Liz’s growing connection with Adam injects a fresh dynamic into the narrative, subtly shifting the power balance in her relationship with Max and raising the agonizing question: can a mortal teenage love survive the weight of alien worlds?

Metz’s writing style remains one of the series' greatest assets. She writes with an urgent, cinematic clarity that propels the reader effortlessly through the chapters. There is little narrative fat; every scene is designed to either advance the plot or deepen the emotional trench between the characters. The dialogue crackles with authentic teenage insecurity, even when the characters are discussing wormholes, alien artifacts, and interstellar rescue missions. By anchoring fantastical elements in the very real, messy emotions of jealousy, grief, and longing, Metz makes the extraordinary feel deeply personal.

The Vanished is a striking and poignant chapter in the Roswell High saga. It does not merely rely on the sci-fi spectacle of its premise; instead, it uses the sci-fi elements as a metaphor for the alienation of growing up. Metz captures the bittersweet reality that sometimes, in trying to save the people we love, we risk losing the very connections that keep us grounded.
