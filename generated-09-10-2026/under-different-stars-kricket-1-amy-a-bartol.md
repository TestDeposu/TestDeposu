# Under Different Stars (Kricket, #1)

**Author:** Amy A. Bartol  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Unknown

In the vast, often over-saturated landscape of young adult speculative fiction, few novels manage to carve out a territory as fiercely compelling and atmospheric as Amy A. Bartol’s Under Different Stars. Though the exact details of its publication and page count fade into the background against the sheer volume of its impact, this first installment in the Kricket series stands as a testament to Bartol’s mastery of high-stakes world-building and emotional intensity. At its core, the novel explores the timeless theme of identity versus destiny, wrapped in a breathless narrative of survival, belonging, and the dangerous pursuit of truth.

The story follows Kricket Hollowell, a young woman living an isolated, guarded existence in the Pacific Northwest. Orphaned and seemingly ordinary, Kricket struggles simply to get by, carrying the weight of a past she cannot fully remember and a future she cannot predict. However, Bartol quickly pulls the rug out from under the reader, revealing that Kricket is anything but ordinary. Thrust unexpectedly into a war spanning across dimensions—transported to a strange, beautiful, and perilous world called Astica—Kricket discovers that her very bloodline holds the key to a fractured empire. 

Bartol’s central theme hinges on the jarring juxtaposition of who we think we are versus who we are destined to become. Kricket is not a typical chosen one who eagerly accepts her fate; rather, she is deeply resistant, fiercely independent, and profoundly human. Her internal conflict forms the beating heart of the novel. Stripped of her familiar reality, she must navigate a deadly political landscape where her heritage makes her both a target and a weapon. Through Kricket’s journey, Bartol examines the resilience of the human spirit. She asks a poignant question: when the universe demands everything of you, how much of yourself do you sacrifice to survive?

Stylistically, Bartol’s writing is electric, fast-paced, and unapologetically immersive. Her prose leans heavily into sensory details, painting Astica with vivid, breathtaking strokes that contrast sharply with the grim realism of Kricket's origins on Earth. The world-building is intricate yet accessible, avoiding the trap of overwhelming infodumps by grounding exposition in Kricket's immediate survival and emotional reactions. 

Furthermore, Bartol excels at crafting razor-sharp dialogue and crackling romantic tension. The introduction of the enigmatic and dangerous Wayfarer guards—most notably the brooding, fiercely protective Alpha-male archetype woven with a complex vulnerability—elevates the narrative tension. The dynamic between Kricket and her captors-turned-protectors pulses with a kinetic energy that keeps the pages turning well into the night. Bartol understands the mechanics of slow-burn attraction and high-octane peril, balancing moments of quiet introspection with explosive action sequences.

Under Different Stars is not merely a portal fantasy or a sci-fi romance; it is a gripping exploration of power, autonomy, and the courage it takes to stand under an alien sky and reclaim your own narrative. Amy A. Bartol delivers a powerhouse opening act that leaves readers breathless and desperate for the next chapter. For fans of immersive world-building, high-stakes romance, and heroines who refuse to be tamed, this novel is an absolute must-read.
