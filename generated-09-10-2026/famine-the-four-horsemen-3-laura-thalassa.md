# Famine (The Four Horsemen, #3)

**Author:** Laura Thalassa  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

If Pestilence brought the sickness and War bathed the world in blood, Famine arrives with something far more insidious: a slow, agonizing starvation of both body and soul. In Famine, the third installment of Laura Thalassa’s bestselling apocalyptic romance series, the stakes are raised, the darkness deepens, and the line between monster and lover blurs into a haunting shade of grey. Thalassa continues her audacious reinvention of biblical lore, crafting a tale where the end of the world serves merely as the backdrop for a consuming, toxic, and ultimately mesmerizing romance.

At the heart of this novel lies the titular horseman, a creature of ethereal beauty and bottomless cruelty. Famine is not just a destroyer of crops; he is a manifestation of mankind’s darkest sins, a being who wears his bitterness like a tailored suit. Unlike his brethren, Famine harbors a specific, deeply personal vendetta against humanity, having suffered unimaginable torment at the hands of the very mortals he was meant to judge. Thalassa explores the theme of vengeance as a cyclical disease. Famine’s cruelty is a mirror reflecting the brutality he once endured, raising the compelling question of whether a monster can be held accountable when he was forged in the fires of human malice. 

Enter Ana da Silva, a woman who has already stared into the abyss and lived to tell the tale. Having crossed paths with the horseman years prior, Ana is no helpless damsel. When Famine brutally leaves her for dead, she doesn’t cower; she returns seeking retribution. This dynamic shifts the narrative away from a standard captive-captor trope into a psychological chess match. Ana’s empty threats and stubborn resilience captivate a horseman who has grown numb to the cries of the dying. Through Ana, Thalassa examines the resilience of the human spirit—how tenacity and a refusal to break can sometimes disarm even the most merciless of immortals.

Thalassa’s authorial style remains signature and sharp. She writes with a visceral, sensory-heavy prose that makes the decay of the world practically tangible on the reader's tongue. The atmosphere is thick with the scent of dust, dried earth, and unrequited desperation. Yet, amidst the bleakness of a starving planet, Thalassa injects a searing emotional intensity. The dialogue is laced with sharp banter and psychological sparring, creating a kinetic friction between the protagonists that keeps the pages turning at a breakneck speed. She excels at writing morally grey characters who are unapologetically flawed, forcing the audience to root for a literal harbinger of the apocalypse.

While the premise leans heavily into dark romance tropes, Famine succeeds because it grounds its supernatural elements in raw, emotional vulnerability. It is a story about hunger in all its forms—the physical need for sustenance, the craving for revenge, and the deeper, terrifying yearning for connection in a dying world. Thalassa does not offer easy redemptions, choosing instead to let her characters claw their way toward each other through blood, pain, and mutual understanding. 

Famine is a seductive, dark, and utterly gripping addition to the Four Horsemen series. It proves that Laura Thalassa’s imagination is as boundless as it is dark, offering a delicious torment that readers of apocalyptic romance will happily devour to the very last page.
