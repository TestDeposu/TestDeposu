# Stardust of Yesterday (de Piaget, #9; de Paiget/MacLeod, #1)

**Author:** Lynn Kurland  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Unknown

Time is rarely a barrier in the hands of a master romance storyteller, but in Lynn Kurland’s enchanting novel Stardust of Yesterday, it becomes something far more exquisite: a canvas for redemption, healing, and an enduring, cross-century love. As the ninth installment in her beloved de Piaget series and the inaugural crossover into the MacLeod lineage, this novel bridges two of historical romance’s most captivating dynasties, proving that Kurland’s narrative magic has lost none of its potency. 

At its core, the novel explores the profound and transformative theme of emotional resurrection. Kurland is not merely interested in the mechanics of time travel; she is deeply invested in the geography of the human heart. Her protagonists are frequently battered by the modern world or weighed down by the rigid expectations of the past. Through the jarring yet seamless collision of eras, the author examines how modern resilience can temper medieval stoicism, and how ancient honor can provide a grounding anchor for contemporary anxieties. The central romance acts as a crucible, burning away the superficial dross of misunderstanding and leaving behind a pure, resilient alloy of mutual respect and fierce devotion. It is a testament to the idea that true connection transcends not just physical distance, but the relentless march of the centuries.

Kurland’s literary style is the beating heart of her enduring appeal. Her prose is lush, immersive, and unhurried—a deliberate stylistic choice that allows readers to steep themselves in the atmosphere of mist-shrouded Scottish highlands and sun-dappled medieval courtyards. Where other authors might rush the mechanics of time-slip fantasy to get straight to the conflict, Kurland lingers in the sensory details. The weight of heavy wool, the scent of woodsmoke, the biting chill of a medieval morn, and the dizzying shock of modern conveniences all receive equal, loving attention. 

Crucially, Kurland avoids the trap of cynical melodrama. Her voice is defined by a gentle, witty humor that naturally bubbles up from the culture clashes of her displaced characters. The dialogue is sharp, intelligent, and rich with underlying warmth. Even the villains or antagonists are rarely one-dimensional; instead, Kurland frames conflict through the lens of misunderstanding and the burden of generational expectations. This gives her stories a comforting, cozy yet intellectually engaging quality that satisfies both the craving for high-stakes emotional drama and the desire for a safe, profoundly satisfying literary escape.

Stardust of Yesterday succeeds because it honors the traditions of both historical romance and contemporary fantasy while pushing past their boundaries. It is a novel that reminds us why we turn to fiction in the first place: to believe that somewhere out there, across the vast expanses of time and circumstance, someone is waiting to rewrite the stars for us. Kurland crafts a tapestry where every thread of past and present is woven with care, resulting in a timeless fairy tale for adults who still believe in the quiet magic of a love that defies the clock.
