# The Kartoss Gambit (The Way of the Shaman #2)

**Author:** Vasily Mahanenko  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

Step back into the immersive, unforgiving digital expanse of Barliona, where pixels carry the weight of life and death. In The Kartoss Gambit, Vasily Mahanenko returns to the sprawling universe of his LitRPG series, picking up the trials of Mahan, the beleaguered Shaman sentenced to eight years in a virtual penal colony. If the first installment introduced us to the brutal mechanics of a world where virtual reality is a harsh substitute for a prison cell, this second volume transforms that premise into a high-stakes chess match of survival, resource management, and cunning betrayal. 

The central theme of the novel pivots on agency within systemic oppression. Mahan is not a classic, overpowered fantasy hero; he is a man cornered by corrupt system administrators, hostile non-player characters, and cutthroat fellow inmates. Yet, Mahanenko uses this confinement to explore how intelligence and unorthodox thinking can subvert a rigged game. Stripped of traditional combat prowess, the Shaman relies on diplomacy, economic maneuvering, and the exploitation of overlooked game mechanics. The Kartoss Gambit brilliantly deconstructs the idea of progression, showing that in a world governed by algorithms, power belongs not to the strongest blade, but to the sharpest strategist. 

Mahanenko’s writing style is brisk, pragmatic, and heavily influenced by the pacing of classic role-playing games. He avoids excessive purple prose, opting instead for a transparent narrative style that mirrors the interface of the game itself. Status updates, skill progressions, and inventory management are seamlessly woven into the narrative fabric, grounding the fantasy elements in hard, quantifiable rules. This approach creates an irresistible feedback loop for the reader. Every minor quest completed, every copper coin earned, and every faction reputation gained feels like a hard-fought victory. 

At the same time, Mahanenko injects a distinct brand of dark, cynical humor into the proceedings. Barliona is a dystopian theme park designed to milk its inmates and players for all they are worth, and Mahan’s internal monologues brilliantly capture the absurdity of bureaucratic red tape existing inside a magical realm. Whether he is negotiating with arrogant nobles or outsmarting murderous goblins, Mahan remains an intensely relatable protagonist—exhausted, perpetually scheming, and fiercely resilient. 

While the novel occasionally leans into genre tropes, its relentless pacing and clever plotting elevate it above standard LitRPG fare. The titular "Gambit" refers to a perilous journey into the hostile Kartoss region, and Mahanenko masterfully ramps up the tension as the stakes shift from mere personal survival to shaping the geopolitical landscape of the entire virtual continent. 

The Kartoss Gambit is a triumphant sophomore effort that solidifies Mahanenko’s place at the forefront of the LitRPG genre. It is a sharp, witty, and deeply engaging read that appeals not only to hardcore gamers and fantasy enthusiasts, but to anyone who loves a classic underdog story set against impossible odds. Roll the dice, check your inventory, and prepare to be hooked.
