# Invitation to the Game

**Author:** Monica Hughes  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

Balancing the sacred and the ordinary is an artistic tightrope walk, one masterfully captured in the collection of stories and prayers brought together under the title Invitation to the Game. Edited by Monica Hughes, this 2015 release offers a deeply human, remarkably candid glimpse into the lives of over fifty clergywomen representing fourteen distinct denominations. Despite the slight irony of the book’s title—which traditionally evokes dystopian young adult fiction, though here repurposed to invite readers into the game of modern ministry—this volume delivers an authentic, soul-stirring exploration of faith, leadership, and resilience.

The central theme of the book hinges on the delicate juxtaposition of divine calling and messy, everyday reality. The contributors do not present themselves as untouchable spiritual icons; rather, they reveal the intimate, often exhausting balancing act of managing the pulpit while navigating the humdrum of domestic life, institutional friction, and personal doubt. As the Foreword by Rev. Carol Howard Merritt aptly notes, these women expertly juggle a chalice in one hand and a baby bottle in the other. They confront head-on the isolating, unrealistic expectations of perfection thrust upon them by congregations, choosing instead to tear down the facade of effortless piety in favor of shared human vulnerability.

Hughes’s editorial style allows these diverse voices to shine with striking individuality while maintaining a cohesive, harmonious rhythm throughout the book. The narrative structure alternates seamlessly between narrative reflections and poignant prayers, creating a dynamic reading experience that moves the heart just as much as it challenges the intellect. What elevates this collection above standard theological commentary is its generous infusion of humor. The authors possess a rare, disarming ability to dish out profound spiritual wisdom garnished with side-aching, self-aware wit. They laugh at themselves, at the eccentricities of church culture, and at the sheer unpredictability of congregational life, making heavy theological concepts accessible and deeply relatable.

While intentionally written to resonate with laypeople, women discerning a call to ministry, and clergy across all traditions, the book’s appeal stretches far wider. Anyone who has ever attempted to balance a deep passion for their work with the messy realities of faith will find solace and encouragement within these pages. Invitation to the Game is not merely a collection of anecdotes; it is a vital, spirited manifesto of modern ministry. It validates the struggles of women in leadership while celebrating the profound joy found in inviting others into holy, everyday moments.
