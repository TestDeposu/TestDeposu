# Now You See Her

**Author:** Linda Howard  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

**Now You See Her by Linda Howard: A Masterclass in Obsession, Art, and the Uncanny**

Linda Howard’s *Now You See Her* is a psychological thriller that weaves together the intoxicating allure of passion, the haunting power of art, and the terrifying ambiguity of human perception. At its core, the novel explores the fine line between inspiration and obsession, creativity and criminality, as it follows Paris Sweeney—a successful but restless painter whose life takes a dangerous turn after a single, electrifying night with a wealthy stranger. What begins as a fleeting infatuation spirals into a nightmare when her paintings start mirroring real-life murders, blurring the boundary between artist and suspect.

Howard’s strength lies in her ability to craft a narrative that is as seductive as it is unsettling. The novel opens with Paris Sweeney, a woman whose life is seemingly perfect—her paintings sell for exorbitant sums, her reputation is untouchable, and her days are filled with the quiet luxury of artistic fulfillment. Yet beneath this polished exterior, there is a simmering restlessness, a yearning for something more. When she meets Richard Worth, a charismatic and enigmatic millionaire, their encounter is nothing short of explosive. The chemistry between them is palpable, written with a sensuality that lingers on the page, making their connection feel both inevitable and dangerous. Howard doesn’t shy away from the raw, unfiltered intensity of desire, portraying it as a force that can be as destructive as it is exhilarating.

But it is Paris’s paintings that truly make *Now You See Her* a chilling exploration of the subconscious. After their night together, she finds herself compelled to return to her canvas, only to discover that her work has taken on a disturbing, almost prophetic quality. The more she paints, the more the details align with a real-life murder—one that she cannot possibly know about. The tension here is exquisite. Howard masterfully builds suspense by leaving the reader (and Paris) questioning whether she is an innocent artist whose mind is playing tricks, a victim of her own subconscious, or something far more sinister. The ambiguity is deliberate, mirroring the way real-life investigations often hinge on perception and interpretation.

What elevates *Now You See Her* beyond a typical whodunit is Howard’s deep dive into the psychology of creativity and guilt. Paris’s struggle is not just about solving a crime; it’s about confronting the demons within herself. Her paintings become a mirror, reflecting not just the world around her but the hidden corners of her own psyche. Howard’s prose is rich and evocative, painting (pun intended) vivid scenes that immerse the reader in Paris’s world. The descriptions of her art are particularly striking, capturing the texture, emotion, and even the moral ambiguity of her work. It’s a testament to Howard’s skill that the reader can almost smell the paint, feel the brushstrokes, and sense the unease that lingers in the air.

The novel also excels in its exploration of the duality of human nature. Richard Worth, for instance, is a character who is as fascinating as he is infuriating. His charm is undeniable, but so is his capacity for manipulation. His presence looms over the story, a shadow that Paris cannot escape, and his influence on her actions—and her art—is a constant source of tension. Howard doesn’t give us a clear-cut villain, which makes the story all the more compelling. The real antagonist, in many ways, is the uncertainty itself—the fear that Paris’s creativity might be a curse, that her talent could be a liability, or worse, that she is complicit in something she doesn’t fully understand.

The pacing of *Now You See Her* is another of its greatest strengths. Howard balances the sensuality of the romance with the urgency of the mystery, ensuring that the reader is never bored. The novel doesn’t rely on cheap scares or clichéd twists; instead, it builds its suspense through psychological depth and atmospheric tension. The climax is satisfying without being predictable, leaving the reader with a sense of satisfaction and unease in equal measure.

Ultimately, *Now You See Her* is a gripping exploration of the power of art, the fragility of perception, and the dangerous allure of obsession. Linda Howard’s writing is sharp, her characters are complex, and her story is impossible to put down. It’s a novel that lingers in the mind long after the last page, a testament to the idea that sometimes, the most terrifying things are the ones we create ourselves.
