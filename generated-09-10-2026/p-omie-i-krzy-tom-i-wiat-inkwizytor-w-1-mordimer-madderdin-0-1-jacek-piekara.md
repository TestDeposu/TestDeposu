# Płomień i Krzyż, Tom I (Świat Inkwizytorów, #1; Mordimer Madderdin, #0.1)

**Author:** Jacek Piekara  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

Jacek Piekara has long established himself as a master of provocative, dark fantasy, and with Płomień i Krzyż, Tom I, he once again invites readers into a morally corrosive universe where absolute faith demands absolute cruelty. Set in a world where Jesus did not die on the cross, but instead descended from it in wrath to punish his tormentors, Piekara constructs an uncompromising theological nightmare. This is not a realm of noble knights and chivalric romance; it is a brutal domain governed by the Holy Office, where heresy, sin, and suspicion lurk behind every corner, and salvation is measured in the blood of the guilty and the unfortunate innocent alike.

At the narrative helm stands Mordimer Madderdin, Piekara’s quintessential protagonist—an Inquisitor of the Inner Circle whose soul is as scarred as his body, yet whose dedication to his grim duty remains terrifyingly absolute. In this installment, Mordimer finds himself thrust far beyond the familiar borders of the Empire alongside Arnold Lowefell. Their destination is a cursed, distant land ruled by amoral lords and degenerate priests, a barbaric frontier where orthodox Christianity has mutated and fused with dark pagan rites. This stark setting allows Piekara to explore a compelling thematic clash: the rigidity of imperial dogma versus the chaotic, seductive pull of primitive corruption. 

What elevates Piekara’s style in this volume is its unflinching grit and unapologetic cynicism. He writes with a sharp, journalistic economy that strips away romantic illusions. The author does not preach to his audience, nor does he offer easy moral catharsis. Instead, he traps the reader in the claustrophobic mindset of a religious fanatic who genuinely believes that torture and terror are acts of profound mercy for the eternal soul. The prose is punchy, atmospheric, and deeply immersive, drenched in the scent of burning wood, old parchment, and copper-rich blood. 

Beyond Mordimer and Lowefell, Piekara expands his canvas by weaving in ancillary yet magnetic figures from the secretive Wewnętrzny Krąg. Readers are introduced to Hildegard Reizend, a formidable traveler whose steps have spanned from the heart of Africa to the freezing heights of the Himalayas, and Barnaba Biber, whose mastery of forbidden magic makes him a dangerous asset. Perhaps most striking is the inclusion of Herenniusz Furius, an immortal Roman imperial spy who bears the weight of having witnessed the Crucifixion and the harrowing Descent with his own eyes. These intersecting subplots thicken the narrative web, transforming what could have been a standard monster-hunting adventure into a sprawling, geopolitically complex tapestry of faith, espionage, and cosmic conspiracy.

Płomień i krzyż, Tom I is a triumph of dark, alternative historical fantasy. It challenges the reader to walk the razor's edge between righteousness and damnation, proving once again that in Jacek Piekara’s universe, the fires of the Inquisition are fueled by the very worst—and most fascinating—aspects of human nature. Fans of morally gray antiheroes, uncompromising world-building, and gritty theological thrillers will find this book an utterly hypnotic and unforgettable experience.
