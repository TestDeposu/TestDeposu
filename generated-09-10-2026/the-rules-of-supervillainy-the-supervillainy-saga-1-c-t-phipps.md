# The Rules of Supervillainy (The Supervillainy Saga #1)

**Author:** C.T. Phipps  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

When a city falls to the undead, governments build walls and look away. But six years after the nation's capital was abandoned to a flesh-eating horde, a presidential election stirs the desperate hope of reclaiming the soul of America. Enter Asher Hawke, the retired CIA operative known as the Kestrel, who is tasked with a perilous mission behind the quarantine zone to retrieve the foundational relics of the nation: the Declaration of Independence and the Constitution. 

In Gnash, author Brian Parker delivers a gritty, relentless post-apocalyptic thriller that seamlessly blends high-stakes political espionage with the sheer, visceral terror of a zombie wasteland. Hailed as a masterclass in tension, the novel acts as a compelling mashup of techno-thriller precision and dark, atmospheric horror. Parker does not merely write about the end of the world; he dissects the human scavengers who crawl out from the wreckage. 

The core theme of the novel examines what happens to society’s moral compass when civilization collapses. Behind the massive wall keeping the horrors contained, Washington D.C. is no longer just a monument to freedom. It has devolved into a lawless playground where organized crime families rule the shadows, treating priceless historical artifacts as mere currency and using the zombie-infested ruins as the ultimate disposal site for their enemies. Hawke's mission quickly spirals from a patriotic retrieval operation into a brutal survival ordeal, forcing him to navigate a treacherous underworld where the living criminals are far more terrifying than the undead shuffling through the fog.

Parker’s writing style is punchy, cinematic, and unyielding. He crafts a seedy, neo-noir atmosphere that evokes the rain-slicked dystopias of classic cyberpunk while remaining firmly rooted in a brutal, grounded reality. The action sequences are choreographed with the meticulous detail of a veteran Clancy novel, keeping readers breathless as every corner turned threatens a new nightmare. Yet, what truly elevates the narrative is its refusal to rely on flawless cardboard-cutout heroes. Hawke and his team are wonderfully flawed, scarred by their pasts, and driven by desperation, making their struggle feel intensely real and high-stakes. 

For fans of dystopian thrillers, tactical espionage, and pulse-pounding survival horror, Gnash is an essential, hard-hitting read that proves the monsters outside the gate are only half the problem.
