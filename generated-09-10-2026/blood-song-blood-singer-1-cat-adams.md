# Blood Song (Blood Singer, #1)

**Author:** Cat Adams  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

Step into a world where high-stakes executive protection collides with the lethal underbelly of the supernatural, and you will find Cat Adams’s 2010 urban fantasy launchpad, Blood Song. This high-octane franchise opener introduces readers to Celia Graves, a tough-as-nails bodyguard whose career path takes a catastrophic detour straight into nightmare territory. Adams wastes no time in plunging her protagonist—and her audience—into a relentless whirlwind of political conspiracies, religious extremism, and monstrous intrigue, establishing a breathless pacing that rarely lets up.

At the beating heart of Blood Song is the profound and agonizing theme of identity and transformation. Celia begins her journey firmly rooted in the mundane human world, relying on her physical training, wits, and combat skills to shield her clients. However, when a high-profile protection detail goes catastrophically wrong and she is betrayed from within, Celia undergoes a terrifying metamorphosis. She wakes neither fully human nor entirely undead, cursed into existence as an Abomination—a creature deemed an unnatural affront by both mortal society and supernatural factions alike. 

Adams uses this monstrous rebirth as a brilliant narrative vehicle to explore alienation and bodily autonomy. Celia’s struggle is not merely about evading executioners; it is a psychological battle to reclaim her sense of self when her very flesh has been hijacked by forces beyond her control. Stripped of her humanity yet refusing to succumb to pure vampiric instinct, she must forge a terrifying new path. This internal friction gives the novel its emotional weight, elevating it above a standard monster-hunting romp. Celia’s journey resonates because her physical monstrosity mirrors the very real human pain of feeling like an outsider, hunted by a society that fears anything it cannot neatly categorize.

Stylistically, Adams employs a gritty, hard-boiled narrative voice reminiscent of traditional noir detective fiction, successfully transplanted into a sprawling contemporary fantasy landscape. The prose is punchy, action-oriented, and laced with a dry, resilient wit that keeps Celia grounded even as she grapples with impossible odds. Adams excels at world-building through integration rather than exposition; the supernatural elements—ranging from arrogant vampires and territorial werewolves to powerful mages and psychic law enforcement—are woven seamlessly into the fabric of modern reality. 

The author crafts a compelling ensemble cast to support her beleaguered heroine. Rather than relying solely on the classic lone-wolf trope, Adams surrounds Celia with a loyal yet dynamically complicated crew, including a brooding mage, an alpha werewolf, and a pragmatic psychic cop. The chemistry among these allies injects much-needed warmth, banter, and tactical depth into a narrative that could otherwise feel relentlessly grim. Their uneasy alliance underscores another subtle theme of the novel: trust is a rare and dangerous commodity when everyone has a hidden agenda.

While the plot moves at a breakneck speed, occasionally sacrificing moments of quiet introspection for the sake of the next adrenaline-fueled chase, the sheer momentum is undeniable. Blood Song does not reinvent the urban fantasy wheel, but it oils it with fresh blood, offering a thoroughly entertaining and emotionally resonant ride. For fans of kick-ass heroines, intricate supernatural politics, and transformative monster lore, Adams delivers a stellar opening act that leaves you eager to turn the page.
