# Mission to Moulokin (Icerigger, #2)

**Author:** Alan Dean Foster  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

In Mission to Moulokin, veteran science fiction architect Alan Dean Foster returns to the frozen, unforgiving expanse of Tran-ky-ky, proving once again why he remains a master of planetary world-building and adventurous camaraderie. As the second installment in the Icerigger trilogy, this novel takes the winning formula of its predecessor and deepens the stakes, shifting the narrative from a desperate survival story into a compelling tale of cross-cultural diplomacy, institutional corruption, and the unexpected responsibilities of reluctant heroes.

At the heart of the novel lies a rich exploration of colonialism, bureaucracy, and the ethics of technological advancement. Foster uses the plight of the Tran—the planet’s native, endearing, cat-like inhabitants—to examine how indigenous populations are frequently exploited by rapacious profiteers and indifferent galactic authorities. Ethan Frome Fortune, a human salesman whose primary motivation has always been personal convenience and profit, undergoes a subtle yet profound transformation. His journey from an unwilling castaway looking merely to punch his ticket off-world to a passionate advocate for Tran self-determination anchors the narrative's central theme: that true civilization is measured not by technological output, but by empathy and justice. Alongside his imposing, unflappable companion Skua September, Ethan embodies the classic sci-fi trope of the ordinary man thrust into extraordinary circumstances, yet Foster manages to keep them grounded through witty banter and very human flaws.

Foster’s signature stylistic prowess shines brightest in his depiction of Tran-ky-ky itself. The ice planet is not merely a static backdrop, but a living, breathing, and violently hostile character. Through vivid, tactile prose, Foster makes the reader feel the biting chill of the blizzards, the creak of the massive sails of the icerigger, and the sheer vastness of the frozen wastes. His pacing is brisk and cinematic, moving effortlessly from high-stakes action sequences—such as harrowing navigation through treacherous ice formations and clashes with brutal adversaries—to quieter, atmospheric moments that highlight the unique culture and history of the Tran. 

While some may argue that the middle entries in trilogies occasionally suffer from narrative stagnation, Mission to Moulokin avoids this trap by continually raising the physical and political hurdles for its protagonists. The quest to find the legendary, fabled city of Moulokin serves as both a literal voyage and a metaphorical quest for historical truth, granting the Tran people a voice and a rightful place within the broader Humanx Commonwealth.

Ultimately, Alan Dean Foster delivers a thrilling, heartwarming, and socially resonant adventure. Mission to Moulokin succeeds because it never loses sight of the human—and alien—heart at its core. It is a triumphant voyage across the ice that reminds us that stepping up for others is the truest test of character. For fans of classic planetary science fiction, expansive world-building, and tales of unlikely heroes finding their purpose, this journey is well worth the frostbite.
