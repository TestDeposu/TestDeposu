# Moonshine (Cal Leandros, #2)

**Author:** Rob Thurman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

**Moonshine: Where the Shadows Bite Harder Than the Claws**

Rob Thurman’s *Moonshine*—the second installment in the *Cal Leandros* series—is a razor-sharp descent into the neon-lit underbelly of New York City, where the rules of survival are written in blood, betrayal, and the unshakable weight of legacy. If the first book in this series established Cal Leandros as a brooding, morally ambiguous guardian of the supernatural, *Moonshine* deepens the stakes, twisting the detective’s world into a labyrinth of shifting loyalties and predators who wear human faces. Here, the city isn’t just a backdrop; it’s a living, breathing entity that chews up the unwary and spits out the broken. And Cal? He’s the kind of man who’s seen too much to be naive, but not enough to be wise.

At its core, *Moonshine* is a story about the cost of trust in a world where everyone has a price—and where the price is often paid in kind. Cal and his half-brother Niko, the ever-loyal but increasingly exasperated straight man to Cal’s cynical charm, are hired to infiltrate the Kin, a werewolf Mafia syndicate, on behalf of a low-level boss who suspects a rival is setting him up. The job seems straightforward: gather evidence, walk away. But Thurman, with his signature blend of gritty realism and supernatural flair, knows better. The moment Cal steps into *Moonshine*—that gambling den where the air hums with tension and the stakes are measured in teeth and bullets—he’s already in over his head. The Kin aren’t just criminals; they’re a family, bound by blood and a code that doesn’t bend. And the people pulling the strings? They’re not just wolves. They’re something older. Something hungrier.

Thurman’s prose is a masterclass in controlled chaos. His writing is lean, precise, and dripping with the kind of atmosphere that makes the reader’s skin prickle. There’s a poetic brutality to his descriptions—like when he paints the glow of a cigarette in the dark, the way the smoke curls like a serpent’s tail, or the way the city’s neon signs flicker like dying stars. But beneath the stylish veneer lies a story that’s as much about psychological tension as it is about supernatural threats. Cal isn’t just fighting werewolves; he’s fighting the ghosts of his past, the weight of his father’s legacy, and the slow realization that the people he’s sworn to protect might be the very ones who will betray him.

What makes *Moonshine* particularly compelling is Thurman’s ability to blend the mundane with the monstrous. The gambling den is a place where humans and supernatural beings mingle, where the line between client and prey is as thin as the blade of a switchblade. The Kin’s world is one of power plays and backroom deals, where a handshake can mean death or salvation. And Cal? He’s the kind of man who’s seen it all before. He’s been burned. He’s been stabbed in the back. But he’s also the kind of man who still believes, just barely, that the world isn’t entirely without honor. That’s the tragedy of his character—he’s a man who’s been broken too many times to be hopeful, but not broken enough to stop fighting.

The real horror of *Moonshine* isn’t just the werewolves or the betrayals; it’s the way Thurman makes the reader feel the suffocating weight of Cal’s world. Every conversation is a minefield. Every ally could be a wolf in sheep’s clothing. And every moment of respite is just another step closer to the knife. The book’s pacing is relentless, with Thurman weaving together action, intrigue, and a slow-burning sense of dread. By the time the final pages land, the reader isn’t just left breathless—they’re left questioning everything they thought they knew about Cal, Niko, and the world they inhabit.

Thurman’s strength lies in his ability to make the supernatural feel visceral and immediate. The werewolves aren’t just mindless beasts; they’re cunning, ruthless, and deeply human in their motivations. The Kin’s world is one of old money and new power, where the rules are as fluid as the city’s streets. And Cal? He’s the perfect guide through this labyrinth—flawed, dangerous, and impossible to look away from. He’s a man who’s seen the worst of humanity, but who still believes, just enough, that there’s a way out. That’s the kind of character that stays with you long after the last page.

*Moonshine* is more than just a supernatural thriller; it’s a story about the cost of survival in a world where trust is a luxury and loyalty is a currency. Thurman’s writing is sharp, his characters are complex, and his world is rich with detail and danger. If you loved the first book in the series, you’ll devour this one. And if you haven’t read it yet, prepare to be pulled into a world where the shadows are always watching—and where the bite is always worse than the bark.
