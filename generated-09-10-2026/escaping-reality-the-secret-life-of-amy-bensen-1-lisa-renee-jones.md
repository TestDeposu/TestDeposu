# Escaping Reality (The Secret Life of Amy Bensen, #1)

**Author:** Lisa Renee Jones  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

**Escaping Reality: A Gritty, Sensual Plunge into Survival and Obsession**

Lisa Renee Jones’ *Escaping Reality*—the first installment in *The Secret Life of Amy Bensen* series—is a searing, emotionally charged thriller that blends psychological suspense with a steamy, almost addictive romance. At its core, this book is a survival story, but it’s not the kind that relies on action-packed chases or explosive confrontations. Instead, Jones crafts a narrative where the real danger isn’t lurking in the shadows of the past—it’s the fragile, fractured psyche of her protagonist, Amy (formerly Lara), a woman who has spent years running from the ghosts of her own making. What makes *Escaping Reality* so compelling is how Jones weaves Amy’s trauma into a love story that feels both intoxicating and terrifying, a dance between desire and destruction.

The novel opens with Amy, now a woman in her late twenties, having finally convinced herself that she’s free from the past—the tragedy that stole her childhood, the secrets that forced her to reinvent herself. But freedom, Jones reminds us, is an illusion when the past refuses to stay buried. The moment Amy boards a plane, her carefully constructed life begins to unravel. And then there’s Liam Stone—a billionaire architect, a man of ruthless ambition and even more ruthless charm. From the first moment he touches her, Amy is ensnared. Liam doesn’t just want her; he *needs* her, and his obsession is the kind that feels like both salvation and a slow, creeping poison. His intensity mirrors Amy’s own unresolved longing, creating a magnetic pull that’s as dangerous as it is exhilarating.

Jones’ writing is a masterclass in tension. She doesn’t shy away from the raw, unfiltered emotions of her characters, particularly Amy, whose internal monologue is a storm of guilt, fear, and desperate hope. The author’s prose is lush yet precise, painting Liam as a man who commands attention—not just with his wealth or his genius, but with the way he *sees* Amy. There’s a dark allure to him, a sense that he’s not just pursuing her but *uncovering* her, layer by layer, until she’s laid bare. The eroticism in this book isn’t just about physical passion; it’s about the psychological unraveling of a woman who has spent years protecting herself, only to find that someone else might be the one to break her down.

What elevates *Escaping Reality* beyond a typical romance or thriller is Jones’ exploration of trauma and the ways it shapes identity. Amy’s past isn’t just a backstory—it’s a living, breathing force that haunts her every decision. The novel doesn’t offer easy answers or neat resolutions; instead, it forces the reader to sit with the discomfort of Amy’s uncertainty, her fear of being found out, and her desperate need to believe that love—even dangerous, obsessive love—can be a form of redemption. Liam’s pursuit of Amy isn’t just about desire; it’s about power, control, and the terrifying possibility that he might be the one person who truly *understands* her.

The pacing is relentless, with Jones balancing Amy’s internal turmoil with Liam’s relentless charm and the slow, creeping dread of her past catching up to her. The chemistry between the two leads is electric, but it’s never reduced to clichés. Instead, their relationship feels like a collision of two broken souls, each searching for something they can’t name. The suspense isn’t just about whether Amy will be caught or whether Liam’s intentions are pure—it’s about whether she’ll survive the process of letting someone in at all.

For readers who crave stories that are equal parts heart-pounding and emotionally devastating, *Escaping Reality* delivers. Jones doesn’t just tell a tale of escape; she tells a story about the cost of survival, the seduction of danger, and the fragile line between love and obsession. It’s a book that lingers long after the last page, leaving the reader questioning how much of Amy’s story is her own and how much is the shadow of the past she can never fully outrun.

If you love stories where the real villain is the protagonist’s own mind, where romance is as much about destruction as it is about desire, and where every page turns with the promise of something both beautiful and terrifying, then *Escaping Reality* is a must-read. Jones has crafted a tale that’s equal parts gripping thriller and soul-stirring romance—a story about the places we run to and the people who find us anyway.
