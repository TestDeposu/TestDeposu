# Rise of the Fallen (Wars of the Realm, #2)

**Author:** Chuck Black  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

Chuck Black returns with *Rise of the Fallen*, the gripping second installment in the *Wars of the Realm* series, delivering a high-stakes theological thriller that seamlessly bridges the ancient supernatural realm with the modern human world. Known for his allegorical storytelling that echoes the epic scope of C.S. Lewis and Frank Peretti, Black once again plunges readers into a breathless, six-thousand-year spiritual war where the invisible battles of eternity dictate the chaotic realities of Earth.

At the heart of *Rise of the Fallen* is the profound theme of divine perspective versus human ambition. Black brilliantly subverts traditional expectations of power by centering the narrative on Validus, described as the last and least of God’s angels. Once a celebrated warrior commanding angelic legions through impossible cosmic odds, Validus suffers a staggering demotion. Stripped of his military glory, he is reassigned to a seemingly mundane and insignificant task: the covert, ground-level protection of Drew Carter, an unbelieving man completely oblivious to the celestial target painted on his back. 

Through this dynamic, Black explores the subversive nature of grace and the immense value the Creator places on a single, seemingly ordinary human soul. While demonic legions converge on Drew, driven by a desperate need to neutralize him, Validus must grapple with his own pride, learning that true strength in the Creator’s army often looks like quiet, unseen obedience rather than ruling from a position of command. Drew’s skepticism clashes sharply with the supernatural reality closing in around him, creating a rich thematic tension between doubt and faith, autonomy and divine providence.

Black’s writing style is punchy, cinematic, and relentlessly fast-paced. He avoids purple prose in favor of kinetic action sequences and taut, suspenseful pacing that keeps the pages turning. The transitions between the grand, sweeping vistas of the angelic war and the gritty, realistic danger surrounding Drew are handled with narrative precision. Black excels at making the metaphysical tangible, painting the demonic Fallen not merely as abstract evils, but as tactical, terrifyingly real adversaries. At the same time, he imbues his angelic protagonists with relatable flaws, struggles, and deep loyalty, making their eternal mission feel deeply personal.

Ultimately, *Rise of the Fallen* is more than just an action-packed fantasy; it is a compelling reminder of the unseen battles fought on behalf of humanity every single day. Chuck Black challenges his readers to look beyond the physical world and reconsider the weight of their own choices in a cosmic war where no soul is insignificant. For fans of speculative fiction with a strong spiritual core, this novel is a thrilling, thought-provoking ride that leaves you eagerly anticipating the next chapter in the war of the realm.
