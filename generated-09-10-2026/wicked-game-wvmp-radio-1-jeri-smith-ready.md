# Wicked Game (WVMP Radio, #1)

**Author:** Jeri Smith-Ready  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Late-night radio has always possessed a certain hypnotic magic, but in Jeri Smith-Ready’s 2009 urban fantasy novel Wicked Game, that midnight broadcast carries a literal, pulse-pounding urgency. Smith-Ready takes the well-trodden tropes of vampire lore and injects them with a sharp, adrenaline-fueled rhythm, creating a world where undead existence is inextricably linked to the soundtrack of the twentieth century. At its core, Wicked Game is a clever exploration of identity, nostalgia, and the struggle to reinvent oneself, dressed up in the neon, leather, and static of independent broadcasting.

The narrative anchors itself around Ciara Griffin, a recovering con artist trying desperately to navigate the mundane terrors of a legitimate career. When she lands an internship at WVMP—a quirky local radio station spinning everything from forties blues to eighties goth with uncanny authenticity—her life pivots dramatically. She soon learns the station's secret: the eccentric disc jockeys aren't method actors; they are vampires, perpetually trapped in the cultural epochs of their respective turnings. 

Smith-Ready’s central theme revolves around preservation and the weight of history. For the DJs at WVMP, their music isn't just a playlist; it is an anchor to their humanity. Without the specific cultural touchstones of their original "Life Times," these vampires face a terrifying dissolution, fading away into mindless specters. When a corporate media behemoth threatens to buy out the station and homogenize its frequency, the impending takeover becomes more than a business hostile negotiation—it is a matter of life and undeath. Ciara’s response is as audacious as her former life as a grifter: she uses the ultimate con, rebranding the vampires’ true nature as a brilliant marketing gimmick and dubbing the station "WVMP, the Lifeblood of Rock 'n' Roll."

The author’s style is brisk, witty, and saturated with musical reverence. Smith-Ready writes with an insider’s appreciation for music history, seamlessly weaving sonic nostalgia into the very fabric of the plot. The prose moves at a rapid-fire tempo, mimicking the crackle of a live broadcast where dead air is the ultimate enemy. Instead of relying solely on dark, brooding gothic gloom, Smith-Ready injects a vibrant, pop-culture-savvy humor into the proceedings. The juxtaposition of ancient, blood-drinking immortals stressing over broadcast ratings and indie-rock branding creates a delightful comedic tension that keeps the pages turning.

Furthermore, the romantic subplot involving Ciara and Shane McAllister—a grunge-era vampire whose flannel-clad cynicism masks a deeper vulnerability—adds emotional resonance to the high-stakes corporate drama. Shane and the other DJs represent the tragedy of being frozen in time, unable to evolve, yet forced to adapt to a modern world that wants to swallow them whole. Ciara, conversely, is someone defined by her ability to change masks and reinvent her path. This dynamic elevates the novel above standard paranormal romance, framing it as a story about finding belonging among the outcasts, whether they are living on the fringe of society or the edge of eternity.

Wicked Game succeeds because it refuses to take itself too seriously while still honoring the emotional stakes of its characters. It is a smart, sassy, and rhythmically compelling start to a series that proves urban fantasy can still surprise readers with a fresh frequency. For anyone who has ever stayed up late listening to the radio in the dark, wondering who is behind the microphone, Smith-Ready delivers an entertaining and deeply satisfying answer.
