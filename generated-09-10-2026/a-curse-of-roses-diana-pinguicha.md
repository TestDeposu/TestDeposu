# A Curse of Roses

**Author:** Diana Pinguicha  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

A Curse of Roses by Diana Pinguicha reads like a whispered legend that has slipped through the cracks of history and landed straight into the heart of modern fantasy. Set against the famine‑stricken courts of medieval Portugal, the novel follows Princess Yzabel, whose very touch transmutes bread into roses and cheese into lilies—a cruel gift that leaves her perpetually hungry while the kingdom starves. The premise is irresistibly poetic: a curse that turns sustenance into beauty, forcing the protagonist to confront the paradox of nourishment and devastation in equal measure.

At its core, the story wrestles with the tension between duty and desire, a theme that Pinguicha renders with both delicacy and urgency. Yzabel’s betrothal to the king represents the political lifeline her nation needs, yet her growing affection for Fatyan, the enigmatic Enchanted Moura who holds the key to reversing her magic, threatens to unravel that lifeline. The novel does not shy away from the painful intersections of religious guilt, self‑harm, and internalized homophobia that surface as Yzabel grapples with her identity. These darker threads are woven into the narrative not as gratuitous shock value but as essential facets of a young woman’s struggle to reconcile love, faith, and survival in a world that demands sacrifice.

Pinguicha’s prose is lush yet restrained, echoing the cadence of oral tradition while maintaining a crisp, contemporary voice. She paints scenes with sensory precision— the scent of wilting lilies, the bitter taste of unsaved bread, the electric chill of a forbidden kiss—allowing readers to feel the weight of each magical transformation. The pacing mirrors the ebb and flow of a tide: quiet, contemplative moments of introspection give way to sudden bursts of tension as political intrigue and magical peril collide. This rhythm serves the novel’s thematic core, underscoring how moments of tenderness can be as fleeting and fragrant as a rose, yet as dangerous as a thorn.

What distinguishes A Curse of Roses within the crowded landscape of historical fantasy is its #OwnVoices authenticity. Pinguicha draws from Portuguese folklore and her own cultural lens, infusing the tale with genuine specificity— from the cadence of courtly speech to the texture of traditional Moorish motifs— while still delivering a universally resonant story about love’s power to both heal and destroy. The novel’s handling of trigger‑laden topics is thoughtful; content warnings are offered upfront, and the treatment of religious‑based self‑harm and eating disorders is handled with nuance, inviting empathy rather than sensationalism.

Ultimately, the book asks a haunting question: when the very act of sustaining life becomes a source of ruin, what does it mean to choose love over duty? Yzabel’s journey is as much about reclaiming agency over her own body as it is about saving a kingdom, and Pinguicha answers with a tale that is as bitter as unsweetened bread and as sweet as the first bloom of a rose— an unforgettable blend of myth, magic, and heartfelt yearning.
