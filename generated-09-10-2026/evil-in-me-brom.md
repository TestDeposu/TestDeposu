# Evil in Me

**Author:** Brom  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2024

When the gates of Hell swing open to the grinding distortion of a punk rock riff, you know you are firmly in the razor-sharp, ink-stained universe of Brom. In his 2024 novel, Evil in Me, the master of dark fantasy and macabre aesthetics returns with a vengeance, delivering a high-decibel tale that fuses ancient damnation with the raw, bleeding energy of the underground music scene. 

At its core, Evil in Me is a story about desperation and the staggering price of ambition. Meet Ruby Tucker, an aspiring musician suffocating in the claustrophobic confines of her small rural town and toxic family life. When a bitter fallout with her best friend and bandmate shatters her final escape route to the Atlanta punk scene, Ruby is left stranded in despair. It is a brilliant psychological setup; Brom understands that demonic possession is often just an external manifestation of an internal rot—the suffocating weight of powerlessness, unrealized dreams, and self-loathing. 

The nightmare truly begins when a cursed ancient ring clamps onto Ruby’s finger while she is helping an eccentric neighbor sort through religious artifacts. Possessed by a blood-thirsty demon, Ruby discovers that the only path to salvation is as bizarre as it is terrifying: hundreds of people must chant a spell to release her. Compounding this grotesque predicament, the ring acts as a beacon, drawing a horde of wicked, flesh-hungry entities straight to her doorstep. To survive, Ruby must do the impossible—reunite her band, face the music, and channel a song powerful enough to raise Hell itself.

What elevates Evil in Me from a standard horror romp into a transcendent reading experience is Brom’s unmistakable signature style. He refuses to treat art and prose as separate entities. Interlaced with nearly two dozen of the author’s immersive, haunting paintings and stunning endpapers, the novel becomes a multimedia sensory assault. Brom’s visual style—gothic, visceral, and uncomfortably intimate—complements his prose, breathing tangible life into the rot, the sweat, and the supernatural terror of Ruby’s world. 

Brom’s thematic exploration centers on the intersection of faith, art, and damnation. The novel questions what sacrifices we are willing to make for our art, and whether the music we create can save us or doom us. In Ruby’s world, punk rock is not merely a genre; it is a primal exorcism, a visceral rebellion against authority, despair, and now, literal infernal forces. 

Striking, gritty, and relentlessly paced, Evil in Me is a triumphant addition to Brom’s dark bibliography. It proves yet again that the author is peerless when it comes to marrying the grotesque with the achingly human. Turn up the volume, open the pages, and prepare to be possessed.
