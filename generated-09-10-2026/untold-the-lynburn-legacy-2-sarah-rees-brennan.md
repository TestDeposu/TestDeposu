# Untold (The Lynburn Legacy, #2)

**Author:** Sarah Rees Brennan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

Beneath the deceptively sleepy surface of Sorry-in-the-Vale lies a festering history of blood, power, and ancient terror. In Untold, the second installment of Sarah Rees Brennan’s spellbinding Lynburn Legacy series, the author brilliantly deepens the stakes of her modern Gothic romance. This is not merely a tale of good versus evil; it is an intoxicating exploration of choice, sacrifice, and the heavy burden of legacy, wrapped tightly in a narrative that crackles with razor-sharp wit and dark, atmospheric tension.

At the pulsating center of the storm is Kami Glass, a take-charge heroine who refuses to be a passive bystander in her own town’s apocalyptic trajectory. Brennan’s greatest triumph in this sequel is the maturation of Kami’s world. With the Lynburn family fully returned and Rob Lynburn orchestrating a terrifying resurgence of the old ways—complete with demands for human blood sacrifices—the sleepy English town is forced to wake up and pick a side. Yet, Brennan resists the temptation to paint the conflict in simple black and white. The central theme of Untold is agency: who wields it, who loses it, and what sacrifices must be made to retain it. 

This thematic weight is mirrored in Kami’s personal journey. Having had her psychic and emotional link to Jared Lynburn severed, Kami is suddenly cast into a disorienting freedom. For the first time, she is untethered from a bond that dictated her heart, leaving her to navigate the dizzying, terrifying landscape of choosing her own path—and her own affections. It is a brilliant metaphor for adolescent self-discovery, amplified to operatic, supernatural proportions. 

Stylistically, Sarah Rees Brennan achieves a rare, delicious alchemy. She blends the brooding, atmospheric dread of classic Gothic literature with a contemporary, breathless pacing that makes the book a true page-turner. Reviewers and bestselling authors alike have lauded the series for its emotional whiplash, and Untold delivers this in spades. One moment, readers will shiver at the visceral danger of dark sorcerers lurking in the woods; the next, they will burst out laughing at the snappy, modern banter and Kami’s indomitable, detective-like resolve. Brennan has a rare ear for dialogue, injecting levity into the darkest of situations without ever undermining the genuine peril her characters face. 

The prose is sparkling, sharp, and relentlessly engaging. Brennan does not just tell a story; she constructs an entire sensory experience where the chill of the English vale seeps off the page, and the romance simultaneously breaks your heart and makes you swoon. By refusing to rely on tired YA tropes, she crafts a narrative where the bonds of friendship, the weight of family expectation, and the courage to stand alone are just as potent as any magic spell.

Untold is a breathtaking, darkly funny, and emotionally resonant triumph. It captures the reader with true magic, cementing Sarah Rees Brennan as a master of contemporary speculative fiction. For fans of atmospheric fantasy and fierce heroines, this is an absolute must-read that proves the Lynburn Legacy is entirely in a class of its own.
