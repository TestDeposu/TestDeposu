# Scarlet (Scarlet Revolution, #1)

**Author:** Genevieve Cogman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

Genevieve Cogman has carved out a distinct niche for herself in the realm of speculative historical fiction, known for her deft blending of brisk pacing, witty banter, and imaginative world-building. With Scarlet, the explosive first installment in the Scarlet Revolution series published in 2023, Cogman takes aim at the classic swashbuckling mythology of Baroness Orczy’s beloved Scarlet Pimpernel and injects it with a dangerously sharp, fang-filled twist. The result is a vibrant, darkly humorous, and deliciously atmospheric reimagining that proves revolution is not only a bloodthirsty business, but a supernatural one as well.

Set against the chaotic backdrop of 1793 Paris, where the guillotine works overtime and the Reign of Terror is in full swing, Cogman introduces a brilliantly macabre premise: the French aristocracy is largely composed of vampires. These immortal elites have long dominated the upper echelons of society, slaking the revolution’s thirst in frighteningly high numbers. Enter the elusive Scarlet Pimpernel and his League, British nobles dedicated to the perilous rescue of condemned aristocrats—human and vampire alike. But the true beating heart of the narrative belongs to Eleanor Dalton, a humble housemaid with dreams no grander than becoming a modiste, who suddenly finds herself thrust onto the geopolitical and supernatural chessboard.

Cogman’s signature style shines brightest in her grounded yet whimsical approach to fantasy. Even amidst the gothic trappings of immortal blood-drinkers and centuries-old secret wars, her prose remains snappy, accessible, and deeply character-driven. Eleanor is far from a typical swashbuckling heroine; her practical background as a housemaid and her artistic aspirations as a dressmaker lend her a grounded perspective that contrasts wonderfully with the melodramatic posturing of the nobility, both revolutionary and royal. When Eleanor is recruited due to her uncanny resemblance to a French aristocrat, she brings a refreshing sense of gritty determination and sharp observation to a world gone mad with magic, intrigue, and political fanaticism.

The central theme of Scarlet explores the intersection of oppression, identity, and the brutal mechanics of systemic change. By framing the French Revolution through the lens of a hidden war between vampires and their ancient enemies, Cogman cleverly interrogates the nature of power. Who truly suffers when a society tears itself apart? When the ruling class literally feeds on the populace, revolution ceases to be merely a political disagreement and becomes an existential survival horror. Yet, Cogman prevents the novel from sinking into bleak despair by infusing the narrative with her trademark banter, rapid-fire plotting, and a palpable sense of theatrical fun. 

Ultimately, Scarlet is a triumph of genre-mashing. It seamlessly welds historical fiction, gothic horror, and cloak-and-dagger adventure into a cohesive, highly entertaining package. Cogman has successfully revitalized a classic literary trope, offering seasoned fantasy readers and historical fiction enthusiasts alike a thrilling ride through the cobblestone streets of revolutionary Paris. Sharp, witty, and dripping with atmosphere, this opening salvo of the Scarlet Revolution promises a dangerous and dazzling journey ahead.
