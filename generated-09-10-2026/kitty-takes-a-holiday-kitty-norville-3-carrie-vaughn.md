# Kitty Takes a Holiday (Kitty Norville, #3)

**Author:** Carrie Vaughn  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2008

In the third installment of Carrie Vaughn’s beloved urban fantasy series, *Kitty Takes a Holiday*, the stakes are raised significantly as the titular werewolf radio host seeks solitude, only to find herself thrust straight into a supernatural nightmare. Following the fallout of her public unmasking on national television, Kitty Norville flees the glaring lights of Denver for the deceptive peace of a mountain cabin. She hopes to heal, process her trauma, and write her memoirs. Instead, Vaughn trades the bustling radio station for a claustrophobic, survival-horror atmosphere that evokes classic cabin-in-the-woods tropes with a distinctly modern lycanthropic twist. 

At its core, the main theme of this novel is the inescapable nature of identity and responsibility. Kitty desperately wants to step away from the spotlight, to cease being the reluctant spokesperson for her kind, and to simply be alone. Yet, Vaughn’s narrative brilliantly demonstrates that true escape is an illusion for someone who has chosen to stand up for the marginalized. Kitty’s internal conflict is palpable; her desire for self-preservation constantly clashes with her innate compassion and sense of justice. When werewolf hunter Cormac arrives at her doorstep carrying her injured lawyer, Ben O'Farrell—closely pursued by a malevolent, glowing-eyed beast—Kitty’s retreat is immediately shattered. She is forced to confront the very dangers she fled, proving that running away does not solve crises; it merely delays them. 

Vaughn’s signature style shines through her deft balance of grit, wit, and genuine heart. Unlike the hyper-sexualized or overtly cynical heroines that often populate urban fantasy, Kitty Norville remains refreshingly grounded. She is vulnerable, deeply flawed, and possesses an ordinary humanity that makes her supernatural struggles all the more resonant. Vaughn excels at pacing, seamlessly shifting the narrative from a reflective character study into a breathless, pulse-pounding thriller reminiscent of Sam Raimi’s *Evil Dead*. The remote mountain setting becomes a character in its own right—cold, isolating, and dripping with mounting dread as the perimeter closes in. 

Furthermore, Vaughn’s world-building continues to mature in *Kitty Takes a Holiday*. By stripping away the urban conveniences of the first two books, the author exposes the raw power dynamics of the paranormal underworld. The uneasy alliance between Kitty, the pragmatic assassin Cormac, and the vulnerable Ben highlights the complexities of trust when survival is on the line. The dialogue is sharp, character-driven, and maintains a wry sense of humor even in the face of imminent doom, providing a necessary counterbalance to the darker thematic elements.

Ultimately, *Kitty Takes a Holiday* is a thrilling, character-defining pivot for the series. It tests its protagonist to her absolute limits, stripping away her safety nets and forcing her to reclaim her power on her own terms. Carrie Vaughn has crafted a taut, atmospheric, and deeply satisfying read that proves even monsters need a vacation—even if they never actually get one.
