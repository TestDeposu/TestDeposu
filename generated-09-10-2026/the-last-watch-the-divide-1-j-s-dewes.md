# The Last Watch (The Divide, #1)

**Author:** J.S. Dewes  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

J.S. Dewes’s explosive 2021 debut, The Last Watch, crashes onto the science fiction scene with the kinetic energy of a railgun slug and the sprawling political grit of a classic space opera. Often pitched as The Expanse meets Game of Thrones, this high-stakes space fantasy wastes no time proving it deserves the lofty comparisons. Set at the edge of a crumbling universe known simply as the Divide, the novel is a masterclass in tension, blending existential dread with the chaotic camaraderie of the ultimate military underdog story.

The central theme of The Last Watch explores the value of the discarded. The narrative unfolds at the absolute periphery of civilization, guarding the edge of an expanding, universe-devouring void. Stationed here are the Sentinels: the dregs, the exiles, the court-martialed, and the forgotten. They are the human garbage of a sprawling empire, sent to the literal end of the world to quietly rot away. Yet, in a brilliant thematic reversal, Dewes positions these societal rejects as the sole bulwark against total annihilation. As the Divide begins to collapse, sweeping away everything in its path, it is precisely those whom the universe deemed worthless who must find their worth. This theme resonates deeply, offering a gritty look at redemption, duty, and how society often fails to recognize true heroism until the monsters are already at the door.

Dewes’s writing style is unapologetically fast-paced, cinematic, and punchy. She leans heavily into hard-edged military science fiction tropes while injecting a fresh, irreverent sense of humor. The prose moves at a breakneck speed, mirroring the panic of a collapsing universe. There is little time for flowery exposition; instead, world-building is delivered organically through razor-sharp dialogue, tactical desperation, and the unrelenting pressure of ticking-clock scenarios. The author excels at choreographing chaos, painting vivid, chaotic pictures of deep-space combat and crumbling infrastructure that keep the pages turning with breathless urgency.

The beating heart of the novel, however, lies in its character dynamics, spearheaded by two brilliantly mismatched leads. Commander Adequin Rake is the ultimate battle-hardened veteran—unyielding, resourceful, and entirely out of patience. Paired against her is Cavalon Mercer, an exiled prince, certified genius, and arrogant asshole who blew up his own family's genetic facility simply because he could. Their verbal sparring matches inject a much-needed levity into the otherwise grim atmosphere, creating a dynamic that crackles with electricity. Dewes crafts characters who are deeply flawed, occasionally obnoxious, but utterly magnetic, ensuring that readers care just as much about their petty interpersonal squabbles as they do about the destruction of the cosmos.

Ultimately, The Last Watch is a triumphant opening salvo for the Divide series. It is loud, proud, and thoroughly entertaining. Dewes takes familiar sci-fi ingredients—doomed soldiers, failing technology, and an unstoppable cosmic threat—and revitalizes them with sharp prose, high-octane pacing, and unforgettable characters. If you are looking for a space adventure that bites hard, moves fast, and makes you root for the absolute worst humanity has to offer, pack your bags for the edge of the universe. You are in for one hell of a ride.
