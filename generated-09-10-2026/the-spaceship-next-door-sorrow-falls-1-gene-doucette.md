# The Spaceship Next Door (Sorrow Falls, #1)

**Author:** Gene Doucette  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

What if an alien spacecraft arrived on Earth, captivated global attention, and then simply... did nothing? 

This is the brilliant, understated premise of Gene Doucette’s 2018 speculative fiction novel, The Spaceship Next Door. Eschewing the traditional sci-fi tropes of explosive invasions, planetary destruction, or grand cosmic diplomacy, Doucette shrinks the aperture of first-contact narratives. He sets his story not in a bustling metropolis or a high-tech government laboratory, but in the quiet, economically stagnant mill town of Sorrow Falls, Massachusetts. 

For three years, the ominous, silent vessel has rested in an open field, its doors stubbornly shut. It has become less of an existential threat and more of a mundane fixture of the local landscape—an alien monument treated with the weary familiarity of a broken traffic light. This clever subversion of scale forms the beating heart of the novel's main theme: the remarkable human capacity to normalize the extraordinary. Doucette explores how grand, paradigm-shifting events ultimately filter down into the petty gossip, economic struggles, and daily routines of ordinary people. The cosmos may have knocked on humanity's door, but the locals still have bills to pay and high school drama to navigate.

Enter Annie Collins, a sharp, sixteen-year-old local fixture whose house happens to be the closest to the silent behemoth. Annie is the anchor of the narrative, perfectly embodying Doucette's knack for creating grounded, highly relatable characters. When a slick government operative arrives in town under the guise of a journalist asking probing questions, the mundane facade of Sorrow Falls begins to crack. Annie, it turns out, holds pieces of a puzzle she doesn’t even know she’s part of, catapulting her from a bored small-town teenager to potentially the most important person on the planet.

What truly elevates The Spaceship Next Door above standard genre fare is Doucette’s distinctive stylistic voice. The author infuses the narrative with a wonderfully dry, observational humor that prevents the apocalyptic undertones from ever feeling overly heavy. His affection for the residents of Sorrow Falls shines through every page, transforming what could have been a cold, high-concept thought experiment into a warm, character-driven adventure. The dialogue is snappy, the pacing is brisk, and the blending of small-town slice-of-life with global sci-fi stakes is executed with remarkable finesse.

Doucette delights in playing against expectations, proving that you do not need collapsing skyscrapers to build intense dramatic tension. Instead, he relies on startling concepts, sharp wit, and an abundance of heart. Whether you are a die-hard science fiction aficionado or a reader who typically prefers contemporary coming-of-age stories, this genre-bending novel delivers a refreshingly original take on the classic first-contact story. 

The Spaceship Next Door is an enormously entertaining, witty, and surprisingly profound debut that reminds us that when the universe finally decides to visit, it just might make itself comfortable right in our backyard—and we will just have to learn to live with it.
