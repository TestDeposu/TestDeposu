# Hammered (Jenny Casey, #1)

**Author:** Elizabeth Bear  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2004

**Hammered: A Cyberpunk Masterpiece of Survival, Betrayal, and the Cost of Being Human**

Elizabeth Bear’s *Hammered*—the first novel in the Jenny Casey series—is a relentless, visceral dive into a dystopian future where humanity’s worst impulses have been weaponized, and the only way to survive is to become something inhuman yourself. Set in the year 2062, the novel follows Jenny Casey, a former Canadian special forces operative whose body, rebuilt through experimental cybernetics and genetic engineering, is slowly failing her. Once a soldier, now a fugitive, Jenny scrapes by in the ruins of Hartford, Connecticut, a city choked by corporate greed, government surveillance, and the ever-present threat of collapse. But her past isn’t over—it’s just waiting to be dragged back into the light, and this time, she’s the prize.

At its core, *Hammered* is a story about the price of survival in a world that has long since abandoned morality. Jenny isn’t just fighting for her life; she’s fighting against the very systems that made her what she is—a weapon, a commodity, a disposable asset. The novel’s strength lies in its unflinching portrayal of a future where technology and biology blur into something monstrous, where the body is a battlefield, and the mind is the last frontier. Bear doesn’t shy away from the brutality of Jenny’s existence, whether it’s the physical decay of her augmented body or the psychological toll of her past. The result is a protagonist who is as compelling as she is flawed—hardened by trauma, driven by survival, but never entirely broken.

Bear’s prose is sharp, immersive, and relentlessly atmospheric. She crafts a world that feels both familiar and alien, a cyberpunk landscape where the remnants of the old world coexist with the cold, calculating logic of corporate and governmental power. The novel’s pacing is relentless, with Jenny’s story unfolding through a series of high-stakes confrontations—street brawls, digital espionage, and high-tech chases—that keep the reader on the edge of their seat. But what truly sets *Hammered* apart is Bear’s ability to balance action with introspection. Jenny’s internal monologues are raw, unfiltered, and deeply human, revealing a woman who is both a product of her environment and a force of nature in her own right.

The novel’s themes are as rich as its prose. At its heart, *Hammered* explores the ethics of human enhancement, the cost of loyalty, and the fragility of identity in a world that sees people as tools rather than individuals. Jenny’s relationship with the crime lord who takes her in is particularly compelling—a dynamic that oscillates between mutual dependence and mutual exploitation, reflecting the moral gray areas of a world where trust is a luxury. Meanwhile, the government’s experiments with Jenny’s body raise haunting questions about consent, autonomy, and what it means to be human when your body is no longer entirely your own.

Bear’s world-building is meticulous, with a keen eye for detail that makes the future feel tangible. The streets of Hartford are alive with the hum of technology, the stench of decay, and the ever-present threat of violence. The digital underworld, where Jenny’s enemies and allies wage their battles, is equally vivid, blending hacking, espionage, and psychological warfare into a high-stakes game of cat and mouse. The result is a setting that feels both claustrophobic and vast, a world where every corner could be a trap, and every ally could be a liar.

What makes *Hammered* truly stand out, however, is its emotional core. Jenny Casey is not a hero in the traditional sense—she’s a survivor, a fighter, and a woman who has been broken by the world but refuses to stay broken. Her journey is one of reclaiming agency in a world that has long since abandoned her, and Bear’s portrayal of her is both nuanced and deeply moving. Jenny’s pain is palpable, her resilience inspiring, and her flaws humanizing. She’s not a perfect character, but she’s a real one, and that’s what makes her so compelling.

The novel’s ending is satisfying but not tidy, leaving room for the next installment in the series while still delivering a sense of resolution. Bear doesn’t offer easy answers, and she doesn’t shy away from the darker implications of Jenny’s choices. Instead, she leaves the reader with a sense of unease, a feeling that the world Jenny inhabits is still spinning out of control—and that the real battle has only just begun.

*Hammered* is more than just a cyberpunk thriller—it’s a meditation on the human condition in an age of technological and moral decay. Elizabeth Bear’s debut is a masterclass in world-building, character development, and narrative tension, a novel that lingers long after the final page. It’s a story about survival, but it’s also about the cost of that survival, and the question of whether it’s even worth paying. In a future where the line between man and machine is blurred, Jenny Casey’s journey is a reminder that humanity is never truly lost—it’s just waiting to be fought for.
