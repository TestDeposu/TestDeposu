# The Dark Monk (The Hangman's Daughter, #2)

**Author:** Oliver Pötzsch  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

**The Dark Monk: A Haunting Masterpiece of Vienna’s Undead Shadows**

Oliver Pötzsch’s *The Dark Monk*—the second installment in *The Hangman’s Daughter* series—isn’t just a sequel; it’s a bold reinvention, a gothic thriller that weaves the birth of modern criminology with the timeless terror of the supernatural. Set in fin-de-siècle Vienna, a city pulsing with artistic decadence and scientific ambition, this novel is a masterclass in atmospheric dread, blending historical rigor with a chilling mystery that lingers like the scent of damp earth in a crypt.

At its core, *The Dark Monk* is a story about the boundaries between life and death, science and superstition. The protagonist, Augustin Rothmayer, is no ordinary gravedigger—he’s a scholar of the macabre, a man who finds wisdom in the silent whispers of the dead. His expertise in burial rites and the psychology of the deceased makes him an unlikely but indispensable ally to Inspector Leopold von Herzfeldt, a young, ambitious cop fresh from Graz, eager to crack a case that defies conventional logic. Their partnership is the novel’s greatest strength: Rothmayer’s unshakable knowledge of the occult clashes with Herzfeldt’s rigid adherence to emerging forensic methods, creating a dynamic that feels both intellectually stimulating and emotionally charged.

The mystery itself is a tour de force. A serial killer is stalking Vienna’s underbelly, leaving behind victims staked like corpses in some twisted ritual. The clues point to an ancient, blood-soaked tradition meant to keep the undead buried—but who is performing these acts, and why? Pötzsch’s prose is rich with historical detail, immersing readers in the city’s opulent cafés, its shadowy alleys, and the intellectual ferment of the era. Think Freud’s Vienna, where the air is thick with theories of the subconscious and the line between madness and genius is perilously thin. Yet beneath the surface, something far darker stirs—a force that refuses to stay buried.

What elevates *The Dark Monk* beyond a typical historical thriller is its exploration of the human condition. The killer’s motives are as layered as the city itself, and Pötzsch doesn’t shy away from the psychological horror of his victims, who are not just statistics but flesh-and-blood women trapped in a nightmare. The inclusion of Julia Wolf, a telephone operator with her own demons, adds another dimension—her sharp wit and quiet resilience make her a standout character, a reminder that even in the darkest times, there is light to be found in unexpected places.

Pötzsch’s style is a perfect fusion of gothic atmosphere and meticulous research. His descriptions of Vienna are so vivid they could be paintings—cramped tenements glowing with gaslight, the hum of the city’s telegraph wires, the eerie stillness of the cemetery where Rothmayer works. The tension builds with surgical precision, each clue a puzzle piece that slowly reveals a horror both personal and cosmic. By the time the truth comes to light, readers will be left breathless, their own assumptions shattered like glass.

For fans of historical mysteries, *The Dark Monk* is a must-read. It’s a story that honors the past while fearlessly diving into the unknown, a testament to Pötzsch’s ability to craft tales that are as intellectually engaging as they are emotionally gripping. Whether you’re drawn to the gothic, the historical, or the psychological, this novel delivers a chilling, unforgettable experience—perfect for spooky-season nights or any reader craving a mystery that stays with them long after the last page.
