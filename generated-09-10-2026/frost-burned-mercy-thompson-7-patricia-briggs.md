# Frost Burned (Mercy Thompson, #7)

**Author:** Patricia Briggs  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

With Frost Burned, the seventh installment in her stellar New York Times bestselling Mercy Thompson series, Patricia Briggs proves once again why she remains an undisputed powerhouse in contemporary urban fantasy. Published in 2013, this gripping installment takes a sharp, breath-stopping turn by weaponizing the ultimate shapeshifter fear: isolation, vulnerability, and the sudden, terrifying disappearance of the pack. 

At its core, the central theme of Frost Burned explores the delicate, often agonizing friction between domestic normalcy and the brutal reality of the supernatural underworld. Mercy’s life has undergone a seismic shift. Having recently stepped into the role of mate to Alpha werewolf Adam Hauptman, she is now navigating the tender, high-stakes waters of being a stepmother to his teenage daughter, Jesse. Briggs crafts these quiet, domestic beats with remarkable empathy, grounding the fantastical elements in genuine human emotion. However, Briggs is too masterly a storyteller to let her characters rest in comfort for long. The central nightmare of the novel strikes when a seemingly mundane traffic accident strands Mercy and Jesse offline, completely cut off from Adam and the entire Columbia Basin pack. They haven't just vanished; they have been methodically abducted.

Briggs’s authorial style is on full display here, characterized by breakneck pacing, razor-sharp dialogue, and a distinct absence of melodrama. Unlike many heroines in the genre who rely solely on overpowered magical abilities, Mercy’s greatest asset is her quick wit, mechanical aptitude, and sheer, stubborn tenacity. As a walker—a shapeshifter who can take the form of a coyote—she is physically outmatched by the apex predators around her. When the entire pack goes missing amidst a treacherous political battle for werewolf civil rights, Mercy is forced to rely on her brains, her instincts, and a motely crew of morally gray, unpredictable allies. Briggs excels at expanding her universe organically, weaving in vampires, fae, and rogue magical entities without ever losing narrative focus.

Frost Burned is a masterclass in tension and stakes. Briggs strips away Mercy’s safety net, forcing her—and the reader—to confront the chilling reality of a world turning hostile all at once. The prose is lean, atmospheric, and punchy, capturing the freezing chill of the titular winter setting and mirroring the emotional dread that creeps into Mercy's heart. For longtime fans and newcomers alike, this novel delivers an adrenaline-fueled, emotionally resonant thrill ride that cements Patricia Briggs at the absolute pinnacle of urban fantasy.
