# Coś się kończy, coś się zaczyna

**Author:** Andrzej Sapkowski  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2003

To step into Andrzej Sapkowski’s universe is to walk through a world where fairy tales have been dragged through the mud, bled upon, and resurrected with a cynical, beating heart. Coś się kończy, coś się zaczyna (Something Ends, Something Begins), published in 2003, stands as a fascinating literary artifact in the legendary Polish author’s bibliography. While it may not be a sprawling, thousand-page epic, this collection of short stories and essays acts as both a bittersweet postscript and a razor-sharp deconstruction of the themes that defined Sapkowski’s monumental Witcher saga. 

At the core of this collection lies a profound meditation on closure, inevitability, and the burden of choice. Sapkowski has never been interested in pristine, happily-ever-after narratives. Instead, his central theme is the messy reality of the aftermath—what happens when the monsters are slain, the kingdoms fall into ruin, and the heroes are left alone with their regrets. The titular story, famously written as a wedding gift for fans, playfully subverts the tragic fate of Geralt of Rivia and Yennefer of Vengerberg, offering an alternate, wildly unorthodox celebration that feels both deeply earned and hilariously irreverent. It is a testament to Sapkowski’s philosophy that endings are rarely absolute; rather, they are merely violent transitions into something new and equally unpredictable.

Stylistically, Sapkowski remains peerless in his command of voice and register. His prose is a masterclass in linguistic alchemy, effortlessly blending high-fantasy grandeur with gritty, colloquial realism. He strips away the purple prose traditionally associated with the genre and replaces it with sharp, biting dialogue that crackles with cynical wit. Sapkowski’s characters do not speak in grand, monological declarations; they argue, they insult each other, they philosophize over cheap ale, and they bleed. This grounded, hard-boiled approach to fantasy gives the collection its distinct flavor. Even when exploring the absurd or the folkloric, Sapkowski anchors his narratives in raw human emotion, political corruption, and moral ambiguity. There are no clear-cut heroes here, only survivors navigating a universe indifferent to their suffering.

Furthermore, the author’s trademark irony permeates every page. Sapkowski delights in pulling back the curtain of myth to reveal the mundane, often petty mechanisms driving history and legend. He mocks dogmatism, skewers romanticized notions of chivalry, and presents a world where destiny is less a divine plan and more a heavy burden forged by bad choices and stubborn persistence. Yet, beneath the cynical exterior and the caustic humor, there beats a surprisingly tender pulse. Sapkowski cares deeply for his outcasts, mutants, and wanderers, granting them a quiet dignity that transcends their grim surroundings.

Coś się kończy, coś się zaczyna is essential reading for anyone who has ever journeyed down the Path with Geralt of Rivia. It is a compact yet potent showcase of Sapkowski’s literary prowess—a brilliant reminder that while stories must eventually conclude, the shadows they cast linger long after the final page is turned. It is cynical, poetic, profoundly human, and entirely unforgettable.
