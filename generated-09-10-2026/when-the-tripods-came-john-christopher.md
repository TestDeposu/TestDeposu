# When the Tripods Came

**Author:** John Christopher  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

Long before the metal giants of John Christopher’s legendary Tripods trilogy walked the Earth, enslaving humanity with the insidious Caps, there was a beginning—a sudden, terrifying shattering of the mundane. When the Tripods Came, published in 1988 as the much-anticipated prequel to the beloved post-apocalyptic series, peels back the layers of history to answer the most haunting question of all: How did the world surrender?

Christopher’s masterstroke in this prequel lies in his exploration of the psychological warfare waged by an insurmountable alien intelligence. Unlike traditional sci-fi narratives that rely solely on fiery explosions and conventional warfare, Christopher examines the subtler, more insidious anatomy of conquest. The invasion begins not with annihilation, but with mass hypnosis disguised as entertainment. The Tripods introduce the "Master-Idea," a mesmerizing television broadcast that effortlessly pacifies the human race, turning independent thinkers into compliant, glassy-eyed zealots. Through this chilling premise, Christopher masterfully explores the main theme of the book: the fragility of human free will and the terrifying ease with which a free society can be lulled into voluntary servitude. 

The narrative is anchored by the perspective of Laurie, a young boy whose family is slowly torn apart as the psychological contagion spreads. Christopher writes with a crisp, unpretentious British realism that grounds the bizarre and horrifying spectacle of towering three-legged machines in believable domestic drama. There is an eerie calmness to his prose, a stark journalistic detachment that makes the creeping dread all the more palpable. As neighbors, artists, and eventually world leaders succumb to the alien trance, the atmosphere thickens with paranoia. You begin to question, alongside the few remaining resisters, who can still be trusted. 

While the original trilogy focuses on the gritty, primitive survival of a conquered world, When the Tripods Came serves as a cautionary elegy for the loss of liberty. It captures the exact moment humanity traded its curiosity and ambition for blissful, engineered ignorance. The pacing is brisk, propelling the reader forward on a wave of impending doom, yet it leaves ample room for philosophical reflection on the nature of control.

For fans of dystopian literature, speculative fiction, and modern survival thrillers akin to The 5th Wave, this prequel is an essential and chilling read. It proves that the scariest monster isn't the one that destroys you with brute force, but the one that convinces you to open the door and put the chains on yourself. Christopher’s chilling vision remains as relevant and provocative today as it was decades ago—a stark reminder that freedom is easily lost and agonizingly hard to reclaim.
