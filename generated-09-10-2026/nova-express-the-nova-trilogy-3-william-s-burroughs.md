# Nova Express (The Nova Trilogy, #3)

**Author:** William S. Burroughs  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

William S. Burroughs was never a writer of mere fiction; he was an intelligence agent reporting from the dark, psychic frontiers of human consciousness. With Nova Express, the explosive final volume of his legendary Nova Trilogy, Burroughs delivers his most ferociously political and prophetic dispatch. Originally published in 1964 and now presented in a definitive, expanded edition meticulously edited by scholar Oliver Harris, this radical text remains as blindingly urgent today as it was at the height of the Cold War. 

At the burning core of Nova Express is a terrifying premise: humanity has been hijacked. We are not merely troubled; we are occupied. Earth has become a contested colony in a vast, interplanetary conflict orchestrated by the Nova Mob—a syndicate of parasitic alien entities whose ultimate weapon is not physical violence, but control. These cosmic saboteurs manipulate human biology, language, desire, and fear, driving a burning planet toward total atomic catastrophe for their own parasitic amusement. Against this apocalyptic backdrop rises the Interzone police force and Inspector Lee, waging a desperate guerrilla war to dismantle the reality-studio that keeps humanity enslaved. 

To tell a story of planetary takeover, Burroughs had to invent a new language, and in Nova Express, his iconic "cut-up" technique reaches its most dizzying, visceral zenith. Abandoning traditional narrative structure, linear plot, and conventional character arcs, Burroughs takes scissors and razor blades to his own typescripts, newspaper clippings, pulp sci-fi tropes, and overheard dialogue, splicing them together into a chaotic, polyphonic collage. The result is a text that mirrors the fragmented, media-saturated consciousness of modern life. Sentences fracture, perspectives shift violently, and time collapses. Reading Nova Express is akin to staring directly into a strobe light; it is disorienting, hallucinatory, and entirely mesmerizing. 

Yet, beneath the sci-fi trappings, the alien terminology, and the avant-garde chaos lies a razor-sharp political critique. Burroughs uses his textual outer space as a high-altitude vantage point to expose the ugly machinations of earthly power. The Nova Mob is a brilliant metaphor for the invisible forces that govern modern existence—totalitarian governments, oppressive institutions, corporate media conglomerates, and, most insidiously, the virus of language itself. Burroughs famously declared that "language is a virus from outer space," and in Nova Express, he attempts to vaccinate the reader against it. He exposes how words and images are weaponized to manufacture compliance, manufacture reality, and lock human consciousness into a rigid, inescapable loop.

This new edition, enhanced by Oliver Harris with an illuminating introduction and never-before-seen manuscript appendices, allows us to appreciate the sheer craftsmanship behind the chaos. We see a writer violently wrestling with the English language, carving away the fat to leave only raw, pulsing nerve endings. It is the shortest of the three books in the trilogy, but arguably the most focused and visionary.

Nova Express is not a comfortable read. It demands active participation, patience, and a willingness to surrender to the rhythm of the madness. But for those brave enough to strap in, it offers something priceless: a visionary demand to take back the world that has been stolen from us. Burroughs does not just critique the system; he hands you the tools to smash it. Essential, dangerous, and profoundly liberating, this is a masterwork of counter-cultural literature that continues to detonate in the mind long after the final page is turned.
