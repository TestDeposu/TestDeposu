# Battlestations! (Star Trek: Fortunes of War, #2)

**Author:** Diane Carey  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2000

Battlestations! thrusts readers back into the familiar yet uneasy world of Starfleet, where Captain James T. Kirk finds himself suddenly accused of stealing the Federation’s most coveted breakthrough—transwarp propulsion. Diane Carey wastes no time in turning a routine shore leave into a high‑stakes manhunt, pairing Kirk’s legendary charisma with Spock’s relentless logic and the determined, if somewhat raw, Lt. Cmdr. Piper. The novel’s core theme revolves around trust versus suspicion: how easily a hero’s reputation can be undermined when secrecy cloaks powerful technology, and what lengths individuals will go to protect—or exploit—the future.

Carey’s style is unmistakably rooted in the rhythm of classic Star Trek episodes. Her prose moves at a brisk, almost cinematic pace, favoring snappy dialogue and vivid, action‑filled set pieces over lingering introspection. She captures the distinct voices of Kirk, Spock, and Piper with an ear for the series’ cadence, letting Kirk’s swagger and Spock’s measured cadence shine through even as the plot pushes them into unfamiliar, morally gray territory. The narrative benefits from Carey’s knack for planting clues that feel both tantalizingly obvious and deftly hidden, inviting readers to piece together the traitor’s identity alongside Piper’s investigation.

That said, the book occasionally leans on exposition to bridge gaps in its fast‑moving plot, and some secondary characters feel more like plot devices than fully realized personalities. The isolated planet setting, while atmospheric, serves largely as a backdrop for a showdown that resolves a bit too neatly, relying on convenient coincidences that seasoned Trek fans might spot. Nevertheless, Carey succeeds in delivering a story that feels both a tribute to the original series and a standalone adventure: the stakes are personal, the technology tantalizingly futuristic, and the moral quandary—whether the ends justify the means when guarding a secret that could shift the balance of power—resonates with contemporary anxieties about innovation and security.

For fans who crave a quick‑paced, character‑driven romp that puts Kirk’s integrity to the test while showcasing Spock’s unwavering fidelity to logic, Battlestations! offers an enjoyable, if imperfect, entry in the Fortunes of War saga. It reminds us that even the most decorated heroes can find themselves questioned, and that the true strength of Starfleet lies not just in its ships or engines, but in the bonds that endure when trust is put to the test.
