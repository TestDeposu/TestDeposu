# Strange Trouble (Rune Alexander #3)

**Author:** Laken Cane  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Unknown

Dark, gritty, and relentlessly fast-paced, Laken Cane’s Strange Trouble plunges readers back into the neon-lit, blood-soaked underbelly of a world where the supernatural is just another day at the office. As the third installment in the Rune Alexander series, this novel does not merely step on the gas; it drives the narrative off a cliff and figures out how to fly on the way down. Cane has carved out a distinct niche in the urban fantasy genre, blending the hard-boiled grit of classic noir detective fiction with the chaotic, fangs-and-claws mythology of modern paranormal romance and thriller. 

At the heart of Strange Trouble lies the central theme of moral ambiguity and the heavy toll of survival. Rune Alexander is no shining hero in silver armor. She is bruised, cynical, highly capable, and morally flexible, operating in a universe where the line separating the monsters from the law enforcement is razor-thin, if it exists at all. Cane uses Rune to explore what it means to retain one's humanity—or whatever version of it is left—while knee-deep in the supernatural muck. The book constantly tests its characters' loyalties, forcing them into impossible corners where every choice is the wrong one, and the only metric of success is waking up breathing the next morning. 

Cane’s writing style is unapologetically punchy. Her prose moves with the rhythmic snap of a .45 caliber handgun, favoring short, impactful sentences and sharp, cynical dialogue that drips with personality. There is no purple prose or agonizingly long exposition to slow down the momentum. Instead, world-building is delivered organically through action and consequence. The author excels at sensory details—the metallic tang of blood, the hum of neon in a dive bar, the bone-deep exhaustion of a protagonist who has been put through the wringer one too many times. 

What sets Strange Trouble apart from the crowded shelves of urban fantasy is its refusal to rely on tropes as a crutch. While the genre often falls into predictable patterns, Cane infuses her plots with genuine unpredictability. Just when the reader thinks they have pegged the villain or anticipated the twist, the narrative shifts, revealing a deeper, more complicated conspiracy. The pacing is relentless, balancing high-octane action sequences with quiet, tense moments of character interaction that reveal the deep-seated trauma and fierce protective instincts driving the cast.

For fans of the series, this installment delivers everything that made the previous books addictive, while raising the stakes significantly. For newcomers willing to jump into the deep end of a well-established universe, Strange Trouble offers a wild, adrenaline-fueled ride that leaves a lasting impression. Laken Cane has crafted a visceral, gripping page-turner that proves urban fantasy can still bite hard when written with genuine attitude.
