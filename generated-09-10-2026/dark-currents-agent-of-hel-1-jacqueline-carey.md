# Dark Currents (Agent of Hel, #1)

**Author:** Jacqueline Carey  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

**Dark Currents: Where Urban Fantasy Meets Norse Myth and Dark Whispers**

Jacqueline Carey, the mastermind behind the *Kushiel’s Legacy* series, delivers another spellbinding world in *Dark Currents*, the first installment of the *Agent of Hel* series—a blend of urban fantasy, Norse mythology, and a dash of supernatural noir. Set in the idyllic yet sinisterly layered resort town of Pemkowet, this novel unfolds like a gothic fairy tale, where the mundane and the eldritch coexist in uneasy harmony. Carey’s signature lush prose and intricate world-building shine here, weaving a tale that is as rich in lore as it is in moral ambiguity.

At its core, *Dark Currents* explores the tension between duty and desire, between control and chaos. Daisy Johanssen, the novel’s protagonist, is a woman of duality—raised by a human mother but fathered by an incubus, she carries the weight of her demonic heritage like a second skin. As Hel’s enforcer, she serves as the bridge between the human police force and the supernatural underworld of Pemkowet, a town teeming with fairies, vampires, and other mythical beings. Her role is one of quiet authority, but beneath the surface, she grapples with the very nature of her existence. The novel’s central question lingers: *How much of herself can she surrender before the darkness she seeks to contain consumes her entirely?*

Carey’s writing is a feast for the senses, dripping with atmospheric detail and a poetic cadence that makes even the most mundane moments feel charged with possibility. The town of Pemkowet is a character in its own right—sun-drenched resorts masking hidden depths, where the air hums with the whispers of the old gods. The author’s knack for blending Norse mythology with contemporary urban fantasy is evident in the way she reimagines Hel, the Norse goddess of the underworld, not as a one-dimensional villain but as a complex, enigmatic figure whose influence shapes the very fabric of the town. The supernatural elements are seamlessly integrated, never feeling like gimmicks but rather as organic extensions of the world’s lore.

The plot itself is a gripping mystery, centered around the drowning of a young college student—a death that hints at something far more sinister than mere tragedy. Daisy, paired with her childhood crush, Officer Cody Fairfax (a werewolf with a secret), must navigate the treacherous waters of Pemkowet’s paranormal underbelly to uncover the truth. Their dynamic is electric, charged with the kind of slow-burn tension that readers of Carey’s work have come to expect. Cody’s werewolf nature adds another layer of complexity, his loyalty to Daisy tested by the very instincts that make him both a protector and a potential threat.

What makes *Dark Currents* particularly compelling is its exploration of identity and power. Daisy’s struggle is not just about solving a crime but about confronting the monster within her own bloodline. Carey masterfully balances the action and intrigue with deeper thematic questions—what does it mean to be an agent of chaos? How far will one go to protect the innocent when the line between them and the monstrous is so thin? The novel’s pacing is impeccable, with moments of dark humor and heartfelt vulnerability that keep the reader hooked.

The world-building is another standout feature. Carey’s ability to make the supernatural feel tangible is unparalleled. The fairies of Pemkowet are not the cutesy creatures of folklore but something far more dangerous, their whimsy masking a predatory nature. Vampires, ogres, and naiads all have their place in this ecosystem, each with their own agendas and secrets. The town’s reliance on paranormal tourism adds a layer of irony—beauty built on the backs of the very creatures that could unravel it all.

One of the novel’s most striking aspects is its tone. Carey strikes a delicate balance between gothic melancholy and dark humor, ensuring that even the most harrowing moments are leavened with wit. The dialogue crackles with personality, from Daisy’s sharp, no-nonsense demeanor to Cody’s gruff charm. The chemistry between them is palpable, their history and unresolved feelings adding depth to their partnership.

*Dark Currents* is more than just a mystery; it’s a meditation on the cost of power, the burden of legacy, and the fine line between hero and villain. Carey’s writing is immersive, her world so vivid that readers will find themselves lingering in Pemkowet long after the final page. It’s a novel that rewards close reading, where every detail—from the eerie beauty of the town to the moral ambiguities of its inhabitants—contributes to a story that is as haunting as it is enchanting.

For fans of urban fantasy with a mythological edge, *Dark Currents* is a must-read. Jacqueline Carey once again proves her versatility, crafting a tale that is as rich in lore as it is in emotional resonance. It’s a story about the monsters we are, the monsters we fear, and the monsters we must become to survive.
