# Pack of Lies (Paranormal Scene Investigations, #2)

**Author:** Laura Anne Gilman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

Urban fantasy has long relied on the hard-boiled detective archetype, but Laura Anne Gilman infuses this well-worn trope with a fresh, gritty pulse in Pack of Lies, the second installment of her Paranormal Scene Investigations series. Returning readers and newcomers alike are plunged back into the asphalt jungle of New York City, where the mundane and the magical collide not in a glitzy wizarding parlor, but in the grime, bureaucracy, and bureaucratic red tape of the city's supernatural underbelly. Gilman’s signature style shines through a sharp, no-nonsense narrative voice that treats the paranormal with the mundane exhaustion of a 9-to-5 job.

At the narrative center is Bonita Torres, whose journey from an adrift, unemployed college graduate to a hardened forensic investigator forms the emotional core of the series. Gilman’s character work remains one of her strongest assets; Bonita is neither a overpowered Chosen One nor a helpless bystander. She is competent, tired, cynical yet hopeful, and deeply human in a world dictated by inhuman forces. Her growth is tangible, anchored by her position within the Private Unaffiliated Paranormal Investigations team. Alongside her colleagues, most notably the enigmatic and formidable Big Dog, Benjamin Venec, Bonita navigates a magical community—the Cosa Nostradamus—that guards its secrets with lethal jealousy. 

The thematic spine of Pack of Lies explores the friction between assimilation and autonomy, the politics of belonging, and the heavy toll of institutional distrust. Gilman masterfully blends classic police procedural elements with supernatural world-building. The magic here is not just a deus ex machina; it leaves forensic traces, requires rigorous investigation, and demands specialized knowledge. This procedural grounding elevates the stakes, transforming supernatural conflicts into puzzles that require intellect, grit, and dangerous negotiation to solve. 

When a new, high-stakes case threatens to ignite a devastating war between humanity and the fatae, Gilman ratchets up the tension. The narrative thrives on paranoia and the constant looming threat that one misstep will dismantle everything the team has fought to build. The pacing is relentless, driven by snappy dialogue, realistic workplace friction among the team, and a cynical New York attitude that grounds the fantastical elements in gritty reality. 

Gilman’s prose is lean, atmospheric, and punchy, favoring sharp observations and kinetic action over purple prose. She treats the streets of New York as a living, breathing ecosystem where monsters pay taxes, mafia syndicates cast spells, and a bad day at the office might involve scraping ectoplasm off a subway car. 

Pack of Lies is a clever, fast-paced, and striking addition to contemporary urban fantasy. It proves that Gilman’s Paranormal Scene Investigations universe is vast, dangerous, and utterly addictive, offering a sophisticated blend of noir grit and magical mystery that leaves you eagerly anticipating the next crime scene.
