# Brothers Majere (Dragonlance: Preludes, #3)

**Author:** Kevin Stein  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

Title: Shadows and Claws: The Deeper Duality in Brothers Majere

In the vast and enduring tapestry of the Dragonlance universe, few characters command as much fascination as the Majere twins. Set against the atmospheric backdrop of Ansalon before the earthshaking events of the Chronicles, Kevin Stein’s Brothers Majere invites readers into a seemingly modest mystery that swells into a gripping exploration of identity, destiny, and the brooding duality of its protagonists. While many tie-in novels of its era risked feeling like mere genre exercises, Stein crafts a surprisingly textured narrative that uses a bizarre premise—the mass disappearance of domestic felines in a backwater village—to probe the psychological depths of Raistlin and Caramon.

The plot kicks off with deceptive charm. Tasked by a striking noblewoman to investigate missing cats in the superstitious village of Mereklar, the physically mismatched brothers, accompanied by the quintessential kender Earwig Lockpicker, stumble into a nightmare. What begins as a whimsical, localized oddity quickly curdles into bloodshed as prominent citizens are brutally murdered by an unseen, gigantic feline predator. Stein expertly modulates the tone, steering the narrative away from high-fantasy bombast and into the realm of a supernatural noir. The village of Mereklar ceases to be just a dot on a map; it becomes a pressure cooker of paranoia, ancient legends, and hidden grudges.

Yet, the true engine of Brothers Majere is not the central mystery, but the friction between the brothers themselves. Stein’s prose thrives on the complex codependency binding the sickly, cynical mage Raistlin to his muscular, fiercely protective warrior brother, Caramon. The author captures Raistlin's quiet malice and burgeoning ambition with sharp acuity, contrasting it sharply with Caramon’s earnest, blunt-force loyalty. Even as they navigate balls of string, glowing magical lines, and lethal beasts, the real suspense stems from the looming shadow of the Night of the Eye—the celestial event destined to test the limits of Raistlin’s power and the strength of their familial bond.

Stein’s style is brisk, accessible, and cinematic, perfectly suited for fans of the Dungeons and Dragons-inspired universe, yet sturdy enough to reward readers looking for character-driven fantasy. He avoids excessive exposition, trusting the rich lore of Ansalon to do the heavy lifting while focusing his lens tightly on the interpersonal dynamics. Earwig Lockpicker provides a necessary splash of classic kender levity, preventing the gloom from suffocating the adventure, but the emotional core remains intensely dark.

Brothers Majere is a compelling, tightly wound prelude that punches above the weight of standard tie-in fiction. It takes a quirky, high-concept premise and uses it as a crucible to forge the legendary dynamic fans have cherished for decades. For anyone eager to witness the early, formative steps of Ansalon’s most iconic sorcerer and his steadfast brother, this book is an essential, darkly entertaining journey into the claws of destiny.
