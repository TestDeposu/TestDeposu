# Crossed Blades (Fallen Blade, #3)

**Author:** Kelly McCullough  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

Kelly McCullough returns with *Crossed Blades*, the compelling third installment of the *Fallen Blade* series, proving once again that urban fantasy can be both deeply personal and relentlessly action-packed. Published in 2012, this tight, fast-paced novel leans heavily into its defining core theme: the inescapable gravity of the past and the high cost of reinvention. 

At the center of the narrative is Aral Kingslayer, a former temple assassin who has spent six years carving out a quiet, shadowed existence as a jack-of-all-trades on the wrong side of the law. McCullough establishes Aral not as a brooding caricature, but as a nuanced survivor trying to outrun a blood-soaked history. The thematic tension of the novel ignites when Aral’s past literally walks back into his life in the form of Jax Seldansbane—a lethal fellow Blade and Aral’s onetime fiancée. Jax brings terrifying news: the dark forces that obliterated Aral’s old life are regrouping, and she needs his unmatched skills to neutralize the threat. 

This sets up a brilliant narrative tug-of-war between nostalgia and necessity, identity and obligation. McCullough explores how difficult it is to truly bury the person you used to be, especially when the world refuses to let you stay hidden. Aral is forced to weigh his hard-won peace and new responsibilities against a looming cataclysm that threatens everything he holds dear. As old loyalties clash with new survival instincts, Aral is thrust into a sprawling conflict where paranoia reigns supreme and he has no idea who—or what—to trust.

McCullough’s authorial style is sharp, witty, and grounded in a rich, mythos-driven world. Where many fantasy authors rely on bloated exposition, McCullough prefers a lean, kinetic prose style that mirrors the razor-sharp reflexes of his protagonist. The pacing is relentless, moving seamlessly from gritty underworld negotiations to high-stakes political intrigue and kinetic magical combat. Yet, what truly elevates *Crossed Blades* above standard genre fare is the emotional resonance McCullough weaves into the violence. The banter is biting, the character dynamics are layered with history and regret, and the stakes feel genuinely perilous. 

Ultimately, *Crossed Blades* is a triumphant mid-series pivot that deepens the mythology of the *Fallen Blade* universe while keeping the adrenaline pumping. It challenges its protagonist—and the reader—to consider whether we define ourselves by the blood on our hands or the choices we make when the blade is drawn once more. For fans of smart, fast-moving urban fantasy with a heavy dose of noir grit and sword-and-sorcery flair, McCullough’s latest outing is an absolute must-read.
