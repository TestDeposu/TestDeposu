# Mark of the Raven (The Ravenwood Saga, #1)

**Author:** Morgan L. Busse  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

Mark of the Raven opens with a tender, almost whispered invitation: “Hello, Reader. I am honored to know you.” Morgan L. Busse uses that direct address to pull us instantly into a world where books are living interlocutors, and grief is the quiet engine that drives the narrative forward. Thirteen‑year‑old Piper, still reeling from the loss of her parents, is thrust into the austere household of Aunt Beryl, a move that feels both geographic and emotional exile. Her younger brother Phoenix, who lives on the autism spectrum, becomes not just a sibling but a mirror—his heightened perception allows him to hear the faint call of *Novus Fabula*, the mysterious volume that beckons from the depths of the Verboten Library.

The library itself is a character: cavernous, labyrinthine, and humming with latent magic. Busse’s prose shines when she describes the shelves as “breathing,” each tome a pulse waiting to sync with a reader’s heartbeat. This tactile, sensory language makes the fantastical feel intimate, turning the act of reading into a ritual of discovery and healing. The central theme—how stories can mend broken hearts—is explored through Piper’s quest to understand the truth behind *Novus Fabula* and, by extension, the truth about her own family’s past. Busse balances the weight of sorrow with moments of wonder; the siblings’ encounters with the library’s sentient volumes are both whimsical and poignant, echoing the gentle awe found in classics like *A Wrinkle in Time* while grounding the magic in the very real, often painful, process of growing up after loss.

Busse’s style is lyrical yet accessible, favoring short, evocative sentences that mirror the rhythm of a child’s wonder. She intersperses dialogue that feels authentic—Piper’s tentative bravery, Phoenix’s literal yet insightful observations—without over‑explaining the magic system. This restraint invites readers to fill the gaps with their own imagination, making the experience personal. The novel’s pacing mirrors a heartbeat: slow, contemplative passages of grief give way to sudden, bright bursts of revelation when the library’s secrets unfold.

What makes *Mark of the Raven* stand out is its refusal to treat grief as a obstacle to be overcome; instead, it is woven into the fabric of the adventure, allowing the characters to grow alongside the magical world they inhabit. Busse’s reverence for books as living entities reinforces a timeless message: stories are not just escapes—they are companions that help us navigate the darkest corridors of our souls.

For middle‑grade readers seeking a tale that blends heart‑felt emotion with enchanting mystery, this debut offers a resonant, beautifully crafted start to the Ravenwood Saga.
