# Raising Steam (Discworld, #40; Moist von Lipwig, #3)

**Author:** Terry Pratchett  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

Progress is a steam engine, and Terry Pratchett is the brilliant engineer shoveling coal into the furnace of human imagination. In Raising Steam, the fortieth installment of the iconic Discworld series and the third outing for the delightfully untrustworthy Moist von Lipwig, Pratchett captures the chaotic, exhilarating, and terrifying birth of the industrial age. Published in 2014, this late-period masterpiece finds the author reflecting on societal evolution, the resistance to change, and the power of innovation, all while maintaining the razor-sharp wit and boundless compassion that defined his legendary career.

The premise is as simple as it is disruptive: progress has finally puffed its way into Ankh-Morpork. Mister Simnel has engineered a great, clanging, fire-breathing iron horse that harnesses earth, air, fire, and water to conquer distance and time. Naturally, this heralds an era of unprecedented disruption. Enter Moist von Lipwig—conman turned bureaucrat, master of the Post Office, the Mint, and the Royal Bank. Drafted by the infallible Patrician, Lord Vetinari, Moist is tasked with managing the newly minted railway system. It is a job he neither wants nor understands, requiring far too much grease and physical labor for a man who prefers the nimble weightlessness of words. 

Yet, Moist must navigate more than just tracks and timetables. The arrival of the iron engine rattles the delicate geopolitical balance of the Discworld. Traditionalist dwarfs, terrified of the cultural erasure threatened by fast travel and open borders, form radical factions. Goblins find themselves caught in the gears of the emerging industrial machine, and Moist must contend with stubborn controllers, treacherous terrain, and the looming threat of the whole venture going catastrophically off the rails. 

At its core, the main theme of Raising Steam is the inevitability of change and the friction it creates between tradition and modernity. Pratchett has always used his fantasy sandbox to hold a mirror up to our own world, and here he tackles the Industrial Revolution with characteristic clarity. He explores how infrastructure connects people, how technology alters our perception of the world, and how institutions must adapt or perish. Yet, Pratchett is careful not to paint progress as an unblemished utopian dream. He acknowledges the soot, the displacement, the fear of the old guard, and the exploitation that often accompanies the march of industry. Through the railway, Pratchett examines how humanity—and trolls, dwarfs, goblins, and vampires—negotiates the bumpy transition from the old ways to the new.

True to form, the author's style remains a masterclass in satirical world-building and linguistic wizardry. Pratchett’s prose is effortlessly conversational, deeply philosophical, and relentlessly funny. He possesses a rare talent for blending high-concept socio-political commentary with utterly absurd visual gags. His characters are never mere caricatures; even the most fantastical beings are imbued with recognizable human vulnerabilities, petty anxieties, and deep-seated yearnings for belonging. Moist von Lipwig is a magnificent vehicle for this style—his inner monologues are a delightful mix of panic, showmanship, and reluctant heroism. 

What makes Raising Steam particularly striking is the palpable sense of urgency that permeates the text. Knowing that Pratchett was battling early-onset Alzheimer's disease during the writing of this book adds a profound, poignant layer to its themes. The book is, in many ways, about racing against time, leaving a legacy, and building something that will outlast the builder. It reads like a parting gift from a literary giant who wanted to remind us that while the world changes fast, decency, adaptability, and a good sense of humor are the best tracks to ride on.

Raising Steam is a triumphant, witty, and deeply resonant addition to the Discworld canon. It proves that even as his world modernized, Pratchett’s storytelling heart remained timeless. For veterans of Ankh-Morpork and newcomers alike, this journey down the tracks is an absolute must-read that will leave you cheering, thinking, and perhaps listening a little more closely for the whistle of a distant train.
