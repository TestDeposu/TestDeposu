# House of Cards (Negotiator Trilogy/Old Races Universe, #2)

**Author:** C.E. Murphy  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

C.E. Murphy returns to the Old Races Universe with House of Cards, the second installment in the Negotiator Trilogy, delivering a sultry, high-stakes collision of law enforcement and underground desire. Set against the sun-baked, deceptively quiet backdrop of Johnson County, Texas, Murphy weaves a tale where the boundaries of duty and temptation blur beyond recognition. This contemporary romance with mature themes successfully marries the gritty realism of police procedurals with the intensely charged atmosphere of an exclusive, secret-shrouding lifestyle club known as The Stables.

At the heart of the novel is a brilliant study in contrasting personalities and authority. Texas Ranger Cole Carter is the quintessential man of action—hardened by tactical training, tactical vests, and the grim realities of the streets. Yet, Cole finds his well-drilled composure thoroughly dismantled not by a cartel or a high-risk hostage situation, but by County Judge Bruce Montgomery. Bruce is a man built like a monolith—proudly self-assured, possessing a sharp legal mind, and radiating an imposing physical presence that earns him the moniker "The Bull." Murphy crafts these protagonists with distinct, authentic narrative voices that immediately ground the reader in their internal conflicts and undeniable mutual magnetism.

The central plot hinges on a classic noir setup: blackmail within an elite, password-locked circle. Judge Montgomery, desperate to unmask the extortionist targeting members of The Stables, enlists Cole for an undercover op. What follows is a delicious narrative subversion. Cole, who has stared down the barrel of a gun and survived police academy tasers, confesses that nothing in his harrowing career could have prepared him for standing half-naked in a leather harness and collar. This moment encapsulates Murphy’s stylistic prowess: she balances visceral tension with wry, self-aware humor, keeping the narrative grounded even as it explores deeply erotic and vulnerable territory.

Murphy’s style in House of Cards thrives on contrast. The sun-drenched, rugged Texan landscape serves as a stark foil to the dimly lit, velvet-and-leather interiors of The Stables. The author uses this dual setting to mirror the psychological states of her protagonists. On the outside, they are pillars of the justice system, men who wield power and uphold the law. Inside the club, stripped of their badges and judicial robes, they must navigate a delicate ecosystem of power exchange, trust, and raw emotional exposure. 

The pacing is brisk, driven as much by the mounting tension of the blackmail investigation as by the slow-burn, combustible chemistry between Cole and Bruce. Murphy excels at dialogue that crackles with understated desire and professional rivalry. Every shared look—like Cole getting caught staring at Bruce’s chest in a parking lot—feels loaded with subtext, raising the emotional stakes of the romance. 

House of Cards is a compelling, striking addition to the Negotiator Trilogy. It proves that C.E. Murphy’s Old Races Universe is vast enough to accommodate not only supernatural elements and epic conflicts, but also deeply human, gritty, and passionately intense contemporary stories. For readers who enjoy high-stakes romance, formidable characters who command the page, and mysteries that test both the heart and the badge, this book delivers a winning verdict.
