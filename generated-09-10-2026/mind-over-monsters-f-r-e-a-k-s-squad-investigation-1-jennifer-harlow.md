# Mind Over Monsters (F.R.E.A.K.S. Squad Investigation, #1)

**Author:** Jennifer Harlow  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

Title: Subverting the Supernatural: Jennifer Harlow’s Mind Over Monsters Delivers a Bite of Urban Fantasy Romance

Urban fantasy is a crowded graveyard. Between lovesick vampires, brooding werewolves, and sarcastic heroines saving the world in stiletto heels, the genre often collapses under the weight of its own clichés. Yet, every so often, a novel arrives that takes these very tropes, grinds them down, and builds something remarkably fresh and fiercely entertaining. Jennifer Harlow’s 2014 novel, Mind Over Monsters, the explosive first installment in the F.R.E.A.K.S. Squad Investigation series, is precisely such a book. It is a witty, adrenaline-fueled genre mashup that pairs high-stakes paranormal investigation with a refreshingly messy, high-tension romance.

At the center of Harlow’s imaginative world is Special Agent Beatrice Alexander, a telekinetic powerhouse whose resume includes taking down zombies, psychotic vampires, and the occasional stubborn troll. Bea is not your typical delicate supernatural flower; she is battle-hardened, sharp-tongued, and utterly relentless. However, as the novel’s clever subtitle suggests—A F.R.E.A.K.S. Love Story—Bea’s most formidable battlefield in Mind Over Monsters isn’t a horde of the undead. It is the perilous, unpredictable terrain of the human (and sub-human) heart. 

The core theme of Harlow’s novel revolves around the intersection of professional duty and visceral, raw vulnerability. While Bea has mastered the art of policing the supernatural underworld, she is entirely unequipped for the siege of romance. Her target? Will Price, her brooding, alpha-male werewolf teammate. Harlow uses this dynamic to subvert traditional paranormal romance expectations. Instead of the typical damsel-in-distress narrative, we get a hyper-competent heroine actively pursuing the man of her dreams, all while dodging the lethal threats of their supernatural occupation. This role-reversal infuses the narrative with an infectious comedic energy and a distinct feminist edge.

Harlow’s writing style is punchy, cinematic, and unapologetically fast-paced. She favors snappy dialogue over agonizing internal monologues, keeping the momentum relentlessly high. The author possesses a distinct knack for blending dark, gritty action with sharp, self-aware humor. When Bea and her team are dispatched to the dense, unforgiving wilds of North Carolina to investigate the bloody killing fields of a rogue werewolf pack, the tone shifts seamlessly from playful workplace banter to pulse-pounding survival horror. 

The world-building in Mind Over Monsters is woven effortlessly into the fabric of the plot. Harlow avoids massive info-dumps, preferring instead to let the F.R.E.A.K.S. bureaucracy and the mechanics of telekinesis and lycanthropy reveal themselves organically through Bea’s lived experience. The North Carolina wilderness setting acts almost as a secondary character—claustrophobic, mysterious, and violently indifferent to the romantic ambitions of the protagonists. It is a backdrop that heightens the stakes, ensuring that the reader never forgets that underneath the snappy banter and flirtatious tension, deadly monsters are hunting them.

What ultimately makes Mind Over Monsters a standout read is its refusal to take itself too seriously while still delivering genuine emotional resonance. Bea’s internal conflict feels authentic; her fear of emotional rejection is every bit as palpable as the physical threat of a rogue werewolf’s claws. Harlow captures the awkward, terrifying, and exhilarating reality of falling for a coworker—amplified, of course, by the presence of fangs and telekinetic outbursts.

For fans of smart, sarcastic heroines, action-packed paranormal mysteries, and romance with a sharp bite, Jennifer Harlow’s series opener is an absolute must-read. Mind Over Monsters proves that sometimes the scariest monster you can face isn't a zombie or a troll—it’s the terrifying vulnerability of letting someone in.
