# Sleeping in Flame (Answered Prayers, #2)

**Author:** Jonathan Carroll  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Unknown

Jonathan Carroll’s *Sleeping in Flame*—the second installment in his *Answered Prayers* trilogy—is a book that dares to unravel the seams of the sacred, the mundane, and the utterly bizarre with the kind of fearless, dark humor that only a master of the macabre can pull off. If the first volume introduced us to a world where prayers are answered in ways both profound and grotesque, this sequel dives deeper into the existential absurdity of divine whimsy, where the line between salvation and damnation blurs into something far stranger than either.

At its core, *Sleeping in Flame* is a meditation on faith, desperation, and the ways in which human longing distorts reality. Carroll’s genius lies in his ability to take the most mundane of human desires—love, revenge, survival—and twist them into surreal, often nightmarish scenarios. The book follows a cast of characters whose prayers, when answered, spiral them into increasingly surreal predicaments. A man’s plea for a second chance at life might manifest as a literal rebirth, only to leave him trapped in a body that refuses to age, while another’s desperate wish for companionship could unravel into a love story so twisted it borders on the Lovecraftian. Carroll doesn’t just play with the idea of answered prayers; he weaponizes them, turning them into a force that exposes the fragility of human identity and the terrifying arbitrariness of existence.

The prose is as lush and unsettling as one might expect from Carroll, a writer who has spent a career perfecting the art of the eerie. His sentences are long, winding, and densely layered with implications, each paragraph dripping with a kind of gothic elegance that makes the reader pause mid-sentence to absorb the weight of what’s being said. There’s a rhythmic cadence to his writing, almost incantatory, as if he’s weaving a spell to lull the reader into a state of uneasy comfort before delivering a punchline that feels like a gut punch. His descriptions of the surreal landscapes that emerge from these answered prayers are vivid and often grotesque, blending the mundane with the monstrous in ways that feel both hilarious and horrifying.

What makes *Sleeping in Flame* particularly compelling is Carroll’s refusal to offer easy answers or moral clarity. The book doesn’t judge the characters for their prayers—it simply observes the chaos that unfolds as a result. This neutrality allows the reader to grapple with the themes on their own terms, whether they’re laughing at the absurdity of it all or shuddering at the implications of a universe where every wish, no matter how selfish or petty, is granted in kind. The result is a narrative that feels both deeply human and utterly alien, a reflection of our own desires and fears held up to a mirror that distorts them beyond recognition.

Themes of identity and transformation are woven throughout, as characters find themselves unrecognizable in the wake of their answered prayers. One might pray for power and end up with a body that’s physically invincible but emotionally hollow, while another might seek redemption and instead be cursed with a literal second chance that feels more like a prison. Carroll’s exploration of these ideas is both philosophical and deeply personal, making the book feel like a conversation about the nature of the self rather than just a collection of bizarre anecdotes.

Visually, the book is a feast for the imagination. Carroll’s world-building is expansive, with each answered prayer creating a new, self-contained universe that feels both intimate and vast. The surrealism isn’t just for show—it’s a tool to highlight the absurdity of human existence, to show how easily our most sincere wishes can be twisted into something monstrous. There’s a sense of inevitability to the chaos, as if the universe itself is a cruel joke, and the characters are just the punchlines.

For fans of dark humor, existential dread, and the kind of literature that lingers in the mind long after the last page, *Sleeping in Flame* is a must-read. It’s a book that challenges the reader to confront their own desires and fears, to ask themselves what they would pray for if they knew the answer might be worse than the problem. Carroll doesn’t offer comfort or resolution—he offers a mirror, and the reflection it shows is never quite what you expected.

In the end, *Sleeping in Flame* is more than just a sequel; it’s a deepening of the themes introduced in the first book, a descent into the heart of the absurd where the only certainty is that nothing is certain. It’s a book that will leave you laughing, unsettled, and perhaps even a little afraid of what you might wish for if you knew the universe would answer.
