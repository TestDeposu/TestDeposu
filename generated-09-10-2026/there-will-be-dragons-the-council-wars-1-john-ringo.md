# There Will Be Dragons (The Council Wars, #1)

**Author:** John Ringo  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

John Ringo’s There Will Be Dragons stands as a fascinating entry into the subgenre of apocalyptic fantasy, boldly asking a deceptively simple question: What happens when the gods fall out of the sky and humanity is thrust overnight from utopia into the raw struggle for survival? Published in 2013, the novel serves as the opening salvo of The Council Wars series, showcasing Ringo’s signature blend of high-concept speculative world-building, military tactical detail, and unabashedly epic storytelling. 

At the heart of the novel lies a striking central theme: the jarring juxtaposition of absolute post-scarcity luxury and brutal, primal necessity. Ringo explores the fragility of a civilization completely dependent on omnipresent technology—the Net—which has eliminated disease, aging, want, and conflict. When the immortal-like ruling Council shatters into factional warfare, this paradise evaporates in an instant. The global infrastructure collapses, stranding a populace wholly unprepared for the realities of hunger, weather, and violence. Yet, rather than wallowing purely in despair, the narrative pivots toward human resilience. Ringo examines how communities adapt, focusing particularly on those who had voluntarily stepped away from the utopia to live agrarian, self-sufficient lives. 

The novel’s protagonist, Edmund Talbot, embodies this thematic dualism. Living quietly as a master smith and historian in the idyllic village of Raven's Mill, Talbot initially appears to be a humble figure of the old-fashioned, simpler life. However, as the world outside crumbles and refugees, bandits, and old enemies converge on his sanctuary, Talbot's true nature is unveiled. He is not merely a craftsman of the soil, but a remnant of the immortal elite, harboring dangerous secrets, formidable combat capabilities, and a past steeped in the very wars that shaped the fallen world. Through Talbot, Ringo explores the idea that beneath the veneer of modern peace and comfort, humanity's warrior instincts never truly vanish; they merely lie dormant, waiting for the spark of necessity to reawaken them.

Ringo’s stylistic approach in There Will Be Dragons is unapologetically maximalist. Known for his backgrounds in military service and heavy sci-fi, the author infuses the narrative with meticulous attention to detail, ranging from the intricacies of medieval blacksmithing to the apocalyptic devastation wrought by high-tech warfare. This creates a fascinating stylistic collision: classic sword-and-sorcery tropes—complete with noble smiths, desperate villagers, and, as the title promises, the resurgence of mythical beasts—are filtered through a hard science-fiction lens. The dragons of the title are not magical creatures born of ancient lore, but rather the terrifying, genetically engineered war-machines of a fallen god-like civilization. 

While the pacing occasionally pauses to indulge in extensive world-building exposition and Ringo’s characteristic socio-political commentary, the sheer momentum of the plot keeps the reader hooked. The transition from a serene, pastoral existence to a desperate siege mentality is handled with visceral intensity. Ringo does not shy away from the darker implications of the collapse, yet he balances the grim realism with robust heroics and moments of deeply human connection. 

There Will Be Dragons is a gripping, high-octane speculative epic that successfully bridges the gap between science fiction and fantasy. By dismantling a high-tech paradise and forcing its inhabitants to pick up the pieces with hammer, anvil, and steel, John Ringo delivers a compelling critique of dependence on technology and a thrilling celebration of old-school grit. It is a striking preview of a massive, imaginative conflict that will leave readers eager to see how humanity claws its way back from the ashes.
