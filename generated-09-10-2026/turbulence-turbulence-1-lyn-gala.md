# Turbulence (Turbulence #1)

**Author:** Lyn Gala  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

In Turbulence, acclaimed speculative fiction and romance author Lyn Gala carves out a gritty, intimate niche in the vast expanse of military science fiction. Known for her ability to weave deeply psychological character studies into high-stakes, hyper-masculine environments, Gala turns her sharp lens toward the monotony and raw tension of border patrols in a galaxy at war. The result is a taut, emotionally resonant novel that balances the visceral claustrophobia of military life with a tender, slow-burning exploration of identity and unexpected connection.

At the narrative’s core is Corporal Jacqs Glebov, a battle-weary veteran whose worldview has been shaped by the unforgiving trenches. Gala brilliantly captures Jacqs’s disillusionment; he is a man craving the simple comforts of a sturdy bunk and the unspoken camaraderie of fellow soldiers, only to find himself babysitting idealistic, greenhorn cadets on the remote outpost of the Candiru. Into this powder keg steps Commander Zeke Waters, a newly promoted officer sent to gain practical leadership experience. Zeke’s arrival disrupts the precarious ecosystem of the base, igniting a sharp, magnetic fascination with Jacqs—an adamantly heterosexual soldier whose rough edges and pragmatic worldview draw Zeke in despite every warning sign of common sense.

Gala’s stylistic prowess shines in how she handles this central dynamic. Eschewing the glossy, technocratic utopianism common in much of the genre, she opts for a tactile, lived-in realism. The military backdrop feels heavy, smelling of ozone, cheap rations, and the constant, low-humming anxiety of a protracted war. Against this bleak canvas, the burgeoning attraction between Jacqs and Zeke feels all the more urgent and dangerous. Gala does not rush their evolution; she masterfully navigates the friction of their differing backgrounds, sexual orientations, and military ranks. The romance is charged with vulnerability, forcing both men to confront the rigid rules that have governed—and stunted—their lives.

The overarching theme of Turbulence examines the human cost of institutionalization. Gala asks a poignant question: when an empire reduces individuals to mere cogs in a war machine, how do they reclaim their capacity for love, choice, and individuality? Both Jacqs and Zeke are trapped not just by the physical boundaries of their remote posting, but by the expectations of the military apparatus they serve. Their growing bond acts as a rebellion against a system designed to strip them of nuance.

Striking, sharply observed, and emotionally grounded, Turbulence is a triumph of character-driven sci-fi romance. Lyn Gala proves once again that the most compelling battles are often fought not against external enemies, but within the confines of the human heart. For readers looking for a smart, gritty romance that refuses to sacrifice world-building for emotional payoff, this novel is an essential deployment.
