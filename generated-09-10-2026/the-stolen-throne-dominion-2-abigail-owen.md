# The Stolen Throne (Dominion, #2)

**Author:** Abigail Owen  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

Stepping back into the scorching, treacherous sands of Aryd with Abigail Owen’s *The Stolen Throne* is a masterclass in high-stakes fantasy romance where the line between survival and damnation blurs into a dangerous haze. As the second installment in the Dominion series, following the explosive events of *The Liar’s Crown*, this novel wastes no time plunging its readers back into a gilded cage of deception, royal intrigue, and a romance as consuming as it is perilous. Owen crafts a gripping narrative that thrives on the tension of dual identities, internal corruption, and the agonizing weight of duty, cementing her place as a compelling voice in modern romantic fantasy.

At the heart of *The Stolen Throne* lies the timeless yet brilliantly executed theme of identity versus destiny. The protagonist is caught in an impossible vice: while her twin sister—the rightful queen—clings to life in the unforgiving desert, she has been forced to wear the crown of a false monarch, sitting beside the monstrous King Eidolon. This duality explores the psychological toll of impostor syndrome taken to literal, life-or-death extremes. The narrative asks a profound question: How much of yourself can you sacrifice to protect the people you love before the mask becomes your actual face? Owen handles this existential dread with sharp pacing, ensuring the protagonist's internal struggle for agency mirrors the external chaos of a kingdom on the brink of collapse.

Complementing this theme of fractured identity is Owen’s signature atmospheric style. Her prose is lush, visceral, and dripping with sensory details that make the heat of the desert and the oppressive shadows of the palace palpable. The author excels at world-building through emotion; every setting reflects the internal state of the characters. When the narrative shifts to Reven—the protagonist's Shadowraith and the beating heart of the book—the style darkens beautifully. Reven’s internal battle with his escalating, increasingly sinister shadows serves as a brilliant externalization of the corruption surrounding them. It is gothic romance meets epic fantasy, where love is not a safe harbor, but a volatile force that demands sacrifice.

What makes *The Stolen Throne* a striking read is its relentless momentum. Owen avoids the dreaded middle-book slump by constantly raising the stakes. Just as escape seems possible from King Eidolon's clutches, the threat multiplies with the revelation of a traitor within their inner circle. This paranoia injects a thrilling whodunit dynamic into the fantasy framework, keeping readers guessing until the final pages. Furthermore, the romantic tension is exquisitely agonizing. The dynamic between the protagonist and Reven is layered with the haunting realization that while one evil actively hunts them, the other loves them to the point of self-destruction. This moral gray area elevates the romance from a standard genre trope to something deeply poignant and complex.

Ultimately, Abigail Owen has delivered a sequel that outshines expectations, offering a breathless blend of courtly betrayal, dark magic, and a desperate fight for a stolen crown. *The Stolen Throne* is a captivating triumph of romantic fantasy that leaves its characters—and its readers—forever changed by the shadows they court.
