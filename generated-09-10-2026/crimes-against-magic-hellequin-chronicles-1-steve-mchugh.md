# Crimes Against Magic (Hellequin Chronicles, #1)

**Author:** Steve McHugh  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

Trapped in a web of political corruption and the crushing weight of institutional betrayal, Steve McHugh delivers a masterclass in modern urban fantasy with Crimes Against Magic. Launching the celebrated Hellequin Chronicles with an electrifying jolt, McHugh proves once again why he remains a towering figure in speculative fiction. This riveting installment shifts the narrative lens to Celine Moro, an elite Blackcoat whose dedication to investigating Union-wide crimes shatters against the bedrock of betrayal by the very allies she trusted. 

At its core, the novel explores the timeless, gripping theme of resilience in the face of absolute systemic corruption. McHugh uses Celine’s descent from a proud upholder of the law to a desperate fugitive as a vehicle to dissect power, institutional rot, and the heavy toll of vengeance. The author brilliantly captures the psychological burden of being cast out by a system one swore to protect. Rather than wallowing in despair, Celine dusts off the lethal skills of her shadowed past, transforming heartbreak into a calculated weapon for truth. This thematic friction between blind obedience to the state and the messy, vital pursuit of personal justice gives the narrative its throbbing pulse.

McHugh’s writing style is unapologetically kinetic, punchy, and atmospheric. He has a rare talent for balancing high-octane action sequences with sharp, biting dialogue and intricate world-building. Union space feels vast, lived-in, and dangerously unpredictable under his pen, serving as a fittingly gritty backdrop for Celine’s crusade. The pacing is relentless, refusing to grant the reader—or the protagonist—a moment to breathe, yet it never sacrifices character depth for the sake of spectacle. 

What elevates Crimes Against Magic above standard genre fare is its delicious tonal duality. It is a story about profound betrayal, yet it is laced with a dark, sardonic humor—most notably encapsulated in Celine’s grim comfort in knowing how much guilt her former friends will eventually suffer. McHugh crafts characters who are delightfully flawed, morally gray, and entirely human, making their triumphs and failures resonate deeply. 

For fans of fast-paced, high-stakes speculative fiction infused with mystery, magic, and relentless attitude, Crimes Against Magic is an absolute must-read. McHugh has crafted a breathtaking opening salvo that demands your immediate attention and leaves you desperate for the carnage that follows.
