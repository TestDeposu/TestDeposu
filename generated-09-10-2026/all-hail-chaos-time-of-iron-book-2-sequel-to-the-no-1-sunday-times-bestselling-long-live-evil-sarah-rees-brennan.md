# All Hail Chaos (Time of Iron Book 2): Sequel to the No. 1 Sunday Times Bestselling Long Live Evil

**Author:** Sarah Rees Brennan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2026

When Sarah Rees Brennan stormed the fantasy landscape with Long Live Evil, she proved that she was playing a completely different game than most contemporary genre authors. Now, with All Hail Chaos, the second installment in the Time of Iron series, she doesn't just lower the stakes—she gleefully burns the board to cinders. This is a novel that thrives on moral ambiguity, genre deconstruction, and the intoxicating thrill of watching a protagonist weaponize her own fandom against a world designed to crush her.

The central theme of All Hail Chaos explores the dangerous gulf between romanticized fiction and lived reality. Rae entered her favorite fantasy universe armed with the comforting knowledge of a dedicated reader, believing she could navigate tropes like a seasoned tactician. Instead, Brennan traps her in a living nightmare where swords are heavy, blood is permanent, and her favorite fictional character—the all-powerful Emperor—turns out to be far more terrifying, complex, and unhinged than the ink-and-paper version ever suggested. The book asks a sharp, uncomfortable question: what happens when the villain you swooned over in chapters written for entertainment turns out to be a monster in the harsh daylight of survival? 

Brennan’s signature style is immediately recognizable here: fiercely witty, unapologetically cynical, and laced with a razor-sharp narrative voice that cuts straight through traditional epic fantasy pomposity. She masters the delicate balancing act of high-stakes political maneuvering and biting meta-commentary without ever losing the momentum of the plot. The prose moves at a relentless pace, effortlessly jumping between the Cobra hiding in a haunted, secret-drenched manor and Emer and Lia sparking a desperate, gritty revolution in the gutter-choked streets of the capital. 

What makes All Hail Chaos truly stand out is its embrace of the wicked bargain. Rae’s journey is not one of traditional hero's redemption, but of clever manipulation, survival, and morally gray diplomacy. Fake engagements and dark pacts replace starry-eyed romance, reinforcing the book's core warning: in a land teetering on the edge of undead invasions and external sieges, the most perilous trap of all is simply believing in someone else's goodness. 

Brennan has crafted a sequel that is wickedly smart, deeply subversive, and intensely addictive. It is a triumph of subversive fantasy that rewards readers who love their heroines a little bit messy and their villains irresistibly complicated.
