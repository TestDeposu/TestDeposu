# Too Many Magicians (Lord Darcy, #2)

**Author:** Randall Garrett  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

Step into a twentieth century that never was, where the steam engine was bypassed in favor of applied thaumaturgy, and the British Empire spans a continent united under the descendants of Richard the Lionheart. In Too Many Magicians, Randall Garrett constructs a world where science did not take the empirical path of physics and chemistry, but rather the rigorous, codified path of ritual magic. Yet, human nature remains stubbornly unchanged. When murder intrudes upon the hallowed halls of the International Sorcerer’s Convention in London, Garrett deftly marries the tropes of classic Golden Age detective fiction with the intricate world-building of alternate history.

At the heart of Garrett’s narrative style is a brilliant commitment to logical deduction. Despite the presence of levitation, scrying, and demonic wardings, Lord Darcy approaches crime-solving with the cold, methodical precision of a classical sleuth in the mold of Nero Wolfe or Sherlock Holmes. Magic in this universe is not a chaotic deus ex machina; it is an exact science with its own immutable laws, limitations, and forensic methods. This creates a fascinating thematic tension between the supernatural and the rational. The book's central theme explores the intersection of order and chaos—how structured systems, whether legal, magical, or political, are constantly tested by the unpredictable passions of human ambition and greed.

The plot kicks into high gear when Sir James Zwinge, London’s Chief Forensics Sorcerer, is discovered dead in a classic locked-room mystery. Suspicion immediately falls upon Master Sean O Lochlainn, Lord Darcy’s own trusted forensic sorcerer and magic-wielding assistant, who had recently locked horns with the victim over a groundbreaking medical enchantment. To clear his friend, Darcy must navigate a veritable convention of master practitioners—each with their own egos, rivalries, and arcane specialties. Garrett weaves this investigation with a tense geopolitical subplot involving naval espionage and simmering hostilities with the formidable Polish Empire, elevating the stakes far beyond a simple domestic murder.

Garrett’s prose is brisk, witty, and deeply atmospheric. He takes immense pleasure in dropping the reader into a familiar yet radically altered society, treating the blending of feudal aristocracy, Catholic-dominated bureaucracy, and everyday sorcery with matter-of-fact realism. There are no infodumps here; the world lives and breathes in the background while Darcy and Master Sean interrogate suspects, analyze magical residues, and untangle a web of professional jealousy. Master Sean serves as the perfect foil to Darcy, grounding the aristocratic investigator’s sharp intellect with practical wit and profound loyalty.

Nominated for the Hugo Award and celebrated as a pillar of alternate history fiction, Too Many Magicians is a triumphant fusion of genres. It respects the reader's intelligence, offering fair clues, a watertight locked-room puzzle, and a rich, immersive setting that begs to be explored. Whether you are a devotee of traditional whodunits or a connoisseur of speculative world-building, Garrett’s classic masterpiece delivers a magical concoction of mystery and imagination that remains utterly irresistible.
