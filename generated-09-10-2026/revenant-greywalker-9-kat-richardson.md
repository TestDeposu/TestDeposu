# Revenant (Greywalker, #9)

**Author:** Kat Richardson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

Kat Richardson’s *Revenant*, the ninth installment in the enduringly popular *Greywalker* series, proves that urban fantasy can mature without losing its teeth. Since her accidental death and resuscitation left her straddling the veil between the living and the paranormal, private investigator Harper Blaine has evolved from a novice stumbling through the occult underground into a seasoned investigator of the uncanny. In *Revenant*, Richardson takes everything that has grounded this series—grit, atmospheric world-building, and a deeply human protagonist—and launches it onto a terrifying, continental scale.

The central theme of *Revenant* is the inescapable gravity of legacy and the dangerous hubris of trying to weaponize history. When waves of inexplicable turmoil and destruction ripple from Europe to Seattle, Harper and her partner, Quinton, trace the corruption back to its source: Quinton’s own estranged father, James Purlis, and his spectral military unit, the Ghost Division. Richardson uses this father-son dynamic to explore deep-seated themes of betrayal, inherited trauma, and the lengths to which people will go to achieve immortality or control. Purlis is not merely a mustache-twisting villain; he is a man blinded by ambition, unwittingly serving as a pawn for something far older and hungrier than himself. 

As the investigation shifts from the Pacific Northwest to the shadowy corners of the Old World, the narrative broadens into a chilling exploration of ancient evil. The introduction of a forgotten cult aligned with Purlis elevates the stakes from a personal vendetta to a global catastrophe. Richardson excels at blending contemporary hard-boiled detective fiction with mythological dread. When the trail leads to Portugal—specifically to a desecrated royal tomb and a macabre temple built entirely of human bones—the book echoes motifs from Harper’s very first case, bringing the series full circle. This cyclical structure reinforces the theme that the past is never truly dead; it is merely waiting to be exhumed.

Richardson’s stylistic signature has always been her ability to render the supernatural tactile and visceral. In *Revenant*, her prose is lean, atmospheric, and unsparing. She treats magic and the paranormal not as dazzling spectacles, but as heavy, draining, and often decaying forces. The descriptions of black magic and necromantic rituals carry a palpable weight of rot and sorrow. Seattle is rendered in shades of perpetual gray and damp asphalt, while Europe—and Portugal in particular—is painted with the ancient, sun-baked melancholy of a continent weighed down by centuries of bloodshed and forgotten secrets. 

Harper Blaine remains the beating heart of the series. Unlike many urban fantasy protagonists who become overpowered demigods by book nine, Harper still feels vulnerable. She gets bruised, she gets tired, and she relies heavily on her wits, her instincts, and her partnership with Quinton. Their relationship provides a crucial emotional anchor amidst the chaos of a plot that threatens to tear the geopolitical and paranormal worlds apart. 

*Revenant* is a masterclass in pacing and escalation. Richardson seamlessly bridges the gap between intimate noir detective work and epic, high-stakes dark fantasy. Fans of the series will be thrilled by how this installment honors Harper’s journey while expanding the mythology in bold, terrifying directions. For newcomers and veterans alike, this journey into the bone temples of Portugal is a haunting, adrenaline-fueled ride that proves Kat Richardson is operating at the absolute top of her game.
