# Ninth City Burning (War of the Realms, #1)

**Author:** J. Patrick Black  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

J. Patrick Black’s explosive 2016 debut, Ninth City Burning, drops readers into a blood-soaked crucible where science fiction meets high-fantasy destruction. Five centuries after an alien invasion nearly erased humanity, the remaining population does not merely survive—they wage a grueling, generational war using the very universe-altering magic-tech stolen from their conquerors. For fans of Ender’s Game and Red Rising, this novel offers a familiar yet exhilarating adrenaline rush, anchored by a central theme of resilience in the face of insurmountable odds.

The core of Black’s narrative explores the heavy toll of prolonged militarism on the human spirit. After five hundred years of continuous warfare, humanity has restructured its entire civilization around annihilation. The conflict is no longer just about defeating an extraterrestrial threat; it has become an inescapable ecosystem shaping culture, society, and identity. Through a sprawling, multi-perspective cast—including the elite cadet Jax and the hardened nomad Naomi—Black investigates how children raised as weapons grapple with their humanity. The thematic weight rests on a sobering question: when you spend half a millennium fighting monsters using their own weapons, how much of your own soul do you sacrifice to win?

Black’s writing style is fast-paced, visceral, and unsparing. He wastes no time on sprawling exposition, instead throwing the reader directly into the kinetic chaos of ruined landscapes and devastating skirmishes. His prose leans heavily into sensory details—the blinding flash of world-shattering alien tech, the grit of nomadic survival in the wastes, and the claustrophobic pressure of military academies breeding child soldiers. The pacing mirrors the relentless nature of the war itself, rarely letting up for breath, which successfully communicates the perpetual state of emergency gripping a fractured Earth.

While the plot treads some well-worn tropes of dystopian young adult and military sci-fi, Black elevates the material through sheer momentum and a colorful ensemble. The juxtaposition between the hyper-regulated, elite military machinery represented by characters like Jax and the raw, unpolished survivalism of Naomi creates a dynamic narrative friction. The world-building feels expansive and lived-in, dripping with the wear and tear of a five-hundred-year stalemate.

Ninth City Burning is a bold, high-stakes opening salvo in the War of the Realms series. It delivers cinematic action, high-concept world-building, and an unflinching look at a society built for war. For readers hungry for sprawling space operas infused with gritty grit and desperate heroism, Black’s debut is a thrilling ride well worth taking.
