# The Valiant (The Valiant, #1)

**Author:** Lesley Livingston  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Title: Blood, Sand, and Sovereignty: A Review of Lesley Livingston’s The Valiant

In the crowded arena of young adult historical fiction, finding a novel that successfully balances brutal historical realism with the beating heart of a classic romance is no small feat. Yet, with her 2017 release The Valiant, author Lesley Livingston achieves just that. Transporting readers to the tumultuous, blood-soaked world of the late Roman Republic, Livingston crafts a riveting tale of survival, identity, and vengeance. At once spectacular and tender, this opening installment of the series redefines the boundaries of the traditional warrior narrative, offering up a heroine who is as fierce with a blade as she is steadfast in her royal heritage.

The novel centers on Fallon, a proud Celtic princess of the Cantii tribe, whose life is irrevocably shattered by the imperial ambitions of Julius Caesar. Haunted by the death of her legendary sister, Sorcha, Fallon is poised on the brink of womanhood and ready to claim her place among her people's fiercest fighters. Instead, she is captured by slave traders and cast into the grim, unforgiving underworld of Rome's elite training academies for female gladiators—ironically, an institution ultimately owned by the very man who destroyed her family. 

Livingston’s primary theme revolves around the reclamation of agency in the face of absolute subjugation. Through Fallon, the author explores what it means to be a warrior when all outward symbols of power—crown, freedom, and homeland—have been stripped away. Fallon’s journey is not merely about physical survival in the amphitheater, where sand drinks the blood of ambitious girls; it is a psychological battle to preserve her soul. Stripped of her royal title, she must forge a new identity from the ashes of her past. She is no longer just a princess, nor is she merely a captive or a disposable gladiator. She is all three, fusing her noble heritage with the ruthless pragmatism required to stay alive. This evolution forms the emotional core of the book, brilliantly illustrating that true strength resides within the unbreakable spirit, independent of circumstance.

The author’s style is energetic, visceral, and unyielding. Livingston does not sanitize the ancient world. The training regimens are grueling, the arena fights are depicted with heart-stopping brutality, and the political machinations of Rome cast a long, suffocating shadow over every page. Yet, amidst the violence, Livingston weaves a surprising lyricism, particularly when exploring Fallon’s connection to her Celtic roots, the memory of her sister, and the haunting landscapes of her lost home. The prose is brisk and cinematic, perfectly suited for a narrative that moves at the breathless pace of a chariot race. 

Crucially, Livingston avoids the trap of reducing her characters to archetypes. The rivalries among the female gladiators are painted in complex shades of gray; trust is a luxury few can afford, yet fierce sisterhoods inevitably bloom in the darkest corners of the ludus. Furthermore, the introduction of Cai, a young Roman soldier who complicates Fallon’s vendetta with forbidden, irresistible feelings, injects a palpable romantic tension into the plot. This relationship acts as a humanizing anchor, reminding the reader that beneath the armor and the vendettas, Fallon is a young woman navigating the treacherous waters of first love amidst the ruins of war.

The Valiant is a triumph of pacing, atmosphere, and character-driven plotting. It delivers everything a reader could crave from historical fantasy: high-stakes danger, visceral combat, and a protagonist who commands the page with unwavering courage. Livingston has constructed a world where honor is bought with blood, and where a captive princess can rise to become the master of her own destiny. For fans of high-octane adventure and resilient heroines, this novel is an absolute must-read that leaves you eagerly anticipating the next round in the arena.
