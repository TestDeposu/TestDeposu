# The World We Make (Great Cities, #2)

**Author:** N.K. Jemisin  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

N.K. Jemisin’s The World We Make arrives as the triumphant, high-stakes conclusion to the Great Cities duology, sealing a modern epic that redefines urban fantasy through the lens of myth, identity, and resistance. Following the breathless momentum of 2020’s The City We Became, this final installment widens the aperture of Jemisin’s vision, shifting from a localized battle for New York City’s birth to a sprawling, interconnected global war for the soul of human civilization itself. 

At its core, the book operates as a fierce meditation on the anatomy of a city and the forces that seek to homogenize and destroy it. While the cosmic entity known as the Woman in White was temporarily held at bay, she returns in The World We Make with a more insidious, deeply recognizable weapon: populist politics. Cloaked in the seductive rhetoric of gentrification, xenophobia, and authoritarian "law and order," a new mayoral candidate emerges to hollow out New York from the inside. Jemisin brilliantly draws a parallel between cosmic horror and mundane, systemic oppression, showing how bigotry and late-stage capitalism are not merely political opinions, but literal world-ending magic.

Stylistically, Jemisin remains a master of voice, rhythm, and world-building. Her prose pulses with the chaotic, vibrant energy of the five boroughs while maintaining the gravity of ancient mythology. The avatars of New York—representing Manhattan, Brooklyn, the Bronx, Queens, and Staten Island—are not just personifications of geography, but vessels of intersectional lived experience, culture, and resilience. In The World We Make, these localized champions cannot stand alone. To defeat an enemy that weaponizes global systems of power, they must forge alliances with the avatars of other Great Cities around the world. This planetary perspective elevates the narrative, transforming urban fantasy into an urgent, geopolitical fable about solidarity against fascism.

What makes Jemisin’s work so striking is her refusal to separate magic from reality. In her hands, systemic racism, housing crises, and cultural erasure are monsters just as terrifying as multi-dimensional parasites. The world-building is razor-sharp, cutting through romanticized notions of urban living to expose the living, breathing, bleeding infrastructure of human communities. The pacing is relentless, balancing intimate character arcs with sweeping, apocalyptic stakes, ensuring that readers remain emotionally anchored even as the universe hangs in the balance.

The World We Make is a love letter to the defiant spirit of cities and the people who fight to keep them diverse, weird, and free. Jemisin has crafted a masterclass in modern speculative fiction—one that warns us of the dangers lurking in our own backyards while reminding us that community is our most powerful magic. It is a vital, unforgettable conclusion to a defining duology of our time.
