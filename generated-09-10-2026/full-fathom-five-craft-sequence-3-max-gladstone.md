# Full Fathom Five (Craft Sequence, #3)

**Author:** Max Gladstone  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

Max Gladstone’s *Full Fathom Five*, the brilliant third installment in the critically acclaimed Craft Sequence, stands as a masterclass in modern secondary-world fantasy. Where previous entries explored the municipal mechanics of divinity and the brutal economics of corporate necromancy, *Full Fathom Five* casts its net into deeper, darker waters. It examines the manufacturing of faith, the commodification of the sacred, and the heavy price paid by those tasked with building and maintaining the architecture of belief.

Set on the island paradise of Kavekana—a tropical contrast to the bleak, slate-and-steel skylines of Alt Coulumb and Dresediel Lex—the novel introduces us to Kai, a priestess who literally builds gods to order. In this universe, where souls function as currency and legal contracts can bind the cosmos, the creation of deities is treated as a high-stakes industrial enterprise. When Kai’s desperate attempt to save a dying idol leaves her physically battered and professionally ostracized, she finds herself gaslit by a system that prefers convenient compliance to inconvenient truths. Branded as unstable by her superiors, her coworkers, and her ex-boyfriend, Kai refuses to be silenced. Her ensuing investigation peels back the glossy veneer of Kavekana’s spiritual economy to expose a sprawling conspiracy rooted in fear, institutional rot, and the slow, agonizing death of genuine divinity.

Gladstone’s signature style is on full, vibrant display here. He possesses a rare talent for translating high-concept, seemingly absurd ideas into visceral, emotionally resonant narratives. In Gladstone’s world, corporate law and magic are indistinguishable; litigation is a form of warfare, and divine bodies are subject to depreciation, bankruptcy, and hostile takeovers. Yet, beneath the witty conceit of divine capitalism lies a profound and melancholy human heart. Gladstone does not write mere world-building exercises; he writes about people caught in the gears of colossal, uncaring systems. His prose is sharp, economical, and effortlessly atmospheric, evoking the salty breeze of the tropics alongside the cold, calculated cruelty of bureaucratic oppression. 

The central theme of *Full Fathom Five* is autonomy in the face of manufactured reality. Kai is not a traditional chosen one destined to save the world; she is a tired, flawed, resilient worker who looks at a broken system and decides that the cost of looking away is too high. Through her, Gladstone explores how societies rebuild after cataclysms like the God Wars, and whether a just society can truly be constructed when the very foundations are built on the exploitation of the sacred. 

Striking, intellectually stimulating, and fiercely imaginative, *Full Fathom Five* proves that Gladstone’s Craft Sequence is one of the most innovative and consistently brilliant fantasy series of the twenty-first century. It is a mystery wrapped in divine mechanics, a political thriller told with oceanic scale, and an essential read for anyone who likes their fantasy intelligent, challenging, and profoundly human.
