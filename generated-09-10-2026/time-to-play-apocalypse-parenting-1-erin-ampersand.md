# Time to Play (Apocalypse Parenting, #1)

**Author:** Erin Ampersand  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2026

Review: Time to Play by Erin Ampersand

When the apocalypse arrives, most protagonists in contemporary science fiction and fantasy are rugged mercenaries, brooding scientists, or cynical teenagers equipped for isolation and violence. But what happens when the intergalactic system game show drops its deadly rules onto the middle-class suburb of a harried homemaker? Erin Ampersand answers this question with a masterclass in genre subversion in Time to Play, the opening installment of the Apocalypse Parenting series. This is a refreshing, wildly inventive LitRPG adventure that replaces toxic masculinity and grimdark brooding with the ultimate test of maternal endurance, transforming the chaotic trenches of raising small children into a literal survival arena.

The premise is brilliantly jarring. Meghan Moretti is in the middle of the mundane domestic grind—racing to wash soccer uniforms while her husband is away on business—when the power grid collapses and an alien voice announces that Earth has been unwillingly drafted into a cosmic competition. Suddenly, monsters spawn on the front lawn, floating status screens materialize in human fields of vision, and every living soul is granted supernatural abilities. Yet, Ampersand pivots away from standard power-fantasy tropes immediately. Meghan’s primary antagonist isn't just the invading monster fauna; it is the sheer, overwhelming logistics of keeping a nine-year-old, a six-year-old, and a three-year-old alive while managing inventory, leveling up, and fighting off sheer panic. 

Ampersand’s greatest stylistic triumph in Time to Play is how seamlessly she marries the crunchy mechanics of LitRPG—stat blocks, skill trees, class progression, and resource management—with the frantic, sleep-deprived reality of modern motherhood. The system interface doesn't just measure attack power and agility; it forces Meghan to calculate how a mother’s protective instincts can be translated into viable game mechanics. The narrative tone walks a razor-thin line between laugh-out-loud absurdity and genuine, white-knuckle tension. When Meghan discovers that her toddler has used her supernatural choice to bring a beloved stuffed turtle to life as a companion combatant, the book achieves a pitch-perfect blend of heartwarming family dynamics and high-stakes peril. 

The thematic core of the novel investigates the evolution of control and trust. Pre-apocalypse parenting is already an exercise in managing chaos, letting children skin their knees, and hoping for the best. In the new reality of Time to Play, the stakes are raised to an unbearable degree. Meghan cannot shield her children from every threat forever, especially when the cosmic rules dictate that every human, regardless of age, must fight to survive. Ampersand crafts a compelling emotional arc where Meghan must learn the hardest lesson a parent can face: how to teach her children to stand on their own two feet, and how to let them. It is this emotional anchor that elevates the book from a clever genre exercise into an unputdownable narrative.

Praised by heavyweights of the genre like Matt Dinniman as pure gold, Time to Play proves that the LitRPG landscape has plenty of room for innovation. Ampersand’s writing is brisk, witty, and grounded in authentic human behavior, making the statistical progression feel earned and the emotional beats resonate deeply. Whether you are a die-hard fan of progression fantasy or simply looking for a clever, fast-paced survival story with an exceptionally unique perspective, this novel deals a winning hand.
