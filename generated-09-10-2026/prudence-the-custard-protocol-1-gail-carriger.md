# Prudence (The Custard Protocol, #1)

**Author:** Gail Carriger  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

Gail Carriger has long been the reigning queen of steampunk etiquette, blending Victorian propriety with the delightfully bizarre conventions of the supernatural. With Prudence, the first installment in The Custard Protocol series published in 2015, Carriger transports her signature blend of biting wit, corset-clad heroines, and eccentric world-building to the exotic expanse of colonial India. The result is a effervescent, globe-trotting adventure that proves the author’s literary tankard remains delightfully full.

At the heart of the novel is Prudence Alessandra Maccon Akeldama, affectionately known as Rue. As the offspring of the formidable Lady Maccon and the fashion-forward vampire Lord Akeldama, Rue is royalty in the London supernatural demimonde. However, she has inherited more than just a penchant for fabulous hats; Rue possesses a rare and disruptive metanatural ability to nullify supernatural powers simply by touching them. When a most unexpected inheritance lands her in possession of a steampunk dirigible christened the Spotted Custard, Rue decides that proper Victorian young ladies are vastly overrated and steers her metallic beast toward India for a spot of tea and an awful lot of trouble.

Carriger’s greatest thematic triumph in Prudence is the brilliant subversion of imperialist tropes through the lens of fantasy. While the British Raj serves as the backdrop for the narrative, Carriger is far more interested in the internal colonization of manners, class mobility, and the constraints placed upon young women. Rue represents the ultimate modern Victorian anomaly: she is fiercely independent yet bound by the ridiculous sartorial and social expectations of her era. The recurring motif of the missing bloomers serves as both comic relief and a sharp feminist metaphor for the literal and figurative restrictions placed on women. Rue must navigate a world where a lack of undergarments is somehow a greater crisis than a looming political insurrection, highlighting the absurd priorities of high society.

Furthermore, the theme of belonging echoes strongly throughout the text. As a person of mixed supernatural heritage who doesn't quite fit into traditional pack or hive structures, Rue’s journey to India mirrors her internal quest for identity. The foreign landscape forces her to rely not just on her aristocratic breeding, but on her own ingenuity, transforming her from a privileged socialite into a capable, pragmatic leader of a wonderfully eccentric crew.

True to form, Carriger’s style remains the true star of the show. Her prose is a masterclass in tone, effortlessly marrying high-society banter with breathless, clockwork adventure. The dialogue crackles with Wildean epigrams and self-aware sarcasm, delivered with the stiff-upper-lip charm that has become the author’s calling card. Carriger creates a sensory-rich environment where the smell of coal smoke and machinery mingles seamlessly with the scent of cardamom and tea. It is a world where werewolves wear bespoke waistcoats, vampires obsess over the latest fashions, and tea is treated as a matter of national security. 

While the plot itself—involving local dissidents, a kidnapped brigadier’s wife, and familiar Scottish werewolves—serves primarily as a fast-paced scaffolding for the character interactions, the sheer velocity of Carriger’s imagination prevents any lag in momentum. It is an episodic, breezy narrative that prioritizes charm and atmosphere over intricate mystery mechanics, yet it succeeds brilliantly because the reader is having far too much fun to care.

Prudence is a sparkling, delightfully frothy confection of a novel that satisfies both returning fans of the Parasol Protectorate universe and newcomers looking for a whimsical escape. Carriger has crafted a protagonist who is impossible not to adore, surrounded by a supporting cast that practically begs for further adventures in the skies. It is smart, silly, impeccably polite, and utterly charming—a literary cup of tea that is impossible to put down.
