# Issola (Vlad Taltos, #9)

**Author:** Steven Brust  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2002

Step into the vibrant, razor-sharp world of Steven Brust’s *Issola*, the ninth installment in the enduringly popular Vlad Taltos series. Published in 2002, this swashbuckling fantasy adventure proves once again why Brust remains a master of blending noir-infused cynicism with high-stakes cosmic fantasy. For fans who have followed the wisecracking human assassin Vlad and his snarky, venom-tongued Jhegaala companion, Loiosh, through the perilous landscape of Dragaera, *Issola* offers a deeply satisfying dive into friendship, loyalty, and the terrifying expanse of unknown dimensions.

At its core, the central theme of *Issola* is the unbreakable weight of obligation and the surprising depths of loyalty among outcasts and elites alike. When Vlad is yanked out of his rustic exile by Lady Teldra—the impeccably courteous servant of his powerful yet exasperating friend, the Dragonlord Morrolan—he is faced with a terrifying reality. Both Morrolan and the formidable sorceress Aliera have vanished, reportedly captured by the Jenoine. These are not mere political rivals or rival mob bosses; the Jenoine are the primordial architects of reality itself, beings who manipulate the very fabric of time and space. 

For a cynical human assassin who usually prefers to stick to underworld contracts and a decent cup of klava, taking on the Jenoine is suicide. Yet, without a second of genuine hesitation, Vlad steps up. Brust uses this perilous rescue mission to explore the subtle nuances of relationships in a society rigidly divided by race and status. The dynamic between Vlad, an Easterner operating in a world of long-lived Dragaerans, highlights how genuine bonds transcend cultural and biological boundaries. Vlad’s reluctant heroism isn't born of a grand destiny, but of a gritty, unyielding commitment to his friends, no matter how much he complains about it along the way.

Stylistically, Brust is at the top of his game. The novel is propelled by the author's signature narrative voice: snappy, first-person narration dripping with dry wit, philosophical musings, and sharp-tongued banter. Vlad Taltos is one of fantasy’s most compelling antiheroes, blending the hard-boiled cadence of a classic detective noir with the expansive imagination of epic fantasy. Brust’s pacing is relentless, balancing high-octane action sequences with intimate, character-driven moments that flesh out the rich lore of the House of the Issola and the broader Dragaeran Empire. Furthermore, Brust employs clever structural choices, using witty chapter-opening quotes and layered dialogue to keep the reader constantly on their toes.

*Issola* is a masterclass in how to keep a long-running series fresh. It balances grand, reality-bending cosmic horror with the grounded, street-level charm of a protagonist who would rather be drinking coffee than saving the universe. For both longtime devotees of the Vlad Taltos saga and newcomers looking for a smart, fast-paced fantasy adventure with immense heart, this book is an absolute triumph.
