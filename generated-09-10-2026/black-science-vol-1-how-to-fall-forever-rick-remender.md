# Black Science, Vol. 1: How to Fall Forever

**Author:** Rick Remender  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

Chaos is rarely as visually arresting or emotionally exhausting as it is in Rick Remender’s Black Science, Vol. 1: How to Fall Forever. Published in 2015, this opening volume of the science-fiction epic wastes no time gripping the reader by the throat and dragging them headfirst into a kaleidoscopic nightmare of infinite dimensions. At its core, the narrative follows Grant McKay, a disillusioned anarchist scientist who has invented the Pillar—a device capable of punching a hole through the barriers of reality to access the Eververse, a boundless collection of alternate universes. But when sabotage strikes from within their own ranks, the Pillar malfunctions, trapping Grant, his family, and his crew of fellow researchers in a relentless, unforgiving slipstream of dimension-hopping survival.

Remender crafts a blistering critique of hubris through the central theme of scientific ambition unchecked by moral foresight. Grant McKay is not your archetypal, squeaky-clean sci-fi hero. He is deeply flawed, arrogant, and relentlessly driven by a savior complex that frequently jeopardizes the very people he is trying to protect. The central conflict of the book explores the devastating consequences of human interference with the fundamental fabric of existence. Every reality the crew stumbles into is stranger, more hostile, and more astonishing than the last, serving as a physical manifestation of Murphy's Law: anything that can go wrong, will go wrong, usually in the most horrific way possible. 

What elevates this volume from a standard sci-fi romp to a gripping psychological thriller is Remender’s signature narrative style. The pacing is relentless. There are virtually no quiet moments of exposition or leisurely world-building; instead, the lore and character dynamics are unspooled organically through frantic dialogue, desperate decisions, and brutal flashbacks. Remender uses the infinite canvas of the multiverse not just for wild, sci-fi spectacle, but as a pressure cooker to test the frayed bonds of family, loyalty, and guilt. The characters are constantly running for their lives, but the true terror lies in the emotional baggage they carry with them from dimension to dimension. 

Visually and tonally, the book acts as a high-octane adrenaline shot, pairing Remender’s frantic storytelling with unbridled imagination. It is a story about the fragility of human existence measured against the terrifying vastness of reality. By the time you reach the breathless conclusion of this first volume, you realize that the most dangerous frontier isn't the outer limits of the multiverse—it is the human ego. Black Science, Vol. 1 is a brilliant, dizzying descent into the unknown, proving that sometimes, the hardest fall is the one from which you can never return.
