# Stealing the Wind (Mermen of Ea #1)

**Author:** Shira Anthony  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

Dive into the vibrant, high-stakes aquatic world of Shira Anthony’s award-winning 2013 fantasy romance, Stealing the Wind. As the opening installment of the Mermen of Ea trilogy, this novel immediately establishes Anthony as a master of world-building, seamlessly blending high seas adventure, intense political conflict, and deeply emotional queer romance. Winner of recognition in the 2013 Rainbow Awards, this story transcends standard genre tropes by anchoring its escapades in a rich, mythic lore and a surprisingly tender exploration of identity and freedom.

At the heart of Anthony’s narrative style is a penchant for sensory-rich storytelling that mirrors the unpredictable, sweeping nature of the ocean itself. The novel wastes no time plunging the reader into the deep end. We meet Taren Laxley, a protagonist whose life has been defined by the crushing weight of servitude. When Taren is snatched from his constrained existence by a pirate captain, Anthony cleverly subverts the classic kidnapping trope. Instead of terror, Taren finds an intoxicating sense of liberation. The resulting arrangement—three years of labor and sexual servitude in exchange for a seagoing life—serves as the catalyst for Taren’s sexual and personal awakening. Anthony handles these explicit dynamics with confidence and heat, ensuring the polyamorous and sensual elements of the plot feel both organic to the rugged pirate culture and crucial to Taren's growing confidence.

Yet, Anthony’s true skill lies in narrative momentum. Just as the reader settles into the rhythmic sway of pirate life, the author pulls the rug out from under her characters. A dramatic storm casts Taren into the sea, plunging the novel into its true, mythical scope. Rescued by Ian Dunaidh, the enigmatic captain of the rival vessel the Phantom, Taren is thrust into a much larger, more dangerous world. It is here that Stealing the Wind truly reveals its thematic depth. 

The central theme of the novel pivots on transformation and belonging. When Taren discovers that Ian and his people are the Ea—ancient, shape-shifting merfolk—and that he is one of them, the narrative shifts from a tale of physical liberation to one of existential discovery. Anthony uses the dual nature of the Ea—creatures who walk the land as men yet belong to the fathomless depths of the ocean—as a brilliant metaphor for Taren's internal journey. Having spent his life as property, Taren must learn what it means to truly own himself, navigating a heritage that is as terrifying as it is magnificent. 

The romance between Taren and Ian burns with a fierce, magnetic intensity, driven by Anthony’s knack for crafting palpable chemistry and emotional vulnerability. But what elevates Stealing the Wind above a conventional fantasy romance is the encroaching shadow of war. The personal stakes are brilliantly magnified by a brewing conflict that threatens the very survival of the Ea people. Anthony orchestrates the transition from intimate cabin dramas to sweeping, high-stakes tactical struggles with impressive fluidity. 

For readers seeking a lushly detailed universe populated by fierce pirates, mesmerizing shape-shifters, and a romance that defies the odds, Shira Anthony has crafted an irresistible maritime epic. Stealing the Wind is a seductive, action-packed triumph that proves the deepest waters hold the most compelling truths.
