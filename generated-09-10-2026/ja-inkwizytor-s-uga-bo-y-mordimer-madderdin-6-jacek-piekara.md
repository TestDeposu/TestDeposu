# Ja, Inkwizytor. Sługa Boży (Mordimer Madderdin, #6)

**Author:** Jacek Piekara  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

Step into a world where history veered into a terrifying alternate reality, a domain forged in the fires of fanaticism and divine retribution. In *Ja, Inkwizytor. Sługa Boży* (God’s Servant), published in 2011, Jacek Piekara expands his monumental dark fantasy saga by plunging readers deeper into a chilling theological nightmare: a universe where Jesus Christ did not die for the sins of humanity on the cross, but instead descended from it with wrath, seizing absolute power and establishing an iron-fisted reign over mankind. This is not a world of grace and mercy; it is a brutal domain defined by torture, towering execution pyres, and relentless religious persecution.

At the center of this grim tapestry stands Mordimer Madderdin, the quintessential inquisitor. Neither a shining knight nor a simple villain, Mordimer is a complex product of his environment—a man who navigates the murky depths of human depravity and divine zealotry with calculated cunning, absolute ruthlessness, and an unyielding conviction that true justice far transcends mere human law. Piekara crafts his narratives with the episodic pacing of a dark, addictive television series, ensuring that whether this novel is your first step into the Inquisitor’s world or the latest chapter in a long journey, the gripping momentum pulls you under immediately.

As Piekara himself notes, *Sługa Boży* is far more than a routine criminal investigation. While the plot drives forward on the adrenaline of a complex, high-stakes inquiry where Mordimer’s intellect and brutality are put to the ultimate test, the novel simultaneously shatters the foundations of the setting's established dogma. Readers are confronted with audacious and deeply unsettling questions, including shocking suspicions surrounding the mysterious disappearance of Jesus Christ himself. Furthermore, the narrative peels back the tightly guarded layers of the Inner Circle of the Inquisitorium, revealing dangerous secrets, while echoing a haunting prophecy that foreshadows the dark destiny awaiting Mordimer.

Piekara’s literary style is unapologetically visceral, punchy, and atmospheric. He rejects cozy fantasy tropes in favor of gritty realism, sharp dialogue, and moral ambiguity that leaves readers questioning the very nature of righteousness. The prose moves with a relentless pace, mirroring the urgency of the Inquisition’s relentless hunt for heresy. It is a style that commands attention, making the horrific beauty of this alternate past utterly mesmerizing. 

For fans of dark fantasy, alternate history, and deeply flawed antiheroes, this installment is an essential masterclass in building a world that is as repellent as it is captivating. Piekara dares you to look into the abyss of a world ruled by a vengeful heaven—and once you open the cover, turning away becomes impossible.
