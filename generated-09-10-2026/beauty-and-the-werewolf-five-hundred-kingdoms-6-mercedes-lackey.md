# Beauty and the Werewolf (Five Hundred Kingdoms, #6)

**Author:** Mercedes Lackey  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

In Beauty and the Werewolf, master fantasy storyteller Mercedes Lackey returns to her beloved Five Hundred Kingdoms series with a razor-sharp wit and a profound subversion of classic fairy tale tropes. Originally published in 2011 and reissued in 2017, this enchanting novel tackles a foundational rule of folklore: being the eldest daughter in a magical realm is usually a one-way ticket to tragedy. Yet, Lackey’s protagonist, Bella, is fiercely determined to rewrite the script, proving that destiny is not a mandatory life sentence.

The central theme of the novel revolves around agency versus predetermination. In the Five Hundred Kingdoms, the whimsical yet rigid "Tradition" dictates the fates of everyone within its borders. Characters are often trapped in narrative loops, forced to play out familiar roles as princesses, witches, monsters, and victims. Lackey uses Bella’s journey to critique the illusion of predestined paths. When Bella dons her iconic red cloak to seek advice from the local wisewoman, she steps off the beaten trail—literally and figuratively. Her encounter with a cursed nobleman trapped in the body of an occasional werewolf sparks a narrative partnership built on mutual respect, intellect, and shared laughter rather than helpless damselhood and forced romance. 

Lackey’s signature writing style shines through her conversational, highly observant narrative voice. She blends cozy, traditional fairy tale elements with sharp modern sensibilities and steampunk-adjacent ingenuity. The cursed nobleman is not merely a brooding beast in a gloomy castle; he is a brilliant inventor whose workshop creates marvels that captivate Bella’s curious mind. Their growing bond is forged through intellectual stimulation and genuine humor, offering a refreshing antidote to the gloomy, high-stakes misery often found in contemporary fantasy romance. Furthermore, Lackey’s world-building excels in its meta-textual awareness. Characters frequently reference the frustrating mechanics of the Tradition, turning the narrative into a delightful game of chess against fate itself. With the added interference of the series' trademark meddling Godmothers, the plot moves at a brisk, engaging pace, balancing cozy domesticity with magical peril.

Ultimately, Beauty and the Werewolf is a triumphant celebration of free will, cleverness, and the joy of defying expectations. Lackey crafts a romance that feels both comforting and brilliantly subversive, making it an essential read for anyone who has ever rooted for the fairy tale character to throw out the rulebook and start over.
