# At the Mouth of the River of Bees: Stories

**Author:** Kij Johnson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

To read Kij Johnson is to step into a shifting landscape where the familiar boundaries between human consciousness and the wild, untamed world dissolve entirely. In her brilliant 2012 debut collection, At the Mouth of the River of Bees, Johnson curates a menagerie of the uncanny, the tender, and the devastating. Featuring award-winning masterpieces alongside daring excursions into myth and speculative history, this volume cements Johnson’s reputation as one of the most imaginative and fearless voices in contemporary speculative fiction. 

At the heart of the collection lies a profound thematic exploration of transformation, otherness, and the complex echoes of communication between species. Animals are rarely mere metaphors here; cats, wolves, dogs, bees, and birds possess their own rich, inscrutable interiorities. Johnson challenges the human-centric bias of storytelling by asking what it truly means to inhabit another skin, another mind, or another state of being. Whether a grieving husband watches his deceased wife reincarnate as a flightless bird in My Wife Reincarnated as a Solitaire, or uplifted dogs grapple with the theological and cultural shifts of a changed world in The Evolution of Trickster Stories Among the Dogs of North Park After the Change, the boundary separating humanity from nature is rendered porous and fragile.

Stylistically, Johnson is a linguistic chameleon. Her prose is precise, elegant, and deceptive in its simplicity, capable of lulling the reader into a sense of pastoral calm just before pulling the rug out to reveal psychological abysses. She seamlessly blends hard-edged world-building with the dream logic of fairy tales and fables. In stories like the Hugo and Nebula-winning The Man Who Bridged the Mist, she constructs a vast, terrifyingly alien landscape with breathtaking architectural and emotional depth. Yet, she is equally at home in the claustrophobic, psychologically complex terror of interpersonal cruelty, as brilliantly evidenced in the haunting, razor-sharp short story Ponies. 

What elevates Johnson above many of her speculative peers is her willingness to court discomfort. Her stories do not always offer neat resolutions; instead, they linger like restless spirits. She investigates grief, love, and isolation through lenses that distort reality just enough to make us see our own world with startling clarity. Whether exploring ancient Japanese folklore in Fox Magic or plumbing the depths of human obsession in 26 Monkeys, Also the Abyss, Johnson writes with an unflinching empathy that honors both our magnificence and our profound fragility.

At the Mouth of the River of Bees is a dazzling showcase of short fiction at its absolute zenith. It is a collection that hums with strange magic, buzzing with the persistent, unforgettable energy of a writer operating at the absolute peak of her powers. For lovers of literary fantasy and science fiction alike, Johnson’s debut is an essential, mesmerizing journey into the unknown.
