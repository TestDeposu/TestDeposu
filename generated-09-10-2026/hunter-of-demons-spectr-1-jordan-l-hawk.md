# Hunter of Demons (SPECTR, #1)

**Author:** Jordan L. Hawk  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

In Hunter of Demons, the explosive first installment of the SPECTR series, Jordan L. Hawk crafts a deliciously dark and fast-paced urban fantasy that boldly reimagines the paranormal procedural. Published in 2013, this genre-bending novel wastes no time plunging its readers into an alternate, historically rich America where the supernatural is not merely a myth whispered in the shadows, but a heavily regulated—and violently contested—reality. Hawk establishes a vibrant, high-stakes sandbox right from the opening pages, blending the gritty realism of a bureaucratic government agency with the visceral, blood-soaked mythology of vampires and infernal possessions.

At the thematic core of the novel is the tension between control and chaos, institutionalized order and deeply personal liberation. The story follows Caleb Jansen, an ordinary man whose life is abruptly upended when he becomes unwillingly possessed by Julian, an opinionated, sharp-tongued vampire spirit with a very specialized and macabre appetite: he drinks the blood of demons. This bizarre symbiosis turns Caleb into an immediate target, hunted not only by the very demons Julian feeds upon, but also by a radical fundamentalist group hell-bent on purging the world of any paranormal aberration. Hawk uses this forced possession to explore themes of identity, bodily autonomy, and unexpected partnership. Caleb is thrust from the safety of mundane anonymity into a terrifying cosmic war, forced to navigate a reality where his own skin is no longer entirely his own.

Enter John Starkweather, a hotshot federal exorcist working for SPECTR—the secretive government agency tasked with policing the boundary between the mortal world and the demonic. Starkweather is the epitome of stoic professionalism and dangerous competence, making him the absolute last man Caleb should be getting involved with, and yet, inevitably, the only one he truly wants. Hawk’s mastery of romantic tension anchors the high-concept supernatural action. The slow-burn dynamic between Caleb and John sizzles with palpable chemistry, balancing the relentless adrenaline of life-or-death shootouts with moments of genuine vulnerability and witty banter. Their developing relationship provides an emotional anchor amidst the chaos, proving that love can bloom even in the shadow of hellfire and ancient bloodlust.

Stylistically, Jordan L. Hawk delivers a masterclass in atmospheric world-building and narrative pacing. The prose is sharp, economical, and effortlessly cinematic, favoring tight dialogue and visceral action sequences that keep the pages turning with relentless momentum. Hawk avoids the trap of excessive exposition, instead weaving the complex lore of SPECTR, demonic hierarchies, and vampire physiology naturally into the narrative flow. The voice of Julian—dry, arrogant, yet fiercely protective—adds a layer of darkly comedic relief that contrasts brilliantly with Caleb’s relatable panic and John’s grim determination. 

Ultimately, Hunter of Demons is a triumph of queer urban fantasy. It successfully marries the procedural grit of Law and Order with the seductive danger of classic gothic horror, all while delivering a swoon-worthy romance that leaves readers eagerly anticipating the next chapter in Caleb and John’s tumultuous journey. Hawk has engineered a phenomenal start to a beloved series, proving that the best monster stories are the ones with a beating—and occasionally possessed—heart.
