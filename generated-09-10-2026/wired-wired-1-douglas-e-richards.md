# Wired (Wired, #1)

**Author:** Douglas E. Richards  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

The Greatest Enemy of Your Wealth Is Looking Back at You in the Mirror

In the realm of personal finance, we are endlessly obsessed with numbers, interest rates, inflation charts, and market projections. Yet, as financial planner and prominent New York Times columnist Carl Richards astoundingly reveals in his brilliant 2012 book, the true driver of our financial success or ruin has almost nothing to do with spreadsheets. It has everything to do with psychology. 

Wired tackles the fundamental flaw in human economic behavior: our hardwired instinct to run from pain and chase after security and pleasure. It is this primitive biological programming that convinces us to panic-sell when the market dips and greedily buy when prices peak. Richards brilliantly identifies and labels this self-destructive disconnect as "the behavior gap"—the costly space stretching between what we logically know we should do with our money and what our raw emotions actually compel us to do.

What makes Richards’s approach so remarkably refreshing is his total rejection of dense financial jargon and intimidating mathematical formulas. Instead, he treats his readers with profound empathy, acknowledging that making financial blunders does not make us stupid; it simply makes us human. Using his signature minimalist napkin drawings and an engaging, conversational prose style, Richards distills complex psychological traps into crystal-clear concepts. He guides us gently toward the realization that financial peace of mind is not achieved by chasing the next big market trend, but by mastering our own internal impulses.

The book functions as both a mirror and a compass. Richards systematically dismantles the illusion of generic financial advice and redirects our focus toward what truly matters: defining our genuine core values, aligning our spending and investing with those values, and simplifying our financial lives. Whether he is teaching you how to invest your most precious non-monetary assets—like time and energy—or helping you initiate honest, pressure-free conversations about money, his guidance is fiercely practical and deeply liberating.

Wired is not a get-rich-quick manual; it is a profound guide to behavioral self-awareness. Richards successfully lifts the fog of financial anxiety, teaching us to give ourselves permission to forgive past mistakes, recognize our personal triggers, and forge a calmer path forward. For anyone tired of losing sleep—and hard-earned cash—over market volatility and poor spending habits, this short, striking, and immensely wise book is an absolute mandatory read.
