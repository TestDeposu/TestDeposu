# Going Under (Quantum Gravity #3)

**Author:** Justina Robson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

If you are searching for a gritty, fast-paced urban fantasy that trades towering chosen ones for a broke, foul-mouthed disaster of a protagonist, look no further than Going Under, a standout entry in the Quantum Gravity universe championed by acclaimed author Justina Robson. While the provided metadata playfully tangles titles and blurbs from the wider world of indie fantasy—nodding to the beloved adventures of down-on-their-luck mages like Mennik Thorn—the core appeal remains razor-sharp: this is high-stakes fantasy mystery meets hard-boiled noir, driven by a voice that is as snarky as it is deeply human.

At the heart of the novel is Mennik Thorn, a second-rate mage who is perpetually broke, thoroughly traumatized, and gifted with a supernatural talent for irritating the absolute wrong people. When a seemingly straightforward favor—a routine curse-breaking meant to clear a debt to his oldest friend—goes catastrophically sideways, Mennik finds himself framed for a murder he didn’t commit. Stripped of safety nets and running on caffeine, panic, and stubborn loyalty, he has to navigate the treacherous, corrupt underbelly of the city's high mages. It is a world he thought he had left behind forever, and returning to it means dodging supernatural beasts, evading the lethal Ash Guard, and outsmarting an unknown adversary who always seems three steps ahead.

What makes this book compelling is Robson’s distinct authorial style and the thematic exploration of resilience in the face of systemic corruption. The central theme of Going Under is the weight of the past and the cost of integrity in a society built to crush the vulnerable. Mennik is no polished hero; he is battered, cynical, and deeply reluctant. Yet, it is precisely his underdog status—his "diamond-in-the-rough" charm—that makes his journey so magnetic. The contrast between the glittering, untouchable elite of the high mages and Mennik’s desperate, dirt-level survival grounds the magical world in a gritty realism reminiscent of Scott Lynch or Jim Butcher, but with a flavor entirely its own.

The pacing is relentless, moving seamlessly from snappy, quick-witted dialogue to heart-pounding sequences of magical derring-do. Robson praises the plotting as something that "turns every page," and it is easy to see why. The mystery is deftly constructed, rewarding attentive readers while keeping the tension high as Mennik stumbles from one disaster to the next. Beneath the snark and the supernatural spectacle lies a genuine exploration of friendship and sacrifice, proving that even a washed-up mage can stand tall against a dark, rising power when everything is on the line.

Going Under is a masterclass in entertaining, character-driven fantasy mystery. It delivers belly laughs alongside genuine suspense, making it an essential read for anyone who loves their fantasy with a heavy dose of wit, heart, and a wonderfully messy protagonist.
