# Touch of Frost (Mythos Academy, #1)

**Author:** Jennifer Estep  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

Step into the glittering, high-stakes halls of Mythos Academy, where ancient legends collide with modern teenage angst in Jennifer Estep’s riveting 2011 young adult urban fantasy debut, Touch of Frost. This compelling opener invites readers into a hidden world populated by descendants of Norse, Greek, and Roman gods and warriors, establishing a vibrant universe where the library doubles as a cache of ancient weapons and the student body is trained in the lethal art of monster-slaying.

At the narrative heart of the novel is Gwen Frost, a self-proclaimed git, geek, and gypsy who possesses a rare and burdensome psychometry gift—the ability to touch an object or person and instantly know their history, secrets, and fate. Estep uses Gwen as an immediately endearing and relatable protagonist. Unlike the polished, elite warriors who traditionally rule Mythos Academy, Gwen is an outsider struggling to find her footing after a personal tragedy. Her insecurities, combined with a sharp, self-deprecating wit, anchor the supernatural chaos in genuine human emotion. Through Gwen, Estep explores the timeless theme of belonging and identity. The academy is a microcosm of high school hierarchy, yet it is elevated by the existential stakes of mythological warfare. Gwen’s journey is not just about solving a crime, but about learning to trust her own inner voice and accepting her unique power as a strength rather than a curse.

Estep’s authorial style is defined by its brisk pacing, snappy dialogue, and immersive world-building. She wastes no time setting the scene, diving straight into the action with a murder mystery that shatters the academy's illusory safety. The brutal killing of a wealthy mean girl, Jasmine Ashton, inside the sacred Library of Antiquities serves as the catalyst for a larger, apocalyptic conspiracy involving the theft of the dangerous Bowl of Tears. Estep weaves mythology seamlessly into contemporary high school tropes, making ancient pantheons feel fresh, dangerous, and entirely relevant to modern teens. The prose is punchy and accessible, driving the narrative forward with cinematic clarity. Action sequences are choreographed with precision, balancing the visceral thrill of sword combat with the psychological tension of investigation.

Furthermore, the romantic and platonic dynamics crackle with chemistry and realism. Characters like the brooding, deadly Spartan warrior Logan Quinn—the quintessential bad boy with a lethal edge—offer delicious friction against Gwen’s defensive nature, avoiding cheesy tropes in favor of hard-won mutual respect. 

Ultimately, Touch of Frost is a triumphant launchpad for a beloved series. Jennifer Estep has crafted a delightfully addictive blend of mystery, myth, and magic that will captivate fans of urban fantasy and supernatural academy settings alike. It is a striking, fast-paced read that proves true strength often lies in the most unexpected places.
