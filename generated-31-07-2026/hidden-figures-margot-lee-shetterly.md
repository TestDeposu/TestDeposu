# Hidden Figures

**Author:** Margot Lee Shetterly  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

**Hidden Figures: A Symphony of Brains, Bravery, and Barrier-Breaking**

Margot Lee Shetterly's "Hidden Figures" is not just a book; it's a revelation, a testament to the unsung heroes who propelled America's space race. With meticulous research and an engaging narrative style, Shetterly brings to life the extraordinary stories of African American women who, armed with slide rules and pencils, calculated the trajectories that launched astronauts into orbit and helped win the Cold War.

At the heart of "Hidden Figures" are four remarkable women: Dorothy Vaughan, Mary Jackson, Katherine Johnson, and Christine Darden. Their lives, intertwined with the broader historical context of World War II, the Civil Rights Movement, and the Space Race, paint a vivid picture of resilience and genius. Shetterly masterfully weaves their personal narratives with the larger story of NASA's triumphs and tribulations, creating a compelling tapestry of progress and perseverance.

Shetterly's prose is as engaging as it is informative. She deftly navigates the complex world of aeronautics, making it accessible to readers without sacrificing depth. Her characters are not just historical figures; they're fully realized individuals, each with her unique voice and story. From Dorothy Vaughan's quiet determination to Katherine Johnson's unyielding precision, each woman leaps off the page, inspiring readers to cheer for their victories and mourn their setbacks.

"Hidden Figures" is more than a book about math or space; it's a story about breaking barriers. It's about women fighting for their place in a male-dominated field, and black women fighting for their place in a segregated society. Shetterly doesn't shy away from the harsh realities of racism and sexism, but she also shows the power of community and the slow, steady march of progress.

The book's pacing is excellent, building to a crescendo with each historical event. Shetterly's ability to balance multiple narratives and time periods is commendable, keeping readers on the edge of their seats. The result is a page-turner that's as hard to put down as it is to forget.

"Hidden Figures" is not just a book about the past; it's a call to action. It's a reminder that progress is not inevitable, but the result of hard work, courage, and the relentless pursuit of justice. It's a testament to the power of diversity and the importance of recognizing and celebrating the contributions of all people.

In a world that often overlooks the achievements of women and people of color, "Hidden Figures" is a beacon of light. It's a reminder that behind every great achievement, there are often unsung heroes. And it's a challenge to us all to seek out these stories, to amplify these voices, and to ensure that no one is left in the shadows.
