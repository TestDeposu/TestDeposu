# Taggerung (Redwall, #14)

**Author:** Brian Jacques  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2001

**A Hero’s Redemption: *Taggerung* and the Timeless Quest for Belonging**

Brian Jacques’ *Taggerung*—the fourteenth installment in the beloved *Redwall* saga—is a masterclass in mythic storytelling, weaving together ancient legend, familial duty, and the rebellious spirit of youth. Set against the lush, war-torn landscapes of Mossflower, this novel is less a standalone adventure and more a deeply personal odyssey, where destiny and identity collide. At its heart, *Taggerung* is a tale about the struggle between who we are told to be and who we choose to become—a theme that resonates as powerfully today as it did when Jacques first penned it in 2001.

The story follows Tagg, a young otter raised by the ruthless Sawney Rath clan, destined to become their legendary "Taggerung," a warrior born of prophecy. But Tagg is no mindless enforcer; he is a boy haunted by the whispers of his true lineage, a Redwall otter stolen as a babe and raised in cruelty. His defiance is the novel’s driving force, a quiet rebellion that sets him on a perilous journey to reclaim his past. Alongside the sharp-witted mouse Nimbalo, Tagg navigates treacherous alliances, betrayals, and the ever-present shadow of Sawney’s enforcers—each one a reminder of the life he was denied. Jacques’ genius lies in his ability to make Tagg’s struggle feel both intimate and epic, a microcosm of the broader *Redwall* saga’s themes of home, honor, and the cost of freedom.

Jacques’ prose is as rich and textured as the forests he describes, brimming with the same vivid, almost fairy-tale-like detail that defined earlier books like *Mattimeo* or *Mossflower*. His world-building is immersive, with every creature—from the cunning badgers of Redwall to the feral rats of the Verminlands—carved with distinct personalities and motivations. Yet *Taggerung* stands out for its emotional depth. Where some *Redwall* novels lean into broad comedy or grand battles, this one lingers on the quiet moments: Tagg’s first glimpse of Redwall’s Abbey, the way Nimbalo’s sarcasm masks her own longing for family, or the haunting realization that some wounds run deeper than years of captivity. Jacques doesn’t shy away from the brutality of Tagg’s upbringing, but he balances it with moments of unexpected tenderness, proving that even in the darkest legends, hope persists.

The novel’s structure mirrors a classic hero’s journey, but with Jacques’ signature twist: the prophecy isn’t just a tool for prophecy—it’s a cage. The Sawney Raths don’t just want Tagg to fulfill their legend; they want him to *become* their weapon. This tension between fate and free will is what elevates *Taggerung* above mere adventure. Tagg’s journey isn’t just about finding his way home; it’s about proving that identity isn’t something bestowed by others, but earned through choice. Nimbalo, with her sharp tongue and sharper instincts, serves as the perfect foil, representing the pragmatism that balances Tagg’s idealism. Together, they embody the *Redwall* ethos: loyalty without blind obedience, courage without recklessness.

For fans of the series, *Taggerung* is a triumph—proof that Jacques could still craft a story as gripping as the early books, even decades into the saga. It’s a novel that rewards those who’ve followed Redwall’s history, but it also stands alone as a compelling tale of belonging. The Netflix adaptation announcement only heightens its appeal, offering a chance to bring this lesser-known gem to a new generation. Whether you’re revisiting the series or diving in for the first time, *Taggerung* is a reminder that the best legends are the ones that refuse to be contained.

In a world where identity is often dictated by others—by society, by family, by the very systems we’re born into—Tagg’s story is a defiant anthem. And that, perhaps, is why *Taggerung* endures: it’s not just a tale of otters and abbeys, but of the universal struggle to be more than what we’re told we are.
