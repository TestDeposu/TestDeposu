# Like Lions (Bull Mountain, #2)

**Author:** Brian Panowich  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

Like Lions, the second installment in the Bull Mountain series by Brian Panowich, is a gripping tale of family, loyalty, and the unrelenting pursuit of justice. Set against the rugged backdrop of North Georgia, the novel masterfully weaves a complex narrative that spans decades, delving into the intricate web of relationships within the Burroughs clan, a family notorious for their involvement in organized crime.

Panowich's writing style is as bold as it is evocative, with a voice that is both haunting and mesmerizing. His prose is akin to a shotgun blast, scattering fragments of history that impale the characters and leave an indelible mark on the reader. The author's use of language is deliberate and unflinching, painting a vivid picture of the mountain and its inhabitants that is at once beautiful and brutal.

At its core, Like Lions is a story about family – the lengths to which men will go to protect it, honor it, or destroy it. The Burroughs clan is a complex tapestry of characters, each with their own distinct voice and perspective. From Clayton, the sheriff who is torn between his loyalty to his family and his duty to uphold the law, to the federal agent who is determined to bring the Burroughs empire crashing down, each character is multidimensional and relatable.

Through the lens of the Burroughs family, Panowich explores the themes of loyalty, power, and the corrupting influence of both. The novel raises important questions about the nature of family and the ties that bind us, as well as the consequences of our actions and the true cost of loyalty.

One of the most striking aspects of Like Lions is its ability to balance the past and the present. The novel seamlessly shifts between different time periods, slowly revealing the history of the Burroughs clan and the events that have shaped them into the people they are today. This narrative structure adds depth and complexity to the story, making it feel both sprawling and intimate at the same time.

In short, Like Lions is a masterpiece of Southern fiction that will appeal to fans of authors like Cormac McCarthy and Daniel Woodrell. Panowich's writing is both beautiful and brutal, and his characters are complex and multidimensional. If you're looking for a novel that will keep you on the edge of your seat and leave you breathless, then Like Lions is the perfect choice.
