# Unbelievable (Pretty Little Liars, #4)

**Author:** Sara Shepard  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

In "Unbelievable", the fourth installment of Sara Shepard's Pretty Little Liars series, the stakes are higher than ever for the four main characters - Aria, Emily, Hanna, and Spencer. The novel picks up where the previous book left off, with the girls' lives turned upside down by the mysterious and sinister forces of "A". As the story unfolds, it becomes clear that the girls' secrets are not the only thing that can destroy them - the truth about Alison's murder is slowly unraveling, and it seems that one of them might be involved.

Shepard's writing style is, as always, engaging and suspenseful. She masterfully weaves together the multiple plot threads, keeping the reader guessing until the very end. The characters are well-developed and complex, with each one struggling to cope with their own personal demons. The author's use of multiple narratives adds to the tension, allowing the reader to see the story from different perspectives.

One of the main themes of the book is the destructive power of secrets and lies. The girls have been keeping secrets from each other and from themselves, and it's clear that this can't last forever. As the truth begins to surface, the consequences are devastating. Shepard explores the idea that secrets can be both a source of power and a source of destruction, and that the truth, no matter how painful, is always better than living a lie.

The author also delves deeper into the psychological effects of being stalked and threatened by "A". The girls are constantly on edge, never knowing when or where "A" will strike next. This creates a sense of tension and unease that permeates the entire book. Shepard's portrayal of the girls' fear and anxiety is realistic and relatable, making it easy for the reader to become fully invested in their story.

The high school setting provides a unique backdrop for the story, highlighting the challenges and pressures of adolescence. The girls' relationships with each other and with their boyfriends are authentic and well-portrayed, adding to the overall sense of realism.

The mystery surrounding Alison's murder is, of course, a central theme of the book. As the girls dig deeper into the truth, they begin to uncover secrets that they never could have imagined. The twists and turns are expertly handled, keeping the reader guessing until the very end.

Overall, "Unbelievable" is a gripping and suspenseful read that will keep you on the edge of your seat. Shepard's writing is engaging and well-paced, and the characters are complex and relatable. If you're a fan of the series, you won't want to miss this installment. And if you're new to the series, this book is a great example of why Pretty Little Liars has become a favorite among readers.
