# Alex, Approximately

**Author:** Jenn Bennett  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

**Alex, Approximately by Jenn Bennett: A Love Letter to the Messy Magic of First Crushes**

Jenn Bennett’s *Alex, Approximately* is the kind of book that feels like stumbling upon a hidden gem—one of those rare YA romances that doesn’t just capture the ache of first love but *celebrates* its chaos. Set against the sun-drenched, small-town backdrop of a California surf town, this story is a delightful, bittersweet twist on the classic *You’ve Got Mail* trope, where the boy you’ve dreamed about online might just be the one you least expected in real life. But Bennett’s genius lies in how she weaves this familiar premise into something fresh, flawed, and deeply human.

At its heart, *Alex, Approximately* is a story about the tension between fantasy and reality—the way we idealize people in our imaginations only to find that love, like life, is rarely as neat as we’ve imagined. Bailey “Mink” Rydell, a self-proclaimed movie buff with a sharp wit and a soft spot for romantic comedies, has spent months falling for a mysterious online persona named Alex. When fate (or perhaps just bad timing) drops her into the same town as her crush, she’s torn between clinging to the perfect version of him she’s built in her head and confronting the messy, unpredictable reality of meeting him in person. The dilemma is relatable, universal—the fear of disappointment, the thrill of possibility, the quiet terror of letting someone in.

What makes Bennett’s execution so compelling is her refusal to sugarcoat the experience. Bailey isn’t just some wide-eyed romantic waiting for her prince to arrive; she’s a teenager navigating self-doubt, insecurities, and the pressure to perform her own life according to the stories she loves. Her internal monologue is witty, self-aware, and painfully honest, making her feel like a real person rather than a trope. And then there’s Porter Roth, the infuriatingly handsome museum security guard who becomes Bailey’s reluctant nemesis—until he isn’t. Their dynamic is electric, a slow-burning friction of attraction and irritation that feels earned rather than forced. Porter isn’t just the love interest; he’s a fully realized character with his own secrets, flaws, and reasons for keeping people at arm’s length.

The real brilliance of *Alex, Approximately* is how Bennett subverts expectations without undermining the emotional payoff. The reveal that Porter is Alex isn’t just a twist—it’s a moment of quiet, devastating truth. It’s the kind of revelation that lingers long after the last page, making you question how much of love is about the person and how much is about the story we tell ourselves about them. Bennett’s writing is sharp and lyrical, blending the humor and heart of a rom-com with the depth of a coming-of-age story. Her prose has a rhythm that mirrors the ebb and flow of teenage emotions—sometimes light, sometimes heavy, but always authentic.

What also sets this book apart is its setting. The California surf town isn’t just a backdrop; it’s a character in its own right. The sun-drenched beaches, the tourist traps, the small-town gossip—it all feels vivid and lived-in, grounding the story in a place that feels real. Bennett captures the bittersweetness of summer, the way time moves differently when you’re young and in love, and the way even the most ordinary moments can feel electric when you’re waiting for something to change.

Ultimately, *Alex, Approximately* is a love letter to the idea that love isn’t about perfection—it’s about the mess, the doubt, the stumbling, and the way it forces you to see yourself in a new light. It’s a story about taking risks, about letting go of the fantasy to embrace the real, and about the quiet courage it takes to love someone even when they’re not who you thought they’d be. Bennett’s writing is witty, warm, and wise, making this the kind of book you’ll want to recommend to anyone who’s ever fallen in love with a story—or a person—only to realize that life is far more complicated (and beautiful) than the movies.

If you loved the charm of *To All the Boys I’ve Loved Before* but crave something with a little more edge, or if you’re a fan of romances that balance humor with heartache, *Alex, Approximately* is a must-read. It’s a book that reminds us that love isn’t about getting everything we want—it’s about learning to love the person we get, flaws and all.
