# What I Loved

**Author:** Siri Hustvedt  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2004

In the intricate tapestry of Siri Hustvedt's "What I Loved," the threads of love, loss, and betrayal are masterfully intertwined to create a narrative that is both poignant and thought-provoking. This sprawling family saga, which spans a quarter of a century, delves into the complexities of human relationships and the enduring power of art to transcend the boundaries of time and tragedy.

At the heart of the novel is the unlikely friendship between Leo Hertzberg, an art historian, and Bill Wechsler, a talented but reclusive painter. Their chance encounter in a SoHo gallery in 1975 sets in motion a chain of events that will forever alter the lives of their families. As their wives, Erica and Violet, and their children, Matthew and Mark, become increasingly entwined, the novel builds towards a devastating crescendo that will test the bonds of love and loyalty.

Hustvedt's prose is characterized by its lyricism and intellectual curiosity, effortlessly navigating the rarefied world of art and academia while plumbing the depths of the human experience. Her writing is both intimate and expansive, capable of conveying the innermost thoughts and feelings of her characters while also situating them within the broader cultural and historical context.

One of the novel's greatest strengths is its ability to balance the personal and the public, the inner worlds of its characters and the outer worlds they inhabit. Hustvedt is equally at home exploring the rarefied world of art criticism and the intricacies of family dynamics, and her novel is enriched by its many allusions to art, literature, and philosophy.

If "What I Loved" is a novel about the power of art to transcend time and tragedy, it is also a novel about the fragility of human relationships and the devastating consequences of loss and betrayal. As the novel builds towards its shattering climax, Hustvedt's characters are forced to confront the darker aspects of their own natures and the terrible secrets they have kept hidden for so long.

In the end, "What I Loved" is a novel about the enduring power of love and the human spirit, even in the face of unimaginable loss and tragedy. It is a testament to the transformative power of art and the boundless capacity of the human heart to love, to suffer, and to persevere.
