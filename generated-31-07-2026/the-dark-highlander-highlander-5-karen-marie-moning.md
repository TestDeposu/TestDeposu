# The Dark Highlander (Highlander, #5)

**Author:** Karen Marie Moning  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2002

Karen Marie Moning is a master of blending high-stakes paranormal romance with rich historical fantasy, and in The Dark Highlander, the fifth installment of her celebrated Highlander series, she delivers one of her most intoxicating and perilous tales yet. Published in 2002, this novel takes the signature elements that made her earlier work a fan favorite—time travel, brooding alpha heroes, and ancient Celtic mythology—and cranks the intensity up to a fever pitch. At its core, the novel is a gripping exploration of the eternal battle between light and dark, salvation and damnation, anchored by a romance that is as emotionally consuming as it is physically electric.

The main theme of The Dark Highlander revolves around the corrupting nature of absolute power and the redemptive power of selfless love. This is embodied masterfully by the protagonist, Dageus MacKeltar. A sixteenth-century Scot stranded in modern-day Manhattan, Dageus is a walking catastrophe. He carries within his body the trapped souls of thirteen ancient, evil Druids who constantly whisper to his darkest instincts, threatening to turn him into a monster. Moning uses Dageus to explore the psychological torment of a man at war with his own mind and flesh. He is a man damned by history, resigned to his fate until Chloe Zanders enters his orbit. 

Chloe, an unassuming yet fiercely resilient student of antiquities, serves as the perfect foil to Dageus's brooding, tortured intensity. When she crosses paths with the dangerous immortal, she is not repelled by his darkness; instead, she is drawn into the magnetic and lethal orbit of an alpha male who is literally fighting for his soul. Moning’s thematic brilliance shines brightest as Chloe refuses to be a passive damsel in distress. She becomes an active participant in the supernatural struggle, proving that true strength often lies in empathy, intelligence, and unwavering emotional courage. 

Moning’s signature style is on full display here, characterized by lush, sensory-rich prose and relentless pacing. She has an uncanny ability to make historical settings and supernatural lore feel visceral and immediate. Whether she is describing the glittering, suffocating heights of a Manhattan penthouse or plunging the reader—and her characters—into the rugged, mist-shrouded dangers of medieval Scotland through time travel, her world-building is impeccably detailed. The atmosphere is thick with ancient magic, brooding tension, and palpable danger. 

Furthermore, Moning excels at balancing high-concept fantasy with scorching sensuality. The romance between Dageus and Chloe is not merely physical; it is a desperate, soul-deep collision of two broken pieces fitting together. The dialogue is sharp, the chemistry is combustible, and the emotional stakes feel monumental because Moning never lets the reader forget that failure means eternal damnation. 

The Dark Highlander is a triumph of paranormal romance and time-travel fantasy. It sweeps the reader across continents and centuries in a breathless narrative that tests the limits of faith, love, and sacrifice. Moning proves once again why she is a titan of the genre, delivering a story that is as intellectually engaging as it is wildly seductive. For fans of dark fantasy, tortured heroes, and epic romance, this book remains an unforgettable journey into the shadows of the human (and immortal) heart.
