# Rise of Empire (The Riyria Revelations, #3-4)

**Author:** Michael J. Sullivan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

**Rise of Empire: A Masterclass in Epic Fantasy**

In 'Rise of Empire,' Michael J. Sullivan delivers a tour de force of epic fantasy, seamlessly blending political intrigue, high-stakes adventure, and a dash of the supernatural. This omnibus volume, comprising books three and four of 'The Riyria Revelations,' is a testament to Sullivan's prowess as a storyteller, offering readers a gripping narrative that keeps them on the edge of their seats.

At the heart of 'Rise of Empire' lies a tale of resistance and unity in the face of tyranny. The newly formed empire, a sinister force led by the enigmatic Emperor, threatens to engulf the kingdom of Melengar. Royce Melborn and Hadrian Blackwater, the dynamic duo at the core of the Riyria Revelations, find themselves entangled in a desperate gamble to forge an alliance with the Nationalists to the south. Sullivan masterfully weaves a tapestry of political machinations, military strategies, and moral dilemmas, creating a richly textured world that feels both familiar and fresh.

Sullivan's strength lies not only in his ability to craft a compelling narrative but also in his character development. Royce and Hadrian, the thief and the swordsman, are a classic pairing, but Sullivan imbues them with depth and complexity. Their bond, forged in the crucible of shared trials, is the beating heart of the series. Yet, it's Royce's quest to uncover Hadrian's hidden past that truly sets 'Rise of Empire' apart. This personal journey, intertwined with the broader political landscape, adds a poignant, emotional resonance to the story.

Sullivan's prose is crisp and engaging, his pacing flawless. He expertly balances action, dialogue, and exposition, ensuring that the story never stagnates. His world-building is meticulous, with a rich history and mythology that feels organic and lived-in. Moreover, Sullivan's willingness to explore the moral gray areas of his characters and their world lends an authenticity to his storytelling that is often lacking in epic fantasy.

'Rise of Empire' is not without its challenges. The sheer scope of the narrative can sometimes feel overwhelming, and the plot can veer into convoluted territory. However, Sullivan's skill as a storyteller ensures that the reader is never lost for long. This is a series that demands investment, but it rewards generously in return.

In conclusion, 'Rise of Empire' is a must-read for fans of epic fantasy. Sullivan's masterful storytelling, compelling characters, and intricate world-building create a narrative that is both engaging and thought-provoking. This is not just a story of war and resistance; it's a meditation on loyalty, friendship, and the price of power.
