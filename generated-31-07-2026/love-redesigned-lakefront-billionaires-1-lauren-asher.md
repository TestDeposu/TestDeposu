# Love Redesigned (Lakefront Billionaires, #1)

**Author:** Lauren Asher  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2024

**Love Redesigned: Where Rivalry Meets Passion in a Billionaire’s Bold Renovation**

Lauren Asher’s *Love Redesigned*—the first installment in the *Lakefront Billionaires* series—is a sizzling blend of architectural passion, childhood grudges, and the kind of slow-burn tension that makes you forget to breathe. At its core, this story is about two people who have spent years burning bridges, only to find themselves trapped in the same room—literally—with no way out. And when the walls of a crumbling mansion become the battleground for their war of wits and wills, the sparks fly in every sense of the word.

Julian Lopez is the kind of man who commands a room before he even enters it: a billionaire developer with a sharp tongue, a sharper mind, and a mother whose whims dictate his life. When Dahlia Muñoz, his childhood nemesis, returns to Lake Wisteria—still nursing the wounds of a broken engagement—Julian sees an opportunity. A project. A way to fulfill his mother’s last request without actually *liking* the woman who once stole his lunch money. What he doesn’t expect is the way her stubbornness, her creativity, and her refusal to back down will unravel every carefully constructed wall between them. Dahlia, a talented but underappreciated architect, has spent years proving herself in a world that dismissed her. Now, forced to collaborate with Julian, she’s determined to show him—and herself—that she’s more than just the girl who once humiliated him in front of the whole school.

Their dynamic is electric, a masterclass in enemies-to-lovers that doesn’t rely on clichés but on the raw, unfiltered chemistry of two people who *know* each other’s buttons—and keep pushing them. Asher doesn’t shy away from the heat; the spice level is there, but it’s never gratuitous. Instead, it’s woven into the fabric of their relationship, a natural byproduct of the friction between them. Julian’s arrogance grates on Dahlia’s nerves, but so does the way he can’t stop looking at her—like she’s a puzzle he’s been trying to solve for years. Dahlia’s sharp tongue cuts through Julian’s usual bravado, forcing him to confront the fact that she’s not just another project to him. She’s a challenge. And challenges, it turns out, are exactly what he needs.

What makes *Love Redesigned* truly compelling is its setting. Lake Wisteria isn’t just a backdrop; it’s a character in its own right. The historic mansion they’re renovating is more than four walls and a roof—it’s a relic of the past, much like the relationship between Julian and Dahlia. Asher does an incredible job of blending the romance with the intricacies of architectural restoration, making the process feel as passionate and fraught as their personal conflict. There’s a beautiful tension here between the old and the new, between the way things *were* and the way they could be. Julian wants to modernize, to erase the past. Dahlia wants to preserve, to honor the history. And in their battle of ideologies, they’re forced to ask themselves: *What do we really want to keep?*

Asher’s writing is sharp, witty, and effortlessly engaging. She has a knack for dialogue that crackles with tension, for scenes that feel lived-in, and for characters who are deeply flawed but undeniably compelling. Julian is a man who’s spent his life playing the role of the unfeeling billionaire, but Dahlia sees through him—and that’s what makes her so dangerous. Dahlia, for her part, is fierce, resilient, and unafraid to call Julian out on his bullshit. Together, they create a dynamic that’s both frustrating and thrilling, a reminder that the best relationships aren’t built on easy agreement but on the messy, beautiful chaos of two people refusing to give up.

The novel also does a fantastic job of exploring themes of redemption and second chances. Julian’s mother’s request to renovate the mansion isn’t just about preserving history—it’s about him confronting his own past, his own failures, and the way he’s spent his life avoiding vulnerability. Dahlia, too, is on a journey of self-discovery, learning to trust herself and her instincts after years of being told she wasn’t good enough. Their renovation of the house mirrors their own personal transformations, brick by brick, paint by paint.

Of course, no enemies-to-lovers story would be complete without its fair share of tension. Asher doesn’t hold back when it comes to the chemistry between Julian and Dahlia. The spice level is rated at 3 out of 5, which means there’s plenty of heat—steamy moments, lingering glances, and the kind of physicality that makes you want to fan yourself—but it’s never overdone. Instead, it’s a slow burn, a simmering undercurrent that builds with every argument, every shared laugh, every moment where they’re so close they could touch. And when they finally do touch? Oh, it’s *good*.

What makes *Love Redesigned* stand out in the crowded romance genre is its balance. It’s not just a love story; it’s a story about two people who have to learn to work together, to compromise, to see each other as more than just rivals. It’s about the beauty of imperfection, the strength of second chances, and the way love—even the messy, complicated kind—can be the most rewarding renovation of all.

If you love stories where the past refuses to stay buried, where two people are forced into close quarters and can’t resist the pull between them, and where the slow burn is as satisfying as the final spark, then *Love Redesigned* is for you. Lauren Asher has crafted a romance that’s as rich in emotional depth as it is in steamy tension, a story that will leave you cheering for Dahlia and Julian long after the last page.
