# Dawnshard (The Stormlight Archive, #3.5)

**Author:** Brandon Sanderson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

"Dawnshard", the latest offering from Brandon Sanderson, is a novella that expertly weaves together two seemingly unrelated storylines to create a rich tapestry of intrigue and adventure. This installment in the Stormlight Archive series takes place between "Oathbringer" and "Rhythm of War", and it's a testament to Sanderson's skill as a storyteller that he can craft a compelling narrative that feels both connected to the larger series and standalone enough to be enjoyable on its own.

At the heart of "Dawnshard" is a mystery: the ghost ship found adrift, its crew presumed dead after attempting to reach the fabled island of Akinah. Navani Kholin, one of the series' most intelligent and resourceful characters, sees this as an opportunity to investigate and ensure the island hasn't fallen into enemy hands. However, the task is fraught with danger, as the Stormlight that sustains the Knights Radiant is somehow drained when they fly too close to Akinah.

Meanwhile, Rysn Ftori, a shipowner who's lost the use of her legs, embarks on a perilous journey with her loyal companion, Chiri-Chiri, a winged larkin that's ingesting Stormlight. With Chiri-Chiri's health in decline, Rysn sees Akinah as the only hope for saving her beloved pet, and she agrees to join Navani's quest. This setup provides a unique opportunity for Sanderson to explore themes of perseverance, friendship, and the unbreakable bonds between people and their companions.

Throughout the novella, Sanderson masterfully blends action, adventure, and subtle hints at the larger Cosmere. He weaves together complex worldbuilding and magic systems with ease, making the fantastical world of Roshar feel both fully realized and vast in scope. His writing style is, as always, economical yet evocative, conjuring vivid images with a few, well-chosen words. Sanderson's ability to balance action and introspection is impressive, creating moments of high tension and quiet contemplation that feel authentic and true to the story.

What strikes me about "Dawnshard" is its focus on underappreciated characters, allowing them to shine and take the spotlight away from the more prominent players in the series. Rysn and Navani, while not entirely new to the series, are given more depth and agency here than ever before. Their relationships with the other characters, particularly Lopen, who appears in a supporting role, add a new layer of complexity to the story.

Overall, "Dawnshard" is a gripping and captivating read that perfectly captures the essence of the Stormlight Archive. Sanderson's mastery of the fantasy genre is on full display, and his ability to craft compelling characters and storylines is truly impressive. Whether you're a seasoned fan of the series or a newcomer, "Dawnshard" is an excellent addition to the Cosmere, and its themes of perseverance, friendship, and the power of connection will linger with readers long after the final page is turned.
