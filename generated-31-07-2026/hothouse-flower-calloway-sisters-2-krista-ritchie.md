# Hothouse Flower (Calloway Sisters, #2)

**Author:** Krista Ritchie  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

In Hothouse Flower, the second installment of the beloved Calloway Sisters series (and a vital extension of the broader Addicted universe), co-authors Krista and Becca Ritchie invite readers back into a high-stakes world where immense privilege collides with raw, suffocating emotional vulnerability. Known for their unflinching exploration of deeply flawed characters, the Ritchie sisters deliver a compelling New Adult romance that trades superficial drama for a gritty, heart-pounding examination of survival, obsession, and forbidden devotion. 

At the core of the novel is the volatile and magnetic dynamic between Ryke Meadows and Daisy Calloway. Ryke, a twenty-five-year-old billionaire heir and daredevil solo-climber, embodies the quintessential alpha archetype, yet he is weighed down by a fierce protectiveness and his own internal demons. Daisy, finally eighteen and thrust into the dizzying, treacherous heights of the Paris Fashion Week scene, is a young woman desperate to outrun a suffocating mother and her own escalating self-destructive impulses. When Daisy finds herself trapped in the dark underbelly of the modeling world, she bypasses her famous family entirely, making a desperate call to the one man she was never, under any circumstances, supposed to love.

The central theme of Hothouse Flower is the dangerous allure of boundaries—and the inevitable devastation that follows when they are shattered. Ryke and Daisy do not simply fall in love; they collide like tectonic plates. The authors brilliantly juxtapose Ryke’s physical risk-taking on the sheer granite walls of Yosemite with the emotional precipice he walks every time he tries to keep Daisy safe. Daisy’s reckless behavior is not framed as mere teenage rebellion, but as a visceral cry for help, masking a profound, aching pain. Ryke becomes her anchor, even as he realizes that loving her might mean watching her—or himself—burst into flames. 

The Ritchie sisters' writing style remains a masterclass in emotional pacing and character-driven narrative. They excel at writing intensely codependent yet fiercely loyal soulmates, grounding their fictional worlds in a hyper-realistic depiction of trauma, addiction, and recovery. The prose is snappy, highly addictive, and drenched in atmospheric tension, effortlessly transporting the reader from the dizzying lights of Paris to the lonely, dangerous peaks of nature. The dialogue crackles with undeniable chemistry, balancing biting banter with heart-wrenching confessions.

Ultimately, Hothouse Flower is a striking critique of the gilded cages we build for ourselves and the terrifying cost of breaking free. It is a testament to the fact that sometimes, the greatest danger isn't the abyss below, but the person who makes you want to live. For fans of intense New Adult romance, broken characters finding their missing pieces in one another, and high-stakes emotional drama, this novel remains an absolute triumph.
