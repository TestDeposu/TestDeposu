# Origin (Corpus, #1)

**Author:** Jessica Khoury  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

**A Descent into the Heart of Genetic Creation**

Jessica Khoury's "Origin" is a thought-provoking and unsettling tale that delves into the darker aspects of scientific innovation. In this gripping novel, the lines between humanity and creation are blurred, posing questions about the very fabric of life and our capacity for curiosity. "Origin" is a meticulously crafted story that propels readers into the midst of a treacherous world where science knows no bounds, and the consequences of pushing beyond the limits of human understanding are devastating.

Khoury masterfully weaves a intricate narrative that centers around Sophie Crue, a determined seventeen-year-old, and her twin sister, Lux, a being of exceptional abilities, crafted by the enigmatic corporation, Corpus. This dichotomous relationship serves as a prism through which Khoury examines the implications of genetic engineering and the ethics of scientific research. The author's writing is characterized by a fluid, lyrical style that paints vivid pictures of the lush, tropical setting, heightening the sense of unease and foreboding that permeates the novel.

As Sophie navigates the complexities of her own identity, she is thrust into a world where science and morality are in a precarious dance. The Vitros, these genetically engineered beings, embody the pinnacle of human ingenuity, yet they also represent the abyss that lies beyond the bounds of nature. Khoury skillfully probes the consequences of tampering with the fundamental building blocks of life, exposing the vulnerabilities of both creator and creation.

One of the most striking aspects of "Origin" is its ability to evoke a multitude of emotions, oscillating between hope and despair, wonder and fear. Khoury's use of language is deliberate and precise, imbuing the narrative with a sense of urgency and fragility. The world of Skin Island, with its lush vegetation and eerie atmosphere, serves as a powerful metaphor for the unknown, emphasizing the inherent risks of scientific exploration.

In "Origin," Khoury raises pressing questions about the role of science in our society, cautioning against the unbridled pursuit of knowledge. The novel serves as a warning, a reminder that humanity's quest for innovation must be tempered by compassion, respect, and a deep understanding of the natural world. As Sophie's story unfolds, the reader is invited to ponder the consequences of our collective appetite for discovery and the impact it may have on our very existence.

Throughout the novel, Khoury's prose is rich, evocative, and immersive, immersing the reader in the treacherous world of Skin Island. Her characters are multidimensional and complex, with Sophie and Lux emerging as nuanced, multidimensional representations of human experience. The supporting cast is equally well-crafted, adding depth and texture to the narrative.

In conclusion, "Origin" is a masterful, unsettling novel that dares to challenge our assumptions about the nature of creation and humanity. Jessica Khoury's writing is evocative, precise, and thought-provoking, transporting readers to a world where science and ethics collide. This novel is a must-read for anyone who has ever questioned the boundaries of human knowledge and the consequences of pushing beyond the limits of understanding.
