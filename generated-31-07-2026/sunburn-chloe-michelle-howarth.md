# Sunburn

**Author:** Chloe Michelle Howarth  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

A blistering debut that captures the exquisite agony of first love, Chloe Michelle Howarth’s Sunburn is a masterclass in atmospheric tension and emotional restraint. Set against the suffocating backdrop of early 1990s rural Ireland, this Indie bestseller arrives not as a gentle coming-of-age story, but as a searing examination of desire, religion, and the heavy price of keeping secrets in a town where privacy is an illusion. 

At its core, Sunburn is driven by the universal terror and intoxicating thrill of knowing you want something—or someone—society forbids. The novel follows Lucy, a young woman trapped in the predetermined script of her small-town existence. In Crossmore, the trajectory of a girl’s life is painted in broad, traditional strokes: marry the neighbor’s son, attend Mass, raise children, and quietly swallow any ambitions that stretch beyond the pasture. Yet Lucy harbors a truth that threatens to shatter this idyllic veneer, a consuming obsession with her close friend, Susannah. 

Howarth’s exploration of queer identity is inextricably linked to the geography of guilt. The oppressive Catholic conservatism of rural Ireland acts almost as an antagonist in its own right. The author expertly evokes the stifling atmosphere of a tight-knit community where everyone knows your name, your family, and your business, turning the long, hazy days of a sweltering Irish summer from a pastoral dream into a pressure cooker. Every glance, every accidental touch, and every unspoken word carries the weight of potential ruin. 

Stylistically, comparisons to Sally Rooney’s Normal People are well-earned, though Howarth carves out a distinct, poetic voice of her own. Her prose is tactile and evocative, burning with the intensity of the book's titular heat. She captures the precise, agonizing psychology of teenage angst—the way a single summer can feel like an entire lifetime, and how the fear of losing someone can manifest as a physical ache. The narrative pacing mirrors the mounting panic of its protagonists; as Susannah’s patience with hiding wears thin, the tension builds toward a devastating crescendo that forces Lucy to choose between self-preservation and authenticity.

Sunburn does not offer easy resolutions or comforting nostalgic tropes. Instead, it honors the messy, painful reality of first love and first heartbreak. It is a poignant reminder of the courage it takes to step out of the shadows of expectation, even when the light threatens to burn you. Howarth has crafted a striking, unforgettable debut that will leave readers aching, breathless, and profoundly moved long after the final page is turned.
