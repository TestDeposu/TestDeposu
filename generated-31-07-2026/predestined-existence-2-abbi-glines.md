Title: The Weight of Choice: A Review of Abbi Glines’ Predestined

When the immutable laws of the afterlife collide with the fierce, untamed pulse of mortal emotion, the result is a narrative territory only Abbi Glines could map. In Predestined, the second installment of her evocative Existence series, Glines returns to a world where destiny is not merely a philosophical concept, but a tangible, breathing force. Stripping away the sugar-coated tropes common to young adult paranormal romance, Glines dives headfirst into the heavy, suffocating atmosphere of sacrifice, exploring the ultimate thematic question: When your entire existence is prewritten, what is the cost of rewriting the ending?

At the heart of Predestined lies the timeless theme of determinism versus free will. Glines uses her supernatural framework not as a gimmick, but as an emotional magnifying glass. Pagan Moore, a girl who can see the dead and the Grim Reapers who escort them, finds herself entangled with Dank—an entity who is, quite literally, Death personified. In this sequel, the stakes are elevated from survival to autonomy. The central conflict forces the characters to grapple with the terrifying reality that every heartbeat, every touch, and every choice might be an illusion engineered by a higher, unyielding cosmic order. Glines masterfully translates this abstract existential dread into visceral, pulse-pounding drama. The universe wants a specific outcome, yet the characters demand a different truth, turning their romance into an act of rebellion against fate itself.

Glines’ authorial style is defined by its unrelenting emotional immediacy. She writes with a cinematic urgency that bypasses intellectual hesitation and strikes straight at the nervous system. There is an unmistakable rawness to her prose; she does not waste time on flowery descriptions or overwritten world-building. Instead, Glines focuses intensely on sensory details—the chill in the air when death approaches, the electric friction of forbidden touch, and the desperate cadence of unspoken words. Her dialogue is snappy, modern, and emotionally naked, allowing readers to feel the raw vulnerability of adolescents forced to shoulder divine responsibilities. This minimalist yet potent style ensures that the pacing never falters, pulling the reader relentlessly forward through waves of angst, longing, and suspense.

What makes Predestined linger in the mind long after the final page is its refusal to offer easy answers. Glines understands that true stakes require genuine peril. The romance between Pagan and Dank is far from a comfortable fairy tale; it is fraught with danger, defined by the agonizing realization that loving someone when the cosmos forbids it is an act of supreme defiance. The author captures the intoxicating intensity of first love and magnifies it against the backdrop of eternity, making ordinary teenage angst feel earth-shattering.

Predestined is a striking, fast-paced exploration of love defying the boundaries of mortality and design. Abbi Glines proves that the most compelling stories are not about where we end up, but how hard we fight to choose our own path. For fans of supernatural romance and emotionally charged storytelling, this novel is a breathless, unforgettable journey into the shadows of destiny.
