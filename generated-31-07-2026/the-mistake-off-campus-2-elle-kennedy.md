# The Mistake (Off-Campus, #2)

**Author:** Elle Kennedy  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

In the bustling landscape of contemporary new adult romance, few authors command the genre with the effortless charm and emotional resonance of Elle Kennedy. Following the massive success of The Deal, the second installment in her sensational Off-Campus series, The Mistake, proves that Kennedy is no one-hit-wonder. Published in 2015, this vibrant college romance shifts the spotlight from the ice rink to the complex, often chaotic internal worlds of youth, expertly balancing sizzling chemistry with genuine character growth. 

At its core, The Mistake explores the universal theme of reinvention under pressure. The narrative introduces us to John Logan, a junior hockey star at Briar University whose outwardly enviable life is a carefully constructed facade. Beneath the killer grins, endless parties, and easy hook-ups lies a profound, quiet despair regarding a predetermined, dead-end future mapped out by an overbearing family. Logan is the textbook definition of a player in more ways than one, using physical distraction to numb his existential dread. Enter Grace Ivers, a sharp-witted freshman who refuses to play by the rules of Logan's usual social orbit. Their initial encounter sparks a fiery connection, but a thoughtless mistake on Logan’s part abruptly shatters the fragile trust building between them. 

What makes Kennedy’s storytelling so compelling is how she subverts traditional romance tropes. Instead of leaning into a cliché pursuit where the male lead simply demands forgiveness, Kennedy flips the dynamic on its head. Grace returns for her sophomore year older, wiser, and entirely unimpressed by Logan’s hockey-star pedigree. She is no longer the quiet, insecure wallflower; she is fierce, self-assured, and determined to hold all the cards. If Logan wants a second chance, he is going to have to sweat for it. This evolution transforms the novel from a standard collegiate fling into a deeply satisfying battle of wits and emotional endurance. 

Kennedy’s stylistic signature lies in her pitch-perfect dialogue and palpable pacing. She captures the authentic cadence of college life—the locker room banter, the dorm room debates, the intoxicating mix of freedom and anxiety that defines early adulthood. Her prose is snappy, highly visual, and effortlessly readable, drawing readers into a world that feels both aspirational and intimately relatable. She possesses a rare knack for blending laugh-out-loud humor with moments of raw vulnerability, ensuring that the heavy emotional stakes never bog down the infectious energy of the narrative. 

Ultimately, The Mistake is a triumphant sophomore slump-breaker. It is a story about shedding expectations, owning up to one’s flaws, and realizing that true strength often lies in vulnerability. Logan and Grace’s journey from careless misstep to hard-earned devotion is as entertaining as it is emotionally rewarding, cementing Elle Kennedy's status as a master of the romantic comedy genre. For anyone seeking a fast-paced, witty, and deeply romantic escape, this Briar University hockey romance is an absolute must-read.
