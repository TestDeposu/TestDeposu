# In the Shadows of the Blitz: A deeply captivating classic crime thriller (The DCI Frank Merlin Series)

**Author:** Mark Ellis  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

Set against the harrowing backdrop of a smoke-choked, bomb-shattered London, Mark Ellis’s *In the Shadows of the Blitz*—the second compelling installment in the DCI Frank Merlin series (previously published as *Stalin's Gold*)—delivers a masterclass in historical crime fiction. Ellis does not merely write a procedural; he resurrects an era. As the Battle of Britain rages in the skies above and air-raid sirens wail below, the author plunges readers headfirst into the chaotic underbelly of a city struggling to survive.

The narrative's central engine sparks when Ziggy Kilinski, a Polish RAF pilot, vanishes without a trace. Tasked with the investigation is Detective Chief Inspector Frank Merlin, a sharp, world-weary investigator whose Anglo-Spanish heritage and keen intellect make him the perfect guide through the labyrinth of wartime London. While Merlin and his team grapple with the rising tide of opportunistic looting spawned by the Blitz, a much deadlier game is afoot in the shadows. Unbeknownst to Scotland Yard, Soviet operatives have infiltrated the capital on a high-stakes mission to retrieve a legendary cache of Stalin’s lost gold. 

What follows is a deliciously complex web of deceit that showcases Ellis’s supreme plotting abilities. The investigation expands outward, entangling a colorful yet dangerous dramatis personae: volatile Polish fighter pilots, a disgraced Cambridge academic turned traitor, figures from the Polish government-in-exile, and a cold-blooded Russian gangster. Ellis brilliantly pivots the narrative across sweeping international geographies—from the brutal ideological battlegrounds of Civil War Spain and Hitler’s menacing Berlin to the corridors of power in Churchill’s London and the distant chill of Stalin’s Russia. 

The true triumph of the novel, however, lies in its atmosphere and style. Ellis writes with a meticulous dedication to historical authenticity that elevates the book above standard genre fare. Reviewers and readers alike have rightly praised the series for feeling genuinely immaculately nuanced—so much so that the prose reads as though it were penned in the mid-20th century itself. The London of the Blitz is rendered in vivid, sensory detail: the acrid smell of cordite, the ominous crunch of glass underfoot, the defiant camaraderie of the pubs, and the ever-present dread lurking in the blackout. 

Merlin’s world is a Dickensian plethora of characters, brought to life with distinct voices and ambiguous moral compasses. The dialogue crackles with period-appropriate tension, and the pacing strikes a rare balance, marrying the relentless suspense of a spy thriller with the methodical deduction of classic noir. 

*In the Shadows of the Blitz* is a spellbinding, multi-layered page-turner that seamlessly bridges the gap between classic espionage and gritty wartime detection. For fans of historical mysteries who crave rich textures, international intrigue, and a detective of sharp distinction, Mark Ellis’s latest offering is an absolute triumph.
