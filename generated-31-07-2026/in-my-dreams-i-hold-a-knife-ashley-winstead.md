# In My Dreams I Hold a Knife

**Author:** Ashley Winstead  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

Ashley Winstead’s 2021 debut novel, *In My Dreams I Hold a Knife*, crashes onto the contemporary thriller scene with the biting precision of a scalpel and the breathless pacing of a midnight sprint. Set against the sun-drenched, ivy-draped backdrop of the elite southern Duquette University, Winstead crafts a deliciously toxic world where ambition is a blood sport and the price of admission is your soul. This is not your standard whodunit; it is a sharp, psychological dissection of toxic female friendship, consuming jealousy, and the heavy, rotting weight of the secrets we bury just beneath the surface.

At the heart of the novel is Jessica Miller, a protagonist who embodies the dark side of reinvention. Ten years after graduating from Duquette—and a decade after the brutal, unsolved murder of her former friend Heather Shelby—Jessica returns for a high-stakes college reunion. Winstead utilizes a racing dual timeline that alternates between the golden, reckless days of the characters' freshman year and the claustrophobic, wine-soaked tension of the present-day reunion weekend. Through this structure, the author masterfully peels back the layers of a seemingly golden circle of six friends, exposing a rot of betrayal, obsessive love, and cutthroat social hierarchy. 

Winstead’s greatest triumph in this debut lies in her unapologetic exploration of female ambition. In many thrillers, female characters are flattened into saints or sinners, but Winstead embraces the messy, unlikable, and fiercely driven psyche. Jessica and her peers are not just victims or suspects; they are architects of their own destruction, driven by a desperate need to belong, to succeed, and to outshine one another at any cost. The author brilliantly captures the specific, localized terror of elite academia—a place where a bad grade or a social misstep feels like a matter of life and death, and where privilege often acts as both a shield and a gilded cage.

The prose is striking, balancing lush, atmospheric descriptions of campus life with a dark, cynical edge that keeps the reader perpetually on guard. Winstead understands that the most effective thrillers rely as much on emotional claustrophobia as they do on plot twists. As the reunion progresses and the past bleeds into the present, it becomes terrifyingly clear that someone among the group is hunting for the truth about Heather’s death—and they are willing to shatter whatever lives are left to get it. 

Tense, propulsive, and entirely addictive, *In My Dreams I Hold a Knife* proves that Ashley Winstead is a major new voice in psychological suspense. It is a razor-sharp critique of the lies we tell ourselves to survive, and a stark reminder that the ghosts of our past rarely stay buried where we left them. For fans of dark academia and messy, complex characters who refuse to be well-behaved, this debut is an absolute must-read.
