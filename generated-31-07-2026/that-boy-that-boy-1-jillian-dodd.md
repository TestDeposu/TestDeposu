# That Boy (That Boy, #1)

**Author:** Jillian Dodd  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

**That Boy by Jillian Dodd: A Love Story That Lingers Like a First Crush**

Jillian Dodd’s *That Boy* isn’t just a romance—it’s a tender, bittersweet ode to the kind of love that starts with friendship and unfolds like a slow, inevitable tide. Set in the kind of small-town America where every street corner feels familiar and every heartache tastes like nostalgia, this book captures the bittersweet ache of first love, the quiet desperation of wanting what you can’t have, and the way two people can be best friends for years before realizing one of them has always been *that boy*—the one who owns your heart without even trying.

At its core, *That Boy* is a story about the tension between two men who are everything to each other—except the one thing that matters most. Danny is the golden boy: effortlessly charming, athletic, and the kind of guy who makes the world feel brighter just by being in it. Phillip is the steady one, the quiet observer who sees you in ways no one else does, the voice of reason when Danny’s reckless energy threatens to pull you under. Together, they’re a perfect storm of opposites—one a whirlwind of laughter and stolen kisses, the other a quiet, unshakable presence. But love, as the story so beautifully reminds us, doesn’t always follow logic. It follows the heart, and in this case, it follows the slow, agonizing realization that you’ve been in love with the wrong one all along.

Dodd’s writing is warm, intimate, and effortlessly immersive. She has a knack for crafting dialogue that feels real, for painting small-town scenes with the kind of detail that makes you feel like you’re standing right there—smelling the fresh-cut grass, hearing the distant hum of a high school football game, feeling the weight of a first kiss that lingers long after the moment passes. Her prose is lyrical without being pretentious, and her emotional beats hit with the precision of a well-aimed football pass. There’s a melancholy undercurrent to the story, a quiet sorrow that makes the happy moments feel even sweeter and the heartbreaks feel all the more real. It’s the kind of book that makes you laugh at Danny’s antics, sigh at Phillip’s quiet devotion, and ache for the love that feels just out of reach.

What makes *That Boy* so compelling isn’t just the slow-burn tension between the two leads—though that’s undeniably a major draw—but the way Dodd explores the complexity of love and friendship. The story doesn’t shy away from the messiness of human emotions. There are almost-kisses that never happen, promises made and broken, and the kind of quiet heartache that comes from loving someone who loves you differently. It’s a story about the way love can be both a gift and a curse, about how the people who know you best might also be the ones who see you in ways you don’t see yourself. And ultimately, it’s a story about the courage it takes to admit that you’ve been in love with the wrong person—and the even harder choice to let go.

The trope list provided in the synopsis doesn’t do the book justice, but it’s not hard to see why readers are drawn to it. Friends-to-lovers dynamics are always compelling, especially when the love is so slow-burning that it feels like a secret only the characters themselves can’t keep. The small-town romance setting adds a layer of nostalgia, making the story feel like a warm hug wrapped in a familiar place. The golden boy athlete and the steady, unassuming love interest create a dynamic that’s both classic and fresh, while the "everyone sees it but them" trope adds a layer of heartbreaking irony that keeps you turning the pages.

But what truly elevates *That Boy* is its emotional honesty. Dodd doesn’t sugarcoat love or make it easy. There are no neat resolutions, no instant fixes—just the messy, beautiful, heart-wrenching reality of falling in love with someone who isn’t the one you’re supposed to be with. It’s a story that will make you laugh, cry, and remember what it’s like to be young and in love, even if you’re not sure which way the wind is blowing. By the end, you’ll be left with that familiar, bittersweet feeling—like the last note of a song you can’t quite forget.

If you love slow-burn romances that linger in your mind long after the last page, if you’re drawn to stories about love that feels like a second chance, or if you just want a book that makes you feel all the things—*That Boy* is the perfect choice. It’s a love story that will stay with you, a reminder that sometimes the most painful loves are the ones that teach you the most.
