# Hold Still

**Author:** Nina LaCour  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Grief is rarely a straight line. More often, it is a suffocating fog that settles over the everyday, blurring the boundaries between memory and reality. In her stunning 2009 debut, *Hold Still*, Nina LaCour approaches this universal truth not with melodrama, but with a quiet, devastating grace. Long before her Printz Award-winning *We Are Okay*, LaCour established herself as a master of emotional topography, charting the invisible landscapes of loss, art, and survival. 

The novel opens in the aftermath of an unimaginable void. Caitlin and Ingrid were inseparable, two halves of a creative whole defined by art, music, shared laughter, and late-night talks. But one morning, the promise Ingrid whispered—"I'll go wherever you go"—is broken by suicide. Ingrid is gone, leaving Caitlin completely unmoored. The life they built together evaporates, and Caitlin is left to navigate a world that has suddenly lost its color and rhythm. 

What makes *Hold Still* a remarkable achievement in young adult literature is its refusal to offer easy resolutions. LaCour is deeply interested in the immobilizing nature of early grief. For Caitlin, the days bleed into one another as she struggles to find her footing in a high school existence that now feels entirely alien. She has lost her best friend, but she has also temporarily lost her voice, her passion for art, and her sense of self. 

Yet, the narrative pivots on a fragile thread of discovery. Ingrid left behind a journal—a visceral, heartbreaking documentation of her final days, filled with words and drawings that capture the depth of her descent. This journal becomes the catalyst for Caitlin’s harrowing journey. By confronting Ingrid’s pain, Caitlin is forced to confront her own. It is a slow, agonizing process of wading through the wreckage of the past to find a way back to the living. 

LaCour’s literary style is the beating heart of the novel. Her prose is deceptively simple, yet it resonates with a profound lyrical intensity. She writes with a visual artist’s eye, capturing the changing seasons of Caitlin’s first year without Ingrid through striking imagery. The transition of the weather mirrors the internal shifts in Caitlin’s psyche, moving from the biting chill of isolation toward the tentative warmth of renewal. By weaving Ingrid’s artwork and journal entries directly into the narrative, LaCour elevates the book into a mixed-media exploration of expression, proving that when words fail, creativity can serve as a lifeline.

*Hold Still* is not an easy read, but it is an essential one. It is a gently moving account of a long goodbye that honors the complexity of mental health and suicide without sensationalizing it. LaCour handles her subject matter with immense empathy and profound respect for her readers. For anyone who has ever had to let go of someone before they were ready, this breakthrough debut offers a poignant reminder that while grief changes us permanently, it is possible to fight our way back to hope.
