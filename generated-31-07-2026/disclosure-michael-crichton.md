# Disclosure

**Author:** Michael Crichton  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1994

**Disclosure by Michael Crichton: A Chilling Tale of Power, Perception, and Predators**

In the vein of his blockbuster hits like *Jurassic Park* and *Sphere*, Michael Crichton delivers another electrifying thriller with *Disclosure*. Published in 1994, this #1 New York Times bestseller is a gripping exploration of power dynamics, sexual harassment, and the perils of perception in the corporate world.

At the heart of the story is Tom Sanders, a rising star at the computer firm DigiCom. His career trajectory seems assured until his former lover and rival, Meredith Johnson, is promoted over him to the position he coveted. When Sanders is subsequently accused of sexual harassment by Johnson, his world spirals into a nightmarish web of deceit and manipulation.

Crichton masterfully weaves a tale that is as much about the blurred lines of sexual politics as it is about the cold, hard logic of corporate ambition. The author's signature style—crisp, concise prose and meticulous research—lends an air of authenticity to the story, making the characters and their struggles feel all too real.

As Sanders fights to clear his name, he uncovers a sinister plot that goes far beyond his personal vendetta with Johnson. Crichton expertly crafts a heart-stopping narrative that runs on multiple tracks, keeping readers on the edge of their seats as they follow Sanders' desperate quest for the truth.

At its core, *Disclosure* is a cautionary tale about the dangers of misperception and the destructive power of unchecked ambition. Crichton's exploration of these themes is as timely today as it was when the book was first published, making it a must-read for anyone interested in the complex dynamics of power and gender in the workplace.
