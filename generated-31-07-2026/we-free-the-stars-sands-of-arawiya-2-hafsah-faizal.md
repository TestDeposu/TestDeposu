# We Free the Stars (Sands of Arawiya, #2)

**Author:** Hafsah Faizal  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

Rating: 4.5/5 Stars

To craft a fantasy world that smells of cardamom, rings with the sharp clash of scimitars, and bleeds with the agonizing weight of destiny is no small feat. To conclude that world with a crescendo of psychological ruin, breathtaking romance, and mythological scale is the triumph of Hafsah Faizal’s We Free the Stars. As the thrilling finale to the Sands of Arawiya duology, following the critically acclaimed We Hunt the Flame, Faizal delivers a devastatingly lush sophomore novel that tests the very limits of its characters' humanity. 

The narrative plunges the reader immediately into the aftermath of the battle on Sharr. The suffocating, mystical mist known as the Arz has fallen, but the reprieve is short-lived. Zafira, the legendary Hunter, and Nasir, the tortured Prince of Death, find themselves marching toward Sultan’s Keep. Their mission is as desperate as it is monumental: restore the hearts of the Sisters of Old to the minarets of Arawiya and awaken the magic long starved from the kingdom. With Altair captive and the tyrannical shadow of the Lion of the Night looming large, Faizal constructs a relentless atmosphere of tension from the very first page. 

Yet, the true brilliance of We Free the Stars lies not in its sprawling political maneuvers or its high-stakes quest, but in its profound exploration of inner darkness. Faizal pivots her lens inward, examining the psychological toll that survival exacts. Zafira is steadily unraveling, tethered to a sentient artifact—the Jawarat—that whispers temptations of chaos, pushing her to the precipice of madness. Nasir, meanwhile, is at war with his own biology, grappling with the volatile magic bleeding from his bones while confronting the nightmarish reality of his possessed father. 

This thematic focus on internal corruption versus redemption elevates the novel above standard YA fantasy fare. Faizal utilizes her protagonists' internal struggles as a brilliant mirror for the fractured state of Arawiya itself. Can a land be healed when its saviors are splintering from within? The author answers this with a poetic, unflinching prose style that remains her strongest signature. 

Faizal’s writing is sensory and intoxicating. Her prose reads like desert poetry, effortlessly weaving together the stark, sun-scorched landscapes with rich cultural texture inspired by Arabian mythology. She does not merely describe a setting; she evokes it, immersing the reader in the scent of spices, the chill of twilight, and the oppressive heat of the dunes. Every sentence is weighed with intention, striking a delicate balance between lyrical beauty and brutal, cinematic action. 

At the beating heart of this sprawling epic is the romance between Zafira and Nasir. Enemies-to-lovers arcs are a staple of modern fantasy, but few achieve the emotional resonance found here. Their connection is forged in mutual trauma and unspoken sacrifices, evolving into a tender, fierce anchor amid the storm. Faizal handles their vulnerability with exquisite care. In a world demanding impossible stoicism, their quiet moments of shared affection feel revolutionary. 

If there is a critique to be leveled, it is that the sheer density of the plot occasionally threatens to eclipse the quieter character beats. With so many magical elements, looming threats, and political machinations to resolve, the pacing occasionally hyper-accelerates toward the climax. Yet, Faizal manages to pull the threads together with a devastating, beautiful finality that rewards the reader's emotional investment tenfold. 

We Free the Stars is a testament to the resilience of the human—and mythical—spirit. It is a story about finding light in the absolute grip of night, about the terrifying cost of power, and about love that defies the gods. Hafsah Faizal has cemented her place as a master of atmospheric fantasy, leaving behind a desert kingdom that readers will be loath to leave.
