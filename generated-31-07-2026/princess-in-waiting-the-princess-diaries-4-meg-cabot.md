# Princess in Waiting (The Princess Diaries, #4)

**Author:** Meg Cabot  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

In "Princess in Waiting," the fourth installment of Meg Cabot's beloved Princess Diaries series, Mia Thermopolis is back, and this time, she's more relatable than ever. As she navigates the complexities of royal life, algebra, and adolescence, Mia proves that being a princess isn't all it's cracked up to be.

Cabot's writing style is as engaging as ever, with a perfect blend of humor, wit, and teenage angst. Mia's inner monologue is laugh-out-loud funny, and her struggles to balance her royal duties with her desire for a normal life are endearing and authentic. The author's ability to capture the voice and emotions of a teenage girl is impressive, making Mia a lovable and relatable protagonist.

One of the main themes of the book is the struggle to find one's identity. Mia is torn between her royal responsibilities and her desire to be a normal teenager. She's constantly trying to prove herself, both to her grandmother and to herself. This internal conflict is something that many readers, especially teenagers, can identify with. Who hasn't felt like they don't quite fit in or that they're struggling to find their place in the world?

Cabot also explores the theme of self-acceptance. Mia is constantly criticized by her grandmother for not being "princess-like" enough, and she begins to doubt her own abilities. However, as the story progresses, Mia learns to accept herself for who she is, royal title and all. This message of self-acceptance is an important one, especially for young readers who may be struggling with their own self-esteem.

The supporting characters in the book are also well-developed and add to the story. Grandmère is as strict and demanding as ever, but her tough exterior hides a soft spot for Mia. Michael, Mia's love interest, is charming and supportive, and their relationship is sweet and realistic.

Overall, "Princess in Waiting" is a delightful and engaging read. Cabot's writing is engaging, and the story is full of humor, heart, and relatable themes. The book is a must-read for fans of the series and for anyone who's ever felt like they don't quite fit in.
