# Everybody Lies: Big Data, New Data, and What the Internet Can Tell Us About Who We Really Are

**Author:** Seth Stephens-Davidowitz  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

**Everybody Lies: A Fascinating Foray into the Power and Perils of Big Data**

In "Everybody Lies," Seth Stephens-Davidowitz, a former Google data scientist, takes us on an illuminating journey into the heart of big data, revealing its immense potential to unmask human behavior and societal truths. With a blend of compelling storytelling, rigorous analysis, and a dash of humor, Stephens-Davidowitz demonstrates how the digital age has bestowed upon us a powerful tool to understand ourselves and our world like never before.

The book's core premise is simple yet profound: people lie, but data doesn't. Stephens-Davidowitz argues that big data, harvested from our online activities, provides an unprecedented window into our true desires, fears, and biases. He masterfully illustrates this point with a myriad of examples, from predicting political elections to understanding racial prejudice, from analyzing suicide trends to exploring sexual preferences.

Stephens-Davidowitz introduces the concept of the "four powers of big data": scale, scope, speed, and structure. Each of these powers, he argues, enables us to uncover insights that were previously hidden or impossible to discern. He shows how these powers have revolutionized fields as diverse as medicine, business, and politics, leading to breakthroughs and innovations that were once unimaginable.

However, "Everybody Lies" is not just a celebration of big data's potential. Stephens-Davidowitz is also acutely aware of its pitfalls. He delves into the ethical dilemmas posed by the collection and use of personal data, warning of the dangers of surveillance, manipulation, and the creation of echo chambers. He argues that while big data can provide valuable insights, it is not a panacea, and its use must be tempered with caution and responsible governance.

Stephens-Davidowitz's style is engaging and accessible, making complex statistical concepts palatable to the lay reader. He draws on a wealth of real-world examples and anecdotes to illustrate his points, making the book a compelling page-turner rather than a dry academic treatise. His prose is clear and concise, his arguments logical and persuasive, making "Everybody Lies" a compelling read for anyone interested in the intersection of data, society, and human behavior.

In essence, "Everybody Lies" is a wake-up call to the power and potential of big data. It is a call to harness this power responsibly, to use it to understand and improve our world, but also to be aware of its limitations and dangers. It is a must-read for anyone interested in the digital age, the future of data, and the human condition.
