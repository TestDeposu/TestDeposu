# Among the Barons (Shadow Children, #4)

**Author:** Margaret Peterson Haddix  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

Among the Barons, the fourth installment in Margaret Peterson Haddix's Shadow Children series, is a gripping and intense tale that delves deeper into the complexities of a society that allows only two children per family. The story follows Luke Garner, now living under the alias Lee Grant, as he navigates the intricate web of relationships at Hendricks School. Luke's ability to blend in with his classmates, who are also "thirds" like him, is put to the test when the real Lee Grant's younger brother, Smits, arrives at the school.

What sets Haddix's writing apart is her masterful ability to weave together themes of identity, class, and rebellion, creating a rich tapestry that is both thought-provoking and engaging. The author's use of the Baron class as the epitome of wealth and power serves as a scathing commentary on the dangers of elitism and the exploitation of the lower classes. The Grants, with their opulent lifestyle and ruthless tactics, embody the corrupting influence of power, and Luke's struggles to maintain his true identity amidst this backdrop of luxury and deception are both captivating and heart-wrenching.

One of the most striking aspects of Haddix's writing is her ability to craft relatable and well-rounded characters. Luke, in particular, is a compelling protagonist, whose growth and development throughout the series have been remarkable. His struggles to reconcile his true identity with his assumed persona are deeply human, and his relationships with his friends and allies are authentic and heartfelt.

The plot of Among the Barons is a masterclass in tension and suspense, with Haddix expertly ratcheting up the stakes as Luke becomes embroiled in Smits's quest for the truth about his brother's death. The pacing is relentless, with each chapter ending on a cliffhanger that propels the reader forward. The author's use of plot twists and surprises is both clever and unexpected, keeping the reader on the edge of their seat as the story unfolds.

The themes of rebellion and resistance are woven throughout the narrative, as Luke and his friends begin to question the authority of the government and the morality of the society they live in. Haddix's portrayal of a world where children are forced to live in hiding, where families are torn apart by the harsh laws of the land, is a powerful commentary on the dangers of totalitarianism and the importance of individual freedom.

In conclusion, Among the Barons is a gripping and thought-provoking addition to the Shadow Children series. Haddix's masterful storytelling, coupled with her well-crafted characters and intricate plot, make for a compelling read that will keep you on the edge of your seat. With its themes of identity, class, and rebellion, this book is a must-read for fans of dystopian fiction and anyone looking for a story that will challenge their perspectives and leave them breathless.
