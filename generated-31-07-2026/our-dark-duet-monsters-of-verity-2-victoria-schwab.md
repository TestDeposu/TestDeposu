# Our Dark Duet (Monsters of Verity, #2)

**Author:** Victoria Schwab  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Victoria Schwab’s *Our Dark Duet* is the explosive, morally fractured sequel to *This Savage Song*—a dark fantasy that doesn’t just deepen its world but shatters the very idea of heroism, loyalty, and what it means to be human (or monster). Six months after Kate Harker and August Flynn were forced into an uneasy alliance, the war between Verity’s monsters and humans has escalated into a brutal, no-holds-barred conflict. But this isn’t a story about sides; it’s about the cost of survival, the rot beneath idealism, and the ways love and vengeance twist into the same thing.

Schwab’s prose is as lush and razor-sharp as ever, weaving a narrative that feels both intimate and epic. The tension between Kate and August, once a reluctant partnership, now simmers with a desperation that’s equal parts tender and terrifying. Kate, the hunter who thrives in the dark, has become a force of her own making—cruel, calculating, and utterly unapologetic. August, the monster who once dreamed of humanity, is now a leader bound by duty and blood, his humanity a fragile illusion in the face of war. Their dynamic is electric, a dance of trust and betrayal where every step could be the last. Schwab doesn’t shy away from the ugliness of their choices; she exposes the cracks in their morality with surgical precision, forcing readers to ask: *Who is the real monster here?*

The novel’s central conflict isn’t just about the war—it’s about the emergence of a new, insidious threat: a creature that preys on the darkest corners of the human (and monstrous) soul. This isn’t a physical battle; it’s a psychological one, a descent into the abyss where Kate must confront not just the monsters she’s hunted, but the ones she’s become. Schwab masterfully blurs the line between villain and victim, making the reader question who is truly in control of their own fate. The result is a story that’s as haunting as it is thrilling, where redemption feels like a luxury and survival is the only certainty.

What makes *Our Dark Duet* truly unforgettable is its refusal to offer easy answers. Schwab doesn’t romanticize monsters or humans; she shows the gray in between, where loyalty is a weapon and love is a liability. The world of Verity is rich with political intrigue, supernatural horror, and the kind of emotional gut-punches that linger long after the last page. Fans of Holly Black’s morally ambiguous worlds or Laini Taylor’s lyrical darkness will find much to love here, but Schwab’s signature voice—equal parts poetic and brutal—makes this stand apart.

If *This Savage Song* was a spark, *Our Dark Duet* is the inferno. It’s a conclusion that doesn’t just wrap up a story but burns it into your bones, leaving you to wonder: *What would you do to survive?* And more importantly, *what would you become?* Schwab doesn’t just write fantasy; she crafts a mirror, reflecting back the worst and best of us—and the monsters we all carry inside.
