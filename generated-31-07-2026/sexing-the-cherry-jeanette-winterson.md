# Sexing the Cherry

**Author:** Jeanette Winterson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

In the realm of literary fiction, few authors have managed to master the art of blending the fantastical with the philosophical as effortlessly as Jeanette Winterson in her captivating novel, Sexing the Cherry. Winner of the E.M. Forster Award, this book is a testament to Winterson's unique narrative voice and her ability to craft a tale that is both a thrilling adventure and a profound exploration of the human condition.

On the surface, Sexing the Cherry appears to be a story about Jordan, a young man found floating in the Thames and rescued by the enigmatic Dog Woman. As Jordan grows up, he embarks on a series of journeys that take him to the farthest reaches of the globe, echoing the travels of Jonathan Swift's Gulliver. However, Winterson's narrative is not content to simply follow Jordan's physical journeys. Instead, it delves deep into the labyrinthine corridors of his mind, where the boundaries between reality and fantasy are constantly blurred.

Through Jordan's story, Winterson explores a multitude of themes, from the nature of time and space to the human obsession with journeying and exploration. Her prose is a marvel of invention, weaving together elements of myth, history, and philosophy to create a rich tapestry of ideas and images. The result is a novel that is both a dizzying whirlwind of epiphanies and a profound meditation on the human condition.

One of the most striking aspects of Sexing the Cherry is its use of language. Winterson's prose is a masterclass in style and elegance, with a lyricism that is reminiscent of Italo Calvino and a philosophical depth that echoes the works of Milan Kundera. Her writing is both precise and evocative, conjuring up vivid images and ideas with a few, well-chosen words.

Throughout the novel, Winterson's narrative voice is both playful and profound, moving effortlessly between humor and pathos. Her characters are equally well-crafted, with Jordan and Dog Woman emerging as fully realized and complex individuals. The supporting cast is equally well-drawn, with a range of characters that add depth and richness to the narrative.

If there is a central theme to Sexing the Cherry, it is the idea that journeying is not just about physical movement, but also about the inner journeys that we undertake as individuals. Jordan's travels are a metaphor for the journey of self-discovery that we all must undertake, and Winterson's novel is a powerful exploration of the ways in which we navigate the complexities of our own minds.

In short, Sexing the Cherry is a masterpiece of literary fiction, a novel that is both a thrilling adventure and a profound exploration of the human condition. With its unique blend of fantasy and philosophy, its elegant prose, and its complex characters, it is a must-read for anyone who cares about fiction that is both idiosyncratic and beautiful.
