# Moon Over Soho (Rivers of London, #2)

**Author:** Ben Aaronovitch  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

In the world of urban fantasy, few authors can seamlessly blend wit, mystery, and magic like Ben Aaronovitch. His Rivers of London series, now in its second installment with Moon Over Soho, is a masterclass in crafting a captivating narrative that will leave readers enthralled and wanting more.

At the heart of this tale lies Peter Grant, a Police Constable with a special gift – he's the first apprentice wizard in fifty years. His journey is not just about solving crimes, but also about navigating the complex world of magic that exists beneath the surface of modern-day London. And in Moon Over Soho, Aaronovitch takes us on a thrilling ride through the city's vibrant jazz scene, where music is not just a form of entertainment, but a source of power that can be both creative and destructive.

The story begins with a mysterious case: a corpse that's playing a tune on a wax cylinder recording, a haunting echo of the jazz classic 'Body and Soul'. As Grant delves deeper into the world of Soho's jazz scene, he discovers that there's something sinister at play. Monsters are stalking the streets, feeding on the beauty and music that makes this city's culture so rich. These creatures leave behind a trail of sickness, failure, and broken lives, and it's up to Grant to unravel the mystery behind their presence.

Aaronovitch's writing shines with a loving attention to detail, transporting readers to the streets of 1950s London, where the sounds of jazz and the pulse of the city create an electric atmosphere. His use of humor, wit, and clever wordplay is reminiscent of the great detective fiction of Sir Arthur Conan Doyle. But what sets Moon Over Soho apart is its unique blend of fantasy and horror, which adds a chilling layer of realism to the narrative.

Throughout the book, Grant's character is skillfully developed, as he navigates the treacherous waters of police politics, magic, and personal relationships. His banter with his colleagues and mentors is witty and insightful, making him a relatable and endearing protagonist. Meanwhile, the world-building is impressive, with Aaronovitch seamlessly integrating various mythologies, histories, and cultural references into the narrative.

One of the standout features of Moon Over Soho is its exploration of the power of music. The author skillfully weaves together the threads of jazz, magic, and the urban myth of the 'Moonstruck', creating a compelling narrative that will resonate with music lovers and fantasy fans alike.

In conclusion, Moon Over Soho is a gripping continuation of the Rivers of London series, solidifying its position as one of the best contemporary urban fantasy series on the shelf. Ben Aaronovitch's writing is top-notch, with a keen eye for detail, clever plot twists, and a dash of humor that will keep you entertained throughout.

If you're looking for a story that combines police procedural, fantasy, and horror with a healthy dose of wit and realism, look no further than Moon Over Soho. This book is a must-read for anyone who enjoys urban fantasy with a strong edge of realism.
