# The Broken Kingdoms (Inheritance, #2)

**Author:** N.K. Jemisin  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

In N.K. Jemisin's 'The Broken Kingdoms', the world of the divine and mortal is brought to a boiling point as the intricate web of myth and magic unravels with unrelenting ferocity. This captivating sequel to 'The Hundred Thousand Kingdoms' presents an unforgettable narrative, a tale that explores the themes of power, identity, and the complexities of humanity against the dark backdrop of a city consumed by violence and despair.

The story revolves around Oree Shoth, a young, blind street artist struggling to find her place in the chaotic metropolis of Shadow. Amidst the city's underbelly, Oree forms an unlikely bond with a mysterious figure, a man shrouded in mystery, with no recollection of his past. As the narrative evolves, Oree and this enigmatic stranger are drawn into a sinister plot that threatens the very fabric of their world. The gruesome murders of godlings, those once-celestial beings now inhabiting the city, propel Oree and her guest into a perilous dance with forces beyond their understanding.

One of the most notable aspects of 'The Broken Kingdoms' is its unwavering exploration of the relationship between god and mortal, highlighting the consequences of such relationships when both parties engage in a delicate balance of power. N.K. Jemisin masterfully weaves together the intricate mythology of the Inheritance trilogy, crafting a rich narrative that not only deepens our understanding of the world but also raises profound questions about the nature of power and its corrupting influence.

Through Oree, Jemisin skillfully probes the complexities of identity, highlighting the tension between self-expression and conformity, as Oree grapples with her own sense of self in a world that constantly seeks to define her. The author's portrayal of a blind protagonist adds an additional layer of depth to her exploration, as Oree navigates this challenging reality within the confines of a society that often reduces identity to physical attributes.

N.K. Jemisin's writing is a true marvel, effortlessly blurring the lines between myth and reality, crafting a narrative that both enchants and terrifies in equal measure. Her mastery of the written language is evident in every sentence, every word infused with an unmistakable emotional resonance that draws the reader in, refusing to release them from the clutches of the story until its haunting conclusion. With 'The Broken Kingdoms', Jemisin showcases her exceptional storytelling abilities, crafting a world and characters that linger long after the final page has been turned.

In conclusion, 'The Broken Kingdoms' is an unmissable read for fans of fantasy, mythology, and compelling storytelling. N.K. Jemisin's bold narrative is an unrelenting exploration of power, identity, and the intricacies of human nature. If you are ready to immerse yourself in a world of wonder, magic, and unadulterated terror, then look no further than 'The Broken Kingdoms'. But be warned: once you enter this captivating world, there's no turning back.
