# Night of the Soul Stealer (The Last Apprentice / Wardstone Chronicles, #3)

**Author:** Joseph Delaney  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

Winter in the County is never kind, but Joseph Delaney’s *Night of the Soul Stealer*, the third installment in *The Last Apprentice* series (published in 2011), plunges young Thomas Ward and his master into a season of bone-chilling dread that easily surpasses its predecessors. Known for crafting folklore-infused nightmares that linger long after the final page, Delaney returns with a masterclass in atmospheric dark fantasy, trading the shadowy corners of Chipenden for the bleak, unforgiving expanse of Anglezarke moor. 

At its core, this novel delves deep into the heavy burden of secrets, trust, and the terrifying weight of legacy. Thomas is no longer just a naive farm boy learning the ropes of dealing local spooks, witches, and boggarts; he is an apprentice being forced to confront the dark history of the very man sworn to protect him. The central theme explores the friction between mentorship and isolation. When an unexpected stranger arrives at the Spook’s doorstep, it shatters the fragile sanctuary of their routine and forces a hasty retreat to a winter house rumored to harbor unspeakable horrors. Delaney uses this physical relocation to mirror Tom’s internal state: isolated, vulnerable, and surrounded by mounting pressures he is ill-equipped to handle. 

Delaney’s writing style is the beating heart of the series, characterized by a stark, conversational, and relentlessly urgent first-person narrative. He writes with the cadence of an old storyteller sitting by a dying hearth, warning listeners of the dark things that wait just beyond the reach of the firelight. There is no unnecessary literary fluff; every sentence is weighed down by a sense of impending doom. The author excels at sensory world-building—the biting chill of the wind, the heavy stench of ancient evil, the oppressive silence of the moors. This stylistic restraint makes the sudden, violent bursts of supernatural terror all the more impactful. 

What makes *Night of the Soul Stealer* particularly striking is Delaney’s willingness to push his youthful protagonist into moral and physical grey areas. The looming threat of the Golgoth—an ancient, malevolent beast of myth—serves as a fantastic external antagonist, but the true monsters in Delaney’s universe are often the concealed truths of the past. As the Spook’s hidden secrets threaten to come to light, Tom must question everything he knows about his master, and by extension, his own future. 

For readers who relish gothic chills, folklore-rich mythologies, and coming-of-age tales with genuine stakes, Delaney delivers a chilling triumph. It is a harsh winter tale that tests the resilience of the human spirit against the encroaching dark, proving once again why this series remains a benchmark in dark fantasy.
