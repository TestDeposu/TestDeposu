# B is for Burglar (Kinsey Millhone, #2)

**Author:** Sue Grafton  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

In "B is for Burglar", Sue Grafton masterfully weaves a complex web of mystery, deception, and suspense, plunging private investigator Kinsey Millhone into a world where nothing is as it seems. On the surface, the case appears routine: Beverly Danziger hires Millhone to track down her missing sister, Elaine Boldt, a wealthy widow with a penchant for luxury. However, as Millhone delves deeper into the case, she uncovers a tangled mess of family secrets, lies, and murder.

Grafton's writing style is a perfect blend of wit, charm, and intrigue, making it impossible to put the book down. Her protagonist, Kinsey Millhone, is a lovable and feisty character, whose sharp instincts and quick wit make her the perfect detective for this complex case. The author's use of vivid descriptions and clever plot twists keeps the reader guessing until the very end, making it a true page-turner.

One of the main themes of the book is the illusion of reality. Grafton skillfully explores the idea that nothing is as it seems, and that people often hide behind masks of deception. This theme is perfectly captured through the character of Elaine Boldt, whose flashy exterior hides a complex and troubled personality. As Millhone digs deeper into the case, she discovers that everyone involved is hiding something, and that the truth is far more sinister than she initially thought.

The author's use of language is also noteworthy. Grafton's writing is concise, yet evocative, and her use of metaphors and similes adds a layer of depth to the narrative. Her descriptions of the characters and settings are vivid and engaging, making it easy to visualize the world she has created.

As the story unfolds, Millhone finds herself in a nightmarish hall of mirrors, where reality is distorted by illusion. The case takes a darker turn, with murder, arson, and burglary all playing a part in the complex web of deceit. Grafton's handling of the plot is masterful, and the reader is kept on the edge of their seat as the mystery unfolds.

In conclusion, "B is for Burglar" is a gripping and suspenseful mystery that will keep you guessing until the very end. Sue Grafton's writing style is engaging and witty, and her protagonist, Kinsey Millhone, is a lovable and feisty character. The book's exploration of the illusion of reality is thought-provoking and timely, and the author's use of language is concise and evocative. If you're a fan of mystery and suspense, then this book is a must-read.
