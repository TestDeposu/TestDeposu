# Queen of Sorcery (The Belgariad #2)

**Author:** David Eddings  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

"Queen of Sorcery" by David Eddings is the second installment in the epic fantasy series, The Belgariad. Published in 1982, not 2016, this novel has captivated readers for decades with its rich world-building, intricate plot, and memorable characters. As the story unfolds, we find ourselves immersed in a realm of magic, politics, and adventure, where the stakes are high and the fate of the world hangs in the balance.

At the heart of the narrative is Garion, a young man with untapped potential, who embarks on a perilous journey to thwart the plans of those who seek to deliver the Orb to the dark God Torak. Alongside his companions, including the enigmatic Polgara and her father Belgarath, Garion navigates treacherous landscapes, battles formidable enemies, and grapples with the complexities of his own destiny. As he grows into his powers and confronts the challenges of adulthood, Garion's transformation is both compelling and authentic.

One of the standout features of Eddings' writing style is his ability to craft a narrative that is both epic in scope and intimate in character development. The world of The Belgariad is meticulously crafted, with a deep history and politics that underpin the story. Yet, despite the grandeur of the setting, the characters remain relatable and human, with all their flaws and strengths on full display. Eddings' prose is engaging, witty, and effortlessly readable, making it easy to become fully invested in the world and its inhabitants.

The themes of power, identity, and morality are woven throughout the narrative, adding depth and complexity to the story. Garion's struggles to come to terms with his own abilities and his place in the world serve as a powerful metaphor for the challenges of growing up and finding one's purpose. Meanwhile, the character of Ce'Nedra, the princess, adds a fascinating dynamic to the story, her own agency and motivations serving as a counterpoint to Garion's journey.

The pacing of the novel is well-balanced, with a mix of action, suspense, and quieter moments of character development. Eddings' use of dialogue is particularly noteworthy, with conversations that feel natural and authentic, revealing character traits and relationships with ease.

Overall, "Queen of Sorcery" is a captivating and immersive read, with a richly detailed world, compelling characters, and a narrative that is both engaging and thought-provoking. As the second book in The Belgariad series, it builds upon the foundations laid in the first installment, expanding the world and deepening our understanding of the characters. If you're a fan of epic fantasy, with a focus on character development and world-building, then this series is definitely worth exploring.
