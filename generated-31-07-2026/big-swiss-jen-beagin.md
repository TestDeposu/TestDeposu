# Big Swiss

**Author:** Jen Beagin  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

Jen Beagin’s 2023 national bestseller and cultural phenomenon, Big Swiss, arrives on the literary scene like a jolt of raw electricity: weird, hilarious, and unapologetically unhinged. Set in the eccentric, drafty environs of Hudson, New York, the novel introduces us to Greta, a woman whose life is as structurally compromised as the ancient, bee-infested Dutch farmhouse she shares with her friend Sabine. Greta’s professional existence is defined by listening to the most private, unfiltered desires of others. She works as a transcriptionist for a local sex therapist who operates under the moniker "Om." 

It is through these audio files that the central obsession of the novel takes root. Greta becomes utterly captivated by a new client—a repressed, married woman she dubs "Big Swiss." When Greta serendipitously crosses paths with her in town, the two women are violently and magnetically drawn to one another. What follows is a clandestine affair built on a foundation of deception; Big Swiss has no idea that Greta already knows the darkest corners of her psyche via hundreds of hours of recorded therapy. Greta’s overwhelming compulsion to sustain the relationship completely overrides her ethical qualms, morphing a potentially squalid premise into something remarkably tender and profound.

At its core, Big Swiss is an audacious exploration of modern intimacy, infidelity, and the lengths to which we go to feel truly seen. Beagin dissects the complexities of human connection by holding up a funhouse mirror to contemporary mental health culture. The novel asks a provocative question: can you truly know someone if you start by eavesdropping on their soul? The thematic weight of the book hinges on the paradox of modern loneliness. Greta is surrounded by countercultural quirks—bees, bizarre local characters, and unconventional living arrangements—yet her emotional isolation is palpable until she anchors herself to the booming voice of Big Swiss. The affair becomes a vehicle for self-actualization, proving that sometimes we must dismantle our boundaries—and our ethics—to finally become ourselves.

What truly elevates the novel, however, is Beagin’s singular, razor-sharp authorial style. Her prose is a masterclass in deadpan delivery and comedic timing, earning its reputation as one of the funniest books in recent memory. Beagin writes with a fearless, transgressive wit that never feels gratuitous; instead, it cuts straight to the bone of human absurdity. She refuses to romanticize her characters, presenting Greta, Om, Big Swiss, and the eccentric supporting cast with all their warts, neuroses, and contradictions fully exposed. There is a cinematic quality to Beagin’s pacing and dialogue, rendering the bizarre utterly believable and the mundane deeply strange. 

Big Swiss is not just a quirky romance or a satire of alternative therapies; it is a vital, fast-paced evaluation of sexual stereotypes and the messy architecture of desire. Beagin has crafted a delightfully chaotic narrative that lingers in the mind long after the final page. It is bizarre, brilliant, and utterly unforgettable—a triumph of contemporary fiction that deserves every ounce of its cult status.
