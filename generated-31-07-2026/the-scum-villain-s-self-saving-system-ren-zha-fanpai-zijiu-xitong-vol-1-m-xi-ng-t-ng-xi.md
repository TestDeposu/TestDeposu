# The Scum Villain's Self-Saving System: Ren Zha Fanpai Zijiu Xitong, Vol. 1

**Author:** Mò Xiāng Tóng Xiù  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

In the world of fantasy novels, it's not often that we come across a story that cleverly subverts the traditional tropes of the genre, but Mò Xiāng Tóng Xiù's "The Scum Villain's Self-Saving System" does just that. This Chinese webnovel turned book is a thought-provoking exploration of the nature of storytelling, morality, and the consequences of one's actions.

The story follows Shen Yuan, a young man who becomes so enraged while reading a trashy webnovel that he dies from a burst of anger. However, instead of moving on to the afterlife, he finds himself reborn into the world of the very novel that led to his demise. But here's the twist - he's not the protagonist, Luo Binghe, the half-demon with unparalleled strength and beauty. Instead, Shen Yuan is reincarnated as Shen Qingqiu, the beautiful but cruel teacher of the young Luo Binghe, who is destined to suffer a horrific punishment for his crimes against the protagonist.

This setup allows the author to cleverly comment on the nature of storytelling and the way we perceive characters. Shen Qingqiu, as the original character, is a one-dimensional villain with no redeeming qualities. However, through Shen Yuan's reincarnation, we see the world through his eyes, and he becomes a complex and multifaceted character. This shift in perspective raises questions about morality and whether people can change.

The author's writing style is witty and engaging, with a keen sense of humor. The world-building is meticulous, with a deep understanding of Chinese mythology and culture. The characters are well-developed, with distinct personalities and motivations. The pacing is well-balanced, with a mix of action, drama, and humor that keeps the reader engaged.

One of the most striking aspects of the book is its exploration of the concept of "scum villains." These are characters who are inherently evil, with no redeeming qualities, and are often used as a plot device to further the protagonist's journey. However, through Shen Qingqiu's character, the author challenges this notion, asking whether people can change and whether they deserve a chance at redemption.

In conclusion, "The Scum Villain's Self-Saving System" is a thought-provoking and engaging read that challenges traditional fantasy tropes. With its witty writing style, complex characters, and meticulous world-building, it's a must-read for fans of the genre. If you're looking for a story that will make you laugh, cry, and question your assumptions about morality, then this book is for you.
