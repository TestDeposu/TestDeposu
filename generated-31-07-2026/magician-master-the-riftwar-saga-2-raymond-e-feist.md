# Magician: Master (The Riftwar Saga, #2)

**Author:** Raymond E. Feist  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1993

Magician: Master picks up where its predecessor left off, thrusting Pug—now the Tsurani‑named Milamber—into the heart of a war that spans worlds. Raymond E. Feist expands the canvas of Midkemia and Kelewan with a deft hand, intertwining personal discovery with the looming threat of an ancient Enemy that predates time itself. The novel’s central theme is the tension between destiny and agency: Milamber’s extraordinary magical gifts are both a burden and a beacon, forcing him to decide whether he will be a pawn in a cosmic chess game or the architect of his own fate. This internal struggle mirrors the external clash between the Tsurani Empire’s rigid, hierarchical magic and the more free‑wielding, instinctual sorcery of Midkemia, highlighting how cultural perspectives shape the understanding and use of power.

Feist’s prose remains unmistakably accessible, a hallmark of his style that makes epic fantasy feel approachable without sacrificing depth. He favors clear, forward‑driving sentences that keep the narrative momentum brisk, allowing readers to stay immersed in battle scenes, court intrigue, and quiet moments of magical apprenticeship alike. While some critics might note a occasional reliance on expository dialogue to explain the intricacies of Tsurani society or the mechanics of the Rift, these passages serve a purpose: they ground the fantastical elements in a coherent world‑building framework that rewards attentive readers. The author’s talent for juxtaposing grand, world‑shaking events with intimate character beats shines here—Milamber’s mentorship under the enigmatic macan‑master, his uneasy friendship with the Tsurani warrior Laurie, and his lingering affection for Midkemian allies all humanize the sprawling conflict.

What truly elevates Magician: Master is Feist’s willingness to let magic feel earned. Rather than presenting Milamber’s abilities as a deus ex machina, the novel shows the painstaking discipline, study, sacrifice, and moral questioning required to harness such force. This approach reinforces the book’s message that true mastery comes not from raw power alone, but from wisdom, humility, and the courage to confront one’s own limitations. The pacing, though occasionally uneven in the middle sections where political maneuvering slows the action, ultimately accelerates toward a climax that feels both inevitable and surprising—a hallmark of Feist’s knack for delivering satisfying payoff after patient buildup.

In sum, Magician: Master is a solid, engaging continuation of the Riftwar Saga that balances sweeping epic scope with personal growth. Feist’s straightforward yet evocative style invites both newcomers and veteran fantasy fans to lose themselves in a tale where the stakes are nothing less than the survival of two worlds, and where the greatest battle often rages within the magician’s own heart.
