# 100 Days of Sunlight (100 Days of Sunlight, #1)

**Author:** Abbie Emmons  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

"100 Days of Sunlight: A Heartfelt Exploration of Resilience"

Abbie Emmons' debut novel, 100 Days of Sunlight, is a poignant and uplifting tale that delves into the complexities of human experience, vulnerability, and the transformative power of relationships. On the surface, this young adult romance follows Tessa Dickinson, a bright and driven 16-year-old whose world is turned upside down after a devastating car accident leaves her without sight for an uncertain period. As she navigates the darkness, both literal and emotional, she finds solace in an unlikely ally, Weston Ludovico, a charismatic and optimistic teenager with a secret of his own.

What sets 100 Days of Sunlight apart from other contemporary romances is its nuanced exploration of disability, identity, and the social constructs that often silence marginalized voices. Emmons skillfully tackles these complex themes without ever feeling didactic or heavy-handed, instead weaving them seamlessly into the narrative's tapestry. Through Weston's character, the author sheds light on the ways in which societal expectations and ableism can both enable and disable individuals, perpetuating a culture of silence and shame.

One of the book's greatest strengths is its masterful pacing, which perfectly captures the ebb and flow of Tessa's emotions as she grapples with her newfound blindness. The narrative is filled with moments of breathtaking beauty, as Emmons deftly conveys the sensory experience of navigating a world without sight. Her prose is descriptive and evocative, imbuing the reader with a deep sense of empathy for Tessa's plight.

At its core, 100 Days of Sunlight is a love story, but one that transcends conventional notions of romance. The relationship between Tessa and Weston is built on a foundation of mutual respect, trust, and acceptance, creating a sense of safety and understanding that allows both characters to confront their deepest fears and desires. Emmons handles the tender moments with sensitivity and care, crafting a narrative that feels both authentic and hopeful.

Throughout the book, Emmons also explores the complexities of family relationships, highlighting the ways in which love, support, and forgiveness can be both a source of strength and a challenge. Tessa's bond with her grandparents is particularly poignant, serving as a reminder that family is a choice, not just a biological tie.

Emmons' style is characterized by a lyrical prose that is both poetic and accessible, making 100 Days of Sunlight a joy to read. Her character development is nuanced and multidimensional, giving voice to Tessa's inner struggles and Weston's inner strength. The author's use of metaphor and symbolism adds depth and richness to the narrative, inviting readers to ponder the themes and motifs long after finishing the book.

Ultimately, 100 Days of Sunlight is a book about the human spirit's capacity for resilience, adaptability, and growth. It is a testament to the transformative power of relationships, love, and acceptance, demonstrating that even in the darkest moments, there is always hope for a brighter future.
