# The Marriage Trap (Marriage to a Billionaire, #2)

**Author:** Jennifer Probst  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

Jennifer Probst returns to the height of contemporary romance with The Marriage Trap, the sizzling second installment in her bestselling Marriage to a Billionaire series. Published in 2012, this novel builds upon the formula that made her a breakout voice in the genre, trading the stoic ranch setting of its predecessor for the opulent, high-stakes backdrop of Milan, Italy. At its core, the novel explores a timeless romance trope—the marriage of convenience—while infusing it with sharp modern wit, intense family dynamics, and a profound examination of personal freedom versus familial obligation. 

The narrative engine is driven by Michael Conte, a classic Probst billionaire who is intensely protective, fiercely loyal, and bound by the heavy chains of traditional Italian heritage. To rescue his sister from an unwanted, culturally mandated wait for marriage, Michael needs a fiancée immediately. Enter Maggie Ryan, a fierce, independent photographer who is the exact opposite of the submissive traditional bride Michael’s family expects. Maggie agrees to the charade as a calculated maneuver to stop Michael from pursuing her best friend, sparking a dangerous game of pretend. 

Probst’s writing style shines brightest in the friction between her protagonists. The author is a master of sexual tension and verbal sparring, crafting a slow-burn battle of wills that never feels rushed. The dialogue is snappy, loaded with subtext, and crackles with an electric energy that keeps the pages turning. Instead of relying solely on physical attraction, Probst anchors the romance in emotional vulnerability. Both Michael and Maggie are guarding deeply rooted insecurities and past wounds, making their eventual surrender to desire feel earned rather than merely convenient. 

Against the romanticized, atmospheric backdrop of Italy, the fake engagement inevitably blurs the line between pretense and reality. Probst handles this transition with a practiced hand, balancing steamy encounters with genuine emotional payoffs. While the novel embraces the indulgent, escapist fantasies typical of billionaire romance, it avoids becoming entirely formulaic thanks to Maggie’s unyielding spine and Michael’s internal struggle to evolve past patriarchal expectations. 

Ultimately, The Marriage Trap delivers exactly what contemporary romance enthusiasts crave: a high-friction romance with high stakes, undeniable chemistry, and characters who must risk their hearts to find true freedom. Probst cements her reputation as a writer who understands how to blend glamour, family drama, and heartfelt passion into an irresistibly addictive read.
