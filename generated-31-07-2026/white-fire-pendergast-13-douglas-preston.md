# White Fire (Pendergast, #13)

**Author:** Douglas Preston  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

When Douglas Preston and his co-author Lincoln Child unleash Special Agent Aloysius X. L. Pendergast upon the literary world, readers know they are in for a ride that defies conventional genre boundaries. White Fire, the thirteenth installment in the iconic Pendergast series, takes this signature blend of forensic science, historical mystery, and Gothic horror to dizzying new heights. Set against the claustrophobic, snowbound backdrop of a wealthy Colorado ski resort, Preston crafts a relentless narrative that proves why this eccentric FBI agent remains one of modern thriller fiction's most enduring creations.

At its thematic core, White Fire explores a compelling literary obsession: the inescapable gravity of the past. Preston constructs a brilliant thematic mirror, pitting a 19th-century frontier tragedy against the hyper-modern, decadent luxury of contemporary wealth. The narrative hinges on a dual mystery—a century-old massacre of miners allegedly by a rogue grizzly bear in the camp of Roaring Fork, and a modern string of calculated arsons targeting multimillion-dollar mansions. By weaving these seemingly disparate threads together through the discovery of a long-lost Sherlock Holmes manuscript, Preston examines how human greed, trauma, and vengeance echo across generations. The past is not merely prologue in Roaring Fork; it is an active, burning participant demanding a final, bloody reckoning.

The author's stylistic mastery shines through the intricate plotting and atmospheric tension. Preston employs a dual-timeline approach that seamlessly bridges the rugged, brutal environment of the Victorian-era American West with the chilling isolation of a modern, snow-locked elite community. His prose is fast-paced yet richly textured, prioritizing sensory details—the crackle of encroaching flames, the biting chill of the Colorado blizzard, and the stifling claustrophobia of wealth under siege. 

Furthermore, White Fire elevates fan-favorite Corrie Swanson, moving her from a supporting role into a vital co-protagonist. Watching Corrie transition from a rebellious, ambitious teenager into a sharp, resilient investigator adds emotional weight to the carnage. Pendergast himself remains as enigmatic and razor-sharp as ever, operating with an effortless, almost supernatural intellect that anchors the more flamboyant elements of the plot. Preston balances high-octane action sequences with cerebral puzzles, ensuring that the intellectual stimulation matches the adrenaline rush.

Ultimately, White Fire is a masterclass in atmospheric suspense. It delivers everything series loyalists crave while offering a standalone thrill that will captivate newcomers. Douglas Preston does not merely write a thriller; he constructs an elaborate, blazing puzzle box that demands to be read in a single, breathless sitting.
