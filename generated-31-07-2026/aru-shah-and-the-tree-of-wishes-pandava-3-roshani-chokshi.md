# Aru Shah and the Tree of Wishes (Pandava, #3)

**Author:** Roshani Chokshi  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

Aru Shah and the Tree of Wishes, the third installment in Roshani Chokshi’s critically acclaimed Pandava series, proves that middle-grade fantasy can be both profoundly heartfelt and wildly imaginative. In this 2020 release, Chokshi deepens the epic journey of her twelve-year-old protagonist, Aru, as she and her sisters navigate treacherous mythological realms to save their loved ones and restore cosmic order. More than just a high-stakes adventure featuring Hindu mythology, this novel stands out as a vibrant exploration of self-doubt, identity, and the intricate bonds of chosen family.

At the heart of the narrative lies the compelling theme of inadequacy versus true worth. Aru Shah is not your traditional, unflinching hero. She is insecure, prone to embellishing the truth to fit in, and haunted by the fear that she is the weakest link among the Pandavas. Chokshi uses the metaphorical framework of the Tree of Wishes to test her characters' deepest desires, forcing them to confront the dangerous illusion that perfection brings happiness. Through Aru’s internal struggles, the novel delivers a resonant message for young readers: flaws do not negate heroism, and vulnerability is a form of strength. The author handles these emotional beats with empathy, ensuring that the magic never overshadows the genuine human heart of the story.

Chokshi’s signature literary style is arguably the true magic of the book. Her prose is relentlessly witty, bursting with a distinct modern voice that effortlessly bridges ancient folklore with contemporary pop culture references. The pacing is breathless, plunging the reader from one perilous, vividly described realm into the next without ever losing narrative momentum. Where many fantasy authors bog down middle-grade readers with excessive exposition, Chokshi infuses her world-building with sensory richness—sparkling skies, terrifying mythical beasts, and the aromatic warmth of home—making the fantastic feel entirely tangible. Furthermore, her dialogue crackles with authentic sisterly banter, capturing the fierce, messy, and irreplaceable nature of sibling dynamics.

Ultimately, Aru Shah and the Tree of Wishes is a triumphant middle-grade adventure that expands its universe while staying deeply grounded in its characters. Chokshi has crafted a story that is as intellectually stimulating as it is emotionally rewarding, offering a masterclass in how to modernize mythology for a new generation. It is a dazzling, laugh-out-loud, and tear-jerking chapter in a saga that refuses to put a foot wrong.
