# American Royals (American Royals, #1)

**Author:** Katharine McGee  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

What if George Washington had not been our first president, but our first king? Katharine McGee’s 2019 bestseller, American Royals, takes this tantalizing alternate-history premise and spins it into an addictive, glossy confection that brilliantly marries the regal pageantry of The Crown with the soapy, high-stakes drama of Gossip Girl. 

At its core, McGee’s novel explores the crushing weight of duty versus the desperate pursuit of personal freedom. The central theme of the book investigates identity within a gilded cage. The House of Washington may rule a democratic superpower, but the family itself is bound by rigid, archaic traditions that leave little room for humanity. Through her multi-perspective narrative, McGee dissects how modern privilege intersects with inescapable public surveillance. Her characters are trapped by their own iconography, forced to perform perfection for a nation that demands both relatability and untouchable majesty from its monarchs. 

Stylistically, McGee adopts a fast-paced, multi-POV structure that acts as the ultimate literary page-turner. She splits her narrative among the glittering young elite of the palace, primarily focusing on the royal siblings: Princess Beatrice, the dutiful eldest daughter poised to become America's first queen regnant; her rebellious twin sister, Samantha; their charming brother, Jefferson; and the ordinary girls orbiting their impossibly bright world. 

McGee’s writing style is unapologetically commercial, highly visual, and saturated with sensory details of designer gowns, secret palace passageways, and flashing paparazzibulbs. She understands the rhythm of modern teen and young adult drama, dropping cliffhangers at the end of nearly every chapter to keep the momentum relentlessly moving forward. Yet, beneath the glossy sheen of luxury and tabloid fodder, McGee infuses her prose with genuine emotional stakes. The romances feel high-stakes and deliciously angsty—especially the forbidden dynamics and love triangles that define Samantha and Jefferson's storylines. 

While purists of deep historical fiction might wish for a more granular exploration of how an American monarchy would alter global geopolitics, McGee wisely keeps her lens tightly focused on the interpersonal. The alternate 21st-century America she builds feels remarkably tangible, blending modern technology and social media culture with royal protocols and coronets. It is a world where historical reverence collides with twenty-first-century rebellion.

Ultimately, American Royals succeeds because it knows exactly what it wants to be: a breathless, aspirational escape. McGee captures the intoxicating fantasy of royalty while subverting it just enough to keep contemporary readers hooked. It is smart, shamelessly entertaining, and impossible to put down. For anyone looking for a glittering literary indulgence, this first installment in the series reigns supreme.
