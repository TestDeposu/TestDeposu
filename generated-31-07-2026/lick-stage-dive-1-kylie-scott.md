# Lick (Stage Dive, #1)

**Author:** Kylie Scott  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

Rock stars, regret, and the ultimate Vegas hangover: Kylie Scott’s 2013 contemporary romance Lick hits all the right notes for fans of the rockstar trope, delivering a fast-paced, witty, and surprisingly grounded entry into the New Adult genre. The story kicks off with every control freak's worst nightmare: twenty-one-year-old Evelyn Thomas wakes up on a sticky Las Vegas hotel room floor, nursing a biblical hangover, only to discover she has accidentally married David Ferris—the charismatic, impossibly gorgeous lead singer of the world’s biggest rock band, Stage Dive. 

The core theme of Lick explores the collision of two vastly different worlds and the vulnerability hidden behind fame. Evelyn is an ordinary, practical girl who values stability, routine, and anonymity. David, on the other hand, lives a life of chaotic excess, constantly hounded by the paparazzi and the unrelenting demands of global stardom. Scott uses this classic accidental marriage setup not just for comedic effect, but as a lens to examine authenticity versus illusion. Trapped in a media storm and legally bound to a stranger, Evelyn is forced to look past the leather, tattoos, and screaming fans to see the lonely man underneath the rock-god persona. Conversely, David finds in Evelyn a rare sanctuary—someone who genuinely doesn't care about his money, status, or fame, and treats him like an ordinary human being. 

Scott’s authorial style is defined by its sharp, snappy dialogue and heavy reliance on authentic banter. She has a rare knack for writing modern heroines who possess genuine backbone, refusing to let herself or her readers be blinded purely by celebrity allure. Evelyn’s dry, self-deprecating humor serves as a brilliant anchor for the narrative, keeping the potential melodrama firmly grounded in reality. The chemistry between the protagonists sizzles off the page, fueled by a brilliant push-and-pull dynamic where David pursues Evelyn with a mix of playful arrogance and tender devotion that is hard to resist. 

While the plot leans into familiar romantic tropes, Scott elevates the material through her pacing and character depth. She avoids making David a one-dimensional bad boy, instead crafting a nuanced portrait of someone suffocating under the weight of his own success. The supporting cast, particularly the members of Stage Dive, adds a layer of found-family warmth and comedic relief that sets the stage for the rest of the series. 

Lick is a delightful, high-energy page-turner that proves love stories set in the glitz and glamour of the music industry still have plenty of heart left to give. It is funny, deeply romantic, and utterly addictive from the very first chapter.
