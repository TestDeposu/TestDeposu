# Bullet (Anita Blake, Vampire Hunter, #19)

**Author:** Laurell K. Hamilton  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

Laurell K. Hamilton's Anita Blake, Vampire Hunter series is renowned for its blend of fantasy, romance, and mystery. In the collection "Bullet", the 19th installment of the series, Hamilton weaves a complex narrative that delves into the intricate world of supernatural creatures and the mortal humans caught in their midst. The four connected novels within the collection showcase Hamilton's mastery of storytelling, as she skillfully explores themes of power, identity, and the blurred lines between good and evil.

The first novel, "Blood Noir", introduces a new layer of complexity to the series. Anita Blake, a preeminent necromancer, becomes embroiled in a scandal that threatens to upend the fragile balance of power among the vampire community. As she navigates the treacherous landscape of ancient vampire politics, Anita is forced to confront her own vulnerability and the consequences of her actions. Hamilton's writing is as compelling as ever, crafting a narrative that is both suspenseful and emotionally charged.

The second novel, "Skin Trade", marks a return to the series' horror-tinged roots. Set in the neon-lit streets of Las Vegas, Anita must team up with federal marshals to hunt down a supernatural serial killer. Hamilton's depiction of the city's dark underbelly is masterful, evoking the sense of unease and disorder that pervades the narrative. Through Anita's partnership with the marshals, Hamilton explores the intersection of human and supernatural justice, raising questions about the morality of violence and the true cost of saving lives.

The third novel, "Flirt", sees Anita facing a new challenge when she's asked to raise the dead. However, the results are far from predictable, and Anita is left to confront the possibility that her actions may have unforeseen consequences. Hamilton's handling of the themes of identity and power is particularly noteworthy, as Anita grapples with her own sense of self and the responsibilities that come with her unique abilities.

The final novel, "Bullet", marks a turning point in the series, as Anita is drawn into a world of ancient mysticism and forbidden knowledge. The Mother of All Darkness, the first vampire, sets her sights on Anita as a vessel for her dark powers. Hamilton's writing is as immersive as ever, conjuring a sense of creeping dread as Anita becomes increasingly entangled in the vampire's plans. The novel raises questions about the nature of identity and the boundaries between self and other, leaving the reader wondering what it truly means to be human.

Throughout the collection, Hamilton's style is both elegant and engaging, weaving together threads of mystery, romance, and suspense to create a narrative that is both gripping and emotionally resonant. Her characters are multidimensional and complex, driven by motivations that are both understandable and relatable. As the series hurtles towards its next instalment, Hamilton leaves her readers on the edge of their seats, eager to explore the next chapter in Anita's journey.

In conclusion, "Bullet" is a triumphant addition to the Anita Blake, Vampire Hunter series, showcasing Laurell K. Hamilton's masterful storytelling and deep understanding of the series' complex world. With its rich exploration of themes and intricate plot twists, this collection is a must-read for fans of the series and newcomers alike.
