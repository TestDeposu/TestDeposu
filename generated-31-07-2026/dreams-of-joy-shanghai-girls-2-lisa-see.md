# Dreams of Joy (Shanghai Girls, #2)

**Author:** Lisa See  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

Dreams of Joy, the second installment in Lisa See's Shanghai Girls series, is a mesmerizing and deeply moving novel that explores the complex and often fraught relationship between mothers and daughters. The story picks up where the first book left off, with Pearl's daughter Joy running away to Shanghai in search of her birth father, the charismatic artist Z.G. Li. As Joy becomes increasingly enamored with the Communist ideology of Red China, Pearl sets out on a perilous journey to rescue her daughter, confronting her own demons and making sacrifices along the way.

One of the most striking aspects of See's writing is her ability to craft characters that are multidimensional and relatable. Pearl, the strong-willed and determined mother, is a particularly well-drawn figure, and her love for Joy is palpable throughout the novel. The relationship between the two women is fraught with tension and misunderstanding, but ultimately, it is a deep and abiding bond that drives the plot forward. See's portrayal of the mother-daughter dynamic is both heart-wrenching and heartwarming, and it is clear that she has a deep understanding of the complexities of female relationships.

See's writing style is also noteworthy, as she weaves together historical fact and fiction with ease. Her depiction of 1950s Shanghai is vivid and immersive, and the reader is transported to a world of crowded streets, bustling markets, and remote villages. The novel is meticulously researched, and See's attention to detail is impressive. From the intricacies of Chinese culture to the harsh realities of life under Communist rule, See's writing is informed by a deep understanding of the time and place in which the story is set.

The novel's themes of love, sacrifice, and redemption are timeless and universal, and See explores them with sensitivity and nuance. The story is also a powerful exploration of the human condition, as the characters navigate the complexities of family, identity, and belonging. Through Joy's journey, See sheds light on the idealism and naivety of youth, as well as the dangers of blind ideology and the importance of critical thinking.

The historical context of the novel is also significant, as it provides a powerful backdrop for the story. The Communist takeover of China in 1949 had a profound impact on the country and its people, and See's portrayal of this period is both informative and engaging. The novel is a testament to the resilience and strength of the human spirit, as the characters face unimaginable challenges and hardships with courage and determination.

In short, Dreams of Joy is a stunning novel that will appeal to fans of historical fiction, family drama, and cultural exploration. See's writing is evocative and engaging, and the story is both emotionally resonant and intellectually stimulating. The novel is a must-read for anyone interested in the experiences of Chinese Americans, as well as those who enjoy stories about strong women and the complexities of family relationships.
