# The War I Finally Won (The War That Saved My Life, #2)

**Author:** Kimberly Brubaker Bradley  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

"The War I Finally Won" by Kimberly Brubaker Bradley is a masterpiece of historical fiction that delves into the complexities of war, identity, and the unbreakable bonds of family. This Newbery Honor Book and #1 New York Times Bestseller tells the story of Ada, a ten-year-old girl with a twisted foot, who has never left her one-room apartment due to her mother's shame. However, when her brother Jamie is evacuated from London during World War II, Ada seizes the opportunity to join him, embarking on a journey that will change her life forever.

Bradley's writing is achingly lovely, nuanced, and emotionally acute, as she weaves a tale that is both an adventure and a poignant exploration of the human spirit. Through Ada's eyes, we experience the harsh realities of war, the kindness of strangers, and the resilience of the human heart. As Ada learns to ride a pony, reads for the first time, and navigates the complexities of relationships, we witness her transformation from a timid, isolated girl to a confident, determined individual.

The author's style is remarkable for its ability to balance the darkness of war with the lightness of hope and redemption. Bradley's prose is economical, yet evocative, conjuring vivid images of the English countryside, the terror of air raids, and the warmth of newfound friendships. Her characters are multidimensional and relatable, from Ada's prickly yet vulnerable exterior to Susan's quiet strength and kindness.

One of the most striking aspects of the book is its exploration of identity and belonging. Ada's physical disability and her mother's rejection have left her feeling like an outcast, yet as she finds acceptance and love with Susan and Jamie, she begins to discover her own sense of self-worth. This theme is echoed in the experiences of the other characters, each struggling to find their place in a world torn apart by war.

The title "The War I Finally Won" is a testament to Ada's triumph over her own personal demons, as well as the external challenges she faces. It is a powerful reminder that even in the midst of chaos and destruction, there is always hope for healing, growth, and redemption.

In short, "The War I Finally Won" is a masterpiece of historical fiction that will leave readers of all ages spellbound and moved. With its vivid characters, evocative prose, and powerful themes, this book is a must-read for anyone looking for a story that will linger long after the final page is turned.
