# Niccolò Rising (The House of Niccolò, #1)

**Author:** Dorothy Dunnett  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1986

Niccolò Rising, the first installment in Dorothy Dunnett's The House of Niccolò series, is a sweeping historical epic that whisks readers away to the vibrant mercantile landscape of 15th-century Europe. Set against the richly detailed backdrop of Bruges, Flanders, and the Mediterranean, this novel masterfully weaves a complex tapestry of adventure, romance, and intrigue, all centered around the enigmatic figure of Nicholas vander Poele, a brilliant and charismatic young dyer's apprentice.

Dunnett's writing style is a marvel to behold, as she effortlessly conjures the sights, sounds, and smells of a bygone era with her characteristic bravura storytelling and meticulous attention to historical detail. Her prose is as lush and evocative as the world she describes, drawing the reader into the midst of a long-forgotten time and place. From the colorful chaos of a Flemish carnival to the treacherous beauty of the Alpine mountains, every setting is vividly rendered, immersing the reader in the world of the story.

At the heart of Niccolò Rising is its eponymous hero, Nicholas vander Poele, a young man of remarkable intelligence, wit, and charm. Beginning as a humble apprentice, Nicholas rapidly rises through the ranks of the mercantile elite, using his natural talents and cunning to outmaneuver his rivals and win the hearts of those around him. His is a classic tale of self-made success, as he navigates the treacherous waters of trade, diplomacy, and romance with a swashbuckling panache that is impossible to resist.

One of the most striking aspects of Dunnett's writing is her ability to balance action, adventure, and romance with a deep understanding of the historical context in which her story takes place. The world of Niccolò Rising is one of rapid change and upheaval, as the old certainties of the medieval era give way to the rising tides of capitalism and exploration. Against this backdrop, Nicholas's own story serves as a powerful metaphor for the emergence of a new kind of hero, one who is driven by intellect, adaptability, and a keen sense of opportunity.

Throughout the novel, Dunnett's characters are multidimensional and richly drawn, with even the most minor figures possessing a depth and complexity that adds to the overall richness of the narrative. From the enigmatic and beautiful Gelis van Borselen to the sinister and ruthless Simon de St. Pol, every character plays a crucial role in the unfolding drama, and each is imbued with a sense of history and motivation that makes them feel fully realized and believable.

If there is a criticism to be made, it is that Niccolò Rising can be a challenging read, particularly for those unfamiliar with the historical period or Dunnett's writing style. The novel's pacing is often rapid-fire, with multiple plot threads and characters competing for attention, and the level of historical detail can be overwhelming at times. However, for readers willing to immerse themselves in the world of the story, the rewards are well worth the effort.

In conclusion, Niccolò Rising is a masterpiece of historical fiction, a novel that will appeal to fans of adventure, romance, and historical drama in equal measure. With its vivid characters, meticulous research, and breakneck pacing, this book is an unforgettable reading experience that will leave even the most discerning reader eager for more.
