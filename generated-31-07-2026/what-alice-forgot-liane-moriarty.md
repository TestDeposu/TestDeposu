# What Alice Forgot

**Author:** Liane Moriarty  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

In "What Alice Forgot," Liane Moriarty crafts a captivating narrative that delves into the complexities of human memory, relationships, and the choices that shape our lives. The story follows Alice Love, a 39-year-old woman who suffers a head injury and awakens with no recollection of the past decade. As she navigates this uncharted territory, Alice must reconstruct her life, relearn her relationships, and rediscover herself.

Moriarty's writing style is engaging and witty, with a keen eye for the intricacies of human emotions. She skillfully weaves together the threads of Alice's past and present, slowly unraveling the mysteries of her forgotten decade. Through Alice's journey, Moriarty explores the themes of identity, love, and the consequences of our choices. The novel is a thought-provoking exploration of how our experiences, both big and small, shape us into the people we become.

One of the most striking aspects of the novel is its portrayal of the complexities of relationships. Moriarty masterfully captures the nuances of Alice's relationships with her husband, children, and sister, revealing the intricate web of emotions, expectations, and disappointments that bind them together. As Alice struggles to recall her past, she must also confront the reality of her present, including the fractures in her relationships and the ways in which she has changed.

The novel also raises important questions about the nature of memory and its role in shaping our identities. As Alice grapples with the loss of her memories, she must confront the possibility that her past may not be what she thought it was. This realization sparks a deeper exploration of the self, as Alice seeks to rediscover her passions, values, and sense of purpose.

Moriarty's prose is effortless and engaging, making it easy to become fully immersed in Alice's world. Her characters are multidimensional and relatable, with all the flaws and quirks that make us human. The novel's pacing is well-balanced, moving seamlessly between moments of humor, pathos, and introspection.

In "What Alice Forgot," Liane Moriarty has crafted a novel that is both entertaining and thought-provoking. It is a story about the power of memory, the complexity of relationships, and the human capacity for growth and transformation. As Alice navigates her forgotten decade, she must confront the ultimate question: can we ever truly start over, or are we forever bound by the choices of our past?
