# God of Wrath (Legacy of Gods, #3)

**Author:** Rina Kent  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2026

When the devil sets his sights on you, there is no salvation—only surrender. In God of Wrath, the third installment of Rina Kent’s explosive Legacy of Gods series, the bestselling author once again plunges her readers into a world where luxury, power, and psychological warfare collide. Released in 2026, this dark romance dives headfirst into the intoxicating and perilous dynamic between two formidable forces: the ruthless Bratva heir Jeremy Volkov and the daring Cecily Knight. 

Known for her unapologetic exploration of the dark romance genre, Rina Kent crafts a narrative style that is as addictive as it is unsettling. Her prose moves with a relentless, breathless momentum, mirroring the psychological trap that closes in around the protagonist. Kent doesn't just write about control; she dissects it. The central theme of God of Wrath is obsession in its purest, most consuming form. Jeremy Volkov is the quintessential antihero—cold, calculating, and frighteningly dominant—yet Kent injects enough vulnerability into his torment to keep him fascinating rather than purely villainous. He is a man who rules through fear, but his fixation on Cecily transforms his cold empire into a cage built specifically for her. 

Cecily Knight is far from a passive victim, adding a vital layer of friction to the story. When she accidentally infiltrates a secretive initiation ceremony on a dark night, she triggers a chain reaction that seals her fate. What begins as a moment of catastrophic miscalculation quickly spirals into a dark dance of power dynamics. Kent excels at writing heroine-villain pairings where the boundary between predator and prey constantly blurs. Cecily sees the ice in Jeremy’s eyes, recognizes the danger, and yet feels the magnetic pull of his darkness. It is this psychological push-and-pull that defines Kent’s signature style: she toys with the concept of dubious consent, primal play, and the terrifying allure of a man who claims he wants to protect you while simultaneously seeking to brand and possess you entirely.

God of Wrath is not a conventional romance, and Kent makes no apologies for it. The novel comes heavily laden with trigger warnings—ranging from primal play to themes of dubious consent and references to sexual assault—serving as a stark reminder that this book lives firmly on the dark side of fiction. For readers who prefer their romance sanitized and safe, Jeremy Volkov’s world will feel like an inferno. But for connoisseurs of dark romance who crave high stakes, intense emotional collateral, and morally gray characters who revel in their own toxicity, Kent delivers a masterclass in tension.

Ultimately, God of Wrath is a hypnotic descent into the underworld of elite obsession. Rina Kent proves once again why she is a powerhouse in the genre, weaving a tale that is as visually striking as it is emotionally draining. It asks a chilling question: when the devil demands your soul, do you run, or do you willingly walk into the fire? For Cecily, the answer comes far too late, and for the reader, turning the final page is an equally inevitable surrender.
