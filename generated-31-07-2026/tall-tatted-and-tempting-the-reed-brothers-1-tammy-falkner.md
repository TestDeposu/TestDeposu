# Tall, Tatted and Tempting (The Reed Brothers, #1)

**Author:** Tammy Falkner  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

Title: The Silence of Secrets and Ink: A Review of Tall, Tatted and Tempting

In the vast and often saturated landscape of New Adult romance, finding a novel that manages to subvert tired tropes with genuine emotional resonance is a rare triumph. Tammy Falkner achieves just that in Tall, Tatted and Tempting, the opening installment of her sprawling Reed Brothers series. Published in 2016, this novel leans into the gritty aesthetics of the genre while anchoring its narrative in a surprisingly tender exploration of trauma, communication, and profound healing.

At the heart of the novel is a compelling thematic exploration of vulnerability through unconventional communication. Falkner constructs a world where speech is not the primary vehicle for connection. Logan Reed, the titular tatted hero, has chosen a life of self-imposed silence for eight years, communicating through art, touch, and presence rather than words. Enter Kit, a fugitive running from a shadowy past, hiding behind a sharp wit and a mean right hook. When their paths cross in a tattoo parlor, the thematic core of the book snaps into focus: two fractured souls trying to redraw the lines of their lives. 

Falkner’s writing style in this novel is defined by its raw immediacy and dual-perspective intimacy. By alternating chapters between Kit and Logan, the author establishes an atmosphere of heightened emotional stakes. Kit’s internal monologue reflects the frantic, paranoid energy of someone whose life is constantly under threat. In sharp contrast, Logan’s chapters—despite his lack of spoken dialogue—exude a grounded, protective stillness. This stylistic juxtaposition effectively mirrors their internal conflicts. Logan’s silence is not a void; rather, Falkner paints it as a canvas filled with acute observation, patience, and fierce loyalty. 

The author deftly navigates the classic romance dynamic of the protector and the protected, yet flips the expectation by making the silent man the anchor for a woman who initially appears utterly uncontainable. Kit is no shrinking violet; she possesses a sharp defensive edge, yet she finds herself utterly disarmed by Logan’s silent perceptiveness. Their slow-burn dynamic is heavily reliant on unspoken tension, shared domesticity in a chaotic household of five brothers, and the metaphorical weight of body art. A tattoo is never just a tattoo in Falkner’s hands; it is a permanent confession, a reclaiming of agency over one's own skin and story.

While the novel carries all the hallmarks of the New Adult genre—high emotional stakes, brooding alpha figures, and a touch of melodramatic danger—Falkner elevates the material through the warmth of the Reed family backdrop. The inner-city home shared by Logan and his brothers provides a vibrant, chaotic contrast to the dark secrets haunting both protagonists, grounding the romance in a tangible sense of belonging. 

Tall, Tatted and Tempting succeeds because it understands that true intimacy requires vulnerability, and sometimes, the loudest connections are forged in absolute silence. For readers seeking a romance where the hero speaks volumes without uttering a single word, Falkner’s opening salvo is an arresting and deeply satisfying read that lays a rock-solid foundation for a massive, beloved series.
