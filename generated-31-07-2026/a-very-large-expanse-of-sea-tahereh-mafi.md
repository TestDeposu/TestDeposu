# A Very Large Expanse of Sea

**Author:** Tahereh Mafi  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

Title: Navigating the Raging Tides of Hate and First Love: A Review of Tahereh Mafi’s A Very Large Expanse of Sea

In her 2002-set contemporary novel A Very Large Expanse of Sea, bestselling author Tahereh Mafi trades the dystopian landscapes of her earlier work for a setting that feels agonizingly real and deeply personal. Drawing from her own adolescence, Mafi crafts a powerful, heartrending narrative about the enduring legacy of prejudice, the suffocating weight of Islamophobia, and the fragile, terrifying beauty of first love. 

The story centers on Shirin, a sixteen-year-old Muslim girl navigating the volatile political and social climate of post-9/11 America. To survive the daily onslaught of degrading comments, xenophobic glares, and physical intimidation, Shirin has erected impenetrable walls around her heart. She expects cruelty from the world, and the world rarely disappoints. Instead of trying to fit into a high school culture that rejects her, she channels her fury into the heavy beats of music and the fierce, expressive art of break-dancing. 

Mafi’s greatest triumph in this novel is the unflinching honesty with which she portrays Shirin’s rage. Too often, young adult fiction sanitizes the trauma of marginalization, forcing protagonists to be endlessly resilient or gracefully forgiving. Shirin, however, is beautifully, authentically angry. She is cynical, guarded, and exhausted. Mafi writes with a striking, visceral intensity that captures the exhausting labor of simply existing in a hostile environment. Every microaggression and overt act of bigotry is rendered with stinging clarity, making Shirin’s defensive armor entirely understandable to the reader.

Into this fortress of isolation steps Ocean James. Unflappable, genuinely curious, and unfazed by the social cost, Ocean refuses to let Shirin push him away. Their developing romance forms the emotional core of the novel, acting as a beacon of vulnerability in a dark sea of hostility. Mafi handles their connection with nuance, avoiding the trope of the magical savior boyfriend. Ocean cannot fix the systemic racism Shirin faces, but he can offer steadfast companionship, forcing her to confront the terrifying prospect of letting someone in. 

Mafi’s writing style is punchy, emotional, and rhythmic. The pacing mirrors the unpredictable currents of teenage emotion, oscillating between crushing despair and soaring hope. Through sharp dialogue and deeply internal prose, the author paints a vivid portrait of a girl caught between two worlds—the vibrant, protective culture of her heritage and the hostile American high school hallway. 

A Very Large Expanse of Sea is more than a romance; it is a vital exploration of the casual cruelty of prejudice and the courage it takes to unarm oneself in the face of hatred. Mafi delivers a poignant reminder that vulnerability is not a weakness, but the ultimate act of bravery. This is a striking, unforgettable read that lingers long after the final page is turned.
