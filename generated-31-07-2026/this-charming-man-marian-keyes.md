# This Charming Man

**Author:** Marian Keyes  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Beneath the polished veneer of Irish politics lies a darker, more insidious brand of manipulation, and in This Charming Man (2009), Marian Keyes strips away the gloss to expose the devastating wake left by a master of emotional destruction. Hailed as the "John F. Kennedy Jr. of Dublin," Paddy de Courcy is a politician whose devastating charisma and winning smile captivate the tabloids and the voting public alike. Yet, as Keyes brilliantly demonstrates, public adoration often masks private tyranny. 

The novel’s narrative engine is driven not by Paddy himself, but by the four distinct women whose lives have been permanently shaped, and frequently battered, by his orbit: Lola, Grace, Marnie, and Alicia. Each woman represents a different facet of victimhood and recovery, offering a mosaic of female resilience against patriarchal charm. Lola, a glamorous journalist; Grace, Paddy’s fiercely protective sister-in-law; Marnie, his jilted former fiancée; and Alicia, the woman currently blinded by his light, are brought together by a shared history of his selfishness and cruelty. 

Keyes’s greatest triumph in this 2009 release is her signature stylistic duality. She possesses a rare literary alchemy that allows her to tackle deeply harrowing themes—domestic psychological abuse, gaslighting, and the systemic silencing of women—without ever sacrificing her trademark wit, warmth, and accessibility. While the subject matter is undeniably heavy, Keyes infuses the prose with a buoyant, conversational rhythm that makes the page-turning experience feel like an intimate late-night conversation with a brutally honest, fiercely loyal friend. 

The central theme of the novel explores the invisible bruises left by charming abusers. Paddy de Courcy is never depicted as a mustache-twirling villain; instead, Keyes paints a far more frightening and realistic portrait of a narcissist who uses charm as a weapon. The author dissects how society so readily forgives charismatic men while scrutinizing their victims, highlighting the complicity of the media and public in enabling toxic power dynamics. As Paddy’s political star ascends, the atmosphere thickens with a delicious, simmering tension: the time has finally come for these women to reclaim their narratives, seeking not just survival, but redemption and a very satisfying brand of reckoning.

This Charming Man is a masterclass in modern women's fiction. It proves that Marian Keyes is far more than just a purveyor of commercial chick-lit; she is a sharp-eyed social satirist and a deeply empathetic chronicler of the female experience. Striking, timely, and emotionally resonant, this novel demands that we look past the winning smiles and question the true cost of charm.
