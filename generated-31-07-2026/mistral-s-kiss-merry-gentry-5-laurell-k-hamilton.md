# Mistral's Kiss (Merry Gentry, #5)

**Author:** Laurell K. Hamilton  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2006

Mistral's Kiss, the fifth installment in Laurell K. Hamilton's Merry Gentry series, is a spellbinding tale of magic, mystery, and seduction. The story picks up where the previous book left off, with faerie princess Meredith Gentry navigating the treacherous world of Faerie politics and her own destiny.

At the heart of the novel is Merry's quest to conceive an heir to the throne of the Unseelie Court, a task that is both crucial to restoring magic to the fae kingdom and a matter of life and death for the protagonist herself. As Merry delves deeper into the world of Faerie, she must contend with the complexities of royal court intrigue, where alliances are forged and broken with ease, and sabotage lurks around every corner.

One of the standout features of Hamilton's writing is her ability to craft a richly detailed world that is both fantastical and believable. The faerie kingdom, with its intricate hierarchies and ancient magic, is a place of wonder and danger, where the boundaries between reality and myth blur. The author's use of descriptive language is lavish, immersing the reader in the sights, sounds, and scents of this mystical realm.

Merry herself is a complex and multifaceted character, driven by a mix of motivations that include duty, desire, and a deep sense of loyalty to those she cares about. Her relationships with her immortal guardsmen, particularly Mistral, are a key aspect of the story, and Hamilton handles the erotic elements with her characteristic frankness and sensuality.

As the plot unfolds, Merry finds herself facing numerous challenges, from the reawakening of the Unseelie Mound to the schemes of her uncle, the King of Light and Illusion. The pacing is quick and suspenseful, with unexpected twists and turns that keep the reader on the edge of their seat.

Throughout the novel, Hamilton explores themes of power, identity, and the complexities of female experience. Merry's struggles to assert her authority in a patriarchal society, while also navigating the demands of her own desires and loyalties, are deeply relatable. The author's portrayal of female characters is nuanced and multidimensional, reflecting a range of perspectives and experiences.

If there is a criticism to be made, it is that the novel's focus on Merry's personal relationships and desires sometimes overshadows the larger plot. At times, the story feels more like a series of interconnected vignettes than a cohesive narrative. However, this is a minor quibble, and the book's many strengths far outweigh its weaknesses.

In conclusion, Mistral's Kiss is a captivating and sensual novel that will appeal to fans of fantasy, romance, and mystery. Hamilton's writing is engaging and immersive, and her characters are complex and memorable. While the book may not be to every reader's taste, it is undoubtedly a compelling addition to the Merry Gentry series.
