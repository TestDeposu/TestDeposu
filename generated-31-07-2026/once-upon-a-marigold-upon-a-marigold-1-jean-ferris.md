# Once Upon a Marigold (Upon a Marigold, #1)

**Author:** Jean Ferris  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2004

A delightful subversion of traditional fairy tale tropes, Jean Ferris’s 2004 novel Once Upon a Marigold brilliantly captures the irreverent spirit of modern fantasy satire while carving out its own distinct, charming path. Often compared to William Goldman’s beloved classic The Princess Bride, Ferris’s work takes the familiar building blocks of kingdoms, princesses, and royal intrigue and gleefully rearranges them into something both utterly ridiculous and surprisingly poignant. 

At the heart of the novel is the central theme of perception versus reality, particularly regarding the dizzying, often baffling nature of romantic love. The story follows Christian, a young man raised in a cave by a troll for a father, who spends his days spying on the royal family through a telescope. Christian is entirely unequipped for the emotional complexities of the outside world. His upbringing among trolls has given him a literal-minded, highly practical view of existence, which contrasts hilariously with the melodramatic posturing of court life. When he falls head over heels for Princess Marigold, he finds himself thrust into a convoluted web of affection that quickly spirals into a multi-sided romantic geometric shape. 

Ferris uses this romance-driven chaos to explore how ignorance of emotional truths can be just as dangerous as ignorance of physical threats. Christian’s cluelessness is not merely a source of easy comedy; it is a lens through which the author critiques the rigid, absurd structures of royalty and societal expectations. While Christian is navigating his newfound feelings, a far more sinister plot unfolds in the background. The scheming queen, hungry for absolute power, plots to usurp the throne—a plan that conveniently involves eliminating her own daughter, Princess Marigold. 

The stylistic brilliance of Once Upon a Marigold lies in Ferris’s razor-sharp wit and her ability to balance slapstick humor with sage, surprisingly grounded observations about human nature. The author employs a playful, conversational narrative voice that constantly winks at the reader, acknowledging the ridiculousness of fairy tale conventions even as the characters live through them. The dialogue is snappy and filled with clever modern sensibilities, making the medieval-esque setting feel fresh and unpredictable. Ferris delights in subverting expectations: trolls are not mindless beasts, princes are not always paragons of virtue, and true love requires far more than just a passing glance across a courtyard. 

Instead of relying solely on parody, Ferris anchors her absurd world with genuine heart. The bond between Christian and his adoptive troll father provides a touching emotional anchor amidst the political assassinations and romantic misunderstandings. By the time the narrative rushes toward its chaotic, laugh-out-loud finale, the reader is treated to a masterclass in how to honor a genre while joyfully turning it upside down. 

Ultimately, Once Upon a Marigold is a triumphant romp that proves happily-ever-afters are rarely neat, and that the best heroes often come from the most unexpected caves. Ferris has crafted a timeless, endlessly entertaining read that rewards both young adult readers and seasoned lovers of fantasy satire with its infectious charm and clever bite.
