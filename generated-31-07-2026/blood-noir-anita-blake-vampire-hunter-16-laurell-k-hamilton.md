# Blood Noir (Anita Blake, Vampire Hunter, #16)

**Author:** Laurell K. Hamilton  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

**Blood Noir: A Darker Shade of Anita Blake**

Laurell K. Hamilton's Anita Blake, Vampire Hunter series takes a darker turn in the 'Blood Noir' omnibus, collecting books 16-19. This is not your average vampire tale; it's a gritty, sensual, and often brutal journey into a world where the supernatural is as common as the mundane, and power is the ultimate aphrodisiac.

The collection opens with 'Blood Noir', where Anita finds herself entangled in a web of ancient vampire politics. The queen of all vampires, Marmee Noir, is on the rise, and Anita is her pawn. Hamilton masterfully weaves a complex plot filled with betrayals, alliances, and a healthy dose of sex, making it impossible to put down.

'Skin Trade' shifts the action to Sin City, where a vampire is on a murderous rampage. Anita must team up with federal marshals to stop the killer, leading to a thrilling cat-and-mouse game. Hamilton's strength lies in her ability to create vivid, flawed characters, and her marshals are no exception, adding depth and tension to the story.

'Flirt' finds Anita raising the dead, but the results are far from what she expected. This book delves into the darker side of necromancy, exploring the consequences of playing with life and death. Hamilton's exploration of the moral complexities of Anita's powers is one of the series' most compelling aspects.

The collection concludes with 'Bullet', where the Mother of All Darkness sets her sights on Anita's body as her new vessel. This is Hamilton at her most intense, pushing Anita to her physical and emotional limits. The action is non-stop, the stakes are sky-high, and the ending is a game-changer.

Hamilton's style is distinctive, with a first-person narrative that immerses readers in Anita's world. Her prose is often poetic, painting vivid images with a dark, sensual edge. However, it's her exploration of power dynamics, both political and sexual, that truly sets her apart. Anita is no damsel in distress; she's a complex, powerful woman navigating a dangerous world, and watching her grow and evolve is a large part of the series' appeal.

Yet, 'Blood Noir' is not for the faint-hearted. It's graphic, both in its violence and its sexuality, and it's not afraid to push boundaries. But for those who can handle it, it's a thrilling, addictive ride into a world that's as fascinating as it is frightening.
