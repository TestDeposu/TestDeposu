# Covet (Fallen Angels, #1)

**Author:** J.R. Ward  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

J.R. Ward has carved out an undeniable empire within paranormal romance, most notably through her juggernaut Black Dagger Brotherhood series. With Covet, the inaugural installment of her Fallen Angels series published in 2009, Ward steps outside the vampire-dominated boundaries of Caldwell, New York, to tackle a much grander, cosmic playing field. The result is a high-stakes, gritty, and fiercely passionate reimagining of eternal salvation, proving once again why Ward is a master of blending high-octane urban fantasy with heart-wrenching human emotion.

At the thematic core of Covet is the brutal, beautiful, and deeply messy concept of redemption. Ward shifts her lens away from traditional angelic purity and instead drops her narrative anchor into the gutter. Meet Jim Heron, a protagonist who knows nothing of grace, dealing instead in the hard currency of revenge. Jim is forced into the ultimate celestial probation as a fallen angel tasked with saving seven souls from the seven deadly sins. Through Jim, Ward explores a compelling philosophical question: Can a man who defines himself by his sins ever truly earn a clean slate? Sin, in Ward's universe, is not a rigid theological checklist; it is relative, deeply personal, and inextricably tied to the flaws that make us human. 

Enter Vin diPietro, a man who has traded his humanity for corporate success, embodying the sin of greed until a hard-riding, tough-talking savior on a Harley-Davidson crashes into his meticulously ordered reality. Vin is paired with a woman who awakens desires he never knew he possessed, forcing him to navigate a treacherous path between earthly love and eternal damnation. The friction between Vin’s corporate pragmatism and the harsh, supernatural reality thrust upon him drives the emotional engine of the novel. 

Stylistically, Covet is vintage J.R. Ward. Readers familiar with her work will instantly recognize her signature voice: raw, kinetic, unapologetically edgy, and laced with a distinct street-smart vernacular. Ward possesses a rare talent for making the supernatural feel gritty and immediate. There are no gauzy, ethereal angels here; her celestial beings bleed, swear, ride motorcycles, and grapple with existential crises in dimly lit bars. The prose moves at a relentless, breathless pace, balancing sprawling supernatural mythology with intensely intimate character studies. 

While the theological stakes—heaven versus hell, salvation versus eternal torment—are vast, Ward smartly anchors the grand cosmic battle in the intimate, fragile mechanics of the human heart. The romance is steamy, intense, and fraught with peril, but it never overshadows the overarching plot. The presence of an ancient evil waiting in the wings adds a palpable sense of dread, ensuring that the pages turn themselves. 

Covet is not merely a romance novel with supernatural elements; it is an urban fantasy deeply concerned with the architecture of second chances. Ward takes classic tropes of good versus evil and subverts them with her trademark swagger, creating a world where even the most broken souls might find a way back to the light. For fans of edgy paranormal romance and high-stakes supernatural drama, this first descent into Ward’s fallen angels universe is an absolute must-read.
