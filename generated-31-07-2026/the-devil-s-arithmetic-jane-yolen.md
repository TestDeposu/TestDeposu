# The Devil's Arithmetic

**Author:** Jane Yolen  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1988

In "The Devil's Arithmetic," Jane Yolen weaves a powerful narrative that delves into the heart-wrenching realities of the Holocaust, while exploring the complex and often fraught relationship between past and present. This critically acclaimed novel, winner of the National Jewish Book Award, is a triumphantly moving tale that will leave readers disturbed, compelled, and ultimately transformed.

Through the eyes of Hannah, a young girl who finds herself transported to a Polish village in 1942, Yolen masterfully conveys the horrors of the Holocaust, imbuing the story with a sense of urgency and immediacy. As Hannah navigates this unfamiliar world, she begins to unravel the mystery of her presence, only to discover the unspeakable atrocities that await the villagers. Yolen's vivid prose and meticulous historical research bring the past to life, rendering the experience both haunting and unforgettable.

One of the most striking aspects of Yolen's writing style is her ability to balance the brutality of the Holocaust with a deep sense of hope and resilience. Despite the overwhelming darkness of the subject matter, the novel is infused with a sense of light and redemption, as embodied by the character of Chaya, Hannah's alter ego. This nuanced approach allows readers to engage with the material on a deeper level, fostering a sense of empathy and understanding that lingers long after the final page has been turned.

Yolen's use of magical realism adds a layer of complexity to the narrative, blurring the lines between past and present, and inviting readers to consider the ways in which history continues to shape our lives. The device of Hannah's transportation to the past serves as a powerful metaphor for the ways in which the past can both haunt and inform us, highlighting the importance of remembering and learning from history.

Throughout the novel, Yolen's prose is marked by a simplicity and directness that belies the complexity of the themes and emotions she explores. Her writing is both accessible and powerful, making the novel an excellent choice for readers of all ages. The characters are richly drawn and multidimensional, with Hannah's voice ringing out as a clear and authentic presence.

In "The Devil's Arithmetic," Jane Yolen has created a work of immense power and beauty, one that adds significantly to our understanding of the Holocaust and its ongoing impact on our world. This is a novel that will resonate with readers, leaving a lasting impression that will continue to reverberate long after the final page has been turned.
