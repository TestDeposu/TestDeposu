# Death Comes as the End

**Author:** Agatha Christie  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

A Masterful Blend of History and Mystery: A Review of Agatha Christie's "Death Comes as the End"

In the vast expanse of Agatha Christie's literary canon, "Death Comes as the End" stands out as a unique gem, a historical mystery novel that transports readers to ancient Thebes in 2000 BC. This singular work, first published in 1944, is a testament to Christie's innovative spirit and her ability to seamlessly blend genres. As a reviewer, I was eager to delve into this lesser-known but equally captivating novel, and I must say, it did not disappoint.

Christie's decision to set her story in ancient Egypt was inspired by her husband, Sir Max Mallowan, a renowned archaeologist, and Egyptologist Stephen Glanville, who not only suggested the setting but also provided valuable insights into daily life in ancient Egypt. This meticulous attention to detail is evident throughout the novel, as Christie masterfully recreates the sights, sounds, and customs of a bygone era.

The story itself is a complex web of family dynamics, power struggles, and, of course, murder. The protagonist, Renisenb, a young woman living in a noble household, finds herself entangled in a series of tragic events that ultimately lead to the demise of several family members. As the body count rises, Renisenb must navigate the treacherous waters of her family's history and alliances to uncover the truth behind the deaths.

One of the most striking aspects of "Death Comes as the End" is its use of the Egyptian agricultural calendar as a theme for chapter titles. This clever device not only adds to the novel's historical authenticity but also serves as a poignant reminder of the cyclical nature of life and death. Christie's writing is, as always, economical and precise, yet rich in atmosphere and suspense.

The novel's pacing is expertly managed, with Christie skillfully ratcheting up the tension as the story unfolds. The characters, while not as overtly eccentric as some of Christie's other creations, are nonetheless well-drawn and nuanced, with Renisenb emerging as a particularly strong and sympathetic protagonist.

In many ways, "Death Comes as the End" can be seen as a precursor to the historical whodunit genre, which would later become a staple of mystery fiction. Christie's bold experiment in combining historical fiction with the detective story pays off handsomely, resulting in a novel that is both a gripping mystery and a fascinating exploration of ancient Egyptian culture.

If I were to quibble, it would be with the novel's conclusion, which, according to Christie's own account, was altered at the suggestion of Stephen Glanville. While the ending is still satisfying, one can't help but wonder what Christie's original intention might have been.

In conclusion, "Death Comes as the End" is a remarkable achievement, a testament to Agatha Christie's boundless creativity and innovative spirit. This historical mystery novel is a must-read for fans of the genre, as well as anyone interested in exploring the lesser-known corners of Christie's oeuvre.
