# The Assassin and the Underworld (Throne of Glass, #0.4)

**Author:** Sarah J. Maas  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

Before the glass shattered and the legendary trials of Rifthold began, there was only the dark, the blood, and the blade. In The Assassin and the Underworld, Sarah J. Maas plunges her readers back into the formative years of Celaena Sardothien, offering a breathless, morally complex glimpse into the life of Adarlan’s most feared assassin. Published in 2012 as a vital piece of the Throne of Glass prequel anthology, this novella may be compact in length, but it packs an emotional and atmospheric punch that resonates far beyond its final page.

At the heart of the narrative lies a compelling exploration of institutional corruption and moral hypocrisy. When Arobynn Hamel, the enigmatic King of the Assassins, tasks Celaena with a mission ostensibly designed to strike a blow against the horrific slave trade operating within the capital, the premise sounds like noble justice. Yet, as Maas expertly unravels the plot, the lines between hero and monster begin to blur. Celaena transitions from the pristine, moonlit rooftops of the city down into the literal and figurative muck of the underworld—the sprawling, treacherous sewers of Rifthold. This descent serves as a brilliant thematic anchor. The physical filth of the sewers mirrors the moral rot festering at the upper echelons of society. Maas uses this stark contrast to dissect the ugly reality of power: those who claim to fight evil are often knee-deep in it themselves. It forces Celaena, and the reader, to confront uncomfortable truths about justice, vengeance, and the compromises required to survive in a merciless world.

Maas’s signature stylistic flair is already operating at a high level in this early work. Known for her cinematic world-building and penchant for high-stakes drama, she crafts an immersive sensory experience. The prose is sharp, rhythmic, and visceral, capturing the lethal grace of Celaena’s movements while laying bare the vulnerability hidden beneath the arrogant facade of the teenage assassin. Maas excels at balancing high-octane action sequences with intimate character introspection. We witness Celaena not just as an unstoppable weapon, but as a young woman caught in the web of a master manipulator, struggling to define her own moral compass while bound to a master who owns her life. The pacing is relentless, pulling the reader through the murky depths of the mystery alongside a protagonist whose lethal competence is matched only by her fierce, untamed spirit.

For both longtime fans of the Throne of Glass series and newcomers looking to dip their toes into Maas’s expansive fantasy universe, The Assassin and the Underworld is an essential read. It enriches the overarching mythos by adding crucial layers to Celaena’s backstory, transforming her from a mythical badass into a deeply human, scarred individual shaped by betrayal and dark revelations. It is a striking, fast-paced appetizer that proves Sarah J. Maas has always known how to construct a labyrinth of secrets, danger, and unyielding resilience.
