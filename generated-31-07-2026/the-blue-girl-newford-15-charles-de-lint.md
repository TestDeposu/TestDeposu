# The Blue Girl (Newford, #15)

**Author:** Charles de Lint  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

"The Blue Girl" by Charles de Lint is a mesmerizing collection of 29 timeless, magical tales that transport readers to a world where mystery and myth are woven into the fabric of everyday life. This enchanting compilation, carefully curated with the help of de Lint's devoted fans, showcases the author's unique ability to balance the light and dark aspects of life, reminding us of the beauty, hope, and strength that often lie just beneath the surface.

De Lint's writing style is a masterful blend of poetic language, vivid imagery, and well-crafted characters that leap off the page. His stories are infused with a deep sense of humanism, exploring the complexities and nuances of the human experience. From the delightful "Pixel Pixies" to the heart-wrenching "In the House of My Enemy," each tale is a testament to de Lint's skill at crafting narratives that are both entertaining and thought-provoking.

One of the standout features of this collection is its ability to seamlessly blend the ordinary with the extraordinary. De Lint's characters are often artists, musicians, and outsiders, living on the fringes of society, yet they are imbued with a sense of wonder and magic that is both captivating and relatable. His use of myth and folklore adds a rich layer of depth to the stories, inviting readers to explore the hidden corners of their own imaginations.

The stories in "The Blue Girl" are not just mere fantasies; they are also explorations of the human condition. De Lint tackles themes of identity, community, and the power of storytelling with sensitivity and nuance. His characters are multidimensional and flawed, yet ultimately, they are hopeful and resilient. This is a collection that will appeal to fans of urban fantasy, mythology, and folklore, as well as anyone looking for stories that will touch their heart and soul.

Throughout the book, de Lint's passion for storytelling shines through, and his dedication to his craft is evident on every page. The result is a collection that is both a testament to his skill as a writer and a gift to his readers. As one reviewer noted, "de Lint will have changed you with his magic" by the time you finish reading this book.

In short, "The Blue Girl" is a must-read for anyone who loves fantasy, mythology, and folklore. It is a collection that will transport you to another world, one that is full of wonder, magic, and possibility. So, come and enter the enchanting world of Charles de Lint, where the boundaries between reality and fantasy blur, and the imagination knows no limits.
