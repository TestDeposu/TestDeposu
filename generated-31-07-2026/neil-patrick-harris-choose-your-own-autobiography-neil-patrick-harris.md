# Neil Patrick Harris: Choose Your Own Autobiography

**Author:** Neil Patrick Harris  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

Celebrity memoirs are a dime a dozen, usually following a predictable, self-serious trajectory: humble beginnings, the intoxicating rush of sudden fame, a dark descent into scandal or addiction, and a redemptive, wisdom-laden conclusion. Enter Neil Patrick Harris, a multi-hyphenate entertainer entirely uninterested in playing by the traditional rules of the genre. With his 2014 release, Neil Patrick Harris: Choose Your Own Autobiography, NPH completely shatters the standard biographical blueprint, delivering a literary stunt that is as audacious as it is deeply entertaining. By hijacking the nostalgic Choose Your Own Adventure format of our youth, Harris hands the steering wheel directly to the reader, crafting a self-aware, highly interactive satire of celebrity narcissism that redefines what a memoir can achieve.

The central theme of the book is the absurdity of public identity and the illusory nature of control. Traditional autobiographies invite readers to passively witness a life unfold, offering a curated illusion of absolute truth. Harris flips this convention on its head. By casting the reader in the second person ("You"), he forces us to actively inhabit his skin, making choices at critical junctures of his life and career. Do you want to audition for Doogie Howser, M.D., or pass on the opportunity? Do you want to grapple openly with your emerging sexuality, or stay in the closet while dining on Elton John’s yacht? Through this playful mechanic, Harris highlights the butterfly effect of life's pivotal moments while simultaneously mocking the very idea that any of us—celebrity or otherwise—are fully in control of our destinies. 

The author’s style is the true star of the show. It is unapologetically zany, aggressively clever, and packed with a hyper-kinetic energy that mirrors Harris’s own stage presence. He leans heavily into absurdity, allowing readers to face consequences that range from Hollywood triumph to a genuinely ridiculous, premature death by piranhas if a poor choice is made. Yet, beneath the gimmick and the relentless barrage of punchlines, a surprising vulnerability emerges. The book manages to chronicle genuine milestones—his professional reinvention, his coming out, and the joys of building a family with his husband, David Burtka—without ever sacrificing its comedic momentum. 

Furthermore, Harris elevates the text beyond a mere narrative experiment by treating the book as a multi-sensory playground. Interspersed throughout the branching paths are cocktail recipes, magic tricks, childhood photographs that induce second-hand embarrassment, and even a closing song. It is a wildly creative scrapbook that demands engagement rather than passive consumption.

Ultimately, Neil Patrick Harris: Choose Your Own Autobiography is a triumph of style and substance. It succeeds precisely because it refuses to take itself too seriously while still honoring the emotional truth of Harris’s journey. For readers weary of ghostwritten sob stories and sanitized Hollywood PR, this book is a breath of fresh, chaotic air. It is funny, inventive, and wonderfully bizarre—proof positive that when it comes to life’s adventures, the journey is vastly more interesting when you are allowed to make all the wrong turns.
