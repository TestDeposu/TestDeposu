# Devolution: A Firsthand Account of the Rainier Sasquatch Massacre

**Author:** Max Brooks  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

Max Brooks has done the impossible. With his 2020 bestselling novel, Devolution: A Firsthand Account of the Rainier Sasquatch Massacre, he takes a creature usually relegated to blurry photographs, campfire ghost stories, and cheap Halloween costumes, and transforms it into a creature of absolute, blood-curdling terror. If his masterwork World War Z redefined the modern zombie narrative through the lens of sociopolitical realism, Devolution does the exact same thing for cryptozoology. This is not just a monster story; it is a meticulously constructed, deeply unsettling examination of human fragility when stripped of modern conveniences.

The narrative framework is classic Brooks. Framed as a work of narrative nonfiction, the novel centers on the mysterious tragedy that befell Greenloop, a high-tech, eco-friendly, and hyper-isolated community nestled in the shadow of Mount Rainier, Washington. When the volcano erupts, it cuts Greenloop off from the rest of civilization. Landslides block the roads, cell towers fall, and the power grid fails. Trapped in a high-end wilderness utopia with dwindling supplies, the affluent and tech-dependent residents suddenly find themselves at the bottom of the food chain. 

At the heart of the book are the recovered journal entries of Kate Holland, a resident whose intimate, increasingly frantic writings capture the descent from mild inconvenience to sheer panic. Brooks weaves Kate's diary with his own framing narrative, conducting interviews, analyzing forensic evidence, and consulting primatologists and anthropologists. This hybrid style creates a powerful documentary effect. By grounding the unbelievable in scientific inquiry and historical precedent, Brooks builds a slow-burn atmospheric tension that makes the reader desperately want to believe every word. 

The central theme of Devolution explores the friction between human hubris and untamed nature. The residents of Greenloop moved to the mountains to disconnect, to live in harmony with the wild while still enjoying espresso machines, smart-home automation, and high-speed internet. They viewed nature as a picturesque backdrop, a peaceful aesthetic to be consumed. Brooks systematically dismantles this delusion. When the volcano strikes and the true apex predators of the Pacific Northwest—a troop of highly intelligent, incredibly brutal Sasquatches—begin to encircle the community, nature bites back with terrifying force. 

Furthermore, Brooks brilliantly deconstructs our psychological reliance on infrastructure. Cut off from Wi-Fi, emergency services, and supermarkets, the residents fracture. The contrast between their civilized self-images and their primal survival instincts drives the psychological horror of the plot. Yet, amidst the carnage, the book also highlights human resilience. Kate Holland’s transformation from an anxious city-dweller into a fierce, resourceful survivor anchors the novel's emotional core. 

When the violence finally erupts, Brooks does not pull his punches. The horror is visceral, relentless, and unflinching. The Sasquatches are portrayed not as gentle forest hermits, but as formidable, strategic hunters—primates of terrifying strength, cunning, and ferocity. 

Part survival narrative, part bloody creature feature, and part scientific exploration, Devolution is a triumph of speculative fiction. Max Brooks has crafted a definitive Bigfoot thriller for the modern era, one that will linger in the dark corners of your mind long after you turn the final page. It is smart, gripping, and deeply scary.
