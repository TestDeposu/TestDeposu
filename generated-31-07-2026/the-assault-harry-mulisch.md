# The Assault

**Author:** Harry Mulisch  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1985

The Assault, written by Harry Mulisch and published in 1985, is a haunting and introspective novel that delves into the profound repercussions of a single, devastating event on the life of its protagonist, Anton. Set against the backdrop of the final days of World War II in occupied Holland, the story is a masterful exploration of the human psyche, trauma, and the unyielding grip of the past.

The novel's narrative is a complex web of memories, chance encounters, and revelations that gradually unravel the mystery surrounding the assassination of a Nazi collaborator and the subsequent burning of an innocent family's home, which leaves Anton, then just twelve years old, the sole survivor. As the story unfolds, Mulisch skillfully crafts a nuanced and multi-layered portrait of Anton's life, marked by a deliberate attempt to erase the memories of that fateful night and assume a carefully normal existence.

Mulisch's writing style is characterized by a distinctive blend of subtlety and intensity, which perfectly captures the intricacies of human emotions and the turmoil that often lies beneath the surface of seemingly ordinary lives. His prose is sparse, yet evocative, and his use of language is both precise and poetic, imbuing the narrative with a sense of depth and complexity.

Through Anton's journey, Mulisch masterfully explores the theme of trauma and its lasting impact on individuals and society as a whole. The novel raises important questions about the nature of memory, guilt, and redemption, and how these concepts intersect and influence one another. As Anton navigates the complexities of his past, Mulisch sheds light on the human condition, revealing the ways in which our experiences, both joyful and traumatic, shape us into the people we become.

One of the most striking aspects of The Assault is its thought-provoking examination of the moral ambiguities that arise in times of war and occupation. Mulisch presents a nuanced portrayal of the Dutch resistance and the difficult choices individuals faced during this period, highlighting the gray areas that often exist between right and wrong.

Ultimately, The Assault is a powerful and moving novel that lingers in the reader's mind long after the final page has been turned. Mulisch's masterful storytelling and his profound insight into the human experience make this book a must-read for anyone interested in literary fiction, historical fiction, or simply great storytelling.
