# The Lost Metal (Mistborn, #7)

**Author:** Brandon Sanderson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

Title: The Final Frontier of Scadrial: A Masterclass in Genre Fusion

When Brandon Sanderson set out to write The Alloy of Law, what began as a whimsical experiment—a Wild West steampunk interlude between the epic fantasy epochs of his sprawling Cosmere—morphed into something far grander. With The Lost Metal (2022), the explosive seventh installment in the Mistborn saga and the capstone to the Wax and Wayne series, Sanderson delivers a breathtaking collision of magic, modernity, and myth that cements his status as the premier architect of modern fantasy.

At its core, The Lost Metal explores the dizzying, often terrifying transition of a magical society stepping into the industrial age. The thematic heartbeat of the novel rests on the friction between duty, faith, and systemic evolution. As Scadrial rapidly modernizes—introducing telephones, skyscrapers, and weapons of mass destruction—its inhabitants are forced to ask a pressing question: what happens to ancient gods and hereditary magic when humanity learns to build its own destiny? Waxillium Ladrian, the frontier lawman thrust into high-stakes senatorial politics, embodies this struggle. He is a man caught between the rustic simplicity of his past and the brutal, technological demands of a future he isn't sure he wants to save. Beside him, Detective Marasi Colms represents the forward-thinking pulse of the narrative, challenging divine authority and terrestrial corruption alike as she uncovers a conspiracy stretching far beyond the borders of Elendel and Bilming.

Sanderson’s signature style is on full display here, characterized by breakneck pacing, ingenious magic systems, and a deeply interconnected universe. True to form, the action set-pieces are cinematic and relentless. Whether it is the mind-bending tactical use of Allomancy and Feruchemy—where pushing, pulling, and weight manipulation turn street corners into three-dimensional battlegrounds—or the looming threat of the mysterious god Trell and the shadow organization known as the Set, Sanderson keeps the tension at a fever pitch. 

Yet, what elevates The Lost Metal above a mere action thriller is Sanderson’s trademark balance of high-stakes world-ending peril and intimate character dynamics. The emotional core of the book anchors the cosmic spectacle. Wayne, the lovable rogue with a heart of gold and a tragic past, and Steris, whose meticulous planning and autistic-coded competence make her one of the most uniquely refreshing heroines in fantasy, provide the emotional ballast that keeps the narrative grounded. 

Furthermore, this installment serves as a massive milestone for the broader Cosmere. By weaving in offworld agents and expanding the lore of the wider universe, Sanderson rewards long-time readers while still delivering a satisfying, definitive conclusion to the specific arcs of Wax, Wayne, and Marasi. 

The Lost Metal is a triumph of pacing, world-building, and emotional resonance. It proves that even as worlds evolve, grow smaller, and face catastrophic new threats, the human element—our capacity for sacrifice, love, and growth—remains the most powerful force in any universe. Sanderson has not merely closed a chapter; he has orchestrated a dazzling explosion of a finale that will echo across the Cosmere for years to come.
