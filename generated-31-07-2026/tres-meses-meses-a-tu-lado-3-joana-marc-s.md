# Tres meses (Meses a tu lado, #3)

**Author:** Joana Marcús  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

Tres meses (Meses a tu lado, #3) by Joana Marcús arrives as the much‑anticipated finale of a trilogy that has turned the simple notion of “spending time together” into a profound exploration of what it means to let someone change you. The novel follows Jack Ross, a self‑declared skeptic of lasting love, whose carefully guarded independence begins to crumble the moment Jenna steps into his orbit. Over the span of three months—a deliberately limited timeframe that feels both intimate and urgent—Jack is forced to confront the very ideas he once dismissed: the power of a shared glance, the vulnerability of opening one’s heart, and the terrifying possibility that everything could fall apart.

At its core, the book is less about the mechanics of romance and more about the internal revolution that love can ignite. Marcús treats the three‑month window as a crucible: each week peels back another layer of Jack’s defenses, revealing fears rooted in past disappointments and a deep‑seated belief that commitment equals loss of self. Jenna, meanwhile, is not merely a catalyst; she is portrayed with her own hopes, insecurities, and quiet strength, making their connection feel reciprocal rather than one‑sided. The narrative oscillates between Jack’s witty, self‑deprecating inner monologue and tender, almost lyrical scenes where words give way to gestures—a lingering touch, a shared silence, a laugh that catches in the throat. This duality keeps the reader anchored in Jack’s cynical voice while simultaneously allowing the softer, more hopeful tone of the romance to seep through.

Marcús’s style is unmistakably contemporary yet timeless in its emotional honesty. She writes with a breezy, conversational rhythm that makes the pages fly, yet she never sacrifices depth for speed. Descriptive passages are concise but evocative; a rainy afternoon in a cramped café becomes a metaphor for the storm brewing inside Jack, while a spontaneous midnight walk along the river mirrors the uncertain yet exhilarating path of new affection. Dialogue crackles with authenticity—Jack’s sarcastic quips feel like armor, Jenna’s earnest responses like gentle probes seeking a chink in that armor. The author’s skill lies in balancing humor and heartache without letting either overwhelm the other; the result is a story that feels both light enough to enjoy in a single sitting and substantial enough to linger after the final page.

What sets Tres meses apart from many romance novels is its refusal to romanticize the idea of “fixing” someone through love. Instead, Marcús presents love as a mirror: it reflects back our own flaws, fears, and desires, urging us to confront them rather than hide behind them. Jack’s journey is not about becoming a different person for Jenna’s sake, but about discovering parts of himself he had long ignored or suppressed. The three‑month limit serves as a narrative device that heightens tension—every shared moment carries the weight of impermanence, making each confession feel earned and each hesitation understandable.

The pacing is deliberate; the first half lingers in the mundane details of everyday coexistence, allowing readers to settle into the rhythm of Jack and Jenna’s life together. As the weeks progress, the narrative tightens, emotions intensify, and the looming deadline forces both characters to make choices that feel inevitable yet surprising. Marcús avoids melodramatic twists; the climax emerges naturally from the characters’ growth, culminating in a resolution that respects their autonomy while affirming the transformative power of genuine connection.

In sum, Tres meses is a satisfying, emotionally resonant conclusion to the Meses a tu lado series. Joana Marcús proves once again that she can capture the flutter of nascent love with the same precision she uses to dissect the complexities of fear and self‑acceptance. Readers who have followed Jack and Jenna from the first installment will find closure that feels both earned and hopeful, while newcomers will be drawn in by the novel’s honest portrayal of how a brief, intense period can rewrite the trajectory of a life.
