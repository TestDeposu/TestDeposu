# Shield of Thunder (Troy, #2)

**Author:** David Gemmell  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

In "Shield of Thunder," the second installment of David Gemmell's epic reimagining of the Trojan War, the author masterfully weaves an intricate tapestry of character-driven drama, politics, and legendary battles. The narrative expertly expands upon the first book in the series, delving deeper into the psyche of the characters and propelling the reader into the midst of a global conflict that will test the mettle of even the strongest warriors.

At the heart of the story lie three travelers: Piria, a priestess hiding a dark secret; Kalliades, a skilled warrior equipped with a legendary sword; and Banokles, an unassuming hero destined for greatness. These protagonists, along with a host of familiar and new characters, are thrust into the midst of a tumultuous gathering of Greek kings, where treachery and deception lurk around every corner. It is here that Gemmell showcases his remarkable storytelling prowess, effortlessly switching between fast-paced action sequences, philosophical introspection, and poignant human moments.

One of the standout aspects of Gemmell's writing is his uncanny ability to breathe life into ancient figures, rendering them relatable, multidimensional, and endearingly human. His portrayal of the epic heroes is both authentic and nuanced, imbuing the tale with a depth and richness that resonates deeply with readers. From the valiant but doomed Hektor to the wise, wily Odysseus, each character is meticulously crafted to embody the essence of their historical counterparts.

The narrative is infused with an undercurrent of existential dread, as the inevitability of war looms over the assembled kings and their armies. This sense of foreboding is palpable, imbuing the story with a tangible weight that propels the reader toward the catastrophic climax. Gemmell's mastery of pacing ensures that the tension builds inexorably, fueled by a series of intense battles, daring escapes, and heart-wrenching betrayals.

At its core, "Shield of Thunder" is a sweeping tale of loyalty, friendship, and redemption, woven from threads of mythology, history, and imagination. Gemmell's writing is a seamless blend of historical and fantastical elements, conjuring the sights, sounds, and scents of ancient Greece with remarkable fidelity. His prose is characterized by a lyrical cadence, rich sensory details, and a profound understanding of the human condition, elevating this epic fantasy to an unforgettable experience.

While "Shield of Thunder" is designed as a sequel, it is also a self-contained narrative that can be fully appreciated on its own. The story is structured to allow new readers to jump in, should they wish, though it is clear that the author has designed the series to be savored in sequence. As the epic reaches its climax, Gemmell's masterful storytelling culminates in a shattering conclusion that will leave readers breathless and, more often than not, yearning for more.

Ultimately, "Shield of Thunder" stands as a testament to Gemmell's artistry as a storyteller, demonstrating a unique ability to balance action, drama, and philosophy in a rich, epic narrative that will captivate fans of historical fiction and fantasy alike. With its vivid re-creation of Bronze Age Greece and its unforgettable cast of characters, this novel is a triumph that deserves to be celebrated alongside the greatest works of the Trojan War.
