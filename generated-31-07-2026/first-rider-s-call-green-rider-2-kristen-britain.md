# First Rider's Call (Green Rider, #2)

**Author:** Kristen Britain  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2003

In the realm of epic fantasy, Kristen Britain's Green Rider series has carved a niche for itself, captivating readers with its rich world-building, intricate magic systems, and memorable characters. The second installment, First Rider's Call, is a testament to Britain's masterful storytelling, as it delves deeper into the world of Sacoridia and the complexities of its magical messengers.

Karigan G'ladheon, the protagonist, is a compelling and relatable character, whose struggles to come to terms with her past and her connection to the mysterious forces that govern the Green Riders, are deeply human. Her return to the court, after a brief respite, is a turning point in the narrative, as she confronts the reality of the weakened Green Riders and the erratic nature of their magic.

Britain's writing style is characterized by its lyricism and attention to detail, which brings the world of Sacoridia to vivid life. The author's use of descriptive language is evocative, yet restrained, allowing the reader's imagination to fill in the gaps. The pacing is well-balanced, with a mix of action, suspense, and introspection, which keeps the narrative engaging and unpredictable.

One of the most striking aspects of First Rider's Call is its exploration of the theme of identity and the power of legacy. Karigan's connection to the First Rider, Lil Ambriodhe, is a central plot point, as she grapples with the weight of her own destiny and the expectations placed upon her. The ghostly apparitions of Lil Ambriodhe serve as a catalyst for Karigan's growth, as she navigates the complexities of her own identity and the role she is meant to play in the world of Sacoridia.

The supporting cast of characters is equally well-crafted, with each one bringing their own unique perspective to the narrative. The world-building is meticulous, with a deep history that underpins the events of the story. The magical system, with its intricate rules and consequences, is both fascinating and terrifying, as Karigan and her fellow Riders navigate the unpredictable nature of their abilities.

In conclusion, First Rider's Call is a captivating and immersive read, which will appeal to fans of epic fantasy and adventure. Britain's writing is engaging, and her world-building is meticulous, making this series a must-read for anyone who enjoys complex characters, richly detailed settings, and a gripping narrative.
