# Moonflower Murders (Susan Ryeland, #2)

**Author:** Anthony Horowitz  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

"Moonflower Murders" by Anthony Horowitz is a dizzying tour de force that will keep you enthralled from the very first page. This ingenious puzzle is an exercise in metafiction, a game of reflections within reflections that blurs the lines between reality and fiction, challenging the reader to stay one step ahead.

The story follows Susan Ryeland, the protagonist of Horowitz's previous novel, "Magpie Murders." Susan, now living a tranquil life on a Greek island, is slowly succumbing to the monotony and feeling increasingly disconnected from her beloved London. Her life takes a dramatic turn when the enigmatic Trehearne family arrives at her hotel, bearing a mysterious tale of an ancient crime that has left a dark stain on their family's past.

What unfolds is a masterfully crafted labyrinth, a maze of twists and turns that defy the reader's expectations. Horowitz deftly manipulates the reader's perceptions, expertly weaving multiple storylines, each with its own distinct voice and style. The narrative is a dazzling dance of literary references, from the works of Agatha Christie to the intricate web of plot devices reminiscent of Hitchcock's thrillers.

As Susan delves deeper into the mystery, she finds herself drawn into a world of fictional narratives, where the lines between reality and fiction begin to blur. The late Alan Conway, a celebrated mystery author known for his detective series featuring Atticus Pund, played a pivotal role in Susan's past. Conway's fictional account of the murder at Farlingaye Hall, an idyllic Suffolk coast inn, has become a bestseller, but its veracity remains a topic of heated debate.

What sets "Moonflower Murders" apart from its predecessors is Horowitz's bold, almost daring, approach to metafiction. The novel is a clever sendup of the crime fiction genre, an homage to its greatest masters, and a sly critique of the industry that often produces these types of stories. Horowitz winks at the reader, inviting them to be complicit in the game, and as we become more engaged, the stakes become increasingly high.

Horowitz's prose is, as always, precise and engaging. He writes with a keen eye for detail, an ability to evoke atmosphere, and a knack for crafting memorable characters. Susan Ryeland remains a compelling protagonist, relatable and multidimensional, and Horowitz skillfully conveys her growth and struggles throughout the novel. His writing is a perfect blend of wit, humor, and suspense, with enough red herrings and misdirections to keep even the most seasoned mystery enthusiast guessing.

Ultimately, "Moonflower Murders" is an exhilarating experience, a joyride of plot twists and clever literary references that will leave you scratching your head and wondering what really happened. Anthony Horowitz, a masterful storyteller, has crafted a true tour de force, a novel that is equal parts tribute, critique, and homage to the crime fiction genre. It's catnip for classic mystery lovers, and a must-read for anyone who enjoys a good puzzle.
