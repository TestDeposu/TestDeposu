# Lothaire (Immortals After Dark, #11)

**Author:** Kresley Cole  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

Dark, seductive, and relentlessly gripping, Lothaire stands as a crowning achievement in Kresley Cole’s bestselling Immortals After Dark series. Published in 2012, this eleventh installment takes the complex, sprawling mythology of the Lore and narrows its venomous gaze onto one of its most notorious antiheroes: Lothaire, the Enemy of Old, a vampire whose ruthlessness is matched only by his centuries of agonizing torment. Cole weaves a tale that is as emotionally harrowing as it is scorching, cementing her status as a master of paranormal romance.

At the heart of the novel lies the explosive collision between ancient vengeance and unexpected redemption. Lothaire is driven by a consuming, millennia-old blood vendetta, plotting to claim ultimate power by sacrificing Elizabeth Peirce, a delicate human woman whose soul he intends to use as currency. Yet, Cole brilliantly subverts the traditional captive-captor dynamic. Ellie is no mere damsel in distress; despite having endured a life of hardship before her abduction, she possesses a fierce, quiet resilience. As Lothaire finds himself inexplicably drawn to her—craving her touch and showering her with intense sexual pleasure—the predator slowly becomes the captive of his own growing desire. 

Cole’s signature style shines through her fearless embrace of high-stakes melodrama, biting humor, and visceral emotion. She refuses to sanitize her protagonists. Lothaire is unapologetically arrogant, ruthless, and deeply flawed, yet Cole manages to make his vulnerability and pain palpable, creating a deeply complex character whom readers will love to hate and hate to love. The pacing is relentless, balancing intricate world-building of the immortal realm with incendiary romantic tension. Every interaction between the ruthless vampire and the resilient human crackles with a dangerous energy, forcing readers to question whether love can truly conquer a lifetime of calculated malice.

Ultimately, Lothaire is a thrilling testament to the power of transformation against impossible odds. It explores the theme that true strength lies not in the capacity for destruction, but in the terrifying courage required to surrender one's heart. Cole delivers a phenomenal dark romance that leaves an indelible mark, proving that even the most damaged souls can find salvation in the unlikeliest of places.
