# Light from Uncommon Stars

**Author:** Ryka Aoki  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

"Light from Uncommon Stars" by Ryka Aoki is a mesmerizing blend of magical realism, science fiction, and queer romance that defies genre boundaries and will leave readers spellbound. This novel is a testament to the power of hope, identity, and the unbreakable bonds of found family.

At its core, "Light from Uncommon Stars" is a story about the complexities of identity, particularly for marginalized communities. Aoki weaves together the lives of three women from different walks of life: Shizuka, a violinist bound by a Faustian curse; Katrina, a young trans runaway with a wild talent for music; and Lan, a retired starship captain and mother of four who has found refuge on Earth. As their paths intersect, the novel explores the intersections of identity, culture, and belonging.

Aoki's writing is a masterclass in subtlety and nuance. She handles sensitive topics with care and respect, never shying away from the complexities of her characters' experiences. The novel is full of moments of quiet beauty, from the descriptions of Shizuka's violin playing to the warmth of Lan's donut shop, which becomes a sanctuary for the characters. Aoki's prose is evocative and immersive, making it easy to become fully invested in the world she has created.

One of the standout aspects of "Light from Uncommon Stars" is its exploration of the immigrant experience. Lan's story, in particular, is a powerful exploration of the struggles and triumphs of finding a new home in a foreign land. Aoki's portrayal of the San Gabriel Valley, with its vibrant cultural landscape, is a love letter to the diversity and resilience of immigrant communities.

The novel also explores the theme of curses and the power of hope. Shizuka's deal with the devil serves as a metaphor for the ways in which societal expectations can trap us, and the ways in which we can break free from those constraints. The novel suggests that even in the darkest of times, there is always hope for redemption and forgiveness.

Aoki's characters are multidimensional and relatable, with rich inner lives that leap off the page. Shizuka, Katrina, and Lan are all complex and flawed individuals, but it is their imperfections that make them so endearing. The novel's exploration of queer identity and romance is also noteworthy, particularly in the way it portrays love as a source of strength and healing.

Overall, "Light from Uncommon Stars" is a triumph of a novel that will resonate with readers long after they finish the book. It is a testament to the power of storytelling to bring people together and to create a sense of belonging. Aoki's writing is a gift, and this novel is a must-read for anyone looking for a story that will touch their heart and expand their mind.
