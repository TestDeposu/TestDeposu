# The Bone Witch (The Bone Witch, #1)

**Author:** Rin Chupeco  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Dark fantasy often treads familiar ground, recycling tired tropes of chosen ones and predictable magical academies. Yet, every so often, a novel arrives that disrupts the landscape with quiet confidence and striking originality. Rin Chupeco’s The Bone Witch (2017) is precisely that kind of disruptive force—a lavishly imagined, atmospheric, and emotionally resonant opening to a trilogy that deserves a prominent place on the shelf of any discerning fantasy enthusiast.

At its core, The Bone Witch is a profound exploration of grief, power, and the terrifying cost of subverting the natural order. The narrative follows Tea, a young girl who discovers her ability to perform the ultimate taboo: raising the dead. When she accidentally resurrects her beloved brother, Fox, she marks herself as a bone witch, a pariah in a society that reviles necromancy. But Chupeco avoids simplistic binaries of good and evil. Instead, the central theme examines what it means to be feared for simply existing, and how a society’s rejection can transform a tender-hearted girl into a force of destruction. Comparisons to Daenerys Targaryen are well-earned as we watch Tea navigate a path where the line between savior and monster blurs beyond recognition.

Chupeco’s prose is nothing short of mesmerizing, utilizing a dual-timeline framing device that immediately hooks the reader. We meet an older, world-weary Tea in the present, lamenting a past drenched in blood and tragedy, before flashing back to her rigorous training as an asha—a wielder of elemental magic and a glamour-filled entertainer akin to a high-fantasy geisha. This juxtaposition of glittering, high-society pageantry with the grim, shadow-soaked reality of necromancy is the author’s masterstroke. It creates a rich, multicultural tapestry that feels both alien and intimately grounded in human ambition and sorrow.

The worldbuilding is meticulously detailed, inviting readers to luxuriate in sensory descriptions of silk dresses, deadly beasts, and political intrigue. Chupeco does not rush. She allows the atmosphere to seep into the reader’s consciousness, rewarding patience with breathtaking stakes and a simmering tension that builds relentlessly toward a truly gripping cliffhanger. 

For readers seeking a lush, dark fantasy with diverse representation, complex anti-heroines, and a magic system that carries genuine weight, The Bone Witch is an absolute triumph. It is a story that whispers of coming devastation, leaving you desperate to turn the page while dreading the darkness waiting at the end.
