# My Father's Dragon (My Father's Dragon, #1)

**Author:** Ruth Stiles Gannett  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

A Timeless Tale Reborn: A Review of Ruth Stiles Gannett’s My Father’s Dragon

In the pantheon of children’s literature, few works possess the whimsical economy and surreal charm of Ruth Stiles Gannett’s 1948 classic, My Father’s Dragon. While the original edition has long enchanted generations of readers, the 2014 iPad-exclusive iteration breathes vibrant new life into this beloved adventure. This modern digital presentation does not merely rehash a nostalgic favorite; it thoughtfully elevates it, proving that the daring exploits of young Elmer Elevator are just as captivating to the modern tablet-wielding generation as they were to post-war readers.

At its core, the novel is a masterclass in imaginative problem-solving, anchored by the timeless theme of resourcefulness triumphing over brute force. When young Elmer encounters an alley cat who tells him of a captive baby dragon suffering on the faraway Wild Island, he does not pack swords or armor. Instead, his arsenal consists of chewing gum, lollipops, a toothbrush, rubber bands, and a magnifying glass. Gannett explores the theme of cleverness versus captivity with a light, humorous touch that never feels preachy. The baby dragon is not rescued through epic battles, but through wits, patience, and a well-placed piece of striped toothpaste. This central motif empowers young readers, validating the idea that a curious mind and an ordinary pocketful of odds and ends are the greatest tools for confronting the unknown.

Gannett’s writing style is a marvel of brisk, deadpan storytelling. There is an absolute lack of unnecessary exposition; every sentence moves the narrative forward with the crisp efficiency of a well-told folklore. The author treats the absurd—such as crocodiles tied together as a bridge or tigers brushing their hair—with utter seriousness, creating a dreamlike logic that completely absorbs the reader. The prose is deceptively simple, masking a sophisticated understanding of pacing and suspense. 

This 2014 digital edition honors Gannett’s literary voice by pairing it with a thoughtful technological upgrade. The inclusion of modern typography makes the text crisp and accessible, ensuring readability for young eyes without losing the story’s vintage soul. More importantly, the presentation preserves the iconic, delicate black-and-white illustrations by Ruth Chrisman Gannett, whose distinct style gives Wild Island its tangible, eerie texture. The crown jewel of this edition, however, is the bonus audio recording for each chapter. Hearing the text read aloud enhances the oral-tradition feel of the narrative, making it an immersive bedtime experience that bridges the gap between traditional reading and modern media.

My Father’s Dragon is a compact, brilliant adventure that proves great storytelling requires no bloat. By marrying Gannett’s genius prose and imaginative world-building with sleek digital design, this edition ensures that Elmer Elevator’s journey to rescue a dragon remains a sparkling jewel in children’s literature. It is a triumphant reminder that with a little imagination, no obstacle is truly insurmountable.
