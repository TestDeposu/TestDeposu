# The Book of Occult

**Author:** Simon W. Clark  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

In the realm of supernatural fiction, few authors have managed to weave a spell as captivating as Robert William Chambers. His 1895 masterpiece, The King in Yellow, has long been regarded as a landmark work in the genre, and its influence can still be felt today. But Chambers' contributions to the world of macabre literature extend far beyond this single, seminal work. It is a testament to his boundless imagination and skill that he continued to produce stories that would send shivers down the spines of readers, even in the latter stages of his career.

The Book of Occult, edited by Hugh Lamb, brings together some of the best of Chambers' supernatural tales, showcasing the author's remarkable range and depth. From the enigmatic Westrel Keen, the 'Tracer of Lost Persons', to the monstrous conspiracy of The Slayer of Souls, these stories demonstrate Chambers' ability to craft compelling narratives that blend elements of horror, mystery, and the unknown.

One of the most striking aspects of Chambers' style is his ability to evoke a sense of atmosphere and mood. His stories are often set in a world that is just slightly askew, where the ordinary and mundane coexist with the strange and inexplicable. This is particularly evident in The Slayer of Souls, where the protagonist must navigate a complex web of intrigue and deception to prevent a catastrophic event. Chambers' use of language is masterful, conjuring up images of dark, foreboding landscapes and eerie, unsettling encounters.

But Chambers' stories are not simply exercises in atmospheric horror. They are also imbued with a sense of intellectual curiosity, a desire to explore the unknown and the unexplained. His characters are often scholars, scientists, or seekers of knowledge, driven by a passion for discovery and a willingness to challenge the conventional wisdom. This is evident in the story of the eminent naturalist who scours the earth for 'extinct' animals, only to find that they are not as extinct as he had supposed.

Hugh Lamb's introduction to The Book of Occult provides valuable context and insight into Chambers' life and work, highlighting his significance as a writer and his enduring influence on the genre. Lamb's own passion for weird fiction is evident throughout the introduction, and his selection of stories showcases the best of Chambers' supernatural output.

In short, The Book of Occult is a must-read for anyone interested in supernatural fiction, horror, or the weird and unknown. Chambers' stories are a testament to the power of imagination and the enduring appeal of the macabre. With its masterful blend of atmosphere, intellect, and horror, this collection is sure to leave readers shivering in delight.
