# Shadows of Self (Mistborn, #5)

**Author:** Brandon Sanderson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

Brandon Sanderson's Shadows of Self is a masterful blend of fantasy, mystery, and adventure, set in a world that is both familiar and yet, utterly unique. This fifth installment in the Mistborn series is a testament to Sanderson's skill as a storyteller, as he weaves a complex narrative that explores the intricacies of power, identity, and the human condition.

The story takes place three hundred years after the events of the original Mistborn trilogy, in a world that is on the cusp of modernity. The city of Elendel is a marvel of technological advancements, with railroads, electric lighting, and steel-framed skyscrapers that seem to touch the clouds. However, beneath the surface of this gleaming metropolis, tensions are simmering. Labor unrest, religious conflicts, and terrorist attacks threaten to destabilize the fragile peace.

It is against this backdrop that Waxillium Ladrian, a Twinborn with the ability to use both Allomancy and Feruchemy, finds himself at the forefront of a brewing storm. Wax, a rugged and charismatic lawman from the frontier, is forced to navigate the treacherous waters of high society, as he takes on the role of head of a noble House. Alongside his eccentric sidekick, Wayne, and the brilliant and beautiful Marasi, Wax must unravel a complex web of conspiracy and deception that threatens to destroy the very fabric of Scadrial.

One of the standout features of Shadows of Self is its thought-provoking exploration of the human condition. Sanderson delves deep into the complexities of identity, power, and morality, raising questions about the nature of self and the consequences of our actions. Through Wax's struggles to reconcile his past and present selves, Sanderson sheds light on the fragility of human identity and the ease with which it can be manipulated.

Sanderson's writing style is, as always, a joy to behold. His prose is crisp and engaging, with a keen sense of pacing that propels the reader through the story. His world-building is meticulous and detailed, with a depth and richness that is unparalleled in modern fantasy. The magic system, which combines Allomancy and Feruchemy, is as complex and fascinating as ever, with a logic and consistency that is a hallmark of Sanderson's work.

The characters, too, are well-crafted and memorable, with Wax and Wayne forming a formidable partnership that is both humorous and endearing. Marasi, the brilliant and beautiful young constable, is a welcome addition to the cast, bringing a fresh perspective and a keen mind to the investigation.

In conclusion, Shadows of Self is a gripping and thought-provoking novel that is sure to delight fans of the Mistborn series. With its intricate plot, memorable characters, and meticulous world-building, it is a testament to Sanderson's skill as a storyteller. If you're a fan of fantasy, mystery, or adventure, then this book is a must-read.
