# North! or Be Eaten (The Wingfeather Saga, #2)

**Author:** Andrew Peterson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

In the world of fantasy literature, few authors have managed to capture the imaginations of readers quite like Andrew Peterson. His Wingfeather Saga, which began with "On the Edge of the Dark Sea of Darkness," continues with the thrilling and emotionally charged "North! Or Be Eaten." This second installment is a masterclass in storytelling, weaving together themes of family, love, and perseverance in the face of overwhelming danger.

At its core, "North! Or Be Eaten" is a story about the unbreakable bonds of family. Janner, Tink, and Leeli Igiby, the Lost Jewels of Anniera, are on the run from the evil forces that seek to destroy them. As they flee across treacherous landscapes and battle fearsome foes, they must also confront the internal struggles that threaten to tear them apart. Peterson's portrayal of the Igiby siblings is both authentic and heartbreaking, capturing the intricate web of emotions that defines family relationships.

One of Peterson's greatest strengths as an author is his ability to craft a richly detailed world that is both fantastical and familiar. The land of Aerwiar, with its strange creatures and mythical landscapes, is a place that readers will want to return to again and again. From the dark forests of Glipwood to the treacherous Stranders of the East Ben, each new location is meticulously crafted to transport readers to a world of wonder and magic.

But "North! Or Be Eaten" is more than just a thrilling adventure story – it's also a deeply thoughtful exploration of the human condition. Peterson's characters are multidimensional and complex, driven by motivations that are both noble and flawed. As they navigate the challenges of their journey, they must also confront the darker aspects of their own hearts, learning valuable lessons about forgiveness, sacrifice, and the true meaning of love.

Throughout the book, Peterson's writing is marked by a lyrical prose that is both beautiful and evocative. His use of language is deliberate and precise, conjuring vivid images that will linger in the reader's imagination long after the final page has been turned. Whether he's describing the majestic beauty of the Ice Prairies or the eerie menace of the Fangs of Dang, Peterson's writing is a constant source of wonder and delight.

In short, "North! Or Be Eaten" is a triumph of fantasy literature, a book that will captivate readers of all ages with its richly imagined world, memorable characters, and universal themes. If you're looking for a story that will inspire, entertain, and maybe even challenge your assumptions about the world, then look no further than Andrew Peterson's magnificent Wingfeather Saga.
