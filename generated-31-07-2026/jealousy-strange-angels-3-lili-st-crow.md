# Jealousy (Strange Angels, #3)

**Author:** Lili St. Crow  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

**Jealousy (Strange Angels #3) by Lili St. Crow: A Gritty, Heart-Pounding Battle for Survival**

Lili St. Crow’s *Jealousy*—the third installment in the *Strange Angels* series—is a visceral, high-stakes thriller that blends supernatural intrigue with raw, unflinching emotion. If you’ve ever wondered what happens when a fierce, street-smart protagonist like Dru Anderson steps into a world of ancient vampires, political betrayals, and deadly rivalries, this book delivers. St. Crow’s writing is sharp, immersive, and unapologetically dark, making *Jealousy* a standout in the YA paranormal genre.

At its core, *Jealousy* is a story about trust—and the cost of betrayal. Dru, a survivor with a knife in her boot and a bullet in her past, has spent her life looking out for herself. But when she arrives at the *schola Prima*, the elite training ground for djamphirs (a mix of vampires and humans with supernatural abilities), she quickly learns that survival isn’t just about fighting—it’s about knowing who to rely on. The Order, supposed protectors, are fractured by power struggles, and their leader, Christophe Reynard, is a man with secrets—and a past that might be more dangerous than Sergej’s bloodthirsty army.

The real tension comes from Anna, the new *svetocha* (a high-ranking vampire with control over the Order). Red-haired, ruthless, and utterly determined to eliminate Dru, Anna represents everything Dru hates: manipulation, cruelty, and the idea that someone else gets to decide her fate. Their rivalry isn’t just personal—it’s existential. Anna wants Christophe for herself, and she’ll burn the world down to keep him. Dru, meanwhile, is caught between loyalty to Christophe, distrust of the Order, and the terrifying realization that she might be the only one who can stop Sergej before he turns the world into his hunting ground.

St. Crow’s prose is a masterclass in tension. She doesn’t just tell you the danger—she *shows* it, with brutal efficiency. The fight scenes are visceral, the political maneuvering is slick, and the emotional stakes are relentless. Dru is a protagonist you can’t look away from: tough, flawed, and fiercely independent. She’s not a damsel in distress; she’s a survivor who’s been burned before and refuses to be played. But even she has her limits, and *Jealousy* forces her to confront the fact that some battles can’t be won alone.

What makes this book so compelling is its balance of action and depth. St. Crow doesn’t just drop Dru into a world of vampires and leave her to fend for herself—she gives her a reason to fight, a reason to care, and a reason to doubt. The supernatural elements are well-integrated, never feeling like gimmicks but rather extensions of the story’s themes of power, control, and survival. The vampires aren’t just monsters; they’re political players, with their own agendas, rivalries, and hidden motives.

The real strength of *Jealousy*, though, is its exploration of jealousy itself—a force that can destroy empires, shatter alliances, and turn allies into enemies. Anna’s obsession with Christophe isn’t just about love or power; it’s about possession, about the fear of losing what she believes is hers. And Dru? She’s caught in the crossfire, forced to question whether she can trust anyone, including the man she’s come to care for.

St. Crow’s writing is darkly humorous at times, adding a layer of edge to the story. There’s a gritty realism to the world she’s built, where no one is purely good or evil, and where survival often means making choices you’d rather not have to make. The pacing is relentless, with twists that keep you guessing and a sense of inevitability that makes the climax feel earned.

If you love YA with bite—books that don’t shy away from violence, betrayal, or complex characters—then *Jealousy* is a must-read. It’s not just another vampire story; it’s a battle for control, a fight for survival, and a story about the cost of trust. And in a world where everyone has something to hide, Dru Anderson is the only one who might just see through the lies.
