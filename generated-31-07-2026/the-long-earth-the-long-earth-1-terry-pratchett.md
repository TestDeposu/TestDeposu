# The Long Earth (The Long Earth, #1)

**Author:** Terry Pratchett  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

**Preview/Critique: The Long Earth by Terry Pratchett and Stephen Baxter**

In "The Long Earth," the first installment of a captivating new series, Terry Pratchett and Stephen Baxter weave a tale that masterfully blends science fiction, alternate history, and a dash of Pratchett's signature humor. The novel explores the tantalizing concept of parallel worlds, each a mere step away from our own, and the profound implications of discovering such a vast, unexplored multiverse.

The story begins in the trenches of World War I, where Private Percy Blakeney finds himself inexplicably transported to an eerily familiar yet unspoiled version of his war-torn world. Meanwhile, in present-day Wisconsin, police officer Monica Jansson stumbles upon a peculiar device in the ashes of a scientist's burned-down home. This simple gadget, a "stepper," allows its user to traverse the endless expanse of parallel Earths, each slightly different from the last, stretching out in an infinite "Long Earth."

Pratchett and Baxter deftly explore the consequences of this extraordinary discovery. As humanity begins to colonize the new worlds, they grapple with ethical dilemmas, environmental concerns, and the challenges of maintaining a global society when the globe has suddenly become infinite. The authors also delve into the political and economic implications, as powerful interests seek to control access to the Long Earth and its resources.

What sets "The Long Earth" apart is its unique blend of hard science fiction and whimsical storytelling. Baxter's scientific background lends the narrative a solid foundation in theoretical physics, while Pratchett's wit and satirical edge infuse the story with humor and heart. The result is a compelling exploration of humanity's insatiable curiosity and our enduring desire to reach for the stars, even when they turn out to be countless and within our grasp.

"The Long Earth" is a thought-provoking meditation on the nature of reality, the power of discovery, and the indomitable spirit of human exploration. It is a testament to the collaborative genius of two of science fiction's most accomplished authors, and a thrilling beginning to what promises to be an unforgettable series.
