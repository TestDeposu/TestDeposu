# The Problem Child (The Sisters Grimm, #3)

**Author:** Michael Buckley  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

The Problem Child, the third installment in Michael Buckley's beloved Sisters Grimm series, is a captivating tale that delves into the complexities of family, magic, and the power of sibling bonds. Buckley's masterful storytelling weaves a narrative that is both a thrilling adventure and a poignant exploration of the human condition.

At the heart of the story are Sabrina and Daphne Grimm, two sisters who have inherited the family legacy of being fairy-tale detectives. Their latest mission is to rescue their parents, who have been kidnapped by the sinister group of Everafters known as the Scarlet Hand. As Sabrina navigates the treacherous world of fairy-tale characters, she must confront her own doubts and fears, as well as the dark secrets of her family's past.

One of the most striking aspects of Buckley's writing style is his ability to balance action, humor, and pathos. The story is full of clever references to classic fairy tales, which will delight both children and adults who are familiar with the originals. At the same time, the author tackles complex themes such as the nature of good and evil, the consequences of playing with forces beyond one's control, and the difficulties of growing up and finding one's place in the world.

The character development in The Problem Child is also noteworthy. Sabrina, the older and more responsible sister, is a richly nuanced protagonist who struggles with her own flaws and weaknesses. Her relationship with Daphne, who is younger and more impulsive, is beautifully portrayed, and their interactions are filled with a deep affection and understanding. The supporting cast of characters, including a long-lost relative and a mysterious ally, adds depth and complexity to the story.

Buckley's use of magic as a plot device is also worthy of praise. In The Problem Child, magic is not just a convenient tool for solving problems, but a powerful force that comes with its own set of risks and consequences. The author's depiction of the magical world is vivid and immersive, with a keen sense of wonder and enchantment.

Overall, The Problem Child is a compelling and engaging read that will appeal to fans of fantasy, adventure, and mystery. Buckley's writing is engaging, witty, and insightful, making this book a must-read for anyone who loves a good story. With its positive themes, memorable characters, and exciting plot, The Problem Child is a great choice for readers of all ages.
