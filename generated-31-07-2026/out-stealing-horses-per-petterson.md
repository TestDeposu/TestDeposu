# Out Stealing Horses

**Author:** Per Petterson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

In "Out Stealing Horses," Per Petterson weaves a poignant and introspective narrative that delves into the complexities of human relationships, loss, and the passage of time. The story revolves around Trond, a 67-year-old man who has retreated to a secluded cabin in the easternmost region of Norway, seeking solace and quiet contemplation. However, a chance encounter with his neighbor disrupts his tranquility, prompting Trond to reminisce about a pivotal summer from his past, one that forever altered the course of his life.

Through Trond's recollections, Petterson masterfully unfolds a narrative that juxtaposes the carefree innocence of adolescence with the harsh realities of adulthood. The summer of 1948, when Trond was 15, was a time of unbridled freedom and adventure, marked by escapades with his friend Jon, including the ill-fated horse-stealing incident that sets off a chain of events with far-reaching consequences. As the story unfolds, Petterson skillfully exposes the intricacies of Trond's relationships with those around him, particularly his father and Jon, revealing the subtle yet profound ways in which they shape his identity and inform his worldview.

Petterson's prose is characterized by a lyrical simplicity, which belies the complexity and depth of the narrative. His writing is economical, yet evocative, conjuring the rugged beauty of the Norwegian landscape and the inner lives of his characters with equal precision. The author's use of language is deliberate and measured, reflecting Trond's own introspective nature and inviting the reader to immerse themselves in his world.

One of the most striking aspects of "Out Stealing Horses" is its exploration of the human experience, particularly the ways in which our past experiences continue to resonate throughout our lives. Petterson's portrayal of Trond's struggles to come to terms with his past, and the losses that have shaped him, is both poignant and profound. The novel raises important questions about the nature of identity, the power of memory, and the human capacity for resilience and forgiveness.

Ultimately, "Out Stealing Horses" is a novel about the fragility and beauty of human relationships, and the ways in which they can both sustain and devastate us. Petterson's masterful storytelling and nuanced characterization make for a compelling and deeply moving reading experience. This is a novel that will linger in the reader's mind long after the final page has been turned, its themes and characters continuing to resonate like the gentle lapping of waves against the shore.
