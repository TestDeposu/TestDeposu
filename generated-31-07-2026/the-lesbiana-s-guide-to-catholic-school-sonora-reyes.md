# The Lesbiana's Guide to Catholic School

**Author:** Sonora Reyes  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

Navigating the turbulent waters of late adolescence is a universal literary rite of passage, but few contemporary authors capture its chaotic, messy beauty quite like Sonora Reyes. Following their critically acclaimed, multi-award-winning debut, Reyes returns with a poignant and searingly honest companion novel that expands the universe of their previous work while standing firmly on its own emotional ground. Centered on the beloved character Cesar Flores, this latest release is a triumph of contemporary young adult fiction, offering a sharp, compassionate, and deeply resonant look at the intersections of queer identity, mental health, and faith.

At seventeen, Cesar is a young man desperately trying to glue together the fractured pieces of his life. Having recently ended things with his ex-boyfriend Jamal in a misguided, internalized bid to stay in the closet, Cesar has since made monumental strides. He has come out to his mother, his fierce sister Yami, and his trusted circle of friends. He takes his medication faithfully and even holds his therapist's blessing to pursue a reconciliation with Jamal. On paper, Cesar is ready for a fresh start. 

Yet, fiction is rarely lived on paper, and Reyes understands this intimately. Beneath Cesar’s polished veneer and sharp, defensive wit lies a storm of Catholic guilt and intrusive psychological pressure that the narrative aptly dubs "The Thoughts." As these internal pressures mount, Cesar falls victim to the insidious lie that he is fundamentally unworthy of love—whether from Jamal or from anyone else. 

What makes Reyes’s authorial style so striking in this novel is the unflinching honesty with which they handle complex psychological realities. When Cesar experiences a manic episode that shatters his carefully constructed defenses, the book refuses to offer an easy, sanitized Hollywood resolution. Instead, Reyes dives headfirst into the mortifying, unglamorous vulnerability of mental health struggles. The narrative explores how shame operates as a cage, and how humor and academic overachievement—being labeled the "gifted" kid—can serve as elaborate hiding spots for profound inner turmoil. 

Reyes writes with a rare combination of razor-sharp wit and profound empathy. Even when Cesar is making mistakes that frustrate the reader, his voice remains utterly compelling, authentically teenage, and deeply human. The dialogue snaps with contemporary realism, grounding the heavy themes of bipolar disorder and religious trauma in the recognizable, everyday banter of high school life. 

The central theme of the novel pivots on a terrifying question: Is the mortifying vulnerability of being truly loved by the people you have hurt a risk worth taking? Reyes answers this not with a platitude, but with a messy, hard-fought journey toward grace. Through Cesar, the novel deconstructs the toxic myth of perfectionism that so often plagues high-achieving queer youth, particularly within culturally conservative or religious households. It reminds us that healing is not linear, and that asking for help is often the bravest act a person can perform.

This companion novel cements Sonora Reyes as an essential voice in modern young adult literature. It is a book that will make readers laugh out loud on one page and tear up on the next. By shining a brilliant, unsparing light on the realities of living with a bipolar diagnosis while navigating queer boyhood, Reyes has crafted a vital story of redemption, self-forgiveness, and the transformative power of community. It is a stunning, unforgettable read that resonates long after the final page.
