# Remy (Real, #3)

**Author:** Katy Evans  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

Title: Inside the Mind of the Riptide: A Review of Katy Evans’s Remy

When Katy Evans first unleashed the brooding, tattooed underground fighter Remington “Riptide” Tate upon the New Romance world in *Real*, readers were instantly captivated. Yet, despite the intense passion that defined the early installments of the series, Remington remained an enigma—a man whose inner workings were largely viewed through the adoring eyes of his heroine, Brooke Dumas. With the 2013 release of *Remy*, Evans takes a bold and necessary narrative gamble, stepping away from the traditional dual-perspective or singular heroine focus to deliver a full-length companion novel told entirely from the hero's point of view. The result is a striking, visceral exploration of obsession, vulnerability, and the tumultuous architecture of a deeply troubled mind.

The central theme of *Remy* is not merely romance, though the chemistry between Remington and Brooke remains incendiary. Instead, the book delves deeply into the theme of psychological preservation through love. Remington Tate is a man at war with his own psyche. Beneath his lethal exterior and the roaring crowds of the underground fighting circuit lies a fragile internal ecosystem plagued by darkness, erratic moods, and a relentless fear of losing control. Evans uses Remington’s unfiltered voice to examine how deeply a person can anchor themselves to another human being. For Remy, Brooke is not just a romantic interest; she is his oxygen, his anchor, and the only "real" thing in a life dictated by violence, adrenaline, and transient fame. 

What makes *Remy* stylistically compelling is Evans’s commitment to capturing the erratic cadence of her protagonist's thoughts. The author’s style has always favored high-octane pacing and emotional immediacy, but here, it adopts a more frantic, urgent tone. Remington’s internal monologue is fragmented yet fiercely protective, oscillating between a profound tenderness for Brooke and the primal, aggressive instincts that fuel his fighting career. Evans does not shy away from portraying the less glamorous aspects of his obsession. Remy’s need to possess, protect, and consume Brooke borders on manic, painting a portrait of a man whose love is as consuming as a wildfire. For readers who enjoy intense, alpha-hero dynamics driven by psychological depth, this stylistic shift offers a thrilling re-contextualization of events previously seen only from the outside.

Admittedly, companion novels from the male perspective can sometimes suffer from redundancy, merely retelling established plot points with a different coat of paint. However, Evans circumvents this trap by leaning heavily into the psychological horror of Remy’s inner demons. We witness the exact moment he fixates on Brooke—the genesis of an obsession that would alter the trajectory of both their lives. By laying bare his insecurities, his silent panic attacks, and the sheer terror of his mental instability, Evans transforms what could have been a simple cash-grab spinoff into a vital, emotional puzzle piece that completes the series' overarching arc. 

*Remy* is a breathless, unapologetic dive into the psyche of one of romance fiction's most iconic fighters. It is fast-paced, emotionally draining, and undeniably seductive. Katy Evans successfully proves that behind the tattoos, the fists, and the swagger lies a man desperately fighting his greatest battle yet: the battle for his own sanity and the heart of the woman who saved him. For fans of the *Real* series, this book is not merely an optional companion; it is the beating heart of the entire narrative, finally laid bare for all to see.
