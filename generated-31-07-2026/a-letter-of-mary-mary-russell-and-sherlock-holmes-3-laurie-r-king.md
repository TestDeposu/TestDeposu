# A Letter of Mary (Mary Russell and Sherlock Holmes, #3)

**Author:** Laurie R. King  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

In "A Letter of Mary," Laurie R. King masterfully weaves a complex tapestry of intrigue, theology, and intellectual curiosity, transporting readers to the sun-drenched Sussex estate of Mary Russell Holmes and her illustrious husband, Sherlock Holmes. This third installment in the Mary Russell series is a thought-provoking and thrilling adventure that delves into the very fabric of faith, history, and the human condition.

At its core, the novel revolves around an ancient manuscript, purportedly penned by Mary Magdalene, which hints at her status as an apostle. This explosive revelation sets off a chain reaction of events, drawing Mary and Sherlock into a world of high-stakes politics, theological debates, and, ultimately, murder. As the investigation unfolds, King's writing is akin to a richly layered puzzle, replete with clever misdirection, unexpected twists, and ingenious deductions.

One of the most striking aspects of King's narrative is her remarkable ability to balance action, suspense, and intellectual curiosity. Her writing is effortlessly engaging, shifting seamlessly between moments of high-octane tension and passages of erudite insight. The author's meticulous research and attention to historical detail are evident throughout, lending an air of authenticity to the proceedings.

The characterizations of Mary and Sherlock are, as ever, superbly nuanced. King's portrayal of their partnership is a masterclass in subtlety, conveying the deep emotional intimacy and intellectual symbiosis that underpins their relationship. Mary, in particular, shines as a fiercely intelligent and independent protagonist, her voice ringing clear and true throughout the narrative.

Throughout the novel, King's prose is a joy to behold – witty, incisive, and possessed of a dry, observational humor that is reminiscent of the great detective himself. Her writing is also notable for its thematic depth, exploring the intersection of faith, history, and personal identity with sensitivity and intelligence.

If there is a criticism to be made, it is that the novel's supporting cast can feel slightly one-dimensional at times, serving primarily to advance the plot or provide expository context. However, this is a minor quibble in an otherwise exceptional work of fiction.

In conclusion, "A Letter of Mary" is a triumph – a captivating, clever, and deeply satisfying novel that will appeal to fans of historical fiction, mystery, and intellectual puzzles. King's writing is a testament to the enduring power of the Sherlockian canon, while also showcasing her own unique voice and vision. This is a novel that will linger in the reader's mind long after the final page has been turned.
