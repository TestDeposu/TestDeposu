# Of Poseidon (The Syrena Legacy, #1)

**Author:** Anna Banks  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

In "Of Poseidon", the debut novel of author Anna Banks, the boundaries between the sea and the land begin to blur as Galen, a brooding and enigmatic Syrena prince, sets out on a perilous quest to find a mysterious girl with a gift that could change the fate of his kingdom. It is on the sun-kissed shores of a small coastal town that Galen discovers Emma, a carefree and spirited teenager whose life is about to be turned upside down by the arrival of this mysterious and charismatic stranger.

From the opening pages, Banks weaves a spellbinding narrative that defies easy categorization, blending elements of fantasy, romance, and adventure to create a story that is at once captivating and unpredictable. As Galen and Emma navigate the complexities of their relationship, which is as intense as it is forbidden, they find themselves embroiled in a web of intrigue and danger that threatens the very foundations of their respective worlds.

One of the greatest strengths of "Of Poseidon" is its vivid and immersive world-building, which brings to life a rich and intricate mythology that is both fantastical and grounded in naturalist principles. Banks' portrayal of the Syrenas, with their shimmering scales and mesmerizing melodies, is at once captivating and believable, drawing the reader into a world of wonder and enchantment.

Banks' prose is also noteworthy for its lyricism and sensuality, which brings a dreamlike quality to the narrative. Her descriptions of the sea and its creatures are so vivid, so alive, that they seem to leap off the page, transporting the reader to a world of breathtaking beauty and danger. At the same time, her portrayal of the complex and often fraught relationships between Galen and Emma, who are drawn to each other like moths to a flame, is both deeply moving and intensely compelling.

But what truly sets "Of Poseidon" apart is its exploration of the central theme of identity, which is at the very heart of the story. As Galen and Emma navigate the complexities of their relationship, they are forced to confront the very limits of their own identities, which are shaped by their respective worlds and the expectations that come with them. Will they find a way to reconcile their differences, or will they succumb to the pressure of the world around them?

Banks' handling of this theme is both nuanced and insightful, drawing on a deep understanding of the tensions and contradictions that often lie beneath the surface of even the most seemingly stable relationships. Her portrayal of the ways in which Galen and Emma struggle to reconcile their love for each other with the expectations and obligations of their respective worlds is both deeply moving and intensely relatable.

Ultimately, "Of Poseidon" is a story about the transformative power of love, which can bridge even the widest of chasms and overcome even the greatest of obstacles. It is a tale of self-discovery, identity, and the courage to follow one's heart, even when the consequences are uncertain. As such, it is a story that will resonate with readers of all ages, from teenagers to adults, who will find themselves drawn into a world of wonder and enchantment that is both timeless and timely.

In short, "Of Poseidon" is a sweeping and romantic tale that will captivate readers from the very first page. With its vivid world-building, lyrical prose, and exploration of complex themes, it is a story that is sure to leave a lasting impression on anyone who reads it.
