# Nice Girls Don't Have Fangs (Jane Jameson, #1)

**Author:** Molly Harper  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

If you thought the vampire literary landscape was entirely overrun by brooding, tragic immortals who sparkle in the sunlight or weep over centuries of lost love, Molly Harper is here to offer a delightfully refreshing stake to the heart of urban fantasy conventions. In her 2009 debut, Nice Girls Don't Have Fangs, Harper delivers a wry, delicious, and laugh-out-loud romp that trades gothic melodrama for small-town Southern charm, wit, and a heavy dose of potato skins.

The novel introduces us to Jane Jameson, a librarian whose life unravels with spectacular comedic timing. Fired by a tyrannical boss and compensated with a paltry twenty-five-dollar gift certificate to a local eatery, Jane does what any self-respecting, repressed nice girl would do: she embarks on a legendary bender. Unfortunately, her celebratory stumble home results in her being mistaken for a deer, shot, and left for dead. Enter a mysterious stranger, a transfusion of the vampiric variety, and a brand-new unlife in the quirky, gossipy town of Half Moon Hollow. 

At its core, the main theme of Nice Girls Don't Have Fangs is the hilarious and poignant negotiation of identity in the face of radical transformation. Harper uses vampirism not as a metaphor for dark, forbidden romance, but rather as an absurdly inconvenient lifestyle change. Jane is not initially thrilled about her promotion to the undead ranks. She must navigate the indignities of faking breathing at family dinners, trading her beloved Southern comfort food for plastic bags of O-negative, and dealing with a ghostly roommate who happens to be her favorite late aunt. Through Jane, Harper explores how we cling to our humanity—our quirks, our manners, and our favorite comfort foods—even when our biology undergoes a terrifying upgrade. Jane’s journey is one of reclaiming her agency, moving from a submissive, underappreciated librarian to an undead woman who refuses to be pushed around, whether by living bosses or murderous shadow figures.

Harper’s authorial style is the true secret weapon of this novel. Her voice is breezy, self-deprecating, and sharply observant. She writes with an effortless comedic timing that pokes fun at the tropes of the paranormal romance genre while simultaneously embracing them. The prose is snappy and conversational, immediately establishing an intimate rapport with the reader, as if Jane were venting to you over a cocktail (or a chilled bag of plasma). The dialogue crackles with snappy comebacks and sparkling banter, particularly in Jane’s fraught, electric interactions with her mercurial vampire sire. 

Furthermore, Harper excels at world-building through local color. Half Moon Hollow feels lived-in and vibrant, populated by a hilarious supporting cast that grounds the supernatural elements in recognizable, small-town reality. The introduction of the Newly Undead Welcoming Committee—complete with a welcome basket—is a masterstroke of satirical humor that sets the tone for the entire series. 

While the plot does feature a mystery involving a series of local vampire murders that threaten to frame Jane, the true joy of the book lies in the character moments and the sheer entertainment value of Jane’s internal monologue. Harper never takes herself too seriously, and that infectious enthusiasm translates into a page-turning experience that leaves the reader grinning from ear to ear. 

Nice Girls Don't Have Fangs is a triumphant subversion of paranormal tropes. It proves that you don't need a heavy, brooding atmosphere to tell a compelling vampire story. With its winning blend of Southern hospitality, sharp wit, and supernatural chaos, Molly Harper’s debut is an absolute must-read for anyone looking to sink their teeth into something delightfully original and wickedly fun.
