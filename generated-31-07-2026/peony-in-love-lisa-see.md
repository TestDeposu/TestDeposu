# Peony in Love

**Author:** Lisa See  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

In seventeenth-century China, the boundary between the living and the dead was often as thin as a sheet of rice paper. Lisa See’s 2007 triumph, *Peony in Love*, explores this fragile threshold with breathtaking grace, transporting readers to the opulent yet suffocating confines of an elite villa on the shores of Hangzhou’s West Lake. At its heart, the novel is a profound meditation on the necessity of self-expression, the intoxicating weight of desire, and a love so fierce it refuses to be contained by mortality.

The narrative centers on Peony, a sheltered fifteen-year-old girl whose life changes forever the night she catches a glimpse of a handsome stranger, poet Mingcai, during a garden performance of the classic opera *The Peony Pavilion*. Consumed by a passion that mirrors the tragic heroine of the play, Peony embarks on a journey of longing that ultimately blurs the lines between reality, literature, and the afterlife. When tragedy strikes and Peony wastes away, she transitions into the realm of hungry ghosts. Yet, her story does not end at death; rather, it evolves. As a spirit, she must navigate the complexities of the afterworld, grapple with the profound power of written words—which can both heal and destroy—and find a way to make her silent voice heard across the eternal divide.

See’s authorial style is nothing short of mesmerizing. Known for her meticulous historical research, she weaves actual historical events and cultural practices seamlessly into a dense, romantic tapestry. Her prose is quietly beautiful, echoing the lyrical cadence of classical Chinese poetry while maintaining an emotional immediacy that sneaks directly into the reader’s heart. She paints seventeenth-century domestic life with vivid sensory details, contrasting the exquisite beauty of silk robes, garden lanterns, and fragrant teas with the crushing patriarchal constraints placed upon women of the era. 

The central theme of the novel is the urgent quest for female agency and voice. Through Peony and the generations of women who surround her, See examines how art, literature, and storytelling serve as lifelines for those trapped by societal expectations. The book is an electrifying exploration of female relationships—capturing the ways women help, hurt, and misunderstand one another across both earthly and spiritual realms. Much like Alice Sebold’s *The Lovely Bones*, See crafts a ghostly narrator who is startlingly empathetic, immediate, and utterly human. 

*Peony in Love* is a transporting read that rewards the patient reader with a rich cultural education and an emotionally resonant story. It asks timeless questions about the meaning of love and the influence of art, proving that true passion transcends even death. For anyone seeking a lush, haunting historical drama that will make both the heart and mind ache, Lisa See’s masterwork is an absolute must-read.
