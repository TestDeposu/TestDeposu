# Perfect Timing (Kendrick/Coulter/Harrigan, #11)

**Author:** Catherine Anderson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

**A Love That Defies the Cold: *Perfect Timing* by Catherine Anderson**

Catherine Anderson’s *Perfect Timing* is a novel that sears into the heart like a winter wind—raw, tender, and impossible to ignore. Set against the stark beauty of Idaho’s snow-laden nights, this story is a masterclass in emotional vulnerability, where love isn’t a choice but a desperate, life-affirming necessity. At its core, *Perfect Timing* is a tale of survival—not just physical, but of the soul. Maggie Stanley, a woman shattered by betrayal and grief, flees into the night with her infant daughter, clutching to the fragile hope that somewhere, in the quiet corners of the world, there might still be warmth. What she finds in Rafe Kendrick is more than shelter; it’s a reckoning with the possibility of redemption.

Anderson’s prose is a delicate balance of grit and grace. Her descriptions of the Idaho wilderness are vivid, almost tactile—the way snow muffles sound, the way the cold seeps into bones, the way a single candle flickers against the dark. But it’s her characters who truly breathe. Maggie is a woman we ache for, her pain etched into every line of her story. She’s not a victim, but a survivor, her resilience forged in the fires of loss. Rafe, on the other hand, is a man of contradictions—a stranger with secrets, a protector with a past, a man who has sworn off love yet can’t resist its pull. Their dynamic is electric, charged with the kind of tension that makes the reader’s pulse quicken. Anderson doesn’t shy away from the messiness of their relationship; she lays bare the fear, the doubt, and the quiet moments of tenderness that prove love can be both a weapon and a balm.

The novel’s strength lies in its exploration of trust. Maggie has spent her life believing men are liars, yet she finds herself drawn to Rafe’s quiet strength, his unspoken understanding. Rafe, for his part, has built a wall around his heart, convinced that love is a luxury he can’t afford. Their journey together is a slow unraveling of those walls, a testament to the idea that sometimes, the people who break us are also the ones who mend us. Anderson doesn’t offer easy answers; she presents the reader with the messy, beautiful reality of healing. There are no grand gestures, no fairy-tale resolutions—just the quiet, hard-won truth that family, in all its imperfect forms, is the one thing that can make the cold world feel a little less lonely.

What makes *Perfect Timing* stand out is its emotional honesty. Anderson doesn’t romanticize pain; she acknowledges it, sits with it, and shows how it can be transformed by love. The story is a reminder that sometimes, the perfect timing isn’t in the stars, but in the moments we choose to reach out, to take a risk, to believe that even when the world feels broken, there’s still a chance for something new. It’s a novel that lingers, that stays with you long after the last page, because it’s not just a story about love—it’s a story about the courage to hope again.

For readers who love tales of second chances, emotional depth, and the quiet power of redemption, *Perfect Timing* is a must-read. Catherine Anderson crafts a story that is as much about survival as it is about the beauty of finding your way back to the light.
