# The Seven Sisters (The Seven Sisters, #1)

**Author:** Lucinda Riley  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

Lucinda Riley’s international bestseller, The Seven Sisters, is a masterclass in modern historical fiction, launching what has quickly become a beloved epic saga. At its core, the novel explores a universal yet deeply personal theme: the relentless human pursuit of identity and belonging. When the enigmatic billionaire philanthropist known to his adopted daughters as Pa Salt passes away, he leaves behind not just grief, but a cryptic legacy. Each of the six sisters—gathered at their secluded Swiss estate, Atlantis—is handed an armillary sphere inscribed with coordinates and a singular clue to her true origins. This clever premise sets off a global treasure hunt of the self, transforming a story of familial loss into a profound meditation on where we come from and how our ancestors shape who we are.

Riley’s storytelling style is nothing short of hypnotic, seamlessly bridging contemporary drama with sweeping historical romance. The narrative is constructed as a dual-timeline puzzle. In this first installment, we follow the eldest sister, Maia D’Aplièse, as her clue pulls her away from her comfort zone and drops her into the vibrant, sun-drenched chaos of 1920s Rio de Janeiro. Riley treats us to a richly textured literary journey that effortlessly pivots between modern-day introspection and historical grandeur. 

What elevates Riley’s prose is her meticulous attention to atmosphere and historical context. When she transports the reader to Rio de Janeiro and the bohemian cafes of Montparnasse in Paris, you can practically smell the coffee, feel the Parisian rain, and hear the chisel striking stone. The historical subplot—centering on the spirited Izabela Bonifacio and the creation of the iconic Christ the Redeemer statue—is woven into the modern narrative with remarkable dexterity. Riley uses historical landmarks not merely as static backdrops, but as living, breathing catalysts for personal transformation. Through Izabela’s passionate rebellion against aristocratic constraints, Riley examines themes of female agency, artistic ambition, and all-consuming love.

While the premise leans heavily on romantic melodrama, Riley grounds the emotional stakes in authentic character development. Maia is not just a passive observer of her family tree; her journey to Brazil becomes a mirror for her own emotional paralysis, forcing her to step out of the shadows of her past. The pacing is deliberate and immersive, designed to make the reader linger in each era alongside the characters.

Ultimately, The Seven Sisters is a dazzling tapestry of love, loss, and legacy. Lucinda Riley has crafted a spellbinding portal into a larger universe, leaving readers not only satisfied with Maia’s resolution, but utterly desperate to unlock the secrets of the remaining sisters. It is a triumphant opening act for a saga that proves our roots run much deeper than we ever imagined.
