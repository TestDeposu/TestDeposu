# At Peace (The 'Burg, #2)

**Author:** Kristen Ashley  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

Kristen Ashley has carved out a distinct, fiercely loyal corner in the romance genre, and her 2011 release At Peace, the second installment in The Burg series, stands as a quintessential example of her signature storytelling. Clocking in with her trademark sprawling narrative style, Ashley delivers a heavy dose of emotional turbulence, hyper-masculine protection, and high-stakes drama. At its core, At Peace is a deeply resonant exploration of second chances, exploring the arduous journey of picking up the shattered pieces of a life and daring to love again under the heavy shadow of profound grief.

The narrative thrust of the novel centers on Violet Winters, a woman intimately acquainted with the cruelest caprices of fate. Having already lost her world to a violent crime, Violet is a heroine defined by resilience, though her armor is understandably thick. Enter Joe Callahan: a scarred, intimidating security specialist who operates as the human embodiment of the classic alpha-hero archetype. Their initial meeting—sparked by a late-night noise complaint—ignites a raw, consuming chemistry that defies easy dismissal. Ashley captures the intoxicating rush of sudden desire, anchoring the physical heat in an emotional necessity. For Violet, giving in to Joe isn't just about satisfying a hunger; it is a terrifying step toward reclaiming her right to live, rather than merely survive.

However, Ashley’s signature style thrives on emotional obstacles, and Joe comes packaged with a heavy baggage train. Haunted by soul-wrenching secrets from his past that have left him emotionally bankrupt, Joe is determined to remain an island. His abrupt retreat after pulling Violet into his orbit delivers a crushing blow, setting up the classic romance trope of the reluctant, wounded hero who must be dragged into redemption. This dynamic showcases Ashley’s penchant for melodrama. The emotional stakes are dialed up to maximum volume, ensuring that every misunderstanding, heartbreak, and pang of longing reverberates with dramatic intensity.

What elevates At Peace beyond a standard contemporary romance is the seamless integration of suspense with deep emotional character work. Just as Violet attempts to reconcile her heartbreak, the ghosts of her past resurface. The obsessive killer responsible for her first husband’s murder returns to haunt her, injecting a palpable sense of danger that shifts the novel into high gear. This external threat forces Joe to confront his inner demons and realize that his emotional isolation is a luxury he can no longer afford. The resulting rescue-and-reconciliation arc is vintage Kristen Ashley: intense, fiercely protective, and ultimately deeply satisfying.

Ashley’s writing style is unapologetically maximalist. She is an author who eschews brevity in favor of immersive world-building, exhaustive dialogue, and meticulously detailed emotional processing. Critics of the genre might find the pacing leisurely and the character dynamics occasionally entrenched in traditional gender roles, but her fans know precisely what they are signing up for: a richly textured community, formidable and flawed characters, and a love story that fights tooth and nail for its happy-ever-after. 

In At Peace, Ashley proves adept at balancing the sweet with the searing. The central theme resonates powerfully because it acknowledges that true healing is not about forgetting the past, but about gathering the courage to risk everything for a future. For readers who revel in emotionally demanding romances where the stakes are life-or-death and the alpha heroes love with a fierce, possessive desperation, Kristen Ashley’s second trip into The Burg delivers an unforgettable, heart-pounding ride.
