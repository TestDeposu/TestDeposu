# White Witch, Black Curse (The Hollows, #7)

**Author:** Kim Harrison  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

**White Witch, Black Curse: A Dark Fantasy Masterpiece Where Vengeance Burns Bright**

Kim Harrison’s *White Witch, Black Curse*—the seventh installment in *The Hollows* series—is a storm of raw emotion, razor-sharp wit, and visceral fantasy, where the line between justice and vengeance blurs into something far more dangerous. This isn’t just another installment in a sprawling saga; it’s a reckoning, a descent into the underbelly of a world where witches, vampires, and other supernatural beings navigate power, betrayal, and the cost of survival. With the death of her lover still fresh and the culprits lurking in the shadows, bounty hunter Rachel Morgan—one of fantasy’s most compelling antiheroes—embarks on a mission that will test her limits, her loyalty, and her very soul.

At its core, *White Witch, Black Curse* is a story about the weight of grief and the seduction of vengeance. Rachel isn’t just hunting the monsters who wronged her; she’s hunting the ghosts of her past, the ones that haunt her nights and whisper promises of retribution. Harrison masterfully weaves this personal vendetta into the larger tapestry of *The Hollows*, where political intrigue, ancient curses, and supernatural warfare collide. The novel doesn’t shy away from the brutality of its themes—there’s no clean resolution, no easy victories. Instead, it forces its characters, and its readers, to confront the messy, often ugly consequences of their choices.

What makes this book so gripping is Harrison’s signature style: a perfect blend of dark humor, sharp dialogue, and unflinching realism. Rachel Morgan is a force of nature—a woman who’s seen too much, lost too much, and refuses to back down. Her voice is as distinctive as it is compelling, crackling with sarcasm one moment and raw vulnerability the next. The world of *The Hollows* feels lived-in, its politics as tangled as its magic, and the supernatural creatures—vampires, werewolves, witches—are anything but one-dimensional. They’re flawed, ambitious, and often terrifyingly human, which makes their conflicts feel all the more visceral.

The pacing is relentless, with Harrison balancing action-packed sequences against quieter, introspective moments that dig deep into Rachel’s psyche. There’s a sense of inevitability here, as if the characters are being pulled along by forces beyond their control, yet they fight tooth and nail against their fates. The novel’s dark fantasy elements—curses, blood magic, and ancient rivalries—are woven seamlessly into the narrative, never feeling like gimmicks but rather as organic extensions of the world’s history and power struggles.

One of the most striking aspects of *White Witch, Black Curse* is its refusal to offer easy answers. Rachel’s quest for vengeance isn’t just about justice; it’s about closure, about making sense of loss in a world that often feels indifferent. The novel doesn’t romanticize her rage, nor does it let her off the hook for the damage she inflicts. Instead, it presents a complex portrait of a woman who is both victim and perpetrator, a theme that resonates deeply in a genre often obsessed with clear-cut heroes and villains.

Harrison’s prose is lean, efficient, and packed with attitude. She doesn’t waste words on unnecessary flourishes; instead, she delivers sharp, punchy lines that land like punches. The dialogue crackles with energy, whether it’s Rachel’s snarky one-liners or the more serious, heartfelt exchanges that reveal the depth of her relationships. Even the secondary characters—from the cunning vampires to the morally ambiguous witches—are given enough depth to feel like real people, not just pawns in a larger game.

For fans of dark fantasy, *White Witch, Black Curse* is a must-read. It’s the kind of book that stays with you long after the last page, its themes of grief, vengeance, and redemption echoing in the mind. It’s also a testament to Harrison’s ability to craft a world that feels both expansive and intimate, where every character has a story, and every choice has consequences.

If you’ve followed Rachel Morgan’s journey from the beginning, you’ll find this installment to be a satisfying, if bittersweet, continuation of her arc. And if you’re new to *The Hollows*, you’ll be hooked almost immediately by the sheer energy and originality of the series. Whether you’re here for the action, the emotional depth, or the unapologetic complexity of its protagonist, *White Witch, Black Curse* delivers in spades.

In the end, this isn’t just a story about hunting monsters—it’s about the monsters we become when we’re broken, and the lengths we’ll go to in order to piece ourselves back together.
