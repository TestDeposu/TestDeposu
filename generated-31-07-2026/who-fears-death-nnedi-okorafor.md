# Who Fears Death

**Author:** Nnedi Okorafor  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

In "Who Fears Death," Nnedi Okorafor masterfully weaves a captivating tale that delves into the complexities of trauma, identity, and the resilience of the human spirit. Set against the backdrop of a post-apocalyptic Africa, Okorafor's story is both a sweeping epic and a deeply personal exploration of the human condition.

At its core, "Who Fears Death" is the story of Onyesonwu, a young girl born of rape and abandoned by her community. As an Ewu, or child of rape, Onye is expected to live a life of violence and rejection. But Okorafor's protagonist defies these expectations, emerging as a remarkable and powerful young woman with a unique magic that sets her apart from her peers.

Throughout the novel, Okorafor effortlessly blends elements of African mythology, history, and culture to create a rich and immersive world that is both fantastical and grounded in reality. Her writing is lyrical and evocative, conjuring vivid images of the desert landscapes and the spiritual realm that Onye inhabits.

One of the most striking aspects of "Who Fears Death" is its exploration of the ways in which trauma can shape and define us. Onye's experiences, from her abandonment to her subsequent abuse and mistreatment, are deeply painful and thought-provoking. And yet, Okorafor handles these themes with sensitivity and nuance, avoiding melodrama and sentimentality in favor of a grounded and realistic portrayal of the human experience.

At the same time, the novel is a powerful exploration of identity and belonging. Onye's journey is one of self-discovery, as she struggles to come to terms with her unique gifts and her place in the world. Her relationships with others – particularly her mentor, a wise and compassionate woman named Mwenge – serve as a testament to the power of love and acceptance in the face of adversity.

Okorafor's writing style is characterized by its lyricism and its willingness to experiment with language. Her prose is often poetic and evocative, conjuring vivid images of the world she's created. At the same time, her writing is clear and accessible, making the novel an easy read for even the most seasoned fantasy fan.

If there's a criticism to be made, it's that the novel's pacing can be a bit uneven at times. Okorafor's world-building is extensive, and she takes care to establish a rich and complex history for her characters. But this can sometimes lead to a sense of overwhelm, as the reader struggles to keep pace with the myriad characters, plot threads, and cultural references.

Overall, however, "Who Fears Death" is a remarkable achievement – a sweeping epic that explores the complexities of trauma, identity, and the human spirit. Okorafor's writing is breathtaking in its scope and ambition, and her world-building is both meticulous and innovative. This is a novel that will stay with you long after you finish reading it, haunting your imagination and challenging your assumptions.

As Okorafor's protagonist might say, "Who Fears Death?" is a question that gets to the heart of what it means to be human. And in this remarkable novel, Okorafor offers a powerful and thought-provoking answer.
