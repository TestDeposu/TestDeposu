# The Dentist (DS Cross Mysteries, #1)

**Author:** Tim Sullivan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

Meet Detective Sergeant George Cross: a man who views the world through a lens of absolute logic, unyielding precision, and uncompromising truth. In The Dentist, the explosive US debut of Tim Sullivan’s million-copy bestselling UK phenomenon, readers are introduced to a protagonist who shatters every conventional trope of the modern police procedural. Cross is on the autism spectrum, a trait that makes everyday human interactions a labyrinth of unspoken rules, yet it is this exact neurodivergence—his hyper-focus, obsessive attention to detail, and fierce moral compass—that grants him an astonishing 97 percent conviction rate. He is not just a detective; he is a human sorting machine designed to extract order from chaos. 

The central theme of the novel hinges on the profound dignity of the forgotten. When the lifeless body of an elderly, destitute man is discovered discarded on the edge of Bristol’s affluent Clifton Downs, the local police force is quick to write it off as a tragic, random act of violence targeting society's invisible margins. But George Cross does not do assumptions. To Cross, a discarded life demands just as much meticulous investigation as any other. Driven by an innate sense of justice rather than political expediency, he digs relentlessly into the victim's past, unearthing a haunting connection to a cold case stretching back fifteen years. The very tragedy that stripped the victim of his home and dignity, Cross suspects, is the very reason someone finally decided to silence him for good.

Tim Sullivan’s writing style is a masterclass in controlled pacing and character-driven storytelling. Eschewing the hyper-stylized grit and sensory overload typical of many contemporary crime thrillers, Sullivan adopts a crisp, observant, and deeply empathetic prose style that mirrors the mind of his protagonist. The narrative does not merely tell us about Cross’s internal world; it seamlessly immerses us in it. We experience the world through the friction between Cross’s literal-mindedness and the messy, emotionally charged reality of police work. 

A vital anchor for the novel is the dynamic between Cross and his partner, DS Josie Drake. Sullivan handles their partnership with immense subtlety, avoiding cheap sentimentality. Josie balances exasperation with deep, protective loyalty, serving as both a translator of human nuance for George and a steadfast ally in the face of institutional skepticism. Through their interactions, Sullivan explores themes of workplace empathy, neurodiversity, and the quiet heroism required to stand up against systemic apathy.

The Dentist is much more than a clever whodunit; it is a striking character study that challenges how society values human life and how we perceive those who think differently. With its razor-sharp plotting, rich Bristol setting, and a detective who is destined to become an iconic figure in crime fiction, Sullivan’s US debut is an absolute triumph. For fans of smart, psychologically astute mysteries who are looking for a protagonist unlike any other in the genre, this is an essential read that resonates long after the final page is turned.
