# Juliet

**Author:** Anne Fortier  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

What if William Shakespeare did not invent the greatest love story in history, but merely transcribed a bloody, forgotten truth? This is the tantalizing, atmospheric premise of Anne Fortier’s 2010 debut novel, Juliet, a sweeping literary thriller that boldly bridges the gap between modern self-discovery and medieval tragedy. 

At its core, Juliet is an exploration of legacy, identity, and the inescapable gravitational pull of the past. The narrative follows twenty-five-year-old Julie Jacobs, an ordinary young woman reeling from the death of her beloved aunt. When she discovers her estranged twin sister has inherited the family estate while she is left with only a mysterious key to a vault in Siena, Italy, Julie embarks on a journey of answers. But the key unlocks more than a safety-deposit box; it opens a portal to fourteenth-century Siena and the harrowing true history of Giulietta Tolomei and Romeo—the real-life flesh-and-blood inspirations behind Shakespeare’s immortal star-crossed lovers.

Fortier’s primary thematic triumph lies in her deft manipulation of duality and time. The novel employs a dual-timeline structure, effortlessly weaving between Julie’s present-day quest for identity and Giulietta’s desperate, high-stakes survival in 1340. As Julie digs deeper into the archives of medieval Siena, the lines between past and present begin to blur. Fortier brilliantly interrogates the weight of ancestral bloodlines. Are we doomed to repeat the mistakes of those who came before us? Can a centuries-old curse still demand fresh blood? These questions drive the narrative forward, transforming a standard treasure-hunt mystery into a profound meditation on fate versus free will. 

The author’s style is lush, cinematic, and relentlessly pacing. Fortier demonstrates an impressive command of historical detail, vividly resurrecting the stone-paved alleys, political factionalism, and atmospheric menace of medieval Italy without ever letting the scholarship bog down the narrative momentum. Siena itself becomes a living, breathing character—seductive, ancient, and quietly dangerous. Where many historical retellings feel dusty or overly reverent, Fortier infuses her prose with the pulse-pounding urgency of a modern thriller. The transition from the grief-stricken vulnerability of contemporary Julie to the fierce, tragic resilience of her ancestor Giulietta is handled with narrative grace. 

While comparisons to sweeping, globetrotting mysteries like The Da Vinci Code are inevitable, Juliet carves out its own distinct niche by anchoring its labyrinth of clues and vendettas in profound emotional resonance. It asks readers to reconsider the power of storytelling—how myths are born, how history is weaponized, and how the echoes of ancient passions can still shatter modern lives. 

Gripping, atmospheric, and ambitiously plotted, Anne Fortier’s novel is a rare page-turner that satisfies both the intellect and the heart. It proves that some love stories are too powerful to stay buried in the past, and that the shadows of Verona and Siena can stretch all the way into the twenty-first century. For anyone who has ever wondered what really happened after the balcony scene, Juliet is an intoxicating and unforgettable journey.
