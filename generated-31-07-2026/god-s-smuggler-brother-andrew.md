# God's Smuggler

**Author:** Brother Andrew  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

If you think espionage is reserved for secular fiction like James Bond or John le Carré, you have yet to meet Brother Andrew. In his extraordinary memoir, God's Smuggler, co-written with John and Elizabeth Sherrill, Andrew delivers a true-life thriller that outpaces the most imaginative Hollywood scripts, substituting high-tech gadgets and political intrigue with something far more powerful: unwavering faith, divine providence, and a blue Volkswagen Beetle packed to the gills with contraband. 

At its core, God's Smuggler is a masterclass in the intersection of radical obedience and sheer adrenaline. The book chronicles the transformation of Andrew van der Bijl, a Dutch boy who grew up dreaming of becoming a daring spy behind enemy lines during World War II. After serving in the military and facing the physical and psychological trauma of war, Andrew finds a profound spiritual awakening. This leads him to a much higher calling: working undercover, not for a terrestrial government, but for God. When the Iron Curtain slams shut across Eastern Europe, cutting off millions of believers from the scriptures, Andrew answers the call to smuggle Bibles right past heavily armed guards and suspicious border patrols into the heart of the communist bloc.

What makes this expanded 2015 edition so striking is how the passage of time has only burnished the legendary status of Andrew's exploits. Sixty years after his first perilous journey, the narrative remains as breathless and urgent as ever. The central theme of the book is not merely the mechanics of smuggling, but the agonizing, beautiful stretch of the human spirit when it relies entirely on the unseen. Andrew’s journey is defined by the famous prayer that became his anchor: Lord, make seeing eyes blind. Readers watch in suspense as border guards rifle through his belongings, completely missing the hidden compartments containing hundreds of forbidden Bibles. It is a theme that challenges the modern reader: How far would you go for what you believe?

From a stylistic standpoint, the writing is remarkably brisk, unpretentious, and vivid. The authors eschew heavy theological lecturing in favor of pacing that reads like a masterclass in suspense fiction. Every near-miss at a checkpoint, every frozen night spent sleeping in a car, and every tense encounter with hostile officials is rendered with tactile immediacy. You can smell the stale cigarette smoke in interrogation rooms, feel the biting chill of the Soviet-bloc winter, and taste the fear that precedes every border crossing. Yet, the tone never sinks into despair or self-aggrandizement. Instead, a current of infectious joy and awe runs beneath every page, reminding the audience that Andrew viewed his dangerous escapades not as a burden, but as a grand adventure orchestrated by heaven.

This updated edition further elevates the critique by including a brand-new interview, rare photographs, and a reflective epilogue detailing six decades of ministry. These additions bridge the gap between historical memoir and contemporary relevance, offering fascinating insights into how Andrew's legacy evolved into Open Doors, a global organization supporting persecuted Christians. 

God's Smuggler is much more than a historical document or a missionary diary; it is a spiritual jolt to the system. It strips away spiritual complacency and replaces it with a bold, heart-pounding challenge to step out of comfort zones and into the realm of the miraculous. Whether you are deeply rooted in the faith or simply looking for a gripping, breathless account of courage against impossible odds, Brother Andrew’s story will leave you sitting on the edge of your seat and questioning the true limits of what faith can accomplish.
