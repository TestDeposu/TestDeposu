# Family Matters

**Author:** Rohinton Mistry  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2002

In "Family Matters," Rohinton Mistry crafts a poignant and thought-provoking narrative that delves into the complexities of family relationships, love, and loyalty, set against the vibrant and often unforgiving backdrop of present-day Bombay. Through the eyes of Nariman Vakeel, a 79-year-old patriarch struggling with Parkinson's disease, Mistry masterfully explores the intricacies of family dynamics, revealing the deep-seated emotions, resentments, and ultimately, the resilience that binds them together.

Mistry's writing is a masterclass in subtlety, weaving a rich tapestry of characters that are at once relatable and flawed. Nariman, with his proud and stubborn demeanor, is a compelling protagonist, whose physical dependence on his family serves as a catalyst for a journey of self-discovery and introspection. His step-children, Coomy and Jal, embody the complexities of familial relationships, their actions and decisions motivated by a tangled web of guilt, resentment, and obligation.

In contrast, Nariman's younger daughter, Roxana, and her family, who take him in, exemplify the selfless love and devotion that often defines family relationships. Through their struggles to care for Nariman, Mistry sheds light on the sacrifices that families make for one another, and the ways in which these acts of love and kindness can both uplift and burden them.

One of Mistry's greatest strengths as a writer is his ability to balance the intimate, domestic drama of the Vakeel family's story with the broader social and cultural context of Bombay. The city, with its vibrant markets, corrupt politicians, and stark contrasts between wealth and poverty, serves as a character in its own right, influencing the lives of the Vakeels and shaping their experiences in profound ways.

Throughout the novel, Mistry's prose is characterized by its simplicity, elegance, and precision. His writing is devoid of unnecessary ornamentation, instead relying on the quiet power of observation and insight to convey the depths of human emotion. This restraint serves to underscore the complexity and nuance of the characters, allowing the reader to inhabit their world and experience their struggles and triumphs firsthand.

Ultimately, "Family Matters" is a novel about the power of love and family to both wound and heal. Through the Vakeels' story, Mistry reminds us that family relationships are multifaceted and often fraught, but also that they have the capacity to transform and redeem us in ways both unexpected and profound. This is a novel that will resonate with readers long after they finish the final page, its themes and characters lingering in the mind like the memories of a loved one.
