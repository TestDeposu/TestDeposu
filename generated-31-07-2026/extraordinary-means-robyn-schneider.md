# Extraordinary Means

**Author:** Robyn Schneider  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

When Robyn Schneider burst onto the young adult literary scene with *The Beginning of Everything*, she established a distinct voice that effortlessly blended sharp wit with profound melancholy. With her 2015 follow-up, *Extraordinary Means*, Schneider returns to territory that will immediately captivate fans of contemporary emotional dramas, evoking inevitable comparisons to John Green's modern classic *The Fault in Our Stars*. Yet, beneath the superficial similarities of teens facing terminal or chronic illnesses in an isolated setting, Schneider carves out a narrative space that is entirely her own, driven by themes of institutional confinement, adolescent rebellion, and the terrifying beauty of reinvention.

The novel centers on Lane, a boy whose meticulously planned, Ivy-League-bound future is abruptly derailed by a drug-resistant strain of tuberculosis. Banished to Latham House, a secluded sanatorium in the California wilderness, Lane expects a bleak prelude to an early grave. Instead, he steps into a bizarre, micro-managed society complete with paradoxical rules, mandatory medical sensors, and a teenage caste system that mirrors the high school he just escaped. Enter Sadie—a former YouTube star and rule-breaker extraordinaire whose outward cynicism masks a desperate longing for normalcy. As Lane and Sadie are drawn into an eccentric circle of fellow patients, their forced quarantine transforms into an unexpected crucible of first love, shifting identities, and second chances.

At its core, *Extraordinary Means* is an exploration of identity stripped down to its barest components. When you are defined entirely by a medical diagnosis, who are you allowed to be? Schneider examines how illness acts as both a leveller and a cage. The sanatorium itself functions almost as a gothic boarding school for the twenty-first century, a liminal space where time feels simultaneously suspended and running out. The author uses this isolated setting not merely for atmospheric melodrama, but to ask sharp questions about how teenagers construct their social personas when the pressures of the outside world are temporarily removed. The med sensors and strict curating of physical contact become metaphors for the invisible barriers we place between ourselves and genuine intimacy.

Stylistically, Schneider excels in maintaining a delicate balancing act between heartbreak and dark humor. The prose is snappy, highly accessible, and peppered with witty banter that prevents the narrative from sinking into unearned gloom. The author understands the teenage psyche intimately; her characters do not speak in poetic platitudes, but rather in the defensive, self-deprecating irony native to modern youth. Even in moments of intense vulnerability, Schneider injects a dose of realism through awkward pauses, miscommunications, and adolescent stubbornness. This stylistic choice makes the emotional peaks hit all the harder. When the humor gives way to raw fear or aching tenderness, the contrast is stark and resonant.

Furthermore, Schneider’s pacing is relentless. By alternating perspectives and keeping the focus tightly on the claustrophobic dynamics of Latham House, she creates a page-turning urgency. The secondary characters—a quirky ensemble of fellow patients—add depth and texture to the world, ensuring that the novel remains a rich character study rather than a standard two-person romance. 

While some critics might argue that the premise treads familiar ground within the YA "sick-lit" subgenre, *Extraordinary Means* distinguishes itself through its refusal to romanticize tragedy for cheap emotional manipulation. Instead, Schneider focuses on the courage it takes to live fully in the present moment, even when your future has been effectively cancelled. It is a story about shedding the expectations of others to find out who you truly are when nobody is watching—or rather, when everyone is watching too closely.

Ultimately, Robyn Schneider has crafted a compelling, sharply observed novel that lingers long after the final page. It is funny, devastating, and stubbornly optimistic. For anyone who has ever felt trapped by the trajectory of their own life, or dared to hope against impossible odds, *Extraordinary Means* is a journey well worth taking.
