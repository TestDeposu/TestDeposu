# Magic's Promise (The Last Herald-Mage #2)

**Author:** Mercedes Lackey  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1990

Magic's Promise, the second installment in Mercedes Lackey's The Last Herald-Mage series, is a captivating and immersive epic fantasy that delves into the complexities of magic, power, and the unyielding bonds of friendship and loyalty. Published in 1990, this Lambda-Award winning novel has stood the test of time, offering a rich and engaging narrative that will resonate with fans of the genre.

Set in the Valdemar universe, Magic's Promise follows the journey of Vanyel, a powerful Herald-Mage, and his Companion, Yfandes, as they navigate the treacherous landscape of war and dark magic. The land is plagued by wild magic, which has claimed the lives of many Heralds and Herald-Mages, leaving Vanyel on the brink of exhaustion. Despite his fatigue, Vanyel is compelled to respond to a desperate cry for help from a neighboring kingdom, where a magical holocaust has devastated the land.

Upon their arrival, Vanyel and Yfandes discover that the destruction may be linked to a young Prince Tashir, a newly Chosen Herald struggling to control his magic. As they work to unravel the mystery, they begin to suspect that Tashir may be a pawn in a larger, more sinister game. With the fate of the kingdom hanging in the balance, Vanyel must summon his remaining strength to uncover the truth and defeat the true mastermind behind the destruction.

One of the most striking aspects of Magic's Promise is Lackey's masterful handling of the fantasy genre. Her world-building is meticulous and detailed, with a richly imagined magical system that underpins the entire narrative. The Valdemar universe is vividly realized, with a complex history and politics that add depth and nuance to the story.

Lackey's writing style is equally impressive, with a keen focus on character development and emotional resonance. Vanyel, in particular, is a compelling protagonist, whose struggles with his own power and the weight of responsibility are deeply relatable. The bond between Vanyel and Yfandes is also beautifully portrayed, highlighting the deep emotional connection between Heralds and their Companions.

The themes of Magic's Promise are equally noteworthy, exploring the consequences of unchecked power and the importance of empathy and understanding. Through Vanyel's journey, Lackey highlights the dangers of assuming guilt or responsibility without evidence, and the need for compassion and kindness in the face of uncertainty.

Overall, Magic's Promise is a captivating and immersive epic fantasy that will appeal to fans of Mercedes Lackey and the Valdemar universe. With its rich world-building, compelling characters, and thought-provoking themes, this novel is a must-read for anyone looking for a engaging and emotionally resonant fantasy experience.
