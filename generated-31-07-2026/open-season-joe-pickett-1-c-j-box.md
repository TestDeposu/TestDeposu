# Open Season (Joe Pickett, #1)

**Author:** C.J. Box  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2001

"Open Season" by C.J. Box is a gripping and thought-provoking novel that delves into the complexities of human nature, power struggles, and the delicate balance between progress and conservation. Set against the stunning backdrop of Wyoming's wilderness, this first installment of the Joe Pickett series introduces us to a protagonist who is as rugged as the landscape he inhabits.

Game warden Joe Pickett is a man of unwavering principles, and his refusal to take bribes or turn a blind eye to the law makes him an outcast in the small town of Twelve Sleep. When a local hunting outfitter is found dead on his property, Joe takes it upon himself to investigate, driven by a sense of responsibility and justice. As the body count rises, Joe becomes increasingly entangled in a web of deceit and corruption that threatens to destroy everything he holds dear.

Box's writing style is as crisp as the mountain air, with a narrative that flows effortlessly from one page to the next. His characters are multidimensional and relatable, with Joe Pickett being a particularly well-crafted protagonist. Box's portrayal of Joe's struggles to balance his duty as a game warden with his role as a husband and father is both authentic and poignant.

One of the most striking aspects of "Open Season" is its thought-provoking exploration of the human relationship with the natural world. Box raises important questions about the consequences of unchecked progress and the importance of preserving our natural heritage. The endangered species that Joe discovers in his woodpile serves as a powerful symbol of the delicate balance between human needs and environmental concerns.

As Joe delves deeper into the mystery of the outfitter murders, he finds himself at odds with the powerful forces of InterWest, a multinational natural gas company that will stop at nothing to build a pipeline across Wyoming. Box's portrayal of the company's ruthless tactics and the corrupt officials who enable them is both chilling and all too familiar.

Throughout the novel, Box's love for the Wyoming wilderness is palpable, and his vivid descriptions of the landscape will leave readers breathless. From the majestic mountains to the rolling hills and forests, Box's Wyoming is a place of stunning beauty and unforgiving harshness.

In conclusion, "Open Season" is a gripping and thought-provoking novel that will appeal to fans of mystery, thriller, and environmental fiction. Box's writing style is engaging, and his characters are well-crafted and relatable. This is a must-read for anyone who loves the great outdoors and is concerned about the impact of human activity on the natural world.
