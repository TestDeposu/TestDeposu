# Forensics: What Bugs, Burns, Prints, DNA and More Tell Us About Crime

**Author:** Val McDermid  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

Forensics: What Bugs, Burns, Prints, DNA and More Tell Us About Crime is a gripping and meticulously researched exploration of the fascinating world of forensic science, penned by the acclaimed crime author Val McDermid. This book is a masterful blend of history, science, and real-life crime stories, woven together by McDermid's engaging narrative style.

McDermid's unique blend of expertise as a crime author and her passion for forensic science makes her the perfect guide on this journey into the world of crime scene investigation. With unparalleled access to top professionals in the field, she delves into the most cutting-edge research and techniques, from the use of maggots to determine time of death to the incredible power of DNA analysis.

One of the standout aspects of Forensics is its ability to balance the scientific and technical aspects of the subject with compelling storytelling. McDermid's writing is engaging, clear, and concise, making even the most complex scientific concepts accessible to a broad audience. Her use of real-life examples and case studies adds a layer of tension and drama to the narrative, drawing the reader into the world of forensic science.

Throughout the book, McDermid's own experiences and insights as a crime author add a unique perspective to the narrative. Her observations on the intersection of science and justice are astute and thought-provoking, highlighting the critical role that forensic science plays in the pursuit of truth and justice.

The book's structure, which takes the reader on a journey from the early beginnings of forensic science to the modern day, is well-conceived and easy to follow. McDermid's use of historical context and anecdotes adds depth and richness to the narrative, while her exploration of the latest scientific advancements and techniques keeps the reader up-to-date with the latest developments in the field.

One of the most striking aspects of Forensics is its ability to convey the human side of forensic science. McDermid's portraits of the scientists, investigators, and experts who work in this field are nuanced and multifaceted, revealing the dedication, expertise, and often the personal costs of working in this high-stakes environment.

In conclusion, Forensics: What Bugs, Burns, Prints, DNA and More Tell Us About Crime is a riveting and informative exploration of the world of forensic science. Val McDermid's engaging narrative style, combined with her meticulous research and unique perspective, make this book a must-read for anyone interested in true crime, science, or the pursuit of justice.
