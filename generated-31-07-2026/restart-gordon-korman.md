# Restart

**Author:** Gordon Korman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Who are you when no one expects you to change, but everyone expects you to stay the same? This is the haunting, deeply resonant question at the heart of Gordon Korman’s 2017 New York Times bestseller, Restart. Blending the gravity of adolescent identity crises with Korman's signature razor-sharp wit, this novel delivers a compelling exploration of redemption, memory, and the choices that define us.

The premise is striking in its narrative economy: Chase Ambrose wakes up in a hospital room with amnesia following a nasty fall from a roof. He knows his name, but his past is a complete blank. He has to reconstruct his identity from scratch, relying on the reactions of the people around him. The trouble is, the pre-amnesia Chase wasn't just a typical teenager—he was a schoolyard bully, the ringleader of a cruel trio that terrorized the halls of Hilt Valley Middle School. 

As Chase returns to classes, Korman orchestrates a brilliant psychological puzzle. The narrative unfolds through multiple perspectives, a stylistic choice that allows the reader to see Chase not just through his own confused eyes, but through the terror of his victims, the exhaustion of the administration, and the wary caution of his family. When a girl he used to torment dumps a cup of frozen yogurt over his head, Chase realizes the magnitude of the ghost he has to live down. He is forced to confront a terrifying possibility: What if the person you used to be is someone you utterly despise?

Korman’s writing style is deceptively breezy, perfectly tailored to middle-grade readers yet rich enough to captivate adults. He avoids heavy-handed preachiness, instead trusting his audience to navigate the moral gray areas alongside the protagonist. The humor is sharp and authentic, keeping the tone buoyant even when tackling heavy themes like peer pressure, cyberbullying, and the complicity of bystanders. 

The central theme of Restart is the power of agency. Amnesia wipes the slate clean, but Korman argues that a clean slate isn't enough; true redemption requires active, daily choices. Chase’s journey is not just about regaining his lost memories, but about discovering who he wants to be now that he has been gifted an unprecedented do-over. When his old friends try to drag him back into his cruel antics, the conflict shifts from a mystery of the past to a battle for the future. 

Ultimately, Restart is a triumph of empathetic storytelling. It reminds us that empathy is a muscle that can be rebuilt, and that no one is entirely defined by their worst mistakes. Korman has crafted a modern classic that challenges readers to examine their own actions, making this book an essential, unforgettable read for anyone who believes in the power of a second chance.
