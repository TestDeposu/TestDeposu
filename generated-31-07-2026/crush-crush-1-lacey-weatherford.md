# Crush (Crush, #1)

**Author:** Lacey Weatherford  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

Lacey Weatherford’s 2012 young adult novel, Crush, plunges headfirst into one of the most enduring and addictive tropes in teen romance: the classic collision of opposites. In this opening installment of the Crush series, Weatherford delivers a fast-paced, emotionally charged narrative that examines the fragile boundary between carefully constructed ambition and the unpredictable nature of teenage desire. At its core, the novel explores the theme of emotional armor—how teenagers build walls to protect their futures, only to find themselves desperately hoping someone will tear them down.

The story centers on Cami Wimberley, a high-achieving senior whose life is mapped out to the millimeter. With her sights set firmly on a musical theater major at her dream college, Cami has zero room in her pristine schedule for distractions, let alone boys. Enter Hunter Wilder: the quintessential high school party animal whose charisma is matched only by his aversion to commitment. Naturally, fate—and the inescapable gravity of YA romance logic—refuses to keep them apart. 

Weatherford’s greatest strength lies in her effortless pacing and her keen understanding of high school social dynamics. The author adopts a breezy, highly accessible style that mirrors the fast-paced inner monologues of her teenage protagonists. Alternating perspectives allow readers to feel the whiplash of both characters as they are magnetically drawn to one another, despite their best efforts. Cami’s initial perfectionism is nicely balanced by Hunter’s devil-may-care facade, creating an immediate friction that powers the first half of the book. 

However, Weatherford avoids letting the novel devolve into a mindless, sunshine-and-butterflies romance. As the sparks between Cami and Hunter inevitably catch fire, the narrative shifts gears, introducing a darker, more precarious undercurrent. Hunter is harboring a secret—a looming complication capable of burning their nascent relationship to the ground. This introduces a compelling element of suspense, elevating Crush from a standard sweet romance into a story about trust, vulnerability, and the terrifying cost of letting someone truly see you.

While the plot relies on familiar genre touchstones, Weatherford infuses the narrative with genuine emotional stakes. Cami is not just a standard goody-two-shoes; she is a driven young woman whose artistic passions validate her rigidity, making her eventual softening feel earned rather than forced. Similarly, Hunter’s reluctance to date hints at a deeper backstory that makes his internal conflict resonate beyond typical teen angst.

Crush is a striking, deeply engaging preview of a series that understands the intoxicating rush of first love. Weatherford captures the sweet agony of wanting someone you know you should avoid, wrapped in a package of high-stakes drama and undeniable chemistry. For readers who revel in high school romance, emotional push-and-pull, and characters forced to confront their deepest secrets, this 2012 release remains a compelling, highly readable entry into the genre.
