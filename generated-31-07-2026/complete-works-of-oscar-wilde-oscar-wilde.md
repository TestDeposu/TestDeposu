# Complete Works of Oscar Wilde

**Author:** Oscar Wilde  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

In the grand tradition of collecting the works of literary giants, the Complete Works of Oscar Wilde is a treasure trove of wit, satire, and profound social commentary. This exhaustive collection, which encompasses Wilde's plays, novels, short stories, poetry, and non-fiction essays, is a testament to the enduring legacy of a writer who continues to fascinate and provoke audiences to this day.

From the lighthearted comedies of Lady Windermere's Fan and The Importance of Being Earnest, to the biting social critique of A Woman of No Importance and The Soul of Man Under Socialism, Wilde's works showcase his trademark mastery of language, his quick wit, and his unyielding commitment to free thought and expression. His plays, in particular, are masterclasses in character development, plot twists, and the deft handling of themes that continue to resonate with modern audiences.

One of the most striking features of this collection is its sheer scope and diversity. Wilde's oeuvre defies easy categorization, and this complete works edition is a testament to his boundless creativity and intellectual curiosity. From the decadent, symbolic poetry of The Chatterer and The Graveyard by Gaslight, to the experimental, avant-garde short stories of The Happy Prince and Lord Arthur Savile's Crime, every aspect of Wilde's work is represented in some way.

And yet, despite its sheer scale, the Complete Works of Oscar Wilde is not just a collection of individual works, but a living, breathing entity that reflects the turbulent historical context in which they were written. Wilde's writing is a reflection of his own life, a life marked by controversy, persecution, and the struggle for artistic integrity in the face of societal pressure. His works are infused with a sense of anger, resentment, and, above all, a deep commitment to individual freedom and self-expression.

As a collection, the Complete Works of Oscar Wilde is also notable for its attention to historical context and textual accuracy. The annotations, introductions, and biographical notes that accompany each work provide invaluable insights into Wilde's life and the literary and cultural currents of the time. The inclusion of bonus texts, such as the rare, unfinished plays and the apocryphal erotic novel Telney, adds to the collection's scholarly value and sheds new light on the writer's creative process.

Ultimately, the Complete Works of Oscar Wilde is a work of genius that will continue to captivate readers for generations to come. It is a testament to the power of literature to transform, provoke, and challenge our understanding of the world around us. As we read through these pages, we are reminded of Wilde's own words, spoken in the face of censorship and persecution: "To love oneself is the beginning of a lifelong romance."

This collection is a must-have for anyone interested in Wilde's life and work, or in the literary and cultural heritage of the late 19th and early 20th centuries. With its meticulous scholarship, insightful annotations, and breathtaking scope, the Complete Works of Oscar Wilde is a triumph of editorial endeavour, a true masterpiece that will be cherished by scholars, readers, and admirers of Wilde's unique spirit for years to come.
