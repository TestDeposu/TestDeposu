# Violet Bent Backwards Over the Grass

**Author:** Lana Del Rey  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

Lana Del Rey has long established herself as the ultimate chronicler of American melancholy, a cinematic songstress mapping the underbelly of glamour, heartbreak, and nostalgia. With her debut collection of poetry, Violet Bent Backwards Over the Grass, she expands this sonic universe onto the printed page, offering readers an intimate glimpse into the unfiltered psyche of a generation’s defining voice. Far from a mere vanity project by a pop icon, this volume stands as a compelling literary artifact that captures the raw, unguarded spirit of its creator.

At the heart of the collection lies a profound exploration of vulnerability, spiritual searching, and the chaotic beauty of ordinary life. Del Rey’s thematic landscape is instantly familiar to anyone who has lost themselves in her discography, yet the poetry strips away the lush orchestration to reveal a starker, more urgent truth. Across more than thirty eclectic pieces—including standout works like The Land of 1,000 Fires, LA Who Am I to Love You?, and the title poem itself—she grapples with the tensions between fame and isolation, domesticity and wildness, and the eternal quest for inner peace amidst external chaos. There is a distinct duality in her subjects: the yearning for transcendence sits side by side with a gritty, sun-bleached realism rooted in the landscapes of California and the American highway.

Stylistically, the book is as much a visual and tactile experience as it is a literary one. The physical presentation of the hardcover edition—featuring Del Rey’s actual typewritten manuscript pages complete with handwritten corrections and strike-throughs, paired with her own evocative original photography—creates an immersive atmosphere. This deliberate aesthetic choice emphasizes the author’s opening sentiment that these pieces are eclectic, honest, and entirely unpretentious. Some poems arrived to her in sudden, complete visions, while others were meticulously labored over, word by word. This dichotomy between automatic stream-of-consciousness and careful curation defines her poetic voice. The writing is conversational yet deeply resonant, echoing the cadence of spoken word and free verse, refusing to conform to rigid academic structures. Instead, it mirrors the erratic, beautiful rhythm of thought itself.

For those who wish to experience the work in its full multi-sensory glory, the accompanying spoken word audiobook—scored by Grammy-winning musician Jack Antonoff—acts as a natural bridge between Del Rey’s lyrical music and her written verse, breathing a haunting, atmospheric life into the text.

Violet Bent Backwards Over the Grass is a striking, deeply personal offering that solidifies Lana Del Rey’s status not just as a songwriter, but as a vital literary voice of her era. It invites readers to sit with discomfort, to romanticize the mundane, and to embrace the beautifully flawed nature of the human experience. It is a triumphant, unguarded debut that demands to be read, felt, and heard.
