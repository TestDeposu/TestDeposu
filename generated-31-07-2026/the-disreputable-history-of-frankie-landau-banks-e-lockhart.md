# The Disreputable History of Frankie Landau-Banks

**Author:** E. Lockhart  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

The Disreputable History of Frankie Landau‑Banks reads like a razor‑sharp manifesto wrapped in a boarding‑school romp. E. Lockhart hands us Frankie, a wickedly clever sophomore who refuses to be sidelined by the old‑boys’ network that rules her elite academy. When she discovers the existence of the Loyal Order of the Basset Hounds—a secret society of privileged boys who stage elaborate pranks and guard their influence with ritualistic secrecy—Frankie decides not just to join, but to rewrite the rules from the inside out. What follows is a battle of wits, language, and subversion as she engineers increasingly elaborate hoaxes that expose the hollowness of the Order’s authority and, by extension, the patriarchal structures that undergird the school’s prestige.

Lockhart’s style is the novel’s true engine. Her prose crackles with a sardonic wit that feels both contemporary and timeless, employing crisp dialogue, rapid‑fire internal monologue, and occasional footnote‑like asides that invite readers to share in Frankie’s conspiratorial glee. The narrative mirrors its protagonist’s love of linguistic play—she treats words as weapons, deploying puns, allusions, and meticulously crafted arguments to dismantle the machismo that surrounds her. This verbal dexterity does more than entertain; it underscores the central theme that power is often maintained through control of narrative, and that reclaiming language is a radical act of resistance.

Beyond the clever pranks and cheeky humor, Lockhart interrogates the ethics of rebellion. Frankie’s escalation from playful sabotage to full‑blown insurgency forces readers to consider where the line between justified dissent and destructive arrogance lies. The novel never offers easy answers; instead, it lets the discomfort linger, prompting us to question our own complicity in systems that reward conformity and punish those who dare to rewrite the script.

At its heart, The Disreputable History of Frankie Landau‑Banks is a feminist coming‑of‑age tale that celebrates intelligence, audacity, and the unsettling pleasure of watching a girl turn the tables on a world built to keep her quiet. Lockhart’s incisive style and fearless protagonist make the book not just a fun read but a provocative commentary on who gets to hold power—and who gets to seize it back.
