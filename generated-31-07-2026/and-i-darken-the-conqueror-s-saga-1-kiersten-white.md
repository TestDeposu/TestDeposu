# And I Darken (The Conqueror's Saga, #1)

**Author:** Kiersten White  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

**And I Darken: A Gritty, Feminist Masterpiece That Rewrites History with Blood and Passion**

Kiersten White’s *And I Darken*—the first installment in *The Conqueror’s Saga*—is a historical fantasy that doesn’t just reimagine the Ottoman Empire; it *reclaims* it. With the razor-sharp prose of a poet and the political cunning of a master strategist, White crafts a world where power is a weapon, love is a liability, and survival demands ruthlessness. This isn’t just a story about a princess seeking vengeance; it’s a brutal, poetic meditation on identity, betrayal, and the cost of ambition—all wrapped in the opulent, violent beauty of a rising empire.

At its core, *And I Darken* is a tale of two brothers and the empire that wants to break them. Lada Dragwlya, the novel’s protagonist, is a force of nature—cold, calculating, and utterly unapologetic. Raised in the Ottoman court after her father abandoned her and her brother, Radu, in Wallachia, she has spent her life honing her cruelty as a survival tactic. But Lada isn’t just a villainess; she’s a survivor, a woman who refuses to be defined by the hands that raised her. Her relationship with Mehmed, the brooding heir to the Ottoman throne, is the emotional heart of the novel—a toxic, intoxicating dance of desire and distrust. Mehmed, torn between loyalty to his empire and the magnetic pull of the Wallachian siblings, becomes the catalyst for a love story that is as dangerous as it is devastating. Their bond is a powder keg of passion and politics, where every glance could be a betrayal and every confession a death sentence.

White’s writing is lush yet lethal, blending the grandeur of historical fiction with the visceral intensity of fantasy. She doesn’t shy away from the gore—heads roll, bodies are impaled, and hearts are shattered—but she elevates the brutality with prose that feels like a blade’s kiss. Her descriptions of the Ottoman court are immersive, painting a world of silk and steel, where intrigue is as much a part of the air as the scent of spices. Yet, beneath the opulence, there’s a raw, unflinching honesty about power and its cost. White doesn’t romanticize conquest; she exposes its ugliness, even as she celebrates the defiance of those who refuse to be broken.

The novel’s feminist undertones are its most striking feature. Lada isn’t a damsel in distress; she’s a woman who weaponizes her femininity while never losing her edge. She’s a princess who knows her worth isn’t in her beauty but in her cunning, and she’s not afraid to use it. Radu, though gentler, is no less resilient—his quiet strength in the face of adversity makes him just as compelling as his sister. Together, they challenge the very idea of what it means to be a hero in a world that demands strength above all else.

The political intrigue is masterfully woven into the narrative, with each character’s motives feeling as real as their fates. The tension between Wallachia and the Ottoman Empire simmers beneath the surface, threatening to boil over at any moment. White balances this with a slow-burn romance that feels earned, not forced. The chemistry between Lada and Mehmed is electric, their attraction as dangerous as it is inevitable. But their love is never allowed to overshadow the larger stakes—because in this world, love is a luxury, and survival is the only certainty.

*And I Darken* is a book that doesn’t just entertain; it *haunts*. It’s a story about the price of vengeance, the fragility of loyalty, and the way power can corrupt even the most noble hearts. White’s world is rich, her characters are unforgettable, and her prose is a joy to read. It’s the kind of book that lingers long after the last page, leaving you with the echo of a sword’s swing and the whisper of a name—Lada—on your lips.

If you love historical fantasy with depth, political maneuvering, and a heroine who won’t back down, this is your book. Just be warned: it will leave you breathless, bleeding, and utterly obsessed.
