# Forgotten

**Author:** Cat Patrick  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

Imagine waking up every single day with a completely blank slate, your yesterdays vanished into thin air, and your only guide to who you are being a hastily written collection of sticky notes. This is the arresting reality of sixteen-year-old London Lane, the compelling protagonist of Cat Patrick’s 2011 debut novel, Forgotten. Far from being a standard high school drama, Patrick’s inventive premise immediately hooks the reader, transforming a classic adolescent journey of self-discovery into a high-stakes psychological puzzle where time itself is the ultimate adversary.

The central theme of Forgotten revolves around the delicate, often painful relationship between memory and identity. Who are we when we are stripped of our past? London lives in a perpetual loop of tomorrow, her brain stubbornly retaining visions of the future while completely purging the events of the day that just passed. This inverted amnesia creates a profound sense of isolation. While her peers stress over exams and weekend parties, London is fighting a daily battle to anchor herself to reality using notebooks, journals, and the unwavering loyalty of her best friend, Jamie. Patrick brilliantly uses this condition as a metaphor for the universal teenage experience of feeling disconnected from one's own life, navigating change, and desperately trying to figure out where one belongs. 

What elevates the novel above typical YA fare is the disruption of London’s predictable routine by a boy named Luke Henry. Luke is an enigma—charming, perceptive, and entirely absent from London’s mental flash-forwards. For a girl who relies on knowing what comes next to feel safe, the presence of the unknown is terrifying yet intoxicating. Patrick handles this budding romance with a refreshing tenderness. Luke does not "fix" London; rather, he challenges her to live in the present moment, a radical concept for someone whose existence is eternally split between a forgotten past and a pre-ordained future. 

Cat Patrick’s writing style is brisk, accessible, and sharply atmospheric. The pacing mirrors London’s frantic daily reality, keeping readers breathless as secrets unravel and the timeline begins to fracture. When London starts experiencing disturbing flash-forwards that hint at danger and tragedy, the book shifts gears from a sweet contemporary romance into a gripping mystery. Patrick cleverly plays with the conventions of speculative fiction, grounding the supernatural element of time-slipping in genuine emotional stakes. The mechanics of London's condition are treated with a quiet seriousness that makes the fantastic feel intimately real.

Ultimately, Forgotten is a poignant reminder that our lives are defined not just by what we anticipate, but by the unscripted moments we choose to embrace today. Cat Patrick delivers a striking, emotionally resonant story about the courage it takes to face a tomorrow when you cannot even remember yesterday. It is a swift, captivating read that lingers in the mind long after the final page is turned, urging us all to pay closer attention to the present.
