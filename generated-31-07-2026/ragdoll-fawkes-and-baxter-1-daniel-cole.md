# Ragdoll (Fawkes and Baxter, #1)

**Author:** Daniel Cole  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

**Ragdoll: A Darkly Brilliant Dance with Death and Defiance**

Daniel Cole’s *Ragdoll*—the debut novel that birthed the Fawkes and Baxter series—is a razor-sharp, blood-soaked masterclass in psychological suspense, where the line between predator and prey blurs with the precision of a surgeon’s scalpel. At its core, this isn’t just a serial killer thriller; it’s a dissection of guilt, obsession, and the fragile line between justice and vengeance. The novel’s genius lies in its ability to weave a relentless, twisty plot with a darkly humorous edge, making it as addictive as it is unsettling.

The story follows Detective William Fawkes, a man known as "The Wolf" for his ruthless efficiency—and his equally ruthless reputation for crossing ethical lines. After a suspension for assaulting a suspect (a move that may have been justified or not, depending on who you ask), Fawkes is reinstated, only to find himself entangled in a case that will test his sanity, his morality, and his very survival. The victim—a grotesque, stitched-together "puppet" of six dismembered bodies—isn’t just a crime scene; it’s a taunt. A warning. And when his ex-wife, a reporter, receives a list of names—including his own—alongside chilling photographs, Fawkes realizes he’s not just hunting a killer. The killer is hunting *him*.

What makes *Ragdoll* so compelling is its unflinching exploration of the psychological toll of law enforcement. Fawkes isn’t just a detective; he’s a man haunted by his past, his failures, and the knowledge that the Ragdoll Killer’s motives might be deeply personal. Cole crafts a narrative where the tension isn’t just external—it’s internal, a battle between Fawkes’ instincts and the demons that drive him. The author’s writing is sharp, visceral, and effortlessly immersive, with a knack for dark humor that keeps the reader’s pulse racing even as they’re laughing at the absurdity of it all.

The dynamic between Fawkes and his partner, Detective Emily Baxter, is electric—equal parts professional respect and mutual distrust. Baxter, a no-nonsense investigator, and Fawkes, the volatile "Wolf," clash in ways that feel both inevitable and electrifying. Their partnership is a microcosm of the novel’s central theme: the fine line between justice and vengeance, between hunter and prey. And then there’s Alex Edmunds, Baxter’s trainee, whose perspective adds another layer of tension, forcing the reader to question who they can trust.

Cole’s style is a perfect storm of grit and wit. The prose is lean, the dialogue crackles with attitude, and the twists come fast and furious. The Ragdoll Killer isn’t just a monster; they’re a puzzle, a game, and a mirror held up to Fawkes’ own dark reflections. The novel’s pacing is relentless, with each chapter turning the page into a gamble—will the next reveal be a gut-punch or a gut-laugh?

But what truly sets *Ragdoll* apart is its willingness to embrace the macabre without shying away from the human cost. The novel doesn’t just entertain; it challenges. It asks: How far would you go to stop a killer? And when the killer knows your name, how do you even begin to fight back? The answer lies in the pages of this book, where every clue, every red herring, and every moment of dark humor serves a purpose. It’s a thriller that lingers long after the last page, a testament to Cole’s skill in crafting a story that’s as unforgettable as it is unsettling.

In an era where crime fiction often leans too heavily on formula, *Ragdoll* stands out as a fresh, fearless, and utterly gripping debut. It’s a book that will leave you breathless, laughing one minute and bracing for the next. And if the AMC Plus adaptation is anything to go by, the story’s dark charm and twisted brilliance are only just beginning.
