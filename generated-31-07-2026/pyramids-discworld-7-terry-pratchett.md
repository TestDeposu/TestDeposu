# Pyramids (Discworld, #7)

**Author:** Terry Pratchett  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

In the seventh installment of the Discworld series, Terry Pratchett weaves a captivating tale of politics, power, and the perils of being a god in "Pyramids". This standalone novel whisks readers away to the Kingdom of the Sun, where the newly deified teenage pharaoh, Prince Teppic, must navigate the treacherous waters of ruling a desert kingdom.

With his signature wit and satire, Pratchett lampoons the conventions of ancient Egyptian culture, sending up the sacred cows of tradition and authority. The result is a sidesplitting romp that is as smart as it is funny. Teppic's journey from assassin-in-training to beleaguered pharaoh is a masterclass in comedic storytelling, replete with mad priests, sacred crocodiles, and marching mummies.

At its core, "Pyramids" is a scathing critique of the dangers of blind tradition and the corrupting influence of power. Pratchett's Teppic is a reluctant hero, thrust into a role he is ill-equipped to handle, and forced to confront the absurdities of his kingdom's customs. As he struggles to build a monumental pyramid to honor his predecessor, Teppic begins to realize that the weight of tradition can be crushing, and that sometimes, the only way to move forward is to challenge the status quo.

Pratchett's writing is, as always, a joy to behold. His prose is effortless, his characters are richly drawn, and his world-building is meticulous. The Discworld is a marvelously realized creation, full of clever references and cleverer characters. From the bumbling priests of Anoia to the enigmatic handmaiden, Ptraci, the cast of "Pyramids" is a veritable embarrassment of riches.

One of the most striking aspects of "Pyramids" is its thematic resonance. Pratchett's exploration of the tensions between tradition and progress, authority and individuality, is both timely and timeless. This is a book that will resonate with readers of all ages, from the cynical teenager to the world-weary adult.

In short, "Pyramids" is a triumph, a hilarious and thought-provoking romp that will leave readers in stitches and in awe of Pratchett's mastery of the comedic fantasy genre. If you're new to the Discworld series, this is an excellent place to start. And if you're a seasoned fan, you'll find plenty to love in this standalone adventure.
