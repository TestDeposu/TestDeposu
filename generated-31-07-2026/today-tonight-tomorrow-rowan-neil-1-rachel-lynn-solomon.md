# Today Tonight Tomorrow (Rowan & Neil, #1)

**Author:** Rachel Lynn Solomon  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

If *The Hating Game* and *Booksmart* had a literary lovechild, it would undoubtedly be Rachel Lynn Solomon’s sparkling 2020 young adult romantic comedy, *Today Tonight Tomorrow*. Smart, fiercely witty, and undeniably swoon-worthy, this novel takes the classic enemies-to-lovers trope and breathes vibrant, modern life into it. Set against the atmospheric backdrop of Seattle on the final day of high school, Solomon crafts a breathless, single-night journey that examines ambition, vulnerability, and the fine line separating hatred from a much more complicated devotion.

At its thematic core, the book is a love letter to the passionate, terrifying, and exhilarating transition of growing up. Rowan Roth and Neil McNair are academic titans who have spent four years locked in intellectual warfare. For Rowan, who secretly harbors dreams of writing romance novels, life is about control, careful planning, and shielding her tender creative heart from rejection. For Neil, the newly crowned valedictorian, precise logic and a penchant for linguistics mask a deeply earnest soul. When they are forced into an uneasy alliance during "Howl"—an epic, city-wide senior class scavenger hunt—their rivalry is stripped away, revealing the real people hiding beneath the armor of competition. 

Solomon’s authorial style is the true engine driving this narrative. Her prose is sharp, contemporary, and effortlessly funny, paced with the frantic, caffeinated energy of teenagers standing on the precipice of adulthood. She treats the young adult experience with immense respect, never talking down to her audience. Instead, she infuses the text with genuine emotional stakes. Rowan’s internal monologue regarding her aspirations as a romance writer brilliantly deconstructs the genre’s inherent stigma, elevating the book’s meta-commentary on the validity of joy and romance in literature. 

What makes *Today Tonight Tomorrow* truly striking is how efficiently it captures the magic of a single night. By condensing the timeline of Rowan and Neil’s paradigm shift into roughly twenty-four hours, Solomon creates an intimate, pressurized crucible. Every landmark in Seattle becomes a silent witness to their shifting dynamic, moving seamlessly from barbed banter to profound emotional intimacy. The chemistry sizzles not just because of physical attraction, but because they genuinely see and challenge one another intellectually.

Ultimately, Solomon delivers a pitch-perfect romantic comedy that satisfies genre purists while offering enough emotional depth to resonate long after the final page. It is a triumphant, joyful read about shedding expectations, embracing one's passions, and realizing that the person you thought was your biggest obstacle might actually be your greatest champion.
