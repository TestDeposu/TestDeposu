# One False Note (The 39 Clues, #2)

**Author:** Gordon Korman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

A Symphony of Danger: Gordon Korman Ramps Up the Race in One False Note

Following the explosive start of a global scavenger hunt that defines The 39 Clues phenomenon, the pressure shifts from world-building to breathless momentum in Book 2, One False Note. Penned by Gordon Korman, this installment takes the reins from Rick Riordan and proves that the multi-author grand experiment is built to last. Korman injects his trademark wit, cinematic pacing, and deeply grounded character dynamics into the Cahill family saga, delivering a middle-grade adventure that hits all the right notes.

The core narrative wastes no time. Fourteen-year-old Amy and her eleven-year-old brother, Dan, are fresh off their discovery in Philadelphia, hurtling toward Vienna with one singular, terrifying objective: stay ahead of their ruthless, cutthroat extended family and solve the next riddle. The central theme of this volume revolves around legacy, hidden genius, and the burdens of history. As the siblings dive into the life and music of Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart, Korman explores how greatness casts a long shadow. The Clue isn't just a physical object to be found; it is an intellectual puzzle that forces Amy and Dan to confront their own insecurities. Amy relies on her encyclopedic intellect and book smarts, while Dan leverages his sharp observation skills, encyclopedic knowledge of trivia, and sheer audacity. Together, they prove that the underdogs of the Cahill family tree possess a resilient root system.

Korman’s writing style is a masterclass in kinetic storytelling. Where Riordan’s opening volume established the sprawling mythology of the Cahill clan, Korman treats the middle-grade audience to a tightly wound mystery driven by immediate stakes. His prose is punchy, brisk, and dripping with situational humor that prevents the high-stakes danger from ever becoming too grim for younger readers. The action set-pieces—ranging from hotel break-ins to high-speed chases and angry mobs—are choreographed with the precision of a summer blockbuster. Yet, amidst the chaos, Korman never loses sight of the emotional core. The heart of the book remains the sibling bond between Amy and Dan, two kids who only truly have each other in a world populated by treacherous adults willing to commit grand larceny for ultimate power.

Furthermore, the introduction of rival family members, particularly the pompous and athletic Holts and the calculating Alistair Oh, adds a delightful layer of interpersonal friction. Korman uses these antagonists not just as physical threats, but as comedic foils that highlight the ordinary, relatable humanity of Amy and Dan. 

One False Note is a thrilling sophomore effort that expands the scope of the 39 Clues universe without sacrificing intimacy or character growth. Korman conducts a fast-paced symphony of riddles, red herrings, and adrenaline-fueled chases that will leave readers frantically turning the pages to see where the music leads next.
