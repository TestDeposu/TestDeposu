# Child of Another Kind

**Author:** Steven Decker  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

**Child of Another Kind: A Gripping Saga of Betrayal and Bloodshed**

In the vein of Conn Iggulden and Lindsey Davis, Steven Decker's "Child of Another Kind" is a breathtaking historical adventure that masterfully weaves together murder, betrayal, bloodshed, and romance. Set against the backdrop of the First Crusade, this novel is a vivid and convincing portrayal of Byzantium and the intricate power struggles that threaten its destruction.

At the heart of the story is Demetrios Askiates, the Emperor's spy, who has grown weary of the crusade's violence and hypocrisy. His longing for home is shattered when a routine diplomatic mission turns into a deadly ambush, ensnaring him in the vast power struggles that underlie the crusade. With no choice but to accompany the army of warlords and fanatics to Jerusalem, Demetrios must navigate a treacherous path through the plague-ridden city of Antioch and the heart of Muslim Egypt.

Decker's prose is engaging and accessible, effortlessly drawing the reader into the unfamiliar time and bringing alive the characters and their motivations. He expertly balances the political intrigue and action-packed sequences, keeping the reader on the edge of their seat until the very end.

The novel explores the theme of betrayal in all its forms - from the personal to the political - and the consequences it has on the individual and the state. Decker masterfully portrays the moral ambiguity of the crusade, challenging the reader to question their own loyalties and beliefs.

"Child of Another Kind" is a must-read for fans of historical fiction, especially those who enjoy complex characters, intricate plots, and a healthy dose of bloodshed. It is a real joy to read, gripping and exciting right to the end. This is a superb read that keeps you wanting to read more.
