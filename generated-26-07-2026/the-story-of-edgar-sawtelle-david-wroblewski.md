# The Story of Edgar Sawtelle

**Author:** David Wroblewski  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

"The Story of Edgar Sawtelle" is a captivating retelling of Shakespeare's Hamlet, set against the backdrop of a rural Wisconsin farm, where the Sawtelle family has long raised and trained a unique breed of dog. Author David Wroblewski masterfully weaves a tale that is at once a coming-of-age story, a mystery, and a profound exploration of language, loyalty, and betrayal.

At the heart of the novel lies Edgar, the mute protagonist, who communicates solely through sign language. His silence, however, is not a barrier to understanding the world around him, nor does it prevent him from forming deep connections with his family and their dogs. Wroblewski's prose is lyrical and evocative, painting vivid portraits of the natural world and the intricate bonds between humans and animals. The dogs, with their distinct personalities and abilities, are not merely secondary characters but integral to the narrative, serving as mirrors to the human condition.

The story unfolds as a mystery, with Edgar's father's sudden death and his stepfather's suspicious behavior driving the plot. Wroblewski expertly builds tension, leading the reader through a labyrinth of secrets and deceit. Yet, the novel is not merely a thriller; it is a meditation on the power and limitations of language. Edgar's silence forces him to observe the world with heightened sensitivity, and his inner monologues are a testament to the richness of human thought and emotion, independent of spoken words.

Wroblewski's style is reminiscent of Cormac McCarthy's, with a spare, poetic prose that is both accessible and profound. He has a knack for capturing the essence of a scene or emotion in a single, beautifully crafted sentence. The novel's pacing is deliberate, allowing the reader to become fully immersed in the world Wroblewski has created.

"The Story of Edgar Sawtelle" is a remarkable feat of storytelling, a modern-day classic that transcends its Hamlet-inspired origins. It is a novel about the power of observation, the strength of silence, and the enduring bonds between humans and animals. Wroblewski's debut is a testament to the enduring power of literature to captivate, challenge, and inspire.
