# Still Life with Woodpecker

**Author:** Tom Robbins  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1984

In the inimitable style of Tom Robbins, Still Life with Woodpecker is a mesmerizing and unorthodox tale that defies easy categorization. Part love story, part philosophical treatise, and part social commentary, this novel is a wildly inventive exploration of the human condition. Set against the unlikely backdrop of a pack of Camel cigarettes, Robbins's narrative is a dizzying kaleidoscope of ideas, characters, and themes that will leave readers enchanted and bewildered in equal measure.

At its core, Still Life with Woodpecker is a book about the tension between social activism and romantic individualism. Robbins's protagonist, Leigh-Cheri, is a young woman torn between her desire for personal freedom and her commitment to a cause. Her story is intertwined with that of Bernard Mickey Wrangle, a charismatic outlaw with a penchant for explosives and a passion for life. As their paths converge, Robbins uses their relationship to examine the complexities of social responsibility and the role of the individual in shaping the world.

One of the most striking aspects of Robbins's style is his use of language. His prose is a joyous, effervescent blend of humor, wit, and lyricism, capable of conveying complex ideas and emotions with remarkable precision. Whether he's riffing on the symbolism of the moon, the mythology of redheads, or the absurdities of contemporary society, Robbins's writing is always engaging, often hilarious, and occasionally profound.

Throughout the book, Robbins's philosophical musings are woven seamlessly into the narrative, adding depth and complexity to the story. He tackles a wide range of topics, from the nature of love and relationships to the impact of technology on human experience. While some readers may find his ideas challenging or unconventional, Robbins's enthusiasm and intellectual curiosity are infectious, making even the most skeptical readers want to engage with his vision.

If there's a flaw to Still Life with Woodpecker, it's that the book can be overwhelming at times. Robbins's imagination is so fertile, his ideas so numerous and varied, that the narrative occasionally threatens to spin out of control. However, this is a minor quibble, and most readers will find themselves swept up in the sheer energy and inventiveness of the story.

In short, Still Life with Woodpecker is a remarkable novel that showcases Tom Robbins's unique blend of humor, philosophy, and literary flair. It's a book that will appeal to fans of experimental fiction, social commentary, and offbeat humor, and its exploration of the human condition makes it a compelling read for anyone interested in the complexities of love, relationships, and personal freedom.
