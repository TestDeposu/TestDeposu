# Eona: The Last Dragoneye (Eon, #2)

**Author:** Alison Goodman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

In the realm of young adult fantasy, few authors have managed to weave a tale as intricate and captivating as Alison Goodman's Eona: The Last Dragoneye. This sequel to the bestselling Eon is a masterclass in storytelling, with a narrative that is both mesmerizingly beautiful and viciously treacherous.

At its core, Eona is a story about the struggle for power and control. The protagonist, Eona, once disguised as a boy, has become the Mirror Dragoneye, a position of immense power and responsibility. However, she soon discovers that her newfound abilities come with a steep price: she cannot control her power. Each time she tries, she opens herself to the ten spirit dragons whose Dragoneyes were murdered, and her ability twists into a killing force.

This internal conflict is expertly woven throughout the narrative, as Eona grapples with the weight of her responsibilities and the danger that her power poses. Goodman's writing is evocative and immersive, drawing the reader into Eona's world and refusing to let go. The action is pulse-pounding, with unforgettable fight scenes that will leave readers on the edge of their seats.

One of the most striking aspects of Goodman's writing is her ability to create complex, morally ambiguous characters. Eona is a flawed and relatable protagonist, driven by a desire to do what is right, but often forced to make difficult choices in order to survive. The supporting cast is equally well-developed, with characters like Kygo, the young Pearl Emperor, and Lord Ido, Eona's old enemy, adding depth and nuance to the narrative.

The world-building in Eona is also noteworthy, with a richly detailed and immersive environment that draws heavily from Asian mythology and culture. Goodman's use of language is vivid and descriptive, painting a picture of a world that is both familiar and yet, utterly unique.

If there is one criticism to be made, it is that the pacing can be somewhat uneven at times. Goodman's narrative is complex and layered, with multiple plot threads that sometimes threaten to overwhelm the reader. However, this is a minor quibble in what is otherwise a masterful tale.

In short, Eona: The Last Dragoneye is a must-read for fans of young adult fantasy. With its pulse-pounding action, complex characters, and immersive world-building, it is a story that will captivate and inspire. Goodman's writing is a testament to the power of storytelling, and Eona is a fitting conclusion to an epic series.
