# Need (Need, #1)

**Author:** Carrie Jones  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

**A Culinary Journey Through Time and Diet: Carrie Jones' "Need"**

In "Need," Carrie Jones presents a unique and captivating exploration of personal growth and dietary evolution, one recipe at a time. This isn't your typical cookbook; it's a memoir in meals, a testament to the power of food to shape our lives and our health.

Jones takes us on a journey through her life, one dish at a time. Each chapter begins with a story from her past, a moment etched in her memory by the scent and taste of a particular dish. These are the recipes of her childhood, filled with nostalgia and warmth, like the comforting aroma of her grandmother's apple pie.

But "Need" isn't just a nostalgic trip down memory lane. It's a testament to change and adaptation. Jones' health journey forces her to reevaluate her relationship with food, leading her to create adapted versions of her favorite childhood dishes. These recipes are a reflection of her teenage years, a time of growth and discovery, where she learns to balance her health needs with her love for food.

The final recipe in each chapter is a vegan version of the original dish, a reflection of Jones' current dietary preferences. This isn't just a change in diet; it's a statement of her evolution as a person. Through her recipes, Jones shows us that our relationship with food is fluid, evolving with us as we grow and change.

Jones' writing style is intimate and engaging, like a conversation with an old friend. She weaves her stories around her recipes, making them more than just instructions; they're narratives, filled with emotion and personal history. Her recipes are not just about the ingredients, but about the moments they represent.

"Need" is more than a cookbook; it's a journey of self-discovery, a testament to the power of food to shape our lives. It's a reminder that our relationship with food is complex and ever-changing, and that's okay. It's a celebration of growth and adaptation, of learning to love food in all its forms.
