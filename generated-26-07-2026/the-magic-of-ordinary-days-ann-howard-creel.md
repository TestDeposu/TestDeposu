# The Magic of Ordinary Days

**Author:** Ann Howard Creel  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2001

The Magic of Ordinary Days, written by Ann Howard Creel, is a captivating tale of self-discovery, love, and resilience set against the backdrop of World War II. The story follows Olivia Dunne, a minister's daughter with a passion for archaeology, as she navigates the complexities of her new life in a rural Colorado outpost. After a whirlwind romance, Olivia finds herself married to a man she barely knows, and her life is forever changed.

Creel's writing style is evocative and immersive, drawing the reader into the quiet, often lonely world of Olivia's new life. The author's use of language is lyrical, with a focus on the nuances of human emotion and the beauty of the natural world. As Olivia struggles to establish a new life, Creel's prose brings the reader along on her journey, conveying the sense of disorientation and disconnection that can come with sudden change.

One of the most striking aspects of the book is its exploration of the human experience during wartime. Creel does not shy away from the harsh realities of the period, including the internment of Japanese-Americans, but she also finds moments of beauty and hope in the midst of turmoil. The friendship between Olivia and the two Japanese-American sisters from the nearby internment camp is a powerful example of this, highlighting the ways in which human connection can transcend even the most difficult circumstances.

Throughout the book, Creel raises important questions about trust, love, and identity. As Olivia navigates her new life, she must confront her own desires and reconcile them with the harsh realities of the world around her. This is a story about the power of ordinary days to transform us, to challenge our assumptions, and to help us grow. It is also a story about the resilience of the human spirit, and the ways in which we can find hope and beauty even in the darkest of times.

The Magic of Ordinary Days is a beautifully written, deeply moving novel that will resonate with readers long after they finish the book. Creel's writing is masterful, and her story is both a tribute to the past and a powerful exploration of the human experience. With its themes of love, loss, and self-discovery, this book is sure to appeal to fans of historical fiction, women's fiction, and literary fiction.
