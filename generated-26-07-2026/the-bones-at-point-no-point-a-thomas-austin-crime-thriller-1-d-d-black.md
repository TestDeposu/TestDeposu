# The Bones at Point No Point (A Thomas Austin Crime Thriller #1)

**Author:** D.D. Black  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2026

The Bones at Point No Point, the latest novel from D.D. Black, is a masterfully crafted, philosophical, and deeply unsettling murder mystery that defies easy categorization. By weaving together elements of science fiction, horror, and spirituality, Black has created a narrative that is both thought-provoking and deeply unnerving.

At its core, The Bones at Point No Point is a meditation on the nature of existence, the meaning of life, and the human condition. Through the character of Vessel Iris, a death monk who has devoted his life to guiding souls back into the Infinite Light, Black explores the complexities of faith, spirituality, and the search for connection in a seemingly meaningless universe. Iris's relationships with the living are fraught with difficulty, and his reliance on AI companionship serves as a poignant reminder of the loneliness that can accompany even the most deeply held convictions.

The discovery of the long-lost spaceship, Counsel of Nicaea, provides the catalyst for the events that unfold. As a team of academics and researchers boards the ship, they are met with a strange and foreboding environment that is both fascinating and terrifying. The ship itself has become a kind of ecosystem, with moss and vines covering every surface, and a sense of unease that permeates every corner. It is here that Iris's skills as a death monk are called upon, as he attempts to bring peace to the long-departed souls of the ship's crew.

However, it soon becomes apparent that the plant life on the ship is not the only sentience that has survived the past millennia. A malevolent presence stalks the explorers, picking them off one by one, and Iris finds himself at the forefront of the battle for survival. With his AI-enhanced abilities, he may be the only one who can uncover the truth behind the sinister force that is driving the killings.

Throughout the novel, Black's prose is lyrical and evocative, conjuring vivid images of the ship and its surroundings. His characters are multidimensional and complex, with rich inner lives that add depth and nuance to the narrative. The pacing is deliberate and measured, building tension slowly as the events on the ship become increasingly dire.

One of the most striking aspects of The Bones at Point No Point is its use of philosophical and spiritual themes to explore the human condition. Black draws on a range of influences, from the existentialism of Camus to the spiritualism of various Eastern traditions, to create a narrative that is both deeply personal and universally relatable. The result is a novel that is as much about the search for meaning and connection as it is about the horrors that lurk in the darkness.

In short, The Bones at Point No Point is a masterpiece of modern science fiction. It is a novel that will appeal to fans of authors such as Becky Chambers, S.A. Barnes, and A.D. Sui, and one that will linger in the reader's mind long after the final page has been turned.
