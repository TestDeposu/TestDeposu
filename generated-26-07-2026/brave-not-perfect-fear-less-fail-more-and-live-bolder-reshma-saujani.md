# Brave, Not Perfect: Fear Less, Fail More, and Live Bolder

**Author:** Reshma Saujani  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

"Brave, Not Perfect" by Reshma Saujani is a powerful and timely book that challenges women to reevaluate their relationship with perfection and instead choose bravery. Saujani, the founder and CEO of Girls Who Code, draws on her own experiences and insights to urge women to let go of the fear of not being good enough and live a more authentic life.

The book is inspired by Saujani's popular TED Talk, and it's clear that she has a gift for communicating complex ideas in a clear and compelling way. Her writing is engaging, accessible, and free of jargon, making the book feel like a conversation with a wise and supportive friend.

At its core, "Brave, Not Perfect" is about the ways in which societal expectations can hold women back. Saujani argues that from a young age, girls are taught to prioritize perfection over bravery, and that this can lead to a lifetime of playing it safe and avoiding risk. She contends that this approach not only limits women's potential but also leads to feelings of anxiety, self-doubt, and burnout.

Saujani's central argument is that bravery, not perfection, is the key to living a fulfilling and joyful life. She offers a range of practical strategies and insights to help women develop the courage to take risks, speak their truth, and pursue their passions. From embracing imperfection and uncertainty to developing a growth mindset and building resilience, Saujani provides a roadmap for women who want to break free from the constraints of perfectionism.

One of the strengths of the book is its emphasis on the importance of community and support. Saujani recognizes that women often feel isolated and alone in their struggles, and she encourages readers to seek out like-minded women who can offer encouragement and support. She also highlights the need for women to lift each other up and celebrate each other's successes, rather than competing and comparing.

Throughout the book, Saujani shares stories from her own life and the lives of other women who have learned to prioritize bravery over perfection. These stories are inspiring and relatable, and they help to illustrate the practical applications of Saujani's ideas.

If you're a woman who feels like you're stuck in a cycle of self-doubt and perfectionism, "Brave, Not Perfect" is a must-read. Saujani's message is one of hope and empowerment, and her book has the potential to be a game-changer for women who are ready to break free from the constraints of societal expectations.

In short, "Brave, Not Perfect" is a powerful and inspiring book that challenges women to rethink their relationship with perfection and instead choose bravery. With its engaging writing style, practical strategies, and emphasis on community and support, it's a must-read for anyone who wants to live a more authentic and fulfilling life.
