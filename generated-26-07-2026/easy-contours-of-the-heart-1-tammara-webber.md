# Easy (Contours of the Heart, #1)

**Author:** Tammara Webber  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

In "Easy" by Tammara Webber, the lines between truth and deception, trust and betrayal, are expertly blurred, creating a narrative that is both poignant and thought-provoking. This New York Times bestseller and Goodreads Choice Finalist tells the story of Jacqueline, a college student whose life is turned upside down after a painful breakup and a traumatic assault.

Through Jacqueline's journey, Webber masterfully explores the complexities of trust, loyalty, and the human condition. The protagonist's struggles to come to terms with her past and navigate her present are deeply relatable, making her a compelling and sympathetic character. The author's portrayal of Jacqueline's emotional turmoil is raw and unflinching, yet ultimately hopeful and empowering.

One of the most striking aspects of "Easy" is its thought-provoking exploration of the consequences of trauma and the importance of human connection in the healing process. Webber tackles these heavy themes with sensitivity and nuance, never shying away from the harsh realities of Jacqueline's experiences. At the same time, the novel is also a powerful exploration of the resilience of the human spirit and the transformative power of relationships.

The writing style in "Easy" is engaging and accessible, making it easy to become fully immersed in the world of the novel. Webber's prose is descriptive and evocative, yet never overly flowery or melodramatic. The pacing is well-balanced, with a narrative that flows smoothly from one scene to the next.

The character of Lucas, the stranger who rescues Jacqueline from her attacker, is particularly noteworthy. His complexities and contradictions make him a fascinating and believable character, and his relationship with Jacqueline is authentic and heartfelt. The chemistry between the two is palpable, and their romance is a beautiful and tender aspect of the novel.

If there is one criticism to be made, it is that the supporting characters in "Easy" are not always as fully fleshed out as the protagonists. Some of the secondary characters feel a bit one-dimensional, which can make it difficult to become fully invested in their stories. However, this is a minor quibble in an otherwise excellent novel.

Overall, "Easy" is a gripping and emotionally resonant novel that will appeal to fans of contemporary fiction and romance. Webber's writing is engaging and thought-provoking, and her exploration of the human condition is both nuanced and powerful. This is a novel that will stay with readers long after they finish reading it, and one that will leave a lasting impression.
