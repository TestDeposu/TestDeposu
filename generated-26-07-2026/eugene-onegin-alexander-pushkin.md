# Eugene Onegin

**Author:** Alexander Pushkin  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1998

In the realm of Russian literature, few works have captivated readers with the same level of nuance and complexity as Alexander Pushkin's magnum opus, Eugene Onegin. This 1998 translation, part of the esteemed Oxford World's Classics series, brings to life the poet's favorite work in a richly annotated and expertly introduced edition that is sure to enthrall both scholars and casual readers alike.

Set against the backdrop of 1820s imperial Russia, Eugene Onegin is a sweeping tale of love, loss, and the human condition, told through the interconnected lives of six central characters. At its core, the novel in verse is a scathing critique of the societal conventions that governed the lives of the Russian nobility, as embodied by the eponymous Eugene Onegin, a bored and world-weary fop who embodies the very essence of ennui.

Through Onegin's character, Pushkin masterfully skewers the pretensions of the aristocracy, laying bare the hollowness of their existence and the devastating consequences of their actions. His is a world of stifling social norms, where emotions are repressed, and true connections are sacrificed at the altar of propriety.

And yet, despite the bleakness of this world, Pushkin's prose is infused with a wit and humor that is nothing short of remarkable. His satirical eye misses nothing, from the absurdities of high society to the posturings of the literary elite. It is a testament to the author's genius that he can balance such biting satire with moments of profound tenderness and insight, as in the character of Tatyana, the provincial beauty whose unrequited love for Onegin serves as the novel's emotional linchpin.

One of the most striking aspects of Eugene Onegin is its innovative use of language, which blends the formal elegance of poetry with the narrative drive of prose. Pushkin's masterful use of meter and rhyme creates a sense of musicality that is nothing short of enchanting, drawing the reader into a world of heightened emotions and vivid imagery.

This new translation seeks to capture the essence of Pushkin's style, retaining both the literal sense and poetic music of the original. The result is a work that is at once faithful to the author's intentions and accessible to modern readers. The introduction provides a wealth of context and background information, while the annotations offer valuable insights into the novel's many literary and philosophical allusions.

In short, Eugene Onegin is a masterpiece of Russian literature, a work of profound beauty and wit that continues to captivate readers to this day. This new translation is a triumph, bringing Pushkin's beloved novel to life in a way that is both authentic and engaging. Whether you are a scholar of Russian literature or simply a lover of great books, Eugene Onegin is a must-read that is sure to leave a lasting impression.
