**Preview/Critique: "Right in Michigan's Grassroots" by JoEllen Vinyard**

In "Right in Michigan's Grassroots," JoEllen Vinyard masterfully unravels the complex tapestry of twentieth-century American political extremism, using Michigan as her lens. This is not a tale of outliers or fringe elements, but a compelling exploration of how everyday men and women, driven by their times and circumstances, shaped the state's political landscape.

Vinyard's strength lies in her meticulous research and balanced narrative. She delves deep into primary sources, including organizational files and interviews with leaders and members, to paint a nuanced portrait of extremist groups like the Ku Klux Klan, Father Coughlin's followers, the John Birch Society, and the Michigan Militia. She doesn't shy away from the ugly truths, but neither does she sensationalize. Instead, she contextualizes, explaining the historical circumstances that led to these groups' emergence and resonated with Michiganders.

Vinyard's prose is engaging and accessible, making complex historical analysis readable for both academic and general audiences. She weaves together political history, social dynamics, and personal stories, creating a rich, multi-layered narrative that keeps the reader turning pages.

At its core, "Right in Michigan's Grassroots" is a cautionary tale about the power of grassroots movements and the dangers of extremism. It reminds us that political extremism is not a distant, foreign phenomenon, but a homegrown product of our shared history. Vinyard's work is a significant contribution to Michigan history and a timely exploration of a topic that continues to resonate in contemporary politics.
