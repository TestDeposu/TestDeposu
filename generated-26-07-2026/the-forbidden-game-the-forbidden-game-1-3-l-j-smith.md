# The Forbidden Game (The Forbidden Game, #1-3)

**Author:** L.J. Smith  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

**A Chilling Game of Chance and Desire**

In "The Forbidden Game" trilogy, L.J. Smith, renowned for her "Vampire Diaries" series, weaves a dark and enthralling tale that blurs the lines between reality and nightmare. The story revolves around Jenny Thornton, a seemingly ordinary teenager who, in her quest to make her boyfriend's birthday memorable, stumbles upon a board game that promises to reveal secrets and indulge dark desires. Little does she know that this game, sold by the enigmatic and preternaturally gorgeous store owner, will unleash a supernatural force determined to claim its players.

Smith's narrative style is engaging and suspenseful, drawing readers in from the very first page. She masterfully creates a sense of foreboding, hinting at the sinister forces at play without revealing too much too soon. The story is told primarily from Jenny's perspective, allowing readers to experience the chilling events as she does, heightening the tension and fear.

The main theme of "The Forbidden Game" is the dangerous allure of the unknown and the consequences of curiosity. Jenny's innocent desire to create a memorable experience for her boyfriend leads her to a world of darkness and danger. The game, once opened, comes to life, pulling the teens into its realm ruled by the Shadow Man, Julian. He brings their nightmares to life, daring them to survive, all while making it clear that Jenny is his ultimate prize.

Smith's exploration of the supernatural is reminiscent of her earlier works, but "The Forbidden Game" stands out with its unique premise and complex characters. The game itself is a fascinating entity, evolving and adapting with each challenge, keeping both the characters and readers on the edge of their seats.

The author's skill in creating compelling, relatable characters is evident in Jenny and her friends. Despite the extraordinary circumstances, their reactions and emotions feel genuine, making their struggles and fears all the more resonant. The dynamic between Jenny and Julian is particularly intriguing, oscillating between fear, fascination, and forbidden attraction.

However, the pacing of the story can feel uneven at times. While the first book is a gripping introduction to the game and its rules, the second and third books could have benefited from a tighter narrative. Some scenes feel repetitive, and the resolution of certain plot points could have been more satisfying.

Despite these minor flaws, "The Forbidden Game" is a gripping and atmospheric trilogy that will appeal to fans of dark fantasy, supernatural suspense, and coming-of-age stories with a twist. Smith's signature style shines through, creating a world that is both terrifying and mesmerizing.
