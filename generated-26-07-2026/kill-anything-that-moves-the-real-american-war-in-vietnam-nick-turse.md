# Kill Anything That Moves: The Real American War in Vietnam

**Author:** Nick Turse  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

Kill Anything That Moves: The Real American War in Vietnam, a meticulously researched and deeply unsettling expose by Nick Turse, shatters the long-held myth that atrocities committed during the Vietnam War were isolated incidents perpetrated by rogue soldiers. This New York Times bestseller and winner of the 2014 American Book Award and the Ridenhour Prize for Reportorial Distinction lays bare the dark truth that violence against Vietnamese civilians was not only pervasive but also systematically encouraged by official military orders.

Turse's investigation, spanning over a decade, delves into secret Pentagon archives and draws on extensive interviews with American veterans and Vietnamese survivors, revealing a military machine that resulted in the deaths and injuries of millions of innocent civilians. The sheer scale of the devastation is staggering, with one soldier describing the carnage as "a My Lai a month." The author's meticulous research and masterful storytelling weave together a narrative that is both devastating and definitive, forcing readers to confront the harsh realities of a war that continues to haunt America to this day.

One of the most striking aspects of Turse's work is his ability to balance the macrocosm of military policy with the microcosm of individual experiences. By juxtaposing official documents with personal accounts, he humanizes the victims of the war while also exposing the systemic failures that led to the atrocities. The result is a nuanced and multifaceted exploration of the conflict that eschews simplistic narratives and instead presents a richly textured portrait of a war that was both complex and deeply flawed.

Turse's writing style is characterized by a measured tone that belies the outrage and horror that permeate the narrative. His use of language is clear and concise, making the book accessible to a broad range of readers. However, the subject matter is unflinchingly graphic, and the author pulls no punches in his description of the atrocities committed during the war. This unvarnished portrayal is a testament to Turse's commitment to telling the truth, no matter how difficult or uncomfortable it may be.

Ultimately, Kill Anything That Moves is a searing indictment of the American military's conduct during the Vietnam War. It is a powerful and thought-provoking work that challenges readers to reexamine their assumptions about the conflict and its legacy. As the San Francisco Chronicle noted, the book is "an indispensable, paradigm-shifting new history of the war...All these decades later, Americans still haven't drawn the right lesson from Vietnam." Turse's work is a crucial step towards rectifying this oversight, and it is essential reading for anyone seeking to understand the true nature of the Vietnam War.
