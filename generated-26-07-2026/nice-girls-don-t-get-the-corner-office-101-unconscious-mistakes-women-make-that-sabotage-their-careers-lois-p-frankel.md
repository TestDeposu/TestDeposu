# Nice Girls Don't Get the Corner Office: 101 Unconscious Mistakes Women Make That Sabotage Their Careers

**Author:** Lois P. Frankel  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2026

Nice Girls Don't Get the Corner Office: 101 Unconscious Mistakes Women Make That Sabotage Their Careers by Lois P. Frankel is a timely and essential guide for women seeking to break down the barriers that hinder their career advancement. This revised and updated 3rd edition is a testament to the enduring relevance of Dr. Frankel's work, which has been empowering women since 2004.

The book's central theme is the "nice girl" mentality, a set of behaviors learned in girlhood that can ultimately sabotage women's careers. Dr. Frankel argues that this mentality is rooted in societal expectations and can lead to unconscious mistakes that hold women back from achieving their full potential. Through 101 examples of these mistakes, Dr. Frankel provides a comprehensive roadmap for women to recognize, challenge, and overcome these self-limiting behaviors.

One of the strengths of the book is its accessibility and practicality. Dr. Frankel's writing style is engaging, conversational, and free of jargon, making the book an enjoyable and easy read. The examples she provides are relatable and realistic, allowing readers to see themselves in the scenarios described. The advice and guidance offered are actionable and achievable, providing women with a sense of control and agency over their careers.

The book is organized around the 101 mistakes, each of which is concisely described and followed by practical advice on how to overcome it. This structure makes the book easy to navigate and allows readers to focus on the areas that resonate with them the most. Dr. Frankel's use of anecdotes and real-life examples adds depth and nuance to the book, making it feel more like a coaching session than a traditional self-help book.

Some of the mistakes highlighted in the book include believing negative self-talk, fearing coming across too strong, and having poor boundaries. Dr. Frankel shows how these mistakes can hold women back from achieving their goals and provides practical strategies for overcoming them. For example, she suggests counteracting negative self-talk with positive affirmations and setting clear boundaries when working from home.

Throughout the book, Dr. Frankel emphasizes the importance of self-awareness, assertiveness, and leadership. She encourages women to take control of their careers, speak their minds, and chase their ambitions without fear or shame. The book is not just about overcoming mistakes but also about building confidence, resilience, and a growth mindset.

In conclusion, Nice Girls Don't Get the Corner Office is a must-read for any woman seeking to advance her career. Dr. Frankel's book is a powerful tool for recognizing and overcoming the unconscious mistakes that hold women back. With its practical advice, engaging writing style, and relatable examples, this book is an essential resource for women seeking to take control of their careers and achieve their full potential.
