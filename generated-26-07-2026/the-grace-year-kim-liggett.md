# The Grace Year

**Author:** Kim Liggett  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

In the haunting and visceral world of Kim Liggett's The Grace Year, the lines between reality and myth blur, and the true horrors of a patriarchal society are revealed. This speculative thriller, reminiscent of The Handmaid's Tale and The Power, delves into the complex and twisted relationships between girls, the women they become, and the difficult decisions they make in-between.

At its core, The Grace Year is a scathing critique of a society that seeks to control and manipulate women's bodies, desires, and lives. In the fictional Garner County, 16-year-old girls are banished for their "grace year," a period of supposed magical potency that makes them a threat to the established order. This forced exile is justified by the notion that their skin emits a powerful aphrodisiac, a myth that serves to further oppress and marginalize them.

Liggett's prose is sharp and unflinching, laying bare the brutal realities of a world where women are pitted against each other, and their worth is determined by their ability to conform to societal norms. The author's gritty realism is unapologetic, making for a reading experience that is both uncomfortable and thought-provoking.

Through the eyes of Tierney James, the protagonist, we witness the inner workings of a society that seeks to break the spirits of its women. Tierney's dreams of a better life, one where women are not forced to compete with each other for the attention of men, are juxtaposed with the harsh realities of her world. As she navigates the treacherous landscape of her grace year, she must confront the darkness within herself and the world around her.

The relationships between the girls in the novel are multifaceted and complex, reflecting the ways in which women are socialized to interact with each other. Liggett masterfully explores the tensions and alliances that form between the girls, revealing the ways in which they both support and betray each other.

One of the most striking aspects of The Grace Year is its examination of the ways in which women internalize their oppression. The girls in the novel are often their own worst enemies, perpetuating the very myths and stereotypes that seek to control them. This self-perpetuating cycle of oppression is a powerful commentary on the ways in which societal norms can become ingrained in our psyche.

Liggett's writing is evocative and immersive, conjuring a world that is both familiar and yet utterly alien. Her use of language is deliberate and precise, creating a sense of tension and foreboding that propels the reader forward.

In conclusion, The Grace Year is a searing critique of patriarchal societies and the ways in which they seek to control women's bodies and lives. Liggett's writing is unflinching and powerful, making for a reading experience that is both haunting and thought-provoking. This novel is a must-read for anyone interested in feminist literature and speculative fiction.
