# The Last Thing She Ever Did

**Author:** Gregg Olsen  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2027

In the gripping new thriller, "The Last Thing She Ever Did" by Gregg Olsen, the dark waters of the Pacific Northwest hold secrets that can no longer stay submerged. This pulse-pounding tale of murder, mystery, and redemption is set to captivate fans of William Kent Krueger, Peter Heller, David Heska Wanbli Weiden, and Nick Medina, with its unique blend of forensics, Native American culture, and small-town politics.

At the heart of the story is Birdy Waterman, a brilliant and fiercely determined Native American forensic pathologist, whose past is inextricably linked to the present. The discovery of a young woman's body, bound in a manner eerily reminiscent of her best friend's unsolved murder sixteen years ago, sets Birdy on a perilous quest for justice. As she delves deeper into the case, she must confront her own demons, defy those who seek to exploit her, and navigate the treacherous landscape of her own community.

Olsen's masterful storytelling weaves together multiple threads, expertly crafting a complex narrative that is both a thrilling whodunit and a nuanced exploration of Birdy's inner world. With each twist and turn, the tension builds, propelling the reader toward a shocking climax that will leave even the most seasoned thriller enthusiasts gasping.

One of the standout features of "The Last Thing She Ever Did" is its well-developed and relatable protagonist. Birdy is a richly drawn character, full of contradictions and complexities. Her struggles to balance her professional obligations with her personal vendetta are deeply human, making it impossible not to become invested in her journey. Olsen's portrayal of Birdy's Native American heritage is also noteworthy, adding a welcome layer of depth and authenticity to the narrative.

Throughout the book, Olsen's writing is evocative and immersive, conjuring the mist-shrouded landscapes and rugged coastlines of the Pacific Northwest. His prose is lean and efficient, propelling the story forward with a relentless energy that mirrors Birdy's own unyielding determination.

If there's one criticism to be made, it's that the supporting cast occasionally feels slightly one-dimensional. However, this is a minor quibble in an otherwise exceptional thriller. Olsen's handling of the plot is expert, and the pacing is well-balanced, making it easy to become fully absorbed in the world he's created.

Ultimately, "The Last Thing She Ever Did" is a gripping and emotionally resonant thriller that will appeal to fans of crime fiction and mystery. With its unique blend of forensics, Native American culture, and small-town politics, it's a must-read for anyone looking for a compelling and thought-provoking page-turner.
