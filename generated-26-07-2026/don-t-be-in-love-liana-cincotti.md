# Don't Be In Love

**Author:** Liana Cincotti  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2026

Liana Cincotti's latest novel, Don't Be In Love, is a captivating and relatable exploration of the complexities of love, friendship, and identity. Set against the vibrant backdrop of London's high society, the story follows Adelaide Adorno, a driven and independent university student, as she navigates the challenges of her senior year at a prestigious university. 

Adelaide's life becomes increasingly complicated when she meets Dorian Blackwood, the youngest son of a prominent British family, who is notorious for his good looks and high-profile dating life. Despite her initial reluctance, Adelaide finds herself drawn into a secret deal with Dorian, which threatens to upend her carefully constructed world. As their relationship evolves, Adelaide must confront her own biases and prejudices, particularly her long-held disbelief in love.

One of the most striking aspects of Cincotti's writing style is her ability to craft relatable and well-rounded characters. Adelaide is a particularly well-developed protagonist, whose thoughts and feelings are expertly woven throughout the narrative. Her struggles to balance her career ambitions with her growing feelings for Dorian are deeply human and relatable, making it easy for readers to become invested in her story.

Cincotti's portrayal of London's high society is also noteworthy, offering a fascinating glimpse into the lives of the rich and famous. The lavish galas, expensive gowns, and high-stakes events that Adelaide and Dorian attend are vividly described, transporting readers to a world of luxury and excess. However, Cincotti also skillfully exposes the underlying tensions and complexities of this world, revealing the cutthroat nature of the entertainment industry and the pressures of living up to familial expectations.

Throughout the novel, Cincotti explores themes of identity, class, and social status, raising important questions about the nature of love and relationships. Adelaide's experiences serve as a powerful commentary on the societal expectations placed on women, particularly those from lower socio-economic backgrounds. Her struggles to navigate the complexities of high society, while remaining true to herself, are a testament to the enduring power of self-discovery and growth.

The author's writing style is engaging and accessible, making it easy for readers to become fully immersed in the world of the novel. Cincotti's use of language is witty and insightful, with a keen eye for detail and a deep understanding of the human experience. The pacing of the novel is well-balanced, with a narrative that flows smoothly and effortlessly, drawing readers into the heart of the story.

Overall, Don't Be In Love is a compelling and thought-provoking novel that is sure to resonate with readers of all ages. With its relatable characters, engaging storyline, and insightful commentary on the human experience, this book is a must-read for anyone looking for a romantic and immersive story.
