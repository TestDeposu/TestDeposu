# Leviathan Wakes

**Author:** James S.A. Corey  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

**Leviathan Wakes: A Stellar Debut in Hard Science Fiction**

In the sprawling expanse of James S.A. Corey's debut novel, *Leviathan Wakes*, we are introduced to a future where humanity has colonized the solar system, yet remains trapped within its gravitational embrace. This is no star-trekking utopia, but a gritty, realistic portrayal of interplanetary politics, power struggles, and the human condition.

At the heart of the story are two compelling protagonists: Jim Holden, the idealistic executive officer of an ice miner, and Detective Miller, a world-weary investigator from Ceres station. Their paths cross when Holden's crew discovers the derelict ship, the *Scopuli*, and Miller is on the trail of a missing girl who may be connected to the mystery surrounding it.

Corey, the pen name of authors Daniel Abraham and Ty Franck, masterfully weaves a complex tapestry of intrigue, conspiracy, and action. The narrative is taut and engaging, with a pace that builds steadily towards a thrilling climax. The authors' backgrounds in fantasy and science fiction are evident in their ability to create vivid, believable worlds and intricate plotlines.

What sets *Leviathan Wakes* apart is its commitment to hard science fiction. Corey's meticulous research shines through in the detailed descriptions of life in the asteroid belt, the politics of the Outer Planets Alliance, and the technological advancements that allow humans to survive in the harshest environments. This attention to detail lends the story a sense of authenticity that is often lacking in space opera.

The novel's main theme is the struggle for power and resources in a world where humanity is no longer confined to a single planet. This is explored through the conflict between Earth and the Outer Planets Alliance, as well as the machinations of the corporations that control much of the solar system's economy. Holden and Miller, each in their own way, challenge these power structures, making them relatable heroes in a world that often feels overwhelmingly vast and indifferent.

Corey's style is straightforward and accessible, making *Leviathan Wakes* an excellent entry point for readers new to the genre. The dialogue is sharp and snappy, the action sequences are visceral, and the characters are well-drawn and engaging. The authors' use of multiple perspectives also adds depth to the story, allowing readers to see the events unfold from different angles.

In conclusion, *Leviathan Wakes* is a standout debut in the realm of hard science fiction. It offers a compelling narrative, well-drawn characters, and a thought-provoking exploration of power and resources in a future where humanity has taken to the stars. Fans of gritty, realistic space opera will find much to love in this novel.
