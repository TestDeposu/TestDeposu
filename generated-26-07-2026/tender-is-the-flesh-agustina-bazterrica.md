# Tender Is the Flesh

**Author:** Agustina Bazterrica  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

**Tender Is the Flesh: A Chilling Dystopia That Haunts the Palate and the Soul**

In Agustina Bazterrica's debut novel, "Tender Is the Flesh," the author serves up a disturbingly plausible dystopia that will leave readers questioning their own appetites and moral compasses. This is not a book for the faint-hearted, but for those who dare to venture into its dark heart, it offers a thought-provoking exploration of humanity's capacity for cruelty and compassion.

The story unfolds in a world where an infectious virus has made all animal meat poisonous to humans. In response, governments have initiated the "Transition," legalizing the consumption of human meat, or "special meat." Marcos, the protagonist, works at a local processing plant, where he is desensitized to his grim task, reducing humans to numbers and consignments. Yet, when he is given a live specimen, a young woman of exceptional quality, he finds it increasingly difficult to maintain his detachment.

Bazterrica's prose is stark and unyielding, much like the world she has created. She spares no detail in her descriptions of the processing plant, the stark reality of which is juxtaposed with the lush, almost sensual, descriptions of the "special meat" auctions. This contrast serves to highlight the perverse beauty that can be found in even the most horrifying of circumstances, and the way in which humanity can adapt to the most grotesque of realities.

At the heart of "Tender Is the Flesh" lies a profound exploration of humanity's capacity for empathy and cruelty. Marcos's struggle to maintain his detachment from his work, and his growing attachment to the young woman he has been tasked with processing, raises questions about the nature of humanity and the extent to which we are capable of dehumanizing others.

Bazterrica also delves into the ways in which society can normalize the most horrifying of practices, and the role that language plays in this normalization. By referring to humans as "specimens" and their flesh as "special meat," the characters in the novel are able to distance themselves from the reality of their actions. This is a chilling reminder of the ways in which language can be used to sanitize and justify the most horrifying of acts.

"Tender Is the Flesh" is not an easy read. It is a book that will haunt your dreams and leave you questioning your own beliefs and values. Yet, it is also a book that is impossible to put down, a testament to Bazterrica's skill as a storyteller and her ability to create a world that is both terrifying and mesmerizing in its horror.

In a world where the line between the human and the animal is blurred, and the act of consumption takes on a sinister significance, "Tender Is the Flesh" is a stark reminder of the ways in which we objectify and dehumanize others. It is a book that will leave you questioning your own appetites, and the extent to which you are willing to compromise your humanity in the pursuit of pleasure.
