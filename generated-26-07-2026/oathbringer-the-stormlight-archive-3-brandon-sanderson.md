# Oathbringer (The Stormlight Archive, #3)

**Author:** Brandon Sanderson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

In the sprawling epic fantasy of Brandon Sanderson's Stormlight Archive series, Oathbringer stands as a testament to the author's masterful storytelling and world-building prowess. The third installment in this sprawling saga, Oathbringer is a sweeping narrative that delves into the complexities of power, identity, and the true cost of unity.

Sanderson's writing style is a character in its own right, weaving together intricate plot threads and character arcs with a level of precision that is nothing short of breathtaking. His prose is both accessible and engaging, making it easy for new readers to jump into the world of Roshar, while still providing ample depth for fans of the series.

At its core, Oathbringer is a story about the consequences of actions, and the difficult choices that must be made in the face of overwhelming adversity. Dalinar Kholin, the Highprince of Alethkar, is forced to confront the darkness of his own past, and the devastating impact it has had on the world around him. Meanwhile, Kaladin Stormblessed must navigate the treacherous landscape of his own emotions, as he grapples with the moral implications of the war against the Parshendi.

One of the most striking aspects of Oathbringer is its thoughtful exploration of the nature of power, and the ways in which it can both unite and divide people. Sanderson presents a nuanced portrayal of the complexities of leadership, highlighting the difficult choices that must be made in the pursuit of a greater good. Through the characters of Dalinar and Kaladin, we see the human cost of these decisions, and the ways in which they can shape the course of history.

The world of Roshar itself is a character in its own right, with a richly detailed history and culture that adds depth and complexity to the narrative. Sanderson's use of magical systems and mythology is, as always, meticulous and well-researched, adding a level of authenticity to the world-building that is unparalleled in modern fantasy.

If there is one criticism to be made, it is that Oathbringer can feel overwhelming at times, with a vast array of characters and plot threads that can be difficult to keep track of. However, this is a minor quibble in what is otherwise a masterful work of epic fantasy.

In conclusion, Oathbringer is a triumph of modern fantasy, a sweeping narrative that explores the complexities of power, identity, and the true cost of unity. With its intricate world-building, nuanced characterization, and thoughtful exploration of the human condition, it is a must-read for fans of the genre.
