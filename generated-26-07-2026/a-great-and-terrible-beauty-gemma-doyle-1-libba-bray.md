# A Great and Terrible Beauty (Gemma Doyle, #1)

**Author:** Libba Bray  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2003

**A Great and Terrible Beauty: A Gothic Gem Bursting with Mystery and Magic**

In the vein of classic boarding school tales meets eerie supernatural, Libba Bray's "A Great and Terrible Beauty" is a captivating debut that weaves a spellbinding narrative around the enigmatic Gemma Doyle. Set in the late 19th century, this New York Times bestseller is a haunting exploration of destiny, friendship, and the dark allure of the unknown.

Gemma, a 16-year-old orphaned by her mother's suicide, is sent from the vibrant chaos of India to the rigid confines of Spence, an English boarding school. Struggling with guilt, loneliness, and unsettling visions of the future, Gemma finds solace in the company of three unlikely friends: Felicity, Pippa, and Ann. Together, they stumble upon a secret society, the Order of the Blue Rose, which delves into the mystical realm of the spiritual world.

Bray's prose is elegant and evocative, painting vivid images of Victorian England and the eerie beauty of the spiritual world. Her characters are complex and relatable, each with their own secrets and struggles. Gemma, in particular, is a compelling heroine, her strength and vulnerability making her a character to root for.

The story is a slow burn, building tension and suspense as Gemma and her friends navigate the treacherous waters of their newfound powers and the sinister forces that seek to control them. The gothic atmosphere is palpable, with Bray masterfully employing shadows and whispers to create a sense of foreboding.

At its heart, "A Great and Terrible Beauty" is a story about the power of friendship and the lengths we go to protect those we love. It's a tale that will captivate readers, drawing them into a world where the line between reality and illusion is blurred, and danger lurks in the most unexpected places.
