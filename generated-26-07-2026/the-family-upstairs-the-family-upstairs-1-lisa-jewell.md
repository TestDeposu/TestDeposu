# The Family Upstairs (The Family Upstairs, #1)

**Author:** Lisa Jewell  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

In "The Family Upstairs", Lisa Jewell masterfully weaves a complex and dark tale that delves into the intricacies of family dynamics, trauma, and the long-lasting effects of buried secrets. This gripping novel tells the story of Libby Jones, a young woman who inherits a mansion on the banks of the Thames in London's fashionable Chelsea neighborhood, only to discover that it holds the darkest of secrets.

As Libby navigates her newfound inheritance, she becomes entangled in a web of mysteries surrounding the family that once lived in the mansion. Twenty-five years prior, a horrific incident occurred at 16 Cheyne Walk, where three dead bodies were found, and four children went missing. The only clue left behind was a hastily scrawled note, leaving the police baffled and the community shaken.

Through Libby's journey, Jewell skillfully unravels the tangled threads of the past, slowly revealing the events that led up to the tragic incident. The novel seamlessly shifts between the past and present, expertly crafting a sense of tension and suspense that keeps readers on the edge of their seats.

Jewell's writing style is both haunting and atmospheric, perfectly capturing the eerie and foreboding atmosphere of the mansion. Her vivid descriptions of the setting transport readers to the misty banks of the Thames, immersing them in the world of the novel. The characters are equally well-crafted, with complex and multi-dimensional personalities that add depth to the narrative.

One of the standout aspects of "The Family Upstairs" is its exploration of the human psyche. Jewell masterfully delves into the minds of the characters, revealing their darkest fears, desires, and motivations. The novel raises important questions about the nature of trauma, the long-lasting effects of buried secrets, and the devastating consequences of keeping the truth hidden.

The theme of family is also a dominant force in the novel. Jewell expertly examines the complexities of family relationships, revealing the intricate web of secrets, lies, and half-truths that can bind families together. The novel shows how family dynamics can be both a source of comfort and a catalyst for destruction.

In conclusion, "The Family Upstairs" is a gripping and unsettling novel that will appeal to fans of domestic noir and psychological thrillers. Jewell's masterful storytelling, combined with her vivid descriptions and complex characters, makes for a compelling read that will keep readers guessing until the very end.
