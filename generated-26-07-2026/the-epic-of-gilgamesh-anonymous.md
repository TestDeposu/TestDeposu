# The Epic of Gilgamesh

**Author:** Anonymous  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

The Epic of Gilgamesh, one of the oldest surviving works of literature, has been revived in a captivating translation by Andrew George. This ancient Sumerian poem, dating back to around 2100 BC, tells the story of Gilgamesh, the king of Uruk, and his quest for immortality. The epic poem is a timeless masterpiece that explores the universal human fear of death, making it a must-read for anyone interested in literature, history, or philosophy.

The story begins with the introduction of Gilgamesh, a demigod and the ruler of Uruk, who is two-thirds god and one-third human. He is a tyrannical king, oppressing his people and taking whatever he wants, including their daughters. The people of Uruk pray to the gods for relief, and the gods create Enkidu, a wild man who is as strong as Gilgamesh. Enkidu is sent to challenge Gilgamesh and to distract him from his oppressive ways. The two men fight, but eventually, they become friends and embark on a series of adventures together.

One of these adventures is the quest for immortality, which is the central theme of the epic. Gilgamesh's friend Enkidu dies, and Gilgamesh is devastated by his loss. He sets out on a journey to discover the secret of immortality, so that he can prevent his own death and bring Enkidu back to life. He travels to the ends of the earth, braving many dangers and challenges along the way, in search of Utnapishtim, the Babylonian Noah, who is the only human to have survived the great flood and to have been granted immortality by the gods.

Throughout the epic, Andrew George's translation brings the ancient world to life with his vivid and engaging prose. His use of language is masterful, conveying the complexity and depth of the original Sumerian text. The translation is both scholarly and accessible, making the epic poem enjoyable for readers who are not familiar with ancient literature.

One of the most striking aspects of The Epic of Gilgamesh is its exploration of the human condition. The poem raises fundamental questions about the nature of humanity, the meaning of life, and the fear of death. Gilgamesh's quest for immortality is a powerful metaphor for the human desire to transcend mortality and to achieve eternal life. The epic also explores the themes of friendship, love, and the duties of kings, highlighting the complexities and nuances of human relationships.

The Epic of Gilgamesh is a masterpiece of ancient literature that continues to captivate readers with its timeless themes and universal appeal. Andrew George's translation is a significant achievement, bringing this ancient poem to life for a new generation of readers. If you are interested in literature, history, or philosophy, this book is a must-read.
