# Chickenhawk

**Author:** Robert Mason  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1984

"Chickenhawk" by Robert Mason is a visceral and unflinching portrayal of the Vietnam War, a true account that delves into the heart of the conflict with unbridled honesty. As a veteran of over one thousand combat missions, Mason's narrative is a testament to the extreme emotions and experiences that defined the war. This bestselling memoir, with over half a million copies sold, is a gripping and thought-provoking read that will leave readers on the edge of their seats.

Mason's writing style is straightforward and unapologetic, reflecting the harsh realities of war. His descriptions of combat missions are vivid and intense, conveying the fear, adrenaline, and chaos that characterized the helicopter war in Vietnam. The author's use of language is evocative and immersive, drawing the reader into the world of the "chickenhawk" – a term used to describe the pilots who flew the UH-1 Huey helicopters that were ubiquitous in the war.

Throughout the book, Mason explores the complexities of human emotion, revealing the contradictions and paradoxes that arise in the midst of conflict. He writes about the bonds of friendship forged in the heat of battle, the quiet moments of introspection, and the sheer terror that gripped him and his fellow soldiers. Mason's narrative is not just a personal story; it's a collective one, reflecting the experiences of countless men who fought in Vietnam.

One of the most striking aspects of "Chickenhawk" is its ability to convey the madness and trauma of war. Mason's descriptions of the combat experience are unflinching and often disturbing, revealing the psychological toll that the war took on those who fought it. The author's use of imagery and metaphor adds depth and complexity to the narrative, making the reader feel like they are experiencing the war firsthand.

The inclusion of photographs taken by Mason during the conflict adds a new layer of authenticity to the narrative. These images serve as a visual reminder of the war's brutality and devastation, underscoring the author's words with a stark and unflinching reality.

In the afterword, Mason reflects on the enduring impact of the war on his life and the lives of those who fought alongside him. This added perspective provides a sense of closure and reflection, highlighting the long-term effects of the conflict on those who experienced it.

In conclusion, "Chickenhawk" is a masterpiece of war literature, a searing and unforgettable account of the Vietnam War. Mason's writing is visceral, honest, and unflinching, making this book a must-read for anyone interested in the human experience of conflict. As the St. Louis Post-Dispatch noted, it's "very simply the best book so far about Vietnam."
