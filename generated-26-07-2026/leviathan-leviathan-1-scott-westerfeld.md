# Leviathan (Leviathan, #1)

**Author:** Scott Westerfeld  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

In "Leviathan", Scott Westerfeld crafts a captivating alternate history that delves into the complexities of World War I, infused with a unique blend of steampunk and science fiction elements. The novel's central premise revolves around the clash between two rival factions: the Clankers, with their mechanized iron machines, and the Darwinists, who wield genetically engineered creatures as their primary arsenal.

Westerfeld's masterful storytelling weaves together the narratives of two protagonists, Aleksandar Ferdinand and Deryn Sharp, who find themselves on opposing sides of the conflict. Aleksandar, a prince of the Austro-Hungarian Empire, is a skilled Clanker, while Deryn, a British airman, is a Darwinist who disguises herself as a boy to join the war effort. Their paths converge in unexpected ways, leading them to embark on a thrilling adventure aboard the majestic Leviathan, a whale airship that serves as the crown jewel of the British fleet.

Throughout the novel, Westerfeld explores the theme of identity and the blurred lines between technology and nature. The Clankers, with their emphasis on mechanization, represent a world where progress is measured by the efficiency of machines. In contrast, the Darwinists embody a world where the boundaries between humans and animals are constantly shifting. This dichotomy serves as a powerful metaphor for the tensions between tradition and innovation, as well as the consequences of playing with the fundamental building blocks of life.

Westerfeld's writing style is characterized by his ability to balance action, suspense, and humor, making "Leviathan" an engaging and immersive read. His vivid descriptions of the fantastical creatures and machines that inhabit this alternate world are a testament to his boundless imagination. The characters, too, are well-developed and complex, with Aleksandar and Deryn serving as compelling protagonists whose motivations and conflicts drive the narrative forward.

One of the most striking aspects of "Leviathan" is its ability to reimagine historical events through a steampunk lens. Westerfeld's vision of World War I is both familiar and yet, utterly alien, with the introduction of fantastical elements that add a layer of complexity to the conflict. This reimagining also allows Westerfeld to explore themes that are relevant to our contemporary world, such as the ethics of scientific progress and the consequences of unchecked nationalism.

In conclusion, "Leviathan" is a captivating and thought-provoking novel that will appeal to fans of science fiction, steampunk, and alternate history. With its unique blend of action, adventure, and social commentary, this book is a must-read for anyone looking to explore the boundaries of imagination and creativity. Westerfeld's masterful storytelling and vivid world-building make "Leviathan" a compelling and unforgettable read.
