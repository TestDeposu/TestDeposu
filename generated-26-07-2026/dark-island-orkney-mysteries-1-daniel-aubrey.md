# Dark Island (Orkney Mysteries, #1)

**Author:** Daniel Aubrey  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2024

In the midst of the harsh, six-hour daylight of an Orkney midwinter, Daniel Aubrey's Dark Island unravels a gripping tale of murder, mystery, and self-discovery. This Scottish crime ebook has already garnered significant attention, shortlisted for the McDermid Debut Award 2024 and the Bloody Scotland Debut Prize 2024, as well as being longlisted for Scotland's National Book Award – First Book of the Year 2024.

At the heart of the novel is Freya Sinclair, a neurodivergent reporter who has returned to her childhood home in Orkney, seeking a quieter life after quitting her job at a top Scottish broadsheet. However, her plans are quickly derailed when human remains are unearthed by a winter storm, sparking a dark and sinister chain of events. As Freya delves deeper into the mystery, her obsessive hunt for answers becomes a perilous cat-and-mouse game, threatening her job, family, and ultimately, her own life.

Aubrey's writing is both evocative and immersive, masterfully transporting the reader to the rugged and unforgiving landscape of Orkney. His vivid descriptions of the island's wild Atlantic coast, coupled with the eerie atmosphere of the midwinter setting, create a sense of foreboding that permeates the entire narrative.

One of the standout aspects of Dark Island is its well-crafted protagonist, Freya Sinclair. Aubrey's portrayal of a neurodivergent character is both authentic and nuanced, offering a refreshing perspective in the crime thriller genre. Freya's complexities and vulnerabilities make her a deeply relatable and human character, and her struggles to navigate the challenges of her investigation are both poignant and compelling.

Aubrey's style is reminiscent of renowned crime writers such as Ann Cleeves, with a keen sense of pacing and plotting that keeps the reader on the edge of their seat. The twists and turns of the narrative are expertly woven, making it impossible to predict the outcome until the very end. The author's use of language is concise and effective, conveying the tension and suspense that drives the story forward.

The themes of identity, community, and the power of the truth are skillfully interwoven throughout the narrative, adding depth and complexity to the story. Aubrey's exploration of these themes raises important questions about the human condition, making Dark Island a thought-provoking and unsettling read.

In conclusion, Dark Island is a gripping and atmospheric crime thriller that marks a stunning debut for Daniel Aubrey. With its unique protagonist, vivid setting, and masterful plotting, this novel is sure to captivate fans of the genre. As a reviewer, it's clear that Aubrey is a talented new voice in Scottish crime writing, and I eagerly anticipate his future work.
