# Savages (Savages #2)

**Author:** Don Winslow  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

Don Winslow's Savages is a gripping and unapologetic portrayal of the dark underbelly of the Southern California marijuana trade. This electrifying novel delves into the lives of Ben, Chon, and O, three twentysomething friends who have built a lucrative empire by producing high-quality marijuana. However, their success does not go unnoticed, and soon they find themselves at odds with the ruthless Mexican Baja Cartel.

One of the most striking aspects of Savages is its unflinching examination of the consequences of indulging in the high-stakes world of organized crime. Winslow's prose is visceral and unapologetic, hurtling the reader through a maze of intense negotiations, shocking plot twists, and brutal violence. The author's writing style is akin to a shot of adrenaline, propelling the narrative forward with breakneck speed and unwavering intensity.

At its core, Savages is a love story, albeit an unconventional one. The bond between Ben, Chon, and O is forged in the fires of their high-risk lifestyle, and their relationship is pushed to the breaking point when O is kidnapped by the cartel. As Ben and Chon navigate the treacherous landscape of the marijuana trade, they must confront the possibility of losing the person they love most. This central theme of love and loyalty is what elevates Savages from a mere crime thriller to a nuanced exploration of the human condition.

Winslow's characters are multidimensional and expertly crafted, with each one bringing their unique perspective to the narrative. Ben, the brains of the operation, is a brilliant and calculating individual who is driven by a desire to protect his friends. Chon, the former Navy SEAL, is a force of nature, using his military training to exact revenge on their enemies. O, the free-spirited and beautiful girlfriend, is the emotional heart of the novel, and her kidnapping serves as the catalyst for the chaos that ensues.

The author's use of language is noteworthy, as he employs a distinctive style that blends the gritty realism of a crime novel with the poetic flourishes of a literary masterpiece. Winslow's prose is replete with clever wordplay, witty dialogue, and a deep sense of empathy for his characters. This unique blend of style and substance makes Savages a truly unforgettable reading experience.

Throughout the novel, Winslow also explores themes of capitalism, addiction, and the corrupting influence of power. The marijuana trade is portrayed as a high-stakes game, where players must be willing to risk everything to succeed. The cartels, with their ruthless tactics and brutal violence, serve as a stark reminder of the consequences of involvement in this world.

In conclusion, Savages is a masterful thriller that will leave readers on the edge of their seats. With its breakneck pace, vivid characters, and unflinching exploration of the human condition, this novel is an absolute must-read for fans of crime fiction. Don Winslow's writing style is unlike anyone else's, and his unique voice brings a fresh perspective to the genre.
