# Linger (The Wolves of Mercy Falls, #2)

**Author:** Maggie Stiefvater  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

In the captivating world of Maggie Stiefvater's The Wolves of Mercy Falls series, Linger is a heart-stopping sequel that delves deeper into the complexities of love, loyalty, and the struggle for survival. Picking up where Shiver left off, Linger masterfully weaves together the intricate lives of its characters, introducing new conflicts and challenges that test the bonds of their relationships.

At the core of Linger is the universal theme of identity, as the characters grapple with their past, present, and future selves. For Sam, the werewolf, this means confronting the traumas of his history and learning to navigate his newfound humanity. His struggles are deeply personal, and Stiefvater's lyrical prose brings a haunting beauty to his inner turmoil. As Sam fights to maintain his human form, he must also confront the harsh realities of his werewolf past, threatening to upend the fragile balance of his relationship with Grace.

Speaking of which, Grace's character undergoes a significant transformation in Linger. Her determination to be with Sam, despite the danger it poses, is admirable, but it also comes with a steep price. Her health begins to deteriorate, and the secrets she keeps from her parents and Sam threaten to destroy the very foundations of her relationships. Stiefvater handles Grace's character with sensitivity and care, never shying away from the darker aspects of her experiences.

The introduction of Cole, a new wolf with a troubled past, adds a fresh layer of complexity to the narrative. His character is a perfect example of Stiefvater's skill in crafting multidimensional characters, full of contradictions and surprises. Cole's story is both heartbreaking and infuriating, making him an excellent addition to the cast.

Isabelle, who lost her brother to the wolves, is another character who undergoes significant development in Linger. Her fascination with Cole is both captivating and unsettling, as she finds herself drawn to the very thing that destroyed her family. Stiefvater's portrayal of Isabelle's inner conflict is nuanced and thought-provoking, raising important questions about the nature of attraction and the dangers of playing with fire.

Throughout Linger, Stiefvater's writing is as evocative and atmospheric as ever, conjuring the misty, mystical world of Mercy Falls with ease. Her prose is like a gentle breeze on a summer's day, soothing and calming, yet capable of whipping up into a frenzy when the situation demands it. The pacing is expertly handled, with a narrative that flows effortlessly from one scene to the next.

In conclusion, Linger is a mesmerizing sequel that cements Maggie Stiefvater's status as a masterful storyteller. With its themes of identity, loyalty, and survival, this book will resonate deeply with fans of the series and newcomers alike. If you're looking for a captivating read that will leave you breathless and yearning for more, look no further than Linger.
