# Crescendo (Hush, Hush, #2)

**Author:** Becca Fitzpatrick  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

In "Crescendo", the highly anticipated sequel to Becca Fitzpatrick's bestselling novel "Hush, Hush", readers are once again thrust into the tumultuous world of Nora Grey and her enigmatic love interest, Patch. This time around, Fitzpatrick delves deeper into the complexities of their relationship, expertly weaving a narrative that is both a tender exploration of love and a heart-pumping thrill ride.

At its core, "Crescendo" is a story about the blurred lines between truth and deception. Nora, still reeling from the events of the first book, finds herself increasingly entangled in a web of secrets and lies. As she struggles to navigate her relationship with Patch, who seems to be growing more and more distant, she begins to uncover clues that suggest her father's death may not have been the accident she once believed it to be. Fitzpatrick masterfully crafts a sense of tension and foreboding, leaving readers questioning everyone's motives and allegiances.

One of the standout aspects of "Crescendo" is its exploration of the darker side of love. Patch, with his devilish charm and mysterious past, is the epitome of the bad boy archetype. Yet, as the story progresses, his actions become increasingly suspect, leaving Nora (and readers) wondering if his intentions are truly pure. Fitzpatrick's portrayal of their relationship is both captivating and unsettling, highlighting the fine line between passion and obsession.

The author's writing style is, as always, engaging and accessible. Her prose is peppered with vivid descriptions and witty dialogue, making it easy to become fully immersed in the world she's created. The pacing is well-balanced, with a mix of action, suspense, and romance that will keep readers on the edge of their seats.

If there's one criticism to be made, it's that the supporting characters feel somewhat one-dimensional at times. Marcie Millar, in particular, is portrayed as a cartoonish villain, with motivations that feel more like a plot device than a genuine character arc. However, this is a minor quibble in an otherwise compelling narrative.

Overall, "Crescendo" is a gripping and emotionally charged read that will appeal to fans of paranormal romance and mystery alike. Fitzpatrick's writing is engaging, her characters are complex, and the plot is expertly crafted to keep readers guessing until the very end.
