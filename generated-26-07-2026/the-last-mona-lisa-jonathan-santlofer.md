# The Last Mona Lisa

**Author:** Jonathan Santlofer  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

The Last Mona Lisa, Jonathan Santlofer's 2021 masterpiece, is a gripping tale that masterfully weaves together art, history, and suspense. This crime novel delves into the infamous 1911 theft of the Mona Lisa from the Louvre, sending the protagonist, Luke Perrone, on a perilous journey to unravel the truth behind the painting's disappearance and the subsequent forgeries that emerged in its wake.

Santlofer's writing style is as captivating as it is informative, seamlessly blending historical facts with fiction to create a narrative that is both engaging and enlightening. His use of language is evocative, painting vivid pictures of the art world's underbelly, where obsession and danger lurk around every corner. The author's own background as a celebrated artist adds an air of authenticity to the novel, making the world of art and forgery feel all too real.

One of the standout aspects of The Last Mona Lisa is its ability to balance action and suspense with intellectual curiosity. Luke's quest for the truth is driven by a desire to understand the motivations behind the theft and the subsequent forgeries, and Santlofer's exploration of these themes raises important questions about the nature of art and authenticity. The novel is as much a mystery as it is a philosophical inquiry, and this blend of genres makes for a truly compelling read.

The characters in The Last Mona Lisa are equally well-crafted, with Luke Perrone being a particularly nuanced and relatable protagonist. His obsession with uncovering the truth about his ancestor, Vincent Peruggia, is palpable, and his interactions with the enigmatic woman who aids him in his quest are laced with tension and intrigue. The supporting cast, including the Interpol detective, adds depth and complexity to the narrative, making it impossible to predict what will happen next.

The Last Mona Lisa has been praised by authors such as Michael Connelly and Ruth Ware, and it's easy to see why. Santlofer's writing is reminiscent of the best in the crime genre, with a narrative that is both propulsive and thought-provoking. The novel's themes of art, history, and obsession will appeal to fans of Netflix documentaries such as This Is A Robbery and Made You Look, as well as readers who devour books about art heists and forgeries.

In short, The Last Mona Lisa is a must-read for anyone who loves a good mystery, is fascinated by the art world, or simply enjoys a well-crafted narrative. With its breakneck pace, intriguing characters, and intellectual curiosity, this novel is sure to keep you on the edge of your seat until the very end.
