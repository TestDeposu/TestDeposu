# The Raven and the Nine (The Viking Blood and Blade Saga, #6)

**Author:** Peter Gibbons  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2026

In "The Raven and the Nine (The Viking Blood and Blade Saga, #6)", Peter Gibbons weaves a masterful narrative that delves into the tumultuous world of 10th-century England, where the clash of Viking steel and Saxon valor sets the stage for an epic tale of blood, sweat, and redemption. This omnibus edition, comprising "Warrior and Protector", "Storm of War", and "Brothers of the Sword", presents a sweeping saga that will captivate fans of historical fiction and action-packed adventures.

Gibbons' writing is akin to a battle-hardened warrior's blade – sharp, visceral, and unyielding. His prose is thunderously atmospheric, transporting readers to the battle-scarred landscapes of Dark Age England, where the very fabric of society is threatened by the relentless onslaught of Viking invaders. With each turn of the page, the author masterfully ratchets up the tension, plunging readers into the heart of the maelstrom, where the clash of steel on steel, the scent of blood and sweat, and the cries of the fallen create an immersive experience that's impossible to shake off.

At the forefront of this saga is Beornoth, a brutal and battle-hardened Saxon Thegn, whose tragic past and personal demons drive him to seek redemption in the heat of battle. Alongside his Lord, Byrthnoth, Ealdorman of the East Saxons, Beornoth must confront the treacherous landscape of Viking invasions, treacherous alliances, and the shadowy spin of Queen Ælfthryth's machinations. As the stakes grow higher, Beornoth finds himself torn between his duty to protect his loved ones and his oath-sworn loyalty to his Lord, setting the stage for a hero's journey that will test his mettle, his honor, and his very soul.

Throughout the narrative, Gibbons' meticulous research and mastery of Viking and Dark Age lore shine through, lending an air of authenticity to the proceedings. From the intricate details of Viking warfare to the complex web of alliances and rivalries that defined the era, the author's commitment to historical accuracy is evident on every page.

One of the standout aspects of "The Raven and the Nine" is its exploration of the human cost of war. Gibbons pulls no punches in depicting the brutal realities of battle, where heroes and villains alike are forged in the crucible of violence, and the lines between good and evil are constantly blurred. This is a world where men are made and broken by the sword, where the cries of the fallen echo through the ages, and where the very fabric of society is threatened by the relentless tide of violence.

In conclusion, "The Raven and the Nine" is a gripping, action-packed, and visceral ride that will appeal to fans of Bernard Cornwell, Ken Follett, and Robert Low. Peter Gibbons' masterful storytelling, coupled with his meticulous research and commitment to historical accuracy, makes this omnibus edition a must-read for anyone interested in historical fiction, Viking lore, or the tumultuous world of Dark Age England.
