# The Girl Who Drank the Moon

**Author:** Kelly Barnhill  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

In "The Girl Who Drank the Moon," Kelly Barnhill masterfully weaves a rich tapestry of fantasy, adventure, and heart, transporting readers to a world of magic, mystery, and the unbreakable bonds of love and family. This Newbery Medal-winning novel is a testament to Barnhill's skill as a storyteller, as she crafts a narrative that is both timeless and timely, speaking to the deepest desires and fears of her audience.

At its core, "The Girl Who Drank the Moon" is a story about the power of love, acceptance, and the transformative magic that can occur when we embrace the unknown. Xan, the witch who dwells in the forest, is a complex and multifaceted character, driven by a deep sense of compassion and a desire to protect the innocent. Her decision to rescue the abandoned babies and deliver them to welcoming families on the other side of the forest is a testament to her kindness and generosity of spirit.

The arrival of Luna, the baby who is accidentally fed moonlight instead of starlight, sets in motion a chain of events that will change the course of Xan's life forever. As Luna grows and her magic begins to emerge, Xan must confront the dangers that come with raising a child with extraordinary abilities. Meanwhile, a young man from the Protectorate is determined to free his people by killing the witch, and the fate of the entire community hangs in the balance.

Barnhill's writing is a true delight, with a lyrical quality that is both poetic and evocative. Her use of language is masterful, conjuring vivid images and emotions that draw the reader deep into the world of the story. The characters are richly drawn and complex, with motivations that are both understandable and relatable. The world-building is equally impressive, with a fully realized landscape that is both fantastical and grounded in reality.

One of the most striking aspects of "The Girl Who Drank the Moon" is its exploration of the complexities of human nature. Barnhill's characters are multidimensional and flawed, with a deep sense of vulnerability and imperfection. Xan, in particular, is a fascinating study in contrasts, embodying both the kindness and the danger that can come with great power. The novel also raises important questions about the nature of identity, community, and the consequences of our actions.

Throughout the book, Barnhill's style is characterized by a sense of wonder, curiosity, and a deep respect for the magic of the everyday world. Her writing is infused with a sense of enchantment, as if the very words themselves are imbued with the power to transform and transcend. This is a novel that will appeal to readers of all ages, from middle-grade students to adults who are young at heart.

In short, "The Girl Who Drank the Moon" is a masterpiece of fantasy literature, a novel that will captivate and inspire readers with its richly imagined world, complex characters, and universal themes. It is a testament to the power of storytelling to transform and uplift us, and a reminder that the magic of the everyday world is always just a step away.
