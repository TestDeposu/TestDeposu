# Strange the Dreamer (Strange the Dreamer, #1)

**Author:** Laini Taylor  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

**Strange the Dreamer: A Mesmerizing Odyssey into the Heart of Myth and Desire**

Laini Taylor, the National Book Award finalist who enchanted readers with her Daughter of Smoke & Bone trilogy, returns with an epic fantasy that is as hauntingly beautiful as it is intricate. *Strange the Dreamer* is a tale that weaves together the threads of obsession, destiny, and the power of dreams, set against the backdrop of a mythical lost city and its dark past.

At the heart of the story is Lazlo Strange, a war orphan turned junior librarian, whose life is consumed by the myth of the lost city of Weep. Taylor's prose is as enchanting as ever, painting Lazlo's world with vivid, almost tactile detail. She masterfully draws us into Lazlo's mind, where dreams and reality blur, and the line between obsession and destiny grows increasingly thin. This is a world where the dream chooses the dreamer, and Lazlo's dream has chosen him, whether he's ready or not.

The narrative is a slow burn, allowing Taylor to build a rich tapestry of history, myth, and lore. Weep, the city that Lazlo is so desperate to find, is a place of dread and wonder, its past shrouded in mystery and its present haunted by the ghosts of murdered gods. Taylor's world-building is nothing short of extraordinary, creating a universe that feels both familiar and utterly otherworldly.

As Lazlo's journey begins, we are introduced to a cast of characters that are as complex and compelling as they are diverse. There's Sarai, the blue-skinned goddess from Lazlo's dreams, and her family, the godspawn, who are as much a part of Weep's dark past as they are its present. There's also the Godslayer, a hero whose legend precedes him, and his band of legendary warriors. Each character is a study in contradiction, their motivations and desires as complex as the mythical world they inhabit.

Taylor's strength lies in her ability to create characters that are both relatable and otherworldly. She explores themes of identity, belonging, and the power of love in a way that feels both timeless and timely. Her prose is lyrical, her pacing masterful, and her ability to weave together multiple storylines into a cohesive whole is nothing short of impressive.

*Strange the Dreamer* is a novel that demands to be savored. It's a story that unfolds like a dream, with twists and turns that are as unexpected as they are satisfying. It's a tale of love and loss, of power and redemption, of the lengths we go to for the people we love, and the dreams we can't let go of.

In a world filled with cookie-cutter fantasies, *Strange the Dreamer* stands out as a unique and captivating tale. It's a reminder that sometimes, the most extraordinary journeys are the ones we take in our dreams.
