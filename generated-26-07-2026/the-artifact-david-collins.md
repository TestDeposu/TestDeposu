# The Artifact

**Author:** David Collins  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

The Artifact by David Collins is a mesmerizing journey through the annals of Texas history, meticulously curated by the renowned musician Phil Collins. This book is not merely a collection of artifacts but a testament to the power of passion and dedication. Collins's love affair with the Alamo and the Texas Revolution began in his childhood, fueled by the iconic television series "Davy Crockett, King of the Wild Frontier." As he grew older, his fascination with history only deepened, eventually leading him to amass an impressive collection of nearly 500 items.

The book is a masterclass in storytelling, with Collins's prose weaving a narrative that is both personal and historical. His descriptions of each artifact are infused with a sense of wonder and awe, making the reader feel like they are experiencing the thrill of discovery alongside him. The stunning photography by Ben Powell and the evocative illustrations by Gary Zaboly bring the artifacts to life, making this book a visual feast.

One of the standout features of The Artifact is the way it seamlessly blends personal anecdote with historical context. Collins's essays are interspersed with contributions from Texas historians Bruce Winders, Don Frazier, and Stephen Hardin, providing a rich tapestry of information that is both engaging and informative. The result is a book that is at once a deeply personal exploration of one man's passion and a serious work of historical scholarship.

Throughout the book, Collins's love for the Alamo and its defenders shines through, and his reverence for the artifacts is palpable. He writes about each item with the same level of excitement and curiosity that he felt when he first encountered it, making the reader feel like they are experiencing the thrill of discovery for the first time.

The Artifact is also a testament to the power of collecting and the importance of preserving historical artifacts. Collins's collection is a treasure trove of relics and documents that provide a unique window into the past. His dedication to preserving these artifacts is inspiring, and his commitment to sharing them with the world is a gift to historians and enthusiasts alike.

In short, The Artifact is a must-read for anyone interested in Texas history, the Alamo, or the art of collecting. Collins's passion and dedication shine through on every page, making this book a truly unforgettable reading experience.
