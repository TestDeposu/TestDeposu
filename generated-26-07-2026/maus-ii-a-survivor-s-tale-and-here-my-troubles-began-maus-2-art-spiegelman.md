# Maus II: A Survivor's Tale: And Here My Troubles Began (Maus, #2)

**Author:** Art Spiegelman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

**Preview/Critique:**

"Maus II: And Here My Troubles Began" is the second volume of Art Spiegelman's groundbreaking graphic novel memoir, a daring and innovative exploration of the Holocaust that transcends literary conventions. Spiegelman's audacious use of animal metaphors—Jews as mice, Nazis as cats—is not merely a gimmick, but a profound and multifaceted tool for navigating the horrors of history.

Spiegelman's style is raw and unflinching, his stark black-and-white illustrations echoing the stark reality of the Holocaust. The animal metaphor serves multiple purposes: it's a distancing device, allowing Spiegelman to grapple with the unimaginable; a defamiliarizing tool, forcing readers to confront the dehumanization of the Holocaust; and a means of reconstructing ethnicity and identity, highlighting the absurdity of racial categorization.

As a second-generation witness, Spiegelman uses the animal characters to explore his own relationship with his father's past, the trauma echoing across generations. The use of masks, both literal and figurative, further underscores this theme, as Spiegelman and his father struggle to come to terms with their shared history.

Yet, Spiegelman's use of animal metaphors is not without its critics. Some argue that it trivializes the Holocaust, while others question the association of Jews with mice, a creature often seen as cowardly. But Spiegelman's intent is not to offend or trivialize, but to challenge our perceptions and force us to confront the complexities of memory and identity.

"Maus II" is not just a Holocaust story; it's a story about storytelling, about the struggle to make sense of the past. Spiegelman's unique style and bold use of metaphor make "Maus II" a powerful and thought-provoking read, a testament to the enduring power of comics as a literary form.
