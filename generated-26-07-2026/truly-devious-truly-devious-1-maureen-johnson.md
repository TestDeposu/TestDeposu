# Truly, Devious (Truly Devious, #1)

**Author:** Maureen Johnson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

Maureen Johnson's Truly, Devious is a captivating and intriguing mystery that seamlessly blends the past and present, weaving a complex tale of murder, twists, and turns. The story revolves around Ellingham Academy, a prestigious private school in Vermont founded by the enigmatic Albert Ellingham, where the brightest minds converge to learn, create, and innovate. However, the school's dark history is marked by the infamous kidnapping of Ellingham's wife and daughter, with the only clue being a cryptic riddle signed by the ominous pseudonym "Truly, Devious."

The narrative is expertly crafted, juxtaposing the past and present through the eyes of Stevie Bell, a true-crime enthusiast who joins Ellingham Academy with a singular focus: to solve the decades-old cold case. As Stevie navigates her new life at the academy, she must balance her demanding coursework, her fascination with the Truly Devious case, and her interactions with her eclectic housemates. Johnson's portrayal of Stevie's character is exemplary, capturing the essence of a brilliant and determined young woman who is both relatable and endearing.

One of the standout aspects of Truly, Devious is Johnson's masterful handling of the dual mysteries that drive the plot. The past and present are intertwined with remarkable finesse, as Stevie delves deeper into the original case, only to find herself entangled in a new, equally perplexing mystery. The pacing is impeccable, with each revelation and plot twist carefully calibrated to keep the reader engaged and invested in the story. Johnson's writing style is engaging, witty, and sophisticated, making it easy to become fully immersed in the world of Ellingham Academy.

Johnson's experience as a New York Times bestselling author is evident in her meticulous crafting of the story, which is both a loving homage to the classic mysteries of Agatha Christie and a refreshingly modern take on the genre. The characters are multidimensional and richly drawn, with each one bringing their unique perspective and talents to the narrative. The setting of Ellingham Academy is equally well-realized, with its labyrinthine pathways, hidden gardens, and secrets waiting to be uncovered.

Throughout the book, Johnson explores themes of obsession, ambition, and the blurred lines between reality and fantasy. Stevie's fixation on the Truly Devious case serves as a catalyst for her growth and self-discovery, as she confronts the darker aspects of her own nature and the true cost of her obsession. The author's treatment of these themes is nuanced and thought-provoking, adding depth and complexity to the narrative.

In conclusion, Truly, Devious is a Gripping and immersive mystery that will appeal to fans of the genre, particularly those who enjoy a blend of historical and contemporary elements. Maureen Johnson's writing is superb, with a keen sense of pacing, character development, and plot intricacy. As the first installment in the Truly Devious series, this book sets the stage for a thrilling and unpredictable ride, with Stevie Bell at the helm, ready to uncover the secrets that lie within the walls of Ellingham Academy.
