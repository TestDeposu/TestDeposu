# Soul Music (Discworld, #16; Death, #3)

**Author:** Terry Pratchett  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

In the vast and wondrous expanse of Terry Pratchett's Discworld, "Soul Music" stands out as a masterpiece of comedic fantasy, weaving a tale that is at once a hilarious send-up of the music industry and a poignant exploration of the human condition. This 16th installment in the Discworld series, and the third book in the Death series, is a testament to Pratchett's boundless creativity and his unique ability to craft stories that are both laugh-out-loud funny and deeply moving.

At the heart of "Soul Music" is the story of Susan Sto Helit, a 16-year-old girl with a rather...unconventional family. Her grandfather, Death, has gone on sabbatical, leaving Susan to take up the family business – collecting souls and guiding them to the afterlife. But Susan is not just a reluctant Grim Reaper; she's also a schoolgirl with a passion for music and a desire to live a normal life. When she meets Imp, a young musician with a dream of making it big, Susan finds herself drawn into the world of "Music With Rocks In," a new sound that's taking the Discworld by storm.

Pratchett's satire of the music industry is biting and hilarious, with clever send-ups of rock stars, groupies, and the excesses of the music business. But beneath the humor, "Soul Music" is also a thoughtful exploration of the power of music to change people's lives. Imp's band, with their magical guitar and addictive new sound, become an overnight sensation, but their music also has a darker side, one that threatens to consume them and everyone around them.

Throughout the book, Pratchett's writing is as witty and engaging as ever, with a keen eye for detail and a deep understanding of the human condition. His characters are richly drawn and complex, with Susan and Imp standing out as particularly well-realized protagonists. The supporting cast is equally well-drawn, from the hapless music producer who becomes Imp's manager to the Discworld's version of a rock critic, who's obsessed with finding the next big thing.

One of the joys of "Soul Music" is its ability to balance humor and pathos, often in the same scene. Pratchett has a remarkable talent for finding the absurdity in even the most mundane situations, and his writing is full of clever wordplay, witty one-liners, and hilarious set pieces. But he's also not afraid to tackle deeper themes, from the nature of mortality to the power of music to transcend our everyday lives.

Overall, "Soul Music" is a triumph, a book that will appeal to fans of comedy, fantasy, and music alike. It's a testament to Terry Pratchett's boundless creativity and his unique ability to craft stories that are both laugh-out-loud funny and deeply moving. If you're new to the Discworld series, "Soul Music" is a great place to start, with its standalone storyline and engaging characters. And if you're already a fan of the series, you'll love this latest installment, which is sure to leave you smiling, laughing, and maybe even humming a few tunes.
