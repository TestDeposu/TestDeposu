# Starsight (Skyward, #2)

**Author:** Brandon Sanderson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

**Starsight: A Stellar Journey of Self-Discovery and Heroism**

Brandon Sanderson, the literary maestro behind the Reckoners series and the Stormlight Archive, returns with the second installment of his Skyward series, "Starsight." This sequel picks up where "Skyward" left off, delving deeper into the cosmos and the complex nature of heroism.

"Starsight" follows Spensa, a young pilot with a chip on her shoulder and a secret that could upend everything she knows. Sanderson masterfully weaves a tale that explores the blurred lines between heroism and villainy, challenging readers to question their perceptions of right and wrong. Spensa's journey is a testament to Sanderson's ability to create relatable, flawed characters who grow and evolve throughout their narratives.

The story unfolds in a richly imagined intergalactic setting, filled with diverse alien species and intricate political intricacies. Sanderson's world-building is nothing short of extraordinary, immersing readers in a universe that feels both familiar and alien. Yet, it's not just the setting that shines; it's the way Sanderson uses it to explore universal themes like identity, loyalty, and the power of choice.

Sanderson's prose is as engaging as ever, balancing action-packed sequences with moments of introspection. He has a knack for making complex ideas accessible, making "Starsight" a thrilling read that also makes you think. The pacing is excellent, keeping readers on the edge of their seats, eager to uncover the next revelation.

"Starsight" is more than just a sequel; it's a testament to Sanderson's growth as a storyteller. He's not afraid to challenge his characters, and by extension, his readers. The result is a book that's as thought-provoking as it is entertaining.

In a nutshell, "Starsight" is a must-read for fans of Sanderson and anyone looking for a gripping, intelligent sci-fi adventure. It's a book that will make you question, make you think, and ultimately, make you appreciate the complexity of heroism.
