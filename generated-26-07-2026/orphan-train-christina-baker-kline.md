# Orphan Train

**Author:** Christina Baker Kline  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

**"Orphan Train" by Christina Baker Kline is a poignant and captivating journey through time, weaving together the lives of two women separated by decades but united by their shared sense of displacement and longing for belonging.**

Kline masterfully explores the little-known history of the orphan trains, which transported thousands of children from East Coast cities to the Midwest between 1854 and 1929. Through the eyes of young Irish immigrant Vivian Daly, we witness the harsh realities and uncertainties faced by these children, whose fates were determined by the luck of the draw. Kline's evocative prose brings to life the stark contrast between the grimy, teeming streets of New York City and the vast, unforgiving farmlands of Minnesota, creating a vivid backdrop for Vivian's story.

The narrative shifts between Depression-era Minnesota and contemporary Maine, where we meet Molly Ayer, a troubled teen with her own history of foster care and displacement. As Molly helps Vivian sort through her attic, the two women form an unlikely bond, and their shared experiences begin to unravel the secrets of Vivian's past. Kline's ability to create compelling, relatable characters is evident in both Vivian and Molly, whose voices are distinct yet equally compelling.

At its heart, **"Orphan Train"** is a story about the search for family and the enduring power of human connection. Kline explores the complex dynamics of motherhood, both biological and chosen, and the ways in which our pasts shape our present. She also delves into the timely issue of immigration, highlighting the struggles faced by those seeking a better life in a new land.

Kline's writing style is engaging and accessible, with a keen eye for detail that brings her characters and settings to life. She expertly balances historical context with contemporary relevance, creating a story that feels both timely and timeless. The dual timeline structure adds depth and resonance to the narrative, as the parallels between Vivian and Molly's lives become increasingly clear.

**"Orphan Train"** is a powerful testament to the human spirit's resilience in the face of adversity. It is a story of loss and longing, but also of hope and redemption, reminding us that family is not always a matter of blood, but of the connections we forge along the way.
