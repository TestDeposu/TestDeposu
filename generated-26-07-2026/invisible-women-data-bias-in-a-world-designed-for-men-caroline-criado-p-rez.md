# Invisible Women: Data Bias in a World Designed for Men

**Author:** Caroline Criado Pérez  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

**Invisible Women: A Revelatory Journey into Gender Bias**

Caroline Criado Pérez's "Invisible Women: Data Bias in a World Designed for Men" is a groundbreaking exploration of the insidious ways gender bias permeates our world, often hidden in plain sight. Pérez, a human rights campaigner and feminist author, masterfully weaves together research, statistics, and personal anecdotes to create a compelling narrative that challenges our understanding of gender equality.

The book is a wake-up call, a clarion call that echoes through every page, exposing the systemic invisibility of women in data, design, and decision-making processes. Pérez's prose is engaging, her arguments persuasive, and her commitment to her subject unyielding. She deftly navigates complex topics, from urban planning to medical research, making them accessible to a wide range of readers.

Pérez's style is that of a relentless investigator, a detective on the trail of a crime - the crime of data bias. She leaves no stone unturned, no assumption unchallenged. She delves into the data, sifts through the evidence, and presents her findings with clarity and conviction. The result is a book that is as enlightening as it is enraging, as sobering as it is inspiring.

"Invisible Women" is not just a book about women; it's a book about all of us. It's about the power of data to shape our world, and the danger of bias in that data. It's about the need for inclusivity, for diversity, for a world designed for everyone, not just the default 'he'.

This is not a book to be read passively. It's a book to be engaged with, to be argued with, to be acted upon. It's a call to arms, a rallying cry for a more equitable world. It's a book that will make you see the world differently, and it's a book that will make you want to change it.
