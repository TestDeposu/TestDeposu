# Bone Crossed (Mercy Thompson, #4)

**Author:** Patricia Briggs  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

In the fourth installment of Patricia Briggs's captivating Mercy Thompson series, "Bone Crossed" masterfully weaves a tale of supernatural politics, loyalty, and the unyielding bonds of friendship. This gripping urban fantasy novel delves into the complexities of Mercy's world, where vampires, werewolves, and witches coexist with humans in the Tri-Cities of Eastern Washington.

Briggs's writing style is a perfect blend of wit, action, and emotional depth, making it easy to become fully immersed in Mercy's world. The author's vivid descriptions of the characters and their surroundings bring the story to life, allowing readers to experience the tension and suspense firsthand. The pacing is well-balanced, with a narrative that seamlessly shifts between moments of humor, heart-pumping action, and poignant introspection.

At the heart of "Bone Crossed" lies the theme of loyalty and the consequences of one's actions. Mercy, a shape-shifter and car mechanic, finds herself at the center of a maelstrom when Marsilia, the local vampire queen, seeks revenge for Mercy's role in slaying a member of her clan. However, Marsilia's wrath is not directed at Mercy, but rather at those she holds dear – her friends. This clever twist raises the stakes and forces Mercy to navigate the intricate world of supernatural politics, where alliances are forged and broken with deadly precision.

Throughout the novel, Briggs explores the intricacies of Mercy's relationships with the people around her. Her bond with Adam, the werewolf pack's Alpha, is a highlight of the series, and their romance continues to simmer in the background, adding a delightful layer of tension to the story. The supporting cast is equally well-developed, with characters like Stefan, the charming but mysterious vampire, and Samuel, Mercy's werewolf friend, adding depth and complexity to the narrative.

One of the most striking aspects of "Bone Crossed" is its ability to balance light and dark moments. Briggs tackles some heavy themes, including violence, betrayal, and the consequences of power struggles, but she also infuses the story with a sense of humor and wit, making it an absolute pleasure to read. Mercy's sarcastic remarks and humorous observations provide a much-needed respite from the tension, making the novel an engaging and entertaining read.

In conclusion, "Bone Crossed" is a captivating addition to the Mercy Thompson series, showcasing Briggs's mastery of the urban fantasy genre. With its richly detailed world-building, complex characters, and gripping plot, this novel is sure to delight both old and new fans of the series. If you're looking for a story that will keep you on the edge of your seat, with a strong and capable heroine at its center, then "Bone Crossed" is an absolute must-read.
