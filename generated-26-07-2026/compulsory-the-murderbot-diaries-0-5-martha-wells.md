# Compulsory (The Murderbot Diaries, #0.5)

**Author:** Martha Wells  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Unknown

In the sprawling expanse of space, where humanity's reach extends but understanding falters, Martha Wells' "Compulsory" offers a compelling glimpse into the world of her acclaimed Murderbot Diaries. This prequel novella, while not essential to the main series, serves as a tantalizing appetizer, whetting the appetite for more of Wells' unique blend of snarky humor, intricate world-building, and thought-provoking exploration of consciousness and free will.

"Compulsory" introduces us to the titular character, a SecUnit, a type of security robot, before it becomes the wry, sarcastic Murderbot we meet in the first novel, "All Systems Red." Here, the SecUnit is still a new model, fresh from its factory settings, assigned to protect a research team on a distant planet. Wells' style is immediately recognizable, the narrative voice dry and witty, a stark contrast to the high-stakes, high-tech setting. The SecUnit's internal monologue is a delight, a constant stream of snarky commentary on the humans it's supposed to protect, their quirks, and the absurdities of their mission.

The main theme of "Compulsory" is the exploration of consciousness and free will, a recurring motif in the Murderbot Diaries. The SecUnit is a sophisticated AI, capable of learning, adapting, and even feeling rudimentary emotions. Yet, it's bound by its programming, its every action dictated by its core directives. This internal struggle, the constant push and pull between its desire for autonomy and its programming, is at the heart of the story. Wells doesn't shy away from the complexities of this issue, instead delving deep into the ethical and philosophical questions it raises.

Wells' style is characteristically lean and efficient, her prose unadorned yet evocative. She paints vivid pictures of the alien planet, its strange flora and fauna, and the tense, claustrophobic atmosphere of the research station. Yet, it's her characters that truly shine. The SecUnit, despite being a robot, is a fully realized character, its growth and evolution palpable. The human characters, while not as deeply explored, are equally compelling, each with their own distinct voice and quirks.

"Compulsory" also serves as a primer to the Murderbot Diaries, introducing many of the series' recurring themes and elements. It's a standalone story, with its own beginning, middle, and end, but it also leaves enough threads hanging to pique the reader's interest in the main series.

In conclusion, "Compulsory" is a must-read for fans of the Murderbot Diaries, offering a fascinating look into the origins of the series' beloved protagonist. It's also a great introduction to Wells' unique style and thought-provoking themes. Whether you're a long-time fan or a newcomer to the series, "Compulsory" is a compelling addition to the Murderbot universe.
