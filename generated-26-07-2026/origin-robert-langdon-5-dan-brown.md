# Origin (Robert Langdon, #5)

**Author:** Dan Brown  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Origin, the fifth installment in Dan Brown's Robert Langdon series, is a thrilling adventure that delves into the mysteries of the universe, blurring the lines between science, history, and faith. The story begins with Langdon, a Harvard professor of symbology, attending the unveiling of a groundbreaking discovery at the Guggenheim Museum Bilbao, hosted by his former student, Edmond Kirsch, a billionaire and futurist. However, the evening's festivities are short-lived, as chaos erupts, and Kirsch's discovery is threatened.

Langdon, along with Ambra Vidal, the museum's director, embarks on a perilous quest to locate a cryptic password that will unlock Kirsch's secret. As they navigate the dark corridors of hidden history and extreme religion, they must evade an enemy whose power seems to emanate from Spain's Royal Palace. Brown masterfully weaves together historical facts, myths, and legends, creating a rich tapestry that is both informative and entertaining.

One of the main themes of the book is the conflict between science and faith. Kirsch's discovery has the potential to upend traditional religious beliefs, and Langdon and Vidal must confront the implications of this revelation. Brown explores this theme with his characteristic nuance, presenting a balanced view of both sides of the argument. Through Langdon's character, he highlights the importance of understanding and respecting different perspectives, even if they challenge our own beliefs.

Brown's writing style is, as always, engaging and accessible. He has a gift for explaining complex concepts in simple terms, making the book an enjoyable read for both fans of science and history. The pacing is fast-moving, with plenty of twists and turns to keep readers on the edge of their seats. The characters are well-developed, with Langdon's trademark wit and intelligence on full display.

The author's use of symbolism is also noteworthy. Brown is a master of using symbols to convey deeper meanings, and Origin is no exception. From the museum's architecture to the cryptic password, symbols play a crucial role in the story, adding an extra layer of depth and complexity.

If you're a fan of Dan Brown's previous works, you'll love Origin. The book has all the ingredients that make his novels so compelling: history, mystery, science, and suspense. Even if you're new to the series, Origin is an excellent starting point, with enough background information to bring you up to speed.

In conclusion, Origin is a gripping thriller that explores the intersection of science, history, and faith. With its richly detailed world-building, engaging characters, and breakneck pacing, it's a must-read for fans of the Robert Langdon series and anyone looking for a thought-provoking adventure.
