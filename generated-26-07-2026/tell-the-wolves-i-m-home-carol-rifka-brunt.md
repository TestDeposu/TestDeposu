# Tell the Wolves I'm Home

**Author:** Carol Rifka Brunt  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

In "Tell the Wolves I'm Home," Carol Rifka Brunt weaves a poignant and evocative tale of love, loss, and the transformative power of human connection. Set against the backdrop of 1987, this mesmerizing debut novel follows the journey of fourteen-year-old June Elbus as she navigates the treacherous landscape of grief and self-discovery.

At its core, "Tell the Wolves I'm Home" is a story about the complexities of human relationships and the ways in which they can both heal and hurt us. June's relationship with her uncle Finn, a renowned painter, is a beautiful example of this. Finn is the only person who has ever truly understood June, and his untimely death leaves her feeling lost and alone. But it is through her connection with Toby, a mysterious stranger who was also deeply affected by Finn's passing, that June begins to find solace and a sense of purpose.

Brunt's writing style is both elegant and evocative, with a keen eye for detail and a deep understanding of the human condition. Her prose is infused with a sense of longing and nostalgia, perfectly capturing the mood and atmosphere of the late 1980s. The characters in the novel are richly drawn and complex, with June being a particularly compelling protagonist. Her voice is authentic and relatable, and her struggles to come to terms with her uncle's death are both deeply moving and profoundly insightful.

One of the most striking aspects of "Tell the Wolves I'm Home" is its exploration of the AIDS epidemic and the stigma surrounding it during the 1980s. Brunt handles this sensitive topic with care and nuance, shedding light on the fear, ignorance, and prejudice that characterized this period in history. Through Finn's story, Brunt highlights the devastating impact of the epidemic on individuals and families, as well as the resilience and courage of those who fought against it.

The title of the novel, "Tell the Wolves I'm Home," is a reference to a phrase that June's uncle used to say to her, a phrase that becomes a source of comfort and strength for her in the aftermath of his death. It is a powerful symbol of the enduring power of love and the ways in which it can transcend even death itself.

In short, "Tell the Wolves I'm Home" is a masterpiece of contemporary literature, a novel that will resonate deeply with readers of all ages. It is a testament to the human spirit, a celebration of the beauty and complexity of life, and a poignant reminder of the importance of connection and community. If you haven't already, do yourself a favor and immerse yourself in this extraordinary novel.
