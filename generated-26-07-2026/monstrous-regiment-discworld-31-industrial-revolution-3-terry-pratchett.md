# Monstrous Regiment (Discworld, #31; Industrial Revolution, #3)

**Author:** Terry Pratchett  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

**Monstrous Regiment: A Hilarious and Thought-Provoking Exploration of Gender and War**

In the sprawling, imaginative universe of Discworld, Terry Pratchett's **Monstrous Regiment** stands out as a masterclass in satirical storytelling, tackling the complex themes of gender roles, war, and identity with his signature wit and wisdom. Published in 2009, this 31st Discworld novel and the fourth in the Industrial Revolution sub-series, is a standalone adventure that packs a punch, leaving readers chuckling and contemplating long after they've turned the final page.

Pratchett's narrative prowess is on full display as he weaves a tale set in the small, patriarchal country of Borogravia, where women are expected to stay in the kitchen and out of the public sphere. Our unlikely heroine, Polly Perks, defies these conventions when she dons a wig and trousers to join the army in search of her missing brother. Polly's transformation into 'Oliver' is a brilliant vehicle for Pratchett's exploration of gender norms and the absurdity of societal expectations.

Pratchett's style is as delightful as ever, with his trademark puns, wordplay, and clever asides that make the Discworld series such a joy to read. Yet, beneath the laughter lies a sharp critique of war, power, and the patriarchy. Pratchett doesn't shy away from the horrors of war, but he also shows its absurdity, from the bumbling generals to the soldiers who'd rather be anywhere else. The camaraderie among the recruits, despite their diverse backgrounds and secrets, is a heartwarming reminder of humanity amidst the chaos.

The supporting cast is as memorable as Polly, from the coffee-loving vampire Sergeant Jackrum to the enigmatic Corporal Strappi. Each character brings their unique perspective, adding depth to the story and reinforcing Pratchett's message that everyone has a story, and everyone deserves to be heard.

**Monstrous Regiment** is a testament to Pratchett's ability to blend humor, social commentary, and compelling storytelling. It's a book that will make you laugh out loud, think deeply, and perhaps even shed a tear. It's a must-read for fans of the Discworld series and anyone looking for a thought-provoking, entertaining exploration of gender and war.
