# The Last Kingdom (The Saxon Stories, #1)

**Author:** Bernard Cornwell  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

**A Thrilling Saga of Identity, Betrayal, and the Birth of a Nation**

Bernard Cornwell's "The Last Kingdom" is a gripping, action-packed novel that immerses readers in the tumultuous 9th and 10th centuries, a time when the fate of England hung in the balance. This is not just a historical tale; it's a vivid, pulsating narrative that brings to life the epic saga of the making of England, as compelling as "Game of Thrones" but rooted in reality.

At the heart of the story is Uhtred, a Saxon nobleman who, after being captured by the Danes as a child, is raised among them. This upbringing leaves Uhtred torn between two worlds, his loyalties tested and his identity questioned. Cornwell masterfully explores this complex character, making Uhtred a relatable hero despite his sometimes brutal and morally ambiguous actions.

Cornwell's style is engaging and fast-paced, his prose lean and muscular, perfectly suited to the story's relentless action. He expertly weaves historical figures like King Alfred the Great into the narrative, bringing them to life with a novelist's touch. The battles are visceral and thrilling, the politics intricate and treacherous, all set against a backdrop of a land ravaged by war and invasion.

"The Last Kingdom" is more than just a story of war and conquest; it's a tale of identity, betrayal, and the struggle to belong. Uhtred's journey is a poignant exploration of what it means to be English in a time when that very identity is under threat. Cornwell's meticulous research shines through, making this not just a gripping adventure, but a fascinating history lesson.

This is the first book in Cornwell's "The Saxon Stories" series, and it's an impressive start. The stage is set for a thrilling saga, and readers will eagerly follow Uhtred's journey as he fights to reclaim his birthright and help shape the destiny of his homeland.
