# Some Girls Bite (Chicagoland Vampires, #1)

**Author:** Chloe Neill  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

**Title: "Some Girls Bite" by Chloe Neill: A Delicious Dive into Urban Fantasy**

In the bustling, windy city of Chicago, Chloe Neill serves up a tantalizing blend of urban fantasy and paranormal romance in her debut novel, "Some Girls Bite," the first installment of the captivating Chicagoland Vampires series. Neill's unique take on the vampire mythos is a refreshing and engaging addition to the genre, offering readers a thrilling ride through the dark, supernatural underbelly of the Second City.

At the heart of the story is Merit, a quick-witted, intelligent graduate student whose life takes an abrupt turn when she's attacked by a rogue vampire. In a twist of fate, she's rescued by Ethan Sullivan, the enigmatic and powerful Master of Cadogan House, one of Chicago's most prestigious vampire havens. Merit's newfound immortality comes with a price, however, as she must navigate the complex politics and hierarchies of the vampire world, all while trying to unravel the mystery behind her initial attack.

Neill's strength lies in her ability to create a richly textured world that feels both familiar and otherworldly. Chicago is more than just a backdrop; it's a character in its own right, with its iconic landmarks and neighborhoods brought to life in vivid detail. The vampire society that Neill has crafted is equally compelling, with its own rules, rituals, and power struggles. Fans of the genre will appreciate the clever nods to vampire lore, while newcomers will find the world-building accessible and immersive.

What truly sets "Some Girls Bite" apart is Neill's sharp, witty prose and her ability to craft memorable, relatable characters. Merit is a heroine who is equal parts strength and vulnerability, her voice snarky and engaging. Ethan Sullivan, the brooding, centuries-old vampire Master, is a classic alpha male, but Neill imbues him with enough complexity and depth to keep readers guessing. Their dynamic is a delight to watch unfold, with a slow-burning romance that simmers beneath the surface of their contentious relationship.

Neill also excels at balancing action, suspense, and humor. The plot moves at a steady clip, with enough twists and turns to keep readers on the edge of their seats. Yet, even in the heat of battle, Neill doesn't shy away from moments of levity, often using Merit's wry observations to defuse tension. This balance creates a story that is both thrilling and entertaining, making it a perfect choice for readers seeking an escape into a world that's equal parts dark and delightful.

In conclusion, "Some Girls Bite" is a standout debut that offers readers a fresh take on the vampire genre. With its engaging characters, richly drawn world, and clever prose, it's a book that's sure to satisfy fans of urban fantasy and paranormal romance alike. So, if you're in the mood for a story that's equal parts bite and sass, look no further than Chloe Neill's "Some Girls Bite."
