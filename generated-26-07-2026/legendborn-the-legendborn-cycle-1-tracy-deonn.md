# Legendborn (The Legendborn Cycle, #1)

**Author:** Tracy Deonn  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

In the realm of young adult fantasy, Tracy Deonn's "Legendborn" is a game-changer. This captivating novel masterfully weaves together elements of Southern folklore, Black Girl Magic, and the timeless legend of King Arthur, yielding a tale that is at once a searing exploration of grief, power, and self-discovery. Deonn's writing is as evocative as it is unflinching, plunging readers into a world of magic and mystery that is as unsettling as it is alluring.

At the heart of "Legendborn" is Bree Matthews, a 16-year-old girl reeling from the sudden loss of her mother. Her decision to enroll in a residential program for high-achieving students at UNC-Chapel Hill is motivated by a desire to escape the painful memories that linger in her childhood home. However, her plans are quickly derailed when she witnesses a magical attack on campus, drawing her into a secret world of "Legendborn" students who are the descendants of King Arthur's knights.

As Bree navigates this hidden world, she must confront her own complicated feelings about her mother's death, which she begins to suspect was not the accident she was led to believe. Her quest for truth is facilitated by her burgeoning partnership with Nick, a self-exiled Legendborn with his own axe to grind against the society. Together, they embark on a perilous journey that delves deep into the society's secrets and the mysteries of Bree's own past.

One of the most striking aspects of "Legendborn" is its thought-provoking exploration of power dynamics. Deonn's portrayal of the Legendborn society, with its rigid hierarchies and arcane traditions, serves as a powerful allegory for the ways in which systems of oppression can perpetuate themselves. Bree's status as an outsider within this world allows her to see its flaws and biases with a clarity that is both disconcerting and empowering.

Deonn's writing is characterized by its lyricism and emotional depth. Her prose is as expressive as it is concise, conveying the complexity of Bree's emotions with a nuance that is rare in young adult fiction. The world-building in "Legendborn" is equally impressive, drawing upon a rich array of mythological and folkloric sources to create a magical system that is at once coherent and fascinating.

If there is a flaw to "Legendborn," it is that the novel's pacing can be somewhat uneven. At times, the action unfolds at a breakneck speed, while at others, the narrative slows to a crawl. However, this is a minor quibble in what is otherwise a compelling and deeply satisfying read.

In conclusion, "Legendborn" is a triumph of young adult fantasy. Tracy Deonn's masterful storytelling, coupled with her nuanced exploration of themes such as power, identity, and trauma, makes for a reading experience that is as unforgettable as it is unmissable.
