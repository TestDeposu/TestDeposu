# The Color of Water: A Black Man's Tribute to His White Mother

**Author:** James McBride  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1997

In "The Color of Water: A Black Man's Tribute to His White Mother," James McBride crafts a poignant and powerful narrative that is both a tribute to his mother and an exploration of his own identity. This memoir is a masterful blend of two distinct voices: McBride's own candid recollections of growing up as a mixed-race child of poverty, and his mother Ruth's remarkable story, told in her own searing and spirited voice.

At the heart of the book lies the enigmatic figure of Ruth McBride Jordan, a woman who defies easy categorization. Born Rachel Shilsky to an Orthodox Jewish family in Poland, she fled her troubled past and reinvented herself in America, eventually marrying a black minister and founding a Baptist church in her living room. Through McBride's meticulous research and his mother's own words, we gain a glimpse into Ruth's complex and often painful history, marked by anti-Semitism, racial tensions, and personal struggles.

McBride's writing is a triumph of lyrical prose and unflinching honesty. He weaves together his mother's narrative with his own experiences, creating a rich tapestry of family history, cultural identity, and personal growth. The author's style is both introspective and expansive, moving seamlessly from the intimate details of family life to the broader social and historical context in which they lived.

One of the book's greatest strengths is its thoughtful exploration of the complexities of racial identity. McBride's own struggles to reconcile his mixed heritage are deeply relatable, and his mother's refusal to be bound by traditional notions of race is a powerful testament to the human spirit's capacity for reinvention and resilience.

Through Ruth's story, McBride sheds light on the often-overlooked experiences of Jewish immigrants and their struggles with identity, community, and belonging. Her remarkable journey from Poland to America, and from Orthodox Judaism to Baptist Christianity, is a powerful reminder of the complexities of human experience and the many ways in which we can define ourselves.

Ultimately, "The Color of Water" is a love letter from a son to his mother, a tribute to her unwavering strength, determination, and love. It is also a powerful exploration of the human condition, a reminder that our identities are complex, multifaceted, and always in flux. This book is a must-read for anyone interested in memoir, cultural studies, or the complexities of human identity.
