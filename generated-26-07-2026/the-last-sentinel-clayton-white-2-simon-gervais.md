# The Last Sentinel (Clayton White, #2)

**Author:** Simon Gervais  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

**Title: The Last Sentinel (Clayton White, #2) by Simon Gervais**

In the vein of classic Regency romances, Simon Gervais's "The Last Sentinel" weaves a tale of unlikely alliances, political intrigue, and an enemies-to-lovers trope that is as engaging as it is entertaining. Gervais, with his signature wit and flair for character development, delivers a story that is as much a delight to read as it is a testament to the genre's enduring appeal.

At the heart of "The Last Sentinel" lies the plight of Vance Clayton, a soldier of fortune who finds himself imprisoned in a French gaol. His sister, Elspeth, a spirited young woman with a penchant for trouble, is determined to rescue him, even if it means aligning herself with the last person she'd ever want to help: Gervaise Valerian, a dandy whose charm is as legendary as his quick temper.

Gervais masterfully paints a portrait of Regency-era England, with its opulent ballrooms and cobblestone streets, but it's his characters that truly bring the story to life. Elspeth, with her fiery spirit and unwavering loyalty, is a heroine worth rooting for, while Valerian, despite his initial arrogance, reveals layers of depth and vulnerability that make him a compelling romantic lead. Their banter, filled with wit and sarcasm, is a joy to read, and their slow-burning romance is as satisfying as it is believable.

The political intrigue that drives the plot is equally engaging, with Gervais skillfully balancing suspense and humor. The stakes are high, and the danger is real, but Gervais never loses sight of the story's heart: the relationship between Elspeth and Valerian. Their journey, from enemies to reluctant allies to something more, is a testament to the power of understanding, acceptance, and love.

Gervais's style is reminiscent of the great Regency romance authors, with a modern twist that makes "The Last Sentinel" feel fresh and exciting. His prose is elegant yet accessible, his pacing is spot on, and his ability to create memorable characters is nothing short of impressive.

In conclusion, "The Last Sentinel" is a delightful addition to the Regency romance genre. It's a story filled with adventure, humor, and heart, and it's a testament to Simon Gervais's skill as a storyteller. Fans of Regency romances, historical fiction, and enemies-to-lovers tropes will find much to love in this engaging and entertaining novel.
