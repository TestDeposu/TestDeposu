# Gandhi: An Autobiography

**Author:** Mahatma Gandhi  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

**Preview/Critique: Gandhi: An Autobiography**

Mahatma Gandhi's "An Autobiography: The Story of My Experiments With Truth" is not merely a historical document; it's a spiritual journey, a philosophical treatise, and a practical guide rolled into one. Written in simple, yet profound language, this book is an intimate exploration of Gandhi's life, thoughts, and the evolution of his philosophy of nonviolence.

Gandhi's narrative style is disarmingly honest and introspective. He doesn't shy away from his flaws and failures, instead, he uses them as stepping stones to illustrate his growth. This is not a chronicle of a perfect man, but a human being striving for perfection, a quality that makes the book deeply relatable and inspiring.

The main theme of the book is Gandhi's lifelong pursuit of truth, both external and internal. He sees truth as a living, evolving entity, not a static absolute. His 'experiments with truth' are not mere intellectual exercises, but practical, often painful, experiences that shape his understanding of the world and his role in it.

Gandhi's philosophy of nonviolence, or Ahimsa, is the cornerstone of this autobiography. He doesn't just preach nonviolence; he lives it, breathes it, and suffers for it. His commitment to Ahimsa is not born out of passivity or fear, but from a deep conviction that violence, in any form, is a barrier to truth and love.

The book is also a testament to Gandhi's extraordinary self-discipline. He doesn't just talk about simplicity, he lives it. He doesn't just advocate for truth, he lives it, even when it's inconvenient or dangerous. This is not asceticism for its own sake, but a means to an end - to free himself from distractions and to serve others better.

"An Autobiography" is not just a book to be read, but an experience to be lived. It's a call to introspection, a challenge to our comfortable certainties, and an inspiration to strive for a higher truth. It's a book that doesn't just inform, but transforms.
