# Roll of Thunder, Hear My Cry (Logans, #4)

**Author:** Mildred D. Taylor  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1976

Roll of Thunder, Hear My Cry, written by Mildred D. Taylor, is a powerful and poignant novel that explores the themes of racial injustice, family, and self-respect during the Great Depression in Mississippi. This Newbery Medal-winning book tells the story of the Logan family, who struggle to maintain their dignity and independence in the face of overwhelming racism and social inequality.

Through the eyes of Cassie Logan, a strong-willed and independent young girl, the reader is drawn into a world of harsh realities and difficult choices. As Cassie navigates the challenges of growing up in a society that seeks to oppress her and her family, she learns valuable lessons about the importance of land ownership, pride, and self-respect. The author's vivid storytelling and well-developed characters bring the Logan family's story to life, creating a sense of urgency and emotional resonance that is hard to shake.

One of the most striking aspects of Taylor's writing style is her ability to balance the harsh realities of racism and oppression with a sense of hope and resilience. The Logan family's story is not just one of struggle and hardship, but also of love, pride, and determination. The author's use of language is evocative and powerful, conjuring up the sights, sounds, and emotions of a bygone era. The characters are multidimensional and relatable, with Cassie being a particularly well-crafted protagonist.

The novel's exploration of the importance of land ownership is a key theme that resonates throughout the story. For the Logan family, owning their own land is a symbol of freedom, independence, and self-sufficiency. It represents a chance to break free from the shackles of racism and oppression, and to create a better future for themselves and their children. Through Cassie's eyes, the reader sees the value of this land, and the sacrifices that the family must make to keep it.

Taylor's writing is also notable for its historical accuracy and sensitivity. The novel is set during the Great Depression, a time of great upheaval and hardship for many Americans. The author's portrayal of the era is nuanced and detailed, capturing the complexities and challenges of life during this period. The novel also sheds light on the experiences of African Americans during this time, highlighting the injustices and inequalities that they faced.

Overall, Roll of Thunder, Hear My Cry is a masterful novel that has captivated readers for generations. Its powerful themes, vivid storytelling, and well-developed characters make it a must-read for anyone interested in historical fiction, social justice, and the African American experience. Mildred D. Taylor's writing is a testament to the power of storytelling, and her ability to convey the complexities and challenges of the human experience.
