# Sorcery of Thorns (Sorcery of Thorns, #1)

**Author:** Margaret Rogerson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

Sorcery of Thorns, the latest novel by Margaret Rogerson, is a captivating tale that delves into the mystical world of magical libraries and sorcerers. This New York Times bestseller has been praised by authors such as Stephanie Garber and Katherine Arden, and for good reason. Rogerson's writing style is enchanting, weaving a spell that transports readers to a world of ancient tomes and forbidden magic.

At the heart of the story is Elisabeth, a young apprentice who has grown up within the walls of a Great Library, surrounded by magical grimoires that hold the secrets of sorcery. Raised to believe that all sorcerers are evil, Elisabeth's world is turned upside down when she is implicated in a crime that threatens the very existence of the libraries she loves. With no one to turn to, she is forced to form an alliance with the sorcerer Nathaniel Thorn, a man she has been taught to despise.

As Elisabeth navigates this treacherous new world, she begins to question everything she has been taught. Rogerson expertly crafts a narrative that challenges the notion of good and evil, revealing that the truth is often far more complex. The author's use of language is vivid and immersive, bringing the world of Austermeer to life in a way that is both captivating and unsettling.

One of the most striking aspects of Sorcery of Thorns is its exploration of the power of knowledge and the danger of unchecked ambition. The magical grimoires that populate the libraries are not just simple books, but living, breathing entities that hold the power to shape reality. Rogerson's depiction of these grimoires is both fascinating and terrifying, highlighting the risks of delving too deep into the mysteries of the universe.

Throughout the novel, Rogerson's writing is infused with a sense of wonder and curiosity. Her characters are multidimensional and complex, with Elisabeth being a particularly well-crafted protagonist. The author's use of dialogue is natural and engaging, bringing the characters to life in a way that feels authentic and relatable.

The world-building in Sorcery of Thorns is also noteworthy, with Rogerson creating a richly detailed and immersive environment that draws the reader in. The magical system is well-developed and consistent, with a clear set of rules that govern the use of magic. The author's use of mythology and folklore adds depth and complexity to the narrative, drawing on a wide range of sources to create a unique and captivating world.

In short, Sorcery of Thorns is a must-read for fans of fantasy and adventure. With its richly detailed world-building, complex characters, and thought-provoking themes, this novel is sure to captivate and inspire. Rogerson's writing style is enchanting, making this a story that will stay with you long after you finish reading.
