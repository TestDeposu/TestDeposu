# The Puppet Show (Washington Poe, #1)

**Author:** M.W. Craven  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

**The Puppet Show: A Masterclass in Dark, Twisty Thrillers**

M.W. Craven's debut novel, **The Puppet Show**, is a chilling, gripping ride that announces the arrival of a powerful new voice in crime fiction. Winner of the prestigious CWA Gold Dagger Award, this book is a testament to Craven's ability to weave a complex, atmospheric tale that will keep readers on the edge of their seats.

At the heart of **The Puppet Show** lies a disturbing theme: the manipulation of lives, much like puppets, by a sinister force. The story unfolds in the seemingly idyllic Lake District, where a serial killer is burning victims alive in prehistoric stone circles. The killer leaves no clues, except for his name carved into the charred remains, drawing disgraced detective Washington Poe back into the investigation.

Poe, a flawed yet compelling protagonist, is an anti-hero for our times. His struggles with his past and his demons make him relatable, even as his sharp intellect and dogged determination make him an formidable adversary for the killer. His unlikely partnership with Tilly Bradshaw, a brilliant but socially awkward civilian analyst, adds a layer of complexity and warmth to the narrative.

Craven's prose is sharp and unflinching, painting a vivid picture of the dark underbelly of the Lake District. The story is told with a deft hand, balancing suspense with moments of black humor, creating a narrative that is both thrilling and engaging. The plot is intricate, with twists and turns that keep the reader guessing until the very end.

What sets **The Puppet Show** apart is its exploration of the human psyche, delving into the depths of obsession, manipulation, and revenge. Craven masterfully builds tension, not just through the investigation, but also through the psychological cat-and-mouse game between Poe and the killer.

In a genre saturated with formulaic plots, **The Puppet Show** stands out as a refreshing, chilling read. It's a testament to Craven's skill that he can make the reader care about his characters even as he puts them through the wringer. This is not a book for the faint-hearted, but for those who dare to venture into the dark, it's a rewarding, unforgettable experience.
