# The Summoning (Darkest Powers, #1)

**Author:** Kelley Armstrong  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

The Summoning, the first installment in Kelley Armstrong's Darkest Powers trilogy, is a captivating and eerie tale that masterfully weaves together elements of horror, romance, and supernatural mystery. Published in 2009, this book has aged remarkably well, offering a nostalgic reading experience reminiscent of the early 2000s YA fiction.

At the heart of the story is Chloe Saunders, a 15-year-old girl who discovers she has the ability to see ghosts. After a terrifying encounter with a malevolent spirit, Chloe is sent to Lyle House, a group home for troubled teens. As she navigates her new surroundings, she uncovers the dark secrets of the home and its residents, who harbor supernatural abilities of their own.

Armstrong's writing style is engaging and suspenseful, with a keen focus on character development. Chloe is a relatable and likable protagonist, whose struggles to control her powers and find her place in the world are deeply human. The supporting cast is equally well-crafted, with complex and intriguing characters that add depth to the narrative.

One of the standout aspects of The Summoning is its exploration of the supernatural. Armstrong's world-building is meticulous and detailed, drawing inspiration from various mythologies and folklore. The author's use of necromancy, sorcery, and shape-shifting adds a rich layer of complexity to the story, making it feel both authentic and immersive.

The romance in the book is also noteworthy, with Chloe finding herself torn between two brothers: Simon, a charming sorcerer, and Derek, a brooding werewolf. The love triangle is expertly handled, with a slow-burning tension that builds throughout the narrative. Armstrong's portrayal of the relationships is nuanced and realistic, avoiding the clichés often found in YA fiction.

The Summoning is a gripping and unsettling read, with a creepy atmosphere that permeates every aspect of the story. Armstrong's use of setting is masterful, transforming the mundane group home into a place of dark wonder and terror. The pacing is well-balanced, with a mix of action, suspense, and quiet moments of introspection.

In conclusion, The Summoning is a compelling and addictive read that will appeal to fans of supernatural fiction, horror, and romance. Armstrong's writing is engaging, and her world-building is meticulous and immersive. With its blend of eerie atmosphere, complex characters, and slow-burning romance, this book is a must-read for anyone looking for a captivating YA novel.
