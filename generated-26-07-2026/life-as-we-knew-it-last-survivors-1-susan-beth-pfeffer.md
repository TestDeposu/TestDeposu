# Life As We Knew It (Last Survivors, #1)

**Author:** Susan Beth Pfeffer  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2008

**Life As We Knew It: A Chilling Tale of Resilience in the Face of Catastrophe**

In the realm of post-apocalyptic literature, Susan Beth Pfeffer's "Life As We Knew It" stands out as a harrowing and heartrending exploration of human endurance. This New York Times bestseller, the first installment in the Last Survivors series, is a gripping narrative that masterfully weaves together themes of family, hope, and survival in a world turned upside down.

The story unfolds through the eyes of Miranda, a high school sophomore, as she chronicles a year in her life following a catastrophic event: a meteor strike that knocks the moon closer to Earth, causing global devastation. Pfeffer's choice to narrate the tale through journal entries lends an intimate and immediate quality to the storytelling, drawing readers into Miranda's world and inviting them to share her fears, hopes, and daily struggles.

As the world outside descends into chaos, Miranda and her family retreat to their sunroom, a makeshift sanctuary where they must rely on their wits and meager resources to survive. The sunroom becomes a microcosm of their shattered world, a testament to the human spirit's ability to endure even in the most dire circumstances. Pfeffer's prose is stark and unflinching, painting a vivid picture of the harsh realities of life in a post-apocalyptic world.

At its core, "Life As We Knew It" is a story about hope. Despite the bleakness of their situation, Miranda and her family cling to the belief that they can weather the storm and emerge stronger on the other side. Pfeffer masterfully explores the delicate balance between hope and despair, illustrating how even the smallest glimmers of optimism can sustain the human spirit in the darkest of times.

Pfeffer's writing style is straightforward and accessible, making the story all the more powerful. She doesn't shy away from the harsh realities of their situation, but neither does she dwell on the gruesome details. Instead, she focuses on the emotional journey of her characters, allowing readers to connect with them on a deep, personal level.

"Life As We Knew It" is a stark reminder that our world is fragile, and that the things we take for granted—sunlight, food, water—are precious and finite. It's a cautionary tale about the consequences of our actions and the importance of cherishing what we have. But above all, it's a testament to the human capacity for resilience and hope in the face of unimaginable adversity.
