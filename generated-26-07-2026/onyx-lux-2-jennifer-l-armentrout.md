# Onyx (Lux, #2)

**Author:** Jennifer L. Armentrout  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

"Onyx," the second installment in Jennifer L. Armentrout's captivating Lux series, is a thrilling ride that builds upon the foundations laid in its predecessor, "Obsidian." Armentrout's signature style, a blend of witty dialogue, compelling characters, and a plot that twists and turns like a rollercoaster, is on full display here, making "Onyx" an unputdownable read.

At the heart of the story lies the complex relationship between Katy, a human girl, and Daemon, an alien with a mysterious past. Their connection, initially fueled by animosity and later by an undeniable attraction, is the driving force of the narrative. Armentrout masterfully navigates the ups and downs of their relationship, keeping readers on the edge of their seats with a mix of heart-pounding action and heartwarming moments.

One of the standout aspects of "Onyx" is its ability to subvert expectations. Just when you think you've got a handle on the story, Armentrout throws in a curveball that changes everything. This is particularly evident in the way she handles the reveal of the Luxen's true origins and the extent of their powers. The more we learn about Daemon and his kind, the more we realize that nothing is as it seems.

Armentrout's characters are another highlight of the book. Katy, our human protagonist, is a refreshing heroine. She's strong-willed, intelligent, and not afraid to stand up for herself, even when faced with otherworldly beings who could squash her like a bug. Daemon, meanwhile, is a fascinating study in contrasts. He's a lethal warrior who can be both charming and infuriating, often within the same scene. Their dynamic is electric, and their banter is one of the many joys of reading "Onyx."

The supporting cast is equally compelling. From Dee, Daemon's feisty sister, to Adam, the enigmatic new Luxen in town, each character brings something unique to the story. Armentrout has a knack for creating characters who feel like real people, with their own quirks, strengths, and flaws. This makes the world of "Onyx" feel lived-in and authentic, even when it's populated by beings from another planet.

"Onyx" is also a masterclass in world-building. Armentrout takes the time to flesh out the Luxen's history, culture, and powers, making them feel like a distinct and believable alien race. She also explores the implications of their presence on Earth, delving into themes of immigration, prejudice, and the ethical dilemmas that arise when two vastly different species are forced to coexist.

In terms of style, Armentrout's writing is fast-paced and engaging. She has a talent for crafting dialogue that feels natural and snappy, even when it's laced with tension or humor. Her prose is accessible and easy to read, making "Onyx" a book that's hard to put down.

However, "Onyx" is not without its flaws. Some readers may find the pacing a bit uneven, with certain sections feeling rushed while others linger on seemingly insignificant details. There are also moments where the plot feels a bit too convenient, with characters conveniently popping up just when they're needed or convenient revelations being made at just the right time.

But these are minor quibbles in an otherwise excellent book. "Onyx" is a testament to Armentrout's skill as a storyteller, her ability to create compelling characters, and her knack for crafting a plot that keeps readers on the edge of their seats. It's a thrilling, emotional, and often humorous ride that will leave you eager for more.

In conclusion, "Onyx" is a must-read for fans of science fiction, romance, and action-packed adventure. It's a book that will make you laugh, gasp, and swoon, often all within the same page. If you're looking for a story that will keep you up at night, turning pages long after you should have turned off the light, then "Onyx" is the book for you.
