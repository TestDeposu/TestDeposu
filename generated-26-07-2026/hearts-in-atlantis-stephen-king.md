# Hearts in Atlantis

**Author:** Stephen King  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1999

"Hearts in Atlantis" is a masterclass in storytelling from the master of suspense, Stephen King. This 1999 collection of five interconnected tales is not your typical King fare, yet it's unmistakably his, brimming with his signature style and themes. The main thread that weaves through these stories is the impact of the Vietnam War on American society and the human psyche, a theme that King explores with his characteristic depth and nuance.

The collection opens with "Low Men in Yellow Coats," a chilling tale of childhood innocence lost. Bobby Garfield, an eleven-year-old boy, discovers the dark underbelly of his seemingly idyllic neighborhood. King's prowess in creating atmosphere is on full display here, as he slowly ratchets up the tension, transforming the mundane into the menacing. The story also introduces King's recurring theme of the corrupting influence of power, as adults are revealed to be not saviors, but part of the terror.

The title story, "Hearts in Atlantis," is a powerful exploration of youthful idealism and its disillusionment. A group of college students become embroiled in a card game that mirrors the moral ambiguity of the Vietnam War. King's ability to blend the supernatural with the mundane is on full display here, as the game's rules begin to reflect the players' deepest fears and desires. The story is a stark reminder of the cost of war, not just on the battlefield, but on the home front as well.

The latter half of the collection follows two men, Pete and John, as they grapple with the aftermath of the Vietnam War. In "Blind Willie" and "Why We're in Vietnam," King explores the theme of guilt and redemption, as Pete and John try to fill the emptiness left by the war. These stories are a poignant exploration of the human condition, and a stark reminder of the lasting impact of war on those who fight it.

The collection concludes with "Heavenly Shades of Night Are Falling," a story that brings Bobby Garfield full circle. Here, King explores the theme of homecoming, and the hope of redemption. The story is a fitting conclusion to the collection, tying up the loose ends of the previous stories and leaving the reader with a sense of closure.

King's style in "Hearts in Atlantis" is more subdued than in some of his other works, but no less powerful. He uses his signature long sentences and descriptive language to create a sense of foreboding, even in the most mundane of settings. His characters are complex and fully realized, their motivations and struggles feel authentic and relatable. King's ability to blend the mundane with the supernatural is on full display here, as he uses the latter to explore the former in a way that is both thought-provoking and chilling.

In conclusion, "Hearts in Atlantis" is a powerful exploration of the impact of the Vietnam War on American society and the human psyche. King's signature style and themes are on full display here, making this collection a must-read for fans of his work. The collection is a stark reminder of the cost of war, not just on the battlefield, but on the home front as well. It's a powerful and astonishingly current read, nearly twenty years after its first publication.
