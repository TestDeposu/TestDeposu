# The Reckoning (Darkest Powers, #3)

**Author:** Kelley Armstrong  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

In "The Reckoning," the final installment of Kelley Armstrong's Darkest Powers trilogy, Chloe Saunders is back, and her life is more complicated than ever. As a genetically altered necromancer, Chloe has always struggled to find her place in the world. With her supernatural abilities, her feelings for two brothers - a werewolf and a sorcerer, and the constant threat of an evil corporation on their heels, Chloe's life is anything but normal.

One of the most striking aspects of Armstrong's writing style is her ability to seamlessly blend the supernatural with the mundane. Chloe's struggles to navigate high school, friendships, and first love are all relatable, despite the extraordinary circumstances. Armstrong's world-building is meticulous, and her vision of a world where supernatural creatures are hidden in plain sight is both fascinating and unsettling.

At its core, "The Reckoning" is a story about self-discovery and acceptance. Chloe's journey is not just about mastering her powers or defeating the bad guys, but about learning to accept herself for who she is. Armstrong explores themes of identity, loyalty, and the complexities of human relationships with sensitivity and nuance.

Chloe's character development is particularly noteworthy. Throughout the series, she has evolved from a timid and uncertain teenager to a confident and determined young woman. Her relationships with the other characters, particularly the two brothers, are multifaceted and authentic. The love triangle is expertly handled, avoiding clichés and instead focusing on the emotional depth of Chloe's connections with each brother.

Armstrong's writing is engaging and suspenseful, with a narrative that hurtles towards a thrilling conclusion. The pacing is well-balanced, with moments of quiet introspection and explosive action. The supporting cast is equally well-developed, with each character bringing their unique perspective and skills to the table.

If there's one criticism to be made, it's that the series concludes a tad too neatly. Some plot threads are resolved a bit too conveniently, and a few characters' fates feel slightly rushed. However, this is a minor quibble in an otherwise satisfying conclusion to the trilogy.

In conclusion, "The Reckoning" is a captivating and emotional ride that will keep readers on the edge of their seats. Armstrong's writing is masterful, and her characters are relatable and well-developed. If you're a fan of supernatural fiction, particularly in the young adult genre, this series is an absolute must-read.
