# Splintered (Splintered, #1)

**Author:** A.G. Howard  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

**A Twisted Tale of Madness and Desire: A.G. Howard's "Splintered"**

In the realm of young adult fiction, few authors have dared to delve as deeply into the rabbit hole as A.G. Howard. Her debut novel, "Splintered," is a dark, twisted, and utterly captivating reimagining of Lewis Carroll's classic "Alice's Adventures in Wonderland." This is not your grandmother's Wonderland; it's a place far more sinister, where the line between reality and madness blurs, and love is a dangerous game.

At the heart of "Splintered" lies the question of inheritance—specifically, the burden of one's ancestors. Alyssa Gardner, our protagonist, is cursed with the ability to hear the whispers of bugs and flowers, a trait that has plagued her family for generations. Her ancestor, Alice Liddell, inspired Carroll's tale, but the true story is far darker. Alyssa must navigate this treacherous Wonderland to right Alice's wrongs and save her family from the curse.

Howard's prose is as rich and decadent as the tea party's spread. She paints a vivid, psychedelic landscape that is as enchanting as it is terrifying. The creatures that inhabit this world are grotesque and beautiful, a testament to Howard's imagination. Yet, it's her characters that truly shine. Alyssa is a heroine who is equal parts strength and vulnerability, a girl who must confront her own madness to save those she loves. Jeb, her best friend and secret crush, is the steadfast anchor in the storm, while Morpheus, the enigmatic guide, is as alluring as he is dangerous.

"Splintered" is a love story, but it's not a simple one. It's a tale of forbidden love, of loving someone despite their darkness, and of loving oneself enough to walk away. It's a story about the power of choice and the consequences that follow. Howard weaves a complex web of romance, intrigue, and suspense that keeps the pages turning long into the night.

At its core, "Splintered" is a exploration of madness and creativity. It's a tale that asks, what if the line between genius and insanity is thinner than we think? What if the stories we tell are reflections of our own darkness? It's a question that Howard herself seems to be asking, and the result is a novel that is as brilliant as it is unsettling.

In a sea of YA novels that often feel formulaic, "Splintered" stands out as a unique and captivating tale. It's a novel that demands to be read, that dares you to look into the abyss and see what's staring back. So, if you're ready to take the plunge, if you're ready to follow the White Rabbit down the hole, then "Splintered" is the novel for you.
