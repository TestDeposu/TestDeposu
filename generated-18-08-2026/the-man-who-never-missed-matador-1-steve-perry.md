# The Man Who Never Missed (Matador, #1)

**Author:** Steve Perry  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Unknown

In the vast and often predictable expanse of science fiction, few authors manage to carve out a niche that feels both intimately gritty and grandly operatic. Steve Perry achieves precisely this in The Man Who Never Missed, the explosive opening salvo of his acclaimed Matador series. Without relying on a pre-existing synopsis, stepping into Perry's universe feels akin to dropping unannounced into a high-stakes interstellar chess match where the pieces are armed, dangerous, and brilliantly unpredictable.

At the core of the novel lies a striking and resonant theme: the corrupting nature of absolute control and the high cost of reclamation. The narrative plunges readers into a heavily bureaucratized galaxy ruled by the oppressive Confederation. Against this monolithic, faceless machine stands the protagonist, Emiliano Saint-Just—a man forged in the crucible of harsh training, deep philosophy, and an unyielding quest for justice. Perry uses Saint-Just not merely as a traditional action hero, but as a vessel to explore the philosophy of resistance. The book asks a fundamental question: how do you fight a leviathan without becoming one? Saint-Just’s journey is less about the elimination of enemies and more about the methodical, psychological dismantling of a system that treats human lives as expendable data points. 

Perry’s authorial style is the engine that drives this thematic exploration forward. Lean, muscular, and unapologetically fast-paced, his prose wastes no time on superfluous world-building exposition. Instead, Perry paints his universe through action, dialogue, and sharp, pragmatic observations. The pacing mirrors the heartbeat of a tactical operation—deliberate in its planning, sudden and devastating in its execution. There is a distinct cinematic quality to his writing; fight scenes and moments of tension are choreographed with a precision that suggests a deep understanding of martial arts and tactical strategy. Yet, what elevates Perry above standard pulp science fiction is his seamless blending of high-octane thrills with Eastern philosophy and ethical discourse. The juxtaposition of deadly combat techniques with deep meditative introspection gives the novel a unique intellectual weight.

Furthermore, Perry’s approach to characterization is refreshingly utilitarian. Every character in The Man Who Never Missed serves a distinct purpose, driving the narrative toward its inevitable, high-stakes climax. Saint-Just is stoic, highly capable, and endlessly fascinating, embodying the ultimate outsider who knows the system better than the architects who built it. The supporting cast—ranging from allies shaped by the same brutal academies to unsuspecting pawns caught in the crossfire—adds emotional resonance to a story that could have easily relied solely on mechanical action.

Ultimately, The Man Who Never Missed is a masterclass in establishing a sprawling, immersive sci-fi series with economy and impact. Steve Perry delivers a novel that is both a thrilling page-turner and a thought-provoking meditation on power, freedom, and the art of survival. For readers looking for intelligent science fiction where brains and martial prowess are equally lethal, Perry's debut stands as an essential, timeless read that commands your attention from the very first page to the last.
