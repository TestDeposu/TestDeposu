# Diamond Mask (Galactic Milieu Trilogy, #2)

**Author:** Julian May  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1994

In the vast expanse of science fiction, few authors have managed to craft a world as intricate and immersive as Julian May's Galactic Milieu Trilogy. The second installment, "Diamond Mask," is a masterclass in storytelling, delving deep into the complexities of human individuality, free will, and the blurred lines between good and evil.

Set against the backdrop of a 21st century on the cusp of Unity with the alien races of the Galactic Milieu, May's narrative expertly weaves together a tapestry of characters, each with their own distinct voice and motivations. At the forefront of this maelstrom is Dorothea Macdonald, a young woman with unparalleled mindpowers, who finds herself at the epicenter of a struggle that will determine the fate of humanity.

May's writing style is a perfect blend of philosophical introspection and breakneck pacing, making "Diamond Mask" a true page-turner. Her prose is evocative and engaging, with a keen eye for detail that brings the world of the Galactic Milieu to vivid life. The author's use of language is deliberate and measured, often blurring the lines between reality and the metapsychic realm, creating an atmosphere of tension and unease that propels the reader forward.

One of the most striking aspects of "Diamond Mask" is its exploration of the human condition. May raises important questions about the nature of individuality, free will, and the consequences of playing with forces beyond human control. Through Dorothea's struggles, we are forced to confront the darker aspects of our own psyche, and the true cost of ambition and power.

The cast of characters is equally impressive, with each one adding depth and complexity to the narrative. Jack the Bodiless, the mutant genius, is a fascinating foil to Dorothea's raw power, while Fury, the insane metapsychic creature, is a masterclass in villainy. May's characterization is nuanced and multifaceted, making it impossible to discern clear-cut heroes and villains, adding to the sense of moral ambiguity that pervades the novel.

If there is one criticism to be made, it is that "Diamond Mask" often feels like a middle book, setting the stage for the final installment in the trilogy. While this does not detract from the overall experience, it does leave the reader with a sense of anticipation, eager to see how the events of this novel will unfold in the conclusion.

In conclusion, "Diamond Mask" is a gripping and thought-provoking novel that cements Julian May's place as one of the most innovative and exciting science fiction authors of her generation. With its intricate world-building, complex characters, and exploration of the human condition, this novel is a must-read for fans of the genre.
