# The Currents of Space (Galactic Empire, #2)

**Author:** Isaac Asimov  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

Isaac Asimov remains an undisputed architect of modern science fiction, and in The Currents of Space, the second installment of his sweeping Galactic Empire series, he demonstrates his unparalleled mastery of sociopolitical world-building and intellectual suspense. Set in an era long before the rise of the iconic Foundation, this novel bridges the gap between planetary feudalism and galactic dominance, offering readers a gripping narrative where the ultimate weapon is not a laser or a starship, but pure, unadulterated scientific truth.

At the heart of the novel lies a striking and prescient theme: the fragility of civilizations blinding themselves to ecological and systemic reality in the pursuit of wealth and power. The story unfolds on Florina, a lush, idyllic world enslaved by the arrogant ruling class of the neighboring planet Sark. Florina’s sole economic engine is the production of kyrt, a miraculous textile luxury that feeds the opulence of Sark and binds the two planets in a toxic dynamic of exploitation. Yet, beneath the beauty of the kyrt fields and the cruelty of the labor camps, a planetary death sentence is ticking. 

Asimov centers this looming catastrophe around a protagonist known only as Rik, a man whose mind has been brutally wiped by a psychic probe. Formerly a high-ranking Spatio-analyst, Rik labors among the mindless masses of Florina while fragmented memories slowly breach the surface of his consciousness. Asimov uses Rik’s amnesia as a brilliant narrative device, allowing the reader to unravel the mystery alongside the protagonist. Rik holds the knowledge that Florina’s sun is undergoing radioactive shifts that will soon unleash the deadly currents of space, incinerating the planet. 

What makes Asimov's style so enduring is his prioritization of intellectual deduction over visceral action. The conflict in The Currents of Space is not merely fought with physical resistance, but through the painful retrieval of facts, bureaucratic corruption, political intrigue involving the mighty world of Trantor, and the desperate race to make the blind see the truth. The antagonists on Sark are not cardboard-cutout villains; rather, they are chillingly realistic products of institutional arrogance, willing to suppress planetary salvation to protect their economic monopoly and social hierarchy. Asimov weaves a tight, claustrophobic mystery across vast interplanetary distances, proving that human stubbornness and greed are far more dangerous than any cosmic anomaly.

While the novel operates on a grand scale, exploring imperialism and the sprawling bureaucracy of an emerging Galactic Empire, it remains fundamentally a human story about identity, perception, and the heavy burden of knowledge. Asimov’s prose is clean, direct, and relentlessly logical, pulling the reader through a maze of interplanetary politics with effortless pacing. 

The Currents of Space is a masterclass in speculative fiction that resonates just as strongly today as it did decades ago, reminding us that ignoring scientific reality for the sake of comfort is a luxury no civilization can afford. For both lifelong fans of the Foundation universe and newcomers looking for smart, provocative science fiction, this novel is an absolute must-read.
