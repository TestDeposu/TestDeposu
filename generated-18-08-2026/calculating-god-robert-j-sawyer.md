# Calculating God

**Author:** Robert J. Sawyer  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

When an eight-foot-tall arachnid alien casually walks into Toronto’s Royal Ontario Museum and demands to see a paleontologist, science fiction immediately steps out of its usual comfort zone of space battles and laser fire, entering instead the profound, dusty corridors of human philosophy. This is the brilliant, disquieting launching pad of Robert J. Sawyer’s Hugo Award-nominated novel, Calculating God. 

At its core, the novel tackles the ultimate heavyweight champion of existential debates: the eternal clash between science and religion. But Sawyer, a master of intellectual speculative fiction, refuses to treat this as a black-and-white battleground. Instead, through the eyes of human paleontologist and lifelong atheist Tom Jericho—who is privately facing a terminal cancer diagnosis—and his extraterrestrial counterpart, a spider-like alien named Hollus, the book orchestrates a nuanced, deeply compelling dialogue about the origins of the universe. 

The premise itself is breathtakingly audacious. Hollus reveals that Earth is not unique in its evolutionary suffering; multiple intelligent species across the galaxy have endured the exact same five great mass extinction events. Far from being random cosmic accidents, the aliens argue that these meticulously timed resets point to one undeniable conclusion: a supreme being is actively tampering with the evolution of life, calculating and calibrating the universe with a divine purpose. For Tom, a man whose life’s work is rooted in the cold, unyielding facts of fossil records and Darwinian evolution, this revelation is nothing short of earth-shattering. 

Sawyer’s greatest triumph in Calculating God lies in his signature authorial style. He has crafted what can best be described as an intellectual thriller with real bite. The narrative bypasses cynical posturing, favoring instead an earnest, fast-paced, and morally challenging exploration of ethics, mortality, and the cosmos. Sawyer skillfully blends hard science—paleontology, astrophysics, and evolutionary biology—with theological inquiry without ever alienating the reader. The conversations between Tom, Hollus, and a reptilian alien named Jascept are laced with wry compassion, sharp wit, and genuine emotional resonance. 

Moreover, the book grounds its grand, cosmic scope in a very human heart. As Tom’s time rapidly runs out, the philosophical debates cease to be merely academic. They become intensely personal. The fear of oblivion, the search for meaning in suffering, and the fragile hope for transcendence give the novel an emotional weight that lingers long after the final page is turned. It is a rare science fiction work that treats the existence of God with as much rigorous curiosity as it treats the laws of physics.

Echoing the grandeur of Arthur C. Clarke and the captivating scientific optimism of Carl Sagan’s Contact, Sawyer delivers an unforgettable epistemological journey. Calculating God is guaranteed to challenge, expand, and provoke the minds of believers and non-believers alike, proving once again why speculative fiction remains our most vital laboratory for exploring what it truly means to exist.
