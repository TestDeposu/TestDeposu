# Undeadly (The Reaper Diaries, #1)

**Author:** Michele Vail  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

Title: High School Is Hell: Michele Vail’s Undeadly Breathes New Life Into YA Paranormal

When sixteen-year-old Molly Bartolucci accidentally resurrects her dream boy on her birthday, she thinks her biggest problem is keeping her supernatural hobbies a secret. But in Michele Vail’s 2012 urban fantasy debut, Undeadly, high school drama is just the gateway to a much darker, afterlife-spanning curriculum. Vail takes the familiar beats of young adult paranormal romance—the ordinary girl thrust into extraordinary circumstances, the brooding mentor, the mysterious boyfriend—and infuses them with a sharp wit, macabre humor, and an undeniably fresh pulse. 

At its core, the main theme of Undeadly explores the heavy burden of agency and the chaotic intersection of life, death, and teenage identity. Molly is not just a passive conduit for supernatural forces; she is a clumsy, relatable protagonist chosen by the Egyptian god Anubis to become a reaper. Her accidental interference with the established cosmic order lands her in the elite Nekyia Academy, a boarding school for necromancers. Vail uses this classic academic setting as a brilliant metaphor for the alienation of adolescence. At Nekyia, learning to navigate mortality, social hierarchies, and dangerous academic workloads mirror the very real, often terrifying growing pains of the teenage years. Molly’s struggle to control her zombie-raising abilities speaks directly to the universal feeling of being overwhelmed by one's own changing body and burgeoning power.

Vail’s writing style is fast-paced, conversational, and laced with a self-aware sarcasm that prevents the darker mythological elements from becoming too oppressive. The author balances high-stakes supernatural intrigue—complete with disappearing instructors and suspicious student deaths—with lighthearted, quirky touches, such as Molly’s very own personal ghoul. The romantic tension between Molly and her assigned reaping tutor, the enigmatic Rath, simmers with classic enemies-to-lovers chemistry, while the sudden, unsettling arrival of her boyfriend Rick introduces a constant undercurrent of psychological suspense. Vail doesn't waste time on overly flowery prose; instead, she relies on snappy dialogue and brisk plotting to keep the pages turning at a relentless speed. 

While the novel leans into beloved YA tropes, Vail’s world-building elevates the material, blending ancient Egyptian mythology with modern boarding school tropes in a seamless, entertaining package. The result is a striking, genre-savvy preview of a much larger supernatural world. Undeadly proves that Michele Vail is a voice to watch in urban fantasy, delivering a clever, spooky, and utterly engaging ride that will leave readers dying to see what happens next.
