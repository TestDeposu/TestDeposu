# This Immortal

**Author:** Roger Zelazny  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

**A Masterclass in Cosmic Intrigue: *Trumps of Doom* and the Enduring Magic of Roger Zelazny**

Roger Zelazny’s *Trumps of Doom*—the sixth installment in *The Chronicles of Amber*—is a book that doesn’t just belong in the pantheon of fantasy; it *defines* it. This is a story where the very fabric of reality is a game, where power is a card to be played, and where the line between hero and villain is as fluid as the shifting shadows of the multiverse. Narrated for the first time by Merlin, the enigmatic son of Corwin, the novel unfolds as a labyrinth of political machinations, supernatural threats, and existential dread—all wrapped in Zelazny’s signature prose, which is as lyrical as it is razor-sharp.

At its core, *Trumps of Doom* is a meditation on fate, identity, and the illusion of control. Merlin, a man who has spent his life dodging an annual assassination attempt on April 30th, is no ordinary protagonist. He is a survivor, a puzzle solver, and—unlike many fantasy heroes—a deeply flawed, introspective character whose greatest weapon is his mind. Zelazny strips away the traditional armor of the chosen one, replacing it with something far more unsettling: the knowledge that the universe is indifferent, and that even the most carefully laid plans can unravel at the whim of a higher power. The April 30th killings aren’t just a personal vendetta; they’re a metaphor for the fragility of existence itself. Who is pulling the strings? Why does the pattern repeat? And what does it mean when the answer lies not in the obvious, but in the hidden, the forgotten, the *other*?

This is Zelazny at his most ambitious. The *Chronicles of Amber* series has always been a love letter to the idea that reality is a construct—a grand, ever-shifting chessboard where the players are gods, demigods, and mortals alike. *Trumps of Doom* deepens that theme, weaving together threads of political intrigue (the court of Amber is a den of snakes, where loyalty is a currency and betrayal is a sport), cosmic horror (the "Other" is never truly gone, and its echoes linger like a bad dream), and existential philosophy (what does it mean to be *real* when you’re just a reflection of a greater truth?). The novel’s structure—with its restored prologue—feels like a secret handshake for longtime fans, a reminder that Zelazny’s world is vast enough to hold mysteries even the author didn’t fully unpack in his lifetime.

Zelazny’s prose is another triumph here. His writing is a symphony of precision and poetry, where every sentence carries weight. He doesn’t just describe a sword fight; he *feels* the clash of steel, the breath of the combatants, the way the world holds its breath. His dialogue crackles with wit and menace, whether it’s the sharp retorts of Amber’s aristocracy or the chilling calm of those who operate in the shadows. And his ability to make the abstract tangible—whether it’s the nature of the multiverse or the psychological toll of being a pawn in someone else’s game—is what elevates *Trumps of Doom* from a good fantasy novel to a *great* one.

What makes this book particularly compelling is its refusal to offer easy answers. Zelazny doesn’t give us a neat resolution; he gives us *layers*. The assassination attempts, the political scheming, the revelations about the Other—all of it is part of a larger, more terrifying truth. The novel forces the reader to ask: *How much of what we perceive is real?* Is Amber the true world, or just another shadow? And if the multiverse is a reflection, who—or what—is the original? These questions linger long after the final page, a testament to Zelazny’s genius for crafting stories that are as much about the *questions* as they are about the answers.

For fans of *The Chronicles of Amber*, *Trumps of Doom* is a homecoming. It’s a book that honors the series’ legacy while expanding its horizons, introducing new perspectives (Merlin’s voice is distinct, brooding, and deeply human) and deepening the lore in ways that feel both fresh and inevitable. For newcomers, it’s a gateway into a world where fantasy isn’t just about dragons and quests—it’s about the nature of power, the illusion of choice, and the terrifying beauty of a universe that doesn’t care about your survival.

Zelazny’s death in 1995 left a void in speculative fiction, but *Trumps of Doom* is a reminder that his work is timeless. This isn’t just a book; it’s an experience—a journey through a world where the rules are written in blood and ink, where every card you’re dealt could be a trump, and where the greatest trick of all is making you believe you’re in control. In a genre often criticized for its escapism, Zelazny’s *Trumps of Doom* is a masterclass in how to make fantasy *matter*—because at its heart, it’s not about magic or swords or kings. It’s about the stories we tell ourselves to make sense of the chaos.

If you haven’t read *The Chronicles of Amber* yet, start here. If you’ve read them before, this is the kind of book that makes you want to go back to the beginning and see everything with fresh eyes. And if you’re a Zelazny purist, this restored edition is a must-have—a secret door into the author’s mind, where the magic is as much in the gaps as it is in the words.
