# Infoquake (Jump 225, #1)

**Author:** David Louis Edelman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

What happens when you cross high-stakes venture capitalism with hardcore biopunk science fiction? You get David Louis Edelman’s blistering 2019 debut, Infoquake, the explosive opening salvo in the Jump 225 series. Eschewing the traditional tropes of galaxy-spanning space operas and brooding, leather-clad cyberpunk hackers, Edelman injects a jolt of pure corporate adrenaline into the genre. He trades stale dystopian clichés for something far more terrifying and utterly plausible: a ruthless future where the boardroom is the ultimate battlefield and code is life itself.

At the center of this hyper-capitalist maelstrom is Natch, a brilliant, cutthroat entrepreneur who has clawed his way to the top of the bio/logics market—the cutting-edge science of programming the human body. Natch is less of a brooding messiah and more of a ruthless, techno-maniacal Steve Jobs, driven by an insatiable hunger for market dominance and personal validation. When Natch is approached by Margaret Surina, owner of a paradigm-shifting, reality-bending technology known as MultiReal, he is thrust into a high-octane game of corporate chess. Pitted against the formidable High Executive Len Borda and a labyrinth of ruthless corporate enemies, Natch and his team face an impossible mandate: decode, develop, and launch MultiReal to the public in a mere seventy-two hours. All the while, the looming threat of the "infoquake"—a catastrophic surge capable of shattering the global bio/logic network—threatens to plunge civilization straight back into the Dark Ages.

Edelman’s greatest triumph in Infoquake lies in his razor-sharp thematic exploration of libertarian entrepreneurship, unfettered capitalism, and the commodification of human biology. While much of science fiction views future economies through a hazy, post-scarcity lens, Edelman leans hard into the mechanics of commerce. He crafts a meticulously realized world where intellectual property law, product development cycles, hostile takeovers, and marketing hype carry the weight of life and death. The novel brilliantly captures the manic, sleep-deprived obsession of internet moguls and startup founders, transforming balance sheets and venture capital negotiations into pulse-pounding drama. 

Stylistically, Edelman writes with a lucid, precise, and breakneck cadence. His prose is dense with technical speculation, yet it remains intensely readable, anchored by slick high-finance melodrama. He doesn’t just tell the reader about the complexities of bio/logics and MultiReal; he immerses them in the frantic, jargon-laden reality of a bleeding-edge tech launch. The world-building is unapologetically maximalist, bursting with breathless invention, vibrant world-design, and a profound understanding of how technology reshapes social structures. 

Infoquake is a stunning, paradigm-shifting debut that revitalized the technological thriller for the twenty-first century. It challenges readers to look at the intersection of business, technology, and human ambition through a startlingly fresh lens. For anyone tired of recycled tropes and looking for a smart, idiosyncratic blend of cyberpunk grit and corporate maneuvering, Edelman’s visionary work is an absolute must-read that will immediately hook you for the rest of the ride.
