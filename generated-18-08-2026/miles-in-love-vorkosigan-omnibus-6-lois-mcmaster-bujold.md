# Miles in Love (Vorkosigan Omnibus, #6)

**Author:** Lois McMaster Bujold  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

Lois McMaster Bujold has carved out a legendary space in modern science fiction not by relying solely on starship armadas or galactic cataclysms, but by anchoring her sprawling universe in the intricacies of human heart and political maneuver. Miles in Love, collecting the pivotal novels Komarr and A Civil Campaign alongside the novella Winterfair Gifts, represents the masterclass apex of her craft. It is an omnibus that brilliantly bridges the gap between high-stakes interstellar espionage and the delightfully chaotic theater of manners, proving once and for all that a well-placed dinner party can carry just as much narrative tension as a collapsing orbital mirror.

At the thematic core of this collection is the arduous, often clumsy, but deeply rewarding process of emotional and physical rehabilitation. Miles Vorkosigan, Barrayar’s diminutive genius, brilliant Imperial Auditor, and survivor of chronic physical trauma, has always understood war, politics, and strategy. Yet, as Bujold charts his trajectory here, Miles must learn that love cannot be conquered through tactical deployments or convoluted master plans. The central narrative tracks Miles from the bleak, dusty landscapes of Komarr—where he investigates a catastrophic sabotage of the planet's terraforming solar mirror—into the lush, deceptively perilous social battlegrounds of the Barrayaran capital. 

It is on Komarr that Miles encounters Ekaterin Vorsoisson, a woman nursing profound emotional wounds from a stifling, abusive marriage. Bujold refuses to offer a simplistic romance; instead, she crafts a delicate, slow-burn courtship defined by mutual respect, shared scars, and the painful unlearning of past betrayals. When Miles attempts to apply his trademark, over-engineered genius to wooing Ekaterin in A Civil Campaign, the result is a comedic and emotional tour de force. Bujold plays the romantic misadventures against a backdrop of political intrigue, bio-engineered delicacies, and familial interference, courtesy of Miles’s chaotic clone-brother Mark and his perpetually reluctant cousin Ivan. 

What makes Bujold’s style so remarkably enduring is her seamless fusion of space opera with the sharp, witty social observation reminiscent of Georgette Heyer or Jane Austen. Her Barrayar is a fascinating culture caught in a violent collision between medieval militarism and the modern galactic age. Bujold uses this societal friction not just for world-building flavor, but to interrogate deeper themes of duty, bodily autonomy, societal expectation, and the courage it takes to be vulnerable. Her prose is effortlessly readable, laced with dry, self-aware humor that never undermines the genuine emotional stakes of her characters. 

Miles in Love is ultimately a celebration of resilience. It reminds the reader that healing is not about returning to the way things were before the break, but about building something new and enduring out of the fragments. Whether you are a veteran of the Vorkosigan Saga or a newcomer stepping into Bujold’s brilliantly realized universe for the first time, this collection offers a masterclass in character-driven storytelling, sparkling dialogue, and profound emotional intelligence.
