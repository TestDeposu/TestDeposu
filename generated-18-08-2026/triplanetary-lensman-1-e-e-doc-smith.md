# Triplanetary (Lensman, #1)

**Author:** E.E. "Doc" Smith  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

E.E. Doc Smith is universally acknowledged as the undisputed godfather of space opera, the visionary architect who taught science fiction how to think in terms of galaxies rather than planets. Triplanetary, originally published in magazine serialization before becoming the foundational cornerstone of the legendary Lensman series, is a breathtaking masterclass in cosmic escalation. For modern readers, approaching Triplanetary is less like reading a conventional novel and more like witnessing the Big Bang of pulp science fiction—a chaotic, magnificent genesis where the template for modern space warfare, superhuman guardians, and multidimensional empires was first forged in atomic fire.

The overarching theme of Triplanetary is the relentless, eons-spanning conflict between absolute cosmic good and absolute cosmic evil, orchestrated by unseen higher intelligences. Smith traces this shadow war across billions of years, beginning with the cataclysmic destruction of Atlantis and the manipulation of Earth's civilizations, before rocketing into the twentieth and twenty-first centuries. Humanity is not merely a bystander on its own world; it is the ultimate genetic crucible. Two cosmic entities—the benevolent Arisians and the malevolent Eddorians—are secretly breeding distinct bloodlines on Earth to serve as their eventual champions. This sweeping historical canvas gives Triplanetary a unique flavor that distinguishes it from its sequels. It is a historical epic of the future and the deep past rolled into one, demonstrating that human history is merely a localized skirmish in a much grander, universe-spanning chess match.

Smith’s literary style is the purest distillation of pulp energy: breathless, maximalist, and unapologetically melodramatic. He does not dabble in quiet introspection or subtle character arcs. Instead, his characters are paragons of virtue or hiss-worthy villains, driven by duty, intellect, and sheer willpower. The prose moves at a hyper-velocity pace, rushing from ancient Earth cataclysms to interplanetary ship-to-ship combat with a breathless enthusiasm that is utterly infectious. Science in Triplanetary is treated less as a rigid set of physical laws and more as a magical toolkit of superscience—heat-beams, negators, inertialess drives, and atomic blasts that shatter continents. 

What makes Triplanetary a striking achievement, particularly within the context of a massive omnibus collection like the Wildside Press Megapack, is its audacity. Smith invents tropes that contemporary science fiction still relies on today: ancient alien architects, secret bloodlines shaping human destiny, ruthless interstellar empires, and protagonists who possess intellects and capabilities that far surpass ordinary mortals. The pacing can occasionally feel episodic, bridging vast gulfs of time as it sets the stage for the true Lensman saga to unfold in subsequent volumes, yet this structural sprawl only adds to its mythological weight. 

For fans of classic speculative fiction, space opera enthusiasts, and historians of the genre alike, Triplanetary is an essential pilgrimage. It is raw, dated in its social assumptions yet wildly progressive in its imagination, and thrillingly boundless in its scope. Smith invites us to look past the stars not with quiet wonder, but with the roar of rocket engines and the clash of cosmic titans.
