# An Oblique Approach (Belisarius, #1)

**Author:** David Drake  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

History is never merely a record of what happened; it is the arena where human will clashes against forces beyond comprehension. In An Oblique Approach, the opening salvo of the celebrated Belisarius series co-authored by science fiction masters David Drake and Eric Flint, this premise is pushed to its absolute, breathless limit. Published in 2013, this gripping novel takes the well-trodden paths of sixth-century Mediterranean history and violently shatters them, injecting a pulse of high-stakes cosmic intervention into the rigid machinery of the ancient world. 

At its core, the main theme of An Oblique Approach is the profound tension between monumental, god-like determinism and the stubborn, gritty agency of humanity. The narrative is set against the backdrop of a fractured world where the superpower of the East, the Byzantine Empire under Emperor Justinian, faces an existential threat. In northern India, the Malwa Empire is rising, fueled by an unspeakable evil and guided—or perhaps possessed—by a hyper-advanced alien intelligence from beyond time. This extra-temporal entity seeks eternal domination over Earth, wielding terrifying new weapons and an uncompromising will to power. Against this cosmic calculus stands a singular, brilliant human mind: Flavius Belisarius, history’s greatest commander. 

David Drake’s signature style is immediately palpable on every page. Known for his hard military science fiction and deep reverence for classical history, Drake brings a gritty, boots-in-the-mud realism to the proceedings. Even as the novel flirts with cosmic opera and speculative time-meddling, it refuses to indulge in abstract hand-waving. Instead, Drake anchors the grand conflict in the tactile, unglamorous reality of ancient warfare. We feel the chafing of leather armor, smell the sweat of marching legions, and hear the metallic clash of cavalry charges. The prose is lean, muscular, and propulsive, eschewing flowery exposition in favor of tactical clarity and visceral tension. 

What makes An Oblique Approach so striking is how it merges the grandiosity of alternate history with the intimacy of character-driven military strategy. Belisarius is not depicted as an untouchable demigod, but as a cautious, brilliant tactician who must constantly adapt to impossible odds. When a mysterious, precognitive crystal enters the equation, offering visions of futures that must be averted, the book elevates itself from a standard historical war drama into a desperate chess match against destiny itself. 

Drake and Flint construct a world where the supernatural and the historical bleed together seamlessly. The Malwa represent totalizing, cold efficiency, while Byzantium—flawed, corrupt, yet fiercely resilient—embodies the messy, enduring spirit of human civilization. The clash between them is not just territorial; it is a battle for the very soul of history.

For readers who crave intelligent military fiction, rich historical texture, and high-concept speculative stakes, An Oblique Approach delivers a masterclass in world-building. It proves that the most compelling science fiction often wears the dusty armor of the past, reminding us that even when facing gods and monsters from beyond time, the fate of the world still rests on the courage of ordinary mortals and the brilliance of a good strategist.
