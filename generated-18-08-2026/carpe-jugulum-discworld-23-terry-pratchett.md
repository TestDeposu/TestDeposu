# Carpe Jugulum (Discworld, #23)

**Author:** Terry Pratchett  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Carpe Jugulum, the 23rd installment in Terry Pratchett's renowned Discworld series, is a scintillating blend of satire, wit, and dark humor that will leave readers spellbound. Published in 1998, not 2009, this novel is the sixth in the Witches collection, following the adventures of the inimitable Granny Weatherwax, Nanny Ogg, Magrat, and young Agnes.

In this clever send-up of traditional vampire lore, Pratchett lampoons the conventions of the undead with characteristic panache. The Magpyr family, a group of stylish and sophisticated vampires, are invited to Lancre by King Verence to celebrate the birth of his daughter. However, these modern vampires have no intention of leaving, and it's up to the coven of witches to stop them.

Pratchett's masterful storytelling is on full display in Carpe Jugulum, as he effortlessly weaves together a complex narrative that pokes fun at everything from Christian superstition to Swiss Army knives. The author's signature style, a unique blend of humor, satire, and social commentary, is perfectly captured in this novel.

One of the most striking aspects of Carpe Jugulum is its exploration of the tension between tradition and modernity. The Magpyr family, with their fancy waistcoats and love of wine, represent a new breed of vampire that is determined to adapt to the changing times. However, their attempts to assimilate into Lancre's society are met with resistance from the coven of witches, who are determined to preserve their way of life.

Through this conflict, Pratchett cleverly examines the challenges of cultural exchange and the difficulties of adapting to change. The novel raises important questions about the nature of identity and the importance of preserving traditional values in the face of modernity.

The characters in Carpe Jugulum are, as always, one of the novel's greatest strengths. Granny Weatherwax, Nanny Ogg, and Magrat are as formidable and fascinating as ever, and young Agnes brings a fresh perspective to the group. The Magpyr family, meanwhile, are deliciously over-the-top, with their pretentious affectations and absurd attempts to fit in.

Overall, Carpe Jugulum is a triumph of comedic fantasy that will appeal to fans of the Discworld series and newcomers alike. With its clever plot, memorable characters, and biting satire, this novel is a must-read for anyone who enjoys a good laugh and a healthy dose of social commentary.
