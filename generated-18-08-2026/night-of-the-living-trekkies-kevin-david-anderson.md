# Night of the Living Trekkies

**Author:** Kevin David Anderson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

**Night of the Living Trekkies: When the Starship Enterprise Runs Aground in Zombie Apocalypse**

Kevin David Anderson’s *Night of the Living Trekkies* is a genre-blending masterstroke—a feverish, fast-paced mashup of *Star Trek* fandom, zombie horror, and dark satire that turns the ultimate geek-out into a full-blown survival nightmare. At its core, this novel isn’t just another zombie romp; it’s a biting commentary on the unshakable devotion of fans, the absurdity of pop culture obsession, and the thin line between passion and paranoia. Anderson, a writer who has long thrived in the intersection of sci-fi and horror (his *Star Trek* novels are legendary), delivers a story that’s as clever as it is chaotic, where the real monsters aren’t the undead—it’s the virus of fandom itself.

The premise is simple yet devastatingly effective: Jim Pike, a disillusioned ex-soldier and hotel assistant manager, is pulled into the chaos when a convention of *Star Trek* enthusiasts descends upon his hotel. What starts as a routine event spirals into a full-blown apocalypse when attendees begin transforming into ravenous, brain-eating zombies—all because of a rogue bioengineered virus designed to target *Trek* fans. Suddenly, Jim and a motley crew of cosplay-clad survivors—including a Klingon warrior, a Vulcan logician, and a Ferengi merchant—must navigate a world where the only thing more dangerous than the undead is the sheer, unhinged loyalty of their fellow fans. Anderson’s genius lies in flipping the script: instead of the usual "smart people survive" trope, the heroes are the ones who *know* the most about *Star Trek*—and that knowledge becomes both their strength and their curse.

The book’s strength is its tone—a perfect balance between dark humor and genuine dread. Anderson’s writing crackles with the energy of a *Trek* convention gone wrong, where inside jokes and pop culture references fly as freely as bullets. The dialogue is sharp, the characters are vividly flawed, and the horror is visceral without being gratuitous. Jim Pike, the protagonist, is a compelling antihero: a man who once believed in the idealism of *Star Trek* but has been hardened by war, now forced to rely on the very fandom he once scorned. His journey—from reluctant participant to reluctant leader—is the emotional core of the story, and Anderson handles it with a mix of cynicism and unexpected warmth.

What makes *Night of the Living Trekkies* particularly compelling is its exploration of fandom as both a shield and a vulnerability. The characters’ deep knowledge of *Star Trek* lore isn’t just window dressing; it’s a survival tool, a psychological crutch, and sometimes a liability. Anderson doesn’t mock his audience—he understands the passion that drives fans, even as he exposes its darker side. The novel’s climax, where the survivors must confront the very ideals they’ve spent their lives idolizing, is both satisfying and thought-provoking. It’s a story about how far people will go for their obsessions—and how quickly those obsessions can turn into a prison.

Visually, the book is a feast for *Star Trek* fans, with Anderson weaving in enough Easter eggs to satisfy even the most die-hard Trekkie. Yet, the horror elements never feel like an afterthought. The zombies aren’t just mindless brutes; they’re infected fans, their bodies twisted by the virus while their minds retain glimpses of their former selves. The horror is personal, the stakes are high, and the tension never wavers. Anderson’s prose is lean and efficient, ensuring that the story moves at a breakneck pace—perfect for fans who want their horror with a side of *Trek* nostalgia.

Ultimately, *Night of the Living Trekkies* is more than just a fun, fast-paced zombie novel. It’s a love letter to fandom, a cautionary tale about obsession, and a testament to the power of pop culture to both unite and destroy. Anderson’s style—sharp, witty, and unapologetically geeky—makes this book a standout in the horror genre, proving that even the most unlikely of combinations can work. If you’re a *Star Trek* fan looking for a thrilling, darkly humorous twist on the franchise, or a horror reader craving something fresh and clever, this is the book for you. Just don’t expect to leave the convention center alive.
