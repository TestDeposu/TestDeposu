# The Invasion (Animorphs, #1)

**Author:** K.A. Applegate  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

The Invasion, the first installment in the revamped Animorphs series by K.A. Applegate, is a gripping and intense ride that catapults readers into a world of intergalactic warfare and teenage angst. Originally published in 1996, this 2011 re-release brings back the classic tale with a fresh twist, reeling in both old and new fans alike.

At its core, The Invasion is a story about the struggle for power, identity, and humanity in the face of an invisible enemy. The Animorphs, a group of five teenagers, stumble upon a dying alien pilot who bestows upon them the power to morph into any animal they touch. This gift, however, comes with a steep price: they must use their newfound abilities to fight a secret war against the Yeerks, parasitic aliens that can infest and control human hosts.

Applegate's writing style is a masterclass in balancing action, suspense, and emotional depth. Her characters are multidimensional and relatable, each with their own distinct voice and struggles. Jake, the reluctant leader, grapples with the weight of responsibility; Rachel, the fashionista, conceals a fierce determination; Tobias, the quiet outcast, finds solace in his newfound powers; Cassie, the animal lover, struggles with the moral implications of their actions; and Marco, the class clown, hides a deep-seated fear of loss.

Through the Animorphs' journey, Applegate explores themes of loyalty, trust, and the blurred lines between good and evil. The Yeerks, as a formidable foe, represent the insidious nature of invasion, where the enemy can be anyone, anywhere. This ambiguity creates a sense of tension and unease, keeping readers on the edge of their seats as they navigate the complex web of relationships and allegiances.

One of the most striking aspects of The Invasion is its ability to tackle mature themes without shying away from the harsh realities of war. Applegate does not romanticize the violence or the consequences of the Animorphs' actions, instead presenting a gritty, realistic portrayal of the costs of resistance.

The pacing of the book is relentless, with each chapter ending on a cliffhanger that propels the reader forward. Applegate's prose is concise, yet evocative, conjuring vivid images of the Animorphs' transformations and battles. The world-building, while not overly complex, is well-integrated into the narrative, providing a solid foundation for the series to come.

In conclusion, The Invasion is a captivating and thought-provoking read that will appeal to fans of science fiction, action, and adventure. With its diverse cast of characters, morally complex themes, and breakneck pacing, this book is an excellent starting point for the Animorphs series. If you're looking for a compelling story that will keep you up all night, look no further.
