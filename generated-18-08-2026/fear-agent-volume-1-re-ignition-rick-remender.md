# Fear Agent, Volume 1: Re-Ignition

**Author:** Rick Remender  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2026

The fall of gods does not liberate a world; it merely shatters the fragile cage that kept the chaos at bay. In Fear Agent, Volume 1: Re-Ignition, Rick Remender returns to his signature brand of cosmic pulp and emotional devastation, proving once again that he is a master of blending high-octane science fiction with profound, character-driven heartbreak. Though the universe has changed, the collateral damage remains deeply, painfully human.

Remender has always excelled at chronicling the lives of broken people thrust into impossible scenarios, and Re-Ignition is no exception. As the catastrophic war of the gods reaches its violent peak and Pigeon’s rebellion ignites, the oppressed rise to strike down the divine tyrants who have fed on their lifeblood for generations. Yet, Remender is too cynical—and too realistic—to offer a clean, fairy-tale resolution. From the smoking ashes of Harlos, only a single scorched hope remains. In the aftermath, characters like Soluna are left to reckon with the brutal, inescapable cost of their choices. Old wounds split wide open, fragile alliances crumble under the weight of survival, and the peace that follows the war proves infinitely more dangerous than the conflict that forged it.

What makes Remender’s style so compelling in this volume is his relentless pacing juxtaposed with moments of agonizing introspection. He writes with a frantic, breathless urgency that mimics the chaos of a dying universe, yet he always knows when to pull the emergency brake to let the silence sting. The narrative thrives on moral ambiguity; there are no clean heroes here, only survivors carrying mountains of guilt. The world-building is expansive and grimy, far removed from the pristine futures of traditional space opera. This is a universe held together by duct tape, whiskey, and sheer stubbornness, where every victory extracts a piece of the victor’s soul.

Re-Ignition serves as both a thrilling continuation for longtime fans and a striking entry point into Remender’s sprawling cosmic tapestry. It is loud, messy, visually explosive, and anchored by a beating heart that refuses to let its characters go numb, no matter how many times the world breaks around them. If you like your science fiction bruised, bleeding, and brilliantly ambitious, this volume is an essential addition to your shelf.
