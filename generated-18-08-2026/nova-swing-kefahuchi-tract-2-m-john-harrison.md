# Nova Swing (Kefahuchi Tract, #2)

**Author:** M. John Harrison  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

M. John Harrison’s 2007 novel *Nova Swing*—the second movement in his loosely connected Kefahuchi Tract sequence—does not merely bend the rules of traditional science fiction; it gleefully dismantles them and leaves the wreckage rusting in an alleyway. Set in a world irrevocably altered by the proximity of the Tract, a sprawling spatial anomaly that occasionally drops incomprehensible debris onto Earth, this is not a story of heroic exploration or triumphant space opera. Instead, Harrison offers something far more visceral, intoxicating, and unsettling: a gritty noir thriller wrapped in the suffocating embrace of cosmic horror.

At the heart of *Nova Swing* is Vic Serotonin, a low-level "travel agent" who guides thrill-seekers and the desperate into the Saudade Event Site. This localized zone of warped geography and fractured physics is a dumping ground for the inexplicable. Within its borders, reality stutters, psychic onslaughts are a hazard of the trade, and ordinary cats emerge in strange, monochromatic waves. Vic’s illegal enterprise is a precarious balancing act, further complicated by an unpredictable new client and a relentless, metaphysically inclined detective determined to shut him down. Meanwhile, the world outside is quietly unraveling, infected by a troubling new class of biological artifacts—living algorithms that rewrite the rules of existence with viral efficiency.

What makes *Nova Swing* a masterclass in speculative fiction is Harrison’s refusal to provide comforting answers. While lesser authors might use a cosmic anomaly as a plot device to be analyzed, quantified, and eventually defeated, Harrison treats the Kefahuchi Tract with a profound, terrifying indifference. It is a force of nature so vast and alien that human ambition, morality, and suffering are rendered utterly minuscule. The novel’s central theme is the friction between human habit and incomprehensible change. In a universe veering toward viral chaos, Harrison’s characters continue to smoke cigarettes, run dodgy businesses, and nurse broken hearts, clinging to their routines on the precipice of oblivion. 

Stylistically, Harrison is operating at the absolute peak of his powers. His prose is lean, muscular, and diamond-sharp, drawing heavily from the cadence of hardboiled noir while drenched in the neon-and-grit aesthetics of cyber-punk and New Weird fiction. He masters the art of the evocative fragment; his descriptions of body-modification chop shops, underground clubs, and the desolate sprawl of Moneytown are cinematic yet deeply atmospheric. Harrison does not waste words on heavy-handed exposition. Instead, he drops the reader directly into the linguistic grime of his world, trusting us to piece together the rules of a reality that is constantly shifting beneath our feet. 

The characters themselves are brilliantly realized precisely because they are so unapologetically flawed. Vic Serotonin is no dashing noir detective; he is a survivor treading water in a sea of bad decisions and cosmic indifference. His interactions crackle with a weary, lived-in cynicism that grounds the book’s more surreal, mind-bending elements in a gritty, human reality. 

*Nova Swing* is a triumph of imagination and style. It is a book that lingers long after the final page, leaving you looking at the ordinary world with a lingering sense of paranoia, wondering what might be falling from the sky just out of sight. For readers tired of predictable genre tropes and looking for speculative fiction with teeth, literary merit, and a total lack of sentimental hand-holding, Harrison’s work is nothing short of essential.
