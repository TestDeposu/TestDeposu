# Traveling with the Dead (James Asher, #2)

**Author:** Barbara Hambly  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

In "Traveling with the Dead," Barbara Hambly masterfully weaves a complex tale of espionage, politics, and the supernatural, set against the rich backdrop of turn-of-the-century Europe. This second installment in the James Asher series is a gripping narrative that delves into the darker aspects of human nature, expertly balancing historical detail with the eerie and unknown.

At the heart of the story lies the intricate dance between the living and the dead, as a catastrophic alliance between an Austrian spy and the undead threatens to destabilize the British Empire. It is here that Hambly's unique blend of historical fiction and vampire lore truly shines, as she navigates the treacherous landscape of early 20th-century politics with ease.

One of the standout features of Hambly's writing is her ability to craft compelling, multidimensional characters. Lydia Asher, in particular, is a force to be reckoned with – a strong-willed and intelligent woman who finds herself drawn into the world of espionage and the supernatural. Her partnership with the enigmatic Don Simon Ysidro is a highlight of the novel, as they embark on a perilous journey across Europe, from the fog-shrouded streets of London to the ancient city of Constantinople.

Throughout the novel, Hambly's prose is evocative and immersive, conjuring the sights, sounds, and smells of a bygone era. Her writing is atmospheric and engaging, with a keen sense of pacing that propels the reader forward. The world of the vampires is richly detailed, with a complex hierarchy and politics that add depth to the narrative.

What sets "Traveling with the Dead" apart from other vampire novels is its thoughtful exploration of the human condition. Hambly's vampires are not simply monstrous creatures, but rather complex beings with their own motivations and desires. This nuanced approach adds a layer of sophistication to the narrative, elevating the novel beyond the realm of simple horror or fantasy.

In short, "Traveling with the Dead" is a gripping and atmospheric tale of espionage, politics, and the supernatural, set against the rich backdrop of turn-of-the-century Europe. With its compelling characters, immersive world-building, and thoughtful exploration of the human condition, this novel is sure to captivate fans of historical fiction, vampire lore, and suspenseful storytelling.
