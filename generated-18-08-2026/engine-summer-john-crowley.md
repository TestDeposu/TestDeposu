# Engine Summer

**Author:** John Crowley  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

Engine Summer by John Crowley is a mesmerizing and thought-provoking novel that delves into the complexities of human nature, identity, and the power of storytelling. The book is a collection of three interconnected narratives, each set in a distinct world, yet unified by the author's exploration of the human condition.

Crowley's writing style is a unique blend of poetic lyricism, philosophical introspection, and imaginative world-building. His prose is rich and evocative, conjuring vivid images of the worlds he creates, from the twilight land of The Deep to the genetically engineered society of Beasts, and the post-apocalyptic landscape of Engine Summer.

At its core, Engine Summer is a novel about the search for meaning, identity, and connection in a world that is constantly changing. The protagonist of the title story, Rush That Speaks, is a young man on a quest to find the Saints who can teach him to speak truthfully and achieve immortality through his stories. His journey is a powerful allegory for the human desire to transcend mortality and leave a lasting legacy.

Throughout the novel, Crowley explores the tensions between tradition and innovation, as well as the consequences of playing with the fundamental building blocks of life. In Beasts, the genetic experimentation that allows humans to combine their DNA with that of animals raises questions about the boundaries between species and the implications of scientific hubris.

One of the most striking aspects of Crowley's writing is his ability to balance complexity and accessibility. His ideas are often abstract and intellectually challenging, but he presents them in a way that is both engaging and easy to follow. This is a testament to his skill as a storyteller and his willingness to take risks in exploring the human condition.

If there is a weakness to the novel, it is the somewhat disjointed nature of the three narratives. While each story is compelling on its own, the connections between them are not always clear, and some readers may find it difficult to navigate the different worlds and characters.

Overall, however, Engine Summer is a masterpiece of speculative fiction that will appeal to fans of authors like Ursula K. Le Guin, Gene Wolfe, and Samuel R. Delany. Crowley's unique blend of philosophical introspection, imaginative world-building, and poetic prose makes for a reading experience that is both challenging and rewarding.
