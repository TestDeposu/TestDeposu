# The Atlantis Plague (The Origin Mystery, #2)

**Author:** A.G. Riddle  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

When A.G. Riddle burst onto the speculative fiction scene with The Atlantis Gene, he established a high-octane standard for merging ancient history with futuristic bio-thrillers. In the second installment of The Origin Mystery series, The Atlantis Plague, Riddle elevates the stakes from a race against time to a desperate fight for the very survival of the human species. 

The narrative thrust of the novel centers on a global pandemic engineered by an ancient race to reboot human evolution. As the deadly "Plague" sweeps across continents, mutating its victims and threatening global annihilation, the boundary between salvation and extinction blurs. Riddle anchors this grand, apocalyptic scale in the human toll of the crisis. We are once again thrust into the lives of geneticist Dr. Kate Warner and intelligence agent David slaike, forced to navigate a labyrinth of shifting alliances, corporate greed, and evolutionary biology. The central theme of the novel explores the ethical boundaries of human progress: if humanity is fundamentally flawed, do we have the moral right to alter our own DNA to survive, and at what point does intervention strip us of our humanity?

Riddle’s authorial style is unapologetically cinematic. Much like a high-budget blockbuster, the book relies on relentless pacing, short cliffhanger chapters, and a rapidly expanding scope that keeps the reader perpetually off-balance. He excels at taking complex, theoretical concepts in genetics, archaeology, and quantum physics and distilling them into accessible, breathless narrative beats. While character development occasionally takes a backseat to plot momentum, the sheer velocity of the narrative makes it easy to forgive. Riddle crafts a world where every revelation spawns three new questions, creating an addictive reading experience that feels less like turning pages and more like watching a countdown timer tick down to zero.

For fans of Michael Crichton and Dan Brown, The Atlantis Plague is a masterclass in modern techno-thriller pacing. It is ambitious, chaotic, and thoroughly entertaining—a gripping reminder that our greatest threat has never been from the stars, but buried deep within our own history and biology.
