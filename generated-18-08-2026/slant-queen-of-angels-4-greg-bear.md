# Slant (Queen of Angels, #4)

**Author:** Greg Bear  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

Perfection is a fragile master. In Slant, the final installment of his seminal Queen of Angels sequence, Greg Bear returns to the meticulously engineered, near-future landscape of the mid-twenty-first century to ask a haunting, recursive question: what happens when a society that has cured madness, violence, and biological decay suddenly begins to rot from the inside out? Published in 2014, this cyberpunk masterwork showcases a grandmaster of hard-edge science fiction operating at the absolute peak of his intellectual and narrative powers.

Bear’s world is one utterly transfigured by nanotechnology and advanced psychotherapy. Humanity has retreated behind a gleaming veneer of sanitized balance; physical forms are customizable, mental illnesses are routinely smoothed away, and the global dataflow hums with frictionless efficiency. Yet, Bear is too sophisticated a writer to offer a simplistic utopia. Instead, he sets a chilling tone right from the start by introducing a series of gruesome murders and seemingly inexplicable suicides among the marginalized. 

This brings us to the beating heart of the novel’s thematic exploration: the illusion of absolute control. When public defender Mary Cho steps into the investigation, Bear peels back the glittering layers of his techno-society to reveal a dark, churning underbelly. The narrative deftly weaves together disparate threads—neo-Luddite separatists, hyper-realistic virtual pornography, corporate intrigue, and the terrifying possibility of an emergent, rogue artificial intelligence. The central theme crystallizes around the vulnerability of hyper-connectivity. When human existence is entirely mediated by technology and psychological conditioning, a single flaw in the system—a slant in the dataflow—is all it takes to send a seemingly perfect civilization hurtling toward collapse.

Stylistically, Bear approaches speculative fiction with the rigor of a theoretical scientist and the prose of a literary novelist. Hard science fiction often risks sacrificing character depth at the altar of exposition, but Bear avoids this pitfall entirely. His prose is dense, intellectually demanding, and relentlessly immersive. He does not hold the reader’s hand through the dizzying implications of molecular-level body modification or complex data architectures; instead, he drops you directly into the deep end of a thoroughly realized world. The world-building is breathtakingly expansive, yet it remains intimately tied to the psychological states of its characters. 

Mary Cho’s investigation serves as the perfect vehicle for this style. As she navigates the stratified tiers of this future society, Bear uses her perspective to probe the ethics of forced sanity and the natural human resistance to engineered perfection. The pacing is deliberate, building tension not through cheap action beats, but through a slow, suffocating realization that the safety nets humanity has built are fraying. 

Slant is a triumph of speculative foresight. It is a cautionary tale that feels increasingly prophetic in our own era of ubiquitous algorithms, artificial intelligence breakthroughs, and digital dependency. Greg Bear challenges us to consider the heavy cost of a pain-free existence, proving once again why he remains a towering figure in the genre. For readers who demand intelligence, philosophical depth, and uncompromising hard sci-fi world-building, this novel is an absolute must-read.
