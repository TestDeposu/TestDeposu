# The Light at the Bottom of the World (Light the Abyss, #1)

**Author:** London Shah  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

Dive into the Drowned Ruins: A Review of London Shah’s The Light at the Bottom of the World

In her striking 2019 debut, The Light at the Bottom of the World, author London Shah reimagines a chillingly plausible and utterly mesmerizing future. Set in the waning days of the twenty-first century, humanity no longer looks up at the stars with longing, but downward into the abyssal depths. Following a cataclysmic shift, sea creatures now swim through the skeletal remains of London, and the remnants of mankind are trapped beneath the crushing weight of the ocean, caught in an eternal tug-of-war between desperate hope and paralyzing fear. 

At its core, the novel pulses with a profound thematic exploration of resilience against systemic oppression. Shah crafts a world where survival is a daily negotiation with a corrupt government that rules from the shadows, controlling information and crushing dissent. Within this claustrophobic aquatic dystopia, the author investigates what it truly means to fight for family and truth when the entire world is stacked against you. Sixteen-year-old Leyla McQueen embodies this relentless spirit. As a talented submersible racer entering the city’s prestigious annual marathon to secure her wrongfully imprisoned father’s freedom, Leyla is the beating heart of the narrative. Her journey brilliantly underscores the theme of agency, illustrating how one individual’s refusal to remain silent can ripple outward and threaten to expose a regime's darkest secrets. 

Shah’s writing style is nothing short of immersive. Utilizing a blend of high-octane thrills and atmospheric world-building, she paints the sunken metropolis with vivid, sensory-rich prose. The aquatic setting is not merely a backdrop; it is a living, breathing entity that dictates the rhythm of the plot. Every shadow cast by the ruined architecture of old London, every bioluminescent flash of deep-sea fauna, and the constant, terrifying threat of the unknown create an almost palpable tension. The pacing is relentless, particularly once the marathon begins and shifts the narrative into a high-stakes race against time. 

Furthermore, Shah expertly weaves in the classic tropes of YA sci-fi—such as the reluctant alliance between Leyla and a guarded, hotheaded companion—while keeping the dynamic fresh and grounded in their harrowing circumstances. The chemistry between the characters never overshadows the overarching mystery, ensuring the plot remains front and center. 

The Light at the Bottom of the World is a triumphant opening to the Light the Abyss series. By fusing breathtaking underwater aesthetics with a gripping political mystery and a deeply personal stake, London Shah has delivered a compelling sci-fi adventure. It is a striking reminder that even in the darkest, deepest trenches of the world, the human spirit refuses to be extinguished.
