# Triss (Redwall, #15)

**Author:** Brian Jacques  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2002

**A Tale of Courage, Cunning, and the Unbreakable Spirit of Redwall**

Brian Jacques’ *Triss*, the fifteenth installment in the beloved *Redwall* series, is a masterclass in adventure storytelling—one that weaves together the timeless themes of rebellion, loyalty, and the indomitable will to defy tyranny. At its heart, this tale is a triumph of the underdog, a celebration of those who refuse to be crushed by oppression, even when the odds are stacked against them. The story follows Triss, a young squirrelmaid enslaved by the cruel Ferret King Agarnu and his daughter, Princess Kurda, whose cruelty mirrors the worst of medieval despots. But Triss is no passive victim; she is a spark of defiance, a warrior in spirit long before she becomes one in deed. Her journey—from captivity to liberation—is a testament to the power of hope, and Jacques crafts it with the same rich, immersive detail that has made *Redwall* a cornerstone of children’s fantasy.

What makes *Triss* particularly compelling is its exploration of resistance without glorifying violence. Jacques, ever the moralist, ensures that the struggle is as much about cunning and solidarity as it is about battle. The Ferrets, though monstrous in their cruelty, are not mere caricatures of evil; they are a regime built on fear and exploitation, and their downfall is as much a product of the enslaved’s unity as it is of any single act of heroism. This nuance elevates the story beyond a simple good-versus-evil narrative, offering instead a meditation on the strength found in collective defiance. It’s a theme that resonates deeply, especially in an era where stories of oppression and liberation continue to captivate audiences of all ages.

Jacques’ signature style shines here as brightly as ever. His prose is lush, his world-building meticulous, and his characters—whether they are the noble creatures of Redwall or the villainous Ferrets—are rendered with a depth that feels both timeless and fresh. The dialogue crackles with wit and warmth, while the action sequences are thrilling without ever veering into gratuitousness. The *Redwall* universe, with its sprawling abbeys, ancient ruins, and mythical creatures, is as vividly realized as ever, making even the most familiar locations feel new. There’s a sense of history and legend woven into the fabric of the story, as if each creature and place carries the weight of generations past.

Yet what truly sets *Triss* apart is its emotional core. Jacques doesn’t shy away from the harsh realities of slavery or the psychological toll of oppression, but he balances these themes with moments of profound hope and camaraderie. The relationships between Triss and her allies—whether it’s the wise old badger or the fierce young warriors—are heartfelt and inspiring, reminding readers that even the smallest acts of kindness can ignite change. The climax, where Triss and her companions stand together against the Ferret regime, is a triumphant affirmation that freedom is not given—it is taken, and it is worth fighting for.

For fans of the series, *Triss* is a worthy addition, continuing the tradition of adventure, moral clarity, and unapologetic joy that defines *Redwall*. For newcomers, it’s an excellent entry point—a story that captures the magic of Jacques’ world while standing on its own as a tale of courage and resilience. The book’s enduring appeal lies in its ability to entertain while also challenging its readers to think about the nature of power, justice, and the courage it takes to stand against injustice.

In the end, *Triss* is more than just another adventure in the *Redwall* saga; it’s a story about the fire that burns in the hearts of the oppressed and the bonds that allow them to rise. Brian Jacques, ever the storyteller, crafts a tale that is both thrilling and deeply moving, proving once again why *Redwall* remains one of the most cherished fantasy series of all time.
