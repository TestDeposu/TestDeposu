# In Fury Born (1) (Fury Series)

**Author:** David Weber  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

David Weber is a master architect of military science fiction, best known for his sweeping space operas and the indomitable heroine Honor Harrington. In In Fury Born, Weber pivots from the grand-scale naval tactics that defined his career to a visceral, deeply personal narrative of vengeance, betrayal, and mythological awakening. The result is a sprawling, high-octane epic that examines the heavy toll of combat and the terrifying rebirth of a warrior pushed past the brink of endurance.

The central theme of In Fury Born is the inescapable bond between the soldier and the battlefield, and the devastating cost of a society that discards its protectors. Alicia DeVries is not merely a protagonist; she is an embodiment of the veteran experience taken to a cosmic extreme. Having led the Imperial Cadre through legendary and bloody conflicts at Zhikotse, Shallingsport, and Louvain, Alicia expects the quiet obscurity of retirement. Instead, she receives the ultimate betrayal from the very empire she bled to protect. Weber uses this setup to explore the alienation of the warrior class, contrasting the cold, bureaucratic ingratitude of civilization with the raw, uncompromising code of the frontlines. 

When tragedy strikes her frontier-world family in a brutal, remorseless attack, Alicia is stripped of everything except her lethal expertise. But Weber introduces a fascinating mythological twist: the awakening of Tisiphone, the ancient spirit of the Fury. This possession elevates the novel from a standard revenge thriller into something mythic. The Fury inside Alicia is not just a supernatural power boost; it is the personification of righteous wrath, an implacable and ancient force that demands a blood price. Through Alicia and Tisiphone, Weber wrestles with the theme of violence as a cycle—whether the rage that saves us is also the thing that consumes us.

True to form, Weber’s signature style shines through every chapter. He is an author famous for his meticulous world-building, exhaustive military detail, and deep psychological framing of his characters. In Fury Born benefits immensely from this approach. The novel is divided into distinct halves—the slow-burn establishment of Alicia’s past, her betrayal, and her civilian life, followed by an absolute explosion of tactical prowess and relentless pursuit. Weber does not rush the narrative. He takes the time to ground the reader in the geopolitics of his universe, the mechanics of futuristic combat, and the emotional bruising of his protagonist. 

When the action finally erupts, it is delivered with cinematic precision. Weber’s technical descriptions of weaponry, starships, and ground engagements give the narrative an anchor of gritty realism, even as it flirts with Greek mythology. Yet, despite the hardware and the tactics, the emotional core remains razor-sharp. Alicia DeVries is a complex, formidable creation—flawed, exhausted, yet terrifyingly competent. Fans of Weber’s Honor Harrington will recognize the fierce moral compass and tactical brilliance, but Alicia possesses a darker, more visceral edge that sets her apart.

In Fury Born is a triumphant blend of military sci-fi and classical myth. It offers the satisfying catharsis of a well-executed revenge story while challenging the reader to consider the true price of empire and the terrible majesty of a warrior unleashed. For both longtime fans of David Weber and newcomers to military fiction, this novel is an unforgettable, pulse-pounding descent into the heart of vengeance.
