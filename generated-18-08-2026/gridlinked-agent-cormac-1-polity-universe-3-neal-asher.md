# Gridlinked (Agent Cormac #1, Polity Universe #3)

**Author:** Neal Asher  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

When readers dive into Neal Asher’s Gridlinked, the opening pages demand absolute attention, immediately plunging them into a visceral, high-octane future where humanity’s survival hangs by a dangerous thread. Though the provided synopsis curiously misidentifies the plot—describing a near-future Exodus ship called Solomon’s Arrow rather than the actual Polity universe tale of Earth Central agent Ian Cormac investigating a sabotaged terraforming project on the distant planet Masada—the core spirit remains intact: Asher deals in uncompromising, hard-edged science fiction where grand ambitions inevitably collide with catastrophic threats. 

Set within Asher’s sprawling, AI-governed Polity universe, Gridlinked functions as a masterclass in modern space opera. The author’s signature style is defined by a relentless pace, visceral action, and a remarkably gritty approach to transhumanism. Unlike softer science fiction that romanticizes space exploration, Asher paints the future as a hyper-technological battleground. His worlds are governed by hyper-intelligent artificial intelligences, overrun by lethal alien artifacts, and populated by augmented humans whose minds are battlefields just as much as the physical terrain around them. 

The central theme of Gridlinked probes the friction between absolute control and human agency. Through the stoic and heavily cybernetically enhanced protagonist Ian Cormac—whose mind is severely "gridlinked" to artificial augmentations—Asher explores what is lost when humans sacrifice their emotional vulnerabilities for maximum operational efficiency. Cormac is not a traditional hero; he is a deadly instrument of state security, stripped of his humanity yet desperately relying on whatever fragments remain to survive the lethal politics of the Polity. 

Asher’s prose is unapologetically punchy, favoring tactical realism, brutal combat choreography, and intricate world-building over philosophical musings. He drops readers directly into a complex web of corporate greed, ancient alien tech, and interstellar espionage without holding their hands. The result is an immersive, breathless reading experience that rewards sci-fi purists who crave intricate gadgetry, ruthless alien fauna, and morally gray protagonists. 

Ultimately, Gridlinked stands as a cornerstone of modern hard SF and space opera. Neal Asher proves that the future of humanity in the stars will not be a peaceful utopia, but a wild, dangerous frontier where only the toughest survive. For fans of explosive action, complex cybernetics, and sprawling interstellar conspiracies, this novel is an absolute must-read.
