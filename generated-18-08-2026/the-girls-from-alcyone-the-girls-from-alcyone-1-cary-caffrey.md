# The Girls From Alcyone (The Girls from Alcyone #1)

**Author:** Cary Caffrey  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

*The Girls from Alcyone* opens with a pulse‑pounding short story that thrusts readers straight into the fray of interstellar commerce and survival. Sigrid Novak, a daring merchant‑pilot with a bounty the size of a planet hanging over her head, is tasked with securing life‑saving supplies for her besieged homeworld, New Alcyone. The Council for Trade and Finance hunts her across the void, turning every docking bay and asteroid field into a potential trap. Yet, as the title hints, the ever‑resourceful Merchantmen—loose‑knit traders who operate on the fringes of regulated space—offer Sigrid a lifeline, if she can navigate their morally gray world without losing herself in the process.

At its core, the novel explores the tension between necessity and ethics in a galaxy where trade is both a lifeline and a weapon. Cary Caffrey uses Sigrid’s single‑minded quest to ask larger questions: What does it mean to be loyal when the system that governs commerce is corrupt? How far will individuals go to protect their community when the odds are stacked against them? The theme of female resilience threads through the narrative, not as a superficial badge but as a lived reality—Sigrid’s competence, stubbornness, and moments of vulnerability paint a portrait of a woman who leads not because she seeks glory but because she has no other choice.

Caffrey’s style is lean and propulsive, echoing the kinetic energy of a star‑fighter dogfight. She dispenses with lengthy exposition, preferring to reveal world‑building through sharp dialogue and visceral action scenes. Descriptions are economical yet vivid: the hiss of a docking seal, the cold glitter of distant nebulae against a ship’s hull, the terse exchange of credits in a back‑alley bazaar. This economy of language keeps the narrative moving at a breakneck pace, making the short story feel like a sprint rather than a marathon, and leaves readers eager to see how Sigrid’s gambit unfolds in the longer sequel.

What distinguishes *The Girls from Alcyone* from other space‑opera entries is its grounding in the gritty realities of trade logistics. Rather than glorifying heroic battles alone, Caffrey lingers on the minutiae of cargo manifests, navigation hazards, and the political machinations that dictate who gets to profit—and who gets left behind. The result is a tale that feels both expansive in its galactic scope and intimate in its focus on one woman’s struggle to keep her people alive.

For readers who crave strong, flawed heroines, a universe where commerce is as dangerous as any battlefield, and prose that hits like a burst of thruster fire, this opening installment delivers a compelling promise. It sets the stage for a series that could redefine what it means to trade—and to survive—among the stars.
