# Space (Manifold, #2)

**Author:** Stephen Baxter  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2001

Step through the quantum gate and leave the familiar confines of humanity behind. In Space, the second installment of Stephen Baxter’s acclaimed Manifold series, the British master of hard science fiction delivers a breathtaking, vertigo-inducing epic that tests the absolute limits of human endurance, intelligence, and cosmic scale. Published in 2001, this novel solidifies Baxter’s reputation not merely as a storyteller, but as a visionary architect of deep-time speculative fiction who makes the universe feel terrifyingly vast and intensely intimate all at once.

At the narrative’s jagged edge is Reid Malenfant, an indomitable, driven astronaut whose insatiable curiosity acts as the engine for humanity’s outward expansion. When Malenfant ventures to the distant fringes of our solar system in the year 2020, he uncovers an enigmatic artifact—a quantum gateway left behind by an unfathomable alien civilization. This gateway promises instantaneous interstellar travel, opening a doorway to the dark unknown. But Baxter is no purveyor of cheap space opera; the leaps through the gate exact a psychological and physical toll, pushing Malenfant toward choices that transcend sanity, terror, and humanity itself. 

Parallel to Malenfant’s reckless stellar ambition is the grounded, yet equally apocalyptic, terrestrial narrative led by Japanese scientist Nemoto. While Malenfant chases the stars, Nemoto confronts a chilling archaeological reality on Earth, the Moon, Venus, and Mars. Her research reveals that life once flourished on these neighboring worlds, only to be systematically and repeatedly extinguished through catastrophic cycles of birth and destruction. Worse still, the data indicates that the next planetary cleansing is not a distant theoretical threat—it is set to begin again right now.

Baxter’s signature style is on full display here: uncompromising, intellectually rigorous, and relentlessly ambitious. True to the hard science fiction tradition, the physics and speculative technologies are meticulously rendered, giving the narrative a grounded authenticity even as it leaps into the realm of the cosmic and the bizarre. Yet, Baxter’s greatest strength has never been mere technical wizardry; it is his profound engagement with deep time and existential dread. He forces the reader to confront the terrifying insignificance of human history against the geological and cosmological eons of the universe. 

The central theme of Space revolves around the tension between human hubris and cosmic indifference. Malenfant represents the defiant spark of life refusing to go gently into the cosmic night, while Nemoto embodies the sobering realization of cyclical annihilation. Together, they form the dual pillars of a narrative that asks what it truly means to be a spacefaring species when the universe itself appears hostile to life’s persistence. 

For readers who crave science fiction that expands the mind, challenges conventional narrative boundaries, and dares to look into the abyss of deep space and deep time, Stephen Baxter has crafted an essential journey. Space is a dazzling, sobering, and utterly unforgettable voyage that proves when the travel bug bites and our home planets no longer suffice, Baxter is the only guide you need to brave the infinite.
