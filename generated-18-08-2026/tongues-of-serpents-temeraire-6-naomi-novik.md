# Tongues of Serpents (Temeraire, #6)

**Author:** Naomi Novik  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

Exile, empire, and the untamed expanse of the antipodes take center stage in Tongues of Serpents, the sixth installment of Naomi Novik’s celebrated alternative-history fantasy series. Following the dramatic events of Victory of Eagles, Captain Will Laurence and his formidable celestial dragon, Temeraire, find themselves stripped of their hard-won military honors and cast out to the far reaches of the British Empire. Convicted of treason for prioritizing the survival of humanity over strict naval protocol, the duo is banished to the penal colony of New South Wales in Australia. Yet, Novik proves once again that true adventure follows her protagonists no matter how far the crown attempts to push them from the map.

In this outing, Novik expertly pivots away from the sweeping, continental battlefields of Napoleonic Europe that defined the earlier novels, trading grand military campaigns for a more intimate, frontier-driven narrative. The thematic core of the novel hinges on the crushing weight of colonial ambition and the complex morality of empire-building. Australia is portrayed not merely as a place of punishment, but as a pressure cooker of political instability, brilliantly anchored by the historical upheaval of the real-life Rum Rebellion. When the infamous Captain William Bligh—late of HMS Bounty—attempts to drag Laurence and Temeraire into his desperate bid to reclaim power, Novik highlights the absurdities and rigid orthodoxies of human governance against the backdrop of majestic, indifferent wilderness. 

The author’s signature style shines brightest in her meticulous blending of historical detail with fantastical world-building. Novik maintains the charming, quasi-Regency prose style of her characters, capturing the formal cadence of Laurence's internal monologues alongside the earnest, intellectually curious voice of Temeraire. The dynamic between the human captain and his dragon remains the emotional anchor of the series, evolving gracefully as they navigate their shared disgrace. Their bond is tested not by cannon fire or aerial combat, but by the grueling realities of survival in a harsh new land.

To escape the petty political squabbles of the colony, Laurence and Temeraire embark on a perilous expedition to forge a path across the forbidding Blue Mountains into Australia’s unknown interior. What begins as a journey of geographical discovery quickly spirals into high-stakes suspense with the theft of a precious dragon egg. This narrative twist transforms the second half of the book into a breathless tracking operation that showcases Novik’s talent for pacing and environmental tension. The Australian bush comes alive under her pen as a character in its own right—ancient, alien, and teeming with unique dangers that dwarf the petty squabbles of European politics.

While some readers might find the scope of Tongues of Serpents more localized than the global chess matches of Empire of Ivory, this deliberate reduction in scale allows for deeper character introspection. Novik uses the isolation of the Australian continent to question what loyalty truly means when the nation one has sworn to protect responds with ingratitude and exile. The shocking discoveries made deep within the interior tease larger global implications, ensuring that even at the ends of the earth, Laurence and Temeraire remain pivotal players in a world at war.

Ultimately, Tongues of Serpents is a rewarding, atmospheric chapter in a beloved saga. It enriches the overarching mythos of the Temeraire universe while offering a standalone adventure rich in historical flavor, ecological wonder, and the enduring power of companionship. For fans of intelligent fantasy and meticulously researched alternate history, Novik’s latest journey is an expedition well worth taking.
