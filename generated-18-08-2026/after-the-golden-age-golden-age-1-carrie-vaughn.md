# After the Golden Age (Golden Age, #1)

**Author:** Carrie Vaughn  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

After the Golden Age invites readers to strap on their imaginations and voyage beyond the edges of familiar maps, echoing the age‑old urge to chase what lies just beyond sight. Edited by John Joseph Adams and featuring contributions from some of today’s most inventive voices in speculative fiction—including Carrie Vaughn—the anthology weaves together tales of forgotten islands, sunken cities, and mythic realms that shimmer between history and possibility. The central thread is unmistakable: the human compulsion to seek the unknown, whether that means plumbing the depths of the ocean, scaling forgotten peaks, or stepping through portals that promise worlds that might have been—or might still be.

Vaughn’s own entry exemplifies this theme with her trademark blend of lyrical precision and emotional immediacy. She opens with a vivid, almost tactile description of a landscape that feels both eerily familiar and utterly alien, grounding the fantastical in sensory detail that makes the impossible feel reachable. Her characters are not mere explorers; they are seekers haunted by personal histories, whose motivations—curiosity, loss, redemption—propel them forward into the unknown. Vaughn’s style shines in the way she balances crisp, economical prose with moments of lingering wonder, allowing readers to feel the weight of a discovery as much as its thrill. This approach mirrors the anthology’s broader aesthetic: each story respects the tradition of classic adventure yarns while injecting contemporary concerns—identity, ecology, the ethics of discovery—into the mix.

The collection succeeds because it never treats the “lost world” trope as a mere backdrop; instead, it treats each setting as a character that challenges and transforms those who enter it. From the steampunk‑tinged dirigible voyages of James L. Cambias to the haunting, myth‑resonant islands of Darcie Little Badger, the stories collectively map a terrain where science and superstition intersect, reminding readers that the edge of the map is as much a psychological frontier as a geographic one. The anthology’s pacing allows for both rapid, pulse‑pounding escapades and slower, meditative passages that linger on the aftermath of discovery—what we bring back, what we leave behind, and how the journey reshapes us.

In sum, After the Golden Age is a celebration of wonder that feels both timeless and urgently relevant. It invites readers to revisit the golden age of exploration not as a nostalgic relic but as a living impulse, urging us to keep asking what lies beyond—and why we dare to find out.
