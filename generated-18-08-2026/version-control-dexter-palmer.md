# Version Control

**Author:** Dexter Palmer  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

Version Control by Dexter Palmer is a thought-provoking and emotionally charged novel that delves into the intricacies of human relationships, grief, and the impact of technology on our lives. Set in a near-future world, the story revolves around Rebecca Wright, a woman who has slowly rebuilt her life after a personal tragedy, and her husband Philip, a brilliant but struggling physicist obsessed with his invention, the causality violation device.

Palmer's writing style is masterful, weaving together multiple narrative threads that seamlessly blend elements of science fiction, drama, and social commentary. The novel's central theme – the effects of science and technology on our friendships, love lives, and sense of self – is expertly explored through the lens of Rebecca and Philip's complex relationship.

One of the novel's greatest strengths lies in its ability to balance intellectual curiosity with emotional depth. Palmer's prose is both lyrical and accessible, making complex scientific concepts feel approachable and fascinating. The world he creates is eerily familiar, with self-driving cars, smartphones, and internet dating sites serving as a backdrop for the characters' struggles and triumphs.

Rebecca, the protagonist, is a richly drawn and relatable character, her inner world marked by a sense of disquiet and disorientation. Her experiences are both deeply personal and universally relatable, making her a compelling and empathetic protagonist. Philip, on the other hand, is a more enigmatic figure, his obsessive focus on his invention both captivating and heartbreaking.

Throughout the novel, Palmer raises important questions about the nature of reality, free will, and the consequences of playing with the fabric of time. The causality violation device, Philip's magnum opus, serves as a metaphor for the unpredictable nature of human relationships and the butterfly effect of our actions.

Palmer's vision of the near future is both unsettling and thought-provoking, inviting readers to reflect on the implications of emerging technologies on our lives and relationships. The novel's climax is both surprising and inevitable, a testament to Palmer's skill as a storyteller.

Ultimately, Version Control is a novel about the human condition, with all its complexities, contradictions, and fragilities. It is a powerful exploration of love, loss, and the search for meaning in a world that is increasingly shaped by technology. With its unique blend of science fiction, drama, and social commentary, this novel is sure to resonate with readers long after they finish reading it.
