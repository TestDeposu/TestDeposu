# A Rage for Revenge

**Author:** David Gerrold  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

In the realm of military science fiction, few authors have managed to craft a narrative as gripping and thought-provoking as David Gerrold's "A Rage for Revenge". The first installment of the Star Wolf trilogy, this 2009 novel is a masterclass in building tension, exploring the complexities of military character, and delving into the darker aspects of human nature.

At its core, "A Rage for Revenge" is a story about the unrelenting pursuit of survival and the all-consuming desire for revenge. Captain Jonathan Korie, the protagonist, is a man driven by the need to protect his crew and exact vengeance upon the Morthan threat that has decimated the human fleet. Gerrold expertly weaves together the threads of Korie's narrative, creating a rich tapestry of emotions, motivations, and conflicts that propel the story forward.

One of the standout aspects of Gerrold's writing style is his ability to balance action and introspection. The novel is replete with rapid-fire sequences of space battles, tactical maneuvers, and hand-to-hand combat, yet these are never at the expense of character development. Gerrold takes the time to explore the inner workings of his characters' minds, revealing their fears, doubts, and strengths in a way that feels authentic and relatable.

The Morthan crew, the antagonists of the story, are a fascinating creation. Their superhuman abilities and enigmatic nature make them a compelling foe, and Gerrold does an excellent job of conveying the sense of unease and uncertainty that they inspire in the human characters. The Morthans are not simply one-dimensional villains; they are a force of nature, driven by their own motivations and desires that are slowly revealed over the course of the novel.

Gerrold's prose is economical and direct, with a focus on conveying the emotional and psychological states of his characters. His writing is free of flowery language and unnecessary descriptions, instead opting for a lean, mean style that propels the narrative forward. This approach pays dividends in the novel's pacing, which is relentless and engaging from start to finish.

If there is one criticism to be made, it is that the novel's supporting cast is somewhat underdeveloped. While Korie is a fully fleshed-out character, some of the secondary characters feel more like sketches than fully realized people. However, this is a minor quibble in an otherwise excellent novel.

In conclusion, "A Rage for Revenge" is a gripping and thought-provoking novel that is sure to appeal to fans of military science fiction. Gerrold's writing style is engaging and immersive, and his exploration of the human condition is both nuanced and compelling. If you're looking for a novel that will keep you on the edge of your seat, look no further.
