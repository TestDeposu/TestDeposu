# A Madness of Angels (Matthew Swift, #1)

**Author:** Kate Griffin  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

A Madness of Angels, the debut urban fantasy novel by Kate Griffin, is a mesmerizing tale of magic, mystery, and revenge set against the vibrant backdrop of modern-day London. With its unique blend of dark humor, witty dialogue, and electrifying action sequences, this book is sure to captivate fans of Neil Gaiman's Neverwhere and leave them eagerly anticipating the next installment in the Matthew Swift series.

At the heart of the story is Matthew Swift, a sorcerer who defies death and returns to life two years after his gruesome murder. With no memory of his resurrection and a burning desire for vengeance, Swift embarks on a perilous journey to uncover the truth behind his death and the mysterious forces that brought him back. As he navigates the treacherous world of London's magical underworld, Swift must confront his own demons and team up with a motley crew of allies to take down his enemies and reclaim his place in the city.

One of the standout features of A Madness of Angels is Griffin's masterful portrayal of London as a living, breathing entity with its own unique magic. From the bustling streets of Camden to the historic landmarks of the city, Griffin's vivid descriptions bring the urban landscape to life in a way that's both authentic and enchanting. Her writing is infused with a deep love and knowledge of the city, making it easy to become fully immersed in Swift's world.

Griffin's prose is also noteworthy for its lush, poetic quality, which adds depth and richness to the narrative. Her dialogue is witty and engaging, with a keen ear for the rhythms and cadences of modern speech. The characters that populate the story are equally well-drawn, with Swift being a particularly compelling protagonist. His voice is distinctive and relatable, and his struggles to come to terms with his new existence are both poignant and humorous.

The world-building in A Madness of Angels is another highlight of the book. Griffin's vision of a magical London is both detailed and expansive, with a complex system of magic that's steeped in history and mythology. The author's use of magical realism adds a layer of authenticity to the story, making it easy to believe in the fantastical elements that drive the plot.

If there's a criticism to be made, it's that the pacing can be a bit uneven at times. Some sections of the book feel a bit rushed, while others are more leisurely in their development. However, this is a minor quibble in what's otherwise a gripping and engaging narrative.

Overall, A Madness of Angels is a fantastic debut from a talented new author. With its unique blend of magic, mystery, and humor, this book is sure to appeal to fans of urban fantasy and beyond. If you're looking for a compelling and immersive read with a strong sense of place and a memorable protagonist, then A Madness of Angels is definitely worth checking out.
