# Betrayer (Foreigner, #12)

**Author:** C.J. Cherryh  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1994

Betrayer, the 12th installment in C.J. Cherryh's iconic Foreigner series, is a gripping and thought-provoking novel that delves into the intricacies of interspecies politics and the consequences of first contact. Set nearly 200 years after the tumultuous events that unfolded on the alien planet of the atevi, this book masterfully weaves a complex narrative that explores the delicate balance of power, loyalty, and deception in a world where humans are vastly outnumbered and outmaneuvered.

One of the most striking aspects of Cherryh's writing style is her ability to craft a richly detailed and immersive world, where the intricacies of atevi culture and politics are meticulously woven into the fabric of the narrative. The atevi, with their 14 words for betrayal and not a single word for love, are a fascinating and enigmatic species, whose motivations and actions are as unpredictable as they are intriguing. Cherryh's masterful handling of this alien culture allows readers to become fully invested in the world of the Foreigner series, where the lines between loyalty, duty, and deception are constantly blurred.

At the heart of Betrayer is the story of a lone human diplomat, who finds himself embroiled in a deadly game of cat and mouse, where the stakes are not just personal, but also interplanetary. As the sole human allowed into atevi society, this character must navigate a treacherous landscape of alliances, rivalries, and ancient grudges, where the slightest misstep could have disastrous consequences. Cherryh's handling of this character's plight is both suspenseful and thought-provoking, raising important questions about the nature of loyalty, duty, and the true cost of peaceful coexistence.

Throughout the book, Cherryh's prose is as elegant as it is economical, conveying a wealth of information and emotion through a deceptively simple writing style. Her characters are multidimensional and complex, with motivations that are both nuanced and believable. The pacing is expertly handled, with a narrative that unfolds at a measured and deliberate pace, building towards a climax that is both shocking and inevitable.

One of the most impressive aspects of Betrayer is its ability to balance action, suspense, and introspection, creating a narrative that is both intellectually stimulating and viscerally engaging. Cherryh's exploration of the consequences of first contact is both timely and timeless, raising important questions about the nature of power, loyalty, and the true cost of peaceful coexistence. As a work of science fiction, Betrayer is a triumph, offering a compelling and thought-provoking vision of a possible future, where humanity must navigate the complexities of interspecies politics in order to survive.

In conclusion, Betrayer is a gripping and thought-provoking novel that is sure to appeal to fans of science fiction and anyone interested in exploring the intricacies of interspecies politics and the consequences of first contact. With its richly detailed world-building, complex characters, and suspenseful narrative, this book is a must-read for anyone looking for a compelling and intellectually stimulating science fiction experience.
