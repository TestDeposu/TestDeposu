# Waldo and Magic, Inc

**Author:** Robert A. Heinlein  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

Robert A. Heinlein remains one of the foundational architects of modern science fiction, a writer whose ability to extrapolate social, technological, and economic systems into wildly entertaining narratives is second to none. In this brilliant compilation featuring two of his classic novellas, Waldo and Magic, Inc., Heinlein showcases the dazzling range of his imagination. While ostensibly dealing with two vastly different realms—the hard-edged, zero-gravity orbital mechanics of the future and a bustling contemporary America where magic is treated as a regulated, corporate utility—both stories share a singular, unifying thematic heartbeat: the friction between individual capability, systemic bureaucracy, and the relentless march of human enterprise.

The first half of the volume, Waldo, introduces us to one of science fiction’s most delightfully eccentric protagonists: Waldo F. Jones. A brilliant, misanthropic genius crippled by a degenerative muscular disease, Waldo lives in solitary splendor in a free-fall orbital home, surrounded by the mechanical waldoes—remote-manipulator arms—he invented. When the power grid and aviation industry on Earth begin failing mysteriously, North Power-Air is forced to humble itself before Waldo's misanthropy. Heinlein uses Waldo's journey not merely as a high-stakes engineering mystery, but as a profound exploration of human adaptability, disability, and psychology. The author’s characteristic style shines here through rigorous extrapolation. Heinlein never just invents a gadget; he builds the economic, physiological, and philosophical framework around it. Waldo is arrogant, cynical, and fascinating, and watching him untangle the cosmic riddle of inertia and energy—while inadvertently healing his own relationship with humanity—is a masterclass in classic speculative fiction pacing.

If Waldo represents Heinlein playing in the sandbox of hard, theoretical futures, Magic, Inc. gleefully somersaults into the absurdly practical. Imagine a world where witchcraft, voodoo, and demonology are not hidden occult arts, but licensed, taxed, and monopolized industries overseen by trade associations and regulatory boards. Into this brilliantly realized bureaucratic nightmare steps Archie Fraser, a businessman who refuses to be squeezed out by a shadowy cartel of corporate magicians. Teaming up with an Oxford-educated African shaman and a sweet old lady who happens to be a master of black magic, Fraser takes the fight all the way to the boardrooms of Hell itself. 

Despite the fantastical premise, Magic, Inc. bears all the unmistakable hallmarks of Heinlein’s signature prose: sharp, pragmatic dialogue, a deep-seated suspicion of monopolies and governmental overreach, and an insistence that even the supernatural must operate by a set of rules, costs, and market forces. Heinlein treats the invocation of demons and the casting of hexes with the dry, matter-of-fact professionalism of a corporate merger. This juxtaposition of the mundane and the mystical is both hilarious and intellectually stimulating, demonstrating Heinlein’s peerless gift for world-building.

Across both novellas, the central theme is the triumph of human agency over systemic decay. Whether it is a misanthropic genius overcoming the physical tyranny of gravity or a stubborn businessman refusing to let a magical cartel dictate his livelihood, Heinlein champions the rugged individualist who asks the hard questions and refuses to accept that things simply cannot be fixed. Enhanced by a fresh, insightful introduction by Tim Powers, this collection is a testament to why Heinlein’s work endures. It is witty, intellectually rigorous, and relentlessly inventive—essential reading for both longtime devotees of the Golden Age of science fiction and newcomers eager to witness a master craftsman at the peak of his powers.
