# For Love of Mother-Not (Pip & Flinx, #5)

**Author:** Alan Dean Foster  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1983

Alan Dean Foster’s 1983 novel *For Love of Mother-Not* occupies a fascinating space in science fiction history. Serving as the chronological starting point for the enduring Pip and Flinx series, this installment strips away the sprawling cosmic bureaucracy of modern space operas to deliver an intimate, planetary-scale adventure. While technically the fifth book published in the wider sequence, it acts as the vital genesis story for one of speculative fiction’s most endearing duos, blending the gritty realism of frontier survival with the wondrous awe of classic planetary romance.

At the heart of the narrative lies its most compelling theme: the ferocious, unbreakable bond of chosen family. Flinx begins his journey as property—a freckle-faced, emerald-eyed urchin sold for a paltry hundred credits on a desolate auction block. Yet, his rescue by the formidable Mother Mastiff transforms his existence from one of commodified abuse to one rooted in fierce, unconditional love. When Mastiff is mysteriously abducted, the emotional core of the novel snaps into sharp focus. Flinx’s subsequent quest across the treacherous terrain of the winged world, Moth, is not merely a generic rescue mission; it is a primal desperate fight to preserve the only home, stability, and maternal affection he has ever known. Foster examines how love anchors the human spirit, preventing a uniquely gifted and isolated boy from drifting into bitterness or exploitation.

Foster’s signature literary style shines brightly throughout the journey. Known for his vivid world-building and accessible, engaging prose, the author transforms the planet Moth into a living, breathing character. The ecosystem is richly textured, teeming with bizarre flora and lethal fauna that demand constant vigilance. Rather than relying on cold, sterile hard-science exposition, Foster immerses the reader in sensory details—the humid press of the swamps, the oppressive density of the alien forests, and the vibrant, dangerous beauty of the skies. This environmental hostility acts as a crucible, testing both Flinx’s developing empathic talents and the deadly, acidic-tailed loyalty of Pip, his miniature flying dragon. 

What makes the book particularly striking is how Foster balances high-stakes peril with a distinct touch of whimsy. Flinx is no polished galactic hero yet; he is vulnerable, occasionally frightened, and profoundly human, even as his strange psionic powers begin to manifest. Pip provides a sharp, instinctual counterpoint, representing nature red in tooth and claw yet fiercely protective. Their dynamic lays the groundwork for a partnership that would captivate readers for decades.

In summary, *For Love of Mother-Not* is a masterclass in introductory character-building and atmospheric adventure. It avoids the trap of heavy-handed exposition by grounding its lore in raw, emotional urgency. For longtime fans of the Humanx Commonwealth universe, it is a treasured foundation stone; for newcomers, it is a thrilling, warmly human entry point into a vast universe. Foster successfully proves that amidst the cold, infinite reaches of space, the most powerful force in the galaxy remains the fierce devotion between a boy and his mother.
