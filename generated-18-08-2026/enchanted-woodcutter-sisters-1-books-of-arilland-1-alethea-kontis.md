# Enchanted (Woodcutter Sisters, #1; Books of Arilland, #1)

**Author:** Alethea Kontis  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

Step into a realm where the ticking of a clock is not merely a measure of seconds, but the very architectural spine of reality. In Enchanted, the first brilliant installment of the Woodcutter Sisters series and the broader Books of Arilland, acclaimed author Alethea Kontis crafts a breathtaking tapestry that redefines the boundaries of fairytale fantasy. While fairy-tale retellings are hardly a novelty in modern literature, Kontis elevates the genre through a stunning display of imaginative world-building, seamlessly weaving classic folklore motifs with high-concept speculative mechanics.

At the heart of Kontis’s narrative lies a captivating alternate history born from a single, catastrophic deviation in time: the success of Guy Fawkes's Gunpowder Plot on November 5, 1605. This pivotal explosion tore the British Prime Universe asunder, fracturing reality into a multidimensional trinity. There is the clockwise universe, moving forward through history as we know it, and the widdershins universe, spinning relentlessly backward into the past. Between these opposing temporal currents lies a fragile, permeable boundary. On All Hallows Eve, when the veil between these planes thins to a whisper, souls can cross the great divide. 

It is across this impossible temporal chasm that the central thematic exploration of the novel takes root. We follow Edith Hornby, anchored in the forward-moving clockwise plane, and Edward Moriarty, existing in the counterclockwise flow of widdershins. Their recurring encounters—defying the very laws of physics and chronology—form an emotional anchor for the novel. Kontis uses their unconventional romance to explore the overarching theme of connection transcending circumstance. Fate in Arilland is not a passive script handed down by tradition; it is a magnetic force, an enduring bond that proves stronger than the vast, ticking machinery of the cosmos itself. 

Kontis’s writing style is both lyrical and sharply pragmatic, perfectly balancing the whimsical wonder of traditional folklore with the gritty stakes of speculative fiction. She possesses a rare literary alchemy: the ability to make talking animals, magical curses, and dimensional rifts feel entirely grounded and emotionally resonant. Her prose dances effortlessly between moments of whimsical domesticity within the bustling Woodcutter household and the dizzying, philosophical vertigo of parallel universes. The author treats the reader with immense respect, avoiding simplistic tropes in favor of layered character development and intricate plotting that rewards close attention.

Enchanted is more than just a clever reimagining of classic Grimm fairy tales; it is a meditation on time, destiny, and the invisible threads that tether us to one another across impossible distances. Alethea Kontis has constructed a universe that feels simultaneously ancient and startlingly original, ensuring that the magic of Arilland lingers long after the final page is turned. For readers hungry for a fairytale that possesses both a beating heart and a brilliantly complex mind, this novel is an absolute triumph.
