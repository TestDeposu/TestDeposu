# Consider (Holo, #1)

**Author:** Kristy Acevedo  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

Senior year is hard enough with college applications and social pressures, but throw in an impending apocalypse, and you have the high-stakes reality of Kristy Acevedo’s compelling dystopian novel, Consider. Originally launched as part of a celebrated sci-fi trajectory that earned critical acclaim including a Philip K. Dick Award finalist nod for its predecessor, this installment plunges readers headfirst into a world redefined by deceitful technology and fierce resilience.

At the heart of the narrative is Alexandra Lucas, a protagonist who immediately shatters the mold of the stoic, invincible YA action hero. Acevedo deserves immense praise for her authentic portrayal of a lead character who struggles with an anxiety disorder. In a genre frequently saturated with polished perfection, Alex’s humanizing frailty offers a refreshing anchor. Her vulnerability does not weaken her; rather, it amplifies her strength, infusing her journey with a raw, relatable grit that resonates deeply long after the final page. 

The central premise hinges on a chilling deception: mysterious holograms arrived promising safe passage through their vertexes, but the holograms lied. Step into this dazzling new world, and you are greeted by an immersive technocratic utopia where clothes and surroundings can be customized with a mere thought. Yet, Acevedo skillfully juxtaposes this hyper-advanced aesthetic with a suffocating undercurrent of paranoia and control. Beneath the glossy exterior of customizable perfection, a rebellion is brewing. Alex’s quest to find her missing boyfriend, best friend, and brother transforms from a personal rescue mission into a dangerous crusade against a system built on a foundation of lies. 

Acevedo’s writing style is defined by its brisk momentum. She crafts a fast-paced narrative that acts as an absolute mind-bender, constantly forcing readers to question the boundaries between safety and subjugation. The pacing rarely lets up, propelling the audience through a maze of high-tech marvels and low-tech betrayals. Yet, amidst the breathless momentum, the author never loses sight of the emotional core. The exploration of trust, betrayal, and the heavy toll of survival elevates the book from a standard sci-fi adventure into a thoughtful commentary on the price of utopia. Every choice carries weight, and every technological luxury comes with a hidden cost.

Consider is ultimately a story about coming back stronger when the systems you trusted have failed you. It speaks directly to anyone who has ever felt betrayed by the promises of the world around them, turning internal anxiety into external empowerment. Acevedo weaves a captivating tapestry of suspense, heart, and high-concept science fiction that will leave readers compulsively turning pages, desperate to see how much humanity can endure before the breaking point is reached.
