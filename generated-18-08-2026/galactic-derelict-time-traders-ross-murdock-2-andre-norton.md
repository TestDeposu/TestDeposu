# Galactic Derelict (Time Traders/Ross Murdock, #2)

**Author:** Andre Norton  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

In the realm of science fiction, few authors have managed to weave a narrative as intricate and captivating as Andre Norton's Galactic Derelict. Originally published in 1959, this sequel to Time Traders has been re-released in 2019, offering a new generation of readers a chance to delve into the thrilling world of time travel and interstellar exploration.

Set against the backdrop of the Cold War, Galactic Derelict masterfully combines elements of espionage, adventure, and science fiction to create a story that is both thought-provoking and action-packed. At its core, the novel explores the theme of discovery and the consequences of playing with the fabric of time.

The story follows Travis Fox, a young Apache man who joins forces with Ross Murdock and Dr. Gordon Ashe, two seasoned time agents, on a perilous mission to retrieve an abandoned alien ship from the distant past. As they attempt to transfer the ship through 20,000 years to the present, the team must navigate a complex web of time paradoxes and cultural clashes.

Norton's writing style is a true testament to her skill as a storyteller. Her prose is concise, yet evocative, conjuring vivid images of the characters and their surroundings. The pacing is relentless, hurtling the reader through a maze of twists and turns that will keep them on the edge of their seat.

One of the most striking aspects of Galactic Derelict is its exploration of the cultural divide between the past and the present. Norton's use of Travis Fox, an Apache protagonist, adds a unique perspective to the narrative, highlighting the tensions between traditional ways of life and the encroaching modern world.

The character development in the novel is also noteworthy, with each member of the team bringing their own distinct personality to the table. Ross Murdock, the seasoned time agent, is a perfect foil to Travis Fox's more impulsive nature, while Dr. Gordon Ashe provides a stabilizing influence on the group.

If there is one criticism to be made, it is that the novel's supporting cast is somewhat underdeveloped. The villains, in particular, feel somewhat one-dimensional, serving primarily as a catalyst for the plot's progression.

Despite this minor quibble, Galactic Derelict remains a gripping and thought-provoking read. Norton's masterful handling of the time travel genre is on full display, and her ability to craft a compelling narrative is undeniable.

In conclusion, Galactic Derelict is a must-read for fans of science fiction and adventure. With its breakneck pacing, memorable characters, and thought-provoking themes, this novel is sure to captivate readers of all ages.
