# Small Great Things

**Author:** Jodi Picoult  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

**Small Great Things: A Powerful Exploration of Race and Privilege**

In her latest novel, Jodi Picoult masterfully tackles the complex and timely issue of race and privilege in *Small Great Things*. Picoult, known for her compelling narratives that delve into societal issues, has crafted a story that is as thought-provoking as it is engaging.

The plot revolves around Ruth Jefferson, a seasoned African American labor and delivery nurse, who finds herself in a predicament when she is reassigned from caring for a newborn because the parents, white supremacists, don't want a Black nurse touching their child. The next day, the baby goes into distress, and Ruth's hesitation to intervene due to her reassignment leads to a criminal charge. The story unfolds as Ruth's public defender, Kennedy McQuarrie, a white woman, navigates the case, advising Ruth to avoid discussing race in the courtroom.

Picoult's strength lies in her ability to create well-rounded, believable characters that readers can invest in. Ruth, Kennedy, and even the baby's parents are not merely stereotypes but fully realized individuals with their own struggles and growth. Picoult's exploration of their perspectives is nuanced and unflinching, challenging readers to confront their own biases.

The narrative is a gripping courtroom drama, but it's the exploration of race and privilege that truly resonates. Picoult doesn't shy away from difficult conversations, instead, she delves into them with a deft hand, weaving a tale that is as much about the legal system as it is about the personal growth of its characters.

Picoult's signature style—interspersing chapters with different perspectives—works exceptionally well here. It allows readers to understand the motivations and thought processes of each character, fostering empathy and understanding. The story is a testament to Picoult's ability to weave compelling narratives that challenge societal norms and prompt introspection.

*Small Great Things* is not an easy read, but it's a necessary one. Picoult doesn't provide easy answers, instead, she poses questions that linger long after the final page. This is a novel that will spark conversation, challenge perceptions, and leave a lasting impact.
