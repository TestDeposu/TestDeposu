# Misguided Angel (Blue Bloods, #5)

**Author:** Melissa de la Cruz  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

Gilded Cages and Fallen Heavens: A Review of Melissa de la Cruz’s Misguided Angel

In the upper-crust echelons of New York City’s immortal elite, the stakes have never been higher, and the blood has never run colder. Melissa de la Cruz returns with Misguided Angel, the fifth installment in her runaway bestselling Blue Bloods series, plunging her characters deeper into a world where high fashion collides with ancient theology. Following the tumultuous events that left the Van Alen Legacy in Schuyler’s unwilling hands, this novel shifts the coordinates of the saga from the familiar cobblestones of Manhattan to the romantic, shadowed piazzas of Florence, expanding the mythos while tightening the narrative noose around its star-crossed protagonists.

The central theme of Misguided Angel is the heavy, often crushing burden of destiny versus the intoxicating pull of personal desire. De la Cruz explores how ancient bloodlines and hereditary duty demand the absolute subjugation of the individual self. Schuyler Van Alen, once just a half-blood outsider trying to fit in at Duchesne Academy, is now cast in the tragic mold of a reluctant messiah. Her flight to Italy with her forbidden love, Jack Force, is not merely an escape from the prying eyes of the Blue Blood enclave, but a desperate attempt to steal a few moments of normalcy before the apocalypse claims them. Yet, the novel demonstrates that running from fate is a fool's errand. As Schuyler and Jack embark on their sacred mission to secure the seven gates guarding Earth from Lucifer, the narrative asks a poignant question: how much of one's soul must be sacrificed to save the world? 

De la Cruz’s signature style remains a potent cocktail of contemporary pop-culture gloss and sweeping, gothic fantasy. She writes with an effortless, breathless pacing that treats Prada bags and angelic warfare with the exact same level of breathless reverence. This juxtaposition of high-society materialism and apocalyptic stakes is the author's greatest stylistic triumph. She constructs a universe where centuries-old vampires sip espresso in Italian cafes, wear haute couture, and fret over teenage angst, yet remain bound by the eternal, terrifying machinery of heaven and hell. The prose is sleek, fast-paced, and effortlessly readable, designed to sweep the audience along on a torrential current of romance and danger. 

However, the real strength of Misguided Angel lies in its willingness to complicate its own mythology. The Blue Blood enclave is fracturing, its foundation rotting from the inside out, mirroring the internal decay of its members. De la Cruz masterfully ramps up the psychological pressure on Schuyler, forcing her toward a terrifying crossroads. The romance between Schuyler and Jack is tested not just by external monsters, but by the very nature of their celestial heritage. It is a love story forged in fire and forbidden by law, and de la Cruz milks the angst for all it is worth, delivering the kind of electric, yearning tension that YA fantasy readers crave.

While some critics might argue that the series leans too heavily into its glossy, supernatural soap-opera tropes, Misguided Angel proves that there is genuine narrative muscle beneath the designer labels. De la Cruz successfully raises the emotional and cosmic stakes, transforming what could have been a standard monster-of-the-week formula into a sweeping epic about sacrifice, betrayal, and the heavy cost of redemption. 

For fans who have followed Schuyler from her days as a lonely outcast to her current status as the linchpin of vampiric survival, this installment offers a thrilling, emotionally resonant ride. It is a stylish, fast-moving descent into the shadows of paradise, proving once again that in Melissa de la Cruz’s universe, the most dangerous thing you can fall for is eternity.
