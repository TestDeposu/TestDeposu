# Winger (Winger, #1)

**Author:** Andrew Smith  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

"Winger" by Andrew Smith is a raw, honest, and often hilarious exploration of adolescence, wrapped in the unlikely package of a boarding school rugby novel. Smith's protagonist, Ryan Dean West, is a 14-year-old navigating the treacherous waters of puberty, friendship, and first love with a wit and wisdom beyond his years. The story is as much about Ryan Dean's internal growth as it is about his external journey, and Smith masterfully balances the comedic and the profound, creating a narrative that is alternately laugh-out-loud funny and painfully relatable.

Smith's writing style is conversational and engaging, drawing the reader in with its immediacy and authenticity. He has a knack for capturing the essence of teenage boy-speak, with its mix of bravado, insecurity, and deadpan humor. The novel is peppered with Ryan Dean's doodles and infographics, which not only add visual interest but also serve to illustrate his thought processes and provide insight into his worldview.

At its heart, "Winger" is a story about resilience and the power of friendship. Ryan Dean's life is far from easy, but he faces his challenges with a resilience that is inspiring. His relationships with his fellow rugby players, in particular, are beautifully drawn, showcasing the complex dynamics of male friendship and the importance of found family. Smith doesn't shy away from the darker aspects of teenage life, including bullying and sexual assault, but he handles these topics with sensitivity and care, using them to deepen his characters and drive the plot.

"Winger" is a standout novel that will appeal to readers who enjoy character-driven stories with a healthy dose of humor and heart. It's a book that will make you laugh, make you think, and maybe even make you cry. It's a book that will stay with you long after you've turned the last page.
