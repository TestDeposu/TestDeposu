# The Once and Future Witches

**Author:** Alix E. Harrow  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

**A Haunting Reimagining of History and Sisterhood: A Review of "The Once and Future Witches" by Alix E. Harrow**

In the late 19th century, women are fighting for the right to vote, and yet, the forces of oppression seem insurmountable. In this powerful and enchanting novel, Alix E. Harrow explores the intersection of magic, feminism, and family through the lives of three sisters: Juniper, Agnes, and Beatrice Eastwood. With its sweeping narrative, evocative prose, and feminist themes, "The Once and Future Witches" is a captivating tale that will leave readers spellbound and inspired.

Harrow's writing is a masterclass in atmosphere and world-building. She transports us to a world where witchcraft has been erased from history, reduced to mere nursery rhymes and harmless charms. Yet, in the shadows, the Eastwood sisters stir, their wild hair and hearts whispering secrets of a long-forgotten past. As they navigate the treacherous landscape of New Salem, where the suffragists are fighting for the right to vote, the sisters must confront the darkness that has haunted their family for generations.

The author's style is a deliberate blend of historical fiction, fantasy, and folk magic. Harrow's use of language is evocative and poetic, conjuring images of old-growth forests, crumbling stone buildings, and moonlit nights. Her characters are multidimensional, their complexities woven into the fabric of the story like a rich tapestry. The Eastwood sisters are a joy to behold – their differences and rivalries making them all the more believable and relatable. Juniper's fiery spirit, Agnes's gentle wisdom, and Beatrice's mystical presence are expertly woven into the narrative, creating a sense of family that is both intimate and epic.

One of the most striking aspects of "The Once and Future Witches" is its feminist core. Harrow's exploration of the women's suffrage movement is both nuanced and unapologetic. The sisters' journey is not just about seeking power and equality but also about confronting the traumas of their past and the violence that has been inflicted upon them. Through the Eastwood sisters, Harrow highlights the resilience and strength of women, their capacity to love and support one another, and their unyielding determination to shape their own destinies.

As the story unfolds, we see the Eastwood sisters delve into the forgotten words and ways of their ancestors. They begin to reclaim their family's history, drawing upon the oldest magics and forging new alliances with other women. This process is both exhilarating and terrifying, as they confront the shadows that have haunted their family for generations. Harrow's portrayal of the sisters' struggles to heal and reconcile is heart-wrenching and deeply moving.

In "The Once and Future Witches," Alix E. Harrow has given us a novel that is both an homage to the indomitable power of women and a powerful exploration of the intersections between history, magic, and family. This book is a testament to the enduring strength of women's voices, their capacity to resist and to imagine a different future. As the author herself reminds us, "There's no such thing as witches. But there will be." Harrow's words are a call to action, a reminder that our collective past is not fixed but rather a living, breathing entity that can be transformed and rewritten through the power of our imaginations and collective efforts.
