# Last Vote (Emma Last FBI Mystery #10)

**Author:** Mary Stone  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

Last Vote, the tenth installment in the Emma Last FBI Mystery series by Mary Stone, is a gripping and thought-provoking thriller that delves into the darker corners of terrorism, social change, and the complexities of the human psyche. The story follows the emergence of a militant group of anarchists, known as EMMA, who seek to force the power elite to listen to their demands through acts of violence against CEOs of major corporations responsible for crimes against humanity.

At the heart of the novel is the character of Brent Cossack, a former black ops officer turned terrorist, whose transformation from a young and idealistic soldier to a radicalized militant is both fascinating and unsettling. Stone masterfully weaves together the threads of Cossack's backstory, revealing the ugly truth he discovered during his time as a CIA operative in Iraq, and the personal tragedy that ultimately pushed him over the edge. This nuanced portrayal of a complex character raises important questions about the nature of terrorism, the consequences of trauma, and the blurred lines between right and wrong.

The investigation into EMMA's activities is led by FBI agent Rick Clark, a character whose personal struggles and emotional baggage are expertly intertwined with the plot. Clark's reliving of the saddest time in his life, coupled with his growing connection with psychologist Marty Robins, adds a layer of depth and emotional resonance to the story. The supporting cast of characters, including the enigmatic and beautiful political activist who captures Cossack's heart, are equally well-crafted, and their motivations and actions drive the plot forward with relentless pace.

One of the standout features of Stone's writing style is her ability to balance action, suspense, and character development with ease. The twists and turns of the plot are expertly handled, keeping the reader on the edge of their seat as the story unfolds. The author's use of language is concise and evocative, painting a vivid picture of the world she has created. The themes of social change, terrorism, and personal redemption are timely and thought-provoking, and Stone tackles them with sensitivity and nuance.

What sets Last Vote apart from other thrillers in the genre is its thoughtful exploration of the human condition. Stone is not content to simply present a simplistic narrative of good vs. evil; instead, she delves into the complexities and motivations of her characters, revealing the gray areas that exist between. This approach makes for a more satisfying and engaging read, as the reader is forced to confront their own assumptions and biases.

In conclusion, Last Vote is a gripping and thought-provoking thriller that will appeal to fans of the genre. With its complex characters, nuanced plotting, and timely themes, it is a must-read for anyone looking for a story that will keep them on the edge of their seat. Mary Stone's masterful writing style and expert handling of the plot make for a compelling narrative that will stay with the reader long after the final page is turned.
