# The New Drawing on the Right Side of the Brain

**Author:** Betty Edwards  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

In "The New Drawing on the Right Side of the Brain," renowned author and artist Betty Edwards presents a revised edition of her classic bestselling book, a comprehensive guide to unlocking the secrets of drawing and harnessing the creative potential of the right hemisphere of the brain. First published in 1979, this iconic book has been translated into more than seventeen languages and has become the world's most widely used instructional drawing book.

Edwards' approach is rooted in the idea that anyone can learn to draw, regardless of their artistic background or experience. She argues that the key to unlocking one's creative potential lies in understanding the distinct functions of the left and right hemispheres of the brain. The left hemisphere is responsible for logical, analytical thinking, while the right hemisphere is the seat of creativity, intuition, and visual perception. By tapping into the right hemisphere, artists can overcome self-doubt, fear, and inhibition, and unlock their full creative potential.

The book is divided into several sections, each addressing a specific aspect of the drawing process. Edwards begins by introducing the concept of "seeing," which involves training the eye to observe the world in a more nuanced and detailed way. She provides a series of exercises and techniques to help artists develop their observational skills, including contour drawing, gesture drawing, and perspective.

One of the most significant updates in this revised edition is the inclusion of recent research on the brain's plasticity and the importance of learning new skills. Edwards draws on the latest findings in neuroscience to demonstrate how the brain can be retrained and rewired, allowing artists to overcome creative blocks and develop new skills. This research is particularly relevant in today's digital age, where the emphasis on linear, analytical thinking can stifle creativity and imagination.

Throughout the book, Edwards emphasizes the importance of practice and patience. She provides a range of exercises and projects to help artists develop their skills, from simple gesture drawings to more complex compositions. Her approach is encouraging and supportive, and she offers practical advice on how to overcome common obstacles and challenges.

The book also includes a fascinating section on the link between early childhood "scribbling" and later language development. Edwards draws on recent research to demonstrate how encouraging children to engage in creative activities can have a profound impact on their cognitive development and future success.

The reproductions of master drawings throughout the book are a highlight, providing inspiration and guidance for artists at all levels. Edwards' selection of drawings is eclectic and diverse, ranging from the works of Old Masters to contemporary artists.

In conclusion, "The New Drawing on the Right Side of the Brain" is a comprehensive and inspiring guide to drawing and creativity. Betty Edwards' approach is engaging, accessible, and empowering, making this book an essential resource for artists, educators, and anyone looking to tap into their creative potential.
