# Okay for Now

**Author:** Gary D. Schmidt  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

In the poignant and powerful novel "Okay for Now" by Gary D. Schmidt, we are introduced to Doug Swieteck, a fourteen-year-old boy who has been written off by everyone in his new town. With an abusive father, a delinquent older brother, and a reputation that precedes him, Doug's future seems bleak. However, through an unlikely friendship with Lil Spicer, the fiery daughter of the local deli owner, Doug begins to redraw his own future and find a way to be okay.

Schmidt masterfully weaves multiple themes of loss and recovery throughout the narrative, creating a story that is both heartbreaking and uplifting. The author's writing style is a perfect blend of humor and tragedy, making the characters feel incredibly real and relatable. The dialogue is witty and engaging, and the characters' interactions are authentic and nuanced.

One of the most striking aspects of the novel is its exploration of the power of art to transform and redeem. Through Doug's discovery of John James Audubon's bird illustrations, Schmidt highlights the impact that creativity can have on our lives. The beautiful and intricate plates become a source of comfort and inspiration for Doug, allowing him to express himself and find solace in a world that often seems hostile.

The novel is also notable for its portrayal of small-town life during the Vietnam War era. Schmidt captures the challenges and uncertainties of this time period, and the ways in which it affects the characters and their community. The return of Doug's older brother from Vietnam, forever changed by his experiences, serves as a powerful reminder of the devastating impact of war on individuals and families.

Throughout the novel, Schmidt's characters are multidimensional and complex, defying easy categorization. Doug, in particular, is a remarkable protagonist, whose struggles and triumphs are deeply moving. Lil Spicer, too, is a standout character, whose fiery personality and kindness serve as a perfect foil to Doug's more introverted nature.

In "Okay for Now," Schmidt poses a profound question: how does a kid that everyone has written off find a way to redraw his own future? The answer, as the novel so eloquently shows, lies in the power of friendship, creativity, and resilience. Through Doug's story, Schmidt reminds us that even in the darkest of times, there is always hope for redemption and transformation.

Ultimately, "Okay for Now" is a stunning novel that will resonate with readers of all ages. Its themes of loss and recovery, its exploration of the power of art, and its portrayal of small-town life during the Vietnam War era make it a truly unforgettable read.
