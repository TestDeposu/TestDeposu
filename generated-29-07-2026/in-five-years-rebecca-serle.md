# In Five Years

**Author:** Rebecca Serle  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

Where do you see yourself in five years? It is the quintessential interview question, a polite inquiry into ambition that usually invites a rehearsed, safe response. But in Rebecca Serle’s 2020 New York Times bestseller, In Five Years, that innocent question morphs into an existential lightning bolt. Serle has crafted a deceptively breezy contemporary novel that quickly sheds its romantic-comedy veneer to reveal a poignant, razor-sharp meditation on grief, destiny, and the profound depth of female friendship.

The story centers on Dannie Kohan, a woman who lives by the spreadsheet. She is a powerhouse New York City corporate lawyer who measures success in precision and timelines. Her life is neatly compartmentalized, perfectly balanced by her free-spirited, chaotic best friend, Bella. When Dannie successfully nails the biggest job interview of her career and immediately accepts a thoroughly predictable, secure marriage proposal from her longtime boyfriend, David, it seems her meticulous planning has yielded perfection. She falls asleep utterly content.

Then, she wakes up. 

Suddenly, Dannie is not in her own bed. She is in a mysterious, unfamiliar apartment, wearing a dazzling new engagement ring, staring at a calendar that reads December 15, 2025—exactly five years into the future. And most shockingly, she is in bed with a strange, intense man who is decidedly not David. The hour she spends in this alternate timeline is visceral, confusing, and terrifyingly real before she snaps back to her present reality on the brink of midnight.

What follows is not a frantic, predictable race to change the future, but rather a slow-burn, emotional reckoning. Serle’s authorial style is the true engine of the novel. Her prose is remarkably clean, accessible, and fast-paced, engineered in a way that practically demands a single-sitting read. Yet beneath the readable exterior lies a sophisticated emotional architecture. Serle refuses to indulge in cheap narrative tricks. Instead of leaning into a conventional romantic triangle, the author subverts expectations entirely, shifting the emotional center of gravity away from romance and squarely onto the unbreakable, complicated bond between Dannie and Bella. 

The central theme of In Five Years explores the collision between destiny and free will. Dannie is a woman who believes control is the ultimate safeguard against pain. Through the uncanny intrusion of her future self, Serle forces Dannie—and the reader—to confront the uncomfortable truth that life cannot be entirely managed. Tragedy strikes in ways we never map out, and love manifests in configurations we never saw coming. 

Comparisons to Me Before You and One Day are entirely warranted, but Serle carves out her own distinct territory. She is not afraid to break the reader’s heart, navigating themes of mortality and profound loss with a tenderness that sneaks up on you. By the time the final pages arrive, the vision in the prologue takes on a heartbreaking, beautiful clarity that reframes the entire narrative. 

In Five Years is clever, moving, and quietly devastating. It asks us to look at our carefully curated plans and wonder: what if the best parts of our lives are the ones we never planned for at all?
