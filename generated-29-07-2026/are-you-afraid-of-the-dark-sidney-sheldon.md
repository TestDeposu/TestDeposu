# Are You Afraid of the Dark?

**Author:** Sidney Sheldon  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

When it comes to the masters of the airport thriller, Sidney Sheldon stands as a titan of pacing, and his 2009 novel, Are You Afraid of the Dark?, serves as a quintessential example of his signature ability to turn a global conspiracy into a claustrophobic nightmare. In this pulse-pounding tale, Sheldon pivots away from his traditional glamour-soaked backdrops to explore a more clinical, terrifying reality: the idea that the world’s most powerful think tank might be using its intellectual capital to orchestrate chaos.

The story kicks off with a rapid-fire sequence of catastrophes that would make a less seasoned writer stumble, but Sheldon handles the carnage with the clinical precision of a veteran. From a vanished woman in Berlin to a deadly descent from the Eiffel Tower, the plot begins as a series of disparate, chilling tragedies. It is only when these threads knot together at the Kingsley International Group (KIG) that the true scope of the threat emerges.

At the heart of this web are Kelly Harris and Diane Stevens. These aren't your typical "damsels in distress"; they are grieving widows thrust into a high-stakes geopolitical hunt. Sheldon’s strength has always been his ability to render characters who are easy to root for, and he excels here by grounding their terror in the relatable exhaustion of sudden, unexplained loss. Their reluctant alliance creates a compelling dynamic of suspicion and dependency, forcing the reader to constantly question who—if anyone—can be trusted in a world where the villain appears to have the resources of a small nation at their fingertips.

Stylistically, the book is lean and hungry. Sheldon eschews flowery prose for short, punchy chapters that act like a ticking clock. He understands that a thriller is essentially a momentum machine, and he refuses to let the reader put the book down. The prose is functional, designed to drag the reader through the harrowing near-escapes and the shadow-filled alleys of New York with an urgency that mimics the pulse of the protagonists. 

The main theme—the terrifying disconnect between the benevolent face of corporate power and the cold, calculated reality of hidden agendas—feels eerily prescient. Sheldon manages to tap into that modern anxiety of being watched, tracked, and ultimately disposed of by forces that operate in the boardrooms of the elite. While some may find the twists reminiscent of his earlier works, there is a certain comfort and thrill in the way Sheldon plays his hand. He isn't interested in moral ambiguity; he is interested in delivering a high-octane confrontation between the vulnerable and the untouchable.

Are You Afraid of the Dark? is a masterclass in the "cat and mouse" genre. It is an unapologetic page-turner that favors entertainment and suspense over complex psychological deep-dives. If you are looking for a story that combines the global scale of a political thriller with the intimate, sweaty-palmed terror of a classic mystery, this is the book to pick up. Sheldon may have been reaching the end of his illustrious career when he wrote this, but he proves once and for all that he never lost his knack for keeping us awake long past our bedtime.
