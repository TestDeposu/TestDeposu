# Half Broke Horses

**Author:** Jeannette Walls  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

"Half Broke Horses" by Jeannette Walls is a captivating, true-life novel that pays tribute to the indomitable spirit of Lily Casey Smith, Walls' grandmother. This book is a testament to the power of resilience, independence, and the unyielding human spirit, set against the backdrop of the American frontier.

Walls' style is as engaging as it is straightforward, much like her grandmother's character. She weaves a narrative that is equal parts adventure, history, and personal memoir, creating a vivid tapestry of a life well-lived. The story is told in the first person, allowing Lily's voice to resonate throughout, making the reader feel as if they are listening to her recount her extraordinary life.

The main theme of "Half Broke Horses" is the unbreakable spirit of a woman who refuses to be defined by her circumstances. Lily Smith is a force to be reckoned with, a woman who can break horses, fly planes, and navigate the harshest of terrains. She is a beacon of strength and courage, a role model for generations of women who dare to defy societal norms.

Walls masterfully captures the essence of the American West, with its vast landscapes, harsh conditions, and the pioneering spirit that defined an era. Yet, "Half Broke Horses" is more than just a historical account; it is a deeply personal story that explores the complexities of family, love, and loss.

The novel is a compelling read, filled with anecdotes that are as entertaining as they are insightful. Walls' ability to balance drama with humor is commendable, making the book an engaging and enjoyable read.

In conclusion, "Half Broke Horses" is a must-read for anyone who appreciates a good story about a strong woman overcoming adversity. It is a celebration of the human spirit, a testament to the power of resilience, and a love letter to the American West.
