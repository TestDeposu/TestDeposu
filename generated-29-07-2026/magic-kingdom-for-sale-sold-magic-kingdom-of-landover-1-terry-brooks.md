# Magic Kingdom for Sale/Sold (Magic Kingdom of Landover, #1)

**Author:** Terry Brooks  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1986

In "Magic Kingdom for Sale/Sold", Terry Brooks embarks on a fascinating journey, diverging from his renowned Shannara series to craft a captivating tale of magic, mystery, and adventure. This 1986 novel whisks readers away to the enchanting realm of Landover, a mystical kingdom that promises an escape from the mundane world. However, beneath its whimsical surface lies a complex exploration of power, identity, and the human condition.

At the heart of the story is Ben Holiday, a disillusioned lawyer who stumbles upon an unusual advertisement for a magical kingdom. Intrigued by the prospect of escaping his mundane life, Ben purchases the kingdom, only to discover that it comes with a plethora of problems. The once-thriving kingdom lies in ruins, its inhabitants plagued by despair, and its ruler, the Iron Mark, a formidable demon lord, poses a constant threat. As Ben navigates this uncharted world, he must confront the harsh realities of leadership, responsibility, and the true meaning of power.

Brooks' masterful storytelling weaves a rich tapestry of characters, each with their unique quirks and motivations. The Court Magician, Abernathy the talking dog, and the enigmatic Willow, all contribute to the novel's humor, wit, and emotional depth. The Paladin, a legendary champion, serves as a poignant reminder of the kingdom's former glory, while the Iron Mark embodies the darkness that threatens to consume it.

One of the most striking aspects of "Magic Kingdom for Sale/Sold" is its thought-provoking exploration of identity. Ben, a man who has lost his sense of purpose, finds himself in a world where he is expected to be a king. As he grapples with the weight of his new responsibilities, he must confront the question of who he truly is. This introspection is mirrored in the kingdom itself, which struggles to reclaim its former glory. Through Ben's journey, Brooks raises important questions about the nature of identity, power, and the human condition.

The author's writing style is characteristic of his signature blend of fantasy, adventure, and humor. Brooks' vivid descriptions of Landover transport readers to a world of wonder, while his well-crafted characters drive the narrative forward. The pacing is well-balanced, oscillating between moments of high-octane action and introspective quietude.

In conclusion, "Magic Kingdom for Sale/Sold" is a captivating tale that will delight fantasy readers of all ages. With its richly detailed world-building, memorable characters, and thought-provoking themes, this novel is a testament to Terry Brooks' skill as a master storyteller. If you're looking for a story that will transport you to a world of magic and wonder, look no further.
