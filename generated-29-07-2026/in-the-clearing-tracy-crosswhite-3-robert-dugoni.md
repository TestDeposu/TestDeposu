# In the Clearing (Tracy Crosswhite, #3)

**Author:** Robert Dugoni  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

**In the Clearing: A Gripping Legal Thriller by Robert Dugoni**

Robert Dugoni, the master of legal suspense, delivers another pulse-pounding thriller with "In the Clearing," the third installment in his Tracy Crosswhite series. This time, attorney David Sloane takes center stage, grappling with a case that threatens not only his unblemished record but also the lives of those he holds dear.

At its core, "In the Clearing" is a story about justice, sacrifice, and the lengths one will go to protect family. When Beverly Ford walks into Sloane's office, seeking justice for her husband's mysterious death in Iraq, Sloane finds himself drawn into a labyrinth of military secrets and deadly cover-ups. The case is fraught with challenges, not least of which is the decades-old military doctrine that could make Ford's case unwinnable. Yet, Sloane, a former soldier himself, is compelled to fight for the widow and her four children, even as the shadows of danger close in around him.

Dugoni's prowess lies in his ability to weave intricate plots that keep readers on the edge of their seats. "In the Clearing" is no exception. The story unfolds at a breakneck pace, as Sloane and his reclusive friend, former CIA agent Charles Jenkins, race against time to unravel the truth behind James Ford's death. Meanwhile, Sloane's new wife Tina and stepson Jake find themselves targeted by a relentless killer, adding a chilling layer of personal stakes to the narrative.

Dugoni's prose is crisp and engaging, his characters well-drawn and compelling. He has a knack for creating protagonists who are both flawed and relatable, making it easy for readers to invest in their journeys. Sloane, in particular, is a protagonist worth rooting for, his moral compass unwavering even as he navigates the treacherous waters of military politics and deadly conspiracies.

The legal aspects of the story are handled with the same deft touch that Dugoni brought to his previous works. He peels back the layers of the complex case with precision, making the legal jargon accessible without sacrificing authenticity. The courtroom scenes are particularly riveting, a testament to Dugoni's ability to create tension and suspense in even the most unlikely of settings.

"In the Clearing" is more than just a legal thriller; it's a story about the human cost of war and the power of perseverance in the face of adversity. Dugoni doesn't shy away from exploring the emotional toll that Ford's death takes on his family, nor does he sugarcoat the harsh realities of military life. Instead, he uses these elements to add depth and resonance to the story, making it a compelling exploration of the themes that matter most.

In conclusion, "In the Clearing" is a must-read for fans of legal thrillers and suspenseful narratives. Dugoni's signature style, coupled with a gripping plot and well-crafted characters, makes for a page-turner that is impossible to put down. This book is a testament to Dugoni's skill as a storyteller and a powerful reminder of the importance of justice and truth.
