# The Power

**Author:** Naomi Alderman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

What if the tables were turned? In her 2017 speculative masterpiece, The Power, Naomi Alderman explores this provocative question not through the lens of political diplomacy or social reform, but through a visceral, biological upheaval. Imagine a world where adolescent girls suddenly develop the ability to generate lethal electrical currents from their collarbones. With a simple twitch, they hold the power of life, death, and absolute domination. 

Rightly heralded as our era’s Handmaid’s Tale, Alderman’s novel takes the familiar architecture of our patriarchal society and subjects it to a high-voltage stress test. The narrative follows four disparate lives whose trajectories collide in the wake of this evolutionary shift: Tunde, an ambitious Nigerian journalist documenting the global upheaval; Roxy, the hardened daughter of a London crime lord; Allie, an abused foster child who transforms into a messianic figure; and Margot, a cunning American politician quick to weaponize the new paradigm. Through these lenses, Alderman traces the ripple effects of a world order being violently upended.

At its core, The Power is an unflinching examination of power itself—who holds it, how it is abused, and whether corruption is an inevitable byproduct of dominance. Alderman masterfully strips away the romanticized notion that subjugated groups, if given supremacy, would naturally create a more egalitarian or enlightened society. Instead, she delivers a chilling anthropological thesis: power does not inherently corrupt because of gender; power corrupts because it is power. As the matriarchy violently displaces the patriarchy, the oppressed quickly become the oppressors, proving that brutality knows no gender.

Alderman’s literary style is as immersive and electric as the phenomenon she describes. Her prose is sharp, unflinching, and laced with a biting, darkly comedic irony. Crucially, the novel is framed as a historical artifact—an epistolary dialogue between a male author seeking validation for a speculative history book about a matriarchal past and a female mentor who gently mocks his naive perspective of history. This brilliant framing device brilliantly mirrors the condescension historically directed at female writers and challenges readers to question their own deeply ingrained biases about gender roles, history, and authority.

Unsettling, fast-paced, and intellectually rigorous, The Power is not a comfortable read, nor is it meant to be. It is a cautionary tale, a mirror held up to the darkest impulses of human civilization, and a masterclass in speculative fiction. Alderman does not offer easy answers; instead, she leaves you with a lingering, visceral jolt that will make you rethink the very structures of the world we live in today.
