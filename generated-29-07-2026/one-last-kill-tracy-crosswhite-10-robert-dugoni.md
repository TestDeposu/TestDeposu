# One Last Kill (Tracy Crosswhite, #10)

**Author:** Robert Dugoni  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Robert Dugoni has long been a master of the legal thriller, but with One Last Kill, he takes the genre and plunges it headfirst into the high-stakes, shadow-drenched world of military conspiracy. Although the book’s publication history sees occasional chronological shufflings in series packaging, the narrative DNA remains pure Dugoni: meticulously researched, relentlessly paced, and anchored by a moral compass that refuses to waver in the face of insurmountable odds. 

At the thematic core of One Last Kill is the sacred, yet frequently shattered, contract between the state and those who wear its uniform. When attorney David Sloane takes on the case of a grieving widow whose National Guardsman husband died under suspicious circumstances in Iraq, he is stepping onto treacherous legal terrain. Dugoni explores the chilling weight of the Feres Doctrine—the decades-old legal precedent that essentially bars members of the armed forces from suing the federal government for injuries or deaths incident to service. By confronting this formidable wall of governmental immunity, Dugoni asks a piercing central question: Who watches the protectors, and what recourse does a family have when the machinery of war grinds up one of its own and tries to bury the evidence?

Sloane is not just a legal eagle looking for another notch on his belt; he is a former soldier himself. This personal history infuses the narrative with an authentic sense of duty and brotherhood. He cannot turn his back on Beverly Ford and her four children, even when the pursuit of truth transforms a standard civil suit into a deadly game of survival. Dugoni’s style shines brightest in the courtroom sequences, where legal maneuvering feels like high-contact sport, but he seamlessly transitions these intellectual chess matches into harrowing sequences of physical peril. 

The author’s pacing is relentless. As Sloane and his formidable investigator friend, Charles Jenkins—a reclusive former CIA operative—dig deeper into the fatal mission, the plot accelerates into overdrive. The realization that surviving soldiers from the ill-fated unit are being systematically silenced turns the legal drama into a pulse-pounding manhunt. Dugoni expertly ratchets up the tension by bringing the threat straight to Sloane’s doorstep, forcing his newlywed wife and stepson into hiding. This narrative choice raises the emotional stakes exponentially, ensuring that the conflict is not merely professional, but intensely, devastatingly personal.

What elevates One Last Kill above standard pulp thrillers is Dugoni’s commitment to character depth and realistic dialogue. The antagonists are not cartoonish villains, but rather the chillingly cold, faceless entities of institutional cover-up and lethal bureaucracy. The prose is lean, sharp, and designed to keep the pages turning late into the night. 

Ultimately, Robert Dugoni delivers a masterclass in suspense that marries the cerebral satisfaction of a courtroom drama with the breathless adrenaline of a geopolitical thriller. One Last Kill is a stark reminder that justice is rarely handed down freely; sometimes, it has to be dragged kicking and screaming into the light. For fans of smart, visceral legal fiction and conspiracy thrillers that punch well above their weight, this novel is an absolute must-read.
