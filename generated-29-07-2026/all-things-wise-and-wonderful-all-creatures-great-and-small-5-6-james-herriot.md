# All Things Wise and Wonderful (All Creatures Great and Small, #5-6)

**Author:** James Herriot  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

In "All Things Wise and Wonderful," James Herriot masterfully weaves a poignant and humorous narrative that transcends the boundaries of war, love, and the unbreakable bond between humans and animals. This fifth and sixth installment in the All Creatures Great and Small series is a testament to Herriot's remarkable storytelling ability, as he seamlessly blends the chaos of World War II with the tranquility of his Yorkshire veterinary practice.

Herriot's writing style is as captivating as ever, with a unique blend of wit, compassion, and insight that has come to define his work. Through a series of vignettes, he recounts his experiences training with the Royal Air Force, his longing for his pregnant wife, and his periodic returns to the people and animals that make his practice so endearing. These episodes are at once humorous and heart-wrenching, offering a glimpse into the complexities of life during wartime.

One of the most striking aspects of "All Things Wise and Wonderful" is its exploration of the human condition. Herriot's characters, from the farmers and villagers of Yorkshire to his fellow servicemen, are multidimensional and relatable, each with their own struggles and triumphs. Through their stories, Herriot reflects on the values that make life worth fighting for – love, loyalty, and a deep connection to the natural world.

The author's love for the Yorkshire countryside and its inhabitants is palpable throughout the book. His descriptions of the rolling hills, the rugged coastline, and the quirky characters that inhabit this world are so vivid that they transport the reader to a bygone era. At the same time, Herriot's account of his wartime experiences serves as a poignant reminder of the devastating impact of conflict on individuals and communities.

What sets "All Things Wise and Wonderful" apart from other war stories is its emphasis on the everyday, the mundane, and the often-overlooked aspects of life during wartime. Herriot's focus on the people and animals that remained behind, waiting and worrying, offers a unique perspective on this period in history. His stories of farmers struggling to maintain their livelihoods, of women coping with the absence of their loved ones, and of animals providing comfort and solace are a powerful tribute to the resilience of the human spirit.

In conclusion, "All Things Wise and Wonderful" is a captivating and thought-provoking read that will appeal to fans of Herriot's previous work and newcomers alike. With its unique blend of humor, pathos, and insight, this book is a testament to the power of storytelling and the enduring appeal of the natural world. As a work of historical fiction, it offers a fresh perspective on World War II and its impact on rural England. As a work of literature, it is a celebration of the human condition, with all its complexities and contradictions.
