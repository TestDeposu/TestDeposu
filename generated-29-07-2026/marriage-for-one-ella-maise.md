# Marriage for One

**Author:** Ella Maise  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2024

Marriage for One thrusts readers into the whirlwind of Rose Coleson’s shattered dreams, where a sudden breakup and a puzzling testamentary clause force her into a marriage of convenience with the enigmatic lawyer Jack Hawthorne. Ella Maise spins this familiar trope into something that feels both fresh and deeply felt, using the contract‑marriage premise not just as a plot device but as a lens through which she explores trust, vulnerability, and the slow, stubborn thawing of two guarded hearts.

The novel’s greatest strength lies in its character work. Rose is introduced as a bright‑eyed entrepreneur whose optimism is palpable, yet Maise doesn’t let her sunshine become saccharine; she grounds Rose’s hope in palpable frustration and a fierce determination to protect her lifelong dream of a café. Jack, meanwhile, starts as the archetypal grumpy lawyer—aloof, blunt, and seemingly uninterested in anything beyond the legal loophole that brings him to Rose’s doorstep. Maise peels back his layers with deliberate restraint, revealing moments of quiet tenderness that feel earned rather than forced. The slow‑burn romance unfolds in a series of understated gestures—a shared glance over coffee, a hesitant hand on the small of her back, a late‑night conversation that lingers longer than either expects—each one building tension without resorting to melodramatic confrontations.

Maise’s prose is crisp and conversational, peppered with witty internal commentary that keeps the narrative light even when the stakes feel high. She balances humor with genuine emotional weight, allowing readers to laugh at Rose’s awkward attempts to navigate a fake marriage while simultaneously feeling the ache of her yearning for something real. The pacing is brisk; scenes transition smoothly from the bustling streets of New York to the intimate confines of the building that houses Rose’s dream café, giving the setting a character of its own. The author’s use of sensory details—the aroma of freshly brewed espresso, the clatter of cups, the city’s relentless hum—immerses the reader in Rose’s world, making the eventual shift from contract to connection feel both inevitable and satisfying.

While the premise leans heavily on familiar romance beats, Maise avoids predictability by giving Jack’s motivations depth beyond mere convenience. His backstory, hinted at through fragmented memories and terse exchanges, adds a layer of intrigue that keeps the reader guessing whether his affection is genuine or merely a strategic performance. This ambiguity fuels the novel’s central question: can a relationship built on deception ever become authentic? Maise answers it with a nuanced optimism that never dismisses the characters’ fears, instead showing how honesty, however painful, can rebuild trust.

One minor drawback is that the secondary cast—friends, family, and the café staff—occasionally feels underdeveloped, serving more as background color than as fully realized allies or antagonists. A richer tapestry of supporting voices could have amplified the stakes of Rose’s professional aspirations and provided additional contrast to the central duo’s intimacy. Nonetheless, these are small quibbles in an otherwise engaging read.

Marriage for One is a compelling addition to the fake‑marriage subgenre, offering a blend of humor, heart, and heat that will appeal to fans of slow‑burn, grumpy‑x‑sunshine romances. Maise’s skillful character shading and evocative storytelling make the journey from contractual obligation to genuine love feel both believable and delightfully rewarding.
