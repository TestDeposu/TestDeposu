# Smokin' Seventeen (Stephanie Plum, #17)

**Author:** Janet Evanovich  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

In the seventeen-book marathon of the Stephanie Plum series, Janet Evanovich has mastered a very specific brand of literary comfort food. By the time you reach Smokin’ Seventeen, you aren’t looking for a radical evolution of the protagonist or a gritty departure into hardboiled noir. You are looking for the familiar chaos of Trenton, New Jersey, the erratic antics of Lula, and the persistent, low-stakes romantic deadlock between Joe Morelli and Ranger. In this installment, Evanovich leans heavily into the absurdity that has become the hallmark of the franchise, delivering a narrative that is essentially a high-speed cartoon in prose form.

The plot of Smokin’ Seventeen kicks off with a grim premise—bodies discovered at the construction site of the bail bond office—but Evanovich handles this with her signature flippancy. The mystery serves less as a cerebral puzzle and more as a frame for the series' true core: Stephanie’s perpetual state of near-catastrophe. Whether she is dodging a ruthless killer or trying to wrangle a dancing bear and a self-proclaimed vampire, the stakes remain high for the characters while remaining remarkably light for the reader.

Evanovich’s style remains razor-sharp in its brevity. Her sentences are punchy, often ending with a dry observation or a comedic beat that highlights Stephanie’s unintentional slapstick lifestyle. This book is a masterclass in the "formulaic" approach done right. The banter between Stephanie and Lula provides the necessary oxygen for the story, grounding the outlandish events in a very real, very exasperated female friendship. When the narrative shifts toward the love triangle, Evanovich knows exactly which buttons to push for her long-term readers. She doesn’t necessarily move the needle on the Morelli-versus-Ranger debate, but she explores it with the kind of humorous, lighthearted tension that keeps the audience engaged without ever demanding deep emotional labor.

What makes Smokin’ Seventeen stand out—or perhaps just blend in perfectly—is its relentless pace. The book moves with a manic energy that matches Stephanie’s own frantic attempts to stay alive. There is a sense of "anything goes" here; the inclusion of the aforementioned vampire and bear is typical of the series' willingness to embrace the ridiculous to keep the reader laughing. It is clear that Evanovich isn't trying to win a Pulitzer with this installment; she is trying to provide an escape. In that regard, she succeeds entirely.

Critics of the series often point to the lack of character growth as a drawback, but in Smokin’ Seventeen, the lack of change is the point. Stephanie Plum is an everywoman who refuses to be crushed by the extraordinary absurdity surrounding her. She is a reluctant hero who keeps showing up for work, keeps getting her car blown up, and keeps finding ways to survive, even when she has no idea how she got into the mess in the first place.

If you are a fan of the series, Smokin’ Seventeen is exactly the book you expect it to be: funny, fast, and entirely unpretentious. If you are new to the series, this is a chaotic place to start, but it provides a perfect encapsulation of why Janet Evanovich remains a titan of the mystery-comedy genre. It is a breezy read that proves that even when your life is going up in smoke, it’s best to laugh through the flames.
