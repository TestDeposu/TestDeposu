# Reunion (The Mediator, #3)

**Author:** Jenny Carroll  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Title: Love Across the Great Divide: Navigating the Perilous Waters of the Afterlife in Jenny Carroll’s Reunion

When it comes to adolescent angst, most teenagers grapple with the usual suspects: shifting friendships, academic pressure, and the agonizing uncertainty of a Friday night crush. Suze Simon, however, faces a slightly more complicated dilemma—her heart is hopelessly entangled with a gorgeous nineteenth-century ghost, while a flesh-and-blood manipulator holds his eternal fate in the palm of his hand. In Reunion, the third installment of the captivating Mediator series (subsequently published under the author's renowned real name, Meg Cabot), the stakes for our snarky, pants-wearing heroine reach a fever pitch, blending supernatural suspense with the sharp, effortless wit that has defined this YA staple for generations.

At its core, Reunion explores the ultimate theme of impossible love and the agonizing lines we blur between friend and foe. Suze’s unique gift as a mediator—someone who acts as a bridge between the living and the dead—has never felt like more of a curse. For months, she has shared an unspoken, aching tension with Jesse, the phantom resident of her bedroom. Yet, death, as it turns out, is a rather stubborn obstacle to traditional domestic bliss. Enter Paul Slater: a living, breathing mediator whose charm is matched only by his lethal danger. Paul understands Suze’s powers in a way no one else in the world does, making him a fascinatingly complex foil. When he uses Jesse’s very existence as leverage, forcing Suze into a blackmail-fueled romance, the narrative plunges headfirst into the delicious agony of a slow-burn, enemies-to-lovers dynamic.

What makes Carroll’s—or rather, Cabot’s—writing style so enduringly effective is her peerless mastery of narrative voice. Suze Simon is a triumph of young adult literature: she is fiercely independent, fiercely protective, and deeply vulnerable beneath a tough exterior of sarcasm and combat boots. She doesn't wait to be rescued; she charges into paranormal peril swinging fists and sharp comebacks. The dialogue crackles with comedic timing that cuts through the heavier, high-stakes gothic gloom of the plot, ensuring that the novel never takes itself too seriously while still honoring the genuine emotional devastation of its romantic core.

In Reunion, the author balances supernatural lore with teenage realism with the precision of a tightrope walker. The romantic tension is palpable, oscillating between the safe, yet frustratingly untouchable ghost of the past and the thrilling, dangerous allure of a living bad boy who knows all her secrets. It is a testament to the author's skill that readers will find themselves fiercely rooting for a romance that defies the very laws of nature. 

Ultimately, this installment takes the danger and the romance to a thrilling new level. It is a witty, fast-paced, and emotionally resonant journey that reminds us why stories about first loves and impossible choices stick with us long after we turn the final page. Whether you are revisiting Suze's world of ghosts and high school halls or stepping into it for the very first time, Reunion proves that true chemistry transcends even the grave.
