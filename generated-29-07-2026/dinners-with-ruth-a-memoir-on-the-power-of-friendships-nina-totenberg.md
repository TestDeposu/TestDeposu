# Dinners with Ruth: A Memoir on the Power of Friendships

**Author:** Nina Totenberg  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

**A Feast of Friendship and Feminist Triumphs: Nina Totenberg's "Dinners with Ruth"**

In "Dinners with Ruth," Nina Totenberg, the renowned NPR correspondent, serves up a sumptuous memoir that is as much about the power of friendship as it is about the feminist revolution she and Ruth Bader Ginsburg helped ignite. Totenberg's prose is as inviting as a well-set table, drawing readers in with her warmth, wit, and wisdom.

At the heart of this memoir lies a nearly fifty-year friendship between Totenberg and Ginsburg, a bond that began with a simple phone call about a legal brief and evolved into a relationship that saw them through personal triumphs and tragedies. Totenberg's storytelling is at its most compelling when she delves into the intimate details of this friendship, from their shared love of opera and shopping to the quiet dinners they enjoyed together in Totenberg's home.

Yet, "Dinners with Ruth" is more than just a chronicle of a remarkable friendship. It is also a testament to the transformative power of women supporting each other in a world dominated by men. Totenberg and Ginsburg were not just friends; they were allies, each helping to pave the way for the other's success. Their story is a powerful reminder of the importance of women lifting each other up, a theme that resonates strongly today.

Totenberg's style is engaging and accessible, making complex legal and political issues understandable to the lay reader. She has a knack for painting vivid portraits of the fascinating characters who populate her life, from her legendary violinist father to her cherished NPR colleagues. Her writing is also marked by a refreshing honesty, as she candidly discusses her own struggles and mistakes.

However, "Dinners with Ruth" is not without its flaws. At times, the narrative feels disjointed, as Totenberg jumps between different periods of her life and the lives of her friends. The book could also benefit from a more structured approach to its themes, as Totenberg touches on a wide range of topics but does not always delve as deeply as she could.

Despite these minor criticisms, "Dinners with Ruth" is a compelling and inspiring read. It is a celebration of friendship, feminism, and the power of women supporting each other. It is a book that will resonate with anyone who has ever found solace and strength in the bonds of female friendship.
