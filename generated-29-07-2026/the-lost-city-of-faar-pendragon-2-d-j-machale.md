# The Lost City of Faar (Pendragon, #2)

**Author:** D.J. MacHale  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Dive Deep Into Peril: A Review of The Lost City of Faar

When D.J. MacHale introduced readers to the sprawling multiverse of the Pendragon series, he established a new gold standard for millennial speculative fiction. In The Lost City of Faar, the second installment of this epic saga, MacHale proves that his inaugural outing was no fluke. He takes the sprawling world-building established in the first book and plunges his adolescent hero directly into an abyss of political unrest, ecological devastation, and aquatic mystery. For fourteen-year-old Bobby Pendragon, suburban basketball courts and mundane teenage anxieties are a distant memory, replaced by the crushing weight of saving entire civilizations.

The novel transports Bobby and his eccentric mentor, Uncle Press, to Cloral, a breathtaking territory entirely covered by water. Unlike the rocky, stratified struggles of Denduron, Cloral initially feels like a serene utopia of floating cities. However, MacHale quickly peels back the shimmering surface to reveal a society teetering on the absolute brink of ruin. The central theme of this volume revolves around greed, resource scarcity, and the devastating cost of societal corruption. Cloral is not merely facing a natural disaster; it is rotting from the inside out due to a treacherous plot involving deadly marauders and internal betrayal. The author uses this aquatic world to subtly critique real-world themes of environmental negligence and the fragility of peace, grounding high-concept science fiction in stark, relatable human consequences.

MacHale’s authorial style remains one of the greatest strengths of the series. His pacing is relentless, designed to mimic the breathless urgency of Bobby’s journals being beamed back home to his waiting friends, Mark and Courtney. This epistolary framing device creates a brilliant dual narrative: the high-octane action on Cloral is constantly punctuated by the grounded, emotional reality of two teenagers in suburban Connecticut trying to comprehend the impossible. MacHale writes with an accessible, conversational tone that effortlessly bridges the gap between middle-grade adventure and young adult stakes. He avoids purple prose, opting instead for punchy dialogue, vivid sensory descriptions of the underwater environment, and genuine emotional resonance. Bobby is not a brooding, pre-destined superhero; he is a reluctant, sometimes overwhelmed nice guy who relies on his empathy, athleticism, and sheer grit to survive.

As the search for the legendary lost land of Faar takes center stage, the plot transforms into a high-stakes treasure hunt where failure means the watery extinction of an entire world. The mythology deepens, expanding the lore of the Travelers and the diabolical machinations of the series' overarching antagonist, Saint Dane, whose shadowy manipulation looms larger and more menacing here than before. 

The Lost City of Faar is a triumphant sophomore effort that expands the boundaries of MacHale’s universe while raising the emotional stakes for its endearing cast. It is a thrilling, immersive page-turner that captures the bittersweet essence of growing up amidst chaos. For returning fans and new initiates alike, this aquatic crusade is an essential journey that sinks its hooks into you and refuses to let go until the very last page.
