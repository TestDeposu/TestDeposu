# Ransom (Highlands' Lairds, #2)

**Author:** Julie Garwood  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Unknown

Ransom, the second installment in Julie Garwood’s Highlands’ Lairds series, thrusts readers back into the rugged splendor of medieval Scotland where honor is as precious as gold and love often arrives on the back of a battle‑scarred steed. The novel follows Laird Alistair MacLeod, a fierce warrior whose clan is threatened not only by rival septs but by a cruel English baron who demands a king’s ransom for his capture. Into this volatile mix steps Lady Isabel Fitzgerald, a sharp‑tongued English heiress whose own family ties make her both a pawn and a potential ally. When Isabel volunteers to deliver the ransom—against her father’s wishes—she finds herself entwined in a web of deceit, bravery, and an undeniable attraction that refuses to be ignored.

At its core, Ransom explores the timeless theme of loyalty tested by circumstance. Garwood deftly examines what it means to remain true to one’s clan, one’s word, and one’s heart when external forces conspire to pull them apart. Alistair’s internal struggle—between the stoic expectations of a Highland laird and the vulnerability that love awakens—mirrors Isabel’s own conflict between duty to her family and the yearning to forge her own path. Their romance blossoms not in quiet parlors but amid the clang of swords, the scent of heather, and the stark reality of political intrigue, making every tender moment feel earned and urgent.

Garwood’s signature style shines through in the novel’s brisk pacing and vivid dialogue. She balances lush, atmospheric description—crumbling stone keeps, mist‑cloaked glens, the roar of bagpipes—with snappy, often humorous exchanges that keep the tension from becoming oppressive. Isabel’s wit cuts through Alistair’s gruff exterior, while his steadfastness grounds her spirited nature. The author’s knack for weaving historical detail into the narrative never feels like a lecture; instead, the customs, clothing, and clan politics serve as a living backdrop that enriches the characters’ choices.

What truly sets Ransom apart is Garwood’s ability to make the stakes feel both epic and intimate. The ransom plot drives the action forward, yet it is the quieter moments—shared glances over a smoldering fire, a whispered promise amidst the clash of steel—that linger longest. Readers who crave a blend of swoon‑worthy romance, high‑stakes adventure, and a heroine who refuses to be sidelined will find plenty to satisfy. Meanwhile, fans of the series will appreciate the continuation of clan lore and the subtle threads that tie Alistair’s story to the larger tapestry of the Highlands’ Lairds.

In sum, Ransom delivers a compelling mix of passion, honor, and raw Scottish grandeur. Julie Garwood once again proves why she remains a master of historical romance: she creates worlds that are as immersive as they are emotionally resonant, and she populates them with characters whose hearts beat as loudly as the swords they wield.
