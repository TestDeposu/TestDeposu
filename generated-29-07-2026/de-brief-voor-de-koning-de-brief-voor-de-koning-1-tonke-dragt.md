# De brief voor de koning (De brief voor de koning, #1)

**Author:** Tonke Dragt  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

In the realm of children's literature, few authors have managed to craft a tale as timeless and captivating as Tonke Dragt's "The Letter for the King". This Dutch masterpiece has been wowing readers since its inception in 1963, and its international allure has only grown stronger with age. What lies at the heart of this book's enduring enchantment?

Dragt's magnum opus delves into the realm of what happens when innocence and duty collide. We are introduced to Tiuri, a bright and ambitious young squire, mere moments away from being knighted. Yet, as he prepares for this momentous occasion, a sudden and unexpected knock at the door sets him on a perilous path that will test the very fabric of his existence. Entranced by the promise of adventure and the whispered words, "Do open," Tiuri is thrust into a world of high stakes and moral dilemmas.

Dragt's writing style is as remarkable as the story itself. Her words paint vivid pictures of Tiuri's journey, from the mystical forests to the bustling streets of the kingdom. With each sentence, we are transported to a world of wonder and awe, where chivalry, courage, and loyalty are tested by the weight of responsibility.

One of the most striking aspects of "The Letter for the King" is its ability to seamlessly balance action and introspection. Dragt crafts a story that is both thrilling and character-driven, where our protagonist's inner turmoil is as compelling as the external challenges he faces. Tiuri's quest is not merely about delivering a letter but about growing into his role as a knight and confronting the uncertainty that lies ahead.

Through Dragt's narrative, we are given a glimpse into a bygone era, where honor, duty, and chivalry are not just empty words but a code of conduct that shapes the very fabric of the kingdom. With an uncanny ability to blend historical and fantastical elements, "The Letter for the King" offers a captivating tale that transcends time and space, speaking directly to our sense of justice and morality.

The author's background as a teacher is also palpable in the story. Dragt's experiences in narrating tales to her students have imbued her writing with a clarity and accessibility that makes the book feel both a page-turner and a valuable education. As we follow Tiuri's journey, we are reminded of the power of storytelling to captivate, inspire, and transform us.

What makes "The Letter for the King" truly remarkable is its ability to tackle complex themes in an accessible and engaging manner. This is a book that defies categorization, blending elements of adventure, fantasy, and coming-of-age fiction to create a narrative that will captivate readers of all ages.

As a testament to its global appeal, "The Letter for the King" has been translated into 30 languages and has earned numerous awards, including the Prix pour le Meilleur Livre de L'Annee (Best Book of the Year) in 1963 and the Griffel der Griffels in 1976. Its Netflix adaptation, released in 2020, has further cemented its status as a beloved and timeless classic.

As Dragt herself notes, "Why would you risk your life for a letter whose contents you do not know?" This question serves as a potent reminder of the sacrifices we make for the greater good and the courage it takes to face the unknown. "The Letter for the King" is a book that will stay with you long after you finish reading it, a reminder of the transformative power of stories to ignite our imaginations, challenge our assumptions, and inspire us to become better versions of ourselves.
