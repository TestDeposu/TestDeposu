# Mary, Mary (Alex Cross, #11)

**Author:** James Patterson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2005

**Mary, Mary (Alex Cross, #11) by James Patterson: A Thrilling Plunge into Hollywood's Dark Heart**

In the eleventh installment of James Patterson's bestselling Alex Cross series, **Mary, Mary**, Patterson masterfully transports his beloved protagonist from the gritty streets of Washington D.C. to the glitz and glamour of Hollywood, delivering a gripping tale that exposes the dark underbelly of Tinseltown.

The story begins with FBI Agent Alex Cross enjoying a much-needed family vacation in Disneyland. However, his respite is short-lived as he's called back to duty following the murder of a prominent Hollywood actress. The case takes a chilling turn when an L.A. Times editor receives an email detailing the crime in gruesome detail, signed 'Mary Smith.' Cross soon realizes he's up against a cunning and calculating killer who's already struck before and plans to do so again.

Patterson excels in creating a sense of urgency and tension, as the body count rises and the list of suspects grows. He expertly weaves a complex web of motives, secrets, and red herrings, keeping readers on the edge of their seats until the very end. The author's signature fast-paced narrative and short, punchy chapters ensure that **Mary, Mary** is an unputdownable thriller.

At the heart of the novel lies Patterson's exploration of the dark side of fame and the price of celebrity. He peels back the glamorous facade of Hollywood to reveal a world riddled with obsession, jealousy, and danger. Through Cross's investigation, Patterson shines a spotlight on the disturbing consequences of idol worship and the fine line between admiration and madness.

Alex Cross, now a seasoned and battle-hardened protagonist, remains a compelling and relatable hero. Patterson skillfully balances Cross's personal life, family dynamics, and professional challenges, adding depth and emotional resonance to the story. Cross's fish-out-of-water experience in Hollywood also provides moments of humor and levity, offering a welcome contrast to the story's darker elements.

In **Mary, Mary**, Patterson once again demonstrates his prowess as a master of the thriller genre. With its compelling premise, intricate plot, and well-drawn characters, this novel is a must-read for fans of the Alex Cross series and thriller enthusiasts alike.
