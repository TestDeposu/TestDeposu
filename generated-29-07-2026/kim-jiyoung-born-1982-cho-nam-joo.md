# Kim Jiyoung, Born 1982

**Author:** Cho Nam-Joo  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

Kim Jiyoung, Born 1982, a novel that has taken the literary world by storm, is a thought-provoking and unflinching exploration of the suffocating pressures that Korean society places on its women. Written by Cho Nam-Joo, a rising star in the literary firmament, this book is a searing indictment of the misogyny that pervades every aspect of South Korean life.

At its core, Kim Jiyoung, Born 1982 is a story about the gradual unraveling of a millennial woman, Jiyoung, who lives in a seemingly ordinary world but whose reality is one of crushing patriarchal norms. From the moment she is born to the expectations of her parents to become a son, Jiyoung is subjected to a lifetime of subtle and not-so-subtle reminders that her very existence is secondary to that of her male counterparts. Her experiences, from the rigid social expectations placed upon her in school to the casual sexism of her coworkers, are a masterclass in the ways in which societal norms can erode a woman's sense of self-worth.

As Jiyoung navigates her life with increasing desperation, her symptoms become more and more bizarre, culminating in a kind of psychic dissolution that blurs the lines between her own identity and that of the countless other women whose voices she begins to hear. It is a terrifying and poignant portrayal of the consequences of living in a society that treats women as mere extensions of their male partners and families.

Cho Nam-Joo's writing is a revelation. Her prose is elegant yet unadorned, conveying the ordinariness of Jiyoung's experiences even as it underscores the profound implications of her story. It is a writing style that is both deeply personal and universally relatable, capturing the quotidian struggles of womanhood in a way that feels both intimate and observational.

One of the most striking aspects of Kim Jiyoung, Born 1982 is its unflinching portrayal of the ways in which patriarchal norms can lead to mental health crises in women. Throughout the novel, Jiyoung's growing mental instability is depicted as a direct consequence of the social expectations placed upon her, and the author's refusal to shy away from this connection is both courageous and necessary.

But Kim Jiyoung, Born 1982 is more than just a novel about the struggles of womanhood; it is also a scathing critique of the ways in which societal norms can create and perpetuate systemic inequality. Through Jiyoung's story, Cho Nam-Joo lays bare the ways in which women's experiences are constantly marginalized and dismissed, and the ways in which this marginalization can lead to psychic and emotional numbing.

It is a message that is both urgent and necessary, and one that feels particularly relevant in the wake of the #MeToo movement and the growing recognition of the ways in which women's bodies and experiences are policed and surveilled. In a world where women's voices are increasingly being heard, Kim Jiyoung, Born 1982 is a powerful reminder of the importance of listening to, believing, and amplifying those voices.

Ultimately, Kim Jiyoung, Born 1982 is a searing and deeply humane novel that will resonate with anyone who has ever felt the weight of societal expectations. It is a testament to the enduring power of storytelling to capture the complexities of human experience, and a powerful reminder of the ways in which fiction can be used to challenge and subvert the dominant narratives of our society.
