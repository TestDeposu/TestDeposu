# I Have Never (First Comes Love, #2)

**Author:** Camilla Isley  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

When life hands you a meticulously curated blueprint for perfection, and you promptly trip over it, what do you do? If you are Blair Walker, the endearing protagonist of Camilla Isley’s 2017 romantic comedy I Have Never, you throw the rulebook out the window and dive headfirst into chaos. This second installment in the First Comes Love series delivers a sparkling, laugh-out-loud exploration of what happens when a control freak is forced to lose control, establishing Isley as a master of contemporary comedic romance.

At the heart of Isley’s narrative is a universally relatable theme: the illusion of the life plan. Twenty-nine-year-old Blair has structured every second of her existence. Her existence is governed by a rigid mental code of conduct—a doctrine of dos and don'ts designed to secure a dream career, flawless social standing, and eventually, Mr. Right. But the universe has a notoriously wicked sense of humor. In a single, cataclysmic twenty-four-hour period, Blair’s carefully constructed reality implodes when she loses both her job and her boyfriend. 

This devastating double-blow serves as the catalyst for the book's central premise. Reeling from the realization that playing by the rules has only led to heartbreak and unemployment, Blair pivots in desperation. She decides to break every single rule she has ever lived by. Never lie. Never pick a fight. Never make a scene. Never make the first move. Never make impulse decisions. Never mix business and pleasure. By actively inverting her life's dogma, Blair embarks on a mission of self-discovery that is as chaotic as it is liberating. 

Isley’s writing style is the engine that drives this comedic vehicle. Her prose is snappy, modern, and relentlessly witty. She has an uncanny ability to capture the internal monologue of the over-analytical woman, translating anxiety and panic into genuinely hilarious external situations. The pacing is brisk, mirroring the breathless frenzy of Blair's newfound impulsivity. Isley avoids the trap of making Blair a caricature; instead, beneath the neurotic need for order lies a deeply vulnerable woman trying to figure out who she is when she is no longer performing for an imaginary audience of perfectionists.

The author’s structural choice to use the forbidden list as a narrative checklist creates a compelling episodic momentum. Each chapter or sequence tackles a broken rule, ensuring the plot constantly moves forward with fresh comedic obstacles. Whether Blair is awkwardly attempting to make the first move on a completely unsuitable man or stumbling into disastrously unprofessional situations by mixing business with pleasure, Isley keeps the tone light, breezy, and effortlessly entertaining. 

Yet, beneath the champagne-soaked humor and screwball antics, I Have Never offers a surprisingly poignant message about growth and authenticity. Isley suggests that the best parts of life usually happen in the messy margins outside the lines. Blair’s journey is not just about rebelling against her own rigid nature, but about unlearning the pressure to be flawless. She learns that vulnerability is far more rewarding than predictability, and that true love and fulfillment rarely arrive with an itinerary attached.

For readers seeking a smart, fast-paced romantic comedy with a heavy dose of heart, Camilla Isley’s 2017 gem is an absolute delight. It is a witty reminder that sometimes, the only way to find yourself is to completely lose your mind and your carefully crafted five-year plan.
