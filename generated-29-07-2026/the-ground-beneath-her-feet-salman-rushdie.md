# The Ground Beneath Her Feet

**Author:** Salman Rushdie  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2000

"The Ground Beneath Her Feet" is a tour de force by Salman Rushdie, a novelist who has always been a master of weaving myth, history, and personal narrative into a rich, intoxicating brew. This novel is no exception, offering a modern retelling of the myth of Orpheus, set against the backdrop of the late 20th century music scene and the seismic shifts of our world.

At the heart of the story is the enigmatic Vina Apsara, a singer whose voice is as captivating as it is elusive. Her lover, Ormus Cama, a musician whose genius is matched only by his obsession, spends his life trying to find her, both literally and metaphorically. Their epic romance is narrated by Rai, Ormus's childhood friend and Vina's sometime lover, a photographer whose camera lens offers a unique perspective on the world.

Rushdie's style is as vibrant and eclectic as ever, drawing on a wide range of influences from mythology to rock 'n' roll. He uses language like a musician uses notes, creating a symphony of words that resonate long after the last page is turned. His characters are complex and compelling, their lives intertwined with the history and politics of their times.

But "The Ground Beneath Her Feet" is more than just a love story. It's a meditation on the nature of reality, on how we perceive the world and our place in it. Rushdie explores the idea that our lives are shaped by forces beyond our control, like the seismic shifts that literally reshape the earth. He suggests that we are all, in a sense, searching for something elusive, like Ormus searching for Vina, and that our lives are defined by these searches.

The novel is also a critique of celebrity culture, of how we elevate certain individuals to god-like status while ignoring the human beings behind the public personas. Rushdie's characters are both larger than life and deeply flawed, a testament to his ability to create complex, believable characters.

In "The Ground Beneath Her Feet," Rushdie has created a novel that is as ambitious as it is accomplished. It's a novel that demands to be read, discussed, and debated. It's a novel that will stay with you long after you've turned the last page.
