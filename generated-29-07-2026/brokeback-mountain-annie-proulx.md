# Brokeback Mountain

**Author:** Annie Proulx  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

In the poignant and powerful "Brokeback Mountain," Annie Proulx weaves a masterpiece of contemporary literature that has captivated readers and critics alike. This standalone edition of the beloved story, first published in the collection "Close Range," is a must-read for anyone who has not yet experienced the haunting beauty of Proulx's prose.

At its core, "Brokeback Mountain" is a tragic love story about two ranch hands, Ennis del Mar and Jack Twist, who find themselves inexplicably drawn to each other while working as sheepherder and camp tender on a remote range. The attraction is casual at first, but as the summer wears on, something deeper and more profound develops between them. Despite their efforts to move on with their lives, marrying and having children, they find themselves inextricably linked, their bond growing stronger with each passing year.

Proulx's writing is a character in its own right, evoking the harsh beauty of the Wyoming landscape and the rugged, stoic men who inhabit it. Her prose is spare, elegant, and precise, conjuring the vast expanses of the American West and the tiny, isolated world of the two protagonists. With a few deft strokes, she captures the essence of Ennis and Jack, their struggles, and their desires, rendering them fully human and relatable.

One of the most striking aspects of "Brokeback Mountain" is its exploration of the human cost of intolerance and societal expectations. Ennis and Jack's love is doomed from the start, not by any inherent flaw, but by the world's refusal to accept it. Proulx's portrayal of their struggles is both heartbreaking and infuriating, highlighting the senseless tragedy that can result from our inability to accept those who are different.

The themes of isolation, loneliness, and the search for human connection are woven throughout the narrative, adding depth and complexity to the story. Proulx's characters are multidimensional and flawed, their relationships messy and often painful. Yet, it is precisely this messiness that makes them so relatable and human.

In "Brokeback Mountain," Annie Proulx has created a masterpiece that is both a powerful love story and a scathing indictment of societal intolerance. Her writing is a testament to the transformative power of fiction, capable of breaking down barriers and challenging our assumptions. This is a book that will stay with you long after you finish reading it, haunting your thoughts and lingering in your imagination.
