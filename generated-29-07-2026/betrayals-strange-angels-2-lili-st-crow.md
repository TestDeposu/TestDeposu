# Betrayals (Strange Angels, #2)

**Author:** Lili St. Crow  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Lili St. Crow’s 2009 supernatural thriller *Betrayals*, the second installment in the *Strange Angels* series, proves that sophomore slumps are entirely optional. Following the gritty and violent awakening of *Strange Angels*, this sequel plunges its resilient, battle-scarred protagonist deeper into a shadowy world where survival is a daily negotiation. St. Crow’s style remains unapologetically raw, fast-paced, and darkly atmospheric, successfully marrying the sharp-edged bite of modern urban fantasy with the high-stakes tension of young adult fiction. 

At the heart of *Betrayals* lies the enduring theme of identity and isolation. Dru Anderson is no ordinary teenager; orphaned, acutely aware of the monster-infested underbelly of the world, and now burdened with the revelation that her own blood is a dangerous cocktail of human and supernatural elements, she is forced to navigate a landscape where trust is a fatal liability. St. Crow excels at exploring what it means to be the ultimate outsider. Hidden away in a secret Schola meant to train djamphir and wulfen teens, Dru finds herself not among safe peers, but isolated as the sole girl in a volatile den of alpha predators. The narrative uses this claustrophobic setting to amplify the psychological weight of isolation. Dru cannot simply blend in; every look, every whispered rumor, and every training exercise serves as a stark reminder that she does not belong. 

The author’s distinctive narrative style is defined by its hard-boiled, sensory-rich voice. St. Crow writes through Dru’s perspective with a gritty authenticity that feels both visceral and intensely immediate. The prose is peppered with sharp internal monologues, cynical humor, and a palpable sense of impending doom. There is a tactile quality to the action sequences; you can almost smell the ozone, the copper of blood, and the wet fur of the wulfen. Instead of relying on sprawling, overly complex world-building lore dumps, St. Crow drops the reader straight into the grime and panic of Dru’s reality. The danger feels immediate because the narrative voice never lets up; it races forward with the adrenaline-fueled heartbeat of a hunted girl.

Furthermore, *Betrayals* elevates itself above standard paranormal romance fare by focusing heavily on paranoia and psychological suspense. The central plot hook—that one of the inhabitants of the Schola wants Dru dead—transforms the school from a sanctuary into a lethal puzzle box. St. Crow masterfully sows seeds of doubt, making the reader question every alliance Dru forms, particularly regarding the enigmatic and dangerously alluring Christophe. The exploration of loyalty, betrayal, and the heavy price of survival gives the narrative a mature, hard-edged gravity.

Ultimately, *Betrayals* is a striking, deeply satisfying continuation that cements Lili St. Crow as a master of dark, dangerous YA fiction. With a heroine who trades whining for weaponry and a style that crackles with urban grit, this novel is a relentless page-turner that bites back.
