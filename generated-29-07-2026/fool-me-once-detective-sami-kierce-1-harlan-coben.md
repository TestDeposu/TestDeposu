# Fool Me Once (Detective Sami Kierce, #1)

**Author:** Harlan Coben  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

**Fool Me Once: A Masterclass in Twisty Thrillers**

Harlan Coben, the grandmaster of suspense, returns with 'Fool Me Once', a gripping tale that will leave you questioning reality and gasping for breath. This isn't just a thriller; it's a psychological rollercoaster that delves into the depths of memory, guilt, and the human capacity for deception.

At the heart of 'Fool Me Once' lies former detective Sami Kierce, a man haunted by a past he can't remember. Twenty-two years ago, he woke up in a Spanish hostel, covered in blood and holding a knife, with his girlfriend Anna lying lifeless beside him. The memory of that morning is a black hole, and the guilt of his unknown actions has shaped every aspect of his adult life. Now, a new father and a private investigator, Kierce teaches aspiring detectives at night school, a job that offers little solace but keeps him afloat. Until, one evening, Anna walks into his classroom, very much alive.

Coben's storytelling prowess is on full display here. He weaves a labyrinth of suspense, each twist more shocking than the last. The narrative is a relentless pursuit of truth, as Kierce races against time to unravel the enigma of Anna's resurrection. Coben masterfully plays with the theme of memory and its fallibility, blurring the lines between reality and illusion. As Kierce digs deeper, he realizes that nothing is as it seems, and everyone, including himself, is a suspect.

Coben's style is as sharp and engaging as ever. He has a knack for creating flawed, relatable characters who draw you in, making you invested in their plight. Kierce is no exception. He's a man struggling with the weight of his past, a past that's about to catch up with him in the most unexpected way. Coben's prose is crisp, his pacing impeccable, and his dialogue authentic, all of which contribute to a reading experience that's as exhilarating as it is thought-provoking.

'Fool Me Once' is more than just a thriller; it's a psychological exploration of the human mind and its capacity for self-deception. It's a testament to Coben's storytelling genius that he can keep readers on the edge of their seats while also exploring such profound themes. This is a book that will stay with you long after you've turned the last page, leaving you questioning your own perceptions of reality.
