# Hexed (The Iron Druid Chronicles, #2)

**Author:** Kevin Hearne  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

Kevin Hearne proves with *Hexed*, the second installment of The Iron Druid Chronicles, that his breakout urban fantasy debut was no fluke. Returning us to the sun-drenched, supernatural-laden sprawl of Tempe, Arizona, Hearne expands his sandbox with supreme confidence, delivering a page-turning caper that effortlessly blends modern cynicism with ancient myth. If *Hounded* introduced us to Atticus O’Sullivan, the two-thousand-year-old last of the Druids, *Hexed* tests his mettle, his friendships, and his sheer, stubborn will to survive in a world determined to carve him into bite-sized pieces.

At its core, the main theme of *Hexed* is the friction between isolation and allegiance. Atticus has survived for millennia by playing the long game, keeping his head down, and trusting very few entities outside of his brilliant, telepathic Irish Wolfhound, Oberon. Yet, as a hostile foreign coven moves into his territory to challenge the local Sisters of the Three Auroras—compounded by wild Bacchants rolling in from Las Vegas and a fallen angel treating local high schoolers like a buffet—Atticus realizes that isolation is a death sentence. To protect his city and his newly acquired apprentice, he is forced to forge fragile alliances. Hearne deftly explores the cost of these favors, particularly through Atticus’s increasingly complicated, morally grey entanglements with his vampire attorney, Leif Helgarson. The narrative brilliantly argues that true survival isn't about avoiding debts; it's about knowing who to owe and having the wit to pay them back before they consume your soul.

Hearne’s signature style is the true engine driving this frantic, chaotic joyride. Written in a sharp, irreverent first-person perspective, the prose crackles with a snarky, laugh-out-loud wit that constantly undercuts the high-stakes danger. Atticus possesses a snarky internal monologue that feels entirely authentic to someone who has witnessed the rise and fall of empires yet remains deeply invested in pop culture, craft beer, and witty banter. Hearne has a rare talent for world-building via cultural collision; watching ancient Celtic magic butt heads with modern bureaucracy, Las Vegas hedonism, and Judeo-Christian theology results in a wonderfully vibrant pastiche. Furthermore, the inclusion of Oberon’s canine observations provides a brilliant comedic counterpoint to Atticus’s supernatural stress, grounding the fantastical mayhem in earthy, canine humor about poodles, sausages, and squirrels.

The pacing in *Hexed* is relentless. Hearne rarely allows the reader a moment to catch their breath, seamlessly shifting from high-octane action sequences—featuring runic magic, severed heads, and illegal neighborly arsenals—to moments of quiet, character-driven tension. The introduction of diverse mythologies feels organic rather than checklist-driven, with Native American trickster figures, Slavic witches, and classic classical deities all playing vital roles in a cohesive ecosystem of the uncanny.

Ultimately, *Hexed* succeeds because it never takes itself too seriously while managing to make every mortal threat feel entirely genuine. It is a masterclass in urban fantasy pacing and voice, offering a cocktail of myth, mayhem, and modern snark that leaves you eagerly flipping the page to see what sort of trouble the two-thousand-year-old Druid will stumble into next. Hearne has crafted an irresistible universe, and *Hexed* is proof that the Iron Druid is here to stay.
