# The Cost of Discipleship

**Author:** Dietrich Bonhoeffer  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

**The Timeless Call to Discipleship: A Review of Dietrich Bonhoeffer's The Cost of Discipleship**

Dietrich Bonhoeffer's "The Cost of Discipleship" is a profound and thought-provoking exploration of what it means to live a life of true discipleship, as laid out in the teachings of Jesus. Written in 1937, this classic book has stood the test of time, its themes and insights continuing to resonate with readers today. Bonhoeffer's seminal work challenges us to rethink our assumptions about the nature of faith and the costs of following Jesus.

At its core, "The Cost of Discipleship" is a powerful critique of what Bonhoeffer terms "cheap grace." This concept, he argues, is a superficial understanding of Christianity that offers forgiveness and salvation without demanding personal sacrifice or transformation. "Cheap grace," Bonhoeffer contends, is a seductive and ultimately destructive form of faith, one that permits us to "bestow" our own brand of spirituality on ourselves without ever truly surrendering to the will of God.

In contrast, Bonhoeffer proposes the concept of "costly grace." This is a faith that demands complete surrender, one that must be "sought again and again" and "asked for" with humility and desperation. Costly grace, Bonhoeffer writes, "is the gospel which must be sought again and again, the grace which must be asked for, the door at which a man must knock." It is a faith that "costs a man his life" but offers in return "the only true life."

Throughout the book, Bonhoeffer draws on the Sermon on the Mount, one of the most powerful and demanding texts in the New Testament. Here, Jesus lays out a vision of what it means to follow him, one that requires us to renounce our attachments to power, wealth, and status, and to commit ourselves fully to the service of others. Bonhoeffer's analysis of the Sermon on the Mount is masterful, and his insights into the meaning of Jesus' words will challenge and inspire readers to re-examine their own assumptions about the nature of faith.

One of the striking features of Bonhoeffer's writing style is its blend of theological depth and personal urgency. This is a book written by a man who lived what he preached, who faced the ultimate cost of discipleship in his own life and death. As a young pastor in Nazi Germany, Bonhoeffer was a vocal critic of the regime and a key figure in the plot to assassinate Hitler. His courage and conviction in the face of overwhelming opposition are a powerful testament to the transformative power of faith.

Throughout the book, Bonhoeffer writes in a clear and direct style that eschews jargon and technicalities in favor of simplicity, humility, and candor. His prose is often lyrical, and his ability to convey complex theological ideas in simple, accessible terms is a hallmark of his writing. This is a book that will resonate with readers from a wide range of backgrounds and denominations, one that challenges us all to rethink our assumptions about what it means to be a follower of Jesus.

In the end, "The Cost of Discipleship" is a book that demands our attention, our reflection, and our commitment. It is a call to follow Jesus, a challenge to surrender our lives to the demands of costly grace. Bonhoeffer's writing is a reminder that faith is not a safe or easy option, but a bold and radical choice that requires us to surrender our attachments to the status quo and to commit ourselves fully to the service of others.
