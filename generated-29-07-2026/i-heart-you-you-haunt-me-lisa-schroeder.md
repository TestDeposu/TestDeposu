# I Heart You, You Haunt Me

**Author:** Lisa Schroeder  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Love is a force that defies logic, but what happens when it defies the very laws of life and death? In her haunting 2009 debut novel, I Heart You, You Haunt Me, author Lisa Schroeder tackles the agonizing depths of grief and the enduring power of first love. The original synopsis frames the story with deceptive simplicity: Girl meets boy. Girl loses boy. Girl gets boy back... sort of. Yet beneath this playful premise lies an emotionally charged exploration of tragedy, acceptance, and the fragile boundary between the living and the departed.

Schroeder makes a bold stylistic choice that immediately sets this novel apart from standard young adult paranormal romance. The entire book is written in verse. Eschewing traditional prose, Schroeder employs lyrical poetry that mirrors the fragmented, floating state of a grieving mind. At first glance, readers accustomed to conventional narratives might hesitate, but the poetic structure quickly becomes the book’s greatest asset. The rhythm of the verse mimics the racing heartbeat of heartbreak and the breathless whisper of memory. Every line feels intentional, carrying a heavy emotional weight that conventional paragraphs simply cannot achieve. This minimalist approach strips away unnecessary exposition, leaving the raw, bleeding core of Ava’s sorrow exposed for the reader to feel.

The narrative centers on Ava, a young woman shattered by the sudden loss of Jackson, the boy she expected to spend her forever with. When Jackson returns not in the flesh, but as a presence she can only sense in dreams and faint mental echoes, Ava stands at a dangerous crossroads. Is she slipping into madness, refusing to let go, or is love genuinely powerful enough to bridge the eternal divide? Schroeder handles this delicate premise with surprising nuance. Instead of treating the haunting as a mere supernatural gimmick, she frames it as a psychological manifestation of grief. Ava’s inability to move on is deeply relatable; her desperate clinging to Jackson’s ghost is a visceral representation of the denial phase of mourning. 

Through Ava’s journey, the novel’s central theme emerges with crystal clarity: true healing requires letting go, not because you love the person any less, but because you must learn to live again. Jackson’s lingering presence is both a comfort and a prison. Schroeder brilliantly uses the supernatural element to explore the stagnation of grief. As long as Ava lives in the past with a ghost, her future remains frozen. 

The author’s style shines brightest in quiet, agonizing moments. There are no grand, sweeping fantasy battles here; the battlefield is inside Ava’s head and heart. The imagery is stark yet beautiful, utilizing sensory details—the chill in the air, the fading warmth of a dream, the silence of a room that once held laughter—to anchor the reader firmly in Ava’s emotional reality. Schroeder captures the suffocating isolation of loss, showing how the world keeps spinning even when your personal universe has utterly collapsed.

While the book is brief, its impact lingers long after the final page. Critics and readers alike have praised its ability to evoke genuine tears in a matter of chapters. Some might argue that the verse format limits deep character development for supporting figures, but in a story so intimately focused on internal grief, expanding the spotlight would only dilute the power of Ava and Jackson’s bond. 

I Heart You, You Haunt Me is not just a ghost story; it is a profound elegy for lost youth and an ode to the resilience of the human spirit. Lisa Schroeder crafted a poignant, fast-paced, and striking read that proves sometimes the most devastating monsters we face are the memories we refuse to bury. For anyone who has ever loved and lost, this book will feel less like a story being read and more like a secret being whispered straight to your soul.
