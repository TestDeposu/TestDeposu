# Gentle and Lowly: The Heart of Christ for Sinners and Sufferers

**Author:** Dane C. Ortlund  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

Title: The Sanctuary of the Savior: A Review of Dane C. Ortlund’s Gentle and Lowly

In a literary landscape saturated with self-help spirituality and tactical guides on how to better manage our faith, Dane C. Ortlund’s Gentle and Lowly: The Heart of Christ for Sinners and Sufferers arrives not as a manual of what we must do, but as a breathtaking revelation of who He is. Published in 2021, this deceptively slim volume strikes at the very core of Christian devotion by tackling a question that haunts believers and seekers alike: When we stumble, struggle, and suffer, how does Jesus actually feel about us? 

The central theme of Ortlund’s work is the emotional interior of Jesus Christ. Drawing heavily from the rich, subterranean wells of seventeenth-century Puritan writers—such as Thomas Goodwin, Richard Sibbes, and John Bunyan—Ortlund constructs a theological sanctuary for the weary. Far too often, modern believers picture Christ as distant, perpetually disappointed, or begrudgingly forgiving. Ortlund shatters this caricature using direct biblical text, presenting a Savior whose deepest, most reflexive disposition toward humanity is not irritation or cold theological calculus, but profound gentleness and lowliness. 

What makes the book truly striking is Ortlund’s authorial style. He writes with a pastoral warmth that feels less like an academic lecture and more like a whispered reassurance across a quiet room. His prose is spare yet potent, punctuated by short chapters that invite slow, contemplative reading rather than rapid consumption. Ortlund does not merely explain biblical concepts; he escorts the reader directly into the emotional gravity of the New Testament, proving that the heart of Christ is most easily drawn to those who are heavy-laden, broken, and acutely aware of their own sinfulness. 

By leaning heavily on the theological heavyweights of the Puritan era, Ortlund rescues neglected historical wisdom and translates it for a contemporary audience that desperately needs it. He demonstrates that holiness and tenderness are not mutually exclusive in Christ, but perfectly unified. 

Gentle and Lowly is not a book to be merely read and shelved; it is an experience of spiritual re-centering. It dismantles the performance anxiety that so often plagues the religious life and replaces it with the unshakable comfort of a Savior who identifies with our deepest distress. For any sinner carrying the weight of guilt, or any sufferer enduring the crushing silence of pain, Ortlund’s work is an essential, soul-restoring compass pointing home to the heart of Jesus.
