# The Seven Year Slip

**Author:** Ashley Poston  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

The Seven Year Slip feels like a love letter to anyone who has ever tried to protect a bruised heart by building walls of routine and ambition. Clementine, a overworked book publicist, has mapped out a life that checks every practical box—steady work, a sensible partner, the occasional whimsical chase of the moon—yet she keeps love at arm’s length, terrified that another loss will shatter the fragile peace she’s painstakingly assembled after her aunt’s death. When a charming stranger with a Southern drawl and an inexplicable fondness for lemon pies appears in her late aunt’s kitchen, the carefully constructed plan begins to crack. The twist? He isn’t just a fleeting encounter; he lives seven years in her past, while she inhabits his future. Their connection flickers across a temporal seam, turning every shared laugh, every quiet moment over pie, into a poignant reminder that love is less about the seconds we spend together and more about the timing of those seconds.

Ashley Poston’s prose shines with the same wit and warmth that made The Dead Romantics a favorite, but here she layers humor with a deeper, almost melancholic resonance. Her dialogue crackles with authenticity—Clementine’s internal monologue is sharp and self‑aware, while the stranger’s drawl feels like a warm breeze through an open window. Poston treats the speculative element not as a gimmick but as a metaphor for the way grief distorts our perception of time: moments stretch, blur, and sometimes bleed into one another, just as watercolors blend on a damp canvas. The apartment itself becomes a character, a “pinch in time” where past and future coexist, reminding readers that healing often requires us to step into the spaces where our histories overlap.

What makes the novel strike a chord is its balance between hope and honesty. Poston never pretends that love erases pain; instead, she shows how vulnerability can be a courageous act of reclamation. Clementine’s journey—from guarding her heart to daring to let someone in despite the impossibility of their circumstances—mirrors the reader’s own negotiations between safety and the yearning for something more. The novel’s pacing is brisk yet contemplative, allowing the romance to bloom naturally while the underlying themes of loss, forgiveness, and the courage to chase one’s own moon linger long after the final page.

In a market saturated with time‑travel tropes, The Seven Year Slip distinguishes itself by anchoring its fantastical premise in genuine emotional truth. Poston’s voice—witty, wise, and tender—invites us to reconsider how we measure love: not by the years we have, but by the moments we dare to inhabit fully, even when they arrive out of sync.
