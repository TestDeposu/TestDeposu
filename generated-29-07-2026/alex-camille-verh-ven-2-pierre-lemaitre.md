# Alex (Camille Verhœven, #2)

**Author:** Pierre Lemaitre  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

**A Heart-Pounding Race Against Time in Pierre Lemaitre's "Alex"**

Pierre Lemaitre, the French master of suspense, delivers another tour de force with "Alex," the second installment in the Camille Verhoeven series. Lemaitre's unique style, praised by Stephen King himself, is on full display in this gripping thriller that will keep you on the edge of your seat until the very last page.

"Alex" opens with a chilling scenario: a woman, Alex, has gone missing, and the only clue is a cryptic message left behind. Enter Commander Camille Verhoeven, a detective still haunted by his past encounters with a serial killer. As he delves into Alex's life, he realizes that she is no ordinary woman, and her disappearance hides a web of secrets that threaten to ensnare him.

Lemaitre's strength lies in his ability to create complex, flawed characters that feel achingly real. Verhoeven is no exception. Haunted by his past and struggling with his personal life, he is a detective you can't help but root for, even as he makes questionable decisions. Alex, too, is a fascinating character, her strength and vulnerability expertly woven together to keep the reader guessing.

The plot is a masterclass in tension-building. Lemaitre expertly juggles multiple timelines and perspectives, slowly revealing the truth about Alex's disappearance. Each chapter ends with a cliffhanger, propelling the reader forward with a relentless momentum. The story is not for the faint-hearted, with graphic descriptions of violence and a healthy dose of rats that will make you squirm.

But "Alex" is more than just a thrilling ride. It's a profound exploration of human nature, of the darkness that lurks within us all. Lemaitre doesn't shy away from the ugly truths about society and the people who inhabit it. He holds a mirror up to our faces, forcing us to confront the monsters within.

"Alex" is not just a great thriller; it's a literary achievement. Lemaitre's prose is sharp and precise, his pacing impeccable. He understands the rhythm of a thriller, the ebb and flow of tension and release, and he uses it to devastating effect. It's no wonder that "Alex" has been adapted for the screen and has won numerous awards, including the Crime Writers' Association International Dagger Award.

If you're a fan of thrillers, of being kept up at night by a book you can't put down, then "Alex" is a must-read. It's a rollercoaster of emotions, a race against time that will leave you breathless. But be warned: once you start reading, you won't be able to stop. And when you finally reach the end, you'll be left reeling, your heart pounding, and your mind racing.
