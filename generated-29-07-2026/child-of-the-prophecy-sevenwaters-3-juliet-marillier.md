# Child of the Prophecy (Sevenwaters, #3)

**Author:** Juliet Marillier  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

**Child of the Prophecy: A Mesmerizing Finale to the Sevenwaters Trilogy**

In the thrilling conclusion to Juliet Marillier's award-winning Sevenwaters Trilogy, "Child of the Prophecy" weaves a spellbinding tale that masterfully blends Celtic mythology, political intrigue, and a coming-of-age story. The stage is set in ancient Ireland, where magic is waning, and the Old Ones are retreating, their sacred groves falling to the axe and greed of men.

At the heart of this captivating narrative is Fianne, a shy, reclusive sorcerer's daughter, who discovers she is the key to preventing Ireland's magical heart from being extinguished. As the granddaughter of the malevolent sorceress Oonagh, Fianne's path is fraught with danger and self-discovery. Marillier's prose is as enchanting as ever, painting vivid landscapes and imbuing her characters with depth and complexity.

The main theme of "Child of the Prophecy" is the struggle between tradition and modernity, and the consequences of turning away from the old ways. Marillier explores this through the conflict between the Sevenwaters clan, who are bound to the land and its magic, and those who would exploit it for personal gain. Fianne's journey is a testament to the power of knowledge, courage, and love in the face of adversity.

Marillier's signature style shines in her ability to create compelling, relatable characters, even in the face of fantastical elements. She expertly balances suspense, romance, and action, keeping the reader on the edge of their seat until the very end. Her use of Celtic mythology is both reverent and innovative, breathing new life into ancient legends.

"Child of the Prophecy" is a fitting conclusion to the Sevenwaters Trilogy, tying up loose ends while leaving room for interpretation and imagination. It is a testament to Marillier's skill as a storyteller that she can make even the most fantastical elements feel grounded and real.
