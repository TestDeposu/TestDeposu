# Ghost Town (The Morganville Vampires, #9)

**Author:** Rachel Caine  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

In the ninth installment of the Morganville Vampires series, Rachel Caine once again masterfully weaves a captivating tale of supernatural intrigue, suspense, and self-discovery. Ghost Town is a gripping narrative that delves into the complexities of memory, identity, and the true nature of power.

The story picks up where the previous book left off, with Claire Danvers, the brilliant and resourceful heroine, working tirelessly to strengthen Morganville's defenses against the outside world. Her latest experiment, aimed at amplifying vampire mental powers, seems to be the solution the town needs – but at a terrible cost. As the memory field begins to affect not only the humans but also the vampires, Claire must navigate the treacherous landscape of a town where everyone is slowly losing their sense of self.

Caine's writing style is as engaging as ever, with a keen sense of pacing that propels the reader through the twists and turns of the plot. Her characters are multidimensional and relatable, with Claire's inner turmoil and determination making her a compelling protagonist. The supporting cast, including Shane, Eve, and Michael, adds depth and complexity to the story, as they struggle to cope with the effects of the memory field.

One of the most striking aspects of Ghost Town is its exploration of the theme of identity. As the characters begin to forget who they are, they are forced to confront the very essence of their existence. Caine raises important questions about the nature of self and how it is shaped by our memories, experiences, and relationships. The vampires, in particular, are fascinating in this context, as they grapple with the loss of their centuries-old identities and the implications of their newfound mortality.

The author's world-building is also noteworthy, as she expands on the mythology of Morganville and its supernatural inhabitants. The town itself becomes a character, with its own unique personality and history. Caine's vivid descriptions of the town's eerie landscapes and crumbling architecture add to the sense of foreboding and unease that pervades the narrative.

If you're a fan of urban fantasy, supernatural thrillers, or just great storytelling in general, Ghost Town is a must-read. Rachel Caine's writing is addictive, and her characters will stay with you long after you finish the book. While it's possible to read Ghost Town as a standalone novel, the series is best enjoyed in sequence, as the characters and plotlines evolve over time.

In conclusion, Ghost Town is a gripping and thought-provoking addition to the Morganville Vampires series. With its unique blend of supernatural suspense, memorable characters, and philosophical themes, it's sure to captivate fans of the genre. So, if you're ready to immerse yourself in a world of vampire politics, supernatural intrigue, and self-discovery, then Ghost Town is the perfect destination.
