# People in Glass Houses (Ghost Hunters, #16)

**Author:** Jayne Castle  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2006

In People in Glass Houses, the sixteenth installment of her beloved futuristic Ghost Hunters series, Jayne Castle—writing under her celebrated sci-fi romance pseudonym—masterfully blends paranormal suspense, corporate intrigue, and a simmering second-chance romance. Set on the vibrant, dust-swept colony world of St. Helens, Castle invites readers back into the subterranean depths of Cadence City, proving that even after sixteen books, her imaginative well is far from dry.

The central theme of the novel pivots on the dangerous friction between transparency and hidden truths, brilliantly mirrored in the book's title. Elly St. Clair, a resilient botanist trying to reinvent herself in a bustling metropolis, is no stranger to the suffocating weight of secrets. Her past relationship with powerful Guild boss and ghost hunter Cooper Boone imploded precisely because of the shadowy, clandestine nature of his world. Yet, when a dear friend vanishes into the labyrinthine catacombs beneath Cadence City, Elly is forced to shatter her protective walls and rely on the very man she once walked away from. Castle uses the subterranean setting as a brilliant metaphor for the subconscious; the catacombs are literally and figuratively crawling with buried ghosts, forgotten myths, and suppressed histories that refuse to stay in the dark. 

Castle’s signature style shines brightly here, offering a seamless fusion of futuristic world-building and classic romantic suspense. Her prose is brisk, engaging, and delightfully witty, laced with the unique jargon of a colonized planet where dust-bunnies are sentient menaces and psychic talents are a daily utility. Unlike traditional grimdark fantasy, Castle infuses her paranormal mysteries with a comforting warmth, relying on the competency and emotional intelligence of her protagonists. Cooper Boone embodies the quintessential strong, silent archetype, but Castle subverts flat machismo by giving him deep emotional stakes and a fierce loyalty that proves irresistible. The romantic tension is palpable, reignited not just by lingering physical chemistry, but by the necessity of mutual trust in high-stakes environments.

While the procedural elements of the missing person investigation move at a brisk pace, the true heart of the novel lies in the interpersonal healing between Elly and Cooper. Castle explores how love requires emotional architecture—you cannot build a future in glass houses if you are still throwing stones from your past. The plot successfully weaves together local Guild politics, ancient subterranean mythologies, and botanical trivia, creating a richly textured backdrop that feels lived-in and vibrant.

Ultimately, People in Glass Houses is a triumphant entry in a long-running series. It delivers everything franchise fans crave while remaining accessible enough to charm newcomers. Jayne Castle proves once again why she is a master of romantic science fiction, offering a thrilling subterranean adventure that reminds us the scariest ghosts are often the ones we create ourselves.
