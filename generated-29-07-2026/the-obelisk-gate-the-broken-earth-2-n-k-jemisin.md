# The Obelisk Gate (The Broken Earth, #2)

**Author:** N.K. Jemisin  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

The Obelisk Gate, the second installment in N.K. Jemisin's Broken Earth trilogy, is a masterful continuation of the story that began with The Fifth Season. This novel delves deeper into the world of the Stillness, a land ravaged by natural disasters called "Fifth Seasons" that have caused widespread destruction and societal collapse. The story follows two main characters: Essun, a powerful orogene searching for her daughter, and Nassun, the daughter in question, whose abilities are growing stronger by the day.

One of the most striking aspects of Jemisin's writing is her ability to craft complex, multi-dimensional characters. Essun, in particular, is a fascinating protagonist, driven by a mix of motivations that make her both relatable and flawed. Her desire to find her daughter is palpable, but it's also tempered by a deep-seated need for revenge against those who have wronged her. This inner conflict makes her a compelling character to follow, and Jemisin's exploration of her emotional state is both nuanced and thought-provoking.

Nassun, on the other hand, is a more enigmatic figure, and her storyline is both captivating and unsettling. Her growth in power is matched only by her growing sense of isolation, and Jemisin's portrayal of her struggles is both poignant and haunting. The way Nassun's narrative intersects with Essun's is a testament to Jemisin's skill as a storyteller, and the tension that builds between the two is expertly handled.

The world-building in The Obelisk Gate is also noteworthy, with Jemisin expanding on the lore and history of the Stillness. The novel is full of clever details and hints about the world's past, and the way Jemisin slowly reveals more information is both intriguing and rewarding. The Stillness itself is a character in the story, with its own rhythms and patterns that shape the lives of those who inhabit it.

The Obelisk Gate is also a novel about power, and the ways in which it can be both creative and destructive. The orogene characters, with their ability to control and manipulate energy, are a manifestation of this power, and Jemisin's exploration of their role in the world is both thought-provoking and timely. The novel raises important questions about oppression, resistance, and the consequences of unchecked power, and Jemisin's answers are both nuanced and unsettling.

Jemisin's writing style is, as always, a highlight of the novel. Her prose is lyrical and evocative, with a keen attention to detail that brings the world of the Stillness to vivid life. The novel's pacing is well-balanced, with a mix of action, suspense, and introspection that keeps the reader engaged. The Obelisk Gate is a novel that demands attention, but it's also a rewarding and thought-provoking read.

In conclusion, The Obelisk Gate is a magnificent novel that builds on the foundations laid in The Fifth Season. Jemisin's world-building, character development, and exploration of power and oppression make for a compelling read, and her writing style is as captivating as ever. If you're a fan of science fiction, fantasy, or just great storytelling in general, then this novel is a must-read.
