# The Upside of Unrequited (Simonverse, #2)

**Author:** Becky Albertalli  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

In "The Upside of Unrequited," Becky Albertalli brings to life a poignant and relatable tale of self-discovery, love, and sisterhood through the lens of 17-year-old Molly Peskin-Suso. As the second installment in the Simonverse series, this novel is more than a worthy companion to Albertalli's debut, instead forging its own unique path with effortless ease.

Molly's narrative is an honest and authentic exploration of the complexities of adolescence, weaving together the intricacies of sisterly love, first love, and identity. At the heart of this story is Molly's struggle to navigate her own desires and self-perception, particularly in a world where societal expectations often dictate the norms of attractiveness and desirability. Albertalli skillfully tackles these themes, crafting a narrative that not only validates Molly's experiences but also inspires readers to reevaluate their own perceptions of self-worth and body image.

Throughout the novel, Albertalli's masterful touch is evident in her portrayal of Molly's witty, sarcastic, and at times, vulnerable voice. Molly is an expert in observing the world around her, but when she finds herself on the cusp of taking a chance, her insecurities and fears come to the forefront. Her narrative is peppered with clever observations and sharp humor, making "The Upside of Unrequited" an endearing and unforgettable read.

While the romantic relationships in the novel are undoubtedly endearing, it is Molly's relationship with her twin sister, Cassie, that truly stands out. Albertalli beautifully captures the nuances of sisterly love, revealing the complexities and depth of a bond that can be both supportive and suffocating. As Molly and Cassie navigate the changing landscape of their relationship, Albertalli masterfully weaves a narrative that is both poignant and authentic.

One of the most striking aspects of "The Upside of Unrequited" is its ability to balance humor and heartache with equal precision. Albertalli's use of humor is deft and understated, never detracting from the emotional resonance of the narrative. Conversely, when the story demands it, the author's grasp of subtlety is equally impressive, allowing readers to feel Molly's pain and vulnerability without ever becoming mawkish or cloying.

In short, "The Upside of Unrequited" is a triumph of a novel, offering a poignant and relatable portrayal of self-discovery, love, and identity. With Albertalli's signature wit and warmth, this novel is an unforgettable read that will leave readers smiling, laughing, and perhaps, most importantly, reevaluating their own perceptions of self-worth and body image.
