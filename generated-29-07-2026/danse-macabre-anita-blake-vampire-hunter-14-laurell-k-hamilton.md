# Danse Macabre (Anita Blake, Vampire Hunter, #14)

**Author:** Laurell K. Hamilton  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

In "Danse Macabre", the 14th installment of Laurell K. Hamilton's Anita Blake, Vampire Hunter series, the author continues to weave a complex web of supernatural politics, steamy romance, and pulse-pounding action. This collection of books 11-15 in the series offers a deeper dive into Anita's world, where vampires, shapeshifters, and magic are an integral part of everyday life.

Hamilton's writing style is as engaging as ever, with a keen focus on character development and relationships. Anita, the protagonist, is a strong and determined heroine who has evolved significantly since the early days of the series. Her growth is evident in her handling of complex situations, her willingness to take risks, and her unwavering commitment to those she cares about.

One of the primary themes of "Danse Macabre" is the exploration of power dynamics within the supernatural world. Hamilton delves into the intricacies of vampire politics, highlighting the delicate balance of power between the various factions vying for control. Anita, as the necromancer and vampire executioner, finds herself at the center of these power struggles, often forced to navigate treacherous alliances and rivalries.

The author's use of sensuality and eroticism is another hallmark of the series, and "Danse Macabre" is no exception. The relationships between Anita and her various lovers, including Micah, Richard, and Asher, are multifaceted and emotionally charged. Hamilton's portrayal of polyamory is refreshing and realistic, avoiding the pitfalls of romanticizing or trivializing non-monogamous relationships.

The world-building in "Danse Macabre" is meticulous and detailed, with a rich history that underpins the entire series. Hamilton's creation of a supernatural ecosystem, complete with its own rules and hierarchies, is a testament to her skill as a storyteller. The various supernatural creatures, from vampires and shapeshifters to fey and werewolves, are fully realized and integral to the narrative.

If there's a criticism to be made, it's that the series has become increasingly complex, with a vast array of characters and plot threads to keep track of. New readers may find it challenging to jump into the series at this point, and even longtime fans may need to refresh their memories on the various storylines and characters.

In conclusion, "Danse Macabre" is a gripping addition to the Anita Blake, Vampire Hunter series. Hamilton's masterful storytelling, coupled with her willingness to push boundaries and explore complex themes, has resulted in a truly captivating read. Fans of urban fantasy, paranormal romance, and supernatural fiction will devour this collection, and eagerly anticipate the next installment in the series.
