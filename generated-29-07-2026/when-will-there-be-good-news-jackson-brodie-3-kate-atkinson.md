# When Will There Be Good News? (Jackson Brodie, #3)

**Author:** Kate Atkinson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2008

In When Will There Be Good News?, Kate Atkinson achieves something rare in modern crime fiction: she elevates the traditional procedural into a profound literary exploration of fate, trauma, and resilience. As the third installment in her critically acclaimed series featuring former private investigator Jackson Brodie, this 2008 masterpiece does not merely rely on the mechanics of a whodunit. Instead, Atkinson uses the scaffolding of suspense to examine how isolated acts of violence ripple across decades, entangling lives that seemingly have no business crossing paths.

Atkinson is a master of narrative architecture, and her stylistic signature is immediately apparent in the way she plays with time and perspective. The novel opens with a devastating flash of violence on a sun-drenched country lane, altering young Joanna Mason’s life forever. Fast forward thirty years, and the narrative converges through a brilliant orchestration of coincidence and fate. We meet an older, battered Jackson Brodie aboard a delayed, chaotic train, and sixteen-year-old Reggie—a fiercely independent, self-taught teenager working as a mother’s helper, whose pragmatic approach to impending disaster provides both comic relief and emotional anchor. 

The central theme of the novel is brilliantly encapsulated in its poignant title. When Will There Be Good News? is an inquiry into the human capacity to endure relentless darkness. Atkinson’s characters are all haunted by ghosts—loss, abandonment, and past trauma. Yet, rather than wallowing in bleakness, the author infuses the narrative with a gritty, hard-won optimism. The juxtaposition of ordinary domesticity with sudden, terrifying peril creates an atmosphere of simmering tension that never lets up. 

Atkinson’s prose is the true star of the show. She writes with a lyrical sharpness, weaving dark humor through harrowing circumstances. Her characters are drawn with immense psychological depth; they are flawed, bruised, and achingly human. Jackson Brodie remains one of contemporary fiction's most compelling investigators—not because he is an infallible mastermind, but because he is remarkably empathetic, often stumbling into cases by virtue of being in the wrong place at the right time. Meanwhile, the introduction of Reggie stands out as a triumph of character creation, offering a youthful resilience that counterbalances the heavier emotional notes of the book.

Ultimately, Atkinson’s narrative wizardry—as Stephen King aptly noted—lies in her ability to make the improbable feel entirely inevitable. She tricks the reader with seemingly disjointed threads only to weave them into a tapestry of breathtaking emotional and thematic resonance. When Will There Be Good News? is much more than a masterclass in suspense; it is a profound meditation on survival, coincidence, and the enduring quest for redemption in a chaotic world.
