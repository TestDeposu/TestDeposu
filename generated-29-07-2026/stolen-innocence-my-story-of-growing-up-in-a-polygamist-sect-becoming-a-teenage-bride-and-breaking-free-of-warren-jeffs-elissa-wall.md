# Stolen Innocence: My Story of Growing Up in a Polygamist Sect, Becoming a Teenage Bride, and Breaking Free of Warren Jeffs

**Author:** Elissa Wall  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

"Breaking Free: My Story of Leaving the Polygamous Sect, Becoming a Surviving Teenage Bride, and Fleeing from the Tyranny of Warren Jeffs" by Rachel Jeffs is a mesmerizing, harrowing account of life within the Fundamentalist Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints (FLDS) under the authoritarian regime of its notorious leader Warren Jeffs.

On the surface, this memoir appears to be a personal narrative of one woman's courage in the face of abuse and oppression. However, as it delves deeper into the inner workings of the FLDS, the reader is presented with a broader exploration of the consequences of blind faith and the cult-like behavior that can result when a group becomes isolated from the outside world.

At the heart of the book lies a painful and thought-provoking account of the author's experiences, which read like a cautionary tale of how a person's life can be torn apart by the toxic grip of organized abuse. Rachel Jeffs's unflinching narrative takes the reader through her early years within the FLDS, where women are systematically demeaned, subjugated, and forced into marriage at an early age.

As the story unfolds, we gain insight into the rigid social hierarchy that governs the FLDS, where the leadership, led by Warren Jeffs, holds absolute power over its members. Under Jeffs' iron-fisted rule, young girls are often forced into marriages with significantly older men, some of whom engage in predatory behavior. Jeffs' actions are a stark example of the devastating consequences that can arise when the pursuit of power and control takes precedence over human dignity and the well-being of others.

Throughout the book, Rachel Jeffs' narrative is both personal and powerful, offering a glimpse into a secret world where human rights are repeatedly disregarded. Her recollections serve as a testament to her resilience and courage, as she navigates the difficulties of breaking free from a life she was not born into. Jeffs' voice conveys a sense of desperation, anguish, and longing, which propels the reader to become increasingly invested in her journey.

One notable aspect of "Breaking Free" is its well-crafted structure, which allows the author to balance a sensitive portrayal of her experiences with an informative exploration of the FLDS. The writing style is both evocative and unflinching, which adds depth and authenticity to the narrative. Jeffs' use of simple yet evocative language helps to humanize the tragic events described, rendering them even more impactful and thought-provoking.

While some readers may find aspects of the narrative disturbing, others will undoubtedly find it necessary to confront the very real issues that are raised in "Breaking Free." As an account of a personal struggle and a courageous journey of self-discovery, the book serves as a reminder of the strength that exists within individuals who have been subjected to systemic abuse.

Ultimately, "Breaking Free" is a story about survival, resilience, and hope—three elements that are desperately needed when facing the complexities of organized abuse. Through Rachel Jeffs' powerful narrative, we are reminded of the importance of human rights, individual freedom, and our responsibility to challenge and expose systems that perpetuate harm.
