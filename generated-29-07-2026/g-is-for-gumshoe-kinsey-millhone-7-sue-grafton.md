# G is for Gumshoe (Kinsey Millhone, #7)

**Author:** Sue Grafton  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1990

In the crowded landscape of 1990s crime fiction, Sue Grafton’s Kinsey Millhone series stood as a beacon of sharp-witted resilience. With G is for Gumshoe, the seventh installment in her legendary alphabetical series, Grafton maneuvers past the comfort of procedural tropes to deliver a high-stakes thriller that feels both intimate and perilously immediate. 

The brilliance of this particular entry lies in how it balances two seemingly disparate threads: a cold case involving an elderly woman’s suspicious death and a visceral, terrifying vendetta against Kinsey herself. By placing her protagonist in the crosshairs of a ruthless hit man, Grafton strips away the professional detachment Kinsey usually employs. We aren’t just watching a detective solve a mystery; we are watching a survivor navigate a world that has suddenly turned lethal.

Grafton’s style remains as crisp and efficient as her heroine. She writes with a dry, observational wit that never compromises the suspense. Her prose is rhythmic, characterized by a certain clipped energy that mirrors Kinsey’s own guarded nature. Even as the narrative pushes toward a darker, more frantic pace, Grafton maintains that quintessential “Millhone attitude”—a blend of fierce independence and a refusal to be intimidated by a world that often wants her to play it safe.

The introduction of Robert Dietz as a bodyguard is a masterstroke in structural tension. It forces Kinsey to relinquish her precious autonomy—a core component of her character—to someone else. This dynamic adds a layer of emotional vulnerability that distinguishes G is for Gumshoe from its predecessors. The sexual tension between them is handled with a mature restraint, serving as a friction point that underscores how difficult it is for someone like Kinsey to let her guard down, even when the threat of violence is literally standing at her door.

The mystery of Agnes Gersh, the elderly woman at the center of the investigation, serves as a poignant reminder that in Grafton’s Santa Teresa, the past is never truly buried. As Kinsey peels back the layers of Agnes’s supposed senility, she uncovers the kind of quiet, devastating malice that thrives in the shadows of ordinary lives. It is a testament to Grafton’s skill that the resolution of the murder mystery feels just as earned and satisfying as the resolution of the immediate danger posed by Tyrone Patty.

Ultimately, G is for Gumshoe is the moment where the series feels like it truly grows up. It moves beyond the mystery-of-the-week format and delves into the psychological toll of the detective life. Grafton reminds us that justice is rarely simple and often requires a toll that the gumshoe must pay out of her own pocket. For fans of hardboiled fiction who prefer their mysteries laced with human frailty and a dry, biting humor, this remains an essential read. Kinsey Millhone isn't just a detective; she is an icon of the genre, and in this volume, we see exactly why she has earned her place in the crime fiction canon.
