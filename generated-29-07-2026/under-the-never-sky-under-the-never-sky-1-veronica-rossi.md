# Under the Never Sky (Under the Never Sky, #1)

**Author:** Veronica Rossi  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

In the ravaged world of Veronica Rossi's "Under the Never Sky," an unlikely alliance is forged between Aria, a Dweller from the enclosed city of Reverie, and Perry, a Savage from the outer wasteland. This "unforgettable dystopian masterpiece" is a gripping tale of survival, redemption, and the blurred lines between hatred and attraction.

The story is told in alternating chapters, shifting between Aria's and Perry's voices, which provides a unique and immersive reading experience. Rossi masterfully weaves together their perspectives, allowing the reader to witness the evolution of their relationship firsthand. The dual narrative not only adds depth to the characters but also heightens the tension and suspense, as the reader is privy to the innermost thoughts and feelings of both protagonists.

Aria, a privileged Dweller, is exiled to the harsh and unforgiving world outside her city, known as the Death Shop. Her chances of survival are slim, and she is forced to rely on Perry, a Savage and a hunter, to stay alive. Perry, on the other hand, needs Aria's help to achieve his redemption, and their initial animosity towards each other slowly gives way to a tentative trust.

Their relationship is built on a foundation of mutual distrust and hostility, with Aria viewing Perry as a savage and Perry seeing Aria as sheltered and fragile. However, as they navigate the treacherous landscape together, they begin to realize that their preconceived notions are not entirely accurate. The line between hatred and attraction blurs, and they find themselves drawn to each other despite their differences.

Rossi's writing style is descriptive and evocative, painting a vivid picture of the post-apocalyptic world. The Death Shop is a harsh and beautiful landscape, full of danger and uncertainty. The author's use of language is engaging, and the pacing is well-balanced, with moments of high tension and action juxtaposed with quieter, more introspective scenes.

One of the most compelling aspects of the novel is the exploration of the theme of survival versus redemption. Aria and Perry are both driven by their desire to survive, but they also grapple with the moral implications of their actions. Perry, in particular, is haunted by his past mistakes and seeks redemption through his actions. This internal conflict adds depth to the narrative and raises questions about the nature of humanity and what it means to be alive.

The special abilities of the Scires, individuals with unnaturally powerful senses, add an interesting layer to the story. Perry's abilities make him a skilled hunter, but they also make him a target, and he must navigate the dangers of his world while also confronting his own demons.

In conclusion, "Under the Never Sky" is a captivating and immersive read that explores the complexities of human relationships and the struggle for survival in a harsh world. Rossi's writing is engaging, and the dual narrative adds depth and tension to the story. The themes of survival, redemption, and the blurred lines between hatred and attraction make for a compelling read.
