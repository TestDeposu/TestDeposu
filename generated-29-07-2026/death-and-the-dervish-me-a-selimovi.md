# Death and the Dervish

**Author:** Meša Selimović  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1996

"Death and the Dervish" by Meša Selimović is a profound and captivating exploration of faith, society, and the human condition, set against the backdrop of 18th-century Sarajevo under Turkish occupation. This literary masterpiece, now available in its first English translation, is a testament to Selimović's mastery of narrative and his ability to delve into the complexities of the human psyche.

The novel is narrated by Sheikh Nuruddin, a dervish who has retreated from the world to lead a spiritual life in a Sarajevo monastery. His isolation is abruptly shattered when his brother is arrested, forcing him to engage with the corrupt and Kafkaesque world of the Turkish authorities. As Sheikh Nuruddin navigates this treacherous terrain, he is compelled to confront not only the fate of his brother but also his own beliefs and life choices.

Selimović's prose is elegant and evocative, painting a vivid picture of a society torn apart by political and religious strife. His exploration of faith is particularly compelling, as Sheikh Nuruddin's spiritual journey is fraught with doubt and questioning. The Sheikh's struggle to reconcile his beliefs with the harsh realities of the world around him is a universal human experience, making the novel deeply relatable despite its historical and cultural specificity.

At its heart, "Death and the Dervish" is a story about the tension between the individual and society, and the role of faith in navigating this tension. Selimović's exploration of these themes is profound and thought-provoking, making the novel a must-read for anyone interested in literature that challenges and inspires.
