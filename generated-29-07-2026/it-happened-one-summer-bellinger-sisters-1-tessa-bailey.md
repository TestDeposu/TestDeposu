# It Happened One Summer (Bellinger Sisters, #1)

**Author:** Tessa Bailey  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

Tessa Bailey’s 2021 rom-com sensation, It Happened One Summer, takes a classic blueprint—the high-society fish out of water—and infuses it with the kind of high-octane chemistry and sharp contemporary wit that has earned the author a devoted global following. Serving as the first installment in the Bellinger Sisters duology, the novel trades the sun-drenched asphalt of Los Angeles for the moody, salt-sprayed shores of a Pacific Northwest fishing village, delivering an opposites-attract romance that is as emotionally resonant as it is scorching.

At its core, the novel explores the theme of reinvention and shedding the expectations of others. Piper Bellinger is the quintessential Hollywood "It Girl," a glittering facade of designer clothes and tabloid headlines. But when a champagne-soaked rooftop party lands her in a jail cell, her fed-up stepfather cuts off her funds and exiles her and her sister to Westport, Washington, to run their late father’s dive bar. Enter Brendan Taggart, a towering, bearded local sea captain with a face perpetually carved into a scowl and zero patience for Beverly Hills royalty. 

What elevates Bailey’s narrative above standard romantic comedy tropes is her commitment to character depth beneath the glossy, meme-ready surface. Piper is frequently underestimated, both by her family and by Brendan, who assumes her penchant for fashion and luxury equates to a lack of substance. Bailey uses this friction to build a compelling character arc. Piper’s journey in Westport is not about changing who she is, but rather uncovering who she was buried beneath layers of privilege and public expectation. She loves being glamorous, and Bailey refreshingly never asks her to abandon that femininity to prove her worth. Instead, Piper learns that she is resilient, capable, and worthy of real, grounded affection.

On the flip side, Brendan is the epitome of the grumpy-sunshine dynamic executed to absolute perfection. He is fiercely protective, deeply rooted in his community, and fundamentally terrified of losing control—until Piper crashes into his orderly world like a neon-pink hurricane. Their forced proximity in a tiny, gossipy coastal town creates delicious tension. Every interaction between them crackles with snappy banter and palpable physical attraction, showcasing Bailey’s distinct talent for writing steam that feels earned, intense, and deeply tied to the emotional growth of the couple. 

Bailey’s writing style is punchy, sensory-heavy, and unapologetically modern. She paints Westport with tactile details—the smell of brine and stale beer, the biting chill of the ocean wind, the comforting warmth of a crowded local pub—making the fictional town feel like a character in its own right. The dialogue zips along at a breathless pace, balancing laugh-out-loud humor with moments of genuine vulnerability. 

Ultimately, It Happened One Summer is a triumph of contemporary romance. It delivers the ultimate comfort-read experience: a gruff hero brought to his knees, a heroine who discovers her own unbreakable spine, and a love story that proves home isn't just a place, but the person who makes you feel safe. Tessa Bailey has crafted an unforgettable modern fairy tale that deserves every bit of its viral acclaim.
