# Dead Witch Walking (The Hollows, #1)

**Author:** Kim Harrison  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Dead Witch Walking launches Kim Harrison’s long‑running Hollows saga with a punch‑y blend of urban grit, supernatural politics, and a heroine who refuses to be pigeonholed. Rachel Morgan—half‑witch, half‑bounty hunter, all attitude—navigates the neon‑slick underbelly of Cincinnati’s Hollows, a hidden district where vampires, were‑creatures, fae, and humans coexist in a fragile, predator‑eat‑predator truce. The novel’s central theme revolves around the negotiation of personal agency within a world that constantly tries to define you by your bloodline, your magic, or your market value as a freelance enforcer. Rachel’s relentless pursuit of justice—whether she’s dragging a rogue vampire back to the precinct or uncovering a conspiracy that threatens the fragile peace—mirrors the broader struggle for autonomy faced by anyone who feels trapped by societal expectations or inherited labels.

Harrison’s style is unapologetically fast‑paced, laced with sharp, witty dialogue and a first‑person narration that feels like a confessional over cheap whiskey and midnight coffee. She balances razor‑thin action sequences with moments of genuine vulnerability, allowing Rachel’s sarcasm to serve both as armor and a window into her insecurities. The world‑building is immersive without becoming burdensome; Harrison drops readers straight into the Hollows’ bustling night markets, shadowy alleyways, and high‑stakes duels, letting the setting reveal its rules through Rachel’s encounters rather than lengthy exposition. This “show, don’t tell” approach keeps the momentum high and makes the supernatural feel palpably real—vampires aren’t just brooding aristocrats; they’re savvy business owners with their own codes, while witches walk a line between ancient tradition and modern street smarts.

What truly elevates Dead Witch Walking beyond a typical paranormal bounty‑hunter romp is its exploration of identity and moral ambiguity. Rachel’s mixed heritage forces her to constantly question where she belongs, and her jobs often require her to choose the lesser of two evils. Harrison treats these dilemmas with nuance, refusing to paint any faction—human, vampiric, or magical—as wholly good or evil. Instead, she presents a mosaic of motivations where survival, loyalty, and personal gain intertwine, prompting readers to consider what they would sacrifice to protect the people they love—or simply to stay alive.

The novel’s romance threads are subtle yet compelling, threading through Rachel’s interactions with the enigmatic, ivy‑covered vampire Ivy Tamwood and the charming, dangerous living‑dead pixie Jenks. These relationships develop organically, never overshadowing the central plot but adding emotional depth that makes Rachel’s stakes feel personal. Harrison’s humor—often dark, self‑deprecating, and peppered with pop‑culture references—prevents the narrative from becoming overly grim, providing levity that feels earned rather than forced.

In sum, Dead Witch Walking succeeds as a vibrant entry point into the Hollows universe because it marries breakneck action with a thoughtful examination of self‑definition in a world where the lines between monster and mankind are perpetually blurred. Kim Harrison’s confident voice, razor‑sharp dialogue, and richly layered setting create a reading experience that is both exhilarating and resonant—a perfect launchpad for a series that has since captivated millions of urban‑fantasy fans.
