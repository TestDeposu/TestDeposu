# Play (Stage Dive, #2)

**Author:** Kylie Scott  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

**Title: "Play" by Kylie Scott: A Rockin' Romance with Heart and Heat**

Kylie Scott returns with "Play," the highly anticipated sequel to her international bestseller "Lick," delivering a captivating blend of heart, heat, and humor that will leave readers craving more. Scott's signature style shines through in this enemies-to-lovers tale, set against the vibrant backdrop of the music industry.

At the heart of "Play" lies the age-old conundrum of opposites attracting. Mal Ericson, the wild and reckless drummer of the world-famous rock band Stage Dive, needs an image makeover, and fast. Enter Anne Rollins, the strait-laced, financially strapped good girl who agrees to play his temporary girlfriend. Their chemistry is undeniable, but their goals couldn't be more different. Mal seeks a quick fix, while Anne needs a steady income to save her family's business. Scott masterfully navigates this tension, creating a slow-burn romance that's as satisfying as it is steamy.

Scott's strength lies in her ability to craft relatable, flawed characters who grow and evolve throughout the story. Mal, with his tattoos and bad boy persona, is more than just a rock star cliché. He's a man struggling with his past, seeking redemption and a sense of belonging. Anne, too, is more than meets the eye. She's a woman of strength and resilience, determined to save her family's legacy, even if it means playing dress-up with a rock god.

The author's signature wit and banter are on full display in "Play." Scott has a knack for writing dialogue that's as sharp and engaging as it is funny. Mal and Anne's verbal sparring matches are a delight to read, their witty retorts and playful jabs a testament to Scott's ability to create chemistry that feels genuine and organic.

Scott also excels in creating a sense of place. The music industry, with its glitz and glamour, provides a vibrant backdrop for Mal and Anne's story. Scott doesn't shy away from the seedier side of fame, exploring the pressures and pitfalls that come with being a rock star. Yet, she also captures the magic of music, the way it can bring people together and heal even the deepest wounds.

"Play" is more than just a romance novel. It's a story about finding your place in the world, about learning to let go of the past and embrace the present. It's about understanding that sometimes, the people who seem the most different from us are the ones who understand us best. And it's about love - the kind that sneaks up on you when you least expect it, the kind that makes you want to play, to live, to love, even when it scares you.

In the end, "Play" is a testament to Kylie Scott's ability to create stories that are as engaging as they are heartfelt. It's a romance that will make you laugh, cry, and swoon. It's a rockin' good time that you won't want to miss.
