# We Were Eight Years in Power: An American Tragedy

**Author:** Ta-Nehisi Coates  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

In "We Were Eight Years in Power: An American Tragedy," Ta-Nehisi Coates delivers a poignant and unflinching collection of essays that masterfully excavate the complexities of America's racial landscape during the Obama era. This book is a culmination of Coates's most iconic works, including "The Case for Reparations" and "Fear of a Black President," alongside eight new essays that delve into the author's personal experiences, observations, and intellectual growth throughout the Obama administration.

Coates's writing is a symphony of elegance and urgency, as he navigates the tumultuous waters of America's history, from the Reconstruction era to the present day. With unflinching candor, he confronts the reader with the brutal realities of racism, white supremacy, and the haunting legacy of America's unreconciled past. His prose is both deeply personal and universally relatable, making this book an indispensable account of modern America.

One of the most striking aspects of this collection is Coates's ability to weave together seemingly disparate threads of American history, politics, and culture into a cohesive narrative that is both heartbreaking and enlightening. He seamlessly transitions between the intimate and the epic, from his own experiences as a young writer in Harlem to the Oval Office, where he interviews President Obama.

The book's central theme – the tragic echoes of Reconstruction-era America in the Obama era – is a powerful lens through which Coates examines the complexities of American democracy. He argues that the election of Barack Obama, America's first black president, was a fleeting moment of multiracial democracy, followed by a vicious backlash that ultimately led to the election of Donald Trump, whom Coates dubs "America's first white president."

Throughout the book, Coates's intellectual curiosity and passion for justice are palpable, as he grapples with the persistent shadow of America's unreconciled history. He shines a light on the new voices, ideas, and movements that emerged during the Obama era, from the Black Lives Matter movement to the struggle for reparations. His assessment of the 2016 election is both bracingly original and devastatingly accurate, illuminating the tragedy of the Obama era in all its complexity.

Ultimately, "We Were Eight Years in Power" is a vital account of modern America, a searing indictment of the country's failure to confront its racist past, and a powerful testament to the enduring spirit of resistance and activism. Coates's writing is a clarion call to action, urging readers to confront the darkness of America's history and to strive for a more just and equitable future.

In short, this book is a masterpiece of contemporary American literature, a must-read for anyone seeking to understand the complexities of America's racial landscape and the ongoing struggle for justice and equality.
