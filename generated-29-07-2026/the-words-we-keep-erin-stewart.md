# The Words We Keep

**Author:** Erin Stewart  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

In "The Words We Keep," Erin Stewart masterfully weaves a poignant and thought-provoking narrative that delves into the complexities of mental health, the power of art, and the resilience of the human spirit. This beautifully crafted novel is a must-read for anyone who has struggled with anxiety, depression, or the weight of perfectionism.

At its core, "The Words We Keep" is a story about the unspoken words that often linger in the shadows of our minds, waiting to be set free. Lily, the protagonist, has been carrying the burden of her sister's mental health struggles, as well as her own, for far too long. Her journey is one of self-discovery, as she learns to confront her fears, anxieties, and compulsions, and finds solace in the words she's been too afraid to speak.

Stewart's writing style is evocative and immersive, drawing the reader into Lily's world with ease. Her prose is lyrical and expressive, capturing the nuances of mental health with a sensitivity and depth that is both remarkable and refreshing. The author's use of language is deliberate and thoughtful, conveying the turmoil and uncertainty that often accompanies mental illness.

One of the most striking aspects of "The Words We Keep" is its portrayal of the healing power of art. Lily's art project, which involves finding poetry in unexpected places, becomes a source of catharsis and liberation for her. Through this project, Stewart highlights the transformative potential of creative expression, demonstrating how it can help us process our emotions, confront our fears, and find our voice.

The supporting characters in the novel are equally well-crafted, particularly Micah, who becomes a source of support and encouragement for Lily. Their relationship is authentic and tender, and serves as a reminder that we are never alone in our struggles.

Throughout the novel, Stewart tackles themes that are both timely and timeless, including the stigma surrounding mental illness, the importance of seeking help, and the need for empathy and understanding. Her approach is nuanced and compassionate, avoiding simplistic solutions or easy answers.

Ultimately, "The Words We Keep" is a testament to the human capacity for resilience, hope, and healing. It is a powerful reminder that our words, our stories, and our experiences matter, and that by sharing them, we can find connection, comfort, and solace.

In short, "The Words We Keep" is a masterpiece of contemporary young adult literature, one that will resonate with readers long after they finish the book. It is a must-read for anyone who has struggled with mental health, and a powerful reminder of the importance of seeking help, finding our voice, and embracing our true selves.
