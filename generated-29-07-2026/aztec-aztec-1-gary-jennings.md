# Aztec (Aztec, #1)

**Author:** Gary Jennings  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

**A Feast of History and Humanity: Gary Jennings' "Aztec"**

Gary Jennings' "Aztec" is a literary banquet, a rich and robust tale that immerses readers in the vibrant, complex world of the last great native civilization of North America. Narrated by Mixtli-Dark Cloud, a man of humble birth who rises to become a priest and advisor to the Aztec emperor, this epic novel is not just a historical account, but a compelling human drama that explores the heights and depths of Aztec civilization.

Jennings' prose is as vivid and colorful as the Aztec world he depicts. He paints with words, creating intricate tapestries of sound, scent, and sight that transport readers to the bustling markets of Tenochtitlan, the bloody sacrificial altars, and the quiet moments of intimacy and reflection. His research is meticulous, yet he wears his learning lightly, weaving historical fact into the narrative with a deft touch that never feels like a lecture.

At the heart of "Aztec" is the story of Mixtli, a character as memorable as any in modern fiction. Jennings has crafted a hero who is both deeply human and larger than life, a man who navigates the treacherous waters of Aztec politics with courage, cunning, and compassion. Through Mixtli's eyes, we witness the grandeur and the brutality of Aztec society, the intricate web of alliances and rivalries that shaped its destiny.

Jennings doesn't shy away from the darker aspects of Aztec culture, most notably the practice of human sacrifice. He presents these acts not as sensationalistic horrors, but as complex rituals rooted in religious belief and political necessity. He challenges readers to understand, if not condone, these practices within their historical and cultural context.

The novel's scope is vast, spanning decades and covering the rise and fall of the Aztec empire. Yet Jennings never loses sight of the personal stories that give this history its heart. From Mixtli's humble beginnings to his role in the empire's final days, "Aztec" is a story of heroism, dignity, and the indomitable human spirit.

"Aztec" is more than just a historical novel; it's a testament to the power of storytelling to bridge the gaps of time and culture. Jennings has given voice to a civilization often misunderstood or misrepresented, creating a narrative that is as engaging as it is enlightening. This is a book to be savored, a feast for the mind and the soul.
