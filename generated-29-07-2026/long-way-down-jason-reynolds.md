# Long Way Down

**Author:** Jason Reynolds  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Jason Reynolds's Long Way Down is a heart-wrenching and thought-provoking novel that delves into the harsh realities of teenage gun violence. The story takes place over the course of sixty seconds, as fifteen-year-old Will stands in an elevator, deciding whether or not to avenge his brother's murder. With a gun shoved in the back waistband of his jeans, Will is driven by the rules of his neighborhood: no crying, no snitching, revenge.

Through Reynolds's masterful use of short, staccato narrative verse, the reader is transported into Will's world, where the lines between right and wrong are constantly blurred. As the elevator stops on each floor, a new character enters, each with their own story to tell and their own connection to Will's brother. These encounters slowly unravel the truth about Shawn's death, forcing Will to confront the complexity of the situation and the devastating consequences of his actions.

Reynolds's writing style is both poignant and powerful, conveying the intensity and urgency of Will's situation. The use of free verse poetry adds to the sense of immediacy, making the reader feel like they are experiencing the story in real-time. The language is raw and unflinching, reflecting the harsh realities of life in the inner city. At the same time, Reynolds's prose is infused with a deep sense of empathy and understanding, making it impossible not to feel a connection to Will and the people he encounters.

One of the most striking aspects of Long Way Down is its exploration of the cyclical nature of violence. As Will navigates the elevator, he is confronted with the ghosts of his past, each one representing a different aspect of the violence that has defined his life. Through these encounters, Reynolds highlights the ways in which violence can become a self-perpetuating cycle, with each act of revenge leading to further violence and devastation.

At the same time, the novel offers a message of hope and redemption. As Will grapples with the consequences of his actions, he begins to see that there are other ways to deal with his emotions, other ways to respond to the violence that has torn his life apart. This is a powerful and timely message, one that resonates deeply in a world where gun violence has become all too commonplace.

In short, Long Way Down is a masterpiece of contemporary young adult literature. Reynolds's writing is both beautiful and brutal, conveying the complexity and urgency of the issues he tackles. The novel is a must-read for anyone looking to understand the harsh realities of teenage gun violence and the ways in which it can be prevented. With its powerful themes and stunning prose, Long Way Down is a book that will stay with you long after you finish reading it.
