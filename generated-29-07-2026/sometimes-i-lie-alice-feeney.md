# Sometimes I Lie

**Author:** Alice Feeney  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

In the realm of psychological thrillers, Alice Feeney's "Sometimes I Lie" stands out as a masterclass in weaving together a complex narrative that expertly navigates the blurred lines between truth and deception. This New York Times and international bestselling novel has captivated readers worldwide with its ingenious plot and razor-sharp characterization.

At its core, "Sometimes I Lie" is a gripping tale about the fragility of the human mind and the malleability of memory. Protagonist Amber Reynolds, a young woman trapped in a coma, recounts her past with an eerie precision, her words dripping with a calculated menace. As Amber's account toggles between her paralyzed present, the tumultuous week leading up to the accident, and a series of cryptic childhood diaries, the story unfolds like a tantalizing puzzle.

Through Amber's narrative, Feeney masterfully exposes the dark underbelly of a seemingly idyllic marriage, cleverly manipulating the reader's perception of reality. With each twist and turn, the lines between truth and fabrication grow increasingly tenuous, forcing the reader to question the reliability of the narrator and second-guess their own perceptions.

Feeney's writing is deceptively simple, yet devilishly effective in conjuring an atmosphere of unease and foreboding. Her prose is as fluid as it is economical, effortlessly skipping between Amber's recollections, the events that led up to the accident, and the ominous undertones of the hospital environment. This deft narrative control is a hallmark of Feeney's skill as a writer, imbuing "Sometimes I Lie" with an irresistible momentum that keeps readers guessing until the very end.

One of the most striking aspects of "Sometimes I Lie" is its unflinching exploration of the human psyche. Feeney's portrayal of Amber, a complex, multidimensional character, is both captivating and unnerving, revealing the cracks in her mental armor as she struggles to maintain a fragile grip on reality. As the story hurtles toward its climax, the reader is left to ponder the veracity of Amber's claims, and the reliability of their own perceptions.

In short, "Sometimes I Lie" is a diabolically clever thriller that will have you questioning everything from the opening pages. With its intricate plot, razor-sharp characterization, and Feeney's unflinching examination of the human psyche, this novel is a must-read for fans of the genre.
