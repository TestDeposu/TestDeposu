# Mortal Coil (Skulduggery Pleasant, #5)

**Author:** Derek Landy  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

In the landscape of middle-grade and young-adult fantasy, few series have managed to maintain the breakneck momentum of Derek Landy’s Skulduggery Pleasant. By the time we reach the fifth installment, Mortal Coil, the stakes have shifted from mere global salvation to the terrifying erosion of personal identity. While Landy has always excelled at blending gothic horror with snappy, noir-inspired dialogue, this entry marks a pivotal, chilling maturation of the series’ core themes.

At the heart of Mortal Coil lies a singular, paralyzing question: How can you fight an enemy that wears the faces of the people you love? The introduction of the Remnants—shadowy, parasitic entities capable of possessing any living host—transforms the narrative from an action-adventure into a claustrophobic psychological thriller. Landy weaponizes trust, forcing his protagonists into a corner where paranoia becomes the only logical defense. For Valkyrie Cain, the tension is twofold; she is not only dodging external threats but also grappling with the gnawing, dark prophecy regarding her own potential for destruction. This internal conflict grounds the fantastical stakes, making the reader feel the weight of every secret kept and every bond strained.

Landy’s prose remains as sharp as a razor’s edge. His ability to balance the macabre with the comedic is a tightrope act that would topple a less confident writer. He possesses an uncanny knack for grounding grotesque, nightmare-fuel imagery—skeletal detectives, body-snatching spirits, and ancient, dusty magics—with a sense of irreverent, Dublin-infused wit. One moment you are recoiling at the visceral horror of a Remnant takeover, and the next, you are laughing at Skulduggery’s unflappable, deadpan dry delivery. This tonal shift is essential; it provides the reader with a necessary breathing room before Landy plunges them back into the fray.

What makes Mortal Coil stand out in the wider arc of the series is its exploration of the "hero’s burden." Valkyrie is no longer a wide-eyed novice; she is a young woman burdened by the knowledge of her own potential monstrosity. Landy writes this transition with surgical precision, showing us that the most dangerous monsters aren't always the ones lurking in the Midnight Hotel, but the ones we fear we might become.

For fans of the series, this book serves as a masterclass in tension building. Landy does not offer his characters easy exits or convenient resolutions. Instead, he traps them in a web of shifting loyalties and shattered perceptions. If you have been following the journey of the living and the dead, Mortal Coil is the point of no return—a dark, exhilarating, and deeply atmospheric chapter that proves why Derek Landy remains a titan of the genre. It is a story about the fragility of the self, the toxicity of secrets, and the terrifying realization that in a world of magic, the most dangerous thing you can do is let your guard down.
