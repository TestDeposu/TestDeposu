# Death du Jour (Temperance Brennan, #2)

**Author:** Kathy Reichs  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1999

In Death du Jour, her triumphant 1999 sophomore novel, Kathy Reichs proves that her debut was no fluke, cementing her place as a master of the medical thriller. Relocating her formidable protagonist, forensic anthropologist Dr. Temperance Brennan, from the chilling familiarity of Montreal to the sultry, oppressive heat of Charleston, South Carolina, Reichs delivers a taut, pulse-pounding narrative that brilliantly examines a chilling central theme: the commodification of human life and the grotesque calculus of modern exploitation.

The novel opens with academic monotony—Tempe is begrudgingly running an archaeological field school—before violently pivoting into terror. Amidst a collection of ancient bones, Tempe uncovers a distinctly modern skeleton. This gruesome anomaly acts as a narrative key, unlocking a Pandora's box of local disappearances, international corruption, and a sinister human trafficking ring operating right under the guise of benevolence. Reichs uses this discovery to explore the moral rot lurking beneath the veneer of respectable society. The central conflict forces Tempe to confront a sickening paradox: a world where vulnerable people are treated as disposable commodities, their bodies harvested or discarded for profit by those who preach salvation. 

What elevates Death du Jour far above standard genre fare is Reichs’s signature style. As a working forensic anthropologist herself, Reichs injects her prose with a staggering level of authenticity. She does not merely describe a crime scene; she dissects it. The language of bones, tissue, and decomposition is rendered with clinical precision, yet it never feels dry. Instead, Reichs translates the cold science of death into a vibrant, urgent quest for justice. The meticulous detailing of forensic procedures grounds the high-stakes suspense in reality, making every revelation hit with visceral impact. 

Yet, Reichs balances this hard-edged clinical realism with messy, deeply human emotional stakes. Tempe Brennan is a compellingly flawed protagonist—brilliant in the lab, yet frequently overwhelmed in her personal life. In Charleston, Reichs complicates Tempe's world by weaving in romantic friction, forcing her to navigate the lingering embers of an old flame with Detective Galiano while mentally tethered to her complicated relationship with Detective Andrew Ryan back in Canada. This personal turbulence adds a layer of emotional vulnerability that prevents Tempe from becoming a mere procedural automaton. 

The pacing of Death du Jour is relentless. Reichs expertly ratchets up the tension as Tempe investigates a free street clinic, locking horns with charismatic televangelists and shady medical practitioners. The suspects are vividly drawn, dripping with hypocrisy and menace, driving a plot that operates on both a local and a terrifyingly global scale. The writing crackles with sharp dialogue and atmospheric descriptions that capture the suffocating, humid dread of the American South, contrasting sharply with the sterile environment of the autopsy room.

Ultimately, Death du Jour is a masterclass in suspense. Kathy Reichs has crafted a smart, expertly paced thriller that satisfies the intellect while gripping the gut. For fans of forensic mysteries and intricate plots driven by fierce, intelligent heroines, this novel remains an essential, riveting read.
