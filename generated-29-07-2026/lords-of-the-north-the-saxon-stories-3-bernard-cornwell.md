# Lords of the North (The Saxon Stories, #3)

**Author:** Bernard Cornwell  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

'Lords of the North', the third installment in Bernard Cornwell's Saxon Stories series, is a triumph of historical fiction that weaves an epic tale of war, loyalty, and self-discovery. Set in 878, the novel picks up where its predecessor 'The Pale Horseman' left off, following the journey of Uhtred, the dispossessed son of a Northumbrian lord, as he navigates the treacherous landscape of post-Viking England.

Cornwell's masterful storytelling is on full display in 'Lords of the North', as he masterfully conjures up a world of intrigue, violence, and deception. Uhtred, now free from his allegiance to the ungrateful King Alfred, must navigate his way through the treacherous world of the North, where his stepsister, a prisoner of the brutal Kjartan the Cruel, awaits her salvation. Cornwell's vivid descriptions of the brutal battles and bloody skirmishes only serve to heighten the tension, drawing the reader into the heart of the action.

At the centre of this epic tale is Uhtred, a complex and multifaceted character that Cornwell brings to life with precision and nuance. As Uhtred embarks on his perilous journey, he is forced to confront his own demons, including his troubled past and his complicated relationships with those around him. Cornwell's exploration of Uhtred's psyche is nothing short of remarkable, offering a nuanced and compelling portrayal of a man caught between his loyalty to his adopted family and his duty to his people.

One of the defining features of Cornwell's writing style is his ability to balance historical accuracy with the demands of storytelling. In 'Lords of the North', he effortlessly weaves together fact and fiction, creating a world that is both meticulously researched and utterly compelling. Cornwell's use of language is characteristically vivid and evocative, conjuring up a world of medieval England that is both familiar and yet utterly alien.

One of the standout features of 'Lords of the North' is its ability to evoke a sense of place and time. Cornwell's descriptions of the rugged landscape of Northumbria, with its windswept moors and foreboding castles, are nothing short of breathtaking. Similarly, his portrayal of the characters, from the ruthless Kjartan to the enigmatic Hild, is equally impressive, bringing to life a cast of characters that are at once memorable and believable.

In short, 'Lords of the North' is a triumph of historical fiction that is not to be missed. With its pulse-pounding action, complex characters, and vivid descriptions of medieval England, it is a must-read for fans of the genre. Cornwell's masterful storytelling and nuanced exploration of Uhtred's psyche make for a compelling and unputdownable read that will leave you begging for more.
