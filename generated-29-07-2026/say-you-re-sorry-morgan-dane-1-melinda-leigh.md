# Say You're Sorry (Morgan Dane, #1)

**Author:** Melinda Leigh  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

Melinda Leigh’s gripping psychological thriller, Say You're Sorry, plunges the reader into a chilling exploration of memory, identity, and the terrifying realization that the past is never truly buried. Published in 2010, this novel establishes Leigh’s signature style: a relentless narrative pace married to deep, visceral psychological suspense. While the publisher’s promotional material bizarrely weaves in unrelated blurbs praising author Peter James, the true beating heart of this book belongs entirely to Leigh’s distinct mastery of domestic unease and supernatural-tinged dread.

At the core of the novel is a compelling thematic exploration of the unreliability of human memory. When protagonist Charley and her husband, Tom, relocate to the seemingly idyllic Elmwood Mill, Charley is immediately struck by an eerie, visceral sense of déjà vu. What begins as a passing, unsettling familiarity quickly spirals into a full-scale psychological crisis as sinister, vivid memories of a life she never lived begin to haunt her waking hours. Leigh uses this premise to toy brilliantly with the boundary between the rational and the irrational. The central theme examines how trauma and repressed history can manifest physically and psychologically, haunting the present until the truth is dragged into the harsh light of day. 

Leigh’s writing style is punchy, atmospheric, and mercilessly efficient. She wastes no time on superfluous exposition, preferring instead to immerse the reader directly into Charley’s mounting paranoia. The author excels at sensory details—the rushing sound of the mill stream, the oppressive stillness of the outbuildings, and the cold sweat of sudden panic. As Charley is pushed toward hypnosis in a desperate bid for answers, Leigh ramps up the tension, turning a quiet suburban dwelling into a claustrophobic trap. Every chapter acts as an escalating shock, keeping the audience off-balance and guessing whether Charley is losing her grip on sanity or uncovering a long-dormant nightmare.

Ultimately, Say You're Sorry is a masterclass in modern suspense. Melinda Leigh crafts a taut, highly readable narrative that forces both her characters and her readers to confront a terrifying question: What do you do when your own mind becomes your worst enemy? It is a sharp, atmospheric page-turner that will linger in your thoughts long after the final chapter.
