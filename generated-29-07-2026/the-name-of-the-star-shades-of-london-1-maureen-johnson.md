# The Name of the Star (Shades of London, #1)

**Author:** Maureen Johnson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

London has always been a city of ghosts—both literal and historical—and in Maureen Johnson’s 2011 atmospheric thriller, *The Name of the Star*, that duality is brilliantly exploited. As the opening installment of the *Shades of London* series, the novel takes a timeless historical terror and transplants it into the bustling, modern-day capital, crafting a narrative that is as deeply unsettling as it is wildly entertaining. 

The story follows Rory Deveaux, a sharp-witted Louisiana teenager who crosses the Atlantic to attend a prestigious boarding school in London, seeking a fresh start. Instead, she steps directly into a waking nightmare. A copycat killer begins carving a bloody path through the city, meticulously mimicking the gruesome modus operandi of Jack the Ripper from the autumn of 1888. As "Rippermania" tightens its grip on the metropolis, the authorities are left grasping at straws. The killer leaves no forensic evidence, no viable leads, and no witnesses—save for one. 

Rory catches a fleeting glimpse of the man police suspect to be the perpetrator. The catch? She is the sole person who can see him. Even her roommate, standing mere inches away, remains oblivious to his presence. This chilling revelation plunges Rory into a hidden world beneath the surface of the city, forcing her to confront not only a modern-day monster but also terrifying, inexplicable abilities of her own.

At its core, the main theme of *The Name of the Star* is the inescapable weight of history and the ways the past bleeds into the present. Johnson masterfully explores how ancient traumas can manifest anew in contemporary settings. London itself becomes a primary character in the novel—glorious, ancient, and deeply menacing. The author strips away the romanticized tourist veneer of the city, replacing it with fog-drenched alleys, historic architecture hiding dark secrets, and an underlying tension that pulses through every chapter. By bridging the gap between Victorian-era horror and twenty-first-century supernatural mystery, Johnson explores how society’s morbid fascination with true crime and serial killers remains unchanged across centuries.

Johnson’s writing style is one of the book’s greatest assets. She balances high-stakes suspense with a distinctly youthful, authentic voice. Rory is a refreshing protagonist—resilient, sarcastic, and deeply observant, making her easy to root for as the danger escalates. The pacing is expertly calibrated, moving from the disorienting culture shock of a Southern teenager in the UK to a breathless, heart-pounding cat-and-mouse game. Johnson employs sharp dialogue, vivid sensory details, and a slow-burn atmospheric dread that keeps the pages turning late into the night. 

While the supernatural elements introduce a high-concept twist to the traditional police procedural, Johnson grounds the narrative in emotional realism. The fear feels tangible, the stakes feel high, and the mystery genuinely baffles until the very end. 

*The Name of the Star* is a masterclass in modern YA thriller writing. It seamlessly merges historical lore with paranormal suspense, resulting in a gripping page-turner that leaves readers desperate to walk the foggy streets of London—and perhaps keep a very close eye on the shadows.
