# Torn (Trylle, #2)

**Author:** Amanda Hocking  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

With *Torn*, the second installment in the wildly popular *Trylle* trilogy, indie publishing phenomenon Amanda Hocking deepens the mythos of her hidden magical world while steering her protagonist directly into the tempestuous waters of young adult responsibility. Following the breakout success of *Switched*, Hocking returns to the secret society of changelings with a narrative that pivots heavily away from mere discovery and plunges straight into the grueling mechanics of duty, power, and sacrifice. 

The central theme of *Torn* is unmistakable: the agonizing friction between personal desire and sovereign obligation. Wendy Everly is no longer just a teenager grappling with the revelation that she is a changeling switched at birth; she is a pivotal figure in an impending war between the Trylle and their fierce rivals, the Vittra. Hocking uses Wendy’s journey to explore how leadership demands the stifling of the heart. To save her people, Wendy must embrace her burgeoning magical abilities and accept a politically advantageous marriage—a mandate that forces her to actively suppress her authentic feelings. 

This thematic conflict is sharply mirrored in the novel's prominent romantic tension, which serves as the emotional beating heart of the book. Hocking crafts a classic, high-stakes YA love triangle, pitting the steadfast, forbidden royal bodyguard Finn against Loki, the alluring, enigmatic Vittra prince. Rather than feeling like a superfluous distraction, this duality embodies Wendy’s internal division. Finn represents the safe, dutiful path tied to her heritage, while Loki represents the dangerous, magnetic pull of the unknown and the enemy. Through these relationships, Hocking examines the cost of loyalty and the rebellion inherent in following one's own desires. 

Hocking’s writing style remains a prime example of accessible, fast-paced contemporary fantasy. Her prose is unpretentious, prioritizing breathless pacing and immediate emotional resonance over dense world-building exposition. This stylistic choice makes the Trylle universe feel remarkably intimate. Hocking excels at snappy dialogue and high-tension domestic and political squabbles within the royal courts, ensuring that the stakes never feel abstract. Even amidst impending warfare and magical training, the narrative remains grounded in the universally relatable insecurities and yearnings of adolescence. 

While *Torn* occasionally leans into familiar tropes of the YA genre, its charm lies in Hocking’s earnest execution and the sheer momentum of the plot. Wendy is a flawed, remarkably human protagonist who makes frustrating mistakes just as often as she displays heroic resilience, making her growth all the more rewarding to witness. Furthermore, the inclusion of the bonus short story, "One Day, Three Ways," serves as a delightful treat for devoted fans, expanding the atmospheric richness of the Trylle lore.

Ultimately, *Torn* is a gripping, emotionally charged bridge in a trilogy that captures the growing pains of a young woman stepping into absolute power. Amanda Hocking successfully raises the stakes, leaving readers thoroughly invested in the fate of a kingdom—and a heart—hanging precariously in the balance.
