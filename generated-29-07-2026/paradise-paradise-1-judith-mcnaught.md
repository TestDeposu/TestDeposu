# Paradise (Paradise, #1)

**Author:** Judith McNaught  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

Judith McNaught has long been celebrated as a titan of contemporary romance, a writer capable of blending high-stakes corporate drama with searing emotional vulnerability. In Paradise, the first novel in her iconic series published in 2016, McNaught operates at the absolute peak of her storytelling powers. She takes the classic tropes of second-chance romance and class division, elevating them through razor-sharp dialogue, complex character psychology, and a sweeping narrative arc that leaves a lasting impression on the reader.

At the heart of Paradise lies the volatile, magnetic collision between Matthew Farrell and Meredith Bancroft. Matthew, a ruthless and self-made corporate raider from a gritty Indiana steel town, is poised to execute the ultimate hostile takeover of the legendary Bancroft department store empire in Chicago. Standing in his way—and simultaneously drawing him into a dangerous game of professional and personal chess—is Meredith Bancroft, the cool, sophisticated heiress to the dynasty. Years prior, these two fiery souls shared a brief, intensely passionate marriage that imploded amidst a devastating betrayal. Now, fate has thrust them back into each other's orbits, forcing them to navigate the treacherous waters where ruthless ambition meets undeniable chemistry.

McNaught’s primary thematic exploration in Paradise centers on the immense chasm between social class and pride, and how these external forces warp authentic human connection. Matthew’s relentless drive to conquer the corporate world is not merely about wealth; it is a profound psychological crusade born from a childhood of poverty and rejection, a desperate need to prove his worth to an elite society that once cast him aside. Meredith, conversely, is a prisoner of her own privilege, bound by family expectations, propriety, and the crushing weight of a legacy she never asked to carry. Their initial romance failed because they lacked the maturity to bridge their vastly different worlds. McNaught brilliantly uses the high-stakes corporate battlefield as a mirror for their internal conflicts, showing that true reconciliation requires both characters to strip away their armor, confront their deepest insecurities, and forgive the unforgivable.

What truly distinguishes McNaught’s style is her ability to infuse intense sensuality with profound emotional stakes. As the Chicago Tribune aptly noted, McNaught comes close to an Edith Wharton edge in her acute observations of upper-class society, yet her prose remains deeply accessible and relentlessly gripping. She avoids simple heroes and heroines; Matthew can be harsh, uncompromising, and driven by vengeance, while Meredith possesses a formidable pride that often blinds her to the nuances of Matthew's pain. Yet, McNaught renders them both so utterly human and fiercely loyal that readers cannot help but root for their turbulent journey toward redemption. The pacing is relentless, balancing the intricate, jargon-filled mechanics of a high-stakes business takeover with moments of breathtaking intimacy, sharp wit, and agonizing emotional confrontation.

Paradise is a masterclass in emotional storytelling, proving why Judith McNaught remains a gold standard in the romance genre. It is a sensual, sweeping epic that reminds us that love, when forged in the fires of adversity, is the most powerful force on earth. For readers who crave a story with high emotional stakes, unforgettable characters, and a love affair that defies the odds, this novel is an absolute must-read.
