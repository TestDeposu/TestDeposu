# Rebel Angels (Gemma Doyle, #2)

**Author:** Libba Bray  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2005

**Rebel Angels: A Haunting Dance of Magic and Mortality**

In the enchanting sequel to "A Great and Terrible Beauty," Libba Bray weaves a captivating tale that masterfully blends Victorian society's opulence with the ethereal allure of the realms. "Rebel Angels" is a mesmerizing journey that explores the depths of friendship, the power of love, and the terrifying price of magic.

Gemma Doyle, our intrepid heroine, finds herself torn between the glittering allure of London society and the haunting visions that beckon her to the realms. Bray's prose is as rich and decadent as the Christmas pudding Gemma indulges in, painting vivid images of both the gilded cage of high society and the otherworldly beauty of the realms. Yet, beneath this sumptuous surface lies a darker truth, one that Gemma must confront if she is to save herself and her friends.

At the heart of "Rebel Angels" lies the complex theme of friendship and the lengths one would go to protect it. Gemma, Felicity, and Ann are bound together by their shared experiences in the realms, but it is their love for each other that forms the unbreakable chain that ties them. Bray expertly explores the nuances of this bond, highlighting the sacrifices made in the name of friendship and the heart-wrenching choices that must be faced.

Bray's signature style is evident throughout, with her signature wit and clever dialogue providing moments of levity amidst the tension. Yet, it is her ability to create atmosphere that truly sets her apart. The realms are a place of both wonder and terror, a reflection of Gemma's own internal struggle. Bray's prose is as haunting as the ghostly figures that plague Gemma's visions, drawing the reader in and refusing to let go.

"Rebel Angels" is not just a story of magic and mystery, but a tale of growth and self-discovery. Gemma must confront her own fears and insecurities, learning to embrace her power and accept the consequences that come with it. Bray handles this journey with sensitivity and grace, creating a character who is both relatable and inspiring.

In conclusion, "Rebel Angels" is a must-read for fans of the Gemma Doyle trilogy, but also for anyone who enjoys a story that combines history, magic, and a healthy dose of girl power. Bray's unique style and compelling narrative make this book a standout in the young adult genre.
