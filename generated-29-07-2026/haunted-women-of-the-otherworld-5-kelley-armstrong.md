# Haunted (Women of the Otherworld, #5)

**Author:** Kelley Armstrong  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2005

Haunted dives back into Kelley Armstrong’s Otherworld with a ghost‑tangled twist that feels both familiar and freshly unsettling. Eve Levine—half‑demon, black witch, devoted mother who cheated death, impossibly wifely mortality by dying three years ago—now haunts a lovely suburban home, juggling an after‑life love life and the relentless worry of reaching her living daughter, Savannah. Armstrong wastes no time thrusting Eve into a cosmic debt: the Fates have called in a favor, demanding she track down the Nix, a malevolent spirit escaped from Hell who thrives on chaos and seduces mortals into murder.

The novel’s core theme pulses through Eve’s struggle to balance maternal love with an impossible quest. Her ghostly state forces her to confront what it means to protect a child when you can’t touch them, to seek redemption not through saintly virtue but through stubborn, flawed determination. Armstrong frames the Nix not just as a external monster but as a mirror of Eve’s own inner turmoil—temptation, rage, and the seductive pull of power that comes with being half‑demon. The hunt becomes a crucible where Eve must decide whether to cling to the darkness that defines her or to forge a new kind of strength rooted in love and sacrifice.

Armstrong’s style remains crisp and kinetic, propelling the narrative with snappy dialogue and tight, scene‑driven chapters that never linger too long in exposition. She blends urban‑fantasy grit with a darkly wry humor that lets Eve’s sardonic voice shine even amid hell‑spawned danger. World‑building feels lived‑in: the after‑life bureaucracy of the Fates, the eerie whisper of the Nix’s influence, and the familiar haunts of Otherworld allies (werewolves, witches, vampires) all interlock seamlessly, giving seasoned readers comfort while inviting newcomers into the mythos.

What elevates Haunted beyond a standard paranormal chase is its emotional core. Armstrong never lets the supernatural spectacle overshadow the very human (or half‑human) yearning at its heart—Eve’s desperate bid to be present for Savannah, even from beyond the veil. The angel’s sword, the only weapon capable of ending the Nix, becomes a potent symbol: true power isn’t inherent to lineage but is wielded through choice, love, and the willingness to risk everything for someone else.

In a series celebrated for its fierce heroines, Eve stands out as a study in contradictions—dead yet nurturing, demonic yet devout, vengeful yet vulnerable. Armstrong captures that tension with a deft hand, delivering a fast‑paced, emotionally resonant installment that reinforces why the Women of the Otherworld saga remains a staple of modern urban fantasy.
