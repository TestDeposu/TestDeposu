# The Book of Cold Cases

**Author:** Simone St. James  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

Simone St. James has carved out a distinct and atmospheric niche in contemporary thriller fiction, blending the compulsive readability of modern true crime with the lingering, spectral chill of traditional ghost stories. In her 2022 novel, The Book of Cold Cases, St. James returns to the formula that made her previous hit, The Sun Down Motel, a fan favorite, delivering a suspenseful narrative where the past refuses to stay buried. The result is a brisk, highly entertaining page-turner that examines the heavy toll of obsession, trauma, and the dark corners of small-town history.

Set against the moody backdrop of Oregon, the novel pivots around two distinct timelines. In 1977, the affluent and eccentric community of Claire Lake was terrorized by the Lady Killer Murders, a chilling pair of execution-style homicides that left local men dead and cryptic notes left behind in their wake. Beth Greer, a twenty-three-year-old heiress, became the prime suspect before ultimately being acquitted, retreating afterward into the gloomy isolation of her sprawling mansion. Fast-forward to 2017, and the case has taken on legendary status. Enter Shea Collins, a mild-mannered medical receptionist by day who moonlights as the zealous operator of the true crime blog, The Book of Cold Cases. Shea’s fascination with violence and survival is deeply personal, rooted in the lingering psychological scars of an childhood abduction attempt she barely escaped. When Shea unexpectedly crosses paths with an aging Beth, she seizes the chance of a lifetime and requests an interview. Miraculously, the reclusive former suspect agrees.

At its core, the main theme of the novel explores the psychology of obsession and the way past trauma dictates the present. Both Shea and Beth are women defined by events that occurred decades prior. Shea’s entire identity is constructed around her survival and her digital crusade to give voice to the forgotten dead, while Beth’s life has been suspended in amber since her 1970s trial. St. James uses their dynamic to interrogate the modern true-crime phenomenon—our collective voyeurism, the parasocial relationships we form with victims and perpetrators, and the ethical gray areas of turning real tragedy into entertainment. Yet, the novel goes beyond a standard procedural by anchoring this thematic weight in something far more ethereal.

St. James’s authorial style is defined by an uncanny blend of journalistic realism and creeping supernatural dread. She writes with a cinematic fluidity that makes the book remarkably difficult to put down. The chapters alternate between Shea’s modern investigative efforts and historical accounts of the 1977 murders, creating a rhythmic, addictive pacing. However, what truly elevates St. James’s style is her mastery of atmosphere. As Shea ventures deeper into the cavernous, gothic confines of the Greer mansion for their interviews, the book shifts subtly from a straightforward investigative thriller into something much more unsettling. Objects vanish and reappear, inexplicable cold drafts sweep through locked rooms, and phantom glimpses of a young girl press against the windows. St. James excels at crafting a sense of place; the gloomy Oregon setting feels damp, shadowy, and suffocatingly alive.

While the pacing occasionally dips into familiar thriller tropes, and seasoned readers might guess certain twists before the final reveal, the sheer charm of the central relationship compensates for any predictability. The chemistry between the cautious, damaged blogger and the enigmatic, perhaps sociopathic heiress is electric. Beth is a fascinating creation—charming, wealthy, and unfrushed, leaving the reader constantly guessing whether she is a victim of a botched investigation, a manipulative killer, or something far more tragic.

Ultimately, The Book of Cold Cases succeeds because it understands what makes a great mystery: not just the mechanics of whodunit, but the emotional scars left in the wake of violence. St. James delivers a satisfying, atmospheric ghost story wrapped in a gripping true-crime investigation, proving once again that she is one of the most reliable architects of modern atmospheric suspense. It is a haunting, swift read that lingers in the mind long after the final page is turned.
