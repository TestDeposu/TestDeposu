# A Touch of Dead

**Author:** Charlaine Harris  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

Title: Shadows and Footprints: A Dual Descent into the Paranormal

When the sun sets over the humid, secretive landscapes of the American South, the veil between the living and the supernatural grows dangerously thin. In the 2013 supernatural anthology A Touch of Dead, bestselling author Charlaine Harris and acclaimed writer Amanda Stevens invite readers into two distinct yet harmoniously chilling worlds where dance, desire, murder, and ancient folklore collide. While anthologies often struggle with consistency, this paired collection succeeds by anchoring its otherworldly horrors in deeply human vulnerabilities, showcasing the distinct narrative styles of two masters of paranormal suspense.

The collection opens with Charlaine Harris’s short story, Dancers in the Dark. Known globally for her inimitable blend of gritty realism and southern gothic whimsy best seen in her Sookie Stackhouse series, Harris once again proves her mastery over the urban fantasy genre. The story centers on Layla Rue Le May, a dancer whose professional life is complicated by her extraordinary partner, Sean McClendon, a three-hundred-year-old redheaded vampire. Harris excels at demystifying the immortal, grounding her supernatural characters in mundane, blue-collar realities. Sean is not merely a creature of the night; he is a coworker, a partner, and a protector. 

The core theme of Dancers in the Dark revolves around agency and the persistence of fear in the face of immortality. When Layla Rue finds herself targeted by a relentless stalker, the narrative shifts from a fascinating peek into the nightlife of a vampire's partner to a tense, claustrophobic cat-and-mouse game. Harris’s style is breezy, colloquial, and deceptively simple. She delivers punchy dialogue and quick pacing, making the supernatural feel startlingly accessible. Yet, beneath the lighthearted banter lies a sharp exploration of how women navigate threat and intimidation, even when backed by centuries-old predatory protection. 

Transitioning from Harris’s sultry, urban rhythm, Amanda Stevens takes the helm with The Devil's Footprints, shifting the tone toward a darker, more atmospheric southern gothic mystery. Set against the sultry, brooding backdrop of New Orleans, the story follows Sarah DeLaune, a woman still emotionally paralyzed years after her sister’s unsolved murder. The past violently intrudes upon the present when two mutilated bodies are discovered near Sarah’s home, marked not by human hands, but by eerie, desecrating cloven footprints. 

Stevens is a virtuoso of dread, and her style here is much heavier, more poetic, and psychologically intense than the opening piece. The main theme of The Devil's Footprints is trauma and the haunting weight of unresolved grief. Sarah has spent years clinging to the belief that a spectral or elusive man named Ashe Cain was responsible for her sister’s death, despite the skepticism of others. When Ashe finally materializes, the line between psychological delusion and supernatural reality blurs brilliantly. Stevens uses the stifling New Orleans setting as a physical manifestation of Sarah’s internal decay and obsession, creating an oppressive atmosphere that lingers long after the final page.

What makes A Touch of Dead a compelling read is the fascinating contrast between the two authors. Harris provides the momentum, the sharp wit, and the breezy charm of modern paranormal romance, while Stevens provides the psychological depth, the gothic shadows, and the bone-chilling dread of traditional horror. Together, they create a cohesive exploration of the things that haunt us—whether they are centuries-old vampires sharing our dance floors or the literal and figurative footprints of past tragedies refusing to be washed away by the rain.

For fans of supernatural mysteries, urban fantasy, and atmospheric gothic tales, this collection is a swift, satisfying, and haunting journey. It reminds us that death is rarely the end, and the past is never truly buried.
