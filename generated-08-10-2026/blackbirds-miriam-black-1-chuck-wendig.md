# Blackbirds (Miriam Black, #1)

**Author:** Chuck Wendig  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

Chuck Wendig’s 2015 dark supernatural thriller Blackbirds introduces readers to a literary antiheroine who is as abrasive, damaged, and fiercely compelling as they come. Miriam Black is not your typical clairvoyant savior. She does not gaze into crystal balls to warn innocent citizens of impending doom, nor does she wear her prophetic burden like a badge of noble martyrdom. Instead, Miriam is a drifter living on the absolute fringes of society—foul-mouthed, hardened by abuse, and thoroughly exhausted by the gruesome visions that plague her mind. 

The core premise of the novel hinges on a terrifyingly simple mechanic: whenever Miriam makes skin-to-skin contact with another human being, she experiences a visceral, unfiltered front-row seat to their death. She has watched hundreds of people die through these involuntary psychic flashes—drownings, heart attacks, horrific car crashes, and suicides. Over time, she has learned a cruel truth about fate: trying to alter these predetermined demises only makes them happen faster, often twisting reality in ironic and brutal ways to ensure the destination is reached. Consequently, Miriam has adopted a lifestyle of extreme isolation, wearing gloves and keeping humanity at arm's length.

That all changes when she accepts a ride from a seemingly ordinary hitchhiker named Louis Darling. When their hands brush, Miriam receives a vision that shatters her carefully constructed detachment. In thirty days, Louis is going to be brutally murdered, and his last words will be her name. Even worse, the psychic echo reveals that Louis will die simply because he crossed paths with her, and Miriam herself is slated to be the killer's next victim. This inescapable countdown serves as the engine for the novel’s main theme: the illusion of free will versus the crushing weight of preordained destiny. Wendig uses Miriam's predicament to explore how humans cope with a universe that feels aggressively indifferent to suffering, where trying to outrun fate often leads you directly into its jaws.

Wendig’s narrative style is a major part of what makes Blackbirds such a striking read. The prose is raw, punchy, and unapologetically visceral, moving at a relentless, breakneck pace that mirrors Miriam’s frantic desperation. Fans of gritty transgressive fiction and dark fantasy will immediately recognize the tonal DNA of the book, which has been aptly described as a collision between Six Feet Under, Stephen King, and Chuck Palahniuk. Wendig does not shy away from the ugly side of humanity. The dialogue is sharp and laced with black humor, and the world Miriam navigates is dusty, violent, and devoid of easy comforts. Yet, beneath the layers of cynicism, bodily fluids, and roadside grime, there is a beating heart. The reluctant bond that forms between Miriam and Louis injects the narrative with a gritty humanity that prevents the unrelenting darkness from becoming numbing.

Blackbirds is not a comfortable journey, nor is it meant to be. It is a high-octane paranormal thriller that grabs the reader by the throat and refuses to let go until the final, breathless page. Wendig crafts a mesmerizing world where fate is a predator and the only way to survive is to bite back. If you like your supernatural fiction drenched in grit, driven by deeply flawed and intensely real characters, and infused with a palpable sense of impending doom, Miriam Black’s twisted odyssey is an absolute must-read.
