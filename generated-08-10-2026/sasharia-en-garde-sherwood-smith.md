# Sasharia en Garde!

**Author:** Sherwood Smith  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

Sherwood Smith has long been a master architect of romantic fantasy, celebrated for her intricate world-building, sharp character dynamics, and an unwavering refusal to lean into lazy genre tropes. With Sasharia en Garde!, published in 2015, Smith returns to the beloved universe of her classic Crown Duel, yet she approaches this sandbox with a delightfully subversive twist. Rather than offering a mere nostalgic victory lap, Smith crafts a high-energy, portal-adjacent adventure that blends martial arts grit with court intrigue, proving once again why she remains a quiet titan of modern fantasy.

At the narrative and thematic heart of Sasharia en Garde! is the deconstruction of the classic fairy tale. The premise immediately upends the traditional happily-ever-after: Sasha’s mother, Sun, was once swept away by a literal prince, but the romance soured into survival. When the prince vanished and a tyrant seized the throne, Sun and her daughter Sasha were forced into hiding on Earth. Here, the central theme emerges not merely as a hero’s journey, but as an exploration of legacy, agency, and maternal fierce protection. Sasha has been raised on discipline and martial arts, primed to fight. When she is tricked back into the magical realm of Khanerenth, she expects a straightforward quest of good versus evil. Instead, Smith plunges her protagonist into a deliciously muddy moral gray area. Is the stylish pirate Zathdar the true villain, or is it Prince Jehan—artistic, dreamy, and cursed with the bloodline of the wicked king? Through this dynamic, Smith asks her readers to question first impressions and look past the aesthetic presentation of heroes and villains.

Equally compelling is the parallel narrative involving Sun. Too often in fantasy, mothers of protagonists are relegated to tragic backstories or sidelined entirely. Smith boldly corrects this oversight. Sun is determined to cross worlds to rescue her daughter, transforming the standard rescue trope into a dual-perspective race against time. Sun may not have fit the mold of a conventional, obedient princess, but Smith uses her to champion a fierce, protective motherhood that refuses to be rendered invisible. The interplay between Sasha’s youthful, impulsive readiness to kick bad-guy butt and Sun’s seasoned determination creates a generational dialogue about power and survival.

Smith’s stylistic signature is on full display here: brisk pacing, witty dialogue, and a deeply tactile sense of place. She avoids the bloated word counts common in contemporary fantasy, favoring instead a lean, punchy narrative structure. Her action sequences are choreographed with the precision of someone who genuinely understands martial arts, while her romantic tensions sizzle with slow-burn chemistry. The world of Khanerenth feels lived-in and vibrant, contrasted sharply and effectively with the mundane reality of Earth that Sasha and Sun left behind. 

Sasharia en Garde! is a triumphant, swashbuckling romp that honors its predecessor while boldly carving its own path. It is smart, funny, action-packed, and anchored by women who refuse to wait to be saved. Sherwood Smith has delivered a sparkling gem of romantic fantasy that deserves a permanent spot on the shelf of anyone who loves swordplay, clever banter, and fiercely independent heroines.
