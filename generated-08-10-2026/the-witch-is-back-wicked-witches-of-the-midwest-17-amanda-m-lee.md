# The Witch is Back (Wicked Witches of the Midwest, #17)

**Author:** Amanda M. Lee  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2026

A witch, a doorstep baby, and talking chipmunks sound like the ingredients for a traditional fairy tale, but Amanda M. Lee subverts expectations with wicked delight in The Witch is Back. Serving as the seventeenth installment in the beloved Wicked Witches of the Midwest series, this novel showcases Lee at the height of her powers, blending laugh-out-loud cozy fantasy with sharp familial dynamics. Far from a standard heroic quest, this story examines the chaotic intersection of motherhood, prophecy, and magical bureaucracy, offering a witty and refreshing take on the chosen-one trope.

At the heart of the novel is the central theme of unconventional motherhood and the lengths to which a cynical guardian will go to protect her child from the perils of success. Gretsella is a delightfully reluctant matriarch. Her initial irritation at finding an infant on her doorstep gradually morphs into a fiercely protective, albeit deeply pragmatic, doting affection for Bradley. When woodland animals start spouting prophecies and thrusting the unsuspecting young man toward a dangerous royal destiny, Lee uses this absurdity to satirize political ambition. Gretsella recognizes that the royal court is a viper's nest where her genuinely kind-hearted son will inevitably be chewed up and used as a political pawn. The irony that a wicked witch must actively sabotage her beloved child to save him from ruin forms the emotional and comedic backbone of the narrative. 

Lee’s authorial style remains one of the genre’s most entertaining assets. She balances fast-paced plotting with snappy dialogue and an irreverent narrative voice that constantly pokes fun at fantasy tropes. The world-building in Brigandale feels lived-in and quirky, populated by oddball coven members, overly dramatic fauna, and scheming courtiers who provide a fantastic foil to Gretsella’s grounded, no-nonsense attitude. Lee avoids the trap of making her protagonist too soft or too harsh; Gretsella walks the line between a traditional dark-forest witch and a fiercely protective mother, making her instantly endearing. The pacing never drags, moving swiftly from domestic comedy to political farce as Gretsella rolls up her sleeves and dips into her cauldron to dismantle her son’s ascension.

For longtime fans of the series and newcomers alike, The Witch is Back is a triumphant addition that proves long-running cozy fantasy universes can still feel fresh, inventive, and genuinely funny. It is a charming celebration of maternal instinct, magical mayhem, and the stubborn refusal to let destiny ruin a perfectly good family.
