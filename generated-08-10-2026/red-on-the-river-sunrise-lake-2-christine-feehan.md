# Red on the River (Sunrise Lake, #2)

**Author:** Christine Feehan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

Christine Feehan, the undisputed powerhouse of romantic suspense, strikes gold once again with Red on the River, the compelling second installment in her Sunrise Lake series. Returning to the rugged, breathtaking, and often treacherous California backcountry, Feehan masterfully weaves a tapestry of psychological torment, visceral danger, and deeply consuming passion. Published in 2021, this novel proves that the bestselling author’s creative engine shows no signs of slowing down, offering readers an intoxicating blend of spine-chilling thrills and heart-melting romance.

At the heart of the novel is its central theme: the heavy, suffocating burden of trauma and the redemptive, healing power of unyielding trust. Feehan explores how past horrors linger like ghosts in the periphery of the mind, dictating the present until someone brave—or stubborn—enough enters the fray to shatter those defensive walls. This theme is embodied brilliantly through the protagonist, Stella Harrison. Having sought refuge by running the secluded Sunrise Lake resort high in the Sierra Nevada mountains, Stella believes she has outrun her demons. Yet, Feehan subverts this sanctuary trope by weaponizing Stella’s own mind against her. Plagued by psychic premonitions and horrific dream flashes of a killer stalking her loved ones, Stella is trapped in a waking nightmare. The author uses these visions not merely as a supernatural plot device, but as a stark metaphor for PTSD—the terrifying feeling that catastrophe is perpetually around the corner, and the agonizing isolation of bearing a secret that sounds like madness.

Enter Sam, the quiet, observant handyman whose slow and steady presence has quietly anchored Stella over the past two years. Feehan’s signature romantic style shines brightest in the slow-burn dynamic between Stella and Sam. While Feehan is renowned for writing alpha heroes, Sam represents a comforting evolution: a man who is fiercely protective yet endlessly patient, respecting Stella's fierce independence while anticipating her every need. Their relationship develops against a backdrop of mounting dread, creating a delicious contrast between the warmth of emotional intimacy and the chilling realization that a murderer is operating in their midst. 

Stylistically, Feehan remains at the top of her game. Her prose is punchy, atmospheric, and relentlessly paced. She has an uncanny ability to make the majestic Sierra Nevada mountains feel both like a breathtaking paradise and a claustrophobic trap. The isolation of the resort heightens the suspense, cutting the characters off from easy rescue and forcing them to rely on their wits—and each other. Feehan’s dialogue is sharp, her action sequences are tightly choreographed, and her ability to sustain tension while simultaneously stoking the flames of romantic chemistry is nothing short of masterclass. 

Red on the River does not rely on cheap jump scares or formulaic tropes; instead, it digs deep into the human psyche, examining what it truly means to feel safe, to share one’s deepest burdens, and to fight tooth and nail for the people you love. For longtime fans of Christine Feehan, this novel delivers everything they have come to adore: high-stakes danger, a fierce heroine, a swoon-worthy hero, and a love story forged in the fires of adversity. For newcomers, it serves as an electrifying gateway into a world where passion burns just as brightly as the threats lurking in the dark. It is a striking, immersive, and utterly unputdownable thriller that lingers in the mind long after the final page is turned.
