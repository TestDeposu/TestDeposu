# Now Wait for Last Year

**Author:** Philip K. Dick  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

Philip K. Dick has never been particularly interested in the grand, triumphant space operas that dominated mid-century science fiction. Instead, his universe is perpetually claustrophobic, morally gray, and fundamentally broken. In his 1966 novel Now Wait for Last Year, published in a definitive edition in 2011, Dick takes these signature anxieties and weaves them into a dizzying tapestry of geopolitical entrapment, marital despair, and chemical escapism. It is a novel that asks a terrifying question: when the present is unlivable and the future is catastrophic, what happens when time itself becomes just another substance to abuse?

At the center of this grimly humorous and deeply paranoid narrative is Dr. Eric Sweetscent, a man desperately seeking refuge from a suffocating marriage. Eric is a transplant surgeon trapped in a nightmare larger than his domestic woes. Earth finds itself caught in the crossfire of a brutal, unwinnable interstellar war between two alien civilizations: the peace-loving yet manipulative Lilbunauts and the formidable Reegs. Earth has allied itself with the Lilbunauts, a choice that feels less like a proud diplomatic stance and more like a humiliating capitulation. Adding to the absurdity of Earth's geopolitical irrelevance is its leader, Gino Molinari, a political tyrant perpetually on the verge of death, whose survival is paradoxically vital to the planet's fragile status. Eric is tasked with keeping Molinari alive, thrusting him directly into the upper echelons of a corrupt and exhausting power struggle.

Enter Uh-Huh, a potent, illegal new hallucinogenic drug sweeping through the disillusioned population. True to Dick’s masterclass in pharmacological fiction—a theme later perfected in A Scanner Darkly—Uh-Huh is no ordinary narcotic. It is a psychological trapdoor. Users do not merely hallucinate; they are violently untethered from the present, haphazardly flung into shifting timelines and alternate futures. When Eric begins experimenting with the drug in a desperate bid to escape his reality, he is exposed to horrifying visions of a destroyed Earth and a doomed future.

The brilliance of Now Wait for Last Year lies in how Dick explores his central theme: the impossibility of escape. Whether through the geopolitical submission of an entire planet, the inescapable misery of a failing marriage, or the chemical illusion of time travel, Dick’s characters are always running away from themselves, only to find that time and reality always catch up. Eric’s journey forces him to confront agonizing moral dilemmas. Is Earth genuinely on the right side of this cosmic war? Is his medical duty to heal Earth’s ailing dictator, or would the planet be better off if Molinari finally succumbed to death? And most hauntingly, if a drug shows you a harrowing future, can you actually change it, or are you merely sleepwalking toward your own destruction?

Stylistically, Dick operates at the peak of his frenetic, paranoid powers. The prose moves with a dizzying rhythm, mirroring the unstable mental states of his characters and the fractured nature of the reality they inhabit. The lines between the subjective experience of the drug and the objective horrors of war blur seamlessly, trapping the reader right alongside Eric in a maze of shifting identities and unstable chronologies. There are no easy resolutions here, no clean ideological victories. Dick offers instead a biting satire of political servitude and a deeply human exploration of personal regret. 

Now Wait for Last Year may not enjoy the immediate household-name status of Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep? or The Man in the High Castle, but it remains a quintessential Philip K. Dick experience. It is a sharp, profoundly unsettling critique of coping mechanisms in a world spinning violently out of control. For fans of psychological science fiction and literature that dares to question the very fabric of time and free will, this novel is an essential, mind-bending journey.
