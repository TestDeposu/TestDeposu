# GhosTV (PsyCop, #6)

**Author:** Jordan Castillo Price  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Unknown

**GhosTV: A Haunting Masterpiece of Psychological Horror and Digital Paranoia**

Jordan Castillo Price’s *GhosTV*—the sixth installment in the *PsyCop* series—is a chilling descent into the abyss of modern obsession, where the line between reality and digital illusion blurs into something far more sinister. This isn’t just another ghost story; it’s a psychological thriller that dissects the human fear of being watched, controlled, and erased by forces beyond comprehension. Price, a master of atmospheric dread, crafts a narrative that lingers like a static-filled broadcast, whispering into the reader’s mind long after the last page.

At its core, *GhosTV* explores the terrifying idea that technology isn’t just a tool—it’s a living, breathing entity with its own malevolent will. The story follows characters trapped in a digital purgatory, where ghosts aren’t just echoes of the past but active, manipulative presences lurking in the code of the internet. Price doesn’t shy away from the modern horror of being haunted by one’s own digital footprint, a theme that feels eerily prescient in an era where privacy is a myth and algorithms dictate reality. The book asks: *What happens when the ghosts aren’t just in your head, but in your Wi-Fi?*

The author’s style is a perfect storm of cerebral tension and visceral terror. Price’s prose is lean, precise, and suffocating—every sentence feels like a breath held too long. The pacing is relentless, with moments of quiet dread punctuated by explosive revelations that leave the reader gasping. Unlike traditional ghost stories that rely on jump scares, *GhosTV* thrives on psychological unease, making the reader question what’s real and what’s a construct of paranoia. The digital setting isn’t just a backdrop; it’s an active participant in the horror, with glitches, echoes, and unseen observers that feel all too plausible in our hyper-connected world.

What makes this book particularly gripping is its refusal to offer easy answers. Price doesn’t let the reader off the hook with neat resolutions or moral clarity. Instead, *GhosTV* leaves you with the unsettling feeling that the ghosts aren’t just gone—they’re still watching, still waiting, still *there*. The story’s themes of surveillance, identity, and the fear of being erased resonate deeply in an age where our lives are increasingly mediated by screens. It’s a horror story for the digital age, where the scariest monsters aren’t under the bed but in the code.

For fans of psychological horror, *GhosTV* is a must-read. It’s the kind of book that stays with you, that makes you glance at your phone with newfound suspicion, and that proves once again why Jordan Castillo Price is a voice worth listening to in the modern horror landscape. If you haven’t experienced the *PsyCop* series before, this is the perfect entry point—a haunting, thought-provoking dive into the darkest corners of the digital unknown.
