# Wicked And Wilde (Immortal Vegas, #5)

**Author:** Jenn Stark  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

In the fifth installment of Jenn Stark’s Immortal Vegas series, Wicked and Wilde, the stakes are raised from the neon-lit grit of Sin City to the literal fires of the afterlife. For those who have followed tarot-reader-turned-artifact-hunter Sara Wilde through her previous exploits, this book serves as a transformative crucible—a narrative dive into the underworld that demands everything from its protagonist, both physically and psychologically.

Stark’s greatest strength has always been her ability to blend urban fantasy tropes with a distinct, noir-inspired edge. In Wicked and Wilde, she moves away from the procedural “hunter” aspect of the series and leans heavily into gothic introspection. By dragging Sara into Dante’s playground, Stark forces a confrontation that is less about fighting monsters and more about wrestling with the mirror image of one’s own potential. The recurring theme here is the shadow self; throughout the novel, Sara is haunted by the version of herself that could succumb to the brutality of her environment. It is a psychological tightrope walk that Stark handles with commendable tension.

The pacing in this entry is breathless. Stark masters the “ticking clock” scenario, splitting the narrative focus between the claustrophobic, surreal horrors of Hell and the impending, high-stakes war brewing in the mortal world. This duality works to the book’s advantage. While the underworld sections provide the dark, atmospheric heavy lifting—exploring grief, betrayal, and the cost of power—the Vegas segments keep the reader tethered to the series' core mission.

Sara Wilde continues to evolve as a compelling lead. She is far from the wide-eyed psychic of the earlier books; here, she is hardened, yet vulnerable. The deepening relationship with the Magician adds a necessary emotional anchor to the story, serving as the counterweight to the nihilism of the underworld. Their dynamic is written with a sensual, high-stakes friction that feels earned rather than forced. Stark does not allow them the luxury of a slow-burn romance; instead, she forces them to navigate intimacy while the world, quite literally, burns around them.

Stark’s prose remains sharp, evocative, and unapologetically pulpy. She has a gift for world-building that doesn’t bog down the reader in excessive lore; she drops you into the deep end and trusts you to swim. If there is a critique to be made, it is that the emotional weight of the underworld sometimes threatens to overshadow the plot-driven war back home, but the sheer momentum of the final act ensures the threads tie together in a climax that feels both inevitable and devastating.

Wicked and Wilde is a confident, dark, and deeply absorbing chapter in the Immortal Vegas saga. It takes the familiar “chosen one” narrative and smears it with the grease and grime of the supernatural underground. For readers who enjoy their urban fantasy with a side of existential dread and a protagonist who is willing to sacrifice everything to save what she loves, this book is an essential read. Stark doesn’t just raise the stakes; she burns the table, and the result is a thrilling, scorching experience.
