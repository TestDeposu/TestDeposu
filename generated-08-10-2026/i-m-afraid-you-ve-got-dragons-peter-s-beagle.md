# I'm Afraid You've Got Dragons

**Author:** Peter S. Beagle  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2024

**A Whimsical, Heartfelt Reunion with Dragons—and the Magic of Belonging**

Peter S. Beagle’s *I’m Afraid You’ve Got Dragons* is a love letter to the unexpected, a story that weaves together the mundane and the mythic with the same effortless charm that made *The Last Unicorn* a timeless classic. Here, dragons are not just fire-breathing villains or noble guardians—they are neighbors, pests, and, most importantly, creatures of deep, unspoken understanding. Beagle, ever the master of subverting expectations, crafts a world where the line between hero and antihero blurs with the grace of a dragon’s wings.

At its core, this novel is about the stories we’re given and the ones we carve for ourselves. Robert—Gaius Aurelius Constantine Heliogabalus Thrax, if you insist on formality—is a dragon hunter by circumstance, not by choice. His father’s legacy is a noose around his neck, a role he resents not just because it’s grueling, but because it denies his true longing: to be more than the son of a dragon slayer. His dream is to become a prince’s valet, a dream so quaint and human it feels like a secret whispered between the pages of a fairy tale. Yet, as the story unfolds, Beagle gently nudges us to ask: *What if the life you’re meant to live isn’t the one you’ve been told to want?*

The magic of *I’m Afraid You’ve Got Dragons* lies in its refusal to take itself too seriously. Beagle’s prose is lush but never pretentious, his humor sharp but never cruel. The world of Bellemontagne is a cozy, lived-in place where dragons are as common as pigeons in a city square—some are tiny, some are terrifying, and some just want to be left alone. Robert’s internal monologue crackles with self-deprecating wit and quiet melancholy, making him an instantly endearing protagonist. His journey isn’t about becoming a hero; it’s about realizing that heroism might not be the point at all. Instead, it’s about finding the courage to be yourself, even when the world has already written your ending.

What elevates this book beyond a simple fantasy romp is its exploration of identity and belonging. Robert’s relationship with dragons isn’t just about empathy—it’s about recognition. He sees in them the same loneliness, the same longing for purpose, that he feels himself. There’s a beautiful moment where he realizes that dragons, like people, are not defined by their size or their fire, but by the stories they choose to live. This theme resonates deeply in a world where so many of us are still fighting to be seen for who we truly are, not who we’ve been told to be.

Beagle’s style here is a delightful blend of nostalgia and freshness. He pays homage to classic fantasy tropes—dragon hunters, princesses, and royal intrigue—while infusing them with a modern, grounded sensibility. The humor is dry and self-aware, the romance is tender without being saccharine, and the world-building is immersive without being overwhelming. It’s fantasy that feels like a warm hug, a story that reminds us that magic isn’t just in the dragons or the castles, but in the quiet moments of defiance and the courage to rewrite your own story.

Ultimately, *I’m Afraid You’ve Got Dragons* is a celebration of the ordinary hero—the one who doesn’t save the world with a sword, but with the quiet rebellion of being true to themselves. It’s a book that will charm readers who love dragons, who love underdogs, and who love stories that remind us that sometimes, the bravest thing you can do is refuse to be what you were meant to be.
