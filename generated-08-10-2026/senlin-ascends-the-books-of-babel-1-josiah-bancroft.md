# Senlin Ascends (The Books of Babel, #1)

**Author:** Josiah Bancroft  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

Step into the wondrous and perilous world of Josiah Bancroft’s modern fantasy masterpiece, Senlin Ascends. While the provided publisher text leans into the explosive culmination of The Books of Babel quartet, the beating heart of this phenomenal series remains rooted in where it all begins: an upward odyssey through a labyrinthine, civilization-stacked monument of myth and madness. Bancroft has crafted something truly rare in contemporary speculative fiction—a work that marries the whimsical architecture of a feverish fairytale with the gritty, psychological evolution of a hardened survivor.

At its core, the main theme of Senlin Ascends is the shattering and rebuilding of identity. Thomas Senlin, a mild-mannered, pedantic schoolmaster from a quiet village, arrives at the legendary Tower of Babel expecting a honeymoon of refined romance and cultural enlightenment. Instead, within moments of entering the lower ringdoms, he is separated from his newlywed bride, Marya. What follows is not merely a rescue mission, but a grueling, beautifully orchestrated deconstruction of the civilized man. As Senlin navigates the shifting, chaotic strata of the Tower—where every ringdom operates as a bizarre, cutthroat micro-society governed by its own cruel logic—his pristine tweed suit is ruined, his gentle disposition is tested, and the rigid confines of his intellect are forced to adapt to a world that defies reason. Bancroft uses the Tower as a brilliant allegorical canvas to explore how environment reshapes ambition, morality, and love, proving that to conquer the world, a man must first conquer the illusions of his own making.

Bancroft's style is nothing short of magical alchemy. Praised by bestselling authors like Madeline Miller as gracefully written, deliriously imaginative, and action-packed, his prose possesses a timeless, Dickensian charm welded seamlessly with inventive steampunk sensibilities. There is a lushness to his world-building that avoids the exhaustive info-dumping common in epic fantasy. Instead, Bancroft paints with sensory richness—the smell of coal smoke and cheap gin in the lower slums, the dizzying glare of glass domes, the mechanical whir of airships, and the suffocating bureaucracy of tiered societies. His writing is simultaneously warm and witty, balancing harrowing sequences of survival with biting social satire and deeply human introspection. Even as the narrative hurtles forward—from the chaotic sieges and zealot plots of the later ringdoms to the dizzying heights of the sphinxes and utopias—the prose maintains an effortless elegance that keeps the pages turning with breathless urgency.

Senlin Ascends and its ensuing volumes stand as towering achievements in speculative fiction, a testament to a visionary author who treats world-building not as a checklist of lore, but as an exploration of the human spirit under immense pressure. It is a journey that will leave you breathless, bewildered, and utterly enchanted.
