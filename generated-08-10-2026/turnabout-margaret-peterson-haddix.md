# Turnabout

**Author:** Margaret Peterson Haddix  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

**Turnabout by Margaret Peterson Haddix: A Time-Bending Race Against the Clock**

Margaret Peterson Haddix’s *Turnabout* is a gripping, thought-provoking novel that flips the script on aging, sacrifice, and the relentless march of time. At its core, the story follows two women—Melly and Anny Beth—who, in a desperate bid to escape death, became unwitting participants in a secret experiment that reversed their aging process. Now, decades later, they’re teenagers again, but their bodies are still ticking backward, and they’re racing against the inevitable: the moment they reach zero, they’ll cease to exist. The question isn’t just *how* they’ll survive, but *who* will help them when they can no longer help themselves.

Haddix’s genius lies in her ability to blend high-stakes suspense with deep emotional resonance. The premise—aging backward—is both bizarre and haunting, forcing readers to confront the fragility of life and the absurdity of human existence. Yet, rather than wallow in despair, Haddix crafts a story that’s as much about hope as it is about horror. Melly and Anny Beth are not just victims of time; they’re survivors, resourceful and determined to outmaneuver the forces—scientific, societal, and supernatural—that threaten to erase them from existence.

The novel’s pacing is relentless, with Haddix weaving together elements of sci-fi intrigue, psychological tension, and a touch of the supernatural. The mystery of Project Turnabout looms large, hinting at darker forces at play—governments, corporations, or even something far more sinister. The author’s writing is sharp and precise, avoiding melodrama while still delivering emotional punches. Her dialogue crackles with authenticity, and her world-building is immersive, making the dystopian future of 2085 feel eerily plausible.

What makes *Turnabout* truly compelling is its exploration of friendship, loyalty, and the lengths people will go to protect one another. Melly and Anny Beth’s bond is the emotional anchor of the story, a testament to the power of human connection in the face of insurmountable odds. Their journey is as much about finding a caregiver as it is about uncovering the truth behind their condition, and Haddix masterfully balances these threads without losing momentum.

The novel also raises thought-provoking questions about ethics, immortality, and the cost of defying nature. If you could reverse time, would you? And at what price? Haddix doesn’t offer easy answers, but she does challenge readers to consider the consequences of playing God—or, in this case, playing time.

For fans of Michael Crichton’s medical mysteries and Robin Cook’s suspenseful thrillers, *Turnabout* delivers the same kind of edge-of-your-seat tension, but with a unique twist. Haddix’s style is accessible yet layered, making the book appealing to both young adult readers and adults who enjoy clever, character-driven sci-fi.

Ultimately, *Turnabout* is a story about defiance, love, and the desperate struggle to hold onto life—no matter how fleeting it may be. It’s a reminder that time is a thief, but so too is the human spirit’s refusal to surrender. Haddix’s writing is as clever as it is compelling, and *Turnabout* is a novel that lingers long after the final page.
