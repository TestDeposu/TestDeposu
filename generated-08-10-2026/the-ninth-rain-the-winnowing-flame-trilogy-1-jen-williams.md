# The Ninth Rain (The Winnowing Flame Trilogy, #1)

**Author:** Jen Williams  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

**A Storm of Gold and Shadows: *The Ninth Rain* and the Birth of a New Epic Fantasy**

Jen Williams’ *The Ninth Rain* is the kind of book that arrives like a thunderclap—loud, unexpected, and impossible to ignore. Winner of the British Fantasy Award for Best Fantasy Novel, this debut novel is the first installment in the *Winnowing Flame* trilogy, a sprawling, atmospheric epic that weaves together political intrigue, mythic horror, and a protagonist so morally complex he feels like a living, breathing contradiction. If you’ve ever craved fantasy with the grit of *The Witcher*, the lyrical depth of *The Lies of Locke Lamora*, and the high-stakes desperation of *The First Law*, Williams delivers it all—with a twist of her own.

At its heart, *The Ninth Rain* is a story about the cost of survival. Tormalin, the "Oathless," is a man haunted by his past—a warrior who abandoned his duty when his kingdom, Ebora, crumbled into ruin. Now, he’s a drifter, a man who’d rather drown his regrets in ale than face the ghosts of what he left behind. But when eccentric explorer Lady Vincenza "Vintage" de Grazon hires him for a perilous mission—one that involves retrieving lost artifacts, battling eldritch horrors, and navigating a web of treachery—he sees an escape. What he doesn’t expect is to be pulled into a conspiracy so vast it threatens to unravel empires.

Williams’ genius lies in her ability to make Tormalin compellingly flawed. He’s not a hero in the traditional sense; he’s a survivor, a man who’s made choices that have left him morally gray. His journey isn’t about redemption but about reckoning—with himself, with the world he failed, and with the forces that now seek to reshape history. This isn’t a tale of a chosen one; it’s the story of a man who stumbles into greatness, not because he’s worthy, but because the world has no other choice.

The world-building in *The Ninth Rain* is nothing short of breathtaking. Ebora, once a golden metropolis, now lies in ruins, its streets patrolled by wolves and its people ruled by fear. The Jure’lia, a shadowy faction, are rising, and their return is tied to an ancient prophecy—the Ninth Rain. Williams doesn’t just drop lore at the reader; she weaves it into the fabric of the story, making the magic feel tangible and the history feel alive. The monsters here aren’t just creatures—they’re manifestations of something far darker, something that preys on the fears of nations. And the witch, a fugitive with a penchant for setting things ablaze, adds a layer of unpredictability that keeps the narrative electric.

What sets Williams apart is her prose—rich, evocative, and unapologetically vivid. She has a knack for turning a phrase that lingers, whether it’s the eerie beauty of Ebora’s decay or the raw, visceral tension of a battle scene. The dialogue crackles with wit and menace, and even the secondary characters—like the enigmatic Vintage or the sinister figures pulling the strings from the shadows—feel like fully realized individuals, not just plot devices.

But *The Ninth Rain* isn’t just about its world or its characters; it’s about the themes of legacy and consequence. Tormalin’s guilt isn’t just personal—it’s tied to the fate of an empire. The choices he makes, no matter how selfish, ripple outward, affecting people he’ll never meet. This is a story about the weight of history, about how the past clings to the present like a second skin. And Williams handles it with a deft hand, never letting the political intrigue overshadow the emotional core of the narrative.

The book’s pacing is another of its strengths. Williams balances action and introspection seamlessly, ensuring that the reader is never left breathless by sheer spectacle or bogged down by exposition. The mystery of the Ninth Rain unfolds gradually, revealing layers of conspiracy that keep the reader guessing until the very end. And when the twists come, they’re not just surprising—they’re earned, woven into the fabric of the story with such skill that they feel inevitable in hindsight.

Critics have raved about *The Ninth Rain*, and for good reason. It’s been called "fantasy adventure at its very best" by *Starburst*, praised for its "eldritch world-building" by *The Guardian*, and hailed as "an original new voice in heroic fantasy" by Adrian Tchaikovsky. Readers, too, have fallen in love with the book, calling it "amazing and captivating," "masterfully handled," and "a new and long-awaited addition to their favorite trilogies." It’s easy to see why.

*The Ninth Rain* is more than just a fantasy novel—it’s a story about the choices we make, the legacies we leave, and the monsters we create when we turn our backs on the world. Jen Williams has crafted a tale that is as ambitious as it is intimate, a book that will stay with you long after the last page. If you’re looking for a fantasy epic that’s as rich in character as it is in world, one that balances darkness and hope with a sure hand, then this is the book for you.
