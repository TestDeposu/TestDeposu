# Across a Star-Swept Sea (For Darkness Shows the Stars, #2)

**Author:** Diana Peterfreund  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

**Across a Star-Swept Sea: A Dazzling, Dangerous Love in a World of Secrets**

Diana Peterfreund’s *Across a Star-Swept Sea*—the sequel to *For Darkness Shows the Stars*—is a spellbinding blend of high-stakes romance, political intrigue, and futuristic rebellion, all wrapped in the timeless allure of *The Scarlet Pimpernel*. Here, the opulent, genetically engineered society of the Ascendants and the rebellious Rampants collide in a world where love is both a weapon and a liability. At its core, this is a story about secrets, sacrifice, and the fragile line between hero and villain—one that unfolds with the same magnetic tension as its predecessor.

Persis Blake, the dazzling Wild Poppy, is a master of disguise, her dual life a testament to the power of deception. By day, she is the epitome of aristocratic frivolity, a woman who laughs at the wrongs of the world while secretly orchestrating daring rescues. By night, she is a revolutionary, risking everything to free prisoners from the oppressive Ascendant regime. But when she meets Justen Helo—a man she’s been taught to despise—she discovers that even the most carefully constructed masks can be torn away. Justen, a soldier of the Ascendants, carries his own dangerous secret, one that forces Persis to question whether her love for him is worth the cost of the world she’s sworn to save.

Peterfreund’s genius lies in her ability to weave romance and rebellion into a narrative that feels both urgent and lyrical. The world she builds is richly textured, a dystopian paradise where beauty masks brutality, and every gown, every dance, could be a ruse. The slow-burn tension between Persis and Justen is electric, their enemies-to-lovers dynamic rooted in mutual distrust that slowly gives way to something far more dangerous—trust. Their chemistry is palpable, a slow unraveling of preconceptions that makes their eventual confession all the more devastating.

What makes *Across a Star-Swept Sea* truly stand out is its reimagining of classic tropes. Where *The Scarlet Pimpernel* was a tale of a nobleman rescuing French aristocrats from the Revolution, Peterfreund’s version is a high-tech, genetically engineered rebellion where the stakes are survival itself. The world is divided by bloodlines—those who were "ascended" through genetic manipulation and those who were left behind, the Rampants. Persis’s mission isn’t just about freeing prisoners; it’s about challenging the very foundations of a society built on inequality. And Justen, though a soldier for the regime, is far from a one-dimensional villain. His secrets add layers of complexity, forcing Persis—and the reader—to question who the true enemy is.

Peterfreund’s prose is as sharp as it is evocative, her descriptions of the Ascendant’s opulent parties and the Rampants’ hidden resistance camps immersive. The world feels alive, each detail serving a purpose—whether it’s the way Persis’s gowns are designed to conceal weapons or the way Justen’s uniform hides a heart torn between duty and desire. The romance isn’t just about sparks; it’s about the moral dilemmas that come with loving someone from the "other side." Can Persis truly trust Justen when he represents everything she’s fighting against? And can Justen reconcile his loyalty to his people with the woman who sees him as an enemy?

The book’s strength lies in its emotional depth, its exploration of what it means to fight for a cause when the people you love are on the other side. Peterfreund doesn’t shy away from the complexity of her characters, giving them flaws, fears, and moments of vulnerability that make their choices feel real. The romance isn’t just a distraction from the political intrigue; it’s a central part of the conflict, a force that could either unite or destroy them.

If *For Darkness Shows the Stars* was a masterclass in world-building and rebellion, *Across a Star-Swept Sea* is its romantic counterpart—a story where love and revolution collide in a dazzling, dangerous dance. It’s a book that will leave you breathless, questioning the nature of heroism, and wondering how far you’d go for the people you love.
