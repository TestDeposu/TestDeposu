# Of Blood and Bone (Chronicles of The One, #2)

**Author:** Nora Roberts  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

In the aftermath of the societal collapse chronicled in Year One, Nora Roberts returns to her post-apocalyptic landscape with Of Blood and Bone. While the first installment was defined by the visceral shock of the “Doom”—the viral catastrophe that decimated humanity—this sequel shifts the tone from a desperate fight for survival to a coming-of-age odyssey rooted in ancient myth and high fantasy.

At the heart of the narrative is Fallon Swift, a girl moving toward her thirteenth birthday with the crushing weight of prophecy upon her shoulders. Roberts does an exceptional job of juxtaposing the mundane realities of farm life with the encroaching shadows of a broken world. The "Purity Warriors" and Raiders provide a terrifyingly grounded human antagonist, serving as a reminder that even in a world reclaiming its magical heritage, humanity’s capacity for fanaticism remains the most persistent threat.

The brilliance of this installment lies in the expansion of its world-building. Roberts masterfully transitions from the gritty, grounded horror of the initial collapse to the wonder of the "old ways." The introduction of Mallick—a mentor figure who bridges the gap between the modern world and an age of faeries, shifters, and primal magick—allows the narrative to breathe. The training sequences in the forest sanctuary are evocative, trading the frantic, survivalist energy of the first book for a structured, patient exploration of power. It is here that Roberts shines, utilizing her signature descriptive style to make the abstract concepts of magic feel as tangible as the sword Fallon eventually learns to wield.

Stylistically, Roberts remains in top form. She possesses an uncanny ability to balance high-stakes action with intimate character development. She avoids the pitfalls of generic “chosen one” tropes by focusing on the psychological toll of destiny. Fallon is not a hardened soldier from birth; she is a young woman grappling with isolation, the loss of her childhood, and the terrifying responsibility of becoming a beacon in a world that has largely forgotten the light.

The pacing in Of Blood and Bone is deliberate, favoring atmospheric development over constant skirmishes. Roberts treats her setting with reverence, ensuring that the reader understands the ecological and spiritual transformation of the Earth as much as they understand the political tensions between the surviving factions. The prose is lush and sensory, particularly during the sequences involving the fey, providing a welcome contrast to the bleakness of the Purity Warriors’ influence.

Ultimately, Of Blood and Bone acts as the necessary bridge between the chaotic death of the old world and the uncertain birth of the new. It is a story about the endurance of the human spirit—and the non-human spirits—when pushed to the brink of extinction. Roberts has successfully pivoted her massive following into the realm of epic fantasy without losing the emotional core that has defined her career. For those who stayed for the survival story, they will find themselves ensnared by the magic; for those seeking a grand, sweeping epic, this installment provides exactly the foundation needed to see the arc of The One reach its inevitable, climactic peak.
