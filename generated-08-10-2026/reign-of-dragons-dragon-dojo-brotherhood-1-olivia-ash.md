# Reign of Dragons (Dragon Dojo Brotherhood, #1)

**Author:** Olivia Ash  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Unknown

Reign of Dragons bursts onto the page with the fierce energy of a hatchling taking its first breath of fire, and Olivia Ash wastes no time immersing readers in a world where martial discipline and draconic majesty are inseparable. The novel follows a tight‑knit brotherhood of warriors‑in‑training who discover that their destinies are entwined not just with each other, but with the ancient dragons that have long watched over their land. As the titular “Reign” threatens to tilt toward tyranny, the brothers must confront what it means to wield power responsibly, balancing loyalty to their code with the fierce, sometimes terrifying, allure of the beasts they are sworn to understand.

Ash’s greatest strength lies in her ability to make the fantastical feel tactile. Her prose is crisp yet lyrical, slicing through battle scenes with the same precision she gives to quiet moments of introspection. When a protagonist feels the weight of a dragon’s gaze, the description is not merely visual; it vibrates through the sentence, letting the reader sense the heat, the rumble, the ancient wisdom lurking behind those scaled eyes. This sensory richness extends to the dojo itself—training halls are depicted with such detail that you can almost hear the slap of wooden staffs against stone floors and smell the incense that mingles with the ozone of dragon breath.

Thematically, Reign of Dragons probes the tension between heritage and self‑determination. The brotherhood’s creed demands obedience to tradition, yet the dragons they study embody change—shifting forms, evolving motives, and a history that refuses to be static. Ash uses this dichotomy to ask whether true strength comes from rigid adherence to the past or from the courage to adapt when the world itself is in flux. The brothers’ internal struggles mirror the external threat of a looming draconic reign that could either uplift or annihilate their civilization, giving the narrative a stakes‑laden urgency that propels the plot forward without ever feeling rushed.

If there is a flaw, it is that the world‑building, while vivid in its immediate settings, occasionally leaves broader geopolitical currents underexplored. Readers hungry for a deep dive into the history of the dragon realms or the intricate politics of neighboring kingdoms may find themselves wishing for a few more expository threads. However, this restraint also serves the story’s tight focus on the brotherhood’s personal journeys, allowing Ash to maintain a brisk, engaging pace that never loses sight of its emotional core.

Olivia Ash debuts with a novel that feels both familiar and fresh—a martial‑arts fantasy that honors the genre’s tropes while injecting them with a palpable, dragon‑fueled vigor. Reign of Dragons is a promising start to the Dragon Dojo Brotherhood series, offering readers a blend of heart‑pounding action, thoughtful meditation on power, and the kind of world that lingers in the imagination long after the final page is turned.
