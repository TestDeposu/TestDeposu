# Designing Data-Intensive Applications: The Big Ideas Behind Reliable, Scalable, and Maintainable Systems

**Author:** Martin Kleppmann  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2026

Navigating the modern data landscape can easily feel like trying to drink from a digital firehose. Every week brings a dizzying parade of new databases, acronym-heavy frameworks, and cloud-native solutions, all promising to cure your architectural woes. Into this chaos steps Martin Kleppmann and co-author Chris Riccomini with the eagerly anticipated updated volume of Designing Data-Intensive Applications: The Big Ideas Behind Reliable, Scalable, and Maintainable Systems. Building firmly upon the legendary status of the first edition, this expanded second edition arrives as a much-needed lighthouse for software engineers, architects, and technical leaders adrift in a sea of buzzwords.

The core theme of the book remains unapologetically fundamental: while technologies come and go with relentless fury, the underlying principles of distributed systems endure. Kleppmann and Riccomini do not write a product manual or a tutorial for a specific vendor. Instead, they peel back the shiny abstractions of modern cloud services, relational databases, NoSQL datastores, and data lakes to expose the raw mechanics humming beneath the surface. The book tackles the holy trinity of system design—reliability, scalability, and maintainability—not as vague aspirations, but as concrete engineering trade-offs. Throughout the text, the authors repeatedly ask and answer the most important question in software engineering: What actually happens when things go wrong? 

What sets this work apart, and cements its status as a modern classic, is Kleppmann’s distinct literary and pedagogical style. Writing about distributed consensus, replication lag, ACID semantics, and stream processing is notoriously difficult to do without inducing a coma. Yet, Kleppmann possesses a rare gift for radical clarity. He demystifies intimidating theoretical computer science concepts with elegant prose, intuitive mental models, and brilliantly clear architectural diagrams. There is no hand-waving here; the book dives deep into the guts of how data is encoded, stored, and moved across networks, yet it never loses the reader in academic pedantry. 

In this updated edition, the inclusion of Chris Riccomini helps seamlessly extend the narrative into the modern era of data engineering. The text aggressively incorporates emerging trends, modern cloud architectures, and new paradigms without losing the timeless foundation that made the original book a bible for backend developers. You will learn to look at the tools you use every day—Kafka, Cassandra, PostgreSQL, Redis, and beyond—not as magic black boxes, but as deliberate collections of engineering choices with distinct strengths and fatal weaknesses.

Ultimately, Designing Data-Intensive Applications is not a book that tells you what technology to choose. It is a book that teaches you how to think. By the time you turn the final page, you will possess a profound mental framework for evaluating trade-offs, anticipating failure modes, and designing systems that can sleep peacefully through the night. Whether you are scaling a startup from a single database instance to a globally distributed architecture, or simply trying to survive a brutal system design interview, this book is arguably the best investment you can make in your engineering career. It is authoritative, deeply illuminating, and unequivocally essential reading.
