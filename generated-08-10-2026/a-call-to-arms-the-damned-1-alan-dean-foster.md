# A Call to Arms (The Damned, #1)

**Author:** Alan Dean Foster  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Alan Dean Foster has long held a well-deserved reputation as one of science fiction’s most inventive world-builders, effortlessly blending high-concept space opera with accessible, character-driven storytelling. In *A Call to Arms*, the first installment of *The Damned* trilogy, Foster takes a classic speculative trope—humanity’s first contact with intelligent extraterrestrial life—and brilliantly inverts it. Instead of depicting humanity as the wide-eyed ingénues of the cosmos or the ruthless conquerors threatening the stars, Foster paints us as something far more dangerous to a civilized universe: the ultimate biological specialists in violence. 

The central theme of the novel explores the stark dichotomy between intellect and instinct, and the ethical compromises required for survival. In the vast expanse of the galaxy, two massive coalitions are locked in an ideological and territorial struggle. On one side is the Weave, a benevolent yet desperately struggling alliance of species; on the other is the Amplitur, a dominant, telepathic collective determined to assimilate all self-aware life into their absolute "Purpose." For both sides, actual warfare has evolved far beyond physical brutality. To the galactic community, the very concept of harming another sentient being is not only repulsive, it is practically unthinkable. War is a cerebral game of logistics, politics, and psychological chess played by gentle souls who have long forgotten how to shed blood.

Then, they stumble upon Earth. 

When scout ships from the Weave discover humanity, they quickly realize that our evolutionary history is steeped in predation, conflict, and a terrifyingly innate capacity for destruction. To the civilized aliens, humans are not merely barbaric; we are walking anomalies, armed with a chaotic killer instinct that neither side fully understands, but both desperately want to exploit. Foster uses this premise to craft a biting, often darkly humorous critique of human nature. We are the galaxy's ultimate loose cannon, thrust onto a cosmic chessboard where we are simultaneously viewed as indispensable assets and ticking time bombs. 

Foster’s signature writing style shines brightly throughout the narrative. Known for his cinematic pacing and light-handed touch with complex philosophical questions, he avoids heavy-handed preachiness. Instead, he grounds the sweeping, macro-level cosmic conflict in the grounded, relatable reactions of ordinary humans caught up in extraordinary, incomprehensible circumstances. The prose is brisk, conversational, and driven by clever dialogue that highlights the profound cultural disconnect between peace-loving aliens and humanity's pragmatic, often cynical survival instincts. 

While the overarching trilogy expands into darker territory—touching upon genetic manipulation in *The False Mirror* and the horrifying realization of humanity's true, uncivilizable nature in *The Spoils of War*—*A Call to Arms* lays the groundwork with infectious enthusiasm. Foster balances tension and wit with the ease of a veteran storyteller, ensuring that the grand stakes never overshadow the sheer entertainment value of the premise. 

For readers who appreciate classic space opera with a clever satirical edge, *A Call to Arms* is a thoroughly engaging romp. It asks a chilling yet oddly empowering question: what happens when the most polite, civilized society in the universe realizes it needs a monster to win its wars? Foster’s answer is as thrilling as it is thought-provoking, proving once again why he remains a master of imaginative science fiction.
