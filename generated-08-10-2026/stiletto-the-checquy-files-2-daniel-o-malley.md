# Stiletto (The Checquy Files, #2)

**Author:** Daniel O'Malley  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

**Stiletto: Where Espionage Meets the Supernatural—With a Sharp Edge of Wit**

Daniel O’Malley’s *Stiletto* is the kind of book that makes you laugh, gasp, and then immediately want to reread it just to catch every clever jab and twist. As the sequel to *The Rook*, this novel doesn’t just continue the story of Myfanwy Thomas—it deepens the absurdity, sharpens the stakes, and delivers a narrative that’s equal parts political thriller, supernatural farce, and razor-witted satire. Here, diplomacy isn’t just about treaties and handshakes; it’s about outmaneuvering vampires, werewolves, and a rogue faction of the occult that might just start a war over a misplaced spellbook.

At its core, *Stiletto* is a story about power, perception, and the art of the deal—literally. The Checquy, Britain’s centuries-old secret agency tasked with containing supernatural threats, is forced into an uneasy alliance with the Grafters, a group of supernatural beings who’ve spent just as long trying to overthrow them. The catch? The Grafters aren’t just monsters; they’re bureaucrats, politicians, and schemers with their own agendas, and they’re every bit as ruthless as the humans they’ve been fighting. Into this powder keg steps Myfanwy Thomas, the Rook, a woman whose supernatural abilities are as unpredictable as her wit. Her mission? To broker peace before London becomes the battleground for an apocalyptic showdown.

What makes *Stiletto* so compelling isn’t just the high-stakes supernatural politics, but O’Malley’s effortless blend of genres. The novel walks that fine line between fantasy, thriller, and spoof with the confidence of a seasoned diplomat. There’s the dark humor of a world where werewolves hold cabinet positions and vampires run black-market spell shops. There’s the tension of a conspiracy thriller, with betrayals lurking around every corner. And there’s the sheer absurdity of a story where the biggest threat to global stability might be a rogue AI or a misplaced artifact from a long-dead occultist. O’Malley doesn’t just play with tropes—he dismantles them, then rebuilds them into something fresh and unpredictable.

The prose is as sharp as the title suggests. O’Malley’s dialogue crackles with sarcasm and subtext, giving every character—from the Rook’s sharp-tongued allies to the Grafters’ smug adversaries—a distinct voice. The world-building is immersive without being overwhelming, with just enough lore to make the supernatural elements feel real while leaving room for mystery. And the pacing? Relentless. O’Malley keeps the plot moving, whether it’s through action-packed chases, high-stakes negotiations, or the slow unraveling of a conspiracy that could doom the world.

But what truly sets *Stiletto* apart is its exploration of power and perception. The novel isn’t just about who holds the most dangerous abilities—it’s about who controls the narrative. The Rook’s greatest weapon isn’t her supernatural gifts; it’s her ability to see through lies, manipulate perceptions, and turn enemies into allies (or at least, temporary allies). In a world where trust is a luxury and betrayal is a given, Myfanwy’s diplomatic finesse becomes the most potent force of all. It’s a theme that resonates deeply in today’s political climate, where alliances shift faster than spells can be cast and the line between friend and foe is often blurred by self-interest.

Of course, no discussion of *Stiletto* would be complete without acknowledging its humor. O’Malley has a knack for writing characters who are as entertaining as they are dangerous. The Rook’s banter with her allies—particularly her two most formidable rivals—is a highlight, filled with snide remarks and understated threats that never lose their edge. The novel’s tone is a perfect balance of dark and light, ensuring that even the most dire scenarios are leavened by wit and wit itself.

For fans of *The Rook*, *Stiletto* delivers everything they loved and more. It expands the world, deepens the lore, and raises the stakes in ways that feel both satisfying and surprising. For newcomers, it’s an instant gateway into a universe where the supernatural isn’t just real—it’s a political minefield, and the Rook is the only one brave (or foolish) enough to navigate it.

In the end, *Stiletto* is more than just a sequel—it’s a testament to O’Malley’s ability to blend genres, characters, and themes into something that’s as addictive as it is thought-provoking. It’s a book that makes you laugh, keeps you on the edge of your seat, and leaves you eager for whatever comes next in the Checquy Files.
