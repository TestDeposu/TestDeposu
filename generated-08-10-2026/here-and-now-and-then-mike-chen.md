# Here and Now and Then

**Author:** Mike Chen  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

**A Time-Traveling Father’s Desperate Choice: *Here and Now and Then* by Mike Chen**

Mike Chen’s *Here and Now and Then* is a gripping, emotionally charged sci-fi novel that blends the thrill of time travel with the raw, heart-wrenching stakes of family. At its core, this story is about sacrifice—specifically, the lengths a man will go to protect the ones he loves, even if it means defying the very rules that govern existence itself. Kin Stewart, a former time-traveling agent from the future, has spent nearly two decades in the 1990s, living a quiet life as a suburban father, all while hiding a past that would make him unrecognizable to his present self. When his rescue team arrives eighteen years late, Kin is forced to confront an impossible choice: return to a future where his memories are erased, or risk everything to stay with the family he’s built in the past.

Chen’s strength lies in his ability to weave a premise that feels both fresh and deeply human. Time travel is a well-trodden genre, but Chen’s twist—where the protagonist is not just a traveler but a man torn between two lives—adds a layer of psychological depth that makes the story resonate. Kin’s struggle isn’t just about the mechanics of time; it’s about identity, love, and the cost of redemption. His journey forces readers to ask: What would you sacrifice to keep your family whole? Would you erase your own past to save them? Chen doesn’t offer easy answers, and that’s what makes the novel so compelling.

The pacing is tight, the tension relentless, and the emotional beats land with precision. Kin’s internal conflict is palpable—his love for his daughter, his guilt over abandoning his original family, and his fear of losing everything he’s fought to reclaim. Chen balances action with introspection, ensuring that even as the plot races forward, the characters remain grounded. The world-building is subtle but effective; the future agency feels real, the rules of time travel are intriguing without being overly complex, and the suburban setting of the 1990s provides a stark contrast to the high-stakes future Kin once knew.

What truly elevates *Here and Now and Then* is its emotional core. Chen doesn’t shy away from the pain of separation or the weight of regret. Kin’s journey is one of self-discovery, where he must confront not just the consequences of his actions but also the man he’s become—or perhaps, the man he was meant to be. The novel’s strength lies in its ability to make time travel feel personal, to turn a genre trope into a deeply human story about love, loss, and the choices that define us.

For fans of sci-fi with heart, *Here and Now and Then* is a must-read. It’s a story about the past, the present, and the future—all intertwined in the hands of a man who refuses to let go. Chen’s writing is sharp, his characters are flawed and compelling, and his premise is both inventive and deeply moving. If you’ve ever wondered what you’d do to keep your family together, even if it meant breaking the rules of time itself, this book will leave you breathless.
