# Legend of the Sword (In Her Name: The Last War, #2)

**Author:** Michael R. Hicks  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

**A Masterclass in Cosmic Conflict: *Legend of the Sword* and the Weight of War’s Shadow**

Michael R. Hicks’ *Legend of the Sword*—the second installment in *In Her Name: The Last War*—is a sprawling, high-stakes epic that doesn’t just deliver on the promise of space opera; it *redefines* it. Where many sci-fi sagas settle for spectacle alone, Hicks weaves together military precision, philosophical depth, and visceral action into a narrative that feels both timeless and urgently relevant. This is a story where the stakes aren’t just lives, but the very soul of humanity—and the cost of survival is measured in blood, betrayal, and the crushing weight of history.

At its core, *Legend of the Sword* is a tale of war’s paradoxes: the illusion of victory, the moral rot of vengeance, and the fragile line between heroism and tyranny. The novel opens with a world already on the brink—Earth’s fragile Confederation, barely recovering from a Kreelan invasion, now faces a new enemy closer to home. The rogue colony of Saint Petersburg, a fractured remnant of humanity’s past, threatens to ignite a nuclear holocaust, while the Kreelan fleet, led by the enigmatic Tesh-Dar, looms like a storm on the horizon. Hicks doesn’t shy away from the grim reality of war: the political maneuvering, the civilian casualties, the soldiers who become pawns in games they never signed up for. Yet, beneath the chaos, there’s a quiet dignity in the characters who refuse to be broken by it.

The prose is sharp, efficient, and immersive, with Hicks’ knack for vivid detail making even the most complex battles feel tangible. His world-building is meticulous—from the political intrigue of the Confederation’s fractured leadership to the cultural clashes between human factions and the alien Kreelan—yet it never overwhelms the story. Instead, it serves as a backdrop that heightens the tension, making every decision a high-stakes gamble. The action sequences are relentless, but they’re never gratuitous; they’re earned, brutal, and often tragic, reflecting the cost of war without glorifying it.

What truly sets *Legend of the Sword* apart is its exploration of morality in extremis. Hicks doesn’t offer easy answers. The Kreelan, often portrayed as the "evil" invaders in sci-fi tropes, are given depth—particularly through Tesh-Dar, whose motivations are as layered as they are terrifying. Meanwhile, the human factions are far from monolithic; alliances shift, loyalties fracture, and even the heroes are flawed. This moral ambiguity makes the story richer, forcing readers to question who the real villains are and whether any side truly deserves victory.

Romance, when it appears, is handled with restraint—never a distraction, but a subtext that adds emotional weight to the larger conflict. The relationships here are built on trust, sacrifice, and the understanding that love in a warzone is both a luxury and a necessity. It’s a far cry from the clichéd "love conquers all" tropes; instead, it’s a reminder that even in the darkest times, human connection can be a beacon.

Hicks’ style is a masterclass in pacing—balancing political intrigue with large-scale battles, personal drama with cosmic stakes. The novel never drags, yet it never feels rushed. Each revelation, each betrayal, each act of heroism is given its due, making the payoff all the more satisfying. By the time the final pages unfold, the reader isn’t just invested in the outcome; they’re *haunted* by it.

*Legend of the Sword* is more than just another space opera—it’s a meditation on war’s cyclical nature, the cost of peace, and the fine line between survival and sacrifice. Hicks doesn’t just tell a story; he forces the reader to *feel* the weight of it. If you’ve ever wanted a sci-fi epic that’s as thought-provoking as it is thrilling, this is the book for you. Just be prepared to be changed by it.
