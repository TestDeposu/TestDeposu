# The Rosewater Insurrection (The Wormwood Trilogy, #2)

**Author:** Tade Thompson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

Tade Thompson’s The Rosewater Insurrection, the gripping second installment in The Wormwood Trilogy, cements the author’s reputation as one of the most inventive and uncompromising voices in modern speculative fiction. Following the groundbreaking success of Rosewater, this middle chapter expands its scope outward, transforming a localized sci-fi mystery into a tense, sprawling geopolitical thriller set against the bizarre, living backdrop of an alien biodome. 

At the heart of the novel lies a compelling thematic exploration of colonization, corporate greed, and the precarious nature of human autonomy. Rosewater is no longer just a weird frontier town; it is a burgeoning metropolis growing on the back of the gargantuan alien entity known as Wormwood. Thompson cleverly uses this biological marvel to mirror the realities of our own world, where human institutions—corrupt politicians, intelligence agencies, and opportunistic corporations—seek to exploit foreign resources for power and control. The alien presence is not merely a sci-fi trope for world-ending destruction, but a complex catalyst that forces humanity to confront its own deeply entrenched cycles of subjugation and resilience. 

The narrative thrust is driven by Aminat, a formidable government agent caught in a cold, silent conflict. Her mission to capture the key to human survival pits her against both human adversaries and the unpredictable machinations of the Mayor of Rosewater. Through Aminat and other returning figures like the retired sensitive Kaaro, Thompson crafts deeply flawed, morally gray characters who feel startlingly authentic. They are ordinary people thrust into extraordinary circumstances, navigating a world where trust is a luxury and survival demands compromise. 

Stylistically, Thompson’s prose is sharp, economical, and relentlessly atmospheric. He effortlessly blends gritty noir sensibilities with high-concept biopunk and vibrant Nigerian culture, creating a uniquely grounded Afrofuturist aesthetic. Comparisons to Jeff VanderMeer and William Gibson are well-deserved, yet Thompson’s voice remains entirely his own—distinctly African, modern, and pulsing with rhythmic vitality. He avoids exposition-heavy info-dumping, instead trusting the reader to piece together the socio-political intricacies of this altered Earth through sharp dialogue and visceral world-building. 

The Rosewater Insurrection is a masterclass in middle-book pacing, raising the stakes exponentially while deepening the philosophical questions introduced in the series opener. It is tense, intellectually stimulating, and vividly original. For readers looking for science fiction that challenges colonial narratives while delivering a high-stakes, intelligent thriller, Thompson’s latest offering is an absolute triumph.
