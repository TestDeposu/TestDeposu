# Cry of the Raven (The Ravenwood Saga, #3)

**Author:** Morgan L. Busse  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

**A Whisper in the Pages: The Heart and Magic of *Cry of the Raven***

Grief is a weight no child should carry alone—but in *Cry of the Raven*, the third installment in Morgan L. Busse’s *Ravenwood Saga*, it becomes the quiet spark that ignites something far greater. Twelve-year-old Piper and her autistic brother, Phoenix, are shattered by loss, their world upended when they’re forced to live with their cold, distant aunt in a house where the walls seem to breathe. But in the labyrinthine depths of Aunt Beryl’s Verboten Library, where books hum with secrets and shelves stretch into the unknown, they find not just refuge—but a voice. A voice in the form of *Novus Fabula*, a book that calls to them across the pages, promising a story that will unravel the threads of their sorrow and weave them into something new.

Busse’s prose is a masterclass in atmospheric storytelling, blending the whimsy of *My Neighbor Totoro* with the cosmic wonder of *A Wrinkle in Time*. The Verboten Library isn’t just a setting; it’s a living entity, its shelves lined with tomes that pulse with energy, its air thick with the scent of old parchment and possibility. Here, Piper and Phoenix discover that books aren’t passive objects—they *speak*. They *remember*. They hold the keys to truths buried beneath the weight of time. This isn’t just fantasy; it’s a celebration of the magic that exists in the quiet corners of the world, the kind that only those who listen closely enough can hear.

At the heart of *Cry of the Raven* lies a theme as tender as it is profound: the way stories—both the ones we read and the ones we live—can heal. Piper and Phoenix are grieving, yes, but they’re also searching. Searching for connection, for meaning, for a way to make sense of the chaos that has become their lives. Busse doesn’t shy away from the raw emotion of their journey; there are moments of heartbreaking vulnerability, where the weight of loss threatens to swallow them whole. Yet, through it all, the novel remains hopeful. The library isn’t just a place of mystery—it’s a sanctuary, a reminder that even in darkness, there are stories waiting to be told, and readers willing to hear them.

What sets *Cry of the Raven* apart is Busse’s ability to make the extraordinary feel intimate. The world she builds is rich with detail, from the way the library’s books shift when touched to the way Piper’s brother, Phoenix, interacts with the world in a way that feels both deeply human and utterly unique. Autism isn’t just a side note here; it’s woven into the fabric of the story, a part of Phoenix’s journey that adds layers of depth to his character. Busse doesn’t romanticize his experiences, nor does she treat them as a barrier—she presents them as a different kind of wisdom, a perspective that enriches the narrative.

The novel’s pacing is a delight, balancing moments of quiet reflection with bursts of adventure. The mystery of *Novus Fabula* unfolds like a slow-burning ember, drawing readers deeper into Piper and Phoenix’s world with each chapter. There’s a sense of wonder here, a feeling that anything is possible if you’re brave enough to turn the page. And yet, for all its magic, *Cry of the Raven* never loses sight of its emotional core. It’s a story about family—not just the blood kind, but the kind you find in the spaces between stories, in the shared laughter and tears, in the quiet understanding that you’re not alone.

Busse’s writing is lyrical, her descriptions vivid enough to make the Verboten Library feel like a real, breathing place. She has a knack for creating characters who feel like old friends, their struggles and triumphs resonating long after the final page. *Cry of the Raven* is more than just a middle-grade fantasy; it’s a love letter to the power of stories, to the idea that even in the darkest moments, there’s always a book waiting to guide you home.

For readers who loved the whimsy of *The Girl Who Drank the Moon* or the heart of *The Inquisitor’s Tale*, this novel will feel like a warm embrace. It’s a story about grief, yes, but it’s also about hope, about the magic that exists when you’re brave enough to listen. And perhaps most importantly, it’s a reminder that sometimes, the greatest adventures begin not with a grand gesture, but with a single, whispered word from a book that’s been waiting for you all along.
