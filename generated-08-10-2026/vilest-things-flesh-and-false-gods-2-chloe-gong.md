# Vilest Things (Flesh and False Gods, #2)

**Author:** Chloe Gong  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2024

In Vilest Things, the dazzling and blood-soaked sequel to Immortal Longings, Chloe Gong cements her reputation as a master of high-stakes fantasy and razor-sharp political intrigue. Loosely inspired by Shakespeare’s Antony and Cleopatra, Gong’s latest installment takes the breathless momentum of the first book and plunges it headfirst into a cauldron of moral ambiguity, shattered trust, and lethal romance. For readers who revel in characters who are just as likely to plunge a dagger into their lover's back as they are to kiss it, Vilest Things is an intoxicating triumph.

The central theme of the novel pivots on the corrupting, yet intoxicating, nature of power and the devastating cost of survival. Gong explores how ambition distorts the human heart, forcing her protagonists to constantly weigh loyalty against self-preservation. Calla Tuoleimi has clawed her way to the top, winning San-Er’s brutal games and orchestrating the fall of a tyrant, only to find herself trapped in a web of deceit. She is now the royal advisor to the new ruler, August Shenzhi—a precarious position made all the more dangerous because Calla is the only one who knows the horrifying truth: the boy on the throne is not August at all. 

Instead, Anton Makusa occupies that stolen vessel, driven by a furious resentment toward Calla for her ultimate betrayal during the games. Gong’s exploration of Calla and Anton’s dynamic is the beating, bruised heart of the narrative. Their relationship is not a safe harbor; it is a battleground. They are bound by history, mutual ruin, and a chemistry that crackles with danger. When Anton’s first love, Otta Avia, awakens from a years-long coma with a secret capable of shattering the Talin monarchy to its foundations, the fragile status quo shatters. Calla and Anton are violently thrust into an uneasy alliance, forced to journey to the kingdom’s far reaches while navigating a labyrinth of knives, political machinations, and unresolved desires. 

Gong’s authorial style remains one of the sharpest in modern YA and crossover fantasy. Her prose is sleek, atmospheric, and relentlessly fast-paced. She has a rare talent for world-building that feels lived-in, brutal, and utterly cinematic, painting San-Er and the broader kingdom of Talin with neon-drenched grit and oppressive royal splendor. She does not shy away from the grotesque or the morally grey; her characters are unapologetically vicious, driven by desperation and fierce intelligence. There are no true heroes here, only survivors wearing crowns and bleeding in the shadows, which makes their triumphs and tragedies land with devastating emotional weight. 

Furthermore, Gong’s plotting is a masterclass in tension. Every chapter feels like a ticking clock, layering secrets upon secrets until the pressure threatens to crack the narrative wide open. The dialogue is snappy and charged with subtext, highlighting the psychological chess match playing out between Calla and Anton. Even as the plot broadens to encompass sweeping anarchy and the potential fall of an entire empire, Gong never loses sight of the intimate, agonizing human heart of the story. 

Vilest Things is a dark, dazzling, and deliciously depraved continuation of a saga that refuses to pull its punches. Chloe Gong has crafted a sophomore triumph that will leave readers breathless, gasping at the audacity of its twists and the sheer volatility of its romance. If you enjoyed the blood-soaked rebellion of the first book, prepare to be completely consumed by a sequel that proves sometimes, the only way forward is to let the empire burn.
