# The Captive Temple (Star Wars: Jedi Apprentice, #7)

**Author:** Jude Watson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

History often remembers Abraham Lincoln as the towering, melancholy sixteenth president who held a fracturing nation together, but long before the weight of the Civil War rested on his shoulders, he was a master trial lawyer in the dusty courtrooms of Illinois. In this gripping adaptation, readers are transported to the humid summer of 1859 to witness Lincoln in his element, orchestrating what would be his final and most complex legal defense before ascending to the presidency. 

The narrative centers on twenty-two-year-old Peachy Quinn Harrison, accused of murdering a young man who happened to be an apprentice in Lincoln’s own law office. To complicate matters further, the accused is the son of a dear friend and loyal political supporter. Jude Watson captures the unbearable tension of a man walking a tightrope between personal loyalty and professional duty. The central theme of the book explores the crucible of integrity, examining how character is forged when the stakes involve not just a human life, but the fragile foundation of a burgeoning political career observed by an increasingly ravenous press.

What makes this account so striking is the authorial style. Adapted from Dan Abrams and David Fisher’s bestselling work and grounded heavily in actual court transcripts, the writing bridges the gap between meticulous historical non-fiction and a pulse-pounding courtroom thriller. Lincoln is not portrayed as a marble statue, but as a living, breathing tactician with weary eyes, dusty shoes, and a mesmerizing command of a jury. The prose moves at a brisk, cinematic pace, sweeping the reader into the sweltering courtroom where every piece of testimony feels like a ticking clock. 

For readers looking for a masterclass in suspense, human drama, and historical immersion, this book delivers an unforgettable glimpse into the mind of a legendary American figure when he was simply a lawyer fighting for justice.
