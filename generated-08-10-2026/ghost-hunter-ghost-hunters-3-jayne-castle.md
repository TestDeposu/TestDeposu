# Ghost Hunter (Ghost Hunters, #3)

**Author:** Jayne Castle  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2006

Beneath the neon-lit veneer of a futuristic colony lies a labyrinth of forgotten catacombs, ancient secrets, and spectral echoes that refuse to stay dead. In Ghost Hunter, the third installment of her captivating futuristic romantic-suspense series, Jayne Castle—writing under one of her many beloved pseudonyms—returns to the vibrant world of St. Helens and the sprawling metropolis of Cadence City. Published in 2006, this novel masterfully blends elements of paranormal romance, speculative sci-fi, and traditional mystery, offering readers a thrilling excursion into a subterranean underworld where the past literally haunts the present.

At the heart of the narrative is Elly St. Clair, a resilient botanist whose quiet academic life is upended when a new friend vanishes without a trace beneath the city streets. Enter Cooper Boone, a formidable local Guild boss, powerful ghost hunter, and Elly’s former flame. Cooper embodies the classic romance archetype—the strong, silent protector whose stoicism often masks a fierce, unwavering devotion. Their history is complicated; Elly previously called off their impending marriage and fled to the big city when Guild secrecy began to threaten her career. Yet, when danger strikes, Cooper reappears precisely when he is needed most, proving that some bonds are too deeply rooted to be severed by distance or pride.

Castle’s signature style shines brightly in her ability to seamlessly merge world-building with interpersonal tension. Set on a distant planet colonized by humans who must navigate energy-based anomalies known as "ghosts" and "dust bunnies," the novel creates an environment that feels both alien and intimately familiar. Castle does not merely use the paranormal as a cheap atmospheric trick; instead, ghost hunting is treated as a practical, blue-collar profession with its own politics, guilds, and hazards. This grounded approach to the extraordinary gives the romance real stakes. As Elly and Cooper descend into the eerie catacombs, the claustrophobic setting amplifies the emotional intimacy between them, forcing them to confront not only dangerous underground myths and legends, but also the unresolved wounds of their past romance.

The thematic core of Ghost Hunter revolves around trust, second chances, and the danger of keeping secrets. Elly’s botanical background serves as a subtle metaphor for her character arc: she is someone who understands growth, the necessity of fertile ground, and what it takes to transplant oneself into a new environment and start over. Cooper, on the other hand, must learn that true strength in a partnership requires vulnerability and transparent communication. As they unravel the mystery behind the missing friend and the unsettling ghost legends tied to the city's foundations, the plot accelerates into a gripping race against time. Castle keeps the pacing brisk, balancing moments of high-stakes peril with witty banter and palpable romantic chemistry.

Ultimately, Ghost Hunter is a triumphant genre-mashup that will satisfy longtime fans of Castle’s distinct brand of futuristic romantic suspense and newcomers alike. It offers a masterclass in how to use an imaginative setting to heighten emotional stakes. By the time the final mysteries of the catacombs are brought to light, readers will find themselves thoroughly enchanted by a world where science, spirits, and true love collide in the most unexpected ways.
