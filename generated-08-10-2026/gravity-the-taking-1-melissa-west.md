# Gravity (The Taking, #1)

**Author:** Melissa West  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

In a literary landscape frequently saturated with recycled dystopian tropes, debut author Melissa West manages to breathe fresh, electrified air into the young adult genre with Gravity, the riveting first installment of The Taking series. Set against a chillingly controlled future where compliance is survival, West crafts a high-stakes narrative that marries classic sci-fi invasion tropes with the volatile, intoxicating chemistry of forbidden adolescent romance. 

At the pulsating heart of Gravity is a brilliantly simple yet terrifying premise: the golden rule of the new world is absolute, unquestioning obedience, encapsulated in a single command—Don't. Ever. Peek. West uses this psychological barrier not just as a plot device, but as the central thematic anchor of her novel. The act of looking—of seeking truth, questioning authority, and refusing to accept the sanitized reality fed to the masses—becomes the ultimate rebellious act. Seventeen-year-old protagonist Ari Alexander embodies this struggle. As a military legacy trained by her father, Ari is a product of strategy and conditioning, yet her innate curiosity proves to be her most dangerous weapon. When she breaks the ultimate rule and discovers that arrogant, untouchable schoolmate Jackson Locke is actually an extraterrestrial entity, the foundation of her reality shatters. 

West’s writing style is brisk, cinematic, and relentlessly action-oriented. She wastes no time on superfluous world-building, instead dropping the reader directly into the suffocating paranoia of Ari’s existence. The author employs a taut, first-person perspective that mirrors Ari’s internal conflict: every sentence feels pressured, echoing the heavy gravity of the choices before her. The pacing operates much like a free-fall, matching the title’s thematic resonance. Once Ari crosses the threshold of forbidden knowledge, there is no upward pull, only a rapid, dizzying descent into a conflict far bigger than high school social hierarchies or local military drills.

What elevates Gravity above standard genre fare is the nuanced dynamic between Ari and Jackson. West avoids the trap of an immediate, uncomplicated surrender to romance. Instead, their relationship is forged in mutual suspicion and high-stakes manipulation. Jackson needs Ari’s tactical brilliance—secrets locked away by her father—while Ari knows that engaging with him is treasonous. This creates a delicious push-and-pull where trust is a rare and perilous commodity. Every interaction crackles with tension, questioning whether Jackson is a savior offering a glimpse of truth or an operative executing a meticulously planned planetary conquest. 

Through this central conflict, West explores broader themes of loyalty, systemic deception, and the heavy price of awareness. In Ari’s world, ignorance is institutionalized to maintain peace, making knowledge a weapon of mass disruption. The author poses a challenging moral dilemma to her protagonist: Is it better to protect a corrupt status quo or burn it down for a chance at true freedom? Ari’s journey from a disciplined soldier’s daughter to an active participant in an interstellar war feels earned, driven by her growing disillusionment with the society she was raised to protect.

Gravity succeeds as both an adrenaline-fueled sci-fi thriller and a compelling coming-of-age story about the terrifying weight of seeing things as they truly are. Melissa West has engineered a confident, suspenseful debut that leaves readers breathless and desperately reaching for the next chapter. For fans of dystopian intrigue, alien mysteries, and high-voltage romance, this book delivers an undeniable gravitational pull.
