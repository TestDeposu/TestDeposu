# Inferno Squad (Star Wars: Battlefront, #2)

**Author:** Christie Golden  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Title: Walking the Imperial Line: Christie Golden’s *Inferno Squad* and the Cost of Obedience

In the vast expanse of the Star Wars expanded universe, stories are frequently drawn along comfortably familiar fault lines. We know the shining heroism of the Rebellion, and we know the shadowy, cartoonish malice of the Galactic Empire. Yet, every so often, a novel arrives that dares to peer into the murky grey areas between the two. Set directly in the smoking aftermath of *Rogue One: A Star Wars Story*, Christie Golden’s *Inferno Squad* is precisely that kind of narrative—a tense, psychological space thriller that forces readers to reconsider what it means to wear the Imperial uniform when the Death Star has just been reduced to space dust.

Golden, a seasoned veteran of tie-in fiction, faces a formidable challenge in this 2017 New York Times bestseller. She must take characters originally designed for a multiplayer shooter video game and imbue them with depth, urgency, and moral complexity. She succeeds brilliantly by centering the book around its core theme: identity versus indoctrination. 

Following the humiliating loss of the Death Star plans, the Empire is bruised, desperate, and defensive. To restore order, Supreme Command dispatches Inferno Squad—an elite, fiercely loyal special forces unit led by Iden Versio—on a high-stakes mission of infiltration. Their target is the Partisans, the radical extremist faction formerly led by Saw Gerrera, who are still waging a chaotic insurgency against the Imperial regime. To destroy them, Inferno Squad must go deep undercover, pretending to share the revolutionary zeal of the very terrorists they have been trained to eradicate.

This premise allows Golden to execute a masterclass in psychological tension. The primary conflict of *Inferno Squad* is not merely tactical; it is internal. As Iden and her team—Gideon, Del, and Seyn—spend weeks living among the Partisans, breathing their air and listening to their grievances, the psychological armor of Imperial absolute loyalty begins to crack. Golden expertly explores how proximity to the enemy forces soldiers to confront uncomfortable truths. They begin to see the human faces behind the rebel label, and worse, they are forced to examine the horrific civilian costs of Imperial policy through the eyes of those who suffered it. 

The author’s style is sharp, economical, and relentlessly driving. Golden understands the pacing required for a military sci-fi thriller. She balances explosive, high-octane set pieces with quiet, suffocating moments of paranoia. Every conversation is loaded with subtext; every lingering glance could mean the difference between blowing their cover and surviving. Golden does not romanticize the Empire, nor does she offer cheap absolution. Instead, she presents Inferno Squad as true believers trapped in an existential crucible. They are deeply competent professionals who genuinely believe they are bringing order to a chaotic galaxy, making their moral compromises all the more tragic and compelling.

While the Rebellion may have its mythic heroes like Jyn Erso and Luke Skywalker, *Inferno Squad* reminds us that the Imperial machine is fueled by flesh, blood, and complex human rationalizations. It asks a chilling question: when you are ordered to commit atrocities in the name of security, how much of your humanity are you willing to sacrifice to preserve the system? 

Christie Golden has crafted an essential read for anyone who prefers their Star Wars with a darker, grittier edge. It is a thrilling, emotionally resonant companion piece to *Rogue One* and a standout achievement in modern franchise fiction.
