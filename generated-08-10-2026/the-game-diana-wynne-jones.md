# The Game

**Author:** Diana Wynne Jones  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

The Game is a deftly layered addition to Diana Wynne Jones’s beloved Chrestomanci canon, and it shines brightest when it turns its whimsical gaze inward—toward the quiet, often overlooked magic of growing up. At its heart lies a simple, glittering premise: a mysterious egg, cloaked in “Don’t Notice” spells, becomes the catalyst for two teenagers—Cat Chant, the ever‑curious ward of Chrestomanci Castle, and Marianne Pinhoe, a reluctant heir to a lineage of rogue witches—to confront the weight of inheritance, both magical and familial. The egg is more than a plot device; it symbolizes latent potential, the fragile boundary between secrecy and responsibility, and the way power can sit unnoticed in the attic of everyday life until someone dares to lift the lid.

Jones’s style remains unmistakable: brisk, witty prose that slips effortlessly between the mundane and the marvelous. She layers humor with a light touch—Gammer’s descent into madness, the inexplicable frog plague, and the forest’s elusive barrier are rendered with the same affectionate absurdity that has delighted readers for decades. Yet beneath the frolic lies a keen observation of how magic mirrors personal growth; Marianne’s journey to accept her own formidable abilities parallels Cat’s quest to uncover the egg’s true nature, each revelation nudging them toward a deeper understanding of self‑trust and cooperation. The narrative never loses its sense of wonder, but it also never shirks from the stakes—bad luck spells and looming interference from the enchanter‑king himself remind us that even the most enchanting secrets demand courage to protect.

The Game thus works on two levels: as a delightful, fast‑paced adventure that will satisfy longtime fans of the Chrestomanci world, and as a subtle meditation on embracing one’s gifts despite fear of the fallout. Jones proves once again that her magic lies not just in spells and enchantments, but in the way she makes the extraordinary feel intimately human.
