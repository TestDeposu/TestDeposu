# Dragons of the Hourglass Mage (Dragonlance: The Lost Chronicles, #3)

**Author:** Tracy Hickman Margaret Weis  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Stepping once more into the rich, sprawling tapestry of Krynn, Tracy Hickman and Margaret Weis deliver a masterclass in dark fantasy character study with Dragons of the Hourglass Mage. As the thrilling conclusion to The Lost Chronicles sub-series and a vital narrative companion to Dragons of Spring Dawning, this 2009 installment achieves something remarkably difficult: it breathes fresh, unsettling life into a legendary lore while keeping the stakes palpably high. The core brilliance of the novel does not merely lie in how it fills the historical gaps of the original Dragonlance Chronicles, but rather in how it anchors itself to the agonizingly complex internal landscape of its protagonist, the iconic hourglass-eyed mage, Raistlin Majere.

At the heart of the novel is a profound exploration of ambition, corruption, and the dizzying seduction of ultimate power. Hickman and Weis dissect the anatomy of betrayal and self-preservation with surgical precision. Raistlin, now cloaked in the ominous black robes of Neraka, walks a perilous tightrope between the machinations of the Dark Queen Takhisis and his own insatiable thirst for mastery over magic itself. The thematic tension of the book pivots on a grim philosophical question: how far can a mortal soul stretch toward absolute darkness before snapping entirely? Raistlins journey into the belly of the beast—the lord city of Neraka and the suffocating dungeons below—serves as a literal and metaphorical descent into the abyss, where every alliance is transactional and every victory demands a piece of the victors humanity. 

The authors signature writing style remains a cornerstone of the Dragonlance appeal, blending nostalgic epic fantasy grandeur with brisk, cinematic pacing. Weis and Hickman excel at world-building through momentum. They do not bog the reader down with superfluous exposition; instead, the world of Krynn feels lived-in, battered by war, and charged with ancient magic because the narrative focus is relentlessly anchored to the immediate perils facing the characters. The prose strikes an effective balance between sweeping battle sequences—as the forces of light and the good dragons clash against the darkness—and claustrophobic, psychological showdowns. When Raistlin confronts the trap set for the Gods of Magic on the Night of the Eye, the prose crackles with electric tension, capturing the volatile volatility of arcane forces on the brink of chaos.

What truly elevates Dragons of the Hourglass Mage above standard tie-in fiction is its emotional resonance, particularly concerning the strained, tragic bond between Raistlin and his twin brother, Caramon. The relationship has always been the emotional beating heart of the Raistlin narrative, and this novel delivers a poignant, haunting reunion in the deepest bowels of Takhisis temple. Hickman and Weis capture the duality of fraternal love entwined with resentment and inevitability. Caramons steadfast, albeit often naive, goodness provides a stark, aching contrast to Raistlins calculated ruthlessness. Their dynamic adds a deeply human layer to a plot that otherwise deals in gods, dragons, and cosmic dominion.

Ultimately, Dragons of the Hourglass Mage is a triumphant return to form that satisfies long-time devotees while offering a compelling entry point into the darker corners of heroic fantasy. It succeeds brilliantly because it refuses to sanitize its antihero. Raistlin is not redeemed here; rather, he is relentlessly understood, exposed in all his brilliant, terrifying complexity. For readers who have walked the roads of Krynn for decades, or those simply eager to witness the crucible that forged a would-be god, this novel is an essential, gripping read that resonates long after the final page is turned.
