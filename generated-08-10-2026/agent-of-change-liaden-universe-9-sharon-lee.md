# Agent of Change (Liaden Universe, #9)

**Author:** Sharon Lee  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

**Agent of Change: A Masterclass in Moral Ambiguity and Cosmic Romance**

Sharon Lee’s *Agent of Change*—the ninth installment in her sprawling *Liaden Universe*—is a masterstroke of space opera, weaving together political intrigue, psychological depth, and a slow-burn romance that feels as electric as it does inevitable. At its core, this novel explores the cost of loyalty, the fragility of identity, and the fine line between hero and villain. Lee, a titan of speculative fiction, crafts a story where the moral gray areas are as vast as the Liaden cosmos itself, and the emotional stakes are as high as the stars above.

Val Con yos’Phelium, once a brilliant First-in Scout, is the novel’s reluctant antihero—a man broken by the Liaden Department of Interior’s sinister "recruitment" process. Brainwashed into becoming an Agent of Change, Val is a weapon of cold efficiency, his conscience long since erased by the Department’s psychological manipulations. His introduction is chillingly effective: Lee doesn’t let us wallow in pity for long. Val is ruthless, calculating, and utterly devoid of remorse, a man who has become the very thing he once despised. Yet, beneath the surface, there’s a flicker of something—something that makes him more than just a hollow shell. His internal conflict is the heart of the novel, a slow unraveling of the facade the Department built around him.

Then comes Miri Robertson, a hardened Terran mercenary with a past as shadowed as Val’s present. Their meeting is a collision of wills, survival instincts, and an undeniable chemistry that Lee teases with masterful restraint. Miri is the grounding force in Val’s moral void, a woman who refuses to be a victim but isn’t afraid to show vulnerability. Their dynamic is electric, charged with tension that isn’t just physical but existential—two people who could destroy each other but are drawn together by forces beyond their control. Lee’s writing here is lush and immersive, painting a vivid picture of a galaxy where politics, war, and personal redemption collide. The romance isn’t just a subplot; it’s the emotional anchor that makes Val’s eventual reckoning with his past feel earned and devastating.

What makes *Agent of Change* truly stand out is Lee’s ability to blend high-stakes action with profound philosophical questions. The Liaden Department of Interior is a chilling metaphor for the dehumanizing effects of state control, a force that doesn’t just recruit operatives—it *remakes* them. Val’s struggle isn’t just about survival; it’s about reclaiming his humanity in a universe that has long since forgotten what that means. Lee doesn’t offer easy answers. There are no neat victories, no sudden epiphanies that erase the past. Instead, she presents a world where redemption is messy, where love is both a weapon and a lifeline, and where the greatest battles are fought not with blasters, but with fractured souls.

The prose is sharp and evocative, with Lee’s signature blend of wit and gravitas. Her worldbuilding is expansive yet intimate, making the Liaden Universe feel both vast and personal. The political maneuvering is intricate, the betrayals are gut-wrenching, and the emotional payoffs are earned through relentless tension. By the time the novel’s climax arrives, you’re not just invested in Val and Miri’s survival—you’re invested in their souls, in their choices, and in the fragile hope that they might find a way to break free from the cycles of violence and control that define their world.

*Agent of Change* is more than just a space opera; it’s a meditation on identity, power, and the cost of survival. Sharon Lee’s writing is as compelling as it is thought-provoking, and this novel is a testament to why her *Liaden Universe* remains one of the most richly realized and emotionally resonant sci-fi series of the last decade. If you’ve ever wondered what happens when a man becomes the very thing he was trained to destroy, or what it takes to reclaim the self you once were, this book is for you.
