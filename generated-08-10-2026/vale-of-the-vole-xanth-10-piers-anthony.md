# Vale of the Vole (Xanth #10)

**Author:** Piers Anthony  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

Piers Anthony returns to the pun-riddled, kaleidoscopic landscape of Xanth with Vale of the Vole, a story that serves as both a reminder of the series’ enduring longevity and a testament to the author’s unwavering commitment to his unique brand of whimsical escapism. Decades after the initial spark of the series, Anthony proves he still possesses the knack for weaving together bizarre magical mechanics and earnest moral quandaries, crafting a narrative that feels like a comfortable homecoming for longtime fans while remaining accessible enough for those unfamiliar with the sprawling history of the magical realm.

The heart of this latest installment is Esk, an ogre-nymph hybrid whose primary "talent"—the literal, magical ability to force others to stop via a single, well-placed "No!"—is a delightful meta-commentary on the nature of protagonist agency. Esk is a character defined by resistance, not just to the external threats plaguing his home, but to the expectations placed upon him by his dual heritage. Anthony’s style remains punchy and irreverent, leaning heavily into the wordplay that has become the hallmark of the Xanth universe. Whether you find the puns charming or eye-rolling, they are the architectural bedrock of this world, and Anthony wields them with the confidence of an author who has spent nearly half a century perfecting his craft.

The plot kicks into gear when Esk, seeking the legendary Good Magician Humphrey, finds himself caught in a classic hero’s journey—a mission of mercy that forces him to step beyond his comfort zone. The introduction of his companions, Chex (a winged centaur of striking presence) and Volney (a courageous burrower), rounds out the adventure with an archetypal party dynamic that keeps the pacing tight. The central conflict, involving a greedy demon horde threatening a pristine river paradise, provides a clear, high-stakes moral anchor for what might otherwise become a meandering trip through Xanth’s geography.

Thematically, the book explores the tension between exploitation and preservation. Beneath the layers of levity and the inevitable slapstick humor of the Xanth landscape, there is a recurring preoccupation with the sanctity of the natural world. By positioning a “protest-ogre” as the defender of an untouched valley, Anthony invites the reader to consider the power of saying no to unchecked consumption and ecological destruction. It is a subtle thread, yet it provides the necessary gravity to keep the reader engaged as the ensemble cast grows in size and complexity.

Stylistically, the prose is unmistakably Anthony: brisk, playful, and relentlessly creative with the rules of its own reality. He maintains a light touch, ensuring that even the most treacherous stretches of the journey never feel truly oppressive. This is fantasy that prioritizes imagination over grit, favoring the wonder of the "what-if" over the grim realities of high-stakes war. 

For those who have walked the paths of Xanth before, Vale of the Vole offers exactly what one expects: a clever, fast-paced quest that highlights the peculiar intersections of biology and magic. It is a work of persistent energy, demonstrating that even after so many volumes, the author’s enthusiasm for his vibrant, nonsensical world has not waned. The book succeeds because it understands exactly what it is—a lighthearted, inventive adventure that invites the reader to suspend disbelief and enjoy the absurdity of a world where a protest can stop a monster in its tracks. 

If you are looking for a story that combines the earnestness of old-school fantasy with a playful, pun-heavy delivery, this volume is a welcome, vibrant addition to the canon. It is a whimsical defense of the vulnerable, written with the characteristic flair that made Xanth a household name for fantasy readers decades ago.
