# Princess of Wands (Special Circumstances, #1)

**Author:** John Ringo  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

John Ringo has built a formidable reputation as a master of military science fiction and high-octane thriller pacing, but in Princess of Wands, he pivots toward the delightfully unexpected. Launching the Special Circumstances series, Ringo takes the suffocating pressures of domestic perfection and crashes them head-on into the occult underworld of the Louisiana bayou. The result is a genre-blending hybrid of Southern Gothic noir and supernatural carnage that reads like Desperate Housewives directed by Sam Raimi. At its core, the novel explores a compelling central theme: the quiet, explosive violence of female emancipation under the crushing weight of impossible societal expectations, paired with the grim realization that sometimes, the only way to reclaim your soul is to unleash absolute hell.

The narrative introduces us to Barbara Everette, a resident of a sleepy Mississippi town who has weaponized suburban perfection. She is a devoted mother, a pillar of the PTA, a model Christian, and, crucially, a practitioner of kung-fu. Barbara has built a gilded cage out of her own relentless competence. Ringo uses her character to skewer the modern myth of the supermom with a sharp, satirical edge, capturing the exact psychological tipping point where endless demands curdle into homicidal exasperation. When Barbara demands just one weekend off for peace and authentic Cajun food, she is not merely escaping chores; she is staging a desperate revolt against the monotony of her own life. 

Parallel to Barbara’s solo road trip is Detective Sergeant Kelly Lockhart of New Orleans Homicide, wrestling with a bizarre string of murders that defy rational police work. Missing suspects, anomalous DNA, and a vanishing fish scale point toward something far more sinister than standard southern crime. Enter local folklore, bayou mysticism, and a cryptic warning about a mysterious "Princess." When Barbara and Kelly's paths inevitably cross in the swampy depths of Louisiana, Ringo seamlessly shifts gears from domestic satire to visceral supernatural thriller. 

Ringo’s signature style is immediately apparent throughout the book: brisk pacing, unapologetic machismo laced with sharp genre self-awareness, and a meticulous attention to the mechanics of action. He treats Barbara’s martial arts training and her subsequent descent into supernatural warfare with the same granular tactical detail he applies to his military sci-fi. Yet, what elevates Princess of Wands above standard genre fare is its sheer audacity and dark humor. Ringo relishes the juxtaposition of mundane suburban anxieties with demonic horrors, creating a breathless atmosphere where a woman craving jambalaya is just as likely to encounter a killer demon as a bad restaurant review. 

While the premise borders on the absurd, Ringo anchors the narrative with robust world-building and an unyielding momentum. The bayou setting feels humid, ancient, and alive with threat, providing a stark, atmospheric contrast to the sterile perfection of Barbara’s former life. It is a world where ancient evils lurk just beneath the surface of everyday life, waiting for someone unhinged enough—or capable enough—to fight back.

Ultimately, Princess of Wands is a wildly entertaining rollercoaster that refuses to take itself too seriously while delivering high-stakes thrills. It is a testament to Ringo’s versatility as a storyteller, proving that he can handle soccer moms turned supernatural warriors just as deftly as interstellar marines. For readers seeking a fast-paced, genre-defying romp driven by an unforgettable protagonist and a healthy dose of dark, southern-fried mayhem, this novel is an absolute must-read.
