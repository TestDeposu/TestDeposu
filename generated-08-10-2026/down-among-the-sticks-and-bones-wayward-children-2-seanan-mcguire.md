# Down Among the Sticks and Bones (Wayward Children, #2)

**Author:** Seanan McGuire  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Every great portal fantasy asks what happens when a child steps through the door into a world of magic. Seanan McGuire’s brilliant Wayward Children series asks the far more devastating question: what happens when they have to come back? 

Following the critical success of *Every Heart a Doorway*, McGuire returns with *Down Among the Sticks and Bones*, a chilling, fiercely poignant prequel that functions as a masterclass in dark fantasy. This standalone novella turns its lens toward twin sisters Jack and Jill, two of the most tragic residents of Eleanor West’s Home for Wayward Children, tracing the exact trajectory of how sweet, stifled girls transform into the hardened, gothic survivors we first met in book one. 

At its core, the novel is a profound exploration of parental toxicity, expectation, and the violence of being misunderstood. Jack and Jill are raised not as people, but as living extensions of their parents' neuroses and unfulfilled ambitions. Jacqueline is molded into her mother’s porcelain doll—silent, impeccably dressed, and suffocating under the weight of forced femininity and emotional sterility. Jillian is her father’s surrogate son—pushed into rough-and-tumble athletics not out of acceptance, but out of his bitter disappointment at not having a boy. They are props in a suburban dollhouse, loved conditionally and governed strictly. 

When the twins stumble upon an impossible staircase in their basement, they don’t just find a fantasy realm; they find an escape from the psychological cage of their upbringing. They tumble into the Moors, a gothic nightmare-scape ruled by mad scientists, vampires, and perpetual twilight. It is a harsh, unforgiving land, yet it paradoxically offers them something the real world never did: agency. 

McGuire’s stylistic brilliance shines brightest in her ability to marry whimsical dark fantasy with genuine emotional horror. Her prose is razor-sharp, lyrical, and unflinching. She treats childhood trauma with a gravity that resonates deeply, making the monsters of the Moors feel almost therapeutic compared to the mundane cruelty of the twins' human parents. The world-building is intensely atmospheric, dripping with the scent of ozone, surgical steel, and copper, painting the Moors not just as a setting, but as a crucible that burns away the girls' humanity and reshapes them into monsters in their own right. 

What makes *Down Among the Sticks and Bones* so striking is its refusal to offer easy redemptions. It is a coming-of-age story told in reverse, where growing up means learning how to bleed and how to harden your heart against a world that demands your compliance. McGuire constructs a narrative that is both heartbreakingly intimate and grandly terrifying, proving once again that she is one of the most distinctive and important voices in modern speculative fiction. For anyone who has ever felt like they were living a life written by someone else, this book is an essential, haunting journey into the dark.
