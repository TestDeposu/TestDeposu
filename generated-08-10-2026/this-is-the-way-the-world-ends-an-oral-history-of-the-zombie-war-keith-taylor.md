# This is the Way the World Ends: An Oral History of the Zombie War

**Author:** Keith Taylor  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Keith Taylor’s 2017 contribution to speculative fiction, This is the Way the World Ends: An Oral History of the Zombie War, arrives not with a cinematic bang, but with the haunting, collective rattle of a dying civilization. While the undead genre has long been saturated with fast-paced action and hollow gore, Taylor pivots sharply toward the intellectual and sociological aftermath of societal collapse. By adopting the oral history format—a structural lineage famously traced by Max Brook’s World War Z—Taylor crafts an intimate mosaic of human failure, resilience, and the bureaucratic absurdity that accompanies the end of days. 

At its core, the novel’s central theme is not the biology of the plague or the mechanics of brain destruction, but the fragility of human systems. Taylor explores how modern institutions—governance, media, global economics, and interpersonal trust—implode under unprecedented pressure. The zombies serve less as traditional monsters and more as a relentless, indifferent solvent, eating away the lacquer of civilization to expose the rotting wood beneath. Through the curated testimonies of politicians, scientists, refugees, and frontline soldiers, Taylor investigates the anatomy of denial. The true horror of the Zombie War, the book argues, was never the dead rising from the graves, but the living refusing to see the cliff until the ground vanished beneath their feet.

Taylor’s stylistic prowess lies in his chameleon-like command of voice. Because the narrative is constructed entirely from interviews, journals, and transcripts, the author must vanish behind his subjects. He succeeds brilliantly. Each speaker possesses a distinct linguistic fingerprint: the panicked, clipped syntax of a mid-level bureaucrat trying to cover his tracks contrasts sharply with the raw, poetic fatalism of a survivor who walked the ruined highways of a forsaken continent. Taylor resists the temptation to make every character a philosopher; instead, they sound frustratingly, brilliantly human. They misremember dates, rationalize their cowardice, romanticize lost loves, and grapple with trauma that defies articulation. 

Furthermore, the pacing of the oral history format gives the book an addictive, documentary-style gravity. Taylor uses temporal jumps and conflicting accounts to create a sense of sprawling, chaotic scale. You do not experience the war from a singular, omniscient vantage point; you piece it together through the static and the smoke. This fragmentation mirrors the psychological state of the survivors themselves, making the reading experience remarkably immersive and disquieting. 

This is the Way the World Ends is a vital, sharply observed entry in apocalyptic literature. It eschews cheap thrills in favor of a profound meditation on memory, culpability, and the stubborn persistence of the human spirit. Taylor has written a war memoir for a war that hasn't happened yet, serving as both a gripping cautionary tale and a masterclass in speculative world-building.
