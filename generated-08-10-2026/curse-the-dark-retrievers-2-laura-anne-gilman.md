# Curse the Dark (Retrievers, #2)

**Author:** Laura Anne Gilman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

**Curse the Dark: A Gritty, Glitching Fantasy Where Magic and Mayhem Collide**

Laura Anne Gilman’s *Curse the Dark*—the second installment in the *Retrievers* series—is a spellbinding descent into a world where the supernatural isn’t just hidden; it’s *breathing in the same air as you*. Set against the neon-lit, tension-charged backdrop of a New York City where humans with arcane abilities (Talent) clash with the ancient, untamed magic of the fatae, this novel is less about whimsical fantasy and more about the kind of danger that seeps into your bones. It’s a story where every shadow could be a curse, every deal could be a trap, and every victory might just be the spark that ignites the next disaster.

At its core, *Curse the Dark* is a thriller disguised as fantasy—a high-stakes chase where the stakes aren’t just lives, but the very fabric of reality. Wren Valere, the series’ fearless protagonist, is a Talent with a reputation for being the best in her field, but her confidence is her greatest liability. When she and her partner, Sergei, are hired to retrieve a cursed parchment—one that promises to unleash a magic so potent it can’t be stopped—she assumes it’s just another job. What she doesn’t realize is that the client has already set a chain reaction in motion, and the retrieval mission is just the first domino. By the time they realize the full horror of what they’ve been hired to do, it’s too late. The magic is loose, the city is on the brink of chaos, and Wren’s signature arrogance might just be the thing that saves them—or dooms them all.

Gilman’s strength lies in her ability to weave a world that feels *alive*, where the supernatural isn’t just a backdrop but a force that warps the mundane. The Cosa Nostradamus setting is a masterclass in blending the familiar with the fantastical—think magic that glitches like a corrupted app, where cell phones and spellbooks coexist in uneasy harmony. The tension between human Talent and the fatae isn’t just political; it’s visceral, a simmering pot of powder kegs ready to explode. Gilman doesn’t shy away from the brutality of her world, and that’s what makes *Curse the Dark* so gripping. There’s no romanticized version of magic here; it’s messy, dangerous, and often indistinguishable from the violence of the human world.

The romance in this series—while never overpowering—isn’t just a subplot; it’s a pressure valve for the explosive energy of the main story. Wren and Sergei’s dynamic is electric, a mix of professional respect and simmering tension that feels earned rather than forced. Their partnership is as much about trust as it is about survival, and Gilman handles their emotional beats with a deft touch, ensuring that the romance never distracts from the overarching threat. It’s a relationship built on shared danger, where love and loyalty are tested as much as the characters themselves.

What truly sets *Curse the Dark* apart is Gilman’s prose—sharp, rhythmic, and dripping with atmosphere. Her writing has a cinematic quality, painting scenes with such vivid detail that you can almost smell the summer-sweltering heat of New York’s streets or feel the weight of the parchment’s curse pressing against your skin. She balances action and introspection seamlessly, giving readers moments of breathless pace alongside quieter, more introspective beats that deepen the characters’ motivations. The pacing is relentless, but never at the expense of clarity; every twist, every revelation, lands with the precision of a well-thrown knife.

Yet for all its strengths, *Curse the Dark* isn’t without its challenges. Some readers might find the worldbuilding slightly dense at first, particularly those unfamiliar with the series’ lore. But Gilman’s writing is so immersive that the details—like the rules of Talent, the hierarchy of the fatae, or the mechanics of the cursed magic—don’t just inform the story; they *drive* it. By the time you realize you’re fully invested, you’ll be racing to keep up.

Ultimately, *Curse the Dark* is a testament to Gilman’s ability to craft a fantasy series that feels fresh, relevant, and utterly compelling. It’s a story about power, consequence, and the fine line between heroism and hubris. Wren Valere is a protagonist you’ll love to hate (and love all the more for it), and the world she inhabits is one you won’t want to leave. If you’re looking for a fantasy that’s equal parts pulse-pounding thriller and thoughtfully constructed world, this is it—a book where the darkest curses are the ones you can’t outrun, and the only miracle you might need is the one that doesn’t exist.
