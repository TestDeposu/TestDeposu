# Fury

**Author:** Laurann Dohner  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

Unleashing the Beast: A Review of Laurann Dohner’s Fury

In the ever-expanding universe of paranormal romance and speculative romantic fiction, few authors command the realm of "species" romance quite like Laurann Dohner. With her 2016 rerelease of Fury, Dohner delivers a high-octane, emotionally charged narrative that leans heavily into the classic Beauty and the Beast dynamic, infused with sci-fi experimentation and raw, unfiltered primal passion. At its core, Fury is a novel driven by the timeless theme of redemption through love, exploring how profound trauma can be dismantled not by force, but by unwavering empathy and desire.

The story centers on Ellie, an ethical whistle-blower trapped within the sterile, nightmarish confines of a corrupt pharmaceutical corporation. When she uncovers the horrors of illegal DNA splicing—creations that bridge the gap between human and animal—she loses her heart to one specific captive. Fury, the titular hero, is the embodiment of malevolent captivity. Having endured a lifetime of chains, torture, and a devastating betrayal by the only human he ever trusted, Fury is pure vengeance incarnate when he finally breaks free. Yet, when he captures Ellie to exact his retribution, the narrative pivots into a breathless study of instinct, chemistry, and emotional reconstruction.

Dohner’s stylistic approach is unapologetic, direct, and fast-paced. She does not waste time with overly florid prose; instead, she immerses the reader immediately into the visceral sensory experience of her characters. Her style is characterized by intense emotional stakes, alpha-hero intensity, and a fierce, protective dynamic that defines the romance genre at its most indulgent. Fury is a creature of shadows and primal urges, yet Dohner paints him with strokes of deep vulnerability. His transition from a tortured prisoner seeking blood to an obsessively devoted protector is executed with the narrative speed and heat that fans of the genre crave.

The central theme of the novel examines the duality of monstrosity. Who are the true monsters here? The scientists in white lab coats playing God behind closed doors, or the genetically altered beings who merely react to the cruelty inflicted upon them? Ellie serves as the moral compass, driven by guilt and profound compassion, striving to prove that humanity is not solely defined by the actions of the worst among us. Her fierce determination to heal Fury's desolate heart elevates the book from a mere creature-feature romance into a deeply satisfying emotional journey. 

While the plot treads familiar waters for readers acquainted with speculative romance tropes, Dohner’s execution is razor-sharp. She knows exactly what her audience wants: high-stakes peril, palpable romantic tension, explicit passion, and a love that conquers insurmountable odds. Fury delivers on every front, offering a striking, pulse-pounding read that lingers long after the final page. For enthusiasts of dark, edgy paranormal romance who love their heroes fierce and their heroines fiercely compassionate, Fury remains a roaring success.
