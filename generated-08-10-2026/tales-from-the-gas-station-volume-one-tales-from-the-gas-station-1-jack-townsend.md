# Tales from the Gas Station: Volume One (Tales from the Gas Station #1)

**Author:** Jack Townsend  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2013

It is a rare and delightful occurrence when a collection of Regency romances manages to balance the rigid social expectations of the nineteenth century with the messy, beating heart of genuine emotion. In this digital omnibus, Candice Hern offers readers a masterclass in the genre, proving once again why she is held in such high regard by fans of the traditional, wit-forward style pioneered by Georgette Heyer.

The collection, featuring A Proper Companion, A Change of Heart, and An Affair of Honor, is anchored by a recurring motif: the transformation of the rakish bachelor. In the hands of a lesser writer, this trope can feel weary and predictable. However, Hern breathes new life into the narrative by focusing on the friction between reputation and vulnerability. Her male leads—Robert, Jack, and Lord Sedgewick—are all men of the ton, burdened by the expectations of their rank, yet they find their carefully constructed worlds dismantled by women who possess something far more valuable than a dowry: intellect, resilience, and a sharp sense of self.

Hern’s prose is elegant and meticulously crafted. She has a keen eye for the architectural details of a ballroom and the subtle, nuanced language of a social faux pas. Her strength, however, lies in her dialogue. She captures the banter of the Regency era with a playful, rhythmic quality that feels authentic without ever becoming inaccessible. When her characters spar, the reader is treated to a duel of wits that serves as the perfect precursor to their eventual romantic surrender.

In A Proper Companion, the tension between Robert and Emily is palpable, fueled by the proximity forced upon them by the dowager countess. Hern navigates the transition from duty to desire with a steady, earned pace. Similarly, A Change of Heart offers a more melancholic, poignant exploration of wounded souls seeking redemption, while An Affair of Honor leans into the dramatic, grounding its romance in the physical reality of a second chance facilitated by an unexpected carriage accident. 

Across these three novels, the theme remains constant: love is rarely a matter of convenience, but rather a sudden, undeniable collision of values. Hern does not rely on forced misunderstandings or dramatic contrivances to keep her stories moving. Instead, she allows the internal growth of her characters to drive the narrative. Whether it is the rugged cliffs of the South Devon coast or the glittering, suffocating atmosphere of London’s elite ballrooms, Hern paints her settings with a vivid brush, making the environment feel like a participant in the unfolding dramas.

This collection is a testament to the enduring appeal of the Regency romance when written with intelligence and heart. Hern’s craftsmanship ensures that these stories feel timeless rather than dated. For those who crave stories that celebrate the triumph of wit over cynicism, and the slow, steady burn of affection over superficial attraction, this volume is an essential addition to any library. It is a work of superior merit—a polished, sophisticated, and deeply satisfying collection that confirms Hern’s position as a stalwart of the genre.
