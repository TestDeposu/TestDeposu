# Empire (In Her Name: Redemption, #1)

**Author:** Michael R. Hicks  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

War, loss, and the heavy burden of survival form the crucible in which Michael R. Hicks forges his gripping military science fiction novel, Empire. Serving as the fourth entry in the expansive In Her Name saga, this novel shifts the focus from grand interstellar politics to a deeply personal, harrowing human story. Hicks masterfully uses the sweeping backdrop of an alien invasion not merely as a spectacle, but as a scalpel to dissect the resilience of the human spirit.

At the heart of Empire is the profound theme of coming-of-age amidst absolute devastation. We first meet seven-year-old Reza Gard as a broken orphan, stripped of his family by the merciless Kreelan warrior priestess Tesh-Dar. Hicks traces Reza’s trajectory from victim to survivor, grounding the cosmic conflict in the stark, muddy reality of slave labor on the agricultural world of Hallmark. The author boldly explores how trauma shapes identity, contrasting the cold brutality of the Kreelan empire with the systemic exploitation found within human civilization itself. Reza's survival is not defined by innate superhuman traits, but by endurance, adaptation, and the unexpected bonds he forms—most notably with his surrogate father figure, the scarred Marine veteran Wiley Hickock. 

Hicks adopts a brisk, action-oriented style that keeps the narrative propulsive without sacrificing emotional resonance. His world-building relies on stark contrasts: the claustrophobic, abusive confines of the orphanage give way to the vast, terrifying majesty of interstellar warfare. The author effectively blends hard military scifi elements with a touch of mystical wonder, particularly through the enigmatic nature of the Kreelans and their warrior priestesses, framing advanced science almost like an ancient, esoteric magic. 

As the narrative leaps forward five years, Hicks introduces layers of forbidden romance and youthful idealism through Reza and fellow orphan Nicole Carré. This brief taste of peace and normality makes the subsequent tragedy all the more impactful. When Tesh-Dar returns, the stakes escalate from mere territorial conquest to a chilling, intimate horror: the harvesting of human children for a mysterious imperial experiment. This plot pivot transforms Empire from a standard revenge tale into a complex philosophical chess match. The fates of two entire civilizations hang in the balance, resting not on the actions of generals, but on the scarred souls of those who refused to break.

Hicks delivers a compelling blend of coming-of-age drama and high-stakes military adventure. Empire succeeds because it never loses sight of the human cost of war, proving that even within the cold machinery of galactic empires, the fiercest battles are fought within the human heart.
