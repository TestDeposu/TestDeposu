# Crossing in Time (Between Two Evils, #1)

**Author:** D.L. Orton  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Unknown

Title: Echoes of Tomorrow: The Chilling Precision of D.L. Orton’s Crossing in Time

Time travel is a notoriously treacherous genre. Writers often get bogged down in the paradoxes, losing the human element in a maze of temporal mechanics. Yet, in Crossing in Time, the inaugural volume of the Between Two Evils series, D.L. Orton bypasses the usual scientific hand-wringing to deliver a taut, deeply psychological thriller where the greatest danger isn't altering the past—it is confronting the darkest reflections of who we might become.

Orton’s central theme pivots on the haunting concept of choice and consequence. The narrative explores how the smallest divergence in a timeline can compound into monumental catastrophe, turning ordinary lives into battlegrounds of survival. Rather than treating time as a vast, abstract playground, Orton brings it down to a suffocatingly intimate scale. The past and future are not distant concepts; they are breathing, bleeding entities pressing in from all sides. The core question driving the plot is deceptively simple yet terrifying: If you could see the ruin ahead, how far would you go to stop it, and what pieces of your soul would you have to sacrifice in the process?

Stylistically, Orton writes with a cinematic urgency that hooks the reader from the opening pages and refuses to let go. Her prose is lean, purposeful, and relentlessly paced. There is very little narrative fat here; every scene functions as a crucial gear in a complex, ticking machine. Orton excels at world-building through implication rather than exposition. Instead of overwhelming the reader with info-dumps about how the timeline fractures, she anchors the high-concept sci-fi elements in stark, gritty realism. The atmosphere is thick with dread, paranoia, and a palpable sense of isolation. 

What truly elevates Crossing in Time above standard genre fare is Orton’s handling of character. The protagonists are refreshingly flawed, driven by desperation and fragmented memories rather than noble heroism. They make mistakes, miscalculate, and often find themselves trapped between two equally undesirable outcomes—hence the apt subtitle of the series. Orton’s dialogue crackles with tension, revealing underlying mistrust and shifting alliances that keep the reader guessing until the very last chapter. 

As a first installment, Crossing in Time lays a formidable foundation. It is a smart, fast-paced speculative thriller that marries the intricate plotting of classic science fiction with the visceral thrills of modern suspense. D.L. Orton has crafted a compelling universe that challenges our perception of destiny and free will. For readers who like their time travel dark, cerebral, and relentlessly suspenseful, this book is an absolute must-read.
