# King Sorrow

**Author:** Joe Hill  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

Joe Hill returns in 2025 with King Sorrow, a razor-sharp, pitch-black descent into the ivy-covered halls of dark academia that proves once again why he is a master of modern horror. Blending the nostalgic vulnerability of youth with the absolute dread of cosmic consequences, Hill crafts a chilling fable about the steep price of desperation. Set against the atmospheric, frost-bitten backdrop of Maine’s Rackham College, the novel takes the classic Faustian bargain and violently drags it into the twenty-first century.

At the heart of the narrative is Arthur Oakes, an unassuming dreamer whose idyllic academic life is upended by a brutal extortion plot. Forced to steal rare volumes from the college’s prized library, Arthur is cornered with no way out. Enter a tight-knit circle of friends, including the charismatic Colin Wren and the brilliant Gwen Underfoot, who rally to his defense. Together, they turn to an artifact of dark lore: the Crane journal, a sinister text bound in human skin. What begins as a desperate, reckless fantasy to summon a dragon quickly spirals into a horrifying reality. They succeed, but in doing so, they bind themselves to King Sorrow—an insatiable, ancient entity that demands an annual human sacrifice to keep its hunger at bay.

Hill’s greatest triumph in King Sorrow lies in his exploration of the novel's central theme: the corrosive nature of vengeance and the terrifying slippery slope of moral compromise. The dragon serves as a brilliant metaphor for the shortcuts we wish for when life backs us into a corner. Arthur and his friends do not start out as monsters; they are merely kids trying to fix an unbearable situation. Hill captures the unbearable weight of adolescence, where every emotional stake feels life-or-death, perfectly mirroring it with literal matters of life and death. The transition from innocent academic camaraderie to blood-pact paranoia is handled with psychological acuity, forcing the reader to constantly ask a haunting question: What would you sacrifice to save the person you love?

Stylistically, Hill is operating at the peak of his powers. He balances visceral, blood-curdling horror with a lyrical, character-driven prose style reminiscent of classic Stephen King, yet stamped with a distinctly contemporary urgency. The academic setting is rendered with tactile precision—you can smell the old paper in the library and feel the biting Maine chill seep through the pages. Furthermore, Hill maintains a relentless pacing that makes the descent into the occult feel terrifyingly inevitable. As the yearly sacrifices loom and the bonds of friendship fray under the pressure of guilt and survival, the tension ratchets up to an unbearable pitch.

King Sorrow is a brilliantly cruel and deeply empathetic novel that cements Joe Hill’s legacy as a titan of contemporary horror. It is a story about the stories we tell ourselves to justify the unjustifiable, anchored by unforgettable characters and a stinging twist in its tail that will leave readers breathless long after the final page is turned.
