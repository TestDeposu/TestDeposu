# Target (Star Wars: Rebel Force, #1)

**Author:** Alex Wheeler  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

The destruction of the Death Star was supposed to be the ultimate triumph for the Rebel Alliance, a brilliant flash of hope in an otherwise dark galaxy. Yet, as Alex Wheeler demonstrates in Target, the first installment of the Rebel Force series, saving the universe is remarkably expensive, and glorious victories rarely pay the bills. Set in the critical narrative vacuum between Episode IV: A New Hope and Episode V: The Empire Strikes Back, Wheeler’s novel strips away the cinematic gloss of open space battles to examine the gritty, bureaucratic, and financial realities of guerrilla warfare. 

The central theme of the novel pivots on vulnerability and the high stakes of anonymity. With the Empire reeling from its humiliating defeat, Darth Vader and Imperial intelligence become singularly obsessed with unmasking the Force-sensitive farm boy who pulled the trigger. Luke Skywalker’s name becomes the most dangerous secret in the galaxy. Wheeler brilliantly contrasts the grand scale of the galactic civil war with the claustrophobic tension of keeping identities hidden. The Alliance is not just fighting for freedom; they are fighting against bankruptcy. This clever narrative hook grounds a sprawling space opera in a surprisingly relatable struggle: the desperate search for funding. To secure their survival, Luke, Leia, Han, Chewbacca, and the droids are forced to journey to Muunilinst, the ancient financial heart of the galaxy. 

Wheeler captures the distinct voices of the classic trilogy effortlessly, balancing Han Solo’s cynical swagger, Princess Leia’s unyielding diplomatic resolve, and Luke’s earnest, conflicted idealism. However, the true standout addition to the narrative ecosystem is X-7, the Empire's premier assassin. X-7 is not merely a blunt instrument of the dark side; he is a chillingly methodical predator designed to infiltrate, manipulate, and destroy from within. By pitting the beloved core trio against an adversary who matches their tactical ingenuity with lethal precision, Wheeler elevates the book from a standard tie-in adventure to a tense, psychological cat-and-mouse game. 

The author’s style is punchy, cinematic, and fast-paced, clearly tailored to hook younger readers while retaining enough traditional Star Wars lore to satisfy longtime enthusiasts. Wheeler wastes no time on sprawling exposition, instead driving the plot forward through high-stakes momentum and snappy dialogue. The pacing mirrors a breathless spy thriller set against the backdrop of a galaxy far, far away, blending the espionage elements of the Rebellion with the iconic iconography of the original films. 

Ultimately, Target succeeds because it reminds us that the road from Yavin to Hoth was fraught with quiet perils long before the Imperial walkers marched across the snow. Wheeler delivers an entertaining, sharp, and engaging opening salvo that proves the Rebel Alliance’s greatest enemy isn't just the might of the Imperial fleet—it's the harsh, unyielding mathematics of survival.
