# Boys, Beasts & Men

**Author:** Sam J. Miller  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

To read Sam J. Miller is to willingly step into a landscape where the tenderest human vulnerabilities and the most ferocious speculative elements tear at one another until they bleed. Boys, Beasts & Men, the 2023 Locus Award-winning collection, is not merely a gathering of short fiction; it is a masterclass in emotional devastation and genre alchemy. Miller takes the raw materials of queer longing, systemic cruelty, and apocalyptic dread and forges them into something luminous, brutal, and entirely unforgettable.

At the beating heart of this collection is a relentless exploration of marginalization and survival. Miller’s protagonists are rarely safe. They are foster teens navigating digital landscapes, queer artists rebelling against the erasure of death, and survivors of shapeshifting alien invasions haunted by what they might have carried out of the ice. Yet, vulnerability in Miller’s universe is never a simple weakness; it is a catalyst. Whether his characters are exacting meticulously calculated revenge upon their bullies using unnatural powers of suggestion or falling ruinously for clever con-men, they burn with a fierce, desperate agency. The collection’s title acts as a guiding compass, mapping out the fragile boundaries between youth, monstrosity, and the heavy burden of manhood. These are figures who threaten to destroy the reader, yet leave us helplessly aching for more.

Stylistically, Miller operates with a rare, intoxicating duality. His prose is lush and deeply poetic, capable of conjuring gorgeous, immersive worlds with just a few strokes. At the same time, his storytelling is anchored by an unrelenting candor and a savage wit that cuts straight to the bone. There is a cinematic immediacy to his narratives—a breathless pacing that pulls you under and refuses to let go until the final, haunting line. He effortlessly blurs the lines between science fiction, dark fantasy, and literary realism, creating a genre-fluid tapestry that evokes the restless, visionary energy of masters like Carmen Maria Machado and Jeff VanderMeer. 

What makes Boys, Beasts & Men an essential career retrospective is its refusal to offer easy consolations. Miller does not wrap his stories in neat, comforting bows. Instead, he lingers in the messy, complex aftermaths of heartbreak, infatuation, and violence. He interrogates the monsters we harbor inside us and the ones we face in the world, reminding us that survival often demands a transformation of our very souls. This is a collection that will haunt you long after the final page, cementing Miller’s rightful place among the elite architects of modern speculative fiction.
