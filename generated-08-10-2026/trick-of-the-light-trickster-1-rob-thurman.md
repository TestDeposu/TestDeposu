# Trick of the Light (Trickster, #1)

**Author:** Rob Thurman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

When Rob Thurman unleashes a universe, readers should brace for a sensory onslaught of neon, blood, and brimstone. With Trick of the Light, the opening salvo in the Trickster series, Thurman does not merely dip a toe into the urban fantasy pool; she dives headfirst into a churning, high-octane maelstrom of celestial warfare and underworld grit. Published in 2009, this novel cemented Thurman’s reputation as a purveyor of unapologetically fierce, fast-paced speculative fiction. At its core, the book delivers a thrilling examination of morality, identity, and the dangerous currency of choice in a universe that has long since forgotten the gray areas between good and evil.

The narrative engine driving Trick of the Light revolves around Trixa, a protagonist whose very existence is a masterclass in survival. When she catches wind of the Light of Life—a legendary artifact potent enough to tip the eternal scale—she realizes she has stumbled upon the ultimate cosmic lottery ticket. Both Heaven and Hell are violently vested in acquiring it, transforming the search into a high-stakes scavenger hunt with apocalyptic consequences. Yet, the true brilliance of Thurman’s thematic exploration lies not in the shiny MacGuffin itself, but in what the pursuit demands of Trixa. Caught in the crossfire of a divine war, she is forced to confront a brutal reality: in a world split strictly between angels and demons, neutrality is an illusion, and picking the wrong side costs far more than mere mortality. 

Thurman’s signature style is immediately recognizable and fiercely intoxicating. Her prose is drenched in attitude, moving with a breakneck velocity that mirrors the chaos of her plots. She writes with a distinct urban snark, infusing dark fantasy tropes with a modern, razor-sharp edge. There is no room for flowery, meandering exposition in Thurman’s world; instead, the world-building is delivered in sharp, visceral bursts that capture the sensory overload of a supernatural underworld hidden just beneath the neon-lit veneer of everyday reality. The dialogue is snappy, aggressive, and laced with gallows humor, serving as both armor and weapon for characters who live on borrowed time. 

Furthermore, Thurman excels at painting the divine and the demonic with strokes of gritty realism. Heaven and Hell are not distant, abstract theological concepts here; they are oppressive, corporate, and terrifyingly hands-on forces meddling in mortal affairs. By anchoring this grand, apocalyptic chess match to the gritty, street-level perspective of a protagonist who operates outside the conventional rules, Thurman grounds the cosmic stakes in deeply personal, visceral drama. 

Trick of the Light is a roaring, kinetic triumph of urban fantasy. It demands your attention from the first page and refuses to let go until the dust of the celestial battlefield finally settles. For readers hungry for high-stakes action, morally gray heroines who bite back, and a world where angels and demons are equally terrifying, Thurman’s 2009 entry remains an absolute must-read. Fast, furious, and fiercely entertaining, it proves that when the world is ending, the only way out is straight through the fire.
