Before diving into the review, it is imperative to address a significant discrepancy in the provided data. While the title of this inquiry is labeled as Dakota Krout’s Dungeon Born, the synopsis and promotional material provided actually describe the second installment of the Ice Trials series, titled Time Trials, co-authored by M.A. Rothman and D.J. Butler. Consequently, this review will focus on the work described in the text provided: the high-stakes, time-traveling adventure found in Time Trials.

Review: Time Trials

In the crowded genre of portal fantasy and LitRPG, many authors struggle to balance mechanical complexity with genuine narrative heart. M.A. Rothman and D.J. Butler, however, have crafted something distinct in Time Trials. By blending the rigorous curiosity of an archaeology thriller with the visceral progression systems of Gamelit, they have created a narrative that feels both intellectually grounded and pulse-pounding.

The story follows Marty Cohen, a brilliant, unconventional Egyptologist, whose team is displaced from modern-day North Africa into a mysterious, isolated island nation through a temporal anomaly. The core theme of the novel centers on the burden of the outsider. Marty and his crew are not merely travelers; they are viewed as harbingers of fate by an ancient civilization teetering on the edge of a cataclysmic flood. This shift in perspective—from explorers to mythic figures—forces the characters to confront their own limitations. The authors expertly mine the tension between the characters’ modern, scientific mindsets and the desperate, prophecy-driven realities of the people they encounter.

Rothman and Butler’s writing style is a masterclass in momentum. The prose is lean and utilitarian, favoring rapid pacing and high-stakes decision-making. There is a palpable sense of scale here, echoing the grand scope of an Indiana Jones adventure filtered through the lens of a Stargate-style sci-fi conundrum. The integration of gaming elements is particularly noteworthy; unlike many in the genre who rely on tedious stat-blocks, these authors weave the mechanics into the fabric of the characters’ survival. It feels organic, acting as an extension of the environment rather than a distraction from it.

What truly elevates this book, however, is the ethical conflict. At its zenith, the plot forces Marty to decide between the lives of his team and the survival of a nation. This is not just a tactical choice; it is a moral one that challenges the reader to consider the cost of interventionism. By positioning Marty as a reluctant leader, the authors ensure the stakes feel personal and heavy, grounding the fantastical premise in human vulnerability.

For fans of historical fantasy and those who enjoy seeing modern intellects forced to survive in brutal, low-tech environments, Time Trials is an essential read. It is a story that manages to be both a rigorous exercise in world-building and an adrenaline-fueled romp. While the intersection of prophecy and modern science can be a difficult tightrope to walk, Rothman and Butler traverse it with confidence. If you have been looking for an isekai narrative that treats its archaeology as seriously as its action, you have found your next obsession.
