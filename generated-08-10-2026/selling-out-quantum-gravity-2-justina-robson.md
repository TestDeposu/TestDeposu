# Selling Out (Quantum Gravity, #2)

**Author:** Justina Robson  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

Meet Mennik Thorn: a broke, traumatized, second-rate mage with a penchant for ticking off the wrong people and a fierce, unwavering loyalty to his oldest friend. In a genre often dominated by chosen ones, untouchable wizards, and grand prophecies, Mennik is a breath of fresh, cynical air. He is a diamond-in-the-rough protagonist who would rather complain about his aching joints than save the universe, yet he finds himself doing just that. 

While the provided metadata occasionally references the series under alternative titles or attributes blurbs from stellar authors like Justina Robson herself, the core of the experience remains brilliantly intact: this is urban-flavored fantasy noir at its finest. The narrative plunges readers headfirst into the decaying, morally bankrupt underbelly of the city of Agatos. When a simple favor—a routine curse-breaking meant to settle an old debt—goes disastrously sideways, Mennik is framed for murder. Suddenly, the most hated man in the room has to navigate the treacherous, glittering corridors of power belonging to the city's high mages—a world he deliberately abandoned years ago.

The central theme of the novel hinges on survival against systemic corruption and the heavy, burdensome cost of true friendship. Mennik is a man caught between his past mistakes and a ruthless, unknown adversary. To clear his name, he must confront supernatural beasts, outrun the lethal, mage-killing Ash Guard, and outsmart enemies who view human lives as disposable currency. It is a classic David-versus-Goliath scenario, but David has a hangover, zero budget, and a foul mouth.

Stylistically, the writing is sharp, fast-paced, and effortlessly quick-witted. The prose walks a delicate tightrope between grim urban grit and laugh-out-loud snark, drawing favorable and well-deserved comparisons to the intricate con artistry of Scott Lynch and the hardboiled supernatural detective work of Jim Butcher. The world-building is immersive without bogging down the pacing, relying on clever dialogue and high-stakes plotting to keep pages turning. Every scene feels earned, driving Mennik further into a corner where his only weapons are his wits, his stubbornness, and whatever meager magic he can summon.

Ultimately, this acclaimed fantasy mystery delivers massive entertainment value. It is witty, deftly plotted, and emotionally grounded by a protagonist you cannot help but root for, even as he stumbles from one disaster to the next. For readers craving a gritty, humorous, and deeply satisfying journey through magical crime and corruption, this adventure is an absolute must-read.
