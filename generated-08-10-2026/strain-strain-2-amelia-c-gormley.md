# Strain (Strain, #2)

**Author:** Amelia C. Gormley  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

In Strain, the compelling second installment of Amelia C. Gormley’s dystopian series, the collapse of civilization serves not merely as a backdrop for action, but as a crucible for human intimacy. Set in the early twenty-second century in the shadow of a devastating, civilization-altering virus, Gormley crafts a narrative where the end of the world is deeply personal. Eschewing grand, sweeping battlefields for the raw, bruised interiors of human survival, the author explores a profound central theme: how love and morality endure when society itself has flatlined.

Gormley’s stylistic mastery lies in her ability to balance harrowing external stakes with tender, agonizing emotional vulnerability. The novel follows two distinct pairs whose lives collide in the ruins of the old world. First are Nico, a rent-boy hardened by circumstance, and Zach, a preacher’s son grappling with a shattered faith. Both carry the crushing weight of accidental culpability for the disaster. Their romance is defined by a visceral, heart-wrenching tension; every touch carries the terrifying possibility of infection, turning physical intimacy into an act of supreme bravery and desperate defiance. 

Parallel to their journey is the dynamic between Darius, a hardened soldier forged in the brutal fires of the plague era, and Rhys, who has grown up under the shadow of the pandemic. When Rhys faces exposure, Darius is confronted with a grim calculus of mercy, yet chooses instead to fight for a life, reclaiming his own long-lost humanity in the process. When these four lives finally intersect, the narrative shifts from mere survival to a quest for retributive justice and, tentatively, a fragile hope for tomorrow.

Gormley’s prose is lean, evocative, and unapologetically emotional. She does not sugarcoat the decay of the twenty-second century, yet she refuses to let cynicism eclipse her characters. The pacing is tight, moving briskly between the psychological claustrophobia of survival and the expansive, burning desire for connection. In Strain, Gormley proves that the most resilient virus of all is the human capacity to care, making this installment a striking, poignant read that lingers long after the final page.
