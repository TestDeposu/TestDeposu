# The Collected John Carter of Mars: A Princess of Mars, The Gods of Mars, The Warlord of Mars (Barsoom, #1-3)

**Author:** Edgar Rice Burroughs  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

Before galaxies far, far away captured the collective imagination of humanity, there was Barsoom. Long before modern science fiction found its footing in cinematic spectacles and hard speculative theory, Edgar Rice Burroughs constructed a vibrant, crimson-hued playground that would fundamentally alter the trajectory of adventure fiction. The Collected John Carter of Mars, housing the foundational trilogy—A Princess of Mars, The Gods of Mars, and The Warlord of Mars—serves as a magnificent monument to the birth of planetary romance, reminding modern readers why imagination once reigned supreme over empirical grit.

At the beating heart of this omnibus is a masterclass in escapism, anchored by the quintessential Victorian gentleman-turned-warrior, John Carter. Transported mysteriously from an Arizona cave to a dying, water-starved Mars in the late 1800s, Carter immediately finds himself entangled in planetary politics, arena combat, and sweeping romantic quests. Yet, beyond the dashing heroics and swashbuckling bravado, the central theme running through these inaugural adventures is the sheer resilience of life against the inevitable tide of entropy. Barsoom is not merely a setting; it is a dying world gasping for its final breath, yet paradoxically teeming with fierce warriors, monolithic cities, terrifying multi-limbed beasts, and complex religious hierarchies. Burroughs uses this desolate yet breathtaking landscape to explore how honor, loyalty, and passion transcend planetary boundaries, proving that the human (or in this case, trans-terrestrial) spirit thrives most brilliantly when pushed to the absolute brink of extinction.

Stylistically, Burroughs writes with a breathless, unapologetic kinetic energy that defines the pulp fiction era. He was not a stylist in the vein of literary heavyweights; rather, he was a grand architect of momentum. Every chapter acts as a cliffhanger, plunging Carter from one perilous frying pan into an even more exotic fire. The prose is grandiose, melodramatic, and fiercely romantic, painting Barsoom with sweeping, Technicolor strokes. While modern critics might point to repetitive narrative structures—where Carter is perpetually separated from his beloved Dejah Thoris only to embark on an epic quest across the red deserts to reclaim her—this cyclical quest mythos is precisely what gives the trilogy its hypnotic, fairy-tale cadence. It reads like the fever dream of a nineteenth-century cartographer who traded reality for pure, unadulterated myth.

The cultural impact of these collected pages cannot be overstated. When legendary science fiction authors, astrophysicists, and space exploration pioneers credit Burroughs with sparking their childhood wonder, it becomes clear that Barsoom was the crucible for the space age. Long before NASA pointed lenses toward the real fourth planet from the sun, Burroughs made humanity look up and wonder what secrets lay beneath the rust-colored dust. 

This 2022 collection is an essential portal. For veterans of classic speculative fiction, it is a nostalgic homecoming to the roots of planetary adventure; for newcomers, it is a thrilling education in how the tropes of modern sci-fi and fantasy were forged. Burroughs invites us to cast off the heavy chains of Earth's gravity, leap great distances through the low Martian atmosphere, and remember what it feels like to read with wide-eyed, unbridled awe.
