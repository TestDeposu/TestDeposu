# Ashes (Ashes Trilogy, #1)

**Author:** Ilsa J. Bick  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

In Ashes, her chilling 2011 young adult dystopian novel, Ilsa J. Bick imagines a modern apocalypse that feels entirely plausible, stripping humanity down to its rawest instincts. The premise begins with a terrifying flash: an electromagnetic pulse (EMP) erupts across the sky, obliterating every electronic device, crashing global infrastructure, and instantaneously killing billions. Into this sudden, terrifying silence steps Alex, a teenager hiking deep in the woods to mourn her parents and grapple with her own terminal illness. When the pulse knocks her to the ground, she emerges into a world turned upside down, eventually crossing paths with Tom, a disillusioned young soldier, and Ellie, a sharp-witted girl reeling from the murder of her grandfather. 

Bick immediately anchors the narrative in the visceral dread of isolation. The central theme of Ashes is not merely survival in a hostile post-collapse landscape, but the haunting question of what remains of human morality when civilization vanishes in a microsecond. The EMP acts as a great equalizer, but it also strips away the social contracts that keep darkness at bay. As Alex, Tom, and Ellie form a fragile, improvised family, Bick forces her characters—and her readers—to confront a deeply unsettling dilemma: in a world where infrastructure has crumbled, how do you distinguish between a fellow survivor and a monster? The psychological toll of the catastrophe is heavier than the physical danger, as the trio must navigate a landscape crawling with those who were spared, yet may no longer be entirely human. 

The author’s style is relentless, cinematic, and unflinching. Bick writes with a sharp urgency that mirrors the chaotic reality of her characters. She does not sugarcoat the brutality of the new world; instead, she leans heavily into sensory details—the suffocating quiet of a dead world, the biting chill of the wilderness, and the constant, gnawing paranoia of not knowing who can be trusted. Her prose is fast-paced yet emotionally grounded, ensuring that the grand-scale devastation of a global EMP is always intimately tied to the personal grief and resilience of her protagonists. The dialogue is snappy and authentic, capturing the distinct voices of youth thrust prematurely into a war zone of their own making. 

Ultimately, Ashes is a striking, adrenaline-fueled entry into the dystopian genre. Bick crafts a world that is as terrifying as it is mesmerizing, offering a stark reminder of how fragile our hyper-connected society truly is. For readers who crave high-stakes tension, complex characters, and a grimly realistic exploration of human nature under pressure, this novel is an unforgettable descent into the dark.
