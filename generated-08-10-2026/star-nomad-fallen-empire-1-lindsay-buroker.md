# Star Nomad (Fallen Empire, #1)

**Author:** Lindsay Buroker  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Desperation is a powerful motivator, but for former Alliance fighter pilot Alisa Marchenko, it is the only thing keeping her breathing. In Star Nomad, the explosive first installment of Lindsay Buroker’s Fallen Empire series, the grand celebrations of a toppled galactic tyranny mean very little when you are stranded on a wretched dustball of a planet, billions of miles away from your young daughter. Buroker immediately subverts the traditional space-opera victory lap. Instead of focusing on the triumphant politicians or celebrated commanders, she zeroes in on the wreckage left behind, crafting a story where freedom is a luxury for the privileged, and survival is a daily hustle for everyone else.

At its core, the main theme of Star Nomad is the relentless, ferocious drive of maternal devotion colliding with the gritty reality of a post-war galaxy. Alisa’s quest is not about saving the universe or restoring honor to a shattered regime; it is intensely personal. Her entire world has shrunk to a singular, agonizing point: getting back to her child. Buroker uses this raw emotional anchor to drive the narrative forward at a breathless, unrelenting pace. When Alisa hatches a plan to steal a broken-down, half-functional freighter from a junkyard crawling with lawless savages, readers know it is a fool's errand. Yet, the sheer force of her desperation makes it utterly believable.

Of course, no good heist goes according to plan. Enter Leon, an elite cyborg soldier squatting in the stolen ship with his own secretive agenda and a well-documented history of killing Alliance pilots. The dynamic between Alisa and Leon forms the beating heart of the book’s style. Buroker leans heavily into the beloved, classic tropes of science fiction—heavily drawing inspiration from the rough-around-the-edges, sarcastic camaraderie of Firefly and the gritty, lived-in galactic aesthetics of early Star Wars—while injecting her own signature brand of snappy, witty dialogue. 

The author’s style is brisk, accessible, and effortlessly entertaining. Buroker does not waste time with overly dense world-building or sluggish exposition; she trusts the environment and the characters' actions to fill in the blanks. The banter between Alisa and Leon crackles with tension and humor, providing a much-needed levity that balances the high-stakes peril. They are an unlikely pair of castoffs forced into an uneasy alliance, and watching their professional friction slowly morph into a deeper, grudging respect is one of the novel's greatest joys. 

Star Nomad does not pretend to be high-minded, pretentious literature. Instead, it offers pure, unadulterated escapism. It is a popcorn space opera delivered with precision, charm, and heart. Buroker has engineered a delightful universe of malfunctioning ships, dangerous cyborgs, and desperate underdogs that will leave genre fans grinning from ear to ear. If you are looking for a fast, fun adventure with high emotional stakes and lovable scoundrels who refuse to stay down, pack your bags and book a trip to the Fallen Empire. You won't regret it.
