# Hidden Prey (Lawmen, #1)

**Author:** Cheyenne McCray  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

**Hidden Prey: A Thrilling Blend of Survival, Danger, and Forbidden Desire**

Cheyenne McCray’s *Hidden Prey*—the first installment in the *Lawmen* series—is a gripping, atmospheric thriller that weaves together the chilling terror of a resurgent cult, the relentless pursuit of a hardened ex-lawman, and a slow-burning tension between two people who can’t seem to escape each other, no matter how hard they try. With sharp prose, morally complex characters, and a relentless sense of dread, McCray delivers a story that lingers long after the final page.

At its core, *Hidden Prey* is a survival narrative, but it’s far more than just a chase or a battle against the supernatural. Lyra Collins, the protagonist, is a woman who has spent years rebuilding her life after escaping the Temple of Light—a cult that once held her captive and twisted her into something she never wanted to be. Now, living quietly in a remote Arizona artists’ colony, she believes she’s finally free. But freedom, as the story ruthlessly reminds us, is an illusion when the past refuses to stay buried. When a mysterious stranger—Dare Lancaster, a former lawman turned private investigator—stumbles into her life, he doesn’t just bring danger; he brings the very forces that once hunted her. And worse, he brings something else: a magnetic pull that defies logic, a connection that feels both inevitable and dangerous.

McCray excels at creating a protagonist who is deeply flawed yet deeply human. Lyra is not a passive victim; she’s a survivor with scars—both physical and psychological—that shape her every decision. Her fear isn’t just of the cult but of the chaos within herself, the remnants of the indoctrination that still whisper in her mind. Dare, on the other hand, is a man haunted by his own failures, his past mistakes, and the weight of the law he once swore to uphold. Their dynamic is electric, charged with an undercurrent of tension that isn’t just about physical danger but about the fragility of trust. Dare is drawn to Lyra not just because she’s in peril, but because she represents everything he’s lost—the ability to believe in redemption, in second chances. And Lyra, despite her initial resistance, can’t deny the way he makes her feel alive in a way no one else ever has.

The horror element in *Hidden Prey* is masterfully subtle. Neal Barker, the grandson of the cult’s original Prophet Jericho, isn’t some mindless monster—he’s a man convinced of his own righteousness, a believer in a twisted destiny that demands Lyra’s compliance. His fanaticism is terrifying precisely because it’s so *human*. McCray doesn’t rely on jump scares or gore; instead, she crafts a slow-burning dread, where the real horror lies in the psychological manipulation, the gaslighting, and the inescapable sense that Lyra is always one step behind. The cult’s presence looms like a storm cloud, and the reader, like Lyra, is left wondering when—and if—they’ll strike.

What makes *Hidden Prey* truly compelling is McCray’s ability to balance the thriller elements with raw emotional depth. The romance between Lyra and Dare isn’t just a love story; it’s a battle of wills, a test of whether two broken people can find solace in each other’s arms without becoming each other’s undoing. The chemistry between them crackles with tension, but it’s never saccharine. There’s a gritty realism to their relationship, a recognition that trust is fragile, that love in the face of such danger is both a luxury and a liability.

The setting plays a crucial role in the story’s atmosphere. The sleepy artists’ colony is a facade, a thin veneer of normalcy that hides the creeping menace of the cult. McCray’s descriptions are vivid, immersive, making the reader feel the heat of the desert, the weight of the silence, and the suffocating dread of knowing that help—or salvation—might never come. The pacing is relentless, with moments of quiet tension punctuated by explosive confrontations that leave the reader breathless.

One of the standout strengths of *Hidden Prey* is its exploration of themes like survival, faith, and the cost of redemption. Lyra’s journey isn’t just about escaping her past; it’s about reclaiming her agency, her identity, and her sense of self. Dare’s arc is equally compelling as he grapples with the consequences of his choices and the possibility that some wounds never fully heal. Together, they represent the push and pull between freedom and fate, between love and survival.

McCray’s writing style is sharp, evocative, and unflinching. She doesn’t shy away from the darker aspects of human nature, and her characters are complex, flawed, and deeply relatable. The dialogue is natural, the action is visceral, and the emotional beats land with precision. *Hidden Prey* is a story that will keep readers up at night, turning pages long after they should have fallen asleep, because they can’t look away.

In the end, *Hidden Prey* is more than just a thriller—it’s a story about the fight for survival, the cost of love, and the question of whether some battles are worth fighting. Cheyenne McCray has crafted a gripping, atmospheric tale that blends horror, romance, and suspense into a narrative that is as unforgettable as it is terrifying. If you love stories where the real enemy isn’t just outside but within, where trust is fragile and danger is always lurking, then this book is for you.
