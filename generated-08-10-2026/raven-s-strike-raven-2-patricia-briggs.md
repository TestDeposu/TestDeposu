# Raven's Strike (Raven, #2)

**Author:** Patricia Briggs  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2005

**Raven’s Strike: A Shadowed Duel of Fate and Fire**

Patricia Briggs’ *Raven’s Strike*—the second installment in the *Raven* series—is a masterclass in blending dark fantasy with the gritty, morally complex storytelling that defines her work. Here, Seraph, the reluctant Traveler, is thrust back into the brutal struggle against the remnants of her people’s cursed legacy, this time facing an enemy as insidious as it is personal: the Shadowed. This isn’t just another battle against supernatural forces; it’s a desperate race to prevent the awakening of the Stalker, a dark god whose rise would drown the world in eternal night. Briggs weaves a tale where every choice is a gamble, where survival hinges on the weight of the past and the cost of redemption.

At its core, *Raven’s Strike* is a story about the burden of legacy. Seraph, having fled her Traveler kin in *Raven’s Summer*, believed she could outrun her destiny—but the Shadowed, a new and terrifying faction of necromantic zealots, has no patience for escape. Their leader, the Stalker’s herald, doesn’t just want to unleash the ancient horror; he wants to *use* Seraph’s bloodline to do it, forcing her to confront the very magic she sought to abandon. Briggs excels at making her characters feel like living, breathing contradictions—Seraph is neither hero nor villain, but a woman torn between self-preservation and the duty she swore to reject. Her internal conflict is raw, her fear palpable, and her defiance, though often flawed, is deeply human. This isn’t a story about a chosen one; it’s about a woman who *has* to choose, again and again, even when the stakes are cosmic.

The Shadowed themselves are a chilling innovation in Briggs’ world. Unlike the more traditional vampires or witches of urban fantasy, they are a cult of death-worshippers who thrive on the energy of the dying, their power drawn from the very decay they inflict. Their leader, the Stalker’s herald, is a master manipulator, using psychological torment and twisted logic to break Seraph’s resolve. Briggs’ prose is sharp, her descriptions visceral—whether it’s the creeping dread of a necromantic ritual or the brutal efficiency of a fight scene, she immerses the reader in a world where magic is as dangerous as it is necessary. The action isn’t just spectacle; it’s a dance of survival, where every spell cast risks unleashing something worse.

What makes *Raven’s Strike* truly compelling is its exploration of power and sacrifice. Seraph’s Traveler magic is a double-edged sword—it’s the key to stopping the Stalker, but it’s also the reason her family was hunted in the first place. Briggs forces her characters to grapple with the ethics of their abilities, asking whether the ends ever justify the means when the alternative is annihilation. The book doesn’t shy away from moral ambiguity; there are no easy victories, no clear-cut heroes. Even the allies Seraph gathers—her brother, her lover, her reluctant mentor—are flawed, their loyalties tested by the same forces that threaten to consume them.

The pacing is relentless, with Briggs balancing political intrigue, personal drama, and high-stakes combat seamlessly. The Shadowed’s plans unfold like a slow-motion disaster, each revelation more chilling than the last, while Seraph’s personal journey feels urgent and inevitable. By the time the climax arrives, the tension is palpable, the consequences devastating, and the reader is left breathless—not just from the action, but from the weight of what Seraph has had to endure.

*Raven’s Strike* is more than a sequel; it’s a deepening of the themes introduced in *Raven’s Summer*, a story about the cost of defiance and the price of power. Briggs’ writing is as evocative as it is dark, her characters as complex as they are compelling. If you loved the first book for its blend of romance, horror, and fantasy, you’ll find even more to admire here—a tale where the shadows stretch long, the choices are brutal, and the only way forward is through the fire.
