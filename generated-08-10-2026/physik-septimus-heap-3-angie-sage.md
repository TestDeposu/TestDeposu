# Physik (Septimus Heap, #3)

**Author:** Angie Sage  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Time, magic, and mortality collide in *Physik*, the third and arguably most exhilarating installment in Angie Sage’s internationally bestselling *Septimus Heap* series. Picking up where *Magyk* and *Flyte* left off, Sage deepens her richly textured, idiosyncratic fantasy world by plunging her beloved characters into a high-stakes, time-bending escapade. At its core, *Physik* is a brilliant exploration of the corrupting nature of power, the fragility of life, and the enduring bonds of family and friendship, all wrapped inside a delightfully eccentric middle-grade adventure.

The narrative engine of *Physik* is kicked into motion by a classic gothic blunder: Silas Heap unseals a long-forgotten room in the Palace, inadvertently unleashing the ghost of Queen Etheldredda. Five hundred years dead, Etheldredda is just as tyrannical, vain, and relentlessly wicked as she was in life. Sage crafts a delightfully despicable villain in the late Queen, whose master plan for everlasting life requires the cooperation of Princess Jenna, the disappearance of our titular hero Septimus, and the alchemical genius of her equally unsavory son, Marcellus Pye. 

What elevates *Physik* above a standard villain-of-the-week plot is Sage’s ingenious use of time travel and historical consequence. When Septimus is hurled five hundred years into the past, the narrative splits into a dual-timeline puzzle. In the past, Septimus must survive a dangerous era while studying the lost arts of *Physik*—the ancient science of healing and alchemy—under the tutelage of Marcellus Pye. Meanwhile, back in the present, a virulent plague known as the Sickenesse ravages the Castle, threatening everyone Septimus holds dear. The cure exists, but it is locked half a millennium away, turning the book into a breathless, imaginative race against time.

Sage’s authorial style is one of the true joys of the *Septimus Heap* series. She writes with an infectious sense of whimsy paired with genuine narrative peril. Where some fantasy authors rely on grimdark bleakness to create tension, Sage relies on boundless imagination, absurd humor, and a deep affection for her characters. The world of the Castle is alive with moving portraits, snarky ghosts, flying dragons like Spit Fyre, and a sprawling, wonderfully wacky cast of supporting figures—from the formidable ExtraOrdinary Wizard Marcia Overstrand to the ever-loyal Beetle and Aunt Zelda. Sage effortlessly balances these eccentricities with moments of genuine heart-stopping action and emotional resonance. 

The central theme of the novel pivots on the title itself: *Physik*. While previous books explored the raw, mystical power of *Magyk* and the soaring freedom of *Flyte*, this volume examines the boundaries between life and death. Queen Etheldredda represents the grotesque obsession with cheating mortality at any cost, a Faustian bargain that strips away humanity. In contrast, Septimus’s journey into the past to learn healing highlights true strength—not the power to destroy, but the knowledge and empathy required to preserve life. Through Septimus, Sage shows that true growth comes from understanding the past to save the future.

Fans of intricate world-building, clever plotting, and endearing characters will find *Physik* to be an absolute page-turner. Sage proves that middle-grade fantasy can be sharply intelligent, visually vivid, and wildly entertaining for readers of all ages. Whether you are returning to the Castle or stepping onto its magical cobblestones for the first time, this time-traveling, plague-fighting adventure is an unmissable triumph.
