# Alliance (Linesman, #2)

**Author:** S.K. Dunstall  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

S.K. Dunstall’s Alliance, the compelling second installment in the Linesman series, deepens the cosmic tapestry first woven in 2015, plunging readers back into a universe where the fate of interstellar civilization hinges not on heavy artillery or political maneuvering, but on music, intuition, and the invisible architecture of space. While the official publisher synopsis occasionally gets tangled in its own continuity—mistakenly applying the introduction of the mysterious alien ship to this volume rather than the series opener—Dunstall’s narrative focus in Alliance remains razor-sharp, expertly pivoting from initial discovery to the high-stakes geopolitical fallout and evolutionary leaps that follow.

At the beating heart of Alliance is the profound theme of connection—both literal and metaphorical. In Dunstall’s universe, humanity’s expansion across the void is entirely dependent on the "lines," conduits of pure energy that make faster-than-light travel possible. Yet, these pathways are not merely mechanical infrastructure; they are sentient, living entities that sing to those who know how to listen. Enter Ean Lambert, a protagonist whose journey remains one of the most refreshing in modern science fiction. Raised in the slums and possessing an unorthodox, self-taught methodology that scandalizes the rigid, elitist linesman cartels, Ean is an outsider given the ultimate responsibility. Unlike his peers who treat the lines as tools to be manipulated, Ean treats them with empathy. He hears their song. 

In Alliance, this central theme expands from a personal calling into a universal dilemma. The alien vessel discovered at the edge of human space is not just a technological marvel waiting to be reverse-engineered; it represents an entirely different paradigm of existence. As major galactic powers scramble to weaponize or claim ownership of the ship—ignoring the catastrophic 200-kilometer defense perimeter that has already proven lethal—Ean finds himself at the convergence of an impending interstellar war. Dunstall uses this tension to dissect human nature. When faced with the unknown, humanity’s default setting is territorial dominance, fear, and exploitation. Ean’s unique ability to communicate and harmonize with the alien technology acts as a direct challenge to this cynical worldview, suggesting that survival relies on alliance and understanding rather than subjugation.

Dunstall’s writing style is a masterclass in blending hard-science wonder with deeply intimate character-driven drama. The author possesses a rare knack for making abstract, invisible concepts—like energy lines and acoustic spatial resonance—visceral and thrilling on the page. You do not just read about Ean working with the lines; you feel the vibration, the dissonance, and the soaring harmony of the void. Furthermore, the pacing in Alliance is relentless. Dunstall avoids the traditional sophomore slump by accelerating the plot, ramping up the external pressures from rival factions and cartels while simultaneously granting Ean quiet, contemplative moments of discovery that anchor the narrative's emotional weight. 

The prose is clean, brisk, and unpretentious, allowing the complex world-building to breathe without suffocating the reader in exposition. The dialogue feels authentic, highlighting the stark cultural divide between the privileged, institutionalized spacefarers and a street-smart linesman who refuses to play by their corrupt rules. Ean is not a traditional, muscle-bound space marine; his strength lies in his vulnerability, his curiosity, and his relentless empathy. This makes the triumphs and the terrifying stakes of Alliance hit all the harder.

Ultimately, Alliance succeeds because it refuses to reduce its cosmic conflict to a simple good-versus-evil binary. It asks hard questions about ownership, progress, and what it truly means to listen to the universe around us. S.K. Dunstall has crafted a stellar science fiction adventure that expands the horizons of the genre, proving that the quietest voice in the room—or the one listening to the music of the stars—just might be the one that saves us all. Essential reading for fans of visionary space opera.
