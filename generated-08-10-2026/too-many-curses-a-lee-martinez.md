# Too Many Curses

**Author:** A. Lee Martinez  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2008

Step into the delightfully chaotic world of A. Lee Martinez’s 2008 whimsical fantasy, Too Many Curses, a novel that takes well-worn genre tropes and gleefully turns them upside down. Known for his talent in mining the strange and humorous depths of speculative fiction, Martinez crafts a narrative that is as endearing as it is wildly inventive. At its core, the novel subverts the traditional epic fantasy by shifting the spotlight away from the Chosen One with a glowing sword and placing it firmly on someone far more relatable: a harried, sensible kobold named Nessy.

The premise itself is brilliantly absurd. The powerful wizard Margle the Horrendous has a unique policy regarding his enemies—he never kills them. Instead, he subjects them to various transformative curses and tucks them away into the labyrinthine halls of his castle. Enter Nessy, whose primary responsibility is not vanquishing evil, but rather housekeeping. She manages a crumbling fortress overflowing with disgruntled heroes, vanquished villains, and collateral damage. However, when Margle dies of entirely natural causes, the castle’s delicate ecosystem of containment collapses. Nessy is suddenly thrust into a high-stakes, low-magic survival scenario involving dark wizardesses, forbidden doors, and an avalanche of supernatural bureaucracy.

What makes Martinez’s style so striking is his ability to blend workplace comedy with high-fantasy peril. The central theme of the novel examines the dignity of labor and the quiet heroism of ordinary maintenance in a world obsessed with grand destinies. Nessy possesses neither legendary might nor arcane magic. Her greatest weapons are common sense, a robust work ethic, and an unwavering commitment to her duties. While self-important heroes and villains posture and plot, Nessy is the literal glue holding the castle together. Martinez champions the underdog—or in this case, the under-kobold—showing that true resilience often looks less like slaying a dragon and more like keeping the dungeons dusted while the forces of darkness bang on the front gate.

The supporting cast is a masterclass in eccentric world-building. Nessy’s ragtag group of allies includes a disembodied voice, a foul-tempered fruit bat, a creature lurking under the bed, a fragmented wizard trapped in a jar, and even a playful nod to a classic novelty song monster. Rather than falling flat as mere comedic props, these characters develop a charming camaraderie that grounds the chaotic narrative in genuine heart. Martinez’s prose is snappy, fast-paced, and effortlessly funny, relying on dry wit and situational irony to keep readers turning the pages. 

Too Many Curses is a refreshing antidote to grimdark fantasy. It invites us to laugh at the ridiculousness of dark lord politics while celebrating the unsung heroes who have to clean up the mess afterward. If you are looking for a clever, fast-moving adventure that values practicality over prophecy, Martinez’s castle of curses is open for business. Just mind the dust, and whatever you do, don't open that door.
