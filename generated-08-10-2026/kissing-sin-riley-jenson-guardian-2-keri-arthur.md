# Kissing Sin (Riley Jenson Guardian, #2)

**Author:** Keri Arthur  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2007

Keri Arthur returns to the dark, pulsing underbelly of Melbourne with Kissing Sin, the second installment in the thrilling Riley Jenson Guardian series. Building upon the urban fantasy foundations laid in her debut, Arthur delivers a breathless, high-octane narrative that leans heavily into the signature themes that define her work: the duality of identity, the struggle for bodily autonomy, and the intoxicating, often perilous intersection of danger and desire. 

At the heart of Kissing Sin is the compellingly complex protagonist, Riley Jenson. As a half-werewolf, half-vampire—a rare and controversial hybrid known as a dhampir—Riley exists between worlds, fully accepted by neither. Arthur uses this supernatural biology as a brilliant metaphor for the central theme of the novel: the battle to claim one’s own identity in a world that seeks to categorize and control. When Riley wakes up naked, battered, and suffering from missing time in a desolate alley, the horror is not just physical; it is an assault on her autonomy. This terrifying vulnerability sets the tone for a novel that examines what it means to be hunted not just for what you do, but for what you are. Her blood holds a genetic secret capable of manufacturing the ultimate warrior, turning her very body into a commodity and a battleground. 

Arthur’s stylistic prowess lies in her ability to seamlessly blend the conventions of hard-boiled detective fiction with paranormal romance and visceral urban fantasy. Her prose is punchy, cinematic, and unapologetically sensory. Melbourne is rendered not merely as a backdrop, but as a living, breathing entity of gleaming skyscrapers and throbbing, neon-lit nightclubs where predators hunt in the shadows. The pacing is relentless. Arthur masterfully balances the external plot—a complex mystery involving a twisted new breed of criminal and a labyrinth of old enemies and former lovers—with the internal turmoil of her heroine. 

Furthermore, Arthur does not shy away from exploring the darker, carnal aspects of her characters. The introduction of Kade, a steely, seductive operative battling his own life-or-death struggle, elevates the romantic tension to a fever pitch. Yet, unlike lesser paranormal romances where the protagonist is subsumed by a love interest, Arthur ensures that Riley remains fiercely independent. Her desires are powerful and often dangerous, acting as both a source of pleasure and a potential weakness, but they never override her drive for survival and justice. Riley’s sexuality is portrayed as an inherent part of her strength, refusing to conform to prudish archetypes while still acknowledging the emotional stakes involved.

Kissing Sin succeeds because it refuses to let its characters—or its readers—find comfort. Arthur keeps the pressure cooker sealed, forcing Riley to outwit tormentors who know her vulnerabilities intimately. The result is a gritty, pulse-pounding read that rewards fans of the genre with complex world-building, high-stakes action, and a heroine who is as flawed as she is fierce. For those willing to dive into the shadows of Melbourne's supernatural elite, Keri Arthur proves that the bite of the forbidden is the hardest thing to resist.
