# Ja, Inkwizytor. Miecz Aniołów (Mordimer Madderdin, #8)

**Author:** Jacek Piekara  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

Step into a world where history took a violently different turn, where the agonizing sacrifice on Golgotha was averted, and Christ descended from the cross to rule mankind not with boundless mercy, but with an iron fist of divine wrath. In Miecz Aniołów (The Sword of Angels), the eighth installment of Jacek Piekara’s immensely popular dark fantasy saga, we are once again plunged into the suffocating, fanatical universe of the Inquisitors. At the center of this grim tapestry stands Mordimer Madderdin—a man of profound, unyielding faith, and simultaneously, your worst nightmare. 

Piekara has crafted a universe where piety is measured in the smoke of burning pyres and the agonizing squeak of the rack. In this alternate reality, sin is tangible, corruption festers in every shadow, and the holy office of the Inquisition acts as the ruthless surgeon cutting away the rotting flesh of human depravity. Mordimer is neither a shining knight nor a simple villain; he is a complex instrument of a vengeful God. He possesses a sharp mind, an unflinching moral compass dictated by dogma, and a terrifying willingness to use every tool at his disposal—from subtle interrogation to the most brutal torture—to extract the truth and save souls, whether their owners wish it or not.

What makes Miecz Aniołów and the broader Mordimer Madderdin series so compelling is Piekara’s distinct, uncompromising literary style. The author writes with a punchy, atmospheric, and cinematic flair. Reading Piekara feels akin to watching a gritty, high-stakes dark drama unfold; the pacing is relentless, the tension is palpable, and the moral ambiguity leaves a lingering, bitter taste long after the final page. Piekara does not coddle his readers with comforting platitudes. Instead, he drags them through the mud and blood of a medieval-inspired nightmare, forcing them to confront the darker impulses of humanity dressed up in holy vestments. 

Even as the eighth book in the series, Miecz Aniołów retains the episodic, gripping energy that defines the saga. Piekara masterfully constructs self-contained mysteries that weave into the broader tapestry of Mordimer’s internal struggles and the heavy burden of his calling. The prose is sharp, economical, and dripping with dark irony. The author effortlessly evokes the sensory details of his world—the stench of damp dungeons, the flickering orange glow of interrogation chamber torches, and the heavy silence of absolute power. 

For fans of dark fantasy, alternate history, and morally gray antiheroes, Jacek Piekara offers an intoxicatingly grim escape. Miecz Aniołów is a testament to the author’s ability to keep a long-running series fresh, provocative, and razor-sharp. Prepare yourself for a journey into the abyss, where justice wears a hood and salvation comes at the point of a blade.
