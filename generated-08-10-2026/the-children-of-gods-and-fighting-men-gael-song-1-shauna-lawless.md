# The Children of Gods and Fighting Men (Gael Song, #1)

**Author:** Shauna Lawless  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

Steeped in the rain-slicked soil and turbulent politics of tenth-century Ireland, Shauna Lawless’s debut novel, The Children of Gods and Fighting Men, is a masterclass in historical fantasy. Set against the volatile backdrop of 981 AD—a fractured land where mortal kings clash for ultimate supremacy and the shadow of the Viking threat looms large—Lawless achieves something remarkably difficult: she weaves ancient Irish mythology so seamlessly into the historical record that the supernatural feels as gritty and inevitable as the mud beneath a warrior’s boots. 

At its core, the main theme of the novel explores the friction between power, survival, and identity, refracted through the prism of a deeply divided island. Lawless introduces readers to a hidden war fought in the margins of history between two immortal races: the fire-wielding Fomorians and the healing-gifted Descendants of the Tuatha Dé Danann. These immortal factions do not merely exist in a vacuum; they pull the strings of mortal ambition, manipulating kings, queens, and warlords like Brian Boru to secure their own survival. Yet, the true heartbeat of the novel lies in its exploration of womanhood amid violence. The narrative is anchored by compelling female figures like Gormflaith, a cunning mortal queen harboring dangerous Fomorian magic, and Fódla, a Descendant healer turned spy trying desperately to stem the tide of bloodshed. Lawless uses these characters to dissect the heavy price of ambition and the agonizing choices forced upon those who wish to protect their people in a world determined to consume them.

Lawless’s authorial style is striking for its rigorous historical grounding and atmospheric precision. Often aptly compared to a Celtic The Last Kingdom, the book avoids the trap of excessive, overly complex world-building. Instead, Lawless trusts her readers, dropping them directly into the geopolitical and cultural quagmire of medieval Ireland with prose that is visceral, cinematic, and unpretentious. Her action sequences are sharp and breathless, but she is equally adept at slowing the pace to capture the quiet, tense claustrophobia of court intrigue, whispered secrets, and ideological divides. The magic system is organic and understated, serving the narrative rather than overwhelming it, which only heightens the realism of the historical setting. 

Ultimately, The Children of Gods and Fighting Men is a triumph of crossover fiction. It honors the rich, mythic traditions of Ireland while breathing vibrant, contemporary life into its ancient past. Lawless has crafted a gripping, richly textured epic that refuses to let go, offering an intoxicating blend of history and magic that will leave readers desperate for the next installment.
