# Anatomy: A Love Story (The Anatomy Duology, #1)

**Author:** Dana Schwartz  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

Dana Schwartz’s #1 New York Times bestselling novel, Anatomy: A Love Story, is a deliciously dark, atmospheric descent into the cobblestone-paved, corpse-strewn streets of 1817 Edinburgh. Part historical fiction, part gothic romance, and entirely gripping, Schwartz crafts a tale that uses the chilling realities of early nineteenth-century medicine as a scalpel to dissect the human condition, societal expectation, and the unyielding nature of ambition. 

At its thematic core, the novel is a brilliant exploration of boundaries—those drawn by class, gender, and the ultimate line between life and death. In Regency-era Edinburgh, Hazel Sinnett is a young woman trapped by the rigid cage of her social standing. While her peers are bred for the drawing-room and the marriage market, Hazel’s mind yearns for the operating theater. She wants to be a surgeon, a profession barred to her entirely because of her gender. Parallel to Hazel’s struggle is Jack Currer, a resurrection man whose existence is relegated to the literal shadows, digging up the recently deceased to supply medical schools. 

Schwartz brilliantly juxtaposes their worlds to highlight the central theme: bodily autonomy and who gets to claim it. Hazel fights for the right to study the human body to heal, while Jack navigates a city where the bodies of the poor are commodified and discarded. When their paths cross, the narrative transforms into a commentary on how society values human life—and human death. The anatomy table becomes a metaphor for the societal dissection of both characters, laying bare their vulnerabilities, resilience, and quiet defiance.

Schwartz’s writing style is effortlessly magnetic. She balances the macabre with the tender, treating the grim realities of grave-robbing and surgical theaters not just for shock value, but with a reverent curiosity that mirrors Hazel’s own fascination with anatomy. The prose is brisk, cinematic, and sharply paced, perfectly capturing the oppressive fog of Edinburgh and the pulsing urgency of the plot. She weaves historical elements—such as the looming threat of the Roman Fever and the shady underworld of body-snatching—into a fictional narrative that feels both authentic and magically eerie.

What elevates the novel beyond a standard historical thriller is the chemistry between Hazel and Jack. Their dynamic bridges the chasm between high society and the destitute underworld. They are two outsiders finding sanctuary in one another, and their burgeoning romance never overshadows the main plot, but rather grounds the high-stakes mystery in genuine emotional stakes. 

Ultimately, Anatomy: A Love Story is a triumph of YA gothic fiction. Schwartz has constructed an intricate puzzle box of medical mystery, social critique, and heart-pounding romance. Like a master surgeon wielding a blade with precision, the author cuts deep, leaving readers with a story that lingers long after the final page is turned. It is a stunning reminder that the most complex anatomy to map is not that of the physical body, but of the human heart.
