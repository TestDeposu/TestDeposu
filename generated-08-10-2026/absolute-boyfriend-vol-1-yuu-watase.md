# Absolute Boyfriend, Vol. 1

**Author:** Yuu Watase  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

When romantic desperation meets high-tech consumerism, the result is the sparkling, bittersweet, and sharply observant world of Yuu Watase’s Absolute Boyfriend, Vol. 1. Known globally for her mastery of emotional resonance and supernatural or fantastical shojo manga—most notably demonstrated in her epic hit *Fushigi Yugi*—Watase brings her signature flair for dramatic pacing and expressive character work into the realm of modern romantic comedy. In this opening volume, she takes a seemingly absurd, high-concept premise and transforms it into a surprisingly poignant exploration of affection, self-worth, and the commodification of love.

The narrative centers on Riiko Izawa, a chronically unlucky high schooler whose perpetual rejections by unattainable bachelors have left her nursing a bruised ego and a desperate longing for affection. Enter the digital underworld of Chronos Heaven, a mysterious corporation offering a free trial of a Nightly Lover figure. What arrives at her doorstep is not just a high-end android, but Night Ten—a meticulously crafted, aesthetically flawless, and relentlessly devoted robotic boyfriend programmed to cater to every whim of his owner. 

At its core, the main theme of Absolute Boyfriend is the tension between manufactured perfection and authentic human connection. Watase uses Ten’s unyielding programming to interrogate what we actually look for in a partner. Ten is sweet, intelligent, an exceptional cook, and programmed to love Riiko unconditionally. Yet, the underlying dramatic irony—and the emotional hook for the reader—is that his affection is a product of algorithms and hardware, not free will. Through Riiko’s internal conflict, Watase asks a probing question: Can a heart find genuine fulfillment in a relationship where devotion is guaranteed by a price tag? 

Watase’s artistic and storytelling style shines brilliantly throughout this volume. Visually, her work is characterized by dynamic paneling, expressive character emoting, and a meticulous attention to detail in character designs. Ten transitions seamlessly from the stoic, uncanny perfection of an unactivated product to the warm, glowing eyes of a devoted companion, while Riiko’s relatable clumsiness and expressive facial reactions anchor the story in genuine adolescent comedy. 

What makes Watase’s style particularly effective here is her tonal balancing act. Absolute Boyfriend easily pivots from laugh-out-loud slapstick—triggered by Ten’s literal interpretations of romance and his startling arrivals—to moments of genuine vulnerability. Watase understands how to weaponize cuteness, only to pull the rug out from under the reader with sobering financial and emotional realities. After all, Ten comes with a staggering million-dollar price tag, turning a fantastical wish-fulfillment scenario into a ticking-clock dilemma of debt and desire.

Ultimately, the first volume of Absolute Boyfriend succeeds because it refuses to treat its premise as a mere gimmick. Beneath the glossy exterior of early-2000s shojo aesthetics lies a clever, heartwarming, and occasionally melancholy satire on modern romance. Yuu Watase proves once again that she can take the extraordinary and make it entirely human, leaving readers eager to see whether love can be truly programmed, or if the real heart belongs to the girl learning to love herself first.
