# Hammered (The Iron Druid Chronicles, #3)

**Author:** Kevin Hearne  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2011

Kevin Hearne’s third installment in the Iron Druid Chronicles, Hammered, demonstrates a confident expansion of a rapidly growing urban fantasy universe. Following the fast-paced events of Hounded and Hexed, Hearne delivers a narrative that elevates the stakes from local supernatural skirmishes to cosmic proportions. At its core, the main theme of Hammered is the inescapable weight of cosmic accountability and the dangerous necessity of keeping promises, even when those promises demand the assassination of a Norse god.

Hearne’s authorial style remains one of the defining charms of the series. Writing in the first-person perspective of Atticus O’Sullivan—a two-thousand-year-old Druid hiding in plain sight as an occult bookstore owner in Arizona—Hearne crafts a voice that is effortlessly sarcastic, deeply knowledgeable about ancient mythologies, and profoundly loyal to his friends. The prose moves at a relentless, page-turning velocity, blending gritty modern-day action with the rich, sweeping tapestries of world folklore. Hearne has an exceptional talent for making ancient pantheons feel contemporary, breathing messy, flawed, and terrifying life into mythological figures. 

In Hammered, Atticus finds himself pressured by wiser entities to take down Thor, the Norse god of thunder. What makes this central conflict compelling is that Atticus is acutely aware of how vastly outmatched he is. Thor is not merely a bully with a magical hammer; he is a force of nature driven by cruelty and unchecked power. Hearne expertly paces the buildup, forcing Atticus to navigate a labyrinth of political tensions among rival vampire factions, zealous mortal mystic hunters known as the Hammers of God, and a loyal pack of werewolves determined to protect their own. The author weaves these disparate subplots together with admirable narrative control, ensuring that the local threats in Tempe, Arizona, continually intersect with the looming, thunderous doom waiting across the nine realms.

The action sequences are visceral and cleverly choreographed, relying as much on Atticus's tactical wit, herbal magic, and iron-clad sword as they do on divine intervention. Yet, what truly anchors the book's style is its distinct brand of humor. Hearne juxtaposes ultra-violence and ancient divine wrath with modern pop culture references and the dry, witty banter of Atticus’s Irish wolfhound, Oberon. Oberon’s telepathic commentary serves as a brilliant thematic and comedic counterweight to the bleakness of the divine hit list, offering readers moments of pure, laugh-out-loud levity amid the high-stakes tension.

Hammered succeeds because it refuses to let its protagonist take the easy way out. Atticus O’Sullivan is an endearing hero precisely because he complains bitterly about the impossible burdens placed upon his shoulders, yet ultimately does the right thing anyway. For fans of fast-witted urban fantasy who revel in sprawling mythologies, clever magic systems, and characters they would gladly share a pint with, Hearne’s third outing is a thoroughly entertaining and striking triumph.
