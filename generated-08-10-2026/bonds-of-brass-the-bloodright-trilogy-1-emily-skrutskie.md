# Bonds of Brass (The Bloodright Trilogy, #1)

**Author:** Emily Skrutskie  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

Title: Empire, Obligation, and the Weight of the Heart: A Review of Bonds of Brass

In the sprawling landscape of modern space opera, it is rare to find a debut that balances high-octane galactic stakes with an intensely intimate emotional core. Emily Skrutskie achieves precisely this in Bonds of Brass, the explosive first installment of The Bloodright Trilogy. Blending the kinetic energy of classic military science fiction with a deeply personal narrative of loyalty and identity, Skrutskie has crafted a novel that asks a devastating question: What do you do when the person you love most is the symbol of everything you have ever hated?

The premise immediately hooks the reader. Ettian is a survivor of the merciless Umber Empire, a regime that shattered his world and forced him to rebuild his life from the ashes. Yet, after years of swallowing his trauma and excelling at a military academy, his carefully constructed reality fractures. His infuriatingly charming roommate, Gal, is revealed to be the crown prince and heir to the very empire that ruined Ettian's life. When an assassination plot forces them on the run, Ettian is thrust into an impossible dilemma that serves as the narrative engine of the book: Does he help his best friend—and the man who has quietly won his heart—claim a throne built on suffering, or does he align with a brewing rebellion determined to tear the empire down?

Skrutskie’s greatest triumph in Bonds of Brass lies in her exploration of loyalty as a multifaceted, often painful obligation. The central theme of the novel is not merely about choosing sides in a galactic war, but about the agonizing friction between personal devotion and systemic justice. Ettian’s internal conflict is handled with a keen edge of psychological pain. He is a protagonist defined not just by his piloting skills, but by his profound capacity for care. Watching him grapple with the realization that his closest confidant is inextricably linked to his greatest trauma creates a magnetic tension that never lets up. The romance between Ettian and Gal feels earned, simmering with a genuine warmth that makes the looming betrayal all the more agonizing. 

From a stylistic standpoint, Skrutskie writes with a relentless, breathless momentum. The action sequences are choreographed with cinematic precision, hurtling the reader through dogfights, narrow escapes, and political subterfuge without sacrificing character development. Yet, what truly elevates the prose is its emotional resonance. Skrutskie understands that the best science fiction uses the vastness of space to magnify human intimacy. The dialogue is snappy and sharp, contrasting nicely with the heavier themes of imperialism, stolen futures, and revolution. 

While the novel carries the expected tropes of the YA and crossover sci-fi genres—such as the academy setting and the hidden royalty trope—Skrutskie subverts them by grounding the narrative in the gritty, unglamorous realities of war and occupation. Gal is not just a bland archetype of a prince in distress; he is complex, conflicted, and painfully naive about the true cost of his birthright. 

Bonds of Brass is a high-octane, emotionally bruising galactic adventure that satisfies on every level. It is a story about the anchors that hold us down and the chains we choose to break, anchored by two unforgettable leads caught in the crossfire of destiny. For fans of fast-paced space opera, rich character dynamics, and high-stakes romance, Skrutskie’s debut is an absolute must-read that leaves you desperate for the next chapter of the rebellion.
