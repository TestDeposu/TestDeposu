# This Is All I Ask (de Piaget, #6; de Piaget/MacLeod, #3)

**Author:** Lynn Kurland  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

Title: Chivalry, Manhattan, and the Magic of the Highlands: A Review of Lynn Kurland’s This Is All I Ask

For readers who have ever traded the endless hustle of modern city life for the comforting daydreams of a medieval romance, Lynn Kurland’s *This Is All I Ask* offers a delightful, albeit brief, escape. Originally published under the title *The Traveller* in the *A Knight’s Vow* anthology, this enchanting novella brings together two of Kurland’s signature strengths: witty contemporary heroines and stoic, honorable medieval knights. 

At its core, the novella explores the timeless theme of displacement—both physical and emotional. Julianna Nelson is the quintessential stressed-out New Yorker living in Gramercy Park, a woman whose yearning for a "knight in shining armor" is born less from naivety and more from sheer exhaustion. When a sudden and unexplained time slip drops her straight into medieval Scotland, Kurland uses this classic fish-out-of-water trope to examine the friction and eventual harmony between the modern mindset and the chivalric code. 

Enter Sir William de Piaget. As part of the beloved de Piaget literary legacy, William embodies the quintessential romance hero: fiercely duty-bound, heavily burdened, and inherently noble. Bound by his honor to protect a strangely dressed, bewildered damsel, William is forced to navigate the unpredictable chaos that Julianna brings into his already complicated quest to reclaim his castle. The central tension of the story is not merely about surviving the past, but about two people from entirely different worlds finding a shared language of the heart. 

Kurland’s narrative style remains one of her greatest assets. She writes with a gentle, humorous grace that separates her work from the grittier, darker edges of historical fiction. Her prose is cozy, conversational, and lighthearted, expertly balancing the high-stakes backdrop of medieval politics with sparkling, modern dialogue. Kurland understands that the best time-travel romances rely heavily on the humor found in cultural misunderstandings, and she milks this dynamic for all its worth without ever sacrificing the emotional authenticity of the growing bond between Julianna and William.

While the novella format inherently limits the depth of world-building and character progression compared to a full-length novel, Kurland wastes no time in delivering maximum charm. The pacing is swift, the chemistry is sweet, and the resolution satisfies the craving for a guaranteed happily-ever-after. 

*This Is All I Ask* is a charming, low-angst treat for established fans of the de Piaget universe and a wonderful, digestible introduction for newcomers to Kurland’s magical brand of historical-meets-contemporary romance. It reminds us that while chivalry might not always be convenient, true love has a funny way of transcending time itself.
