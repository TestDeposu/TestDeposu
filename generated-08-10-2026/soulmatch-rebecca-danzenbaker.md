# Soulmatch

**Author:** Rebecca Danzenbaker  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2025

**Soulmatch by Rebecca Danzenbaker: A Dazzling Dystopia Where Fate Is a Game—and the Stakes Are Souls**

Rebecca Danzenbaker’s *Soulmatch* is a razor-sharp, morally complex speculative romance that reimagines destiny as a high-stakes chess match—where the board is a fractured world, the pieces are stolen lives, and the queen? A girl who never asked to be the center of everything. Set two centuries after World War III, this novel weaves a dystopian tapestry where the government’s soul-identification system, *kirling*, dictates not just careers but entire futures. But when Sivon, a prodigy chess player with an unsettling knack for winning, discovers her soul isn’t what it seems, she becomes the unwitting pawn in a conspiracy that could unravel the very fabric of existence.

At its core, *Soulmatch* is a story about the illusion of control. The world Danzenbaker crafts is one where fate is a construct—one that can be manipulated, betrayed, or shattered. Sivon’s journey is less about finding a soulmate (though romance simmers beneath the surface) and more about confronting the terrifying reality that her past lives were never hers to begin with. The novel’s brilliance lies in its refusal to offer easy answers. Is Sivon a victim? A rebel? A weapon? The ambiguity forces readers to question whether destiny is a gift or a cage, and whether love—even the kind that defies logic—can ever be free.

Danzenbaker’s prose is as sharp as Sivon’s chess moves, blending wit with a creeping dread that lingers like a shadow. Her dialogue crackles with tension, whether it’s Sivon’s snappy retorts against her infuriatingly assigned bodyguard or the chilling whispers of a world that’s slowly turning against her. The political intrigue unfolds with the precision of a well-played game, where every move has consequences, and every ally could be a traitor. The romance, when it arrives, isn’t the sappy kind—it’s a fragile, dangerous thing, built on trust in a system that rewards deception.

What makes *Soulmatch* truly gripping is its exploration of identity. Sivon’s struggle isn’t just about survival; it’s about reclaiming agency in a world that has already decided her fate. The novel asks: If your soul isn’t yours, who are you? And if love is a choice, can it be real when the rules are written by someone else? Danzenbaker doesn’t shy away from the bleakness of her vision, but she balances it with moments of defiance, humor, and unexpected tenderness. The result is a story that feels both urgent and deeply human, where the line between hero and villain blurs, and the only certainty is that no one gets to play by the rules.

For fans of *Matched*’s eerie precision and *Scythe*’s philosophical depth, *Soulmatch* delivers a fresh, morally complex twist on dystopian romance. It’s a book that demands engagement—one that will keep you up at night, questioning the nature of fate, love, and the souls we’re given (or stolen). Danzenbaker’s writing is immersive, her world-building intricate, and her characters unforgettable. *Soulmatch* isn’t just a story about a girl who sees too far into the past—it’s about the cost of seeing at all.
