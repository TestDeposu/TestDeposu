# Infinite (Newsoul, #3)

**Author:** Jodi Meadows  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2014

The conclusion to any ambitious trilogy is a precarious balancing act, requiring the author to bridge the gap between high-stakes world-building and the intimate, fraying nerves of its protagonists. In Infinite, the final installment of the Newsoul trilogy, Jodi Meadows succeeds in elevating the stakes from a mere quest for identity to an existential struggle for the very mechanics of her world.

The central thesis of Meadows’ series—the ethical horror buried within a cycle of reincarnation—reaches its zenith here. In the society of Heart, death is not an ending but a repetitive glitch, a cycle that Ana, the first “newsoul,” has spent the previous two books exposing as a parasitic system. The strength of Infinite lies in how Meadows forces her characters to confront the utilitarian math of immortality: for one soul to return, another must be consumed. It is a chilling premise that transforms the typical Young Adult trope of a "chosen one" into a philosophical dilemma. Ana is no longer just fighting a villain; she is fighting a history that demands blood to perpetuate itself.

Meadows’ writing style remains as evocative as ever, characterized by a lyrical precision that makes the fantastical elements—the sylph, the encroaching dragons, and the crumbling architecture of a world that has lived too many lives—feel tactile. She avoids the pitfalls of bloated fantasy, opting instead for a brisk, urgent pace that reflects Ana’s desperation. Her prose shines brightest when focused on the tension between Ana and Sam. Their relationship serves as the narrative’s anchor, providing a necessary human contrast to the cold, clinical nature of reincarnation. Watching their bond fray under the pressure of an impending apocalypse is genuinely harrowing; Meadows manages to make the reader believe that the end of the world is secondary to the potential loss of a connection that defies the standard cycle of souls.

If there is a critique to be leveled, it is that the world-building is so vast and intricate that the resolution feels breathless, perhaps even hurried, as Meadows works to tie off the sprawling threads of Janan’s machinations. However, this sense of frantic pacing mirrors Ana’s internal state perfectly. The "cost of immortality" is not just a plot point; it is a burden that weighs on every page, creating a sense of inevitability that is both tragic and compelling.

Infinite is a triumphant finish that refuses to provide easy answers. It acknowledges that true change rarely comes without a devastating price. Jodi Meadows has crafted a finale that honors the journey of its heroine while successfully challenging the reader to consider what it truly means to live a life that is finite. For fans who have followed Ana from the beginning, this is a heart-stopping, thought-provoking conclusion that lingers long after the final page is turned. It is a testament to the idea that our worth is not measured by how many times we return, but by how we choose to spend the one life we can truly call our own.
