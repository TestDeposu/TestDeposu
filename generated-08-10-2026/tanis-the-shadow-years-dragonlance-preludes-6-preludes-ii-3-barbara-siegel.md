# Tanis, the Shadow Years (Dragonlance: Preludes #6; Preludes II, #3)

**Author:** Barbara Siegel  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2012

Unearthing the Past: A Critical Look at Tanis, the Shadow Years

Within the vast, richly mapped tapestry of the Dragonlance universe, few characters carry the existential weight of Tanis Half-Elven. Caught eternally between the immortal grace of the elves and the rugged mortality of men, Tanis has always been defined by his internal division. In Tanis, the Shadow Years, author Barbara Siegel takes this foundational conflict and distills it into an intimate, psychological crucible. Serving as the sixth and final installment in the celebrated Preludes series, this 2012 novel does not rely on sweeping armies or cataclysmic dragon-wars to drive its narrative. Instead, it turns inward, exploring the misty peaks near Solace to answer a long-standing chronological mystery: what happened during the hero's sudden, unexplained disappearance? 

The primary theme of Siegel’s narrative is the inescapable gravity of heritage and the painful necessity of confronting memory. The plot itself is deceptively straightforward. Driven by a burning need to uncover the truth about his long-dead human father, Tanis is joined by an unlikely and gritty dwarf companion named Clotnik. Their quest quickly intersects with Kishpa, a dying and lovesick mage. Yet, the physical journey is merely a vessel for the true narrative engine: time travel through memory. To secure the answers he desperately seeks, Tanis must literally step into Kishpa’s mind, becoming a traveler in a realm where past regrets and chronological physics collide in an impossible battle against time itself. 

Siegel’s authorial style shines brightest in these psychological landscapes. Transitioning a high-fantasy hero out of his tactical element and into a surreal, memory-driven dreamscape requires a delicate touch, and Siegel delivers with prose that feels both evocative and deeply introspective. She strips away the armor of the iconic Companions leader, leaving a vulnerable, questioning traveler. The dialogue between Tanis and the eccentric mage Kishpa anchors the metaphysical weirdness with genuine emotional stakes. Furthermore, the inclusion of Clotnik provides a grounded, pragmatic counterweight to the ethereal nature of the memory-delving plot, ensuring the narrative never drifts too far from the classic, earthy sensibilities of traditional fantasy.

While some readers of the broader Dragonlance saga crave the epic scale of the War of the Lance, Tanis, the Shadow Years offers a rewarding detour for those who prefer character-driven depth. Siegel successfully transforms a minor historical footnote in Solace lore into a poignant coming-of-age revelation for a character who thought he had already grown past his doubts. It is a tale about the ghosts that haunt us before we ever learn to wield a sword against them—a fitting, reflective conclusion to the Preludes series that leaves Tanis changed, ennobled, and finally at peace with the shadows of his past.
