# Silver in the Wood (The Greenhollow Duology, #1)

**Author:** Emily Tesh  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

In *Silver in the Wood*, Emily Tesh achieves the rare feat of making a familiar, moss-draped forest feel entirely brand new. Winner of the 2020 World Fantasy Award, this slim yet resonant novella is a masterclass in atmospheric storytelling, weaving together folklore, queer romance, and a profound meditation on the cost of immortality. 

At its heart, Tesh’s debut is an exploration of belonging and the heavy burden of stewardship. The narrative centers on Tobias, the Wild Man of Greenhollow, an ancient entity tethered to the very roots and soil of the deep woods. Tobias lives a carefully curated life of quiet solitude, tending to his cottage, his feline companion, and the local dryads. He is a creature of the canopy and the damp earth, content to let the centuries blur together without having to look back at the mortal life—and the mortal heart—he left behind. 

That is, until the arrival of Henry Silver. The handsome and relentlessly curious new owner of Greenhollow Hall crashes into Tobias’s sequestered world like a fresh gale through autumn branches. Henry is not merely a polite neighbor; he is a man with questions, charm, and a quiet determination to unearth the secrets that the woods have spent centuries burying. As Henry draws Tobias out of his shell, the novella blossoms into a tender, hesitant romance, but one underscored by the eerie, unpredictable nature of old magic.

Tesh’s stylistic prowess is immediately evident in her prose, which reads like an incantation. Her writing is lush, sensory, and deeply tactile—you can smell the damp pine needles, hear the rustle of dryads shifting beneath the bark, and feel the damp chill of the shadows. Rather than relying on frantic plotting, Tesh builds tension through rhythm, mood, and atmosphere. She captures the duality of the natural world: it is simultaneously breathtakingly beautiful and terrifyingly indifferent to human concerns. The forest in *Silver in the Wood* is not just a setting; it is a breathing, active character with its own desires, rules, and dark heart.

The novella’s central theme revolves around the collision between the ancient and the modern, the wild and the domestic, and the scars left by both. Tobias represents the eternal, cyclical nature of the earth, bound by ancient pacts and old grief, while Henry embodies human curiosity and the desire to connect, to know, and to be known. Their dynamic asks a compelling question: Can someone tied to the earth ever truly belong to another person?

Clocking in at novella length, *Silver in the Wood* wastes not a single word. Tesh’s economic storytelling delivers the emotional weight and world-building depth of a full-length epic fantasy within a fraction of the pages. It is a darkly enchanting fairy tale for adults—one that honors the traditions of classic British folklore while subverting them with modern emotional depth and queer romance. 

For readers seeking a story that is as poetic as it is captivating, a tale that tastes of rain, old magic, and quiet longing, Emily Tesh’s debut is an absolute triumph. Step into the green shadows of Greenhollow; you will not emerge unchanged.
