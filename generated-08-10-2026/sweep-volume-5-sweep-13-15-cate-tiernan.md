# Sweep: Volume 5 (Sweep, #13-15)

**Author:** Cate Tiernan  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2001

In the world of Young Adult supernatural fiction, few series captured the turn-of-the-millennium zeitgeist quite like Cate Tiernan’s Sweep. Collecting volumes 13 through 15, this specific installment serves as a testament to the series' enduring draw: the seductive, often perilous intersection of adolescent awakening and ancient, untamed power.

At the heart of the narrative is Morgan Rowlands, a protagonist whose journey from the mundane to the mystical serves as the perfect vessel for the classic teen struggle of identity. Tiernan excels here by grounding the esoteric elements of Wicca in the visceral, messy realities of high school life. Morgan is not merely discovering she is a witch; she is navigating the terrifying realization that her agency is shifting. When she encounters Cal, the charismatic coven leader, the reader is lured into a romance that feels both fated and fundamentally dangerous.

Tiernan’s writing style is deceptively simple, favoring a brisk, almost breathless pace that mirrors the frantic intensity of a first love. However, beneath the accessible prose lies a darker, more deliberate thematic exploration. The central tension—the revelation that Cal is harboring secrets that threaten to consume their connection—serves as a cautionary tale about the cost of power and the blindness of infatuation. Tiernan masterfully depicts how the lure of “knowing” oneself can often be clouded by the desire to belong to someone else.

What makes this collection particularly striking is the atmosphere. Tiernan constructs a world that feels cinematic; the rituals are painted with enough sensory detail to feel immersive, yet never so bogged down in jargon that the emotional stakes are lost. The darkness in these pages is not just performative or aesthetic—it is rooted in the moral ambiguity of characters who are still learning the difference between what they want and what they can control.

For those returning to the series or discovering it for the first time, this volume captures the “Sweep” experience at its most compelling. It is a story about the fragility of trust and the volatile nature of discovery. While the tropes of the star-crossed lovers are present, Tiernan elevates them through her sharp focus on the psychological toll of practicing craft. She understands that for a teenager, magic is rarely just about spells; it is about the desperate search for control in a world that feels increasingly out of one's hands.

Ultimately, these volumes remind us why the series remains a staple of the genre. It is an exploration of the heavy burden of heritage and the dangerous, intoxicating pull of someone who promises to complete you. Tiernan does not shy away from the idea that discovering your true self can, at times, mean burning your old world to the ground. It is an engaging, provocative, and thoroughly magnetic read that proves that while the times have changed, the teenage desire for autonomy and connection remains as potent as ever.
