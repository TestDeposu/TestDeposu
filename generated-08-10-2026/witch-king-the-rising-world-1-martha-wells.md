# Witch King (The Rising World, #1)

**Author:** Martha Wells  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

**Witch King: A Masterclass in Power, Betrayal, and the Weight of Legacy**

Martha Wells has done it again. With *Witch King*, the author behind the beloved *Murderbot Diaries* and the *All Systems Red* series delivers a fantasy that is as sharp as it is haunting—a story where magic isn’t just a tool, but a curse, and where the line between ally and enemy blurs with every betrayal. This isn’t just another heist or war epic; it’s a deep dive into the cost of power, the fragility of trust, and the families we forge when the world tries to tear us apart.

At its core, *Witch King* is a tale of vengeance and reckoning. Kai, the titular demon-turned-antihero, wakes from a long, unnatural slumber to find himself trapped—not just in a literal water prison, but in a world that has moved on without him. His murder was no accident; it was a calculated strike against the demonic elite, and now, the remnants of his power are being exploited by a lesser mage desperate to claim what isn’t his. But Kai isn’t just a pawn in someone else’s game. He’s the last of his kind, a being bound by ancient curses and the weight of his own cruelty. His magic, once a weapon of pain, now feels like a noose tightening around his neck. And when he finally breaks free, he’ll have to decide: does he reclaim his throne of blood and fire, or burn it all down to save what’s left?

Wells’ prose is as deft as ever, blending razor-sharp dialogue with a lyrical, almost mythic quality. The world-building is immersive without being overwhelming, grounding the supernatural in tangible stakes. The Rising World Coalition looms like a storm on the horizon, its influence creeping into every corner of politics and magic, and Kai’s allies—those who once trusted him, those who never did—are scattered like shards of glass. There’s no easy path here. Every choice Kai makes will have consequences, and Wells doesn’t shy away from the moral gray areas. Is Kai a villain? A victim? Both? The answer is as complicated as the man himself, and that’s what makes him so compelling.

What truly sets *Witch King* apart is its exploration of power and the people who wield it. Wells doesn’t romanticize dominance; she dissects it. Kai’s demonic nature isn’t just a gimmick—it’s a reflection of his isolation, his rage, and his refusal to be anything less than feared. His relationships with his allies are raw and real, built on mutual survival rather than blind loyalty. There’s no grand, sweeping romance here; instead, there’s the quiet desperation of friends who know they might not make it out alive. And when betrayal strikes, it’s not just personal—it’s existential. The world Kai knew is collapsing, and he’s the only one who can decide whether to rebuild it or let it burn.

Thematically, *Witch King* is a meditation on legacy. Kai isn’t just fighting for his own survival; he’s fighting for the legacy of his people, for the world he once ruled, and for the future he’s willing to sacrifice everything to protect. It’s a story about the families we choose—those who stand by us in the dark, those who turn their backs when the going gets tough, and those who never knew us at all. Wells weaves these threads together with a narrative that’s as gripping as it is thought-provoking, ensuring that every page turns with a sense of inevitability.

Visually, the book is a feast for the imagination. The water prison is a masterclass in atmospheric dread, a labyrinth of reflections and echoes where Kai’s consciousness is both trapped and sharpened. The magic system is intricate yet accessible, with pain as much a weapon as it is a curse—a reminder that power often comes at a price. And the world itself feels alive, with factions clashing and secrets buried beneath layers of deception.

For fans of Wells’ previous work, *Witch King* will feel like a natural evolution of her signature style: witty, introspective, and deeply human. But it’s also a standout in the broader fantasy landscape, offering something rare—a story where the stakes are as high as the emotional weight. It’s not just about saving the world; it’s about saving the parts of yourself you thought were lost forever.

If you’ve ever wanted a fantasy that’s as much about the soul as it is about the sword, *Witch King* is your book. It’s a tale of redemption and ruin, of trust and treachery, and of a man who refuses to be anyone’s hero—because the world doesn’t deserve one. And in Martha Wells’ hands, that’s the most dangerous kind of story.
