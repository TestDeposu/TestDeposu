# Heat of the Everflame (Kindred's Curse, #3)

**Author:** Penn Cole  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2026

**Heat of the Everflame: A Fire That Burns Too Bright**

Penn Cole’s *Heat of the Everflame*—the third installment in the *Kindred’s Curse* saga—is a storm of political intrigue, personal reckoning, and a love story that feels like it’s been forged in the heart of a war. This isn’t just another fantasy epic; it’s a high-stakes drama where loyalty is a blade you must wield carefully, and every choice could be the last. Cole, known for her razor-sharp dialogue and morally complex characters, delivers a narrative that pulses with tension, where the line between hero and villain blurs with every page.

At its core, *Heat of the Everflame* is a story about the cost of power—and the price of being the one who holds it. Diem, our fierce and flawed protagonist, stands at the precipice of a war that threatens to consume everything she’s fought for. The Descended and the Guardians have turned her into a pawn, a symbol, a target—each side demanding her allegiance as if her heart were a battlefield. But Diem isn’t just a figurehead; she’s a woman grappling with the truth of her own blood, the weight of her mother’s legacy, and the terrifying realization that her greatest strength might also be her undoing. Cole doesn’t shy away from the brutality of this revelation. The answers Diem seeks come with scars, both physical and emotional, and the journey to uncover them is as brutal as it is necessary.

What makes this book so gripping is how Cole weaves Diem’s internal struggle with the external chaos of war. The political maneuvering is sharp, the betrayals sting, and the alliances feel earned—not just because of who’s on which side, but because of the sacrifices each character is willing to make. Luther, the grumpy love interest with a heart as hardened as his exterior, is a study in contrast. His loyalty to Diem is tested in ways that force her to question whether she deserves it, while his own demons threaten to pull him apart. Their dynamic is electric, a slow-burn tension that simmers beneath the surface of the war, a reminder that even in the midst of apocalypse, love can be both a weapon and a vulnerability.

Cole’s prose is lean, efficient, and dripping with attitude. Her worldbuilding is immersive without being overwhelming, her dialogue crackles with wit and bite, and her characters—especially Diem—are so vividly flawed that you can’t help but root for them even when they make terrible choices. The book’s themes of identity, legacy, and the cyclical nature of violence are handled with a deft hand, never preachy but always resonant. The slow reveal of Diem’s origins isn’t just a plot device; it’s a mirror held up to her character, forcing her—and the reader—to confront the truth that some wounds run deeper than they ever knew.

But perhaps the most compelling aspect of *Heat of the Everflame* is its refusal to offer easy answers. The Crowns, those shadowy figures pulling strings from the north, add a layer of dread that lingers like smoke. Are they allies? Enemies? Something worse? Cole keeps the reader guessing, ensuring that even the most well-laid plans feel precarious. The book’s pacing is relentless, each chapter a new challenge, a new betrayal, a new step closer to the inevitable confrontation. And yet, for all its darkness, there’s a flicker of hope in the found family Diem has built—a ragtag group of misfits, rebels, and true believers who refuse to let her face the world alone.

In the end, *Heat of the Everflame* is a book about the fire that burns within us all—the fire of ambition, of love, of survival. Diem is a warrior, but she’s also human, and that humanity is what makes her story so powerful. Cole doesn’t romanticize war or power; she shows them for what they are—messy, brutal, and often unjust. But she also shows that even in the ashes of conflict, there’s room for redemption, for second chances, and for the kind of love that can outlast everything else.

If you’ve followed Diem’s journey from the first book, you’ll know this isn’t just another fantasy sequel. It’s the culmination of a character’s arc, a war’s turning point, and a love story that refuses to be contained. And if you haven’t started the series yet, *Heat of the Everflame* is the perfect entry point—a book that demands your attention, challenges your sympathies, and leaves you breathless by the final page.
