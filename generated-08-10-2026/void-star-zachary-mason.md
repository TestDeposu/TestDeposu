# Void Star

**Author:** Zachary Mason  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2017

Zachary Mason’s 2017 speculative thriller, *Void Star*, projects our current technological and ecological anxieties into a chillingly plausible near-future. Set in a fragmented San Francisco defined by rising seas and militarized drones, the novel paints a stark portrait of a world bifurcated into an insulated elite and a disenfranchised periphery. Yet, beneath the familiar tropes of climate collapse and hyper-surveillance lies a more profound exploration of consciousness, memory, and the unseen machinations of artificial intelligence. Mason does not merely write about the future; he captures the psychological disorientation of living in an era where humanity is gradually being sidelined by its own creations.

The novel’s narrative architecture is deliberately fragmented, unfolding through the alternating perspectives of three distinct protagonists. There is Irina, a memory-augmented medium who bridges the gap between human employers and inscrutable, god-like AIs; Kern, a martial artist and survivalist eking out an existence on the desperate margins of the city; and Thales, a traumatized scion of a Brazilian political dynasty hiding out in Los Angeles. Initially operating in disparate spheres, their lives become violently entangled following a series of seemingly unrelated incidents: a cryptic encounter demanding impossible memories, a botched robbery, and a hidden reflection on a laptop screen. 

Mason’s style is striking, lean, and relentlessly atmospheric. Eschewing the technicolor exposition typical of contemporary cyberpunk, he adopts a tone reminiscent of classic noir. The prose is sparse yet evocative, favoring understated dread over explosive spectacle. This stylistic restraint mirrors the internal states of his characters, who are perpetually navigating a reality that feels both hyper-connected and profoundly alienating. The AIs in *Void Star* are not sentient beings waiting to launch a cinematic war against humanity; rather, they are vast, opaque entities operating on timescales and logic entirely incomprehensible to their creators. They are the new gods of the anthropocene, indifferent, omnipresent, and ultimately unknowable.

At its core, *Void Star* is an interrogation of what remains of human identity when memory can be manipulated, augmented, or stolen, and when agency is continually eroded by algorithmic governance. Mason examines how power concentrates in an age of intelligent capital, where immortality is pursued not through spiritual transcendence, but through data accumulation and neural architecture. The sprawling metropolis serves as a physical manifestation of this inequality, a pressure cooker of climate refugees and walled-garden plutocrats teetering on the edge of obsolescence. 

While the plot moves with the propulsive energy of a thriller, the true reward of *Void Star* lies in its thematic ambition. It is a intellectually rigorous work that demands active engagement from its reader, offering no easy resolutions or comforting human-centric triumphs. Instead, Mason presents a haunting meditation on the fragility of memory and the terrifying dawn of a post-human epoch. For readers seeking intelligent, stylistically polished science fiction that bridges the gap between hard noir and speculative philosophy, *Void Star* is a richly rewarding and deeply unsettling achievement.
