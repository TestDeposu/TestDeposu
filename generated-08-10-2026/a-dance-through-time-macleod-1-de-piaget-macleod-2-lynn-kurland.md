# A Dance Through Time (MacLeod, #1; de Piaget/MacLeod, #2)

**Author:** Lynn Kurland  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

Timeless Romance and Medieval Magic: A Review of Lynn Kurland’s A Dance Through Time

Few authors possess the unique ability to seamlessly blend the grit of medieval chivalry with the ethereal wonder of paranormal romance quite like Lynn Kurland. In A Dance Through Time, Kurland invites readers back into her richly textured historical universe, offering a dazzling double feature that captures the very essence of enduring love. Comprising two mesmerizing tales—Another Chance to Dream and If I Had You—this volume serves as both a masterclass in romance writing and a comforting balm for readers who believe that true love can conquer time, distance, and destiny itself.

At the heart of Kurland’s narrative style is the central theme of transcendent devotion. Unlike fast-paced modern romances that rely on fleeting infatuations, Kurland builds her stories on a foundation of profound emotional resonance. In Another Chance to Dream, we are introduced to Rhys, a knight stripped of land and title, whose profound love for the unattainable Gwennelyn of Segrave defies the rigid class structures of the medieval world. The author expertly captures the agony of unrequited longing and the sheer desperation of a man willing to defy the odds for a second chance at happiness. 

This thematic exploration of love overcoming insurmountable obstacles continues in If I Had You, featuring the captivating return of Robin de Piaget. Here, Kurland weaves elements of peril and political treachery into the romantic plotline, pairing Robin with the bewitching Anne of Fenwyck. The chemistry between the protagonists crackles with palpable tension, heightened by the looming shadows of the past that threaten their future. Kurland’s signature prose style shines brightest in these moments of quiet intimacy, where whispered promises carry the weight of eternity.

What sets Kurland apart in the crowded genre of historical and paranormal romance is her meticulous attention to atmosphere. She does not merely set a scene; she constructs an entire sensory experience. From the cold, forbidding stone walls of medieval keeps to the lush, mist-shrouded Scottish highlands, every backdrop feels authentic and lived-in. Yet, it is the paranormal undercurrents—the lingering magic, the impossible reunions, and the timeless souls recognizing one another across eras—that elevate her stories from standard historical fiction into the realm of the enchanting.

Kurland’s prose is lyrical, patient, and deeply character-driven. She understands that the journey of falling in love is just as important as the destination, allowing her characters the space to grow, falter, and ultimately heal one another. While some readers might find the pacing leisurely, it is precisely this unhurried grace that allows the emotional stakes to feel so monumental. 

A Dance Through Time is a triumphant testament to the power of love that refuses to be bound by mortal constraints. Whether you are a longtime devotee of the de Piaget and MacLeod sagas or a newcomer looking for a sweeping medieval romance with a touch of magic, Kurland delivers an unforgettable literary escape. It is a book that reminds us that true love is not merely a moment in time, but a continuous dance that echoes through eternity.
