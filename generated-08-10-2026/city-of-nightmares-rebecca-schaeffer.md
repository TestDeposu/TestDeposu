# City of Nightmares

**Author:** Rebecca Schaeffer  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

Nightmares made flesh. This is the haunting, adrenaline-fueled premise at the beating heart of Rebecca Schaeffer’s 2023 young adult fantasy, City of Nightmares. Merging the gothic, sprawling grit of a fictional metropolis with the surreal, body-horror-tinged whimsy of high-concept fantasy, Schaeffer crafts a world where the boundary between sleep and waking is a razor-thin line of absolute terror. In Newham, the city that never sleeps, the subconscious is not a realm of fleeting imagery, but a ticking time bomb. Dream, and you risk waking up as your deepest, darkest fear—whether that is a towering, man-eating arachnid like the one that shattered protagonist Ness’s family, or a twisted, unrecognizable abomination destined for the city morgue. 

At its core, City of Nightmares is an unflinching exploration of trauma, anxiety, and the paralyzing nature of fear. Schaeffer uses her high-stakes premise as a brilliant thematic mirror for the real-world anxieties of growing up in a hostile environment. Ness, our nineteen-year-old protagonist, is refreshingly far from the typical stoic, weapon-wielding hero of contemporary YA. She is unapologetically cowardly, driven not by a thirst for glory or a desire to save the world, but by a desperate, hyper-vigilant need to survive. Her neuroses and terror are palpable, anchoring the fantastical elements in a gritty, emotional reality. The central theme examines how we cope with the monsters—both literal and metaphorical—that haunt our pasts. Can you truly live a life worth living when you are merely hiding from your own mind? Ness’s desperate alignment with the Friends of the Restful Soul, a morally grey, quasi-religious organization, speaks volumes about the lengths to which traumatized individuals will go to find safety, even if it means walking directly into another trap.

Stylistically, Schaeffer’s prose is punchy, cinematic, and relentlessly fast-paced. She writes with a visual flair that makes Newham feel suffocatingly alive, a place where neon lights bleed through fog and corruption oozes from every political office and cultish sanctuary. The world-building is visceral and unapologetically macabre, drawing clear tonal parallels to the grim urbanity of Gotham, yet infused with a darkly whimsical, dream-logic nightmare quality reminiscent of Strange the Dreamer. Schaeffer doesn't shy away from the body horror inherent in her concept; transformations are described with a chilling intimacy that makes the threat of sleep feel like a genuine psychological torture. 

Yet, what elevates the novel beyond a mere exercise in dark fantasy is Schaeffer’s sharp, often cynical wit. The narrative balances its heavy themes of grief and survival with razor-sharp dialogue and a delightfully messy dynamic between Ness and her reluctant, potentially cannibalistic Nightmare companion. Their forced alliance injects a frantic, enemies-to-reluctant-partners energy that keeps the pages turning at a breakneck speed. As a political conspiracy unravels following a literal explosive assassination, Schaeffer tightens the narrative noose, forcing her characters—and her readers—to confront the uncomfortable truth that sometimes the monsters we make in our heads are entirely eclipsed by the monsters created by society.

City of Nightmares is a wildly entertaining, darkly imaginative triumph that proves Rebecca Schaeffer is a voice to watch in modern speculative fiction. It is a thrilling ride through the neon-lit gutters of a city built on exhaustion, offering a deeply human story wrapped in a terrifying package. For readers craving a fantasy that bites back, refuses to sugarcoat trauma, and champions a heroine whose greatest superpower is simply refusing to stop running, this book is an absolute must-read.
