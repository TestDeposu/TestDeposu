# Among the Free (Shadow Children, #7)

**Author:** Margaret Peterson Haddix  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2006

Title: The Cost of Liberty: A Critique of Margaret Peterson Haddix’s Among the Free

When Margaret Peterson Haddix first introduced readers to Luke Garner in Among the Hidden, she presented a chillingly plausible dystopia: a totalitarian society rigidly enforcing a two-child law where third-born children were forced to live as ghosts in their own homes. Over the course of the Shadow Children series, we watched Luke grow from a terrified, hidden boy into a courageous young man willing to risk everything for the mere possibility of a normal life. Now, in the seventh and final installment, Among the Free (2006), Haddix brings her groundbreaking saga to a breathless, morally complex conclusion. 

At its core, Among the Free grapples with a profound and timeless philosophical question: What happens the day after the revolution? For six books, the narrative momentum has been fueled by the desperate struggle to overthrow the oppressive Population Police. When Luke inadvertently sparks the final, successful rebellion that dismantles the old regime, the euphoria of victory is immediately replaced by a dizzying vacuum of power. Haddix brilliantly shifts the thematic focus from survival against tyranny to the messy, terrifying reality of nation-building. Freedom, the novel argues, is not a neat finish line; it is a chaotic, fragile beginning. 

Haddix’s authorial style shines brightest in her ability to maintain relentless suspense while never talking down to her young adult audience. Known for her short, punchy chapters and relentless cliffhangers, she wastes no time on superfluous exposition. The opening pages immediately plunge the reader into mortal peril with the cocking of a gun, establishing an atmosphere of uncertainty where old enemies wear new faces and yesterday’s allies become tomorrow’s dictators. 

What elevates Among the Free above standard dystopian fare is Haddix’s refusal to provide easy, fairy-tale answers. The overthrow of the government does not instantly erase years of paranoia, brainwashing, and scarce resources. Instead, Luke and his peers must navigate the treacherous waters of societal reconstruction. Who gets to make the rules now? How do you govern a populace that only knows how to hide? By forcing her characters to wrestle with these heavy ethical dilemmas, Haddix treats her readers with immense respect, challenging them to consider the true price of liberty and the heavy responsibilities that accompany human rights.

Luke Garner’s evolution as a protagonist reaches a poignant crescendo in this volume. He is no longer just a boy hiding in the shadows, nor is he a classic, swaggering action hero. He is a reluctant catalyst, profoundly exhausted yet stubbornly idealistic. His internal conflicts—torn between the desire for a quiet, ordinary life and the inescapable duty to shape a better world—anchor the narrative's emotional core. 

Among the Free is a masterclass in dystopian pacing and thematic payoff. It avoids the trap of wrapping up every loose end with a neat bow, opting instead for a chilling, realistic, and ultimately hopeful conclusion. Haddix leaves her audience with a lingering reminder that freedom is an ongoing project, requiring constant vigilance and moral courage. For anyone who has followed Luke’s journey from the floorboards of a rural farmhouse to the halls of power, this final volume is an unforgettable, thought-provoking triumph.
