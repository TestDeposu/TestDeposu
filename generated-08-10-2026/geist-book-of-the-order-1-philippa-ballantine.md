# Geist (Book of the Order, #1)

**Author:** Philippa Ballantine  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

Philippa Ballantine’s Geist, the explosive first installment in the Book of the Order series, plunges readers into a richly atmospheric imperial fantasy where the dead refuse to stay buried. In a world teetering on the edge of supernatural chaos, Ballantine crafts a compelling narrative that marries gritty dark fantasy with procedural mystery, establishing a chilling universe where spirits are not merely memories, but active, predatory threats.

At the heart of the novel is the Order of the Deacons, a specialized frontline defense force tasked with warding off and neutralizing the malevolent geists that plague the Empire. Ballantine’s world-building is both intricate and visceral. She eschews glossy, high-fantasy tropes in favor of a soot-stained, perilous reality where magic comes with a heavy toll and the supernatural is an ever-present claustrophobic danger. The lore of the geists and the ancient, twisted rituals unearthed throughout the narrative provide a strong mythological backbone that keeps the pages turning. 

The stylistic strength of Geist lies in Ballantine’s handling of character dynamics through an enforced, high-stakes partnership. Sorcha Faris, a seasoned and profoundly powerful deacon, serves as a sharp, pragmatic anchor. Her forced mentorship of Merrick Chambers, an untested novice, introduces a classic dynamic of experience versus raw potential, yet Ballantine avoids cliché by grounding their interactions in genuine tactical necessity and mutual wariness. 

The introduction of Raed Rossin—a shape-shifter, Imperial Pretender, and bearer of a lethal curse—elevates the narrative from a standard monster-hunting procedural into a complex political and personal crucible. Ballantine excels at interpersonal friction. The tension between the trio is palpable, driven not just by their disparate backgrounds and dangerous secrets, but by the desperate need to survive an escalating supernatural threat in an isolated, deeply unsettling village. 

The author’s prose is lean, atmospheric, and propulsive. She uses sensory details effectively, conjuring the cold dread of a haunting and the suffocating isolation of the frontier settlement. The threat they face is not just another wandering spirit, but an apex predator of the undead realm that challenges the very limits of the Deacons' abilities. This escalation transforms the latter half of the book into a breathless race against time, blending action with dark historical mystery.

Geist succeeds brilliantly as an opening salvo. It delivers a masterclass in establishing high stakes, complex protagonists, and a deeply immersive dark fantasy setting. Philippa Ballantine has built a world where the past literally haunts the present, and readers looking for intelligent, gritty fantasy with a supernatural edge will find themselves thoroughly enthralled by the journey.
