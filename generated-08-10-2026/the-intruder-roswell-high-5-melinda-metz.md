# The Intruder (Roswell High, #5)

**Author:** Melinda Metz  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2001

Stakes have never been higher in Roswell, and Melinda Metz proves in The Intruder, the fifth installment of the iconic Roswell High series, that young adult science fiction can successfully blend high-octane paranoia with the agonizing pangs of teenage romance. Published in 2001, this lean and breathless novel captures the exact sweet spot of turn-of-the-millennium YA fiction: fast-paced, emotionally raw, and utterly addictive.

Metz wastes no time plunging the reader into a claustrophobic nightmare. The narrative hinges on a terrifyingly intimate premise: Michael Guerin, the brooding alien rebel of the group, is locked away in a secret government compound. Sheriff Valenti is closing in, turning the screws with ruthless interrogation tactics designed to extract the ultimate secret—the identities of the extraterrestrials hiding in plain sight. Metz crafts a palpable sense of dread. Michael’s primary concern is not his own survival, but the safety of his friends, Max, Maria, Liz, Alex, and Isabel. This selflessness elevates Michael from a standard bad-boy trope into a deeply tragic, compelling figure.

Yet, the true emotional engine of The Intruder lies elsewhere, steering the series into fascinating territory. While Michael faces torture and interrogation, the narrative brilliantly pivots to Isabel. Previously defined by her cool detachment and sibling-like dynamic with Michael, Isabel undergoes a startling psychological shift. As Michael is held captive, a bizarre and intense psychic bridge forms between them. Isabel becomes a vessel for his suffering; she hears his every thought, bleeds his every emotion, and shares his fevered dreams. 

Metz handles this telepathic connection with surprising nuance. It blurs the lines of identity and autonomy, forcing Isabel to question the nature of her own feelings. Is this profound intimacy real, or merely a byproduct of their shared alien biology under duress? This romantic pivot is handled with the signature melodrama that fans of the series adore, yet it carries genuine emotional weight. Time is ticking, and the urgency of Michael’s rescue mirrors the frantic realization that Isabel’s heart might be changing too late.

Metz’s writing style in The Intruder is defined by economy and momentum. She avoids bloated exposition, opting instead for short, punchy chapters that alternate perspectives, keeping the adrenaline high. The prose is unpretentious, tailored perfectly to capture the heightened emotional states of teenagers dealing with world-ending secrets. Every glance, touch, and telepathic whisper feels amplified, reflecting how teenagers experience love and betrayal as matters of life and death.

The book’s central theme is captivity—both literal and emotional. Michael is physically trapped within concrete walls and government surveillance, while Isabel is trapped by her own denial and the sudden, overwhelming realization of what Michael means to her. Furthermore, the shadow of Sheriff Valenti hangs heavy, representing the ultimate adolescent fear: being exposed, misunderstood, and hunted by the adult world. 

The Intruder is a masterclass in transitional storytelling. It deepens the mythology of the Roswell universe while delivering a deeply personal story about sacrifice, awakening, and unexpected love. Metz delivers a taut, emotionally resonant thriller that will leave readers turning pages well into the night, desperate to know if love can conquer a government conspiracy.
