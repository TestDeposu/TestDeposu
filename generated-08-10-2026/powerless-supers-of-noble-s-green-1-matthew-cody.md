# Powerless (Supers of Noble's Green, #1)

**Author:** Matthew Cody  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2009

Imagine a childhood where invulnerability, flight, and invisibility are your everyday playground perks, only to come with a ticking clock that counts down to absolute oblivion. This is the high-stakes, bittersweet reality at the heart of Matthew Cody’s 2009 debut, Powerless, the opening installment of the Supers of Noble’s Green series. Cody takes the well-trodden tropes of comic book mythos and breathes fresh, youthful air into them by anchoring his narrative in a profound and poignant coming-of-age theme: the inevitable loss of childhood wonder.

The story introduces us to twelve-year-old Daniel, a newcomer to a seemingly sleepy town that harbors an extraordinary secret. His new friends are a tight-knit clique of superpowered children, soaring through skies, manipulating electricity, and vanishing into thin air. Yet, this is no standard spandex-clad fantasy. A cruel twist of biology and a lingering local curse dictate that the moment these kids turn thirteen, their powers vanish entirely, taking with them every single memory of ever having been extraordinary. They are living on borrowed magic, racing against a deadline that strips away not just their abilities, but their very identities. 

Cody’s primary thematic triumph lies in this tragic countdown. By tying superpowers directly to the fleeting nature of childhood, the author elevates a fun adventure into an emotional exploration of growing up. The impending thirteenth birthday serves as a brilliant metaphor for the end of innocence, the loss of imagination, and the terrifying threshold of adolescence. The children are not just fighting a mysterious, memory-stealing supervillain connected to a World War II-era comic book and a fateful meteor strike; they are fighting the passage of time itself. 

In terms of style, Cody writes with a brisk, cinematic pace that effortlessly captures the breathless energy of middle-grade fiction while respecting the intelligence of his readers. His prose is punchy, action-oriented, and rich with atmospheric nostalgia. Noble’s Green feels simultaneously timeless and deeply grounded, blending the small-town charm reminiscent of classic adventure tales with the neon-infused mythology of vintage comic books. The incorporation of lore—from the legendary hero Johnny Noble to the eerie green flames of Witch Fire—creates a textured world that feels much larger than the page count suggests.

What ultimately holds the novel together, however, is its clever subversion of the protagonist. Daniel is "powerless," a regular kid navigating a world of gods in miniature. Yet Cody uses Daniel’s perspective to explore the true essence of heroism. Powerless suggests that courage, loyalty, and the willingness to stand up for your friends do not require an extraordinary genetic mutation or a lightning strike. True power is found in ordinary choices made under extraordinary circumstances. 

Powerless succeeds because it understands that the most compelling superhero stories are never really about the powers—they are about the human heart beating beneath the cape. Cody delivers a fast-paced, imaginative, and surprisingly touching mystery that will leave readers questioning what parts of their own childhood magic they might be in danger of forgetting.
