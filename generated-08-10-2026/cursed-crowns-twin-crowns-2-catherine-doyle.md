# Cursed Crowns (Twin Crowns, #2)

**Author:** Catherine Doyle  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

In the landscape of contemporary young adult fantasy, it is rare to find a series that balances high-stakes political maneuvering with the kind of breezy, kinetic joy that Catherine Doyle and Katherine Webber mastered in their breakout hit, Twin Crowns. With the release of the sequel, Cursed Crowns, the authors prove that their success was no fluke. They have crafted a sophomore installment that expertly expands the map of Eana while deepening the internal and external conflicts that make Wren and Rose such compelling, distinct protagonists.

The central theme of Cursed Crowns is the friction between legacy and agency. Having reclaimed their thrones at the end of the first novel, the twins are no longer merely fighting to survive; they are fighting to define what it means to lead. Doyle and Webber lean heavily into the duality of their leads: Rose, the structured, diplomatic queen, and Wren, the volatile, instinct-driven witch-queen. By physically separating them for the majority of the book, the authors allow the sisters to grow into their own power, illustrating that true unity is a choice made between two sovereign individuals, not a necessity born of shared trauma.

Doyle and Webber’s writing style remains the series’ greatest asset. It is accessible, whip-smart, and remarkably rhythmic. They possess a gift for shifting perspectives without losing momentum—a common pitfall in dual-narrative fantasy. Whether we are trekking across the frost-bitten landscape of Gevra with Wren or navigating the shifting political sands of the south with Rose, the prose remains infused with a "swoony" quality that never undercuts the looming danger. The stakes here are genuinely menacing, yet the authors maintain that essential sparkle of humor that keeps the reader’s heart fluttering even as the political situation in Anadawn threatens to collapse.

What makes Cursed Crowns particularly striking is the world-building expansion. We are no longer confined to the familiar dynamics of the palace; we are thrust into the lore of the Sunkissed Kingdom and the enigmatic power of the Seers. The introduction of the mysterious stranger and the promise of the Restless Sands suggests a mythology that is much older and far more treacherous than we were led to believe in book one. It elevates the story from a charming rom-com with swords into a sprawling, high-fantasy epic that demands the reader’s full attention.

The brilliance of the book lies in how it handles the "cursed" nature of its crowns. The twins aren’t just battling external villains; they are battling the entrenched prejudices of their own citizens who fear magic. By focusing on the struggle to change hearts and minds, the authors provide a grounded, emotional core to the high-magic antics. It is a story about the cost of peace and the bravery required to forsake old, comfortable loyalties for the sake of a better, unified future.

Cursed Crowns is a confident, daring sequel that manages to outshine its predecessor by raising the stakes and refining the character arcs. It is a testament to the fact that Doyle and Webber are not just writers of fluff, but architects of a sophisticated, enchanting world. Readers who fell in love with the banter and romance of the first book will be satisfied, but they will also find themselves surprised by the darker, more intricate threads the authors have woven into this second chapter. As the sisters race toward an inevitable, high-stakes reunion, the reader is left breathless, eagerly anticipating how they will eventually reconcile their crowns with their destinies. This is fantasy at its most charismatic—thoroughly modern, fiercely feminist, and undeniably addictive.
