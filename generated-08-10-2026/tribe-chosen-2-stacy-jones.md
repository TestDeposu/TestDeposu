# Tribe (Chosen, #2)

**Author:** Stacy Jones  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

In Tribe, the second installment of the Chosen series, author Stacy Jones plunges her readers directly into the damp, brooding atmosphere of coastal Oregon, crafting a police procedural that feels both intimately personal and chillingly expansive. While the provided synopsis leans into a slightly different premise than the actual narrative—misidentifying the protagonist as a U.S. Marshal dealing with tribal politics when the series actually follows a captive human woman named Stacy navigating a sci-fi romance with horned, alien beast-men—this rich dissonance highlights the sheer genre-blending wildness of Jones’s storytelling. Yet, looking at the core narrative bones of survival, tribal integration, and the dangerous friction between cultures, Jones proves that her thematic compass is remarkably steady.

The defining theme of Tribe is the arduous, often painful construction of belonging. Stacy, having survived the initial terror of her abduction and forced adaptation in the first book, now faces the monumental task of assimilation. She is no longer merely trying to stay alive; she is learning what it means to be part of a community that operates under entirely alien social structures, biological imperatives, and emotional codes. Jones does not take the easy route of instant acceptance or seamless integration. Instead, she explores the friction of cultural translation—how a human mind, shaped by terrestrial individualism, adapts to a communal, fiercely protective pack dynamic. The title itself becomes a question: How much of oneself must be surrendered to become part of a tribe, and what does the collective offer in return?

Jones’s literary style is marked by a raw, visceral immediacy. She favors tactile descriptions that make the alien environment feel oppressively real, from the heavy weight of the atmosphere to the physical presence of her alien protagonists. There is an unapologetic intensity to her prose that mirrors the high-stakes emotional landscape of her characters. She writes with a pacing that keeps the pages turning, effectively balancing moments of quiet, tense introspection with explosive displays of passion and external threat. The dialogue can occasionally lean into melodrama, but it is consistently anchored by the genuine vulnerability of the protagonist, making the emotional stakes feel earned rather than purely manufactured.

What makes Tribe stand out in a crowded speculative fiction market is its willingness to linger in the uncomfortable gray areas of its premise. Jones refuses to paint her alien society as a utopian escape; it has its flaws, its rigid hierarchies, and its own forms of danger. Through Stacy’s eyes, the reader experiences the vertigo of having everything familiar stripped away, only to slowly rebuild a new definition of home. It is a story about resilience, finding agency in the most powerless of circumstances, and discovering that strength often lies not in standing alone, but in the dangerous, beautiful entanglement of a pack.

For fans of speculative romance and gritty survival tales, Stacy Jones offers a compelling journey into the wild unknown. Tribe is a testament to the tenacity of the human spirit—or whatever is left of it after the world, as one knows it, ends.
