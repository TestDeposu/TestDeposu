# The Liar's Crown (Dominions, #1)

**Author:** Abigail Owen  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2022

The Liar’s Crown pulls readers into a glittering yet treacherous court where identity is both armor and weapon. Abigail Owen crafts a world that feels both mythic and immediate, using lush, sensory prose to paint the cold stone corridors of the palace and the whispering shadows that cling to its walls. From the opening line—“Everything about my life is a lie”—the narrative thrusts us into the mind of a hidden twin princess whose very existence is a decoy, a sacrifice waiting to be called upon. This premise fuels the novel’s central theme: the cost of living a lie and the fierce agency required to reclaim one’s truth.

Owen’s style shines in the way she balances intimate character moments with sweeping, high‑stakes action. The protagonist’s internal conflict—loyalty to her sister versus the burgeoning distrust of the ageless King Eidolon—is rendered with razor‑sharp clarity, allowing readers to feel every heartbeat of hesitation and resolve. When the Shadowraith abducts her, mistaken for the queen, the story pivots into a taut chase that intertwines physical danger with psychological unraveling. Owen’s handling of the shadow magic is particularly striking; she treats darkness not merely as a visual motif but as a living, shifting force that mirrors the protagonist’s fragmented self. The Shadowraith’s own struggle to contain the faces and identities he harbors becomes a dark mirror to the princess’s quest to shed the false skin she’s worn since birth.

The novel’s pacing is relentless yet never feels rushed. Owen intersperses quieter, introspective scenes—moments of whispered confessions, furtive glances in moonlit halls—with bursts of sword‑play and shadow‑laden confrontations that keep the adrenaline high. This rhythm reinforces the theme that truth, once uncovered, cannot be contained; it erupts, reshaping loyalties and threatening the very foundations of the dominion. The looming winter at the walls serves as a potent metaphor for the encroaching inevitability of revelation, while the promise to “kill Eidolon…and the Shadowraith who has stolen my heart” raises the stakes to a personal, almost mythic level.

What sets Owen apart is her ability to make fantasy feel emotionally resonant without sacrificing the genre’s requisite spectacle. Her dialogue crackles with wit and tension, and her world‑building is delivered through vivid, concrete details rather than exhaustive exposition. Readers are invited to infer the history of the Dominions through the protagonist’s fragmented memories and the cryptic hints dropped by the Shadowraith, creating an engaging sense of discovery that rewards attentive reading.

The Liar’s Crown is more than a thrilling fantasy romp; it’s a meditation on the masks we wear and the courage required to tear them away. Owen’s lyrical yet incisive style ensures that each twist feels earned, each revelation both shocking and inevitable. For fans of YA fantasy who crave both heart‑pounding action and profound emotional depth, this debut promises a compelling start to a series that will linger long after the final page is turned.
