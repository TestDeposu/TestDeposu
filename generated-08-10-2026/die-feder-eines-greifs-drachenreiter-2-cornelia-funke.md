# Die Feder eines Greifs (Drachenreiter, #2)

**Author:** Cornelia Funke  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2016

Return to a World of Wonder: A Review of Cornelia Funke’s *The Griffin's Feather*

Cornelia Funke has long held a revered place in the pantheon of modern fantasy literature, known for her ability to weave tactile, breathing worlds out of pure imagination. With *The Griffin's Feather*—the long-awaited sequel to her beloved international bestseller *Dragon Rider*—Funke proves once again that her creative well is inexhaustible. Returning to the enchanting universe she forged decades ago, Funke delivers a masterclass in middle-grade adventure that effortlessly captivates readers of all ages, blending high-stakes environmental urgency with the timeless magic of mythical creatures.

Set two years after the triumphant defeat of the golden dragon-eater Nesselbrand, the novel reunites us with familiar heroes Ben, Barnabas, and the homunculus Fliegenbein. However, peace is fleeting in the realm of fable and myth. When the fragile offspring of the world's last remaining Pegasus face a fatal affliction, a desperate race against time begins. The only known cure lies in the elusive, fiercely guarded sun-feather of a griffin. This quest hurls our intrepid companions across the globe to the lush, unpredictable landscapes of Indonesia, dragging along a delightfully chaotic entourage that includes a flying rat, a steadfast Fjord troll, and a chronically nervous parrot. Naturally, true success remains out of reach until they call upon the ultimate heavy-hitters: a silver dragon and his fiercely loyal brownie.

At the heart of *The Griffin's Feather* lies a profound and resonant theme: the precarious coexistence between humanity and the natural world, both real and imagined. Funke uses her menagerie of mythical beasts not merely as decorative set pieces, but as emotional stand-ins for Earth's endangered species. The vulnerability of the Pegasus brood mirrors the fragile state of our own planet's biodiversity. Through this lens, the quest for the griffin’s feather transcends a standard treasure hunt; it becomes a meditation on stewardship, empathy, and the heavy responsibility of preserving wonder in a world increasingly hostile to magic. Funke reminds young readers—and reminds older ones with nostalgic force—that protecting the vulnerable is a noble, necessary burden.

Stylistically, Funke is at the absolute peak of her powers. Her prose is lush, sensory, and wonderfully atmospheric. She possesses the rare gift of making the fantastical feel utterly tangible. When her characters traverse the Indonesian jungles or soar through cloud-drenched skies, you can practically feel the humid air, smell the ancient moss, and hear the rustle of invisible wings. This rich texture is paired with breakneck pacing and relentless suspense. Danger lurks behind every frond and beneath every wave, ensuring that the pages turn themselves. 

Yet, what truly elevates the novel is Funke’s characteristic warmth and humor. The dynamic among the eclectic band of travelers provides a brilliant counterweight to the overarching peril. The bickering, the accidental triumphs, and the deep-seated loyalty forged in the crucible of danger give the story its beating heart. Funke treats her characters—human, beast, and magical alike—with profound dignity, endowing even the smallest flying rat with distinct motivations and emotional depth.

*The Griffin's Feather* is more than a nostalgic cash-in on a legacy property; it is a vital, thrilling expansion of a beloved universe. Cornelia Funke has crafted a triumphant return that honors its predecessor while charting bold new emotional and geographical territories. It is a dazzling reminder that as long as there are storytellers like Funke, magic will never truly go extinct. For anyone who has ever looked up at the clouds and wished for the shadow of a dragon to cross the sun, this book is an absolute must-read.
