# Trouble Magnet (Pip & Flinx, #12)

**Author:** Alan Dean Foster  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2006

Title: Finding Humanity in the Mouth of Chaos: A Review of Alan Dean Foster’s Trouble Magnet

For decades, science fiction has explored the grand machinery of space opera—galactic empires, world-destroying threats, and god-like weapons. Yet, the most enduring space opera tales rarely hinge on the cold mechanics of destruction; they hinge on the messy, unpredictable nature of the human heart. In Trouble Magnet, the twelfth installment of Alan Dean Foster’s beloved Pip & Flinx series published in 2006, the veteran author returns to his signature formula, offering a rollicking, high-stakes interstellar escapade that cleverly balances cosmic dread with intimate soul-searching.

The plot operates on the grandest possible scale. Flinx, the galaxy’s reluctant savior blessed with formidable mental powers, and Pip, his fiercely loyal, venomous minidrag, are tasked with a seemingly impossible mission. Somewhere in the remote dark of space floats a gargantuan, sentient Tar-Aiym weapon system. Flinx needs to locate this ancient superweapon and somehow convince it to obliterate a colossal evil hurtling toward the Commonwealth. It is a classic save-the-universe narrative, but Foster wisely subverts expectations by refusing to let the plot rush straight to the finish line. 

Before Flinx can save civilization, he feels compelled to answer a more agonizing, deeply personal question: Are humans actually worth saving? 

This existential query forms the beating heart of Trouble Magnet and highlights Foster’s greatest thematic strength. Rather than plunging immediately into the cosmic quest, Flinx detours to Visaria, a dangerously depraved planet known for its lawlessness and moral decay. Here, Foster crafts a narrative that feels part planetary romance and part picaresque adventure. Flinx finds himself acting as a reluctant, lawless Pied Piper to a pack of lying, thieving juvenile delinquents. 

This detour is not merely filler; it is a vital thematic crucible. By immersing his protagonist in the grime and grit of Visaria's criminal underworld, Foster forces Flinx—and the reader—to confront the paradox of humanity. Visaria is a place where high moral values and utopian ideals are entirely absent. Yet, as Flinx interacts with these abandoned youth, the novel explores themes of empathy, inherent worth, and the possibility of redemption in the darkest corners of society. When Flinx inevitably crosses paths with the planet’s ruthless crime king, the danger escalates from a philosophical musing into a visceral, pulse-pounding survival test.

True to the book's title, trouble follows Flinx like gravity. Foster populates his journey with the chaotic supporting cast that fans of the series have come to expect: official snoops, crazed zealots, assorted goons, and opportunistic assassins. This delightful menagerie of pursuers injects a steady dose of chaotic energy and witty banter into the narrative, preventing the existential themes from ever feeling overly heavy or self-serious. 

Furthermore, Foster brilliantly weaves the external chaos together with Flinx’s internal journey by introducing a shocking clue regarding the protagonist’s shadowy past. Visaria proves to be more than just a moral testbed; it is a psychological mirror, offering devastating, heartening, and jaw-dropping revelations about where Flinx comes from. By tying the fate of the galaxy to the protagonist's personal origin story, Foster ensures that the stakes remain profoundly intimate.

Stylistically, Trouble Magnet showcases Alan Dean Foster at the height of his powers. His prose is brisk, cinematic, and conversational, effortlessly blending hard-edged sci-fi concepts with whimsical creature features and snappy dialogue. Foster has always excelled at world-building through color and texture—Visaria practically drips off the page with neon squalor and visceral menace, contrasting sharply with the cold, majestic void where the Tar-Aiym weapon waits. 

For longtime fans, this novel delivers everything that makes the Pip & Flinx series enduring: danger, humor, exotic alien vistas, and the unbreakable bond between a telepathic redhead and his winged companion. For newcomers, Trouble Magnet serves as a surprisingly accessible entry point into a rich universe, proving that even when the entire galaxy is on the brink of annihilation, the most fascinating frontier left to explore is still the human soul.
