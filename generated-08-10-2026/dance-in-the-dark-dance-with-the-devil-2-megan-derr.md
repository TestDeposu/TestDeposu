# Dance in the Dark (Dance with the Devil, #2)

**Author:** Megan Derr  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

Megan Derr has long established herself as a master of weaving intricate, character-driven fantasy where the supernatural is treated with a refreshing blend of matter-of-fact practicality and deep emotional resonance. In Dance in the Dark, the second installment of her Dance with the Devil series, Derr returns to a shadowy world of vampires, magic, and hidden lore, delivering a narrative that is as intellectually stimulating as it is emotionally complex. At its core, the book transcends the boundaries of traditional urban fantasy by focusing heavily on a profoundly compelling theme: the labyrinth of identity, trauma, and the twisted bonds of family.

The story centers on Johnnie Desrosiers, a man whose very existence is defined by a supreme paradox. Traumatized at age nine when his parents were murdered by a blood-crazed vampire, Johnnie was subsequently adopted by the very man married to the creature responsible for his orphanhood. Now an adult, he navigates a strange, twilight existence as a cold, beautiful scholar translating ancient grimoires and solving occult mysteries. Derr uses Johnnie to explore how humans survive proximity to monsters, not through violent resistance, but through adaptation, compartmentalization, and quiet endurance. Johnnie is not a roaring action hero; he is a quiet archivist of the strange, a man who buries his profound grief and displacement in dusty texts and intellectual puzzles. 

What makes Dance in the Dark striking is Derr's signature understated style. Unlike many modern fantasy authors who rely on sensory overload and breakneck pacing, Derr favors a measured, atmospheric approach. The prose is clean, sharp, and focused heavily on internal monologue and interpersonal dynamics. She builds her world from the inside out, prioritizing the quiet spaces between characters over grand, world-ending battles. When Johnnie is approached by his friend Rostislav to solve what appears to be a routine mystery, the plot unfurls with a deliberate, hypnotic rhythm. The investigation quickly evolves from a simple puzzle into a deeply personal excavation of Johnnie's past, forcing him to confront uncomfortable truths about the family that adopted him and the nature of the world he thought he understood.

Derr excels at subverting expectations within established supernatural tropes. Vampires in her universe are not merely slavering beasts or tragic, romantic heartthrobs; they are part of a complex, bureaucratic, and often messy society that mirrors our own. This grounds the fantasy elements in a gritty realism that makes the emotional stakes feel entirely authentic. Johnnie’s journey is not just about solving a crime, but about reclaiming a narrative that was stolen from him the day his parents died. It is a story about the ghosts we live with—both literal and figurative—and the arduous process of stepping out of the shadows of our upbringing to find out who we are on our own terms.

For readers who appreciate intelligent supernatural mysteries, rich world-building, and protagonists who rely on their wits and emotional resilience rather than brute force, Dance in the Dark is a compelling read. Megan Derr proves once again that the most terrifying and rewarding monsters we face are the ones buried deep within our own histories.
