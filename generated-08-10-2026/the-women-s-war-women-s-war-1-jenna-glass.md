# The Women's War (Women's War, #1)

**Author:** Jenna Glass  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2019

Title: Sowing the Seeds of Revolution: A Review of Jenna Glass’s *The Women’s War*

What happens to a patriarchal world when the ultimate instrument of control—reproduction—is suddenly ripped from the hands of men and placed firmly into the minds and wills of women? This is the explosive premise of Jenna Glass’s 2019 epic fantasy, *The Women’s War*. In a genre historically dominated by medieval tropes, chosen farm-boys, and male-centric quests, Glass crafts a refreshing and fiercely relevant narrative that serves as both a sprawling secondary-world fantasy and a sharp-edged allegory for the modern feminist movement. 

At its core, *The Women’s War* explores the theme of bodily autonomy as the ultimate catalyst for systemic collapse. In the novel’s rigidly patriarchal societies, women are treated as little more than chattel—incubators meant to secure bloodlines and consolidate male power. A nobleman's primary currency is the male heir, and female existence is tightly regulated. Glass brilliantly subverts this dynamic by introducing a revolutionary spell that grants women absolute control over their own fertility. This magic acts as a slow-burning fuse, detonating centuries of oppression. The resulting cultural shockwaves expose the fragility of male dominance, proving that an empire built on the subjugation of half its population cannot survive when that half learns the taste of freedom. 

Glass’s authorial style anchors this high-concept magical premise in deeply grounded, character-driven storytelling. Eschewing the typical roster of untouchable, sword-wielding teenage heroines, Glass makes a bold and welcome choice to center her narrative on women navigating the complexities of maturity and middle age. Through the dual perspectives of Alys—a disinherited king’s daughter and widowed mother discovering her own political and magical prowess—and Ellin, a shockingly young queen expected to hand her crown over to a husband, the novel portrays a nuanced spectrum of female resilience. Glass does not paint her characters as flawless paragons; instead, they are flawed, pragmatic, and desperate women forced to strategize within a wretchedly unfair system. Their triumphs feel hard-won because the stakes are absolute: life, death, and the reclamation of human dignity. 

Furthermore, Glass weaves multiple subplots together with impressive pacing. The introduction of exiled women and disgraced soldiers discovering a raw, female-centric magic in the harsh desert expands the scope of the world organically. The prose is unflinching, capturing both the visceral brutality of a threatened patriarchy and the electric euphoria of liberation. Glass acknowledges a timeless historical truth: men who hold power will use every violent instrument at their disposal to retain it. Yet, the true power of the narrative lies in its answer to that oppression—the unbreakable, cascading resolve of generations of women waking up to their own worth.

*The Women's War* is a triumphant blend of traditional epic fantasy world-building and the fierce energy of the #MeToo era. It challenges readers to examine how easily societal structures are maintained through bodily control, and how radically they can be dismantled when bodily autonomy is restored. Jenna Glass has delivered a compelling, urgent, and deeply satisfying kickoff to a fantasy series that demands a place on the shelf of every reader who loves smart, socially conscious storytelling.
