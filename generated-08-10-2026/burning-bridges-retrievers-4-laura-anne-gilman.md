# Burning Bridges (Retrievers, #4)

**Author:** Laura Anne Gilman  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2020

Title: Ashes of the Syndicate: Laura Anne Gilman’s Burning Bridges Tests the Soul of New York

In the fourth installment of the acclaimed Retrievers series, Burning Bridges, Laura Anne Gilman takes a match to the delicate criminal ecosystem she has spent three books constructing—and watches it burn brilliantly. Released in 2020, this entry proves that urban fantasy can be both gritty and deeply philosophical. Gilman abandons any comfort zones left for her characters, forcing them to navigate a metropolis tearing itself apart at the seams. The result is a sharp, atmospheric, and relentlessly paced novel that redefines the stakes for its iconic duo.

At the heart of Gilman’s narrative style is her mastery of gritty, street-level world-building. Rather than relying on sweeping, Tolkien-esque epic fantasy, Gilman treats magic and mythology like blue-collar trades. The Cosa Nostradamus—the supernatural syndicate governing New York—is not an abstract council of wizards; it is a sweating, bleeding, highly dysfunctional corporate entity. In Burning Bridges, that entity is having a heart attack. 

The central theme of the novel is the fragility of systemic alliances and the heavy cost of coexistence. When attacks against the non-human Fatae escalate from territorial disputes into full-blown hate crimes, the shockwaves don't just hit the margins; they threaten to consume the humans as well. Gilman uses this urban unrest as a razor-sharp mirror to modern societal fractures. Prejudice, fear-mongering, and the weaponization of difference are dissecting tools in her hands. As the Mage Council turns inward, paralyzed by internal power struggles, the Talented and the Fatae are forced into a desperate, fragile coalition. 

Wren, the pragmatic and weary retriever, anchors this chaotic landscape with her signature dry wit and fierce resilience. Gilman’s character work shines brightest here, particularly in Wren’s dynamic with her partner, Sergei. While the city burns around them, Sergei is slowly self-destructing from the inside out, providing an intimate, emotional counterweight to the macro-level political chaos. Wren is caught between playing keeper to a self-destructing friend and acting as the linchpin for a supernatural peace treaty, alongside the ever-delightful and dangerous demon P.B. 

Gilman’s prose is lean, muscular, and steeped in noir tradition. She doesn't waste words on flowery exposition; instead, she lets the action, the snappy dialogue, and the heavy atmosphere do the talking. New York City feels alive—grimy, neon-lit, and pulsing with ancient magic hiding beneath asphalt and subway tunnels. 

What makes Burning Bridges a striking triumph is its refusal to offer easy Hollywood resolutions. Gilman pushes her characters toward a terrifying precipice, forcing them to contemplate a drastic measure that will irrevocably alter the city’s landscape. It asks the ultimate question: how much of your home are you willing to destroy in order to save it?

For fans of urban fantasy that favors psychological depth, high-stakes politics, and characters with real scars over glittering escapism, Burning Bridges is an essential read. Gilman doesn't just bridge communities; she lights the fuse on a brand-new era for the genre.
