# The Sunken Mall (The Tarot Sequence, #1.5)

**Author:** K.D. Edwards  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** Unknown

In the neon-drenched, mythic ruins of New Atlantis, K.D. Edwards has carved out a distinct corner of modern urban fantasy that feels both intimately lived-in and dangerously volatile. The Sunken Mall, a pivotal entry in The Tarot Sequence, acts as a masterclass in world-building economy, proving that a shorter page count is no barrier to emotional resonance or narrative punch. For fans of the series, this installment is an addictive pit stop; for newcomers, it serves as a razor-sharp thesis statement on what makes Edwards’ writing so utterly compelling.

At its core, the main theme of The Sunken Mall—and indeed the broader Tarot Sequence—is the heavy, often agonizing cost of survival and the fiercely protective bonds of chosen family. Edwards is relentlessly preoccupied with the wreckage left in the wake of systemic trauma. His protagonist, Rune Saint John, is a survivor of a genocide that wiped out almost his entire magical Court. Yet, rather than wallowing solely in grief, Rune tackles his reality with a brilliant, biting sarcasm and a desperate, unwavering loyalty to those he loves. The "sunken mall" of the title is more than just a brilliant setting; it is a physical manifestation of buried history, forgotten consumerism, and the metaphorical graveyard of a past society. Edwards uses this claustrophobic, watery labyrinth to explore how the past refuses to stay submerged, forcing his characters to confront not only external threats but the internal fractures they carry like scar tissue.

Edwards' stylistic signature is defined by its kinetic energy and emotional vulnerability. His prose moves with the speed of a thriller, blending high-stakes magical action with snappy, razor-sharp dialogue that crackles off the page. There is a specific cadence to his writing—a unique fusion of ancient mythological gravitas and modern, millennial exhaustion—that creates an immediate intimacy with the reader. He writes trauma without losing humor, and romance without sacrificing plot momentum. Even in a shorter format, Edwards excels at sensory details: the hum of lingering magic, the chill of subterranean water, the flash of gold and Tarot-inspired iconography. He avoids purple prose, opting instead for a lean, muscular style that prioritizes emotional authenticity and pacing above all else.

What makes The Sunken Mall particularly striking is how effortlessly it balances grand, apocalyptic stakes with deeply personal, quiet moments of healing. It reminds us that saving the world is exhausting, but sharing a sarcastic remark or a moment of tenderness in the ruins is where true resistance lies. K.D. Edwards delivers a punchy, atmospheric triumph that solidifies his place as one of the most exciting voices in contemporary queer urban fantasy. It is brief, brilliant, and impossible to put down.
