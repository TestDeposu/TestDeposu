# Bran Mak Morn: The Last King

**Author:** Robert E. Howard  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2005

Before Conan strode the jewel-napped kingdoms of Hyboria and before Solomon Kane stalked the shadows of the Puritan world, there was Bran Mak Morn. In him, Robert E. Howard poured the rawest, most melancholic depths of his singular genius. Bran Mak Morn: The Last King is not merely a collection of sword-and-sorcery tales; it is a dark, elegiac monument to a dying civilization, capturing the heartbeat of a creator whose fascination with doom, heritage, and primal fury defined an entire genre.

At the core of this definitive volume lies a haunting central theme: the tragic inevitability of decline. Unlike Howard’s more triumphant barbarian conquerors, Bran Mak Morn is a monarch burdened by the encroaching twilight of his people. The Picts, once the undisputed masters of ancient Europe, have degenerated into fragmented tribes of savages, caught in a merciless vice between the advancing legions of Rome and the encroaching might of the Celtic tribes. Bran stands as the sole unbroken thread to a glorious past, a tragic figure destined to wage a heroic yet doomed war against the currents of history. This thematic preoccupation with entropy and lost glory gives the collection a brooding, atmospheric weight that sets it apart from standard pulp adventure.

Howard’s literary style in these pages is nothing short of visionary. He writes with a feverish, rhythmic intensity that evokes the mists of antiquity and the blood-soaked soil of ancient Britain. His prose is visceral and sensory, painting landscapes drenched in ominous shadows, pagan ritual, and primordial dread. Howard does not merely tell stories; he conjures an emotional state. The horror elements woven into the narrative—most notably in the undisputed masterpiece “Worms of the Earth,” where Bran descends into horrifying depravity to achieve his vengeance—showcase an author fearless of the dark. Furthermore, tales like “Kings of the Night” demonstrate Howard’s sweeping historical and mythic scope, cleverly bridging the epochs of his fictional universes by summoning the mighty King Kull across the gulf of time to fight shoulder-to-shoulder with the Pictish king.

This 2005 publication elevates Howard’s written word through stunning material curation. The volume is wonderfully enriched by the evocative, gothic illustrations of award-winning artist Gary Gianni, whose artwork serves as the perfect visual echo to Howard’s shadowy prose. For both scholars and devoted fans, the inclusion of previously unpublished stories, story fragments, and photographic reproductions of Howard’s own handwritten manuscript revisions offers an intimate, fascinating glimpse into the creative crucible of a master storyteller. 

Bran Mak Morn: The Last King is an essential volume for anyone who wishes to understand the foundations of modern dark fantasy. It proves that Howard’s brilliance extended far beyond muscular barbarians and swinging broadswords; it lived in the tragic poetry of a fallen king crying out against the night.
