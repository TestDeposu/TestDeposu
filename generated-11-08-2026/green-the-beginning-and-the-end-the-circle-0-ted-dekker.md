# Green: The Beginning and the End (The Circle, #0)

**Author:** Ted Dekker  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2010

**Green: The Beginning and the End – A Cosmic Battle Between Light and Shadow**

Ted Dekker’s *Green: The Beginning and the End* is not merely a sequel—it is a thunderous, apocalyptic reckoning, a sprawling epic that blurs the line between myth and modern fantasy. Set in a world where humanity has been reborn after a cataclysmic collapse, the novel unfolds as a desperate struggle between the forces of *Green*—a divine, life-giving energy—and the encroaching darkness of *Black*, a malevolent corruption that seeks to consume all. Dekker, master of high-stakes theological allegory, weaves a tale where faith, sacrifice, and cosmic warfare collide, demanding nothing less than the reader’s full engagement.

At its core, *Green* is a battle for the soul of the world. Two thousand years after the original apocalypse, humanity has been granted a second chance—one that comes with a terrifying responsibility. The *Green* is not just a force; it is a revelation, a divine gift that makes the unseen visible, the spiritual tangible. Yet, this very visibility is a double-edged sword. The ancient evil Teeleh, long dormant in the Black Forest, senses the vulnerability of a world now exposed to its influence. When Thomas Hunter—a man who once fought to restore the Circle—awakens in that cursed land, he finds himself thrust into a war that transcends time and dimension. The stakes could not be higher: the fate of the Circle, the survival of Samuel (Thomas’s estranged son), and the very existence of *Green* hang in the balance.

Dekker’s prose is as relentless as it is vivid, a hallmark of his signature style. He doesn’t shy away from the brutal, the grotesque, or the sublime—each element serves a purpose in his grand narrative. The world-building is immersive, painting a landscape where nature itself is a battleground. The *Green* is described with almost sacred reverence, while the *Black* is a creeping, suffocating horror, a force that twists reality into something unrecognizable. Dekker’s ability to blend biblical prophecy with modern dystopian elements creates a unique fusion, where the old meets the new in a clash that feels both timeless and urgently relevant.

What makes *Green* particularly compelling is its exploration of redemption and betrayal. Thomas Hunter, a man broken by loss and doubt, is forced to confront not only the darkness of Teeleh but also the betrayal of his own son, Samuel. Dekker masterfully crafts a father-son dynamic that is as heart-wrenching as it is morally complex. Samuel’s defection is not just personal—it is a turning point in the cosmic struggle, a moment where the line between hero and villain blurs. Dekker forces his readers to question: How far would you go to save the world? And at what cost?

The novel’s pacing is relentless, with Dekker dropping the reader into the thick of the action while still allowing room for theological musings. The *Green* is not just a power—it is a philosophy, a way of seeing the world that demands faith in the unseen. Dekker’s writing is unapologetically bold, often pushing the boundaries of what is acceptable in mainstream fantasy. There are moments of raw, visceral horror, but also profound moments of hope, where the *Green* shines through the darkness like a beacon.

One of the most striking aspects of *Green* is its refusal to offer easy answers. Dekker doesn’t present the *Green* as a flawless force—it is beautiful, but it is also demanding, requiring sacrifice and faith. Similarly, the *Black* is not just an evil force; it is a reflection of human corruption, a temptation that even the most devout can falter under. This moral ambiguity elevates the story beyond a simple good-vs-evil narrative, making it a deeply philosophical exploration of faith, free will, and the cost of redemption.

For fans of Dekker’s work, *Green* will feel like a natural evolution of his themes—cosmic battles, divine intervention, and the struggle between light and dark. However, it also stands on its own as a gripping, high-concept fantasy that will appeal to readers of both Christian allegory and mainstream epic fantasy. Dekker’s ability to blend the two seamlessly is what makes this book so compelling.

In the end, *Green: The Beginning and the End* is more than just a story—it is a call to arms. It is a reminder that the battle for the soul of the world is never truly over, and that even in the darkest of times, there is always the possibility of *Green*. Dekker delivers a tale that is as epic as it is intimate, a story that will leave readers breathless and eager for what comes next.
