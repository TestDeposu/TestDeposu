# Empire Ascendant (Worldbreaker Saga, #2)

**Author:** Kameron Hurley  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2015

**Empire Ascendant: A World of Blood, Betrayal, and Cosmic Horror**

Kameron Hurley’s *Empire Ascendant* is the kind of book that doesn’t just rewrite the rules of fantasy—it dismantles the genre’s foundations and builds something far stranger, darker, and more morally complex in its place. The second installment in the *Worldbreaker Saga*, this novel doesn’t just expand on its predecessor’s themes; it deepens them into a sprawling, visceral epic where power is a curse, loyalty is a liability, and the universe itself seems determined to unmake everything.

At its core, *Empire Ascendant* is a story about the cost of survival in a world where the line between ally and enemy is blurred by shared blood—and shared fate. The novel opens with the aftermath of invasion, where an empire from another world has descended upon Dhai, not as conquerors, but as doppelgängers. These invaders don’t just look like the people they’ve come to destroy; they *are* them, reincarnated in a grotesque, cyclical war that spans millennia. The empire’s soldiers are the reanimated ghosts of Dhai’s own people, their faces twisted into something unrecognizable yet undeniably familiar. This isn’t just a war—it’s a horror, a cosmic punishment where the victims become the executioners.

Hurley’s prose is as sharp as it is unflinching, weaving together political intrigue, brutal warfare, and surreal magic with a prose style that’s equal parts poetic and clinical. Her descriptions of the "sentiment plants"—living organisms that absorb and reflect emotion like a living mirror—are haunting, while her portrayal of the dark star Oma, a celestial harbinger of doom, feels like something plucked from a Lovecraftian nightmare. The worldbuilding is dense but never overwhelming, with each culture, magic system, and historical detail serving a purpose—whether to deepen the mystery of the empire’s origins or to underscore the futility of resistance.

The novel’s strength lies in its characters, who are as flawed and morally ambiguous as the world they inhabit. The illegitimate ruler, the scullery maid with unpredictable magic, the enslaved scholars carrying a secret that could unravel empires—each is forced to confront the question: *How far will you go to save what you love?* Hurley refuses easy answers, painting a world where heroism is often indistinguishable from cruelty, and where the only certainty is that every choice comes with a price. The betrayals are inevitable, the alliances temporary, and the stakes—both personal and cosmic—are staggering.

What makes *Empire Ascendant* truly unforgettable is its willingness to embrace the grotesque. The empire’s soldiers, reanimated by dark magic, are not just monsters—they are *people*, their souls twisted into something inhuman yet still recognizable. The magic system, rooted in blood and memory, feels both ancient and terrifying, a force that can heal or destroy with equal ease. And the world itself is a character, a living, breathing entity that seems to mock the characters’ struggles, indifferent to their fates.

Hurley’s style is a masterclass in controlled chaos. She balances grand, sweeping narratives with intimate, character-driven moments, ensuring that even as the world collapses around them, the characters remain vivid and compelling. The prose is lean, efficient, but never at the expense of atmosphere. There’s a sense of inevitability to the story, as if the characters are caught in a wheel of history they can’t escape, their actions doomed to repeat in an endless cycle of violence.

For fans of dark fantasy, *Empire Ascendant* is a must-read. It’s a story about the horrors of war, the weight of legacy, and the cost of survival in a world that seems determined to break you. Hurley doesn’t just tell a story—she immerses you in a nightmare, one where the only way forward is through the blood and ashes of the past. And if you thought the first book was ambitious, wait until you see where this saga is headed.
