# To Green Angel Tower, Part 2 (Memory, Sorrow, and Thorn, #3; Part 2)

**Author:** Tad Williams  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 1994

To Green Angel Tower, Part 2 brings the sprawling saga of Osten Ard to a breath‑taking crescendo, and Tad Williams proves once again why his name sits alongside the greats of modern fantasy. The central theme that pulses through this final volume is the fragile line between remembrance and oblivion—how the past, when unearthed, can either arm a desperate resistance or unmake the very fabric of reality. As the Storm King’s undead horde tightens its grip, Prince Josua’s dwindling coalition and Simon’s besieged League of the Scroll race against a blurring of time to recover long‑lost magics. Their quest is not merely for a weapon; it is a search for meaning in a world where memory itself becomes a weapon and a curse.

Williams’ style shines in the way he weaves lyrical description with relentless plot momentum. His prose feels both epic and intimate—vast battlefields are rendered with the same careful attention he gives to a whispered confession in a candle‑lit hall. The narrative shifts fluidly between the grand scale of kingdoms on the brink of collapse and the minute, internal struggles of characters haunted by loss, duty, and the seductive pull of forbidden knowledge. This duality creates a rhythm that mirrors the story’s own theme: the ebb and flow of memory, the rise and fall of empires.

What sets this installment apart is Williams’ willingness to let the world breathe. He does not rush to resolve every thread; instead, he allows the lingering scent of ancient ruins, the echo of forgotten songs, and the weight of ancestral guilt to settle in the reader’s mind. The result is a sense of profundity that lingers long after the final page, inviting contemplation on how societies remember their heroes—and how they sometimes forget the cost of those memories.

Fans of the series will recognize the hallmarks that earned Williams accolades from George R. R. Martin, Patrick Rothfuss, and Christopher Paolini: intricate world‑building, morally complex characters, and a mythic resonance that feels both timeless and freshly urgent. Even readers new to Osten Ard will find themselves drawn into a tale where the battle is not just for a throne, but for the very soul of a world teetering on the edge of forgetting.

In short, To Green Angel Tower, Part 2 is a masterful climax that balances spectacular spectacle with quiet, meditative depth—a testament to Tad Williams’ enduring power to make fantasy feel both monumental and intimately human.
