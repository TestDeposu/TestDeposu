# Gotrek & Felix: The First Omnibus

**Author:** William King  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2018

**Review: Gotrek & Felix: The First Omnibus by William King**

Step into the grim, perilous world-that-was with William King’s *Gotrek & Felix: The First Omnibus*, a definitive cornerstone of Black Library publishing and a masterclass in dark fantasy. This sprawling collection—anchored by the iconic novels *Trollslayer*, *Skavenslayer*, and *Daemonslayer*, alongside supplementary short stories—captures the chaotic essence of the Warhammer Fantasy universe. At its heart, the book is a timeless exploration of an unlikely and utterly compelling bond: that between the brooding, doom-seeking Dwarf Slayer, Gotrek Gurnisson, and his chronicler, the disgraced poet-turned-warrior, Felix Jaeger.

The central theme of the omnibus revolves around redemption, fate, and the heavy burden of honor. Gotrek is bound by a sacred oath to seek a glorious death in battle to expunge a forgotten sin, while Felix is bound by a drunken promise to witness and record his companion’s epic demise. This dynamic creates a rich narrative tension. Every clash against trolls, skaven, and daemons carries profound weight because the reader knows that while Felix desperately wants to survive to write the tale, Gotrek is constantly hurling himself toward oblivion. Their partnership bridges two vastly different cultures of the Warhammer world, grounding the grand, apocalyptic scale of high fantasy into a deeply human (and demi-human) buddy-adventure format.

William King’s writing style is the engine that drives this legendary saga. King possesses a rare gift for balancing bleak, gritty horror with genuine wit and swashbuckling adventure. His prose is fast-paced, punchy, and evocative, painting the forests, sewers, and frozen wastes of the Empire with visceral clarity. He avoids the trap of taking the grimdark setting too seriously all the time; instead, he injects moments of dark humor and genuine camaraderie that make the protagonists instantly endearing. The action sequences are visceral and breathless, yet King never loses sight of character development. Even as Nuln is overrun by rat-men or a Chaos-tainted expedition tests their limits in the far north, the emotional anchor remains the evolving trust between the towering Slayer and his human companion.

For both longtime veterans of the Warhammer hobby and newcomers looking for a thrilling entry point into fantasy fiction, this omnibus is essential reading. It establishes the tropes, the atmosphere, and the sheer grit that defined an entire era of tabletop tie-in literature. King laid down a gold standard for dynamic duos, crafting a saga that is as humorous as it is terrifying, and as tragic as it is triumphant.
