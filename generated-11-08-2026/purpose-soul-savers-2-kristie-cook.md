# Purpose (Soul Savers, #2)

**Author:** Kristie Cook  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2021

Purpose, the second installment in Kristie Cook’s Soul Savers series, plunges readers deeper into the tumultuous world of Alexis Ames—a woman torn between her mortal façade and the divine‑infernal lineage that courses through her veins. While the novel can be enjoyed on its own, its true power emerges when read as part of the trilogy, where each book reframes the stakes of Alexis’s struggle and amplifies the series’ central question: what does it mean to fulfill a purpose when that purpose demands the sacrifice of everything you hold dear?

At its heart, Purpose explores the theme of identity forged in crisis. Alexis begins the story already besieged by erratic impulses and disturbing delusions, symptoms of the demonic blood that threatens to overwhelm her humanity. Cook uses these psychological fissures not merely as plot devices but as a lens through which to examine the universal tension between who we are expected to be and who we feel compelled to become. Alexis’s internal battle mirrors the external war raging between the Angels’ army and Satan’s minions, suggesting that the true battlefield lies within the soul. The novel asks whether purpose is a predetermined destiny or a choice forged in the crucible of suffering—a question that resonates strongly with readers navigating their own crossroads of ambition, family, and belief.

Cook’s storytelling shines brightest in her ability to weave high‑octane action with intimate, character‑driven moments. The prose is kinetic yet lyrical; battle scenes pulse with visceral detail—clashing wings, crackling ethereal energy, the scent of ozone—while quieter passages linger on Alexis’s raw, almost confessional introspection. This duality creates a rhythm that propels the narrative forward without sacrificing emotional depth. The author’s style is notably accessible, avoiding overly ornate language in favor of clear, immediate imagery that allows readers to feel the heat of a fight or the chill of doubt in the same breath. Dialogue snaps with authenticity, particularly in the exchanges between Alexis and her husband Tristan, whose own hybrid nature offers a counterpoint to her turmoil. Their conversations often serve as the novel’s moral compass, highlighting themes of loyalty, trust, and the willingness to endure pain for the sake of a greater good.

The world‑building in Purpose is both expansive and tightly integrated with the plot. Cook introduces new factions—mages, shifters, vampires—each with distinct customs and hidden agendas, enriching the mythos without overwhelming the primary storyline. The matriarch’s council, depicted as a web of political intrigue reminiscent of contemporary power struggles, adds a layer of satire that grounds the supernatural elements in recognizable human frailty. By presenting the council members as “seedy, power‑hungry, greedy politicians,” Cook invites readers to reflect on how corruption can infiltrate any institution, celestial or otherwise. This grounding enhances the sense that Alexis’s fight is not just against external demons but against the perennial human temptation to sacrifice principle for power.

One of the novel’s most striking achievements is its handling of sacrifice. Alexis’s journey forces her to confront the possibility that fulfilling her Amadis purpose may require relinquishing the very people she loves most—her son, her husband, her friends. Cook does not shy away from portraying the agony of such choices; instead, she renders them with a palpable sense of grief and resolve. The narrative’s tension builds as Alexis’s writing—an outlet through which she processes her turmoil—becomes both a refuge and a weapon, symbolizing how creativity can transmute pain into purpose. This metafictional touch underscores the author’s belief that storytelling itself can be an act of resistance and reclamation.

While the novel’s pacing is relentless, there are moments when the sheer volume of supernatural lore threatens to overshadow character development. Some secondary characters, intriguing as they are, receive brief sketches that leave readers wanting more depth. However, this minor shortfall does not detract significantly from the overall impact; the focus remains firmly on Alexis’s internal evolution, and the supporting cast serves primarily to illuminate different facets of her conflict.

In summary, Purpose delivers a compelling blend of heart‑pounding action, poignant introspection, and richly layered world‑building. Kristie Cook’s style—direct, emotive, and vivid—propels the reader through a narrative that is as much about the battle for one’s soul as it is about the war between angels and demons. The novel’s exploration of purpose as both burden and blessing offers a resonant meditation on the lengths we will go to honor the calling we feel, even when that calling demands the ultimate sacrifice.
