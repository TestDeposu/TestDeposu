# Thousand Autumns: Qian Qiu (Novel), Vol. 1

**Author:** Meng Xi Shi  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2023

In the sprawling landscape of Chinese danmei and historical fantasy, few authors dissect the human condition with the surgical cynicism of Meng Xi Shi. With the first volume of Thousand Autumns: Qian Qiu, Meng Xi Shi invites readers into a morally suffocating world where virtue is treated as a terminal illness and cruelty is the ultimate currency. This is not a tale of star-crossed romance or comfortable heroism; it is a masterclass in philosophical warfare, wrapped in the gorgeous, bloody aesthetics of traditional wuxia.

At the center of this gripping narrative is a collision of irreconcilable worldviews, embodied by two men standing on opposite poles of the martial world. On one side stands Yan Wushi, the supreme leader of the demonic Huanyue Sect. Yan Wushi is a delicious literary archetype: a brilliant strategist, an unapologetic sadist, and an incurable cynic. To Yan Wushi, altruism is a delusion, kindness is a calculated weakness, and every human heart beats to the rhythm of selfishness. He views the world as a grand, grotesque theatre, and he takes perverse pleasure in exposing its rot.

Opposite him is Shen Qiao, the abbot of the prestigious Xuandu Mountain Daoist Sect. Shen Qiao is the antithesis of the demonic master—gracious, unyielding in his principles, deeply charitable, and utterly devoted to the greater good. He is precisely the kind of idealistic fool that Yan Wushi despises. 

The brilliance of Meng Xi Shi’s narrative engine ignites when these two orbits violently collide. Following a devastating and treacherous duel that leaves Shen Qiao broken, blinded, and left for dead, Yan Wushi stumbles upon his rival. Rather than ending his life, the demonic leader concocts a cruel, fascinating experiment. He takes the ruined Daoist under his wing with a singular, malicious goal: to break Shen Qiao's unwavering faith in humanity. Yan Wushi wants to drag him through the mud, test the absolute limits of his moral fortitude, and ultimately corrupt him into the demonic fold. 

This dynamic forms the thematic beating heart of the novel. Meng Xi Shi asks a timeless, provocative question: Is goodness merely a luxury of the privileged, easily abandoned when survival requires savagery? As the synopsis astutely notes, it is remarkably easy to remain righteous atop a mountain peak that touches the heavens, but a thousand autumns toiling on the blood-soaked earth will test the resolve of any man. Through Shen Qiao’s agonizing descent and Yan Wushi’s relentless psychological prodding, the author explores whether moral integrity is a fragile illusion or an unbreakable steel spine.

Meng Xi Shi’s writing style in Thousand Autumns is sharp, atmospheric, and intellectually stimulating. The prose dances effortlessly between high-stakes martial arts choreography and deep philosophical discourse. The pacing of Volume 1 is deliberate, prioritizing character psychology and world-building over instant gratification. Readers will find themselves intoxicated by the verbal sparring between the two leads—conversations laced with double meanings, biting sarcasm, and subtle psychological dominance. Yan Wushi’s dialogue, in particular, is a masterclass in toxic fascination; he is a character you love to hate, yet cannot look away from.

Furthermore, the world of Jianghu painted here is gritty, politically volatile, and drenched in historical realism. Meng Xi Shi does not romanticize the pugilistic world; it is a harsh environment where alliances shift like sand and weakness is punished by death. Yet, even within this bleakness, Shen Qiao’s quiet resilience acts as a stubborn beacon of light, making his struggle against Yan Wushi’s psychological torture all the more compelling.

Thousand Autumns: Qian Qiu, Vol. 1 is a triumph of character-driven historical fantasy. It challenges the reader's own perceptions of morality and offers a slow-burn dynamic that is as intellectually thrilling as it is emotionally turbulent. For fans of intricate power plays, philosophical debates disguised as martial arts duels, and complex, morally grey relationships, this novel is an absolute must-read. Meng Xi Shi has crafted a dark, beautiful epic that proves true strength is not forged in comfort, but discovered in the ashes of utter devastation.
