# Love by the Book

**Author:** Jessica George  
**Page Count:** Unknown  
**Publication Date:** 2026

In her eagerly awaited sophomore novel, Love by the Book, Jessica George cements her reputation as one of the most perceptive chroniclers of modern female vulnerability. Following her breakout success, George returns with a story that pivots away from traditional romantic tropes to champion a far more enduring and often overlooked bond: the profound strength of platonic female friendship. 

The narrative is driven by two distinct, sharply drawn protagonists navigating the destabilizing friction of modern womanhood. Remy is an author whose life has unraveled just as professional demands reach a fever pitch. Her debut novel, built upon the bedrock of her close-knit friend group, became a runaway hit. Yet, reality proves less malleable than fiction. As her best friends drift away due to geographical relocation, impending motherhood, and a toxic romantic reunion, Remy finds herself creatively paralyzed and profoundly isolated. Parallel to her is Simone, a kindergarten teacher balancing a lucrative, clandestine side hustle that affords her luxury while demanding emotional detachment. When her family discovers the true nature of her work and cuts her off, Simone is forced to confront the chilling reality of her own self-imposed solitude.

When Remy and Simone literally collide in a bookstore, George orchestrates a meeting that avoids the lazy clichés of instant cinematic chemistry. Instead, their initial encounter is prickly, guarded, and laced with the defensive mechanisms of two women holding heavy, unshared secrets. George’s stylistic brilliance shines in these quiet, character-driven interactions. She does not rush the healing process. The gradual dismantling of their emotional armor feels earned, messy, and deeply authentic. 

The central theme of Love by the Book is a refreshing rejection of the cultural script that positions romantic love as the ultimate climax of a woman’s journey. George explores the restorative nature of platonic love with tenderness, wit, and emotional intelligence. She asks a vital question: what happens when the friends who anchored your life suddenly pull away, and how do we learn to build new sanctuaries of support from unexpected places? 

George’s prose is effortlessly readable, balancing laugh-out-loud humor with piercing emotional resonance. She captures the anxieties of contemporary life—creative burnout, the loneliness of sudden success, family estrangement, and the delicate art of letting people in—without ever losing her buoyant, soulful touch. Love by the Book is a funny, compassionate, and deeply reassuring read that proves the most enduring love stories of our lives are often the ones we share with our chosen sisters.
